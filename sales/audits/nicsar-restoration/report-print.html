The 5 problems costing you the most 🔴

In order of how much money we believe each one is costing you.

1. Your phone number is hidden on mobile phones Losing calls now

The call button in your website header is set to hide itself on phones and tablets, and the phone number shown on your city pages is plain text — tapping it does nothing. Someone standing in a flooded basement at 2am is almost always on their phone. Right now, the site makes them work to call you.

The fix: two settings changes in the page builder — un-hide the header phone button and make every number tap-to-call. About 1–2 hours.

2. Only 2 Google reviews Biggest ranking lever

This is the single biggest gap between you and the two companies above you on the map. It's not a website change — it's a habit: every completed job ends with a review request (a text with a direct link works best).

The fix: start a simple review-request routine today. A realistic target is 15 new reviews in 60 days, which changes how both Google and customers see you.

3. One of your pages is about the wrong town Broken page

Your "Fire Damage Restoration — Barrington Hills" page actually says Lemont everywhere — the headline, the text, even the behind-the-scenes data Google reads. It looks like a copy-paste step got missed when the page was created. That page can't rank for Barrington Hills, and it confuses Google about Lemont too.

The fix: rewrite that one page for Barrington Hills. About 1 hour.

4. Google sees three different home cities for your business Trust damage

Your Google Business Profile address says Hoffman Estates. The description on that same profile says "Located in Carpentersville, IL." And your BBB listing says Plainfield — filed under "Kitchen Remodel." Google cross-checks these sources to decide how much to trust you. Right now they contradict each other.

The fix: edit the Google profile description (5 minutes) and update the BBB listing's address and category (~30 minutes).

5. Your homepage competes with itself SEO error

Your homepage and your Hoffman Estates water damage page have the exact same title in Google — so Google has to pick one and usually demotes the other. The homepage is also missing its main headline tag (an "H1"), which is the first thing Google reads on a page.

The fix: give the homepage a broader title (e.g., "Nicsar Restoration — 24/7 Water, Fire & Storm Damage Restoration | Chicagoland") and one settings change to restore the headline. About 30 minutes.

The full findings, in plain English

All findings shown in full.

Your 28 city pages look like copies to Google Content · 34

You have a water-damage page and a fire-damage page for each of ~14 towns. We compared them side by side: pages for different cities share 88–92% identical text — only the town name really changes. All 28 pages also share:

  • The same single customer quote ("Michael R.") — word for word, on every page
  • The same map, pinned to Hoffman Estates — even on the Naperville and Schaumburg pages
  • The same photos, steps, and FAQ answers

Google calls these "doorway pages" and quietly stops showing them — which is exactly why your Schaumburg and Naperville pages don't appear in the top 10 for those towns. The fix isn't more pages, it's making the important ones genuinely local: a real review from that town, that town's map, a sentence or two about actual local jobs or flood-prone areas.

Other trust gaps: no street address anywhere on the site, no owner or technician named, "Certified Team" without saying certified by whom (the industry credential is called IICRC — if you hold it, say so), and the words "24/7" and "emergency" never appear in the site's text. Competitors who outrank you all promise a specific arrival time ("on-site in 60 minutes"); you say "fast response" with no number.

Small but embarrassing: the "Read More" buttons on your Services page link to pages from your old Carpentersville website that no longer exist, and the Facebook icon in the footer links to nothing.

Page speed — the site is slow for first-time visitors Performance · ~35

The most important visitor — someone who's never been to your site — waits about 2.7 seconds before anything starts appearing, because the site has no caching switched on. (Repeat visits are fast, which hides the problem from you.)

  • No caching plugin and Cloudflare's page caching is off — one switch fixes most of this
  • The same icon font is loaded six times; 32 style files load before anything shows
  • Two homepage slideshow photos are ~400KB each — 4–5× heavier than they need to be
  • The big homepage image is loaded in a way browsers can't prioritize, delaying the first thing visitors see

Speed matters double for you: Google ranks faster sites higher, and someone with water pouring in doesn't wait for a slow page.

Invisible to ChatGPT & Claude — by accident AI visibility · 28

More and more homeowners ask AI assistants "who should I call for water damage near me?" Your website's settings (added automatically by Cloudflare) currently block ChatGPT and Claude from reading your site at all — so to those tools, your business barely exists. Ironically, the settings don't block the bots that collect data for AI training, which is what that blocklist was presumably for.

  • Good news: Google's AI answers and Perplexity can still see you
  • The fix is 10 minutes: turn off two specific blocks in Cloudflare, keep the rest
  • To actually get recommended by AI tools you also need what they look for: a Yelp presence, marked-up FAQs, and one genuinely helpful guide page (e.g., "What to do in the first hour after water damage")
Business info Google can read — mostly missing Schema · 18

"Schema" is a machine-readable business card embedded in your website — it's how Google confirms your phone, address, hours, and service area without guessing. Yours currently contains only your business name and logo. No phone. No address. No hours. No list of towns you serve.

  • Your FAQ answers and customer reviews exist on the pages but aren't marked up, so Google can't use them for the enhanced listings competitors get
  • The review stars markup on your Reviews page is broken in a way Google can't read at all
