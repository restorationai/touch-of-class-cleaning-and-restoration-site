## What to Look For Before You Call Any Company

The company you call in the first hour after a water loss sets the trajectory for your entire claim. A few things to verify before you commit:

**IICRC certification.** The [IICRC S500 standard](https://www.iicrc.org) is the industry benchmark for water damage mitigation. An IICRC-certified firm has trained technicians who know the difference between a Class 1 loss (small area, low absorption) and a Class 4 loss (specialty drying required). Ask directly: "Are your technicians IICRC certified?"

**Contractor license.** In Washington State, any company performing reconstruction after mitigation must hold a valid General Contractor Certificate of Registration from the [WA Department of Labor and Industries](https://www.lni.wa.gov). Verify the license number before signing anything.

**Written scope before work begins.** A reputable company provides a written scope of work and a cost estimate before extraction starts. If a company wants you to sign an open-ended authorization, slow down.

**In-house reconstruction.** Many mitigation-only companies hand off the rebuild to a third contractor. This creates gaps in accountability and can slow your claim. If you want one point of contact from extraction through finished drywall, confirm the company handles reconstruction internally.

**Insurance documentation.** Your adjuster will want moisture readings, drying logs, and photo documentation. Ask whether the company uses industry-standard software (Xactimate is the most common) and whether they communicate directly with your adjuster. For more on what your policy is likely to cover, see our post on [average insurance payouts for water damage](/blog/average-insurance-payout-for-water-damage/).

## What to Do Right Now

If water is still entering the structure, stop the source first: shut off the main water supply valve (usually in the garage, utility room, or near the meter at the street). If the source is a roof leak or exterior intrusion, move valuables out of the affected area and document everything with your phone camera before touching anything.

Then call a certified company. The longer wet materials sit, the more expensive the loss becomes.
