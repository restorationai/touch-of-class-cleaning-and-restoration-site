## What to Look for When Choosing a Fire Restoration Company

Choosing the right company after a fire comes down to three things: credentials, scope, and communication.

**Credentials** mean IICRC certification (specifically the Fire and Smoke Restoration Technician, or FSRT, credential) and a valid Washington State contractor license. You can verify any WA contractor license at the [L&I Contractor Lookup](https://secure.lni.wa.gov/verify/). An unlicensed contractor cannot legally pull permits for structural repairs, which creates problems when you go to sell the property.

**Scope** means whether the company can handle the entire loss or only part of it. Fire damage almost always requires: emergency stabilization (board-up, tarping), contents pack-out and cleaning, structural drying if water was used to suppress the fire, soot and smoke cleaning on all affected surfaces, odor treatment (thermal fogging, hydroxyl generation, ozone), and structural repair or full reconstruction. A company that only does mitigation will hand you off mid-job, which creates coordination gaps and insurance documentation headaches.

**Communication** means a single point of contact who speaks to your adjuster directly, provides a written scope of work before any work begins, and documents every phase with photos. The [fire damage restoration process](/blog/fire-damage-restoration-process/) post covers what each phase involves and what your adjuster will expect to see.

For a detailed breakdown of what fire restoration typically costs in Washington, see the [fire damage restoration cost guide](/blog/fire-damage-restoration-cost/).

## What to Do Right Now If You've Had a Fire

Before you call any restoration company, do these three things:

1. **Get clearance from the fire department.** Don't re-enter until the structure is declared safe.
2. **Call your insurance company to open a claim.** You don't need a restoration estimate first. Opening the claim starts the clock on your coverage.
3. **Document everything before cleanup begins.** Photos and video of every affected room, every piece of damaged contents. This protects your claim.

Then call a restoration company. The [what to do after a house fire](/blog/what-to-do-after-a-house-fire/) guide walks through the full first 72 hours step by step.
