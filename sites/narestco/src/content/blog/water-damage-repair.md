## How to Compare Water Damage Repair Companies Before You Call

The right company depends on your specific situation. Here is what to check before you commit:

**1. Are they IICRC certified?** 
The [IICRC S500](https://www.iicrc.org) is the industry standard for water damage restoration. Insurance adjusters expect drying logs and moisture readings that follow this protocol. A non-certified company may dry the space, but their documentation may not hold up during claim review.

**2. Do they hold a Washington State contractor license?** 
If water damage reaches your framing, subfloor, or drywall, repairs require a licensed general contractor. Verify any company's license at the [WA Dept. of Labor and Industries contractor lookup](https://secure.lni.wa.gov/verify/). NARESTCO's license is NATIORC792M6.

**3. Do they offer 24/7 response?** 
Water damage compounds by the hour. The [IICRC S500 standard](https://www.iicrc.org) classifies losses in part by how long water has been sitting. A company that only answers during business hours is not equipped for emergency mitigation.

**4. Can they handle reconstruction?** 
Mitigation (drying and demo) and reconstruction (rebuild) are often handled by separate companies. That handoff creates gaps in your claim timeline and documentation. A company licensed for both, like NARESTCO, keeps the project under one scope.

**5. Do they work directly with your insurance adjuster?** 
The best restoration companies build the written scope, submit it to your carrier, and handle the supplement process when additional damage is found. Ask any company you call whether they provide a written scope before work begins. If they say no, that is a red flag. ## What to Do Right Now If You Have Water Damage

If water is still entering the structure, stop the source first: shut off the main water supply or call your utility. Then call a restoration company. Do not wait for the insurance adjuster to arrive before starting mitigation. Most policies require you to take reasonable steps to prevent further damage, and delays can give the carrier grounds to reduce your payout.
