## Who Should I Call First for Water Damage in Federal Way?

For water damage restoration in Federal Way, call National Restoration Construction at (206) 883-0333. They answer 24/7, are IICRC certified, and carry Washington State license NATIORC792M6. That said, restoration is not always the first call depending on what is happening in your home right now.

Here is the triage order:

1. **911 or your utility (Puget Sound Energy: 1-888-225-5773)** if there is any electrical hazard, a gas smell, or structural collapse risk. Water and live wiring are a life-safety issue. Do not enter a room with standing water if you are unsure about the electrical status.
2. **Your plumber or building maintenance** if water is still actively flowing and you cannot shut it off at the main. A restoration crew cannot start extracting until the source is controlled. Most burst-pipe calls in Federal Way's older 1970s-80s ramblers involve a supply line that needs a plumber to cap before mitigation can begin.
3. **Your restoration company** once the source is stopped and the scene is safe. This is the call that stops secondary damage (mold can begin colonizing wet materials within 24-72 hours per [IICRC S500 guidelines](https://www.iicrc.org)).
4. **Your insurance company** after you have called restoration, not before. Mitigation must start immediately to limit your loss. Most policies require "prompt notice" but do not require you to wait for adjuster approval before stopping active damage. Document everything with photos and video before any work begins.

## What Happens When You Call a Restoration Company?

A qualified restoration company will walk you through the immediate steps on the phone, then dispatch a crew to assess and begin mitigation. Here is what the process looks like from that first call forward.

**The first call (5-10 minutes):** You will describe the source, the affected rooms, and whether the water is still flowing. A good dispatcher will ask whether you have shut off the main, whether there are any safety hazards, and will confirm your address and insurance carrier. Have your policy number ready if you have it, but do not delay the call to find it.

**Arrival and assessment:** The crew arrives with moisture meters, thermal imaging cameras, and extraction equipment. They map the moisture migration, which in Federal Way's older housing stock often travels farther than expected. A 1980s rambler's subfloor can wick water under vinyl flooring for 15-20 feet before it becomes visible. The crew documents everything with photos and moisture readings, which becomes part of your insurance claim file.

**Extraction and drying setup:** Standing water is extracted first using truck-mounted or portable extractors. Then the crew sets up a drying system: typically LGR (low-grain refrigerant) dehumidifiers and high-velocity air movers positioned to create airflow across wet surfaces. The [IICRC S500 standard](https://www.iicrc.org) defines drying goals by material class and water category. A legitimate crew dries to standard, not just until things feel dry to the touch.

**Monitoring:** Crews return daily (or every other day) to check moisture readings and adjust equipment. Drying a typical residential loss takes 3-5 days. If readings are not dropping on schedule, the scope expands, which is documented and reported to your insurer. For a deeper look at timelines, see [how long water damage restoration actually takes](/blog/how-long-does-water-damage-restoration-take/).

**Documentation and claim support:** A restoration company handles the scope of work documentation that your adjuster needs. This includes moisture logs, equipment placement records, and a written scope before reconstruction begins. Understanding what your policy covers is worth reviewing before the adjuster calls. See the [average insurance payout for water damage](/blog/average-insurance-payout-for-water-damage/) for what homeowners in Washington typically receive.

## What Should I Have Ready When I Call?

You do not need everything on this list, but having it speeds up the process.

- **Your address and the best way to access the property** (gate code, lockbox, etc.)
- **Where the water came from**, if you know (supply line, appliance, roof, sewer)
- **Whether the water source is stopped**
- **How long the water has been present**, even a rough estimate
- **Your homeowners insurance carrier and policy number** (find it on your declarations page or your insurer's app)
- **Whether anyone in the home has health conditions** that affect PPE or containment decisions (mold-sensitive occupants, for example)

If you are dealing with a sewage backup rather than clean water, say so on the call. Category 3 water, also called black water, requires different PPE and containment protocols and affects how the crew stages the job. The [health risks of sewage backup](/blog/sewage-backup-health-risks/) are serious enough that this detail matters from the first conversation.

## What About Mold? When Do I Need to Worry?

Mold becomes a real concern if wet materials are not dried within 24-72 hours. Federal Way's climate does not help: with 37+ inches of annual rainfall and persistent humidity, the Pacific Northwest baseline moisture level is already elevated. A loss that sits over a weekend before drying starts is a loss that almost certainly has secondary mold growth by the time crews arrive Monday.

If the water event happened more than 48 hours ago and you are seeing discoloration, musty odor, or visible growth, mention it on the first call. The restoration scope may need to include mold inspection and testing alongside the water mitigation. The [crawl space mold removal guide for Federal Way](/blog/crawl-space-mold-removal-federal-way/) covers why this is especially common in South King County homes.

The [EPA's mold guidance](https://www.epa.gov/mold) recommends professional remediation for any mold growth covering more than 10 square feet, and that threshold is reached faster than most homeowners expect once drywall and subfloor are involved.
