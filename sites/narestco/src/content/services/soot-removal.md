Soot doesn't wait. Within hours of a fire being extinguished, fine acidic particles begin etching into drywall, discoloring grout lines, and embedding in carpet fibers. The longer it sits, the deeper it bonds — and the more surfaces become permanent casualties instead of salvageable ones. ## How We Handle Soot Removal

Soot residue isn't one thing. A kitchen grease fire leaves a wet, oily film that smears if you wipe it wrong. A structural fire involving drywall, insulation, and synthetic materials produces dry smoke residue that can travel through HVAC ducts and coat surfaces two rooms away from the fire itself. Protein smoke — from burning food or animal matter — is nearly invisible but produces a sharp, persistent odor that clings to painted walls and cabinet interiors.

Our technicians identify the residue type first, because the cleaning chemistry and technique depend entirely on what burned. Dry soot on flat latex-painted walls responds to dry chemical sponges before any wet cleaning begins; applying water first drives the particles deeper. Wet or oily residues require alkaline degreasers applied in controlled passes. Porous masonry and textured ceilings get HEPA-vacuum pre-treatment before any chemical contact.

Equipment on a typical soot damage cleanup job includes:

- **HEPA air scrubbers** running continuously to capture airborne particulates during cleaning
- **Thermal foggers and hydroxyl generators** for odor neutralization — not masking
- **Dry chemical sponges, micro-fiber systems, and low-alkaline cleaning agents** matched to surface type
- **Moisture meters** to confirm walls haven't absorbed water from firefighting efforts

Soot wall cleaning follows a top-down, room-by-room sequence so dislodged particles don't re-contaminate cleaned surfaces. We document every affected area with photos before and after — a record your insurance adjuster will want.

## When to Call Us

The obvious trigger is a fire — but many calls we receive aren't from total-loss situations. A stovetop fire that was extinguished in minutes can still coat an entire kitchen in oily residue. A furnace puff-back can push fine black soot through every supply vent in the house. A neighboring unit fire in a condo building can push smoke through shared wall cavities and HVAC systems into units that never saw flames.

Call us if you notice any of these after a fire event, large or small:

- Black or gray film on walls, ceilings, or window frames
- A persistent smoky or acrid smell that doesn't clear after ventilating
- Discoloration around electrical outlets, light switches, or HVAC vents
- Gritty texture on countertops or furniture surfaces
- Visible streaking on painted surfaces, especially near doorframes and corners

**What not to do before we arrive:** Don't run your HVAC system — it will redistribute soot through the ductwork. Don't wipe walls with a wet cloth or paper towel. Don't use household cleaners; most are the wrong pH for soot chemistry and will smear residue into the surface. Open windows if it's safe to do so, and limit foot traffic through affected rooms.

Time matters. Soot's acidic chemistry begins etching glass within 24 to 48 hours and can permanently stain grout, natural stone, and uncoated metal within 72 hours. Post-fire soot cleanup is not a weekend project.
