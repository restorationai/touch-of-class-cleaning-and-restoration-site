## What vandalism and graffiti removal actually involves

Graffiti removal is not one method. What works on glass will ruin brick, and what cleans brick will strip the paint off siding. The whole job starts with reading the surface. Porous materials, brick, concrete, unsealed masonry, absorb spray paint down into their pores, so removing it means chemical removers formulated for that substrate or low-pressure media blasting, both applied carefully to avoid etching the surface underneath. Non-porous surfaces like glass and metal usually give the paint up more easily to solvents or careful mechanical removal. Painted siding sits in the middle and is the most deceptive, because a remover strong enough to lift the graffiti can lift the underlying paint with it, which sometimes makes a clean repaint the better repair than an aggressive scrub.

The honest part of this work is knowing the limits. On sealed surfaces, full removal is usually achievable. On raw, weathered brick or concrete that has soaked up a tag, a faint shadow can remain no matter how careful the cleaning, and the realistic fix at that point is resealing or repainting rather than chasing a stain that has bonded into the material.

Vandalism is broader than graffiti. Break-ins and malicious damage leave broken windows, forced doors and frames, damaged locks, and interior damage from the intrusion itself. That work runs the other direction, from securing the open property to repairing the structure, and it often has to happen fast because an open building keeps losing.

## Our process

1. **Assessment and substrate identification.** We identify the surface, brick, concrete, siding, glass, metal, and the type and age of the damage, then match the removal method to the substrate so the fix does not create a permanent stain or etching. For break-ins, we scope the full loss, not just the obvious broken pane.

2. **Secure and board up first.** If the property has been left open by a break-in or a smashed window, we secure and board the opening to keep it weather-tight and closed to further entry before other work begins. In this climate, that step protects the interior from rain overnight.

3. **Graffiti removal matched to the surface.** Chemical removers, substrate-safe low-pressure blasting, or solvent-and-scrape work is applied according to the material. Where a shadow persists on porous surfaces, we discuss resealing or repainting rather than over-scrubbing.

4. **Property repair.** Broken windows are replaced, damaged frames, doors, and locks are repaired, and interior damage from the intrusion is restored. As a licensed general contractor we can carry the repair through to finish rather than handing it off.

5. **Documentation and cleanup.** The damage and the completed work are photographed and logged in the format insurance adjusters expect, the work area is cleaned, and debris is hauled away.

## What separates a good vandalism job from a bad one

The most common way graffiti removal goes wrong is the wrong method on the wrong surface. Pressure-washing raw brick at high pressure drives paint deeper and pits the masonry. Using an aggressive solvent on painted siding strips the paint and leaves a worse mark than the tag. Good operators identify the substrate first and test in an inconspicuous spot before committing, because on porous surfaces there is no undo.

For break-ins, the failure is treating the visible damage and stopping there. A smashed window frequently comes with a bent frame, a compromised lock, and interior water or entry damage that a fast patch job misses. Scoping the whole loss, and securing the property before it takes on more damage, is what keeps a one-night vandalism event from turning into a much larger repair.

The third is leaving a property open. Every hour a building sits unsecured after a break-in is an invitation for weather intrusion and a second incident. Board-up is not a formality; it is loss prevention.

## Seasonal and regional considerations

The Puget Sound climate raises the stakes on the board-up side of this work. Persistent rain through the wet season means an unsecured window or forced door can let significant water into a Federal Way property overnight, turning a break-in into a water damage loss on top of the vandalism. Securing openings quickly is more urgent here than in a dry climate.

Weather also affects graffiti removal itself. Cold, wet conditions slow the chemical removers used on masonry and complicate resealing and repainting, which need dry surfaces and moderate temperatures to cure properly. Scheduling substrate-safe removal and any follow-up sealing around dry weather windows produces a more durable result.
