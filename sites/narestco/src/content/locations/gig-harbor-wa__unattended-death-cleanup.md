## Why Gig Harbor's Housing Stock Complicates After-Death Cleanup

Many of the homes closest to the Gig Harbor waterfront and throughout the Millville and Harborview neighborhoods were built during the mid-20th century fishing and boatbuilding era. These houses typically sit on vented crawl spaces designed for a drier era — crawl spaces that now trap ground moisture year-round under the salt-air marine climate. When an unattended death goes undiscovered for days or weeks, biological material can migrate through subfloor gaps and saturate the vapor barrier, insulation, and framing below the living space. That secondary contamination is invisible from above and easy to miss without thermal imaging and air sampling.

Older plaster-and-lathe wall assemblies common in these homes also absorb odor compounds differently than modern drywall. Hydroxyl and ozone treatment cycles need to run longer to penetrate dense plaster, and deodorization that appears complete at the surface can resurface weeks later if the underlying structure wasn't addressed. Our IICRC-certified technicians account for these building characteristics before closing out any job in the area.

## Our Unattended Death Cleanup Process in Gig Harbor

Every job begins with a confidential assessment — no marked vehicles, no unnecessary personnel on site. We document affected areas, establish containment, and remove all biologically compromised materials following Washington State Department of Health and EPA guidelines for biohazardous waste transport and disposal.

Deodorization follows remediation, not the other way around. For homes in ZIP code 98335 and surrounding areas, where marine air infiltrates through older window seals and crawl space vents, we use a layered approach: HEPA air scrubbing, enzyme-based treatment of porous surfaces, and extended hydroxyl generator cycles calibrated to the square footage and construction type. When subfloor or crawl space contamination is confirmed, we coordinate structural drying and, if needed, limited reconstruction — framing, subfloor sheathing, insulation replacement — under our General Contractor Certificate of Registration (#NATIORC792M6) issued by WA State Labor & Industries.

We work directly with the property owner, estate attorney, or designated family representative — whoever is managing the situation — and keep communication discreet throughout.
