## Why Pacific Properties Face Particular Challenges After a Fire

Pacific sits in a low-lying stretch of the Green River valley where humidity rarely drops low enough for smoke-damaged materials to dry on their own. That persistent moisture matters because soot is not just a staining problem. The acidic residue left by burning synthetic materials, insulation, and household contents begins etching metal surfaces within the first 24 to 72 hours. On uncoated copper plumbing, aluminum window frames, and appliance finishes, that window is short.

The city's housing stock adds another layer of complexity. A meaningful share of Pacific's residential properties were built during the post-war suburban expansion of the 1950s through 1970s, when builders used wood-lath plaster walls, older-growth fir framing, and construction methods that are no longer standard. Smoke infiltrates the cavities behind plaster walls differently than it does modern drywall assemblies: the surface may look manageable while the lath and framing behind it have absorbed odor-carrying particles deeply enough that surface cleaning alone will not resolve the smell. Identifying that hidden contamination requires thermal imaging and cavity sampling, not a visual inspection.

## Our Fire Damage Restoration Process in Pacific

The first step on any fire loss is stabilization. Before cleaning begins, the structure needs to be secured against weather intrusion, because an open roof or broken windows in Pacific's wet climate will introduce secondary water damage within a single rain event. Board-up and tarping happen the same day crews arrive.

From there, the process follows a documented sequence:

- **Scope and documentation**: Every affected surface is photographed and catalogued for the insurance carrier before any material is disturbed. This protects the claim and establishes a clear baseline.
- **Dry demolition**: Charred and smoke-saturated materials that cannot be restored are removed carefully to avoid cross-contaminating salvageable areas.
- **Soot and smoke cleaning**: Depending on the fire type, crews use dry chemical sponges, HEPA-filtered vacuuming, and wet cleaning agents appropriate to the surface. Protein fires from kitchen events require different chemistry than structural fires involving building materials.
- **Odor neutralization**: Thermal fogging and hydroxyl generation reach into wall cavities and HVAC pathways that surface wiping cannot access.
- **Structural repair and reconstruction**: As a licensed general contractor, the same team that handles mitigation can carry the job through drywall, framing, painting, and finish work, so the property owner is not managing two separate contractors.

All drying and cleaning protocols are performed to IICRC standards.
