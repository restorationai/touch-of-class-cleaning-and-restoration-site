## Why Fife Properties Face Distinct Fire Damage Challenges

Fife's position in the Puyallup River lowlands shapes how fire and smoke damage behaves after a loss. The area's characteristically damp Pacific Northwest air, especially during the long stretch from October through April, means that smoke residue and soot absorb ambient moisture quickly. What looks like a surface deposit on drywall or trim can become a bonded, oily film within days if not treated promptly. Homes built before the 1980s in Fife frequently have original wood-framed interiors with little vapor barrier, which allows smoke odor to migrate into wall cavities and subfloor assemblies faster than in newer construction.

The industrial character of Fife's commercial zones also matters for residential properties nearby. Fires that start in or near warehouses, auto shops, or freight facilities can introduce petrochemical residues into the smoke plume. Those residues behave differently than residues from a kitchen fire, requiring different cleaning chemistry and longer deodorization cycles to fully neutralize.

## Our Fire Damage Restoration Process in Fife

Every fire loss is different, but the sequence of decisions in the first 24 to 48 hours determines how much of the structure and contents can be saved. The process begins with a thorough assessment: documenting the burn pattern, identifying which materials are structurally compromised, and testing air quality to understand how far smoke has migrated. From there, the work moves in a deliberate order.

Board-up and weatherization come first to secure the property and stop additional moisture intrusion, which is a real concern given Fife's rainfall averages. Debris removal and controlled demolition of unsalvageable materials follow, with careful separation of materials that may contain lead-based paint or asbestos, both of which appear in Fife's older housing stock. National Restoration Construction holds Lead-Safe Certified Firm status and EPA certification, so that part of the process is handled under the correct regulatory framework without requiring a separate contractor.

Structural drying, smoke and soot cleaning, and odor neutralization run in parallel once the affected areas are opened up. IICRC-certified technicians guide the drying and cleaning phases, using thermal fogging and hydroxyl generation for deep odor removal in spaces where smoke has penetrated porous materials like insulation, carpet padding, and wood framing.

## Local Note

Fife's older residential blocks, particularly those developed in the 1950s and 1960s closer to the Puyallup River side of the city, often have original hardwood floors and plaster-and-lath interior walls. After a fire, plaster walls present a specific challenge: they can appear intact and cleanable on the surface while harboring smoke odor deep in the substrate, because plaster is porous and the lath behind it is raw wood. Crews who treat these walls the same way they would treat modern drywall often leave odor behind. Recognizing this pattern early changes the cleaning and deodorization plan before it becomes a callback.
