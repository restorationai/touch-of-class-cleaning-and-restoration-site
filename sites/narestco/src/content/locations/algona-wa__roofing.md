Algona sits in the rain shadow of the Green River Valley corridor, but that shadow offers no real shelter from the sustained wet seasons that push through the Puget Sound lowlands every fall and winter. Roofs here deal with months of low-angle rainfall, moss-friendly humidity, and temperature swings that cycle roofing materials through expansion and contraction more times per year than homeowners typically realize. When a roof reaches the end of its service life in this climate, the deterioration tends to accelerate fast, and waiting a season too long often means interior damage follows close behind.

## Why Algona Properties Face Accelerated Roofing Wear

The Pacific Northwest's wet season is long, but the specific conditions in and around Algona add a few layers of pressure that homeowners should understand before scheduling a roof evaluation.

First, the tree canopy. Much of the residential stock in the area sits beneath or adjacent to mature Douglas fir and cedar. These trees deposit debris continuously, and that debris holds moisture against shingles for days after rain stops. Organic material trapped in valleys and along flashing lines is one of the fastest routes to granule loss and eventual sheathing rot.

Second, the housing mix. Algona's residential properties include a range of construction eras, and older homes in the area frequently have rooflines with lower pitches that were common in mid-century Pacific Northwest construction. Low-pitch roofs drain more slowly and are more vulnerable to standing water if gutters back up or flashing lifts at the edges.

Third, moss and algae are not cosmetic problems here. In this climate, biological growth on asphalt shingles breaks down the mat beneath the granule surface, shortening the effective life of a roof by years if left untreated between replacements.

## Our Roofing Installation and Replacement Process in Algona

Every roofing project begins with a full inspection of the existing system: decking, underlayment, flashing at every penetration, ridge and hip conditions, and soffit and fascia integrity. In the Algona area, we pay particular attention to the condition of the plywood or OSB sheathing beneath the surface layer, because sustained moisture infiltration in this climate often means sheathing damage extends well beyond the visible leak point.

Once the scope is confirmed, tear-off and disposal are handled in a single mobilization whenever possible to minimize the window your home is exposed. New underlayment goes down before any other material, with ice-and-water shield extended further up the eaves than the minimum code requirement, because the freeze-thaw cycles that occur at the lower elevations around the Green River Valley can drive ice damming even on roofs that don't see heavy snowfall.

Material selection is discussed with each homeowner based on the specific roof geometry, budget, and how long they plan to hold the property. Architectural asphalt shingles remain the most common choice in this area for cost-to-lifespan balance, but standing seam metal and composite options are increasingly requested on homes where moss management is a recurring concern.

All work is performed under National Restoration Construction's General Contractor Certificate of Registration with Washington State Department of Labor and Industries, and the company carries the IICRC certification and Lead-Safe Certified Firm status that matter when older homes involve legacy materials during tear-off.

## Local Note: Moss Treatment Timing in This Climate

One detail that comes up repeatedly on roofing projects in the Algona and broader Auburn-area corridor: moss treatment applied at the time of installation is only effective if the product has time to cure before the first sustained rain. In this region, the installation window between late summer and early October is genuinely the best time to apply zinc or copper-based moss inhibitors and get the cure time the product needs. Homeowners who schedule replacement in November or December often find that the inhibitor washes off before it bonds properly. If your project lands in the wet season, we discuss this tradeoff directly and adjust expectations about the first-year moss suppression results accordingly.
