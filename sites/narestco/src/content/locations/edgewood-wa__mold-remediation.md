Whether the problem is behind drywall in a newer construction home near the city's southern edges or inside a crawl space that has been slowly wicking ground moisture for years, mold rarely announces itself until the colony is already well established. ## Why Edgewood Properties See Mold Issues

Edgewood occupies a stretch of the Puget Sound lowlands where the water table can be surprisingly shallow, particularly in lower-lying sections closer to the valley floor. Homes built on crawl space foundations, which are common throughout this part of Pierce County, are especially vulnerable. Vapor barriers degrade, vents get blocked by debris, and condensation builds up on floor joists and subfloor sheathing over years of wet winters. By the time a homeowner notices a musty smell in the living space above, the mold colony underneath may already cover a significant portion of the crawl space.

Edgewood also has a notable mix of housing built from the 1970s through the early 2000s, a period when building codes permitted vapor management practices that are now understood to be inadequate. Fiberglass batt insulation stapled between floor joists, for example, holds moisture against wood framing rather than allowing it to dry, and that trapped moisture is exactly where Cladosporium and Penicillium species tend to colonize first. Bathrooms, laundry rooms, and any area near a plumbing penetration are secondary hot spots, particularly in homes where exhaust fans were undersized or vented into the attic rather than outside.

## Our Mold Remediation Process in Edgewood

Every remediation starts with a thorough inspection, not a visual scan. We use moisture meters and thermal imaging to locate moisture that has migrated behind surfaces, because mold growing on visible drywall is almost always fed by a wetter zone you cannot see. Once we understand the extent of the problem, we establish containment using negative air pressure and poly barriers to prevent spores from spreading to unaffected areas of the home during the removal process.

Affected materials are removed, bagged, and disposed of according to Washington State guidelines. HEPA air scrubbers run continuously throughout the project. Structural surfaces that can be dried and treated rather than removed are cleaned using EPA-registered antimicrobial agents and then dried to the moisture content thresholds outlined in the IICRC S520 standard. We do not close up walls or restore finishes until moisture readings confirm the substrate is genuinely dry, not just dry at the surface. Our team holds IICRC certification, Lead-Safe Certified Firm status, and EPA certification, which matters on older Edgewood homes where disturbing building materials can involve lead paint.

## Edgewood Insurance Coordination

Mold remediation coverage depends heavily on the underlying cause. If mold developed because of a sudden covered loss, such as a burst pipe or a roof failure after a windstorm, most standard homeowner policies will cover at least a portion of the remediation cost. Mold that developed slowly from a long-term moisture condition is often excluded, though the water damage that caused it may still be a covered claim. We document the loss thoroughly, photograph affected materials before removal, and communicate directly with adjusters to give your claim the clearest possible paper trail.

## Local Note

Crawl spaces in Edgewood and the surrounding parts of Pierce County often have vents positioned on only two sides of the foundation, a layout that was code-compliant when many of these homes were built but that creates stagnant air pockets in corners where moisture accumulates. When we inspect a crawl space here and find mold concentrated in the corners farthest from the vents, that pattern almost always points to inadequate cross-ventilation rather than a plumbing leak. Addressing the mold without correcting the airflow means the colony comes back. We flag ventilation deficiencies in our inspection report so the homeowner can address the root cause, not just the visible symptom.
