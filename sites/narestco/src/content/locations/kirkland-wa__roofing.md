## Why Kirkland Properties Face Distinct Roofing Challenges

Kirkland's climate is shaped by its position on the east shore of Lake Washington. Moisture-laden air off the lake keeps humidity elevated even during dry spells, and the city's elevation changes, from the waterfront in Juanita and Houghton up through the forested ridgelines of Finn Hill, create microclimates where rainfall totals and wind exposure vary meaningfully block by block.

Older lakefront homes, particularly those built in the mid-century era, were often constructed with low-slope rooflines that looked elegant but were designed before modern waterproofing membranes existed. Those roofs accumulate standing water at valleys and around chimneys. Moss and lichen, which thrive in Kirkland's shade and moisture, are more than cosmetic problems: the root systems of moss physically lift shingle tabs, breaking the seal and creating pathways for water to reach the decking. Once the decking is saturated, the damage moves fast.

In newer developments around Totem Lake and in the HOA-governed townhome communities that have expanded along the 98034 corridor, roofing work carries a different set of constraints. Architectural review boards often specify approved shingle colors, profiles, and even manufacturer lines. Getting the wrong material approved after the fact adds weeks to a project timeline.

## Our Roofing Installation and Replacement Process in Kirkland

Every project begins with a full roof inspection, not just the surface but the decking, underlayment, ventilation pathway, and all penetrations including skylights, vents, and chimney flashings. In Kirkland's climate, ventilation is frequently the hidden problem: attic condensation caused by inadequate airflow degrades decking from the inside out, and a new roof over compromised decking is a short-term fix at best.

Once the scope is established, we handle material selection with the local conditions in mind. Architectural asphalt shingles with a Class 4 impact rating perform well against the hail events that occasionally accompany spring storms in the greater Seattle area. For homes where an HOA has specified materials, we work directly from the approved product list and coordinate any required pre-approval documentation before ordering.

During installation, existing shingles and underlayment are removed to bare decking. Damaged decking sections are replaced before any new material goes down. Ice-and-water shield is applied at all eaves, valleys, and penetrations, which is standard practice in this climate and not optional. New ridge ventilation is installed or restored to ensure the attic breathes correctly year-round.

Homes along the Juanita Bay Park waterfront and the streets immediately above the Marina Park area tend to have older cedar shake roofs that were installed when shake was the regional default material.
