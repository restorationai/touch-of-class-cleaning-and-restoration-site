## Why Kent's Housing Stock and Climate Shape This Work

Kent sits in a geography that creates specific conditions for unattended death situations. East Hill and West Hill neighborhoods are dense with single-family homes built between the 1960s and 1990s — construction eras that commonly used particleboard subflooring, fiberglass batt insulation, and wall cavities with limited vapor barriers. These materials are highly absorbent. When a situation goes undetected for an extended period, biological material and associated odor compounds migrate into subfloor layers and wall assemblies far more deeply than in newer construction with sealed concrete or modern moisture-resistant materials.

Kent's climate compounds this. Annual rainfall averages around 40 inches, and interior humidity stays elevated through most of fall, winter, and spring. That ambient moisture slows the drying of affected materials and creates conditions where secondary biological concerns can develop quickly in adjacent areas. Homes near Lake Meridian or in lower-lying sections of the Kent Valley — where crawl space moisture is already a known issue — require extra assessment of what's beneath the affected floor plane before any remediation plan is finalized.
