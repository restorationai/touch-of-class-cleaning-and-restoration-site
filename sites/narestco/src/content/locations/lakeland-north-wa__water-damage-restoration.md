## Why Lakeland North Properties See Water Damage Issues

The housing stock across Lakeland North skews toward construction from the 1980s and 1990s, a period when builders in this part of Pierce County relied heavily on OSB subfloor panels and paper-faced drywall. Both materials absorb and retain moisture aggressively. A slow leak behind a dishwasher or a pinhole failure in a copper supply line can saturate a 10-square-foot section of subfloor within 24 hours, and the damage is rarely visible until flooring begins to buckle or a musty odor develops in an adjacent room.

The regional water table and the clay-heavy soils common throughout this stretch of the Puget Sound lowlands also contribute to crawl space intrusion during the November-through-March rainy season. Homes with vented crawl spaces and older vapor barriers are particularly vulnerable. When groundwater pressure builds against a foundation and the vapor barrier fails, the crawl space becomes a reservoir that slowly wicks moisture upward into floor joists and insulation.

## Our Water Damage Restoration Process in Lakeland North

Every job begins with a thorough moisture mapping survey using thermal imaging and calibrated moisture meters. This step matters more than it might seem in Lakeland North's typical construction: OSB reads deceptively dry at the surface while the core remains saturated, and thermal imaging catches temperature differentials that reveal hidden moisture pathways behind walls.

Once the scope is documented, the water removal phase uses truck-mounted extraction equipment capable of pulling standing water and embedded moisture from carpet, pad, and subfloor simultaneously. Structural drying follows immediately, using industrial-grade desiccant or refrigerant dehumidifiers and high-velocity air movers positioned according to the IICRC S500 standard for psychrometric drying. Moisture readings are logged at each visit so the drying curve is documented for your insurance carrier and for any permit-required inspections.

If building materials cannot be dried in place within acceptable timeframes, controlled demolition removes only what is necessary. National Restoration Construction holds a General Contractor Certificate of Registration with Washington State Department of Labor and Industries, which means the same team that performs mitigation can carry the work through to reconstruction without a handoff to a separate contractor.
