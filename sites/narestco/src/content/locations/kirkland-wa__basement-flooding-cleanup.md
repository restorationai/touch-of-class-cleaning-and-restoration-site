## Why Kirkland Properties See Basement Flooding Emergencies

Kirkland sits along the western shore of Lake Washington, and the surrounding topography — rolling hills, clay-heavy soils, and a water table that rises sharply through the wet season — creates real drainage pressure on basement walls and floor slabs. From October through April, the region averages 35–40 inches of annual rainfall, most of it arriving in sustained, ground-saturating events rather than quick downpours. That means sump pumps run hard, window wells overflow, and older homes built before modern waterproofing standards — many of Kirkland's residential neighborhoods date to the 1960s through 1980s — face hydrostatic pressure their original foundations weren't designed to handle long-term.

Beyond weather, aging municipal infrastructure in older pockets of the city occasionally contributes to sewer backups and lateral line failures. The result is that wet basement restoration calls in Kirkland tend to cluster after multi-day rain events and after hard freezes that stress pipes.

## Our Basement Flooding Cleanup Process in Kirkland

When we arrive, the first priority is stopping ongoing water intrusion if the source is still active — shutting off supply lines, identifying sump pump failure, or documenting the entry point for your insurance adjuster. From there, the process follows a structured sequence:

**1. Moisture Mapping and Documentation**
Before extraction begins, technicians use thermal imaging cameras and calibrated moisture meters to map the full extent of saturation — including water that has migrated behind baseboards, under flooring, or into wall cavities. This documentation matters for your insurance claim.

**2. Bulk Water Extraction**
Truck-mounted extraction units pull standing water out of the space at a rate that portable shop-vacs simply can't match. For deeper flooding — anything over two inches — this step alone can take 30 to 90 minutes depending on square footage.

**3. Structural Drying**
High-capacity LGR (low-grain refrigerant) dehumidifiers and axial air movers are placed in a calculated pattern to drive moisture out of concrete, framing, and subfloor materials. Drying typically runs three to five days, with daily moisture readings to confirm progress. We don't pull equipment early just to close the job.

**4. Antimicrobial Treatment**
Any surfaces that contacted category 2 or category 3 water — which includes most sewer backups and ground-water intrusion — receive EPA-registered antimicrobial treatment to inhibit mold colonization during the drying window.

**5. Rebuild Coordination**
If drywall, flooring, or framing needs to come out, our general contractor license (WA State L&I #NATIORC792M6) means we can handle the rebuild phase without you coordinating a second company.
