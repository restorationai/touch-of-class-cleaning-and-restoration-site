## Why Bellevue Properties Are Vulnerable to Sewage Backups

Bellevue's combination of aging lateral sewer lines in established neighborhoods like Factoria, Crossroads, and Lake Hills — many of which were installed in the 1960s and 70s — and the relentless wet season the Pacific Northwest delivers from October through April creates a reliable recipe for sewer line backups. When heavy rainfall saturates the ground, it infiltrates cracked clay or cast-iron pipes, overwhelming the municipal system and forcing sewage back up through the lowest drain in your home. Homes in lower-elevation areas near Mercer Slough or along the I-90 corridor can be particularly susceptible when storm events coincide with high groundwater.

Septic systems in Bellevue's more rural eastern parcels face a different pressure: seasonal saturation of drain fields that have no room to absorb effluent, leading to septic overflow cleanup situations that can affect both the interior and the yard. Tree root intrusion is another consistent culprit — the same mature firs and maples that make Bellevue neighborhoods beautiful will find and exploit any crack in a sewer lateral over time.

## Our Sewage Cleanup and Sanitization Process in Bellevue

When our IICRC-certified technicians arrive, the first priority is containment — isolating the affected area to prevent cross-contamination from spreading to unaffected rooms. Here's what the process looks like from arrival to clearance:

**1. Safety assessment and containment.** We identify the source, confirm the water has been shut off or the backup has been controlled, and establish physical barriers. Category 3 "black water" — the classification for raw sewage — requires full PPE and strict containment protocols.

**2. Extraction.** Truck-mounted extraction units pull standing sewage and contaminated water from hard floors, carpet, and subfloor cavities. For sewage that has wicked into wall cavities, we use specialized injectidry systems to reach moisture that surface equipment can't touch.

**3. Demolition of unsalvageable materials.** Porous materials — drywall below the flood line, saturated insulation, carpet and pad — cannot be effectively sanitized once contaminated with raw sewage. We remove them, document everything photographically for your insurance claim, and dispose of waste in compliance with Washington State Department of Ecology guidelines.

**4. Sanitization and antimicrobial treatment.** Once the structure is clean and dry, we apply EPA-registered antimicrobial agents to all affected surfaces. This step addresses the bacteria, viruses, and pathogens that sewage carries — not just the visible mess.

**5. Drying and monitoring.** Commercial-grade desiccant dehumidifiers and air movers run until moisture readings in walls, floors, and framing return to pre-loss baselines. We monitor daily and document readings throughout.

**6. Reconstruction.** As a licensed General Contractor (WA L&I #NATIORC792M6), we handle the rebuild — new drywall, flooring, cabinetry — so you're not coordinating a second contractor after the remediation crew leaves.

Whether your sewage backup is covered depends on your specific policy — standard homeowner's policies often exclude sewer backup unless you've added a rider, while commercial property policies vary widely. What we can do is make the documentation airtight regardless of coverage outcome.
