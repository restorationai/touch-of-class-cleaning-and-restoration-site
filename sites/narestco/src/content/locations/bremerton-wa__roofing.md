On older homes in Charleston and East Bremerton, the underlying roof decking is often skip-sheathing, spaced boards rather than plywood, which was standard for cedar shake roofs but leaves gaps that modern shingles can't fully bridge without a solid decking layer installed first.

A roof replacement here isn't just a material swap, it's often a structural reassessment.

## Our Roofing Installation and Replacement Process in Bremerton

Every project starts with a full roof inspection that documents decking condition, flashing integrity, ridge ventilation, and the condition of any penetrations, skylights, plumbing vents, and chimney saddles are common leak sources on older Bremerton homes. We photograph and report on everything before a single shingle comes off.

Once the scope is confirmed, the process moves in a deliberate sequence:

- **Tear-off and decking evaluation**: Old material is removed and the decking is inspected for rot, soft spots, and structural compromise. Skip-sheathing is replaced with OSB or plywood where needed before any new material goes down.
- **Underlayment and ice-and-water barrier**: Given Bremerton's rainfall volume, we install a synthetic underlayment across the full deck and extend ice-and-water shield at eaves, valleys, and around all penetrations, not just the minimum required by code.
- **Shingle or material installation**: We work with architectural asphalt shingles, metal roofing, and flat-roof membrane systems depending on the structure. Material selection accounts for roof pitch, HOA restrictions if applicable, and the home's existing ventilation profile.
- **Flashing, ridge cap, and ventilation**: Step flashing at walls and chimneys, continuous ridge vent where attic geometry allows, and proper soffit-to-ridge airflow are finished before final inspection.
