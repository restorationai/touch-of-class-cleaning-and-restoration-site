## What Trauma Scene Cleanup Actually Involves

The term covers a range of situations: unattended deaths, accidents involving bodily fluids, and other events that leave behind biological material requiring professional decontamination. What they share is this — standard cleaning products and household contractors are not equipped for it. Bloodborne pathogens and other biohazards require EPA-registered disinfectants, personal protective equipment, and disposal through licensed biohazard waste carriers. Our technicians are IICRC certified and work under OSHA bloodborne pathogen protocols. We handle the regulatory chain of custody so families don't have to.

The process typically moves in three phases: assessment and containment, decontamination and material removal, and final verification. Affected porous materials — carpet, subflooring, drywall — are removed when saturation cannot be fully addressed through surface treatment alone. ## How University Place Homes Affect the Cleanup Process

The dominant housing stock here — split-levels and ramblers built between roughly 1960 and 1980 — creates specific conditions our crews account for before they start. When biological material reaches a subfloor in a home like this, it can wick downward faster than it would in a slab-on-grade or newer construction. We probe affected areas carefully and, when necessary, access the crawl space to confirm containment.

Homes near the Chambers Creek corridor and in the Narrows View area often have daylight basements — partially below grade, partially exposed — which affects ventilation during decontamination.
