When something unthinkable happens inside a Seattle home or building, the last thing a family or property manager should have to navigate alone is what comes next. ## Why Seattle's Building Stock Complicates Crime Scene Remediation

A significant portion of Seattle's residential properties were built before 1950, and that matters in ways that aren't obvious at first glance. Plaster-over-lath walls common in Magnolia and Queen Anne absorb fluids differently than modern drywall — contamination can wick laterally through wall cavities and settle into subfloor assemblies before it's visible on the surface. Original fir flooring, still intact in many Wallingford and Greenwood homes, is often tongue-and-groove construction with no vapor barrier beneath it, which means biological material can reach the crawlspace.

Seattle's climate adds another layer. The city averages roughly 150 days of measurable precipitation per year, and ambient indoor humidity in older homes frequently runs higher than in drier climates. That moisture environment accelerates the timeline for secondary contamination concerns, which is one reason rapid, certified response matters more here than in, say, a Phoenix property of similar age.

For properties in newer construction — the mid-rise condos near South Lake Union or the stacked flats along Capitol Hill's dense corridors — the concern shifts. Shared HVAC risers and plumbing chases mean that odor and particulate can travel between units, creating coordination requirements with building management that single-family work doesn't involve.

## Our Crime Scene Cleanup Process in Seattle

Every job begins with a thorough assessment before any materials are removed. Technicians document affected surfaces, identify hidden migration paths, and determine whether structural components — subfloor, wall cavity, insulation — need to be addressed beyond the primary area. This step is not rushed; skipping it is how contamination gets missed.

All biological waste is handled and transported in compliance with Washington State Department of Ecology regulations and disposed of through licensed medical waste channels. Our team is IICRC certified, EPA certified, and holds Lead-Safe Firm certification — relevant in Seattle's pre-1950 housing stock where lead paint is common in wall layers that may need to be disturbed during remediation. We carry full licensure through WA State Department of Labor & Industries (General Contractor Certificate of Registration #NATIORC792M6).

Once physical remediation is complete, affected areas are treated with hospital-grade antimicrobials and tested to confirm contamination levels meet or exceed applicable standards. We do not consider a job finished until that confirmation is in hand. For properties in ZIP codes like 98103 or 98117 — dense Fremont and Greenwood corridors where homes share walls or sit on narrow lots — we also coordinate with neighbors or adjacent unit owners as needed to address any cross-boundary concerns.

## Local Note

In Seattle's older craftsman and Tudor homes — particularly in Ballard and Magnolia — we routinely find that original horsehair plaster has been skim-coated with modern joint compound at some point in the building's history. That layering creates a contamination trap: the outer compound layer may test clean while the original plaster beneath has absorbed material through micro-cracks. Our technicians probe both layers before signing off, a step that adds time but prevents callbacks and, more importantly, ensures the property is genuinely safe for reoccupancy.
