## Why Bellevue Properties See Roofing Deterioration Faster Than Expected

Bellevue's elevation gradient is the first thing a roofer notices. Homes in Somerset and the hillside streets above Factoria sit several hundred feet higher than the valley floor near Mercer Slough, and that difference matters in winter. Cold air pools at those elevations long enough to freeze meltwater at the eaves, forcing water back under shingles and into the decking before it can drain. Down in lower-lying areas like Lake Hills and Crossroads, the problem shifts: those neighborhoods see prolonged standing moisture from slower-draining soils, and moss and lichen colonize asphalt shingles within a few seasons if the roof pitch is shallow.

Bellevue's housing stock adds another layer of complexity. The city built aggressively through the 1970s and 1980s, and a large share of single-family homes in ZIP codes 98006 and 98008 are now carrying original or first-replacement roofs that predate modern synthetic underlayment standards. Those roofs were installed with felt paper that degrades faster under repeated wet-dry cycling, and once the felt fails, the decking absorbs moisture even when the shingles above look intact from the street.

## Our Roofing Installation and Replacement Process in Bellevue

Every project starts with a full deck inspection, not just a shingle count. We remove existing materials down to the decking, assess for soft spots, delamination, or rot, and replace compromised sections before anything new goes on top. Skipping that step is the most common reason a new roof underperforms in the Northwest climate.

Underlayment selection is calibrated to Bellevue's conditions. We use synthetic underlayment rated for high-moisture exposure rather than standard felt, and on roofs with lower pitches or known ice-dam history, we install a self-adhering ice-and-water shield that extends well beyond the eave line. Ventilation is addressed at the same time: many older Bellevue homes are under-ventilated at the ridge, which traps heat in summer and accelerates freeze-thaw cycling in winter.

Flashing around chimneys, skylights, and dormers is replaced rather than reused. Reusing old step flashing is a shortcut that shows up as a leak within two or three rainy seasons. We close out every project with a documented inspection and photo set, which matters for both insurance records and HOA submittals.

Somerset-area homeowners frequently discover that their roof's ice-dam damage is more extensive than the visible interior staining suggests. Because those hillside homes often have cathedral ceilings or minimal attic space, water that enters at the eave travels along the roof deck and surfaces inside at a point several feet away from where it actually infiltrated.
