Seattle's older neighborhoods carry a particular weight when something goes wrong inside a home. In craftsman bungalows across Ballard and the Tudor revivals that line the hillsides of Queen Anne, porous plaster walls, original hardwood subfloors, and decades of accumulated materials mean that biological contamination from a trauma event can penetrate surfaces that modern construction would shed. ## Why Seattle's Housing Stock Complicates Trauma Cleanup

Most trauma cleanup guidance is written for post-1980 construction: vinyl flooring, drywall, easy-to-seal surfaces. Seattle doesn't work that way. The pre-1950 homes that dominate Magnolia, Wallingford, and much of Capitol Hill were built with old-growth fir subfloors laid directly over crawlspaces — and those crawlspaces already carry ambient moisture from the region's wet winters. Biological material that reaches the subfloor in these homes doesn't stay on the surface; it wicks into the grain and, if not addressed within the right window, creates a secondary contamination problem beneath the visible floor plane.

Older plaster walls present a related issue. Unlike drywall, plaster is alkaline and dense, which changes how disinfectants interact with it and how thoroughly affected sections can be cleaned versus removed. Our IICRC-certified technicians assess each surface type before choosing a protocol — there is no single-pass solution in a 1920s Craftsman.

## Our Trauma Scene Cleanup Process in Seattle

Every job begins with a scene assessment and containment setup before any cleaning begins. We establish a controlled work zone, document conditions for insurance purposes, and identify which materials can be decontaminated and which require disposal under Washington State Department of Ecology guidelines for biohazardous waste.

From there, the process moves through three phases: removal of affected materials, surface decontamination using EPA-registered disinfectants appropriate to the substrate, and verification testing to confirm the space meets established safety thresholds before we leave. On Capitol Hill condos and newer South Lake Union units — where plumbing-stack failures and shared walls are common — we coordinate with building management to ensure containment doesn't affect adjacent units. On older single-family homes, we pay particular attention to subfloor integrity and crawlspace conditions before closing out.

All waste is transported and disposed of through licensed biohazardous waste carriers operating under Washington State permit requirements. We provide documentation of disposal for your records.
