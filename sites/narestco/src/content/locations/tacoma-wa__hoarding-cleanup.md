## Why Tacoma Homes Present Unique Challenges for Hoarding Remediation

Tacoma's marine climate means relative humidity rarely drops below 60% for much of the year. In a home where airflow has been restricted by years of accumulation, that ambient moisture creates conditions that accelerate secondary damage — mold colonization can begin on organic materials within 24 to 48 hours of a moisture event, and in a hoarding environment, moisture events often go undetected for far longer.

The housing stock in ZIP codes like 98403 and 98406 — covering much of North Tacoma and the Proctor District — skews heavily pre-1950. These homes frequently have uninsulated crawl spaces, single-pane windows, and original plaster walls that absorb and hold moisture differently than modern construction. What looks like surface-level gross filth cleanup on intake can involve underlying structural moisture, compromised subfloor, or pest activity that only becomes visible once the space is cleared. Our teams assess for all of it before the first item is moved.

## Our Hoarding Cleanup Process in Tacoma

Every project begins with a private, judgment-free walkthrough. We work with the property owner, family members, or estate representatives — whoever is coordinating — to understand what needs to be preserved, donated, or removed. Nothing is discarded without clear authorization.

Once the scope is agreed upon, our crew handles sorting, removal, and transport using sealed containers and covered vehicles. We do not use open-bed trucks that expose contents to neighbors or passersby. Discretion is built into the logistics.

After clearance, the remediation phase begins. Tacoma's municipal requirements for restoration work that generates airborne particulate mean we deploy HEPA filtration and negative air pressure containment as standard — not optional — on every hoarding cleanup job in the city. Our technicians are IICRC Certified and Lead-Safe Certified, which matters in pre-1978 homes where disturbing debris can expose lead-based paint dust. Surfaces are cleaned, treated, and documented with before-and-after photography for insurance and legal purposes.

Final steps include structural assessment, odor neutralization, and a written clearance report. If mold remediation or reconstruction is needed, we handle that in-house — no handoff to a second contractor.
