## Why Milton Properties Face Distinct Fire Damage Challenges

Milton's housing stock spans several decades, and that mix matters when it comes to fire and smoke restoration. Homes built before the 1980s frequently used wood-lath construction, older wiring configurations, and materials that absorb smoke residue differently than modern composites. Soot from a kitchen fire in a 1960s-era home can penetrate unpainted wood framing and original plaster in ways that surface cleaning alone won't address. The char goes deeper, and so does the odor.

The city's position along the Puyallup River valley also means humidity levels stay elevated for much of the year. ## Our Fire Damage Restoration Process in Milton

Every fire loss is different, but the sequence follows a logic grounded in the IICRC S700 standard for fire and smoke restoration. The process starts with a thorough assessment: what burned, what materials were affected by smoke, and what water damage the suppression effort left behind. Fire damage and water damage almost always arrive together.

From there, the work moves through structural stabilization, debris removal, and content separation. Salvageable items are catalogued and moved to a clean staging area. Affected structural materials are removed to verified clean lines, not just to where things look undamaged. Smoke residue can be present in materials that show no visible staining, and odor-causing particles embedded in insulation or framing will re-volatilize in warm weather if they aren't addressed at the source.

Odor neutralization follows physical cleaning. Thermal fogging and hydroxyl treatment reach into cavities and porous surfaces that wiping can't access. After that, the rebuild phase begins: framing, drywall, insulation, and finishes restored to pre-loss condition. National Restoration Construction holds a General Contractor Certificate of Registration with Washington State's Department of Labor and Industries, which means the restoration and the reconstruction happen under one roof rather than requiring a separate contractor handoff.

## Local Note

Older homes in Milton's established residential areas often have what contractors call "balloon framing," a construction method common before platform framing became standard after World War II. In a balloon-framed house, wall cavities run continuously from the foundation sill to the roof, with no fire blocking between floors. A fire that starts in a wall on the ground level can travel vertically through that open cavity to the attic before it's visible in any room. Restoration crews working in these homes probe wall cavities with thermal imaging and moisture meters well above and below the visible burn zone, because the actual extent of smoke and heat damage is rarely limited to what the eye can see at first walk-through.
