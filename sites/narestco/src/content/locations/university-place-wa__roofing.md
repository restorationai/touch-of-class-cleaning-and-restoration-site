## Why University Place Roofs Age the Way They Do

The Pacific Northwest climate is rarely dramatic, but it is relentless. University Place sits in the rain shadow of the Olympics just enough to reduce annual totals, but the moisture that does arrive tends to linger. Low-angle winter sun means north-facing roof planes on split-levels stay damp for days after a storm, accelerating moss and algae colonization at the shingle surface. Once moss rhizomes work under tab edges, they lift the adhesive bond and create pathways for water to reach the decking.

Properties along the Day Island waterfront face an additional variable: salt-laden air off the Sound degrades asphalt shingles and metal flashing faster than homes a mile inland. Galvanic corrosion at flashing joints is a common finding on Day Island homes that look fine from the street. Bluff-top lots above Chambers Creek Regional Park deal with a different problem: winter slope drainage that saturates the soil and pushes hydrostatic pressure against fascia and soffit systems, rotting the substrate before the visible shingles show any distress.

The split-level and rambler floor plans common in 98466 and 98467 also tend to have low-pitch roof sections over garages and additions. Low-pitch asphalt shingle installations require a different underlayment approach than steep-slope work, and skipping that step is one of the most common reasons a roof fails well before its rated lifespan.

## Our Roof Installation and Replacement Process in University Place

Every project starts with a documented inspection that goes beyond the shingle surface. We probe decking for soft spots, check ridge and hip lines for sagging, examine all penetrations (vents, skylights, chimney saddles), and photograph flashing conditions at every transition. That documentation matters for insurance claims and, in some cases, for HOA approval submissions.

Once scope is agreed, we strip to bare decking, replace any deteriorated sheathing, and install a code-compliant underlayment system appropriate to the roof pitch. On low-slope sections, that means a self-adhering membrane rather than felt. We then install the selected roofing material, whether architectural shingles, metal standing seam, or composition, and replace all flashing rather than reusing old material. Reusing original flashing on a re-roof is one of the most common shortcuts in the trade, and it is the reason many roofs leak within a few years of replacement.

Final inspection includes a water test at all penetrations and a debris sweep of gutters and downspouts. We pull the required Pierce County permit for every full replacement, and the inspection record stays with the property.

## Local Note: Low-Pitch Additions Are a Hidden Risk in University Place

Many of the original ramblers and split-levels in University Place were expanded in the 1980s and 90s with garage conversions, sunroom additions, or master suite bump-outs. These additions almost always have shallower roof pitches than the main structure, and they were often permitted under older code that did not require the same underlayment standards now in effect. When we inspect a home in Beckonridge or near the Fircrest border and see a newer addition attached to a 1960s original, we pay close attention to that transition point. Water tends to collect at the valley where the two rooflines meet, and if the addition's underlayment was installed to an older standard, that is typically where the leak originates, not at the oldest part of the roof.

If your home has an addition built before the mid-2000s, it is worth having that section evaluated separately during any roofing estimate.
