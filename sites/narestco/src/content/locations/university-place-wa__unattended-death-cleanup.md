## Our Unattended Death Cleanup Process in University Place

Every job begins with a contained, documented scope assessment — no assumptions, no shortcuts. A certified technician walks the property to identify all affected surfaces, materials, and airflow pathways before any work begins. From there, the process follows ANSI and IICRC standards for biohazard remediation:

- **Containment and access control** — affected areas are sealed to prevent cross-contamination to unaffected rooms, and we manage entry and exit carefully, particularly in homes with shared driveways or close neighboring properties.
- **Removal of affected porous materials** — subfloor sections, insulation, and soft goods that cannot be decontaminated to a verifiable standard are removed and disposed of in compliance with Washington State Department of Ecology requirements for biohazardous waste.
- **Surface treatment and decontamination** — EPA-registered disinfectants are applied to all affected hard surfaces, followed by verification testing.
- **Odor remediation** — this is often the step families underestimate. Odor compounds penetrate drywall, wood framing, and HVAC components. We use hydroxyl generators and thermal fogging calibrated to the specific square footage and material types in the home, not a one-size approach.
- **Final documentation** — a written clearance report is provided, which is typically required by estate attorneys, real estate agents, and insurers handling the property.
