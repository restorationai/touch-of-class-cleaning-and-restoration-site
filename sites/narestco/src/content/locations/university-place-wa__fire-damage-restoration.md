## Our Fire Damage Restoration Process in University Place

The first hour on-site is about containment and documentation, not cleaning. We photograph and document every affected room before anything is moved — a step that matters because Washington State insurance carriers require a complete pre-mitigation record for structural fire claims. We then seal HVAC returns to prevent soot from recirculating, board windows and doors, and place temporary roof tarps if the fire breached the roof deck.

Demolition of unsalvageable material comes next. In University Place's older split-levels, that often means removing original tongue-and-groove paneling and blown-in insulation that has absorbed smoke deeply enough that surface cleaning won't reach the odor source. Structural members are assessed individually — a beam that looks intact may have lost 40% of its load capacity depending on char depth, and our IICRC-certified technicians document those findings for the structural engineer of record.

Odor elimination follows a layered approach: thermal fogging to reach cavities, hydroxyl generation for occupied or sensitive areas, and ozone treatment in sealed spaces. We hold the space for 48 hours post-treatment and re-test before declaring odor mitigation complete. Reconstruction is handled under our Washington State General Contractor license (#NATIORC792M6), so there's no handoff gap between the mitigation crew and the rebuild crew.
