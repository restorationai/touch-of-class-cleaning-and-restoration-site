## Why Des Moines Properties Present Distinct Fire Damage Challenges

The residential neighborhoods closest to the Des Moines Marina and along Marine View Drive include a significant share of homes built between the late 1950s and early 1980s. That era of construction used materials that respond to fire and smoke differently than modern assemblies: original cedar siding chars deeply and holds odor compounds in its grain, older blown-in insulation can smolder without visible flame, and single-pane aluminum windows allow smoke to migrate into adjacent rooms faster than modern glazing would.

The marine climate adds another layer. Des Moines averages higher relative humidity than inland King County communities, which means smoke odor compounds bond more aggressively to porous surfaces when moisture is already present in the air and in the structure. Soot that might brush off cleanly in a drier climate can penetrate deeper into drywall paper and wood grain here, requiring more aggressive chemical sponging and, in some cases, controlled abrasive cleaning before any sealing or repainting begins.

Older homes in this area also commonly have crawl spaces rather than full basements, and fire suppression water drains directly into those crawl spaces. If that water isn't extracted and the space dried properly, secondary mold growth can begin within days, turning a fire loss into a compound claim.

## Our Fire Damage Restoration Process in Des Moines

The process starts with a thorough structural assessment before any debris removal begins. Smoke and heat travel in patterns that aren't always visible at first inspection, and charring on a surface beam doesn't tell the whole story about what's happened inside a wall cavity. The crew documents damage photographically and in writing, which supports the insurance claim and establishes a clear scope of work.

Once the scope is set, the sequence is: board-up and weather protection if the structure is open to the elements, controlled demolition of non-salvageable materials, dry ice blasting or chemical sponging on smoke-affected surfaces, HEPA air scrubbing to pull particulate from the air column, and odor neutralization using thermal fogging or hydroxyl generation depending on the materials involved. Structural repairs, framing replacement, and finish work follow after the affected areas pass clearance.

Because National Restoration Construction holds a General Contractor Certificate of Registration with Washington State Department of Labor and Industries, the same team that handles the cleanup can carry the project through permitted reconstruction, which avoids the gap that often delays timelines when a separate GC has to be brought in.

## Des Moines Insurance Coordination

Fire losses in Des Moines follow Washington State's standard homeowner policy structure, but the age of the housing stock creates some friction points with adjusters. Older materials, like original hardwood flooring or tongue-and-groove paneling, are sometimes flagged for depreciation rather than replacement cost, and documenting their pre-loss condition accurately matters. The team photographs and itemizes affected materials before any removal, providing the kind of detailed loss documentation that supports a full replacement-cost settlement rather than an ACV payout.

The crew also communicates directly with assigned adjusters and can meet on site during the initial walkthrough, which typically shortens the approval timeline for larger scopes.

## Local Note

Homes along the bluff above the Des Moines waterfront are built on slopes that were graded decades ago, and some of those lots have retaining structures or split-level foundations that affect how fire suppression water moves through and under the structure. On hillside properties, water from a second-floor fire can travel laterally inside the wall assembly and appear in an unexpected location on the lower level, well away from the visible burn area. The crew checks below and lateral to the fire origin point on any multi-level or slope-built home, because the water damage footprint on those properties rarely matches what the fire damage footprint suggests.
