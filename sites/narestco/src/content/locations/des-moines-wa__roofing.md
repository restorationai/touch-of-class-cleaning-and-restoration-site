Des Moines sits right along the Puget Sound shoreline, and that waterfront position means roofs here take a different kind of beating than properties even a few miles inland. The persistent marine moisture, the wind-driven rain off the Sound, and the freeze-thaw cycling that hits elevated lots near the bluffs all accelerate shingle degradation and push flashing failures years ahead of schedule. National Restoration Construction has been handling roofing installation and replacement in this corner of King County since 2004, and the work here calls for material choices and installation details that account for what this specific climate actually does to a roof over time.

## Why Des Moines Roofs Wear Out Faster Than You'd Expect

The marine climate along Puget Sound is relentless in ways that don't show up on a standard weather chart. Algae and moss colonize asphalt shingles quickly in the persistent low-light, high-humidity conditions that define fall and winter here. Once moss takes hold, it lifts shingle edges and allows water to wick underneath, where it sits against the decking. By the time a homeowner notices interior staining or a soft spot in the attic, the decking may already be compromised.

Homes closer to the water, particularly on the hillside lots that step down toward the Des Moines Marina area, also face wind exposure that flat-lot properties don't. Roofing systems on those elevated, west-facing slopes need higher nail counts and starter-strip adhesive that meets the wind uplift requirements for coastal exposure. Standard installation specs written for inland neighborhoods simply aren't adequate here.

Older housing stock in Des Moines adds another layer of complexity. Many homes built in the 1960s and 1970s were originally roofed with heavier materials, and the roof framing was sized accordingly. When those roofs were re-covered with lighter asphalt shingles in later decades, ventilation was sometimes left unchanged, which creates moisture trapping in the attic that shortens shingle life from below even when the installation above looks fine.

## Our Roofing Installation and Replacement Process in Des Moines

Every project starts with a full roof inspection that goes beyond counting missing shingles. We check decking condition, attic ventilation ratios, flashing integrity at every penetration and valley, and the condition of the drip edge along the eaves. In a coastal climate, those details matter as much as the shingles themselves.

Once the scope is confirmed, we pull the required permit through the City of Des Moines Building Division before any work begins. Material selection is discussed with the homeowner in the context of the specific site: a steep-slope west-facing roof near the water warrants a different product than a low-slope rear addition on a sheltered lot. We are a licensed General Contractor in Washington State (Certificate of Registration #NATIORC792M6), and our team carries the credentials, including IICRC Certification and Lead-Safe Certification, that matter when older homes are involved.

During installation, we use ice-and-water shield at all eaves and valleys as a baseline, not an upgrade, because the freeze-thaw conditions here make it a practical necessity rather than an optional add-on. Roof decking that shows rot, delamination, or soft spots is replaced before new material goes down. Covering compromised decking is a shortcut that shows up as a callback within a few years.

## Des Moines Insurance Coordination

Storm damage claims are common after the strong southwesterly systems that push through King County in late fall and winter. If your roof sustained damage from wind, hail, or falling debris, the documentation process matters as much as the repair itself. We photograph affected areas in detail, note the extent of shingle loss or damage, and provide written scope estimates formatted to work with the standard carrier review process.

We bill most major insurance carriers directly and can communicate with your adjuster throughout the claim. Homeowners should be aware that Washington State insurance policies vary in how they handle actual cash value versus replacement cost coverage, and that distinction affects your out-of-pocket exposure. We walk through that with you before work begins so there are no surprises at invoice time.
