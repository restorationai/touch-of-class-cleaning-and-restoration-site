## Why Seattle's Building Stock Complicates Decomposition Cleanup

Most of Seattle's residential neighborhoods — Wallingford, Greenwood, Magnolia, and much of West Seattle — were built before 1950. That era of construction means tongue-and-groove subfloor planks, horsehair plaster, and in many cases crawlspaces with minimal vapor barriers. When a decomposition event occurs in one of these homes, biological material doesn't stay contained the way it might in a newer build with concrete slab or modern OSB subfloor.

The region's climate compounds this. Seattle averages over 150 days of measurable precipitation annually, and ambient humidity rarely drops low enough for natural off-gassing to dissipate on its own. In a closed home — common in unattended death situations where utilities may still be running — that humidity accelerates odor saturation into porous materials. Remediation in these properties requires a more aggressive dehumidification and negative-air protocol than a comparable job in a drier climate would.

Condos in Capitol Hill and South Lake Union present a different set of variables: shared HVAC risers, adjacent units, and HOA oversight mean that odor containment and discreet access are as important as the technical work itself.

## Our Unattended Death Cleanup Process in Seattle

Every job begins with a thorough assessment before any materials are removed. Our IICRC-certified technicians identify the full extent of affected surfaces — visible and hidden — using moisture meters and thermal imaging where appropriate. This matters especially in pre-1950 Seattle homes, where contamination can wick into subfloor joists or behind plaster without any visible surface indication.

Once the scope is confirmed, we establish containment and begin the removal of affected materials following EPA and OSHA biohazard protocols. All waste is transported and disposed of through licensed channels in compliance with Washington State Department of Ecology requirements. After physical remediation, we apply EPA-registered disinfectants and begin the odor neutralization process — not masking agents, but treatments that address the source compounds at a molecular level.

Throughout the process, we work quietly and without unnecessary foot traffic. We use unmarked vehicles on request, and our crews are trained to communicate only with the authorized contact — whether that's a family member, property manager, or estate attorney.
