## Why Seattle Roofs Wear Differently

Seattle averages roughly 150 days of measurable precipitation per year, and the real threat is not any single storm but the sustained dampness between October and April. Moss and algae colonize asphalt shingles faster here than in drier climates, and once a mat of moss lifts shingle edges, water wicks underneath rather than shedding off. On older Craftsman and Tudor homes common to Greenwood and Wallingford, the original roof framing often used dimensional lumber that has dried and shifted over time, meaning a replacement project may reveal rafter notches, low-slope sections, or valley angles that a standard installation crew is not prepared to address. Proper replacement on these homes requires a crew that reads the structure before ordering materials, not after.

Flat and low-slope roof sections, common on mid-century additions throughout West Seattle, present a separate challenge. Standing water on an improperly drained flat section accelerates membrane failure and can push water into wall cavities before any interior sign appears. Identifying slope, drain placement, and membrane condition is part of every assessment we conduct before a single shingle is pulled.

## Our Roofing Installation and Replacement Process in Seattle

Every project begins with a documented roof inspection that covers decking condition, flashing at chimneys and dormers, soffit and fascia integrity, and attic ventilation. Seattle's climate makes ventilation a non-negotiable factor: inadequate airflow causes condensation to build against the underside of the decking year-round, accelerating rot from the inside while the exterior still looks intact.

Once the scope is confirmed, we handle permitting through the Seattle Department of Construction and Inspections. Most full replacements in the city require a permit, and the inspection process varies by project scope and existing structure. We manage that coordination so the homeowner is not navigating permit portals mid-project.

Material selection accounts for local conditions. Architectural shingles rated for high-wind exposure and algae resistance are standard for Seattle's climate. For low-slope sections, TPO and modified bitumen systems are matched to the specific drainage geometry of each roof. Flashing details at skylights, chimneys, and parapet walls receive particular attention because those transitions are where Seattle's lateral rain, driven by southwest winds, finds its way in.

## Local Note

On pre-1950 homes throughout Queen Anne and Magnolia, the original skip-sheathing beneath the existing roofing is often still structurally sound but gapped in ways that modern synthetic underlayment handles differently than the original felt paper did. When we pull old roofing on these homes, we assess whether to install a solid sheathing layer over the existing skip boards before laying new underlayment. That extra step adds material cost and time, but it produces a flat nailing surface, better wind resistance, and a roof that performs the way the new materials are designed to perform. Skipping it is a shortcut that shows up in callbacks.
