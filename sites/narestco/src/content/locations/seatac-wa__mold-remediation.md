SeaTac sits in one of the wettest corridors in King County, where marine air funnels off Puget Sound and settles against rooftops, crawlspaces, and wall cavities for months at a stretch. That sustained humidity, combined with the dense housing stock built to accommodate airport workers and transit commuters, creates conditions where mold can move from a slow drip to a full colony in less than 72 hours. National Restoration Construction has been handling mold remediation in SeaTac and the surrounding South King County area since 2004, and the work here has its own character.

## Why SeaTac Properties See Mold Issues

The geography around SeaTac International Airport shapes more than flight paths. The city's relatively flat terrain and clay-heavy soils slow surface drainage, meaning water from fall and winter storms lingers against foundations and under slabs longer than it would in areas with sandier ground. Crawlspace moisture is a persistent issue in the mid-century ranch homes that make up a large portion of SeaTac's residential stock. Many of these homes were built with minimal vapor barriers, and the ones that do have plastic sheeting often have tears or gaps that let ground moisture climb into floor joists and subfloor sheathing.

Condensation is the other common culprit. Kitchens and bathrooms in older SeaTac homes frequently have undersized exhaust fans or ducts that terminate in the attic rather than through the roof, pushing warm moist air directly into insulation where mold spores are already present. A slow roof leak around a vent boot or flashing can go unnoticed for an entire rainy season before the smell of mildew reaches the living space below.

## Our Mold Remediation Process in SeaTac

Every job starts with containment, not cleaning. Before any material is disturbed, affected areas are isolated with poly sheeting and negative air pressure is established using HEPA-filtered air scrubbers. This keeps spores from migrating to unaffected rooms while work is underway.

From there, the process follows a consistent sequence:

- **Moisture mapping**: Thermal imaging and calibrated moisture meters locate hidden saturation behind drywall, under flooring, and inside wall cavities. In SeaTac's older plaster-and-lath construction, surface readings can look dry while the lath behind holds significant moisture.
- **Material removal**: Porous materials that cannot be cleaned to clearance standards, including drywall, insulation, and compromised subfloor sections, are bagged and removed under containment.
- **Antimicrobial treatment**: Structural framing and hard surfaces are treated with EPA-registered antimicrobial agents. No surface is treated before it is dry, because applying product to wet wood traps moisture and can accelerate regrowth.
- **Drying verification**: Clearance readings are taken across multiple points before containment comes down. The IICRC S520 standard guides our drying targets.
- **Post-remediation testing**: When requested or required, we coordinate with an independent industrial hygienist for air sampling before reconstruction begins.

## SeaTac Insurance Coordination

Mold claims in Washington State depend heavily on the documented cause of loss. Insurers distinguish between mold that resulted from a sudden covered event, such as a burst pipe or appliance failure, and mold that developed from long-term elevated humidity or deferred maintenance. The difference affects what gets covered and what does not.

National Restoration Construction documents the moisture source, photographs affected materials before removal, and provides scope-of-loss reports that adjusters can work from directly. We bill most major carriers and can communicate with your adjuster throughout the process so you are not managing that conversation alone while also managing a disrupted household.

## Local Note

Homes in SeaTac built during the postwar boom of the late 1940s and 1950s frequently used board sheathing rather than plywood on exterior walls. That solid wood sheathing absorbs and holds moisture differently than modern OSB or plywood, and it can sustain mold growth at moisture content levels that would not support colonization in newer materials. When we are working in that era of construction, moisture readings that look borderline on a standard scale often warrant extended drying time and a second round of verification before we close containment. Knowing the building stock changes how aggressively we approach the drying phase.
