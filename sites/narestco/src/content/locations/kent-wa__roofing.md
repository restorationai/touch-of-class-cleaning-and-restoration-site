## Why Kent Roofs Wear Differently Than the Regional Average

The Kent Valley's industrial corridor and the residential hillsides above it experience meaningfully different roofing conditions. Homes in the Lake Meridian and Panther Lake neighborhoods sit at elevations where standing water from clogged gutters freezes overnight in January and February, forcing ice under flashing at valleys and around chimney bases. That same freeze-thaw stress is largely absent on the valley floor, where commercial warehouse roofing faces a different challenge: ponding on low-slope membrane systems caused by the area's notoriously flat grade and heavy annual rainfall.

Older residential construction in Kent adds another layer of complexity. Many homes built in the 1960s and 1970s on East Hill used 1x6 skip sheathing under wood shakes. When those shakes finally fail and a full replacement is due, the skip sheathing often needs to be overlaid with solid OSB or plywood decking before a modern asphalt or composite system can be installed correctly. Skipping that step produces a wavy, structurally compromised deck that voids manufacturer warranties on the new shingles.

## Our Roofing Installation and Replacement Process in Kent

Every project begins with a documented roof inspection: we photograph the deck, flashing, ridge, valleys, and penetrations before a single shingle comes off. That documentation matters for insurance claims and for permit applications filed with the City of Kent's Building Services division.

Once permits are pulled, the process follows a consistent sequence:

- **Tear-off and debris removal**: Old material is stripped to the deck. We check for soft spots, rot, and prior water intrusion at the sheathing level before proceeding.
- **Deck repair or overlay**: Damaged sections are replaced. On East Hill homes with skip sheathing, solid decking is installed across the full field.
- **Underlayment and ice-and-water shield**: Ice-and-water shield is applied at eaves, valleys, and around all penetrations. In Kent's climate, this is not optional on any pitch below 6:12.
- **Shingle or membrane installation**: Residential projects typically use architectural asphalt shingles rated for high-wind zones; low-slope commercial work uses TPO or modified bitumen membrane systems.
- **Flashing, ridge cap, and final inspection**: All flashing is replaced, not reused. A final walkthrough confirms alignment with permit requirements before the inspection is scheduled.

National Restoration Construction holds a Washington State General Contractor Certificate of Registration (#NATIORC792M6) and is a Lead-Safe Certified Firm, which matters on pre-1978 homes where disturbing old roofing materials can expose lead-based paint in underlying trim and fascia.
