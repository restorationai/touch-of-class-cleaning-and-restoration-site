Whether it's a supply line that failed overnight, a crawl space that collected runoff during a November storm, or a slow roof leak that finally soaked through the ceiling framing, water damage here tends to move fast and hide deeper than it looks. ## Why Des Moines Properties See Water Damage Issues

The geography of Des Moines works against homeowners in a few specific ways. The hillside terrain between Marine View Drive and the waterfront means that surface water during heavy rain doesn't sheet away evenly. It follows grade, pools against foundations, and finds its way through crawl space vents and aging concrete block walls. Homes built in the 1960s and 1970s, which make up a significant portion of the housing stock in the inland neighborhoods east of Pacific Highway, were often constructed with minimal vapor barriers and crawl spaces that were never designed to manage the moisture load that comes with sustained Pacific Northwest winters.

The proximity to Puget Sound also keeps relative humidity elevated even on dry days, which slows evaporation and extends the window during which wet framing, insulation, and subfloor materials remain at risk for secondary damage. A soaked crawl space that might dry out in four days in a drier climate can stay problematic for twice that long here without active dehumidification.

## Our Water Damage Restoration Process in Des Moines

When we arrive, the first step is containment and assessment, not just a visual inspection. We use moisture meters and thermal imaging to map where water has actually traveled, because water behind plaster or under vinyl flooring rarely shows itself at the surface. In older Des Moines homes with original hardwood subfloors or lath-and-plaster wall assemblies, moisture can read dry at the surface while the structural layer underneath stays saturated.

Once we understand the full extent of the loss, we begin water extraction using truck-mounted and portable extraction units, followed by the placement of air movers and commercial dehumidifiers calibrated to the actual square footage and material types in the affected space. Drying progress is logged daily against the IICRC S500 standard so there is a documented record for your insurance carrier. Affected materials that cannot be dried in place, such as wet insulation or buckled flooring, are removed and disposed of properly before the structural drying phase begins.
