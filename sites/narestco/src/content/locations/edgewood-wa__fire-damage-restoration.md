Smoke travels through HVAC systems, soot settles into insulation and wall cavities, and the water left behind by suppression efforts begins its own clock the moment the hose shuts off. ## Why Edgewood Properties Face Particular Fire Damage Challenges

Edgewood's housing stock is a mix of mid-century construction and homes built during the suburban expansion of the 1980s and 1990s. Homes from that earlier era often have original cedar shake or composition roofing, wood-framed soffits, and attic insulation that was never designed to resist prolonged smoke infiltration. When fire breaches the attic plane, blown-in insulation absorbs odor compounds deeply enough that surface deodorization alone will not resolve the problem. The insulation typically needs full removal before structural drying and odor neutralization can begin.

Pierce County's wet climate adds another layer of complexity. The Pacific Northwest's persistent moisture means that structural lumber in older Edgewood homes often carries a higher baseline moisture content than lumber in drier climates. After suppression water is introduced, that lumber can reach saturation levels that dramatically extend drying timelines and raise the risk of secondary mold colonization if the structure is not dried aggressively within the first 48 to 72 hours.

## Our Fire Damage Restoration Process in Edgewood

Every fire loss is assessed differently, but the sequence follows a disciplined path. The first step on-site is a full scope of damage: structural integrity, smoke migration mapping, moisture readings throughout affected assemblies, and documentation for your insurance carrier. That documentation matters because Washington State carriers vary in how they handle smoke-only claims versus structural losses, and a thorough initial report prevents disputes later in the process.

Demolition of unsalvageable materials comes next, followed by HEPA vacuuming of all surfaces, thermal fogging or hydroxyl treatment for odor, and encapsulation of any residual soot on framing that will be enclosed. Structural drying runs concurrently where suppression water is present. Reconstruction, handled under National Restoration Construction's General Contractor Certificate of Registration (#NATIORC792M6), brings the property back to pre-loss condition without the homeowner managing a separate contractor relationship.
