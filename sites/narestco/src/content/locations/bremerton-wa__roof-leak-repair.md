## Why Bremerton Roofs Leak the Way They Do

Bremerton's housing stock tells the story. The neighborhoods closest to the Puget Sound Naval Shipyard — Charleston, Sheridan Park, and the streets radiating out from the Bremerton ferry terminal — were built fast during the 1940s to house shipyard workers and their families. Builders used what was available: simple gable roofs, minimal overhangs, and galvanized metal flashing that has long since corroded. The hilly terrain around the Port Washington Narrows accelerates the problem: water sheds quickly off slopes, drives under lifted shingles at the ridge, and pools at low-slope valleys where debris collects.

Colder winters here also mean freeze-thaw cycles that crack caulk seals around skylights, chimneys, and pipe boots faster than in milder climates. A small breach that leaks a tablespoon of water per storm in November can open to a steady drip by February. By the time a homeowner notices a brown ring on the drywall below, the attic insulation above it may already be saturated — compressed fiberglass batts hold water like a sponge and don't dry on their own.

## Our Roof Leak Cleanup and Repair Process in Bremerton

When we arrive on-site — typically within 90 minutes of your call for most Bremerton addresses — the first priority is stopping any active intrusion and documenting the damage for your insurance carrier. We use thermal imaging cameras to map moisture migration through the ceiling and attic deck, which often reveals water traveling several feet horizontally from the visible stain before it drips.

From there, the process moves in a clear sequence:

1. **Extract standing water** from attic cavities and ceiling voids using truck-mounted or portable extraction equipment.
2. **Remove saturated insulation** — wet batts or blown-in cellulose must come out before structural drying can work; we bag and haul it.
3. **Set drying equipment** — commercial-grade desiccant or refrigerant dehumidifiers and axial air movers positioned to dry rafters, sheathing, and ceiling framing to IICRC S500 moisture content targets.
4. **Perform temporary weatherproofing** if the roof surface is still open — tarping or emergency membrane application to prevent additional intrusion while drying is underway.
5. **Complete the roof repair** — shingle replacement, flashing re-sealing, valley repair, or whatever the source requires — once the structure reads dry on calibrated moisture meters.
6. **Restore interior finishes** — insulation reinstallation, drywall patching, texture matching, and paint to return the space to pre-loss condition.

Because we hold a Washington State General Contractor license (#NATIORC792M6) and carry Lead-Safe Certification, we can handle the full scope in-house — no handoff to a second contractor mid-job.
