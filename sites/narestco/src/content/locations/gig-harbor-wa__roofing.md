The salt air rolling off Puget Sound does not spare roofing materials, and in Gig Harbor the exposure is constant. Homes along the harbor rim and up into the Harborview and Millville neighborhoods sit in a maritime climate where moss colonizes asphalt shingles within a few years, metal flashings oxidize faster than inland, and the persistent drizzle of a Western Washington winter finds every aging seam. When a roof reaches the end of its service life here, the consequences move quickly from a stained ceiling to rot in the sheathing beneath. National Restoration Construction handles roofing installation and replacement for Gig Harbor properties with the specific conditions of this peninsula in mind.

## Why Gig Harbor Roofs Wear Differently Than Inland Homes

Gig Harbor sits across the Tacoma Narrows Bridge from the mainland, and that geography shapes everything about how roofs age here. The marine layer brings sustained moisture rather than heavy isolated storms, which means roofing systems face low-grade but relentless saturation rather than the dramatic freeze-thaw cycles common east of the Cascades. Algae and moss growth accelerate on north-facing slopes, and the older cottages and fishing-era houses in Millville and Harborview often have low-pitched roofs with minimal overhang, a design choice that made sense for the original builders but creates standing-moisture problems on aging shingles.

The tree canopy throughout Artondale and Rosedale adds another layer of stress. Fir and cedar debris accumulates in valleys and around chimney bases, holding moisture against the roof surface for weeks at a time. Sheathing rot often starts there, invisible from the ground until a full tear-off reveals soft spots across multiple sheets. Any honest assessment of a Gig Harbor roof has to account for what is happening under the surface, not just what is visible from the curb.

## Our Roofing Installation and Replacement Process in Gig Harbor

Every project starts with a full inspection that goes beyond the shingles. The crew examines decking condition, flashing at all penetrations, ridge and soffit ventilation, and the condition of any existing underlayment. In a climate with this much ambient moisture, ventilation is not a code checkbox; it is the difference between a roof that lasts two decades and one that develops condensation-driven rot from the inside out.

Once the scope is confirmed, the existing system is removed down to the decking. Damaged sheathing is replaced before any new material goes on. Underlayment selection matters here: a synthetic high-perm underlayment handles the wet installation windows common in a Kitsap Peninsula winter better than traditional felt, which can absorb moisture during a multi-day job and wrinkle under the finished layer. Flashings are replaced rather than reused, because reusing aged flashing around chimneys and skylights is one of the most common sources of early failure on otherwise sound new roofs.

Final installation follows manufacturer specifications for the selected product, which matters for warranty validity. National Restoration Construction holds a General Contractor Certificate of Registration with Washington State Department of Labor and Industries (#NATIORC792M6), and the team is IICRC Certified and a BBB Accredited, Lead-Safe Certified Firm, so the paperwork that insurers and HOAs request is already in order.
