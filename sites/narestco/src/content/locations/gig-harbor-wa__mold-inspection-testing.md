## Why Gig Harbor Homes Are Especially Prone to Hidden Mold

The peninsula's maritime climate is the starting point. Average relative humidity in the Gig Harbor area routinely sits above 80 percent from October through April, and the dense tree canopy on hillside lots — common in Artondale and along the Crescent Valley corridor — slows drying further by blocking afternoon sun. Foundations on steep shoreline lots drain toward the house rather than away from it, and that groundwater pressure pushes moisture vapor directly into unencapsulated crawl spaces.

The older fishing-era cottages and bungalows in the Harborview and Millville neighborhoods compound the problem. Many of these homes were built in the 1940s and 1950s on shallow perimeter foundations with vented crawl spaces that made sense for the era but now trap ground moisture rather than ventilate it. When we pull access panels in those crawl spaces, we frequently find fiberglass batt insulation that has absorbed enough moisture to sag off the joists — a near-perfect substrate for Penicillium and Cladosporium growth. The wood framing itself is often old-growth fir, which resists rot longer than modern lumber but still colonizes with mold once moisture content climbs above 19 percent for more than 48 hours.

## Our Mold Inspection and Testing Process in Gig Harbor

Every assessment starts with a visual walk-through calibrated to the specific property type. For a waterfront home along the Gig Harbor rim, that means paying close attention to the leeward walls and window sills that face the harbor — salt-air condensation cycles accelerate paint failure and allow moisture intrusion at the glazing. For an older Millville cottage, we focus first on the crawl space and the band joist area where exterior sheathing meets the foundation.

After the visual survey, we use a combination of tools to map moisture without tearing into walls unnecessarily:

- **Thermal imaging** identifies cold spots where evaporative moisture is active inside wall cavities
- **Calibrated pin and pinless moisture meters** give us wood moisture content readings at suspect framing members
- **Air sampling** draws a measured volume of indoor air through a spore trap cassette; samples are sent to an AIHA-accredited laboratory for analysis against outdoor baseline counts
- **Surface swab or tape-lift sampling** targets visible growth to identify genus and species, which matters for remediation planning and for insurance documentation

Results from the lab typically return within 24–48 hours. We walk you through the report in plain language — what species were found, at what concentrations, and whether those counts indicate an active indoor source or normal background levels for the Pacific Northwest. National Restoration Construction has been doing this work since 2004 and holds IICRC certification, so the written report carries weight with insurance adjusters, real estate agents, and lenders who require third-party documentation.
