## What Crime Scene Cleanup Actually Involves in Everett

Forensic cleanup — sometimes called biohazard remediation — begins only after law enforcement has released the scene. At that point, the property is a biohazard, not a crime scene in the legal sense, and the responsibility for cleanup falls to the property owner or family. That distinction matters because homeowners insurance often covers the remediation cost, but the claim process has to start immediately.

In Everett, the work itself is shaped by the housing stock. ## Our Process: Discretion, Documentation, and Clearance

Every job follows the same clinical sequence regardless of the address:

1. **Scene assessment** — technicians in full PPE evaluate the affected area, identify secondary contamination pathways (HVAC returns, subfloor gaps, adjacent rooms), and document findings for the insurance carrier.
2. **Containment** — the affected space is isolated to prevent cross-contamination and to give the family or property manager privacy from neighbors. In denser Bayside blocks where homes sit close together, we stage equipment discreetly and work without unnecessary foot traffic.
3. **Removal and packaging** — all biohazardous materials are removed, packaged, and transported in compliance with Washington State DOT and EPA regulations for biohazardous waste. Nothing goes into standard waste streams.
4. **Surface treatment** — EPA-registered hospital-grade disinfectants are applied to all affected surfaces, followed by ATP testing to verify pathogen reduction.
5. **Reconstruction** — if flooring, drywall, or cabinetry was removed, our crew handles the rebuild. One company, one invoice, one point of contact for the insurance adjuster.
