## What Auburn Homeowners Are Usually Dealing With

Auburn sits at the base of the Green River Valley, and that geography shapes what we see on job sites here. The city's housing stock spans everything from mid-century ramblers near downtown to newer construction in the Lakeland Hills area, and each era of building brings its own set of vulnerabilities.

Older homes — particularly those built before 1978 — often contain lead-based paint and aging infrastructure that complicates any renovation. Our Lead-Safe Certified Firm status means we handle that material safely and in compliance with EPA requirements, which matters both for your family's health and for passing final inspections.

Wet winters are the other constant. Auburn averages over 40 inches of rain annually, and crawl spaces, basements, and poorly flashed roof lines absorb a lot of that moisture over time. We regularly start what a homeowner thinks is a straightforward kitchen remodel and find subfloor rot or compromised framing that has to be addressed before any finish work begins. That's not a surprise we spring on you mid-project — it's something we assess and price honestly upfront.

## Our Renovation and Remodeling Process in Auburn

Every project starts with a thorough walkthrough. For post-damage rebuilds, that means documenting existing conditions with photos and moisture readings before a single board is pulled. For planned remodels — a kitchen remodel, bathroom remodel, or whole-room addition — it means understanding how you use the space, what your permit requirements are through the City of Auburn's Community Development department, and what the realistic timeline looks like given current material lead times.

From there, the process generally moves through these phases:

**Scope and permitting.** We pull the permits. You shouldn't have to navigate the City of Auburn's online portal or stand in line at the permit counter — that's our job as your licensed general contractor (WA State L&I #NATIORC792M6).

**Demolition and structural work.** This is where hidden problems surface. We document everything, communicate changes to scope in writing, and don't proceed with work that affects your budget without your sign-off.

**Mechanical rough-ins.** Electrical, plumbing, and HVAC work is coordinated with licensed subcontractors we've worked with for years. Scheduling these trades correctly is what separates a project that finishes on time from one that drags for months.

**Finish work and final inspection.** Drywall, flooring, cabinetry, tile, fixtures — and then a final walkthrough with you before we consider the job closed.

## Post-Damage Rebuilds and Insurance Claims

When a renovation is driven by damage — a flooded bathroom, a kitchen fire, a roof collapse — the insurance process adds a layer of complexity that most homeowners haven't navigated before. We work directly with adjusters and can provide the line-item documentation that carriers require. What we handle: scope of work documentation, material specifications, supplement requests when the initial estimate misses items, and coordination with any mitigation work that was done before reconstruction began.

What you handle: filing the initial claim, paying your deductible, and communicating with your adjuster on coverage decisions. We can walk you through what to expect, but we're your contractor, not your public adjuster.

If you received a low initial estimate from your carrier and aren't sure it covers the actual scope of repairs, reach out before you sign anything. It's much easier to negotiate a supplement before work starts than after.
