## Why Tacoma Properties Are Vulnerable to Burst Pipes

Puget Sound winters are mild compared to eastern Washington, but that mild reputation is exactly the problem. Tacoma homes — especially the Craftsman and Victorian-era stock in neighborhoods like Stadium District, North End, and South Tacoma — were often built with supply lines running through uninsulated crawl spaces or exterior walls. When temperatures dip into the mid-20s, which happens several times each winter, those lines have no buffer. A hard freeze that lasts only 12 hours is long enough to split copper or galvanized steel.

Tacoma's wet season compounds the risk. Pipes that survive a freeze can still fail weeks later when the ground shifts with saturation, stressing older joints and fittings. Properties near the Tideflats or along Commencement Bay waterways also deal with elevated humidity year-round, which accelerates corrosion on aging plumbing. If your home was built before 1970, galvanized pipe is still a realistic possibility — and galvanized corrodes from the inside out, so failure often comes without warning.

## What Our Burst Pipe Response Looks Like, Step by Step

When we arrive, the first priority is stopping active damage — not paperwork. A technician locates the source of the pipe break, assesses how far water has traveled through flooring, walls, and subfloor, and identifies any secondary risks like compromised electrical panels or saturated insulation.

From there, the process moves in a logical sequence:

1. **Extraction** — Truck-mounted extractors pull standing water from hard surfaces and carpet. For water that has migrated under hardwood or into wall cavities, we use targeted injection extraction tools rather than tearing out material prematurely.
2. **Moisture mapping** — Thermal imaging cameras and calibrated moisture meters give us a complete picture of where water traveled, including areas that look dry but aren't. This step protects you from hidden mold growth that can begin colonizing porous materials within 24–48 hours of a water line break.
3. **Structural drying** — Industrial air movers and desiccant or refrigerant dehumidifiers run in a calculated pattern based on the room's cubic footage and the materials involved. We monitor readings daily and adjust equipment until readings return to baseline.
4. **Repair and reconstruction** — Once the structure is dry and documented, our general contractor license (WA State L&I #NATIORC792M6) allows us to handle the repair side — replacing drywall, flooring, insulation, and the failed pipe itself — under one contract rather than coordinating separate trades.

This single-company model matters when you're dealing with insurance. Adjusters prefer a unified scope of loss with documented moisture readings, equipment logs, and repair costs rather than invoices from three different contractors.

## Navigating the Insurance Claim

Sudden and accidental pipe burst damage is typically covered under standard homeowners policies. What gets complicated is the language around "maintenance" versus "sudden loss" — insurers sometimes push back on claims if they can argue the failure resulted from deferred maintenance on corroded or aging pipes.

We've worked with every major carrier that operates in the Tacoma market, including State Farm, Farmers, PEMCO, and USAA. Our documentation process — moisture logs, photo evidence from the first hour on-site, and a written scope of work — is built to support your claim, not just satisfy our own records. We communicate directly with your adjuster and can provide supplemental documentation if the initial estimate is disputed.

What you'll need to handle: filing the first notice of loss with your carrier and paying your deductible. Everything else — estimates, inspections, negotiations over scope — we can walk you through.
