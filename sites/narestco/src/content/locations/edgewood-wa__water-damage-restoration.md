When a supply line fails or a storm overwhelms a crawl space drain, that standing water does not just sit on the surface, it wicks into subfloor sheathing, insulation batts, and wall cavities before most homeowners realize the extent of what they are dealing with. ## Why Edgewood Properties See Water Damage Issues

The residential areas of Edgewood developed largely through the 1970s and 1980s, which means a significant share of the housing stock was built before modern moisture barriers and vapor retarders became standard practice in Western Washington construction. Crawl space encapsulation was rarely specified in that era, and many homes still rely on passive venting that underperforms during the prolonged damp stretches Pierce County sees from October through April. The result is a predictable pattern: groundwater pressure builds against older concrete block or poured-wall foundations, vapor migrates upward into floor systems, and by the time a homeowner notices a musty odor or a soft spot underfoot, the subfloor has been absorbing moisture for weeks.

Pierce County's clay-heavy glacial till also means that even a modest roof drainage failure, a clogged downspout, a separated gutter seam, can channel water directly against a foundation wall rather than dispersing it across the yard. Homes on sloped lots, which are common throughout the Edgewood area, face additional hydrostatic pressure on the uphill side of the foundation during heavy rain events.

## Our Water Damage Restoration Process in Edgewood

The first priority on any water loss is stopping the spread. Once the source is controlled, the crew maps moisture using thermal imaging and calibrated moisture meters to identify exactly where water has traveled, not just where it is visible. In Edgewood homes with older plank subfloors or tongue-and-groove hardwood, water can travel laterally along the grain for several feet beyond the visible wet zone, so surface readings alone are not reliable.

Extraction comes next, using truck-mounted and portable units sized to the volume of standing water and the type of flooring material. After bulk water is removed, the drying phase begins with industrial air movers and refrigerant or desiccant dehumidifiers placed according to the IICRC S500 standard for structural drying. Drying logs are recorded daily so documentation is available for the insurance claim and so the crew can confirm when materials have reached acceptable moisture content before any reconstruction begins. National Restoration Construction holds a General Contractor Certificate of Registration with Washington State's Department of Labor and Industries, which means the same team that dries the structure can carry the repair phase through to completion without a handoff to a separate contractor.
