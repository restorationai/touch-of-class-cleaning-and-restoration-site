Gig Harbor's salt-air maritime climate does something predictable to older homes: it works its way into everything. The fishing-era cottages and bungalows clustered around Harborview and Millville were built for a working waterfront, not for the renovation expectations of today's buyers and owners. When you're planning a kitchen remodel, a bathroom overhaul, or a full post-damage rebuild on one of these properties, you need a general contractor who already understands what's behind those walls — and what the marine moisture has been doing to them for decades.

## Why Gig Harbor's Housing Stock Shapes Every Renovation

The homes along the harbor rim and up into Artondale weren't built to modern moisture standards. Many sit on vented crawl spaces that were designed for an era before vapor barriers were standard practice, and the persistent marine layer that rolls in off Puget Sound keeps ground moisture elevated year-round. That means a kitchen remodel that looks straightforward on paper can reveal subfloor rot, compromised rim joists, or fungal growth the moment you pull up the first layer of flooring.

Older homes in the 98335 ZIP code — particularly the pre-1960 stock in Millville — frequently have knob-and-tube or early aluminum wiring that must be addressed before any permitted renovation can proceed. Pierce County and the City of Gig Harbor both require electrical upgrades to current code when walls are opened during a remodel, which adds scope to projects that owners sometimes don't budget for in advance. Knowing that going in, rather than discovering it mid-project, is the difference between a renovation that finishes on schedule and one that stalls for weeks waiting on an electrical permit amendment.
