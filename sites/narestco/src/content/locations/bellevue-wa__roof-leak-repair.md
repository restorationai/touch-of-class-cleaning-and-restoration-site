## Why Bellevue Roofs Take a Beating

Bellevue sits in a climate that punishes roofing systems in ways that don't always show up until a storm is already underway. The Cascade foothills to the east funnel moisture-laden air directly over the Eastside, and Bellevue averages around 37–40 inches of rain annually — much of it arriving in sustained, low-intensity events that give water time to find every compromised flashing, cracked shingle, or clogged valley. Neighborhoods like Somerset and Factoria sit at higher elevations where wind-driven rain hits rooflines at angles that standard shingle overlap wasn't designed to handle.

Older housing stock in areas like Bellevue's west side — homes built in the 1960s and 70s — frequently has original or once-replaced roofing that's past its service life. Skylights and dormers, common in the craftsman and split-level homes throughout Crossroads and Lake Hills, are notorious leak entry points once their seals age out. Cedar shake roofs, still present on many mid-century Bellevue properties, can look intact from the ground while allowing water to wick through moss-covered surfaces into the attic below — sometimes for months before a visible ceiling stain appears.

## What Our Roof Leak Cleanup and Repair Process Looks Like

When a technician arrives, the first priority is stopping active water intrusion. Depending on conditions, that may mean applying a temporary weatherproof tarp over the damaged section of the roof while the interior assessment begins — you don't want a crew pulling wet insulation out of your attic while rain is still pouring in.

Inside, we use moisture meters and thermal imaging to map exactly how far water has traveled. Ceiling water damage is rarely limited to the stain you can see. Water follows framing, insulation batts, and HVAC ducts before it finds a low point and drips. Attic water damage is particularly deceptive — saturated insulation loses most of its R-value and becomes a growth medium for mold within 24 to 48 hours of a significant leak event.

Once the scope is mapped, the process moves through extraction of standing water (where present), controlled demolition of unsalvageable drywall or sheathing, and structured drying using commercial-grade desiccant dehumidifiers and air movers. We document moisture readings at every stage — that documentation matters for your insurance claim. Permanent roof repairs follow after the structure has dried to acceptable levels, typically within three to five days depending on the extent of saturation.

As an IICRC Certified firm and licensed Washington State General Contractor (#NATIORC792M6), we handle both the restoration side and the structural repair under one roof — no handoff to a second contractor mid-project.

## Navigating Your Insurance Claim

Most homeowner policies cover sudden and accidental water intrusion — a storm-damaged shingle, a wind-lifted flashing, a tree limb that punctures a roof deck. What they typically don't cover is damage attributed to long-term neglect or deferred maintenance. That line matters, and how the damage is documented in the first 24 hours often determines which side of it your claim lands on.

We work directly with all major carriers and can communicate with your adjuster on your behalf. Our technicians photograph and moisture-map the damage before anything is moved or removed, creating the kind of timestamped, measurement-backed record that adjusters need to process a claim efficiently. You handle the call to open the claim; we handle the documentation, scope of loss, and coordination from there.
