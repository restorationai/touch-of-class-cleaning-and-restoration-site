## Why Federal Way's Housing Stock Matters for Trauma Scene Cleanup

Not every structure responds the same way after a traumatic incident. Older construction in Federal Way's established neighborhoods often features hardwood subfloors over concrete slabs, tongue-and-groove paneling, and porous grout lines in tile work — materials that absorb biological matter at different rates than modern vinyl plank or engineered flooring. The Puget Sound climate compounds this: Federal Way averages more than 37 inches of rainfall annually, and ambient humidity stays elevated for much of the year. That moisture baseline means porous surfaces can begin harboring secondary contamination concerns faster than in drier climates, which is why our technicians assess structural materials — not just visible surfaces — during every initial walkthrough.

Homes built during the vapor-barrier era of the late 1970s and early 1980s, common in neighborhoods like Twin Lakes and Lakeland, sometimes have crawl spaces with compromised or missing vapor barriers. If a trauma event occurs near a subfloor penetration, affected material can migrate downward. ## Our Trauma Scene Cleanup Process in Federal Way

Every job begins with a discreet arrival. Our vehicles are unmarked, and we coordinate arrival times with whoever is managing access to the property — a family member, an estate attorney, a property manager, or law enforcement if the scene has been released. We do not begin work until we have written confirmation that the scene has been cleared by the appropriate authorities.

Once on-site, our IICRC-certified technicians establish containment zones, document affected areas for insurance purposes, and begin systematic decontamination using EPA-registered disinfectants rated for bloodborne pathogen remediation. All biohazardous waste is packaged, manifested, and transported to a licensed disposal facility in compliance with Washington State Department of Ecology regulations — the paperwork trail matters for both insurance claims and future property transactions. Structural materials that cannot be safely decontaminated are removed, and the space is treated and tested before any reconstruction begins.
