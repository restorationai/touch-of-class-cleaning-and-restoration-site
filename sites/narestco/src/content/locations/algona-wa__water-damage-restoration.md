## Why Algona Properties Are Vulnerable to Water Damage

The Green River valley floor was shaped by glacial outwash and centuries of river migration, leaving behind soils that compact tightly and resist percolation. During heavy rain events, that means surface water moves laterally rather than downward, finding its way through foundation cracks, window wells, and crawl space vents instead of draining away from the structure. Homes built on slab foundations in this area can experience hydrostatic pressure that pushes moisture up through concrete, a problem that often goes undetected until flooring adhesive fails or a musty odor develops in a back bedroom.

Algona's housing stock includes a meaningful number of homes built in the 1970s and 1980s, when builders commonly used particleboard subflooring and paper-faced insulation in crawl spaces. Both materials absorb water aggressively and begin to degrade within days of saturation. Unlike modern OSB or closed-cell foam, they don't signal visible damage quickly, which means the window for effective mitigation is shorter than homeowners expect.

## Our Water Damage Restoration Process in Algona

Every job starts with a moisture mapping survey using thermal imaging and calibrated meters, not a visual inspection alone. Water behind a wall reads dry at the surface while the framing and insulation behind it stay saturated, a detail that matters especially in older construction where vapor barriers were installed inconsistently.

Once the affected boundary is established, extraction comes first. Standing water is removed with truck-mounted or portable extraction units, followed by targeted drying using desiccant or refrigerant dehumidifiers and high-velocity air movers positioned to create a drying chamber around the wet assembly. Drying progress is logged daily against the IICRC S500 standard so there is a documented record for your insurance carrier. Affected materials that cannot be dried in place, such as saturated particleboard or compromised insulation, are removed and properly disposed of before reconstruction begins.

As a licensed General Contractor (WA State L&I, #NATIORC792M6), the team can carry the project from emergency mitigation straight through structural repairs and finish work, so you are not coordinating between separate contractors during an already stressful period.
