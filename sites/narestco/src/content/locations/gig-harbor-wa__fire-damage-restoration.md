A house fire on the harbor rim hits differently than a fire anywhere else. Salt-laden marine air moves through Gig Harbor's older Harborview and Millville cottages year-round, and the moment a fire is out, that moisture-laden atmosphere begins bonding smoke residue into porous wood siding, vented crawl spaces, and the soft plaster walls common in fishing-era homes. The window for effective fire and smoke restoration is shorter here than in drier inland climates — and the damage compounds faster if the response does too.

## Why Gig Harbor Homes Are Particularly Vulnerable After a Fire

The housing stock along the harbor waterfront and up into Crescent Valley was built across several decades, with many structures predating modern vapor barriers and fire-stop framing. Vented crawl spaces — standard in this area because of the steep shoreline lots and high ground moisture — act like chimneys during a fire, drawing smoke and combustion byproducts deep under the floor system. After the flames are out, that sub-floor cavity holds odor and soot long after the visible damage upstairs looks cleaned up.

The maritime climate adds another layer. Relative humidity in Gig Harbor regularly runs above 80 percent through the fall and winter months. Smoke residue is hygroscopic — it pulls moisture from the air — which means soot that isn't removed within the first 24 to 48 hours begins to etch into painted surfaces, corrode metal fixtures, and stain natural wood permanently. In a drier climate, you might have a slightly longer window. Here, you don't.

Older homes in Millville and along Harborview Drive also frequently contain lead-based paint in window trim and door casings. Fire and demolition work on those surfaces triggers EPA RRP (Renovation, Repair, and Painting) rules. National Restoration Construction holds Lead-Safe Certified Firm status and handles the required containment and documentation so homeowners aren't left exposed to regulatory liability on top of everything else.

## Our Fire Damage Restoration Process in Gig Harbor

The first step on any fire loss is a room-by-room scope assessment — not a quick walk-through, but a documented evaluation of every surface, cavity, and mechanical system affected by heat, smoke, and firefighting water. In Gig Harbor's older homes, that means pulling up floor registers to check crawl space smoke migration, probing plaster walls for heat damage behind intact surfaces, and testing HVAC ducting for soot distribution before the system is ever turned back on.

From there, the process moves through structural stabilization (boarding, tarping, temporary shoring where needed), controlled demolition of char and compromised materials, dry-ice or soda blasting for smoke residue on framing, HEPA air scrubbing, and odor neutralization using hydroxyl generators rather than masking agents. Hydroxyl treatment is slower than ozone — typically 3 to 5 days for a mid-size home — but it's safe to run while occupants are re-entering and doesn't damage rubber seals or electronics the way ozone can.

Reconstruction follows the same licensed general contractor framework (WA L&I Registration #NATIORC792M6) used on every job: permitted work, inspected finishes, and a documented close-out that satisfies both your insurance carrier and any lender requirements on the property.
