## Why Lakeland South Properties See Roofing Problems Early

Lakeland South sits in the shadow of the Green River Valley's moisture patterns. Rainfall here is persistent rather than dramatic, weeks of low-grade drizzle rather than single heavy storms, and that sustained dampness is harder on roofing systems than people expect. Asphalt shingles lose their protective granule coating gradually under this kind of exposure, and once the mat underneath is exposed, UV damage accelerates even through overcast skies. Algae and moss growth follow quickly, and in Lakeland South's tree-lined residential pockets, roof surfaces stay shaded long enough for biological growth to take hold between dry spells.

The housing stock in this part of South King County also matters. Many homes here were built during the suburban expansion of the 1980s and 1990s, which means original roofing systems are at or past their design life. Three-tab shingles installed in that era were rated for 20 to 25 years under ideal conditions, conditions that the Pacific Northwest does not reliably provide. Homes with lower-pitch rooflines, common in the ranch and split-level styles built during that period, also shed water more slowly, giving moisture more time to work under flashing and around penetrations.

## Our Roofing Installation and Replacement Process in Lakeland South

Every roofing project begins with a full inspection of the existing system: decking condition, flashing at chimneys and vents, soffit and fascia integrity, and attic ventilation. Ventilation is a detail that gets skipped on rushed jobs, but in a climate like Lakeland South's, inadequate attic airflow traps moisture that degrades new roofing from the inside out. We address ventilation as part of the replacement, not as an afterthought.

Once the scope is confirmed, the old roofing is stripped to the deck. Any soft or delaminated sheathing is replaced before new underlayment goes down. We use synthetic underlayment rather than traditional felt on most Lakeland South installations because it handles repeated wet-dry cycles without wrinkling or tearing during the layover period between tear-off and final installation. Ice-and-water shield goes along all eaves and valleys, where standing water from clogged gutters or slow drainage is most likely to back up under the roofline.

Material selection is matched to the home's pitch, exposure, and HOA guidelines where applicable. Architectural shingles are the most common choice in this area for their wind resistance and longer warranty profiles. We walk through options with each homeowner before ordering, and we do not subcontract the installation crew.
