Algona sits in the low-lying corridor between the Green River and the Auburn Valley floor, and that geography matters when it comes to mold. The area's combination of saturated soils, heavy Pacific Northwest rainfall, and the persistent marine air that settles into the valley each fall creates conditions where moisture finds its way into crawl spaces, wall cavities, and attic sheathing faster than most homeowners expect. When mold takes hold here, it rarely stays contained to the surface, and surface cleaning alone will not solve it.

## Why Algona Properties See Mold Issues

The Green River Valley's high water table is a real factor for homes in Algona. During wet seasons, hydrostatic pressure can push moisture through concrete block foundations and slab perimeters, feeding mold colonies in crawl spaces and lower-level framing before any visible staining appears upstairs. The region averages well over 150 days of measurable precipitation annually, and homes that were built without modern vapor barriers, which describes a significant portion of the housing stock in this part of King County, are especially vulnerable.

Crawl spaces in Algona-area homes frequently show evidence of long-term condensation rather than a single flood event. That slow, chronic moisture is actually harder to address than a burst pipe, because the affected materials have had time to absorb deeply into wood framing, OSB sheathing, and insulation batts. By the time a musty odor reaches the living area, the underlying colony is often well established.

## Our Mold Remediation Process in Algona

Effective mold abatement starts with measurement, not assumption. Before any containment goes up, the crew uses thermal imaging and moisture meters to map exactly where moisture is elevated, because mold follows water and the visible growth is rarely the full picture. That assessment shapes the containment strategy, the scope of material removal, and the drying plan.

Containment uses negative air pressure and heavy-gauge poly barriers to isolate the work area so that spores disturbed during removal do not migrate to unaffected rooms. Affected porous materials, drywall, insulation, and compromised wood, are removed and bagged on-site. Structural framing that can be retained is treated and encapsulated. HEPA air scrubbers run throughout the project and continue after physical work is complete. Clearance testing by an independent industrial hygienist confirms the space is safe before the containment comes down. The remediation work is performed in accordance with IICRC S520 protocols, and National Restoration Construction holds IICRC certification, EPA certification, and a Lead-Safe Certified Firm designation, which matters on older homes where disturbing wall materials can mean disturbing lead paint.

Mold claims in Washington can be complicated by how the originating water loss is categorized. ## Local Note

Homes in the Algona and Pacific area that were built during the postwar housing expansion of the late 1940s and 1950s often used a combination of concrete block foundations and minimal subfloor ventilation that was standard practice at the time but performs poorly by modern standards. Those crawl spaces tend to trap humid air against the framing year-round. When remediating mold in these homes, the remediation scope almost always needs to include a ventilation correction or vapor barrier installation, otherwise the conditions that grew the mold in the first place will simply regenerate it. Treating the mold without addressing the moisture source is a temporary fix.
