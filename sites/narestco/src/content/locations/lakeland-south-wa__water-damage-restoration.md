## Why Lakeland South Properties Are Vulnerable to Water Damage

The Pacific Northwest's rainfall pattern is less about dramatic single-day deluges and more about sustained, low-intensity saturation that accumulates over weeks. Lakeland South and the surrounding unincorporated Pierce County corridor share a soil profile that drains slowly, meaning water that pools against a foundation or under a slab has nowhere to go quickly. Crawl spaces are common in this area's residential construction, and a crawl space that takes on standing water during a wet winter can saturate floor joists and subfloor sheathing before the surface above shows any visible sign.

Plumbing failures compound the problem. Homes built in the 1980s and 1990s, which make up a significant portion of Lakeland South's housing stock, often have polybutylene or early CPVC supply lines that become brittle over time. A slow leak behind a vanity or inside a wall can go undetected for weeks, allowing mold colonization to begin within 24 to 48 hours of initial saturation.

## Our Water Damage Restoration Process in Lakeland South

Every job starts with a moisture assessment, not a visual inspection. Thermal imaging and calibrated moisture meters reveal what is wet behind drywall, under flooring, and inside wall cavities before a single panel comes down. That data shapes the drying plan and becomes part of the documentation package for your insurer.

Once the scope is mapped, water removal begins using truck-mounted and portable extraction units sized to the volume of standing water. Structural drying follows: commercial-grade desiccant or refrigerant dehumidifiers and high-velocity air movers are positioned based on the moisture readings, not on a generic room layout. Daily monitoring adjusts equipment placement as materials dry. When readings confirm materials have reached acceptable moisture content per the IICRC S500 standard, equipment is removed and a final report is generated. Nothing is closed out on a timeline, only on data.

## Local Note

Crawl spaces in Lakeland South and the broader unincorporated Pierce County area frequently have vapor barriers that were installed years ago and have since shifted, torn, or been compromised by pests. When a crawl space floods, water often pools on top of a damaged barrier rather than draining, which means the standing water reading on the surface can understate how saturated the soil and the framing above it actually are.
