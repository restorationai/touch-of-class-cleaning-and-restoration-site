## How Much Does Water Damage Restoration Cost in Nevada?

In Nevada, water damage restoration typically costs between $1,500 and $8,000 for a standard residential loss, with the average homeowner paying around $3,500. That range is wide because a Category 1 supply-line leak caught in two hours is a completely different job from a Category 3 sewage backup that soaked a finished basement for a weekend.

Here are typical cost ranges by scenario:

| Scenario | Typical Cost Range |
|---|---|
| Single room, clean water (Cat 1), caught early | $1,200 - $3,500 |
| Multi-room, clean water, 24-48 hours of saturation | $3,000 - $6,500 |
| Gray water (Cat 2) - appliance leak, toilet overflow | $2,500 - $7,000 |
| Black water (Cat 3) - sewage backup or floodwater | $4,000 - $15,000 |
| Slab leak with flooring removal and structural drying | $5,000 - $12,000 |
| Attic water intrusion with insulation replacement | $3,500 - $9,000 |

These are industry-typical figures for the Nevada market. Your actual cost depends on the specific conditions at your property. A written scope from a certified restorer is the only reliable number for your loss.

## What Factors Drive the Cost Up or Down?

Five things move the price more than anything else on a water damage job.

**Water category.** The [IICRC S500 standard](https://www.iicrc.org) classifies water losses into three categories. Category 1 (clean water from a supply line or rain intrusion) is the least expensive to remediate. Category 2 (gray water from a dishwasher overflow or washing machine) carries microbial risk and requires antimicrobial treatment. Category 3 (black water, also called grossly contaminated water, from sewage backups or floodwater) requires full PPE, containment, and disposal protocols that add significant labor and material cost.

**Water class (how deep the saturation goes).** Class 1 losses affect a small area with minimal absorption into materials. Class 4 losses involve deeply saturated materials like hardwood subfloors, concrete, or plaster that require specialty drying techniques and much longer equipment run times. The IICRC S500 drying standard governs how far moisture readings must drop before equipment comes out.

**Square footage affected.** Restoration pricing is partly calculated per square foot. A 200-square-foot bathroom loss costs far less than a 1,200-square-foot main floor.

**Materials involved.** Drywall and carpet are relatively inexpensive to remove and replace. Hardwood flooring, tile, cabinetry, and insulation add material and labor cost. In Henderson and the Las Vegas Valley, stucco construction and concrete slab foundations are common, and slab moisture migration can complicate drying timelines.

**Response time.** Water damage follows a predictable timeline: within the first 24 hours, porous materials absorb moisture but structural damage is limited. Between 24 and 72 hours, mold begins to colonize wet materials (the [EPA notes](https://www.epa.gov/mold) that mold can start growing in as little as 24 to 48 hours). After 72 hours, secondary damage compounds rapidly. Every hour of delay adds to the remediation scope. Calling a certified team immediately is the single most cost-effective decision you can make.

## What Does the Water Damage Restoration Process Look Like?

Knowing what you are paying for helps you evaluate any scope of work you receive. A properly executed water damage restoration follows these phases:

1. **Emergency response and inspection.** A certified technician arrives, identifies the water source, stops ongoing intrusion if possible, and uses moisture meters and thermal imaging to map the full extent of saturation. This step defines the scope.
2. **Water extraction.** Truck-mounted or portable extractors remove standing water. This step is fast but critical: the more water removed mechanically, the shorter the drying time.
3. **Structural drying.** High-velocity air movers and commercial-grade LGR (low-grain refrigerant) dehumidifiers run continuously. The IICRC S500 standard sets the drying goals: materials must reach their equilibrium moisture content before equipment is removed. In Nevada's dry climate, ambient conditions can help, but affected materials still need targeted drying.
4. **Demolition of unsalvageable materials.** Wet drywall, saturated insulation, and soaked flooring that cannot be dried in place are removed. This is called a flood cut when drywall is removed to a certain height.
5. **Antimicrobial treatment.** Applied to affected structural cavities and surfaces to prevent mold colonization during and after drying.
6. **Moisture verification.** Daily moisture readings document drying progress. Equipment stays until readings meet the standard, not just until the space feels dry.
7. **Reconstruction.** Once the structure is dry and cleared, rebuild begins: new drywall, insulation, flooring, and paint. This phase is priced separately from mitigation. Life Savers Restoration LLC handles both mitigation and reconstruction, so you work with one team from water extraction through finished repairs.

## Will Homeowners Insurance Cover Water Damage Restoration in Nevada?

Most standard homeowners policies in Nevada cover sudden, accidental water damage but exclude gradual leaks and flood events. The distinction matters a lot for your out-of-pocket cost.

**Typically covered:** A pipe bursts, a water heater fails, an appliance supply line gives out, or rain enters through a storm-damaged roof. These are sudden and accidental events that most HO-3 policies cover, minus your deductible.

**Typically NOT covered:** A slow leak under a sink that went undetected for months, groundwater flooding, or damage from a backed-up municipal sewer without a specific endorsement. Flood damage from rising water requires a separate [NFIP flood insurance policy](https://www.fema.gov/flood-insurance).

**What protects your claim:** Documentation. Moisture readings taken at the time of discovery, photos before any extraction begins, and a written scope of work tied to the IICRC moisture standard give your adjuster the evidence they need to process the claim. Life Savers Restoration LLC documents every job with moisture logs and photos from day one, which supports a clean claim submission.

If you have already filed a claim and are working through the adjuster process, the written scope from a certified restorer is your single most important document. Adjusters work from line-item estimates, and a scope built to the IICRC standard is harder to dispute than a verbal quote.
