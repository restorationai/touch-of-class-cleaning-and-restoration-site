## What Should You Look For in a Water Damage Restoration Company?

The most important credentials for a water damage restoration company are IICRC WRT and ASD certifications, 24/7 emergency availability, and the ability to produce a compliant drying log for your insurance claim.

Here is what each of those actually means in practice:

**IICRC WRT (Water Damage Restoration Technician):** Trained to classify water losses by category (clean water, gray water, black water) and class (amount of moisture absorbed into materials). This classification drives the entire mitigation scope and directly affects what your insurer will authorize.

**IICRC ASD (Applied Structural Drying):** The technician can calculate the correct number of air movers and dehumidifiers for your specific structure, set target moisture content goals for each material type, and document daily readings until the structure reaches those goals. Without this, a crew might pull equipment too early, leaving moisture in wall cavities that becomes a mold problem three weeks later.

**24/7 availability:** Water damage follows a well-documented timeline. Within the first hour, water absorbs into drywall and subfloor. Within 24 hours, Category 1 water (clean) can degrade to Category 2 as it contacts building materials. By 48-72 hours, mold colonization is possible. A company that can only respond during business hours costs you real structural damage. The [EPA's guidance on mold](https://www.epa.gov/mold) recommends beginning drying within 24-48 hours to prevent mold growth.

**Insurance documentation:** Your adjuster will want a moisture map (pre-drying readings by location), a daily drying log, and a written scope of work with line-item pricing. A company that cannot produce these documents in a format your insurer accepts can delay your claim by weeks. For more on what your policy actually covers, see our guide on [homeowners insurance and water damage](/blog/does-homeowners-insurance-cover-water-damage/).

**Licensed and insured:** In Nevada, restoration contractors should carry general liability insurance and, for certain work, a contractor's license. Ask for the license number and verify it with the [Nevada State Contractors Board](https://www.nvcontractorsboard.com) before authorizing work.

## What to Do Before the Restoration Crew Arrives

Stop the source of water first, then document everything, then call a restoration company. Do not wait to document before calling.

If a pipe is still flowing, locate your main shutoff and turn it off. If you do not know where it is, your water meter typically has a shutoff at the street. Once the source is controlled:

1. Photograph every affected room before touching anything. Walk the perimeter, shoot from corners, get close-ups of waterlines on walls and flooring.
2. Move valuables and electronics out of the wet area if you can do so safely.
3. Do not run fans or your HVAC system. Unconditioned airflow in a water-damaged space can spread contamination and complicate moisture mapping.
4. Call your restoration company and then call your insurance company to open a claim. The restoration company should be on-site before your adjuster, not after.
