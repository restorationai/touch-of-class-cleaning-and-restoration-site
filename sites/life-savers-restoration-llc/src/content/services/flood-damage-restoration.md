**Flood damage in Henderson rarely looks like a broken pipe.** It looks like water that pushed in from outside, under a slider door, through a stem wall, up through a saturated yard, carrying silt, lawn chemicals, or wash runoff with it. We answer 24/7, and the first few hours after flood water enters a home determine whether you're dealing with a drying job or a demolition job.

## What flood damage restoration actually involves

Flood water is almost never clean. Once water has touched soil, landscaping, or a street gutter before entering a structure, it's treated as Category 3, contaminated water, which changes the entire scope of work compared to a supply line leak. That means more material removal, not less, and antimicrobial treatment on everything the water touched. In a typical Henderson home, that's drywall at the base of walls, baseboards, carpet pad, and sometimes cabinetry toe-kicks or crawlspace insulation. Equipment on site usually includes submersible pumps or extraction wands for standing water, air movers and low-grain-refrigerant dehumidifiers for structural drying, and moisture meters to track drywall, subfloor, and framing readings over several days rather than relying on a single visual check.

## Our process

1. **Safety and contamination check.** Before anything else, we confirm the water source and category, check for electrical hazards, and identify any standing water near outlets or appliances.
2. **Extraction and debris removal.** Standing water, mud, and silt get pumped and extracted first. Flood water carries sediment that regular extraction wands can clog, so heavier debris often gets removed by hand before extraction equipment runs.
3. **Flood cut and material removal.** Drywall and insulation that absorbed contaminated water typically get cut several inches above the waterline, not just at the visible stain, because wicking moves water higher than it appears.
4. **Antimicrobial application.** Surfaces that stay (studs, concrete, subfloor) get treated to address the bacterial load that comes with Category 3 water, following IICRC S500 guidance for contaminated water categories.
5. **Structural drying and monitoring.** Air movers and dehumidifiers run until moisture readings in framing and subfloor return to normal, not until the carpet feels dry, with daily or near-daily monitoring logged for the insurance file.

## What separates a good flood response from a bad one

The most common mistake is treating flood water like a clean water loss to save time and money. Drying over mud residue without removing it, skipping the flood cut because the drywall "looks fine," or declining antimicrobial treatment all lead to secondary problems, usually odor and microbial growth, within a week or two. Adjusters reviewing a flood claim typically want to see moisture maps showing readings room by room, photos of the waterline and any removed materials, and a clear equipment log showing when drying started and ended. Restoration work that's missing that documentation often gets the claim pushed back for more information, which delays payment and repairs.

## What does flood damage restoration cost?

Costs vary widely based on how much water entered, how long it sat before extraction, and whether contamination requires material removal versus drying in place. Every loss is different, which is why Life Savers Restoration LLC provides a written scope of work before any work begins. Homeowners insurance typically does not cover flood damage from external sources like overflowing washes or storm surface water unless a separate flood policy is in place, though water that enters through a storm-damaged roof or window is often treated differently, so it's worth having the cause documented clearly for your carrier.

## Seasonal & regional considerations

Henderson's monsoon season, roughly July through September, brings short, intense storms that overwhelm desert washes and arroyos faster than the hardpan caliche soil can absorb water. Homes near wash corridors or at the base of slopes in areas like McCullough Hills or near Lake Las Vegas see the highest risk of sudden surface water intrusion. The dry climate the rest of the year actually works in a homeowner's favor for drying, but it can also mask a problem: a home can look and feel bone-dry on the surface within a day while framing and subfloor stay wet underneath for much longer.
