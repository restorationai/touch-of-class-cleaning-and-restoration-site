## What water leak detection actually involves

A leak that's been running behind drywall or under a slab for weeks doesn't always show up as a wet spot. More often it shows up as a water bill that's climbed for no reason, a baseboard that's started to cup, a musty smell near a closet wall, or a warm patch on the tile floor that wasn't there last year. By the time a leak is visible, the damage underneath is usually well ahead of what you can see. Water leak detection in Henderson means finding that source before it becomes a drywall, flooring, or foundation problem, using equipment that doesn't require tearing the house apart to look.

The work itself is diagnostic, not demolition. Technicians use acoustic listening devices that pick up the sound of pressurized water moving through a pipe, infrared thermal cameras that read temperature differences in flooring and walls, and moisture meters that confirm what the thermal imaging suggests. For slab leaks specifically, this often includes isolating sections of the plumbing system under pressure to narrow down which line is losing water. A typical residential leak detection visit runs one to three hours depending on house size and how many plumbing zones need to be checked.

## Our process

1. **Symptom and history review.** We start by asking what prompted the call: a spike in the water bill, a sound of running water with no fixture on, a damp smell, a warped floor. That narrows where to start looking before any equipment comes out.
2. **Non-invasive scanning.** Acoustic sensors and infrared thermal imaging are used first to map likely source areas without opening walls or cutting slab.
3. **Isolation and pressure testing.** For suspected slab or under-slab leaks, we isolate sections of the supply line and monitor pressure drop to confirm which zone is losing water.
4. **Pinpointing and moisture mapping.** Once the general area is narrowed down, moisture meters confirm the exact location and the extent of the wet zone, so any access point is as small as possible.
5. **Written findings before repair.** You get a report showing where the leak is, how we found it, and what access (if any) is needed for a plumber to repair it, before anything is opened up.

## What separates a good leak detection response from a bad one

The most common mistake is guessing based on a single wet spot and cutting open drywall or slab in the wrong place. Water travels. A stain on a ceiling below a bathroom doesn't mean the leak is directly above it, it often means the leak is several feet away and running along a joist or following the slope of the subfloor before dripping down. Good leak detection confirms the source with instruments before any cutting happens.

Another place corners get cut is skipping the pressure isolation step on suspected slab leaks and just recommending a full re-pipe because it's faster to quote. That's an expensive answer to a problem that's sometimes a single fitting. On the documentation side, insurance adjusters want to see the detection method used, the moisture readings, and photos or thermal images, not just a verbal description of where a tech thinks the leak is. That documentation is also what supports a fair settlement if the leak has caused secondary damage like mold or subfloor rot.

## What does water leak detection cost?

Cost depends on how accessible the suspected leak area is, whether it's a slab leak requiring pressure isolation, and how much square footage needs to be scanned. Every property is different, which is why Life Savers Restoration LLC provides a written scope of the detection method and findings before any repair or demolition work begins. Homeowners insurance sometimes covers the cost of locating a leak when it's tied to a covered water damage claim, but routine leak detection with no resulting damage is often treated as a maintenance expense, so it's worth checking your policy language.

## Seasonal & regional considerations

Henderson's clay-heavy soil and the shifting that comes with our wide temperature swings between summer and winter put real stress on slab plumbing over time, which is part of why slab leaks are a regular call here rather than a rare one. Many homes in this area were built on copper supply lines that are now decades old, and pinhole leaks in copper tend to show up gradually rather than as a sudden break, which is exactly the kind of leak that acoustic and thermal detection catches before it becomes a flooring replacement.
