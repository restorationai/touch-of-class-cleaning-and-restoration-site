**Water pooling under a server room floor at 2 a.m., or smoke damage closing a retail storefront before the morning rush?** We answer 24/7 and understand that every hour a business stays closed is lost revenue, not just property damage. Commercial losses move differently than residential ones: more square footage, more stakeholders, and a landlord, tenant, or corporate office that all need updates at once.

## What commercial restoration actually involves

Commercial restoration covers the mitigation and rebuild work that happens after water, fire, or storm damage hits an office, retail space, restaurant, warehouse, or multi-tenant building. The scope is broader than a single-family home: commercial HVAC systems, suspended ceiling grids, raised server flooring, point-of-sale wiring, walk-in coolers, and tenant improvement finishes all behave differently than residential materials when they get wet or scorched. A single-story strip mall unit might dry out in days. A multi-floor office building with a failed rooftop unit can mean water tracking through several tenant spaces, each with its own lease terms and insurance policy. Equipment on a commercial job scales accordingly: low-grain refrigerant dehumidifiers, high-velocity air movers sized for open floor plans, and negative air machines when fire or mold work requires containment near occupied suites.

## Our process

1. **Business impact assessment.** Before any equipment goes in, we walk the space with the property manager or business owner to identify what has to stay operational (point-of-sale systems, walk-in refrigeration, server racks) and what can be shut down for mitigation.
2. **Emergency mitigation.** Water extraction, structural drying setup, or fire and smoke stabilization begins immediately, prioritizing areas that affect life safety, egress, and any equipment still under power.
3. **Phased work to protect occupancy.** Where possible, we section the job so part of the space can reopen while drying or rebuild continues elsewhere, which matters most for retail, restaurants, and multi-tenant office buildings.
4. **Coordination with ownership and carriers.** We document room by room with photos and moisture or soot readings so the building owner, property manager, and insurance adjuster are working from the same record, not separate accounts of the loss.
5. **Reconstruction and sign-off.** Once drying or cleaning verifies clearance, we move into rebuild: flooring, drywall, paint, and any tenant finish work, closing out with a final walkthrough before the space goes back into use.

## What separates a good commercial restoration response from a bad one

The most common mistake on commercial jobs is treating them like an oversized residential loss. A less experienced crew will dry a space by square footage alone without accounting for ceiling plenum airflow, HVAC return paths that can recirculate contaminated air through the whole building, or wiring and low-voltage systems hidden above drop ceilings. Adjusters reviewing commercial claims look closely at documented moisture logs over time, not just a single reading, and they expect a clear separation between mitigation costs and reconstruction scope. Another frequent gap is failing to coordinate with other tenants in a shared building; water or smoke rarely respects a lease line, and a response that only protects one unit often creates a second claim next door. A thorough commercial response documents affected square footage by tenant, keeps a written log of equipment placement and runtime, and loops in property management before work expands beyond the original space.

## What does commercial restoration cost?

Commercial costs vary more than residential ones because building size, occupancy type, and how much of the space can stay operational during work all factor in. Most commercial losses are billed by scope, drying equipment days, and square footage affected rather than a flat rate. Every loss is different, which is why Life Savers Restoration LLC provides a written scope of work before any repair work begins, separate from the emergency mitigation call. Most commercial property policies cover sudden water and fire losses and often include business interruption coverage, though policy language and deductibles vary by carrier and lease structure.

## Seasonal & regional considerations

Henderson's summer monsoon storms can overwhelm flat commercial roofing systems that weren't built for sudden heavy rainfall, leading to ceiling leaks in retail and office spaces built in the growth years of the 1990s and 2000s. Many commercial buildings in the valley also rely on rooftop package HVAC units, and a failed condensate line or refrigerant leak can go unnoticed for days in a space that isn't walked daily, especially over a long weekend when a business is closed.
