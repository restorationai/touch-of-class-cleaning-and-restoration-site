A musty smell that won't go away after the AC has been running, a water stain on the ceiling from a slow roof leak two summers ago, a swamp cooler that got shut down for the season but left moisture behind in the attic. These are the calls that come in before anyone has actually seen mold, just the suspicion of it. Mold inspection and testing tells you what's actually growing, where, and at what concentration, so you're not guessing or paying for remediation you don't need.

## What mold inspection and testing actually involves

A proper mold inspection starts with a visual walkthrough of the property, looking at areas where moisture collects: bathrooms, under sinks, around window frames, attic sheathing, crawlspaces, and anywhere there's been a past water event. From there, the inspector uses a moisture meter and often a thermal imaging camera to find damp building materials that aren't visibly stained yet. Mold spore testing comes next, usually through air sampling with a calibrated pump and spore trap cassettes, sometimes paired with surface samples (swab or tape lift) if visible growth is present. Samples go to a third-party laboratory for analysis, not an in-house lab, so the results hold up if you need them for an insurance claim or a real estate transaction. Turnaround on lab results typically runs a few business days. The whole on-site process for a single-family home usually takes under two hours.

## Our process

1. **Visual assessment and moisture mapping.** We walk the property with a moisture meter and thermal camera to identify damp materials, past water intrusion points, and conditions that support mold growth, even where nothing is visible yet.
2. **Indoor air quality testing.** Spore trap air samples are taken in suspect rooms and compared against an outdoor baseline sample, since every property has some ambient mold spore count, the question is whether indoor levels are elevated relative to outdoor.
3. **Surface and material sampling.** When we see suspected growth on drywall, wood framing, or HVAC components, we take tape lift or swab samples to identify the specific mold genus present.
4. **Lab analysis.** Samples are sent to an accredited environmental lab. We don't read our own samples, that's a conflict of interest we won't create.
5. **Written report and recommendations.** You get a document with lab results, photos, moisture readings, and a clear recommendation: no action needed, monitor, or remediate, with a defined scope if remediation is warranted.

## What separates a good mold inspection from a bad one

The most common shortcut is skipping the outdoor baseline air sample. Without it, an indoor spore count is just a number with no context, you can't tell if it's elevated or normal for the region. Another is relying on a single sample location in a multi-room property, mold doesn't move evenly through HVAC systems or wall cavities, so one air sample in the living room tells you nothing about what's happening behind the washer. A third issue, and one adjusters specifically watch for, is a company that inspects and then quotes its own remediation work off the same report. That's a conflict of interest, and some insurance carriers will flag it. Our inspectors hold IICRC AMRT mold credentials and the report stands on its own whether or not we're the ones doing follow-up work.

## What does mold inspection and testing cost?

Typical industry costs scale with the size of the property and how many sample locations are needed, not just square footage. Every property and every suspected source is different, which is why Life Savers Restoration LLC provides a written scope of the inspection, including number of samples, before any testing begins. Homeowners insurance typically covers mold testing only when it's tied to a covered water loss, not as a standalone preventive service, so check your policy's mold endorsement or sublimit before assuming coverage.

## Seasonal & regional considerations

Henderson's dry desert climate keeps ambient mold spore counts lower than humid regions most of the year, which actually makes elevated indoor readings easier to spot by comparison. The exceptions are monsoon season, typically July through September, when short bursts of high humidity and flash flooding can push moisture into homes fast, and older homes still running evaporative coolers, which introduce water vapor into attics and wall cavities on a daily basing cycle. Homes built before the 2000s with original swamp cooler ductwork are among the more common calls we see for attic moisture and related air quality questions.
