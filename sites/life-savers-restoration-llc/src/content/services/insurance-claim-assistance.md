A water, fire, or storm loss is stressful enough without a claim that stalls. Most homeowners file a property damage claim only a handful of times in their life, and the paperwork, the moisture readings, the scope of loss, the back-and-forth with an adjuster is unfamiliar territory at exactly the moment the house is torn up and the clock is running. Insurance claim assistance means the restoration company handling the damage also documents it the way the carrier needs to see it, so the claim is built on evidence from the first day instead of reconstructed after the fact.

## What insurance claim assistance actually involves

Insurance claim assistance is not a separate errand you hire out. It runs alongside the actual mitigation work, and that is exactly why it matters: the crew drying your home is the same crew capturing the readings and photos that prove what happened and how bad it was. Once the water is gone or the smoke is cleaned and the property is back to normal, that evidence is gone with it. Documenting as we go is the only way to build a claim that holds up.

In practice, that means capturing dated photos and video before anything is moved, logging moisture and water-category readings, writing an itemized scope of what is affected, and keeping drying logs that show the work met the IICRC standard. Life Savers Restoration LLC is an IICRC Certified Firm, and that documentation follows the same standards your carrier's guidelines reference. When the file an adjuster receives already answers their questions, the claim moves.

We also coordinate directly with your adjuster once a claim number is assigned, so the scope we document and the scope they approve match. Where your policy allows, we bill the carrier directly for covered work, which in most cases leaves you responsible for your deductible rather than the full invoice up front.

## Our process

1. **Mitigation and documentation together.** From the first visit we stabilize the property and document the loss at the same time: photos and video before anything is moved, moisture mapping, water category, and a written scope of the affected areas. This is the evidence your claim is built on.

2. **Coverage review with you.** Before major work begins we confirm what your policy covers and how billing will work, so you know what the carrier is expected to pay and what falls to your deductible. No work of consequence starts on an assumption about coverage.

3. **Adjuster coordination.** Once your claim number is assigned, we communicate directly with your adjuster on the technical facts of the loss, share our documentation, and work to align our scope with the approved scope so the two do not drift apart.

4. **Direct billing where allowed.** For covered work, we bill your insurance carrier directly wherever the policy permits it, and provide you a clear accounting of the deductible and any items the policy does not cover.

5. **Supplements when the scope changes.** Restoration often uncovers damage that was not visible on day one. When that happens we document the additional loss and submit a supplement with the supporting readings and photos, so the final settlement reflects the full covered scope rather than the first guess.

## Where homeowners lose money on a claim

The most common way a claim comes up short is thin documentation. An adjuster approves what the file supports, and a loss recorded with a couple of phone photos and no moisture data invites a low estimate or a request for more information that drags the claim out for weeks. A file with dated photos, category readings, drying logs, and an itemized scope is far harder to discount.

The second is scope drift, where the work the restoration crew performs and the scope the adjuster approved are two different documents. Keeping those aligned from the start, and supplementing properly when hidden damage surfaces, is what prevents an out-of-pocket surprise at the end.

The third is waiting. Most policies require the homeowner to take reasonable steps to prevent further damage, so delaying mitigation to "wait for the adjuster" can actually reduce what the carrier pays, because the secondary damage that grows in the meantime may be treated as preventable.

## Payment and financing options

Not every loss is fully covered, and a deductible can be a stretch to cover all at once. Financing and flexible payment options are available to help spread the cost of the portion insurance does not pay. The right option depends on the size of the project, so we go over what is available with you before work begins rather than committing you to anything up front. We keep the money side as clear as the restoration side: you should always know what your insurance is covering, what your deductible is, and what any remaining balance looks like.

## An honest note on what we do and don't do

Life Savers Restoration LLC documents and bills the restoration work and coordinates with your adjuster on the facts of the loss. We are not a public adjuster and we do not give legal advice on your policy. What we bring is a thoroughly documented file built to the IICRC standard, which is the single biggest factor in getting the full covered scope approved.
