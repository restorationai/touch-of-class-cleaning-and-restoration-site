## Why Nellis AFB Properties See Biohazard Needs

Base housing and the neighborhoods that serve it turn over on a different rhythm than a typical civilian subdivision. Permanent change of station moves, young enlisted families, and shared barracks or dormitory space all mean more units in use at any given time, and a wider range of incident types, from unattended deaths to accidental exposures to bodily fluids in common areas. The desert climate adds its own pressure: once contamination sits for even a day or two in Southern Nevada's summer heat, odor and bacterial growth accelerate faster than they would in a cooler, more humid region, which narrows the window before a job goes from manageable to much harder to fully remediate.

Much of the housing on and around Nellis AFB was built on concrete slab foundations, common across the Mojave Desert region, with minimal subfloor ventilation compared to raised-foundation construction elsewhere.
