## Why Nellis AFB Properties See Water Damage Issues

Most structures around Nellis AFB sit on slab-on-grade foundations, a standard for Las Vegas valley construction since the postwar building boom that shaped this part of Clark County. Slab construction hides plumbing runs under a layer of concrete and, often, a hardpan caliche soil base that does not drain the way topsoil does elsewhere. When a supply line under the slab fails, water has nowhere to go but sideways and up, soaking baseboards and flooring edges long before a homeowner notices a wet spot. Add in the region's evaporative coolers, which many older homes and base-adjacent properties still run through peak summer, and you get a second common failure point: clogged float valves and cracked water lines that dump gallons into attics and ceiling cavities. Then there's monsoon season, typically July through September, when short, intense downpours overwhelm roof drains and flat or low-slope commercial roofing faster than residential gutters ever would.

## Our Water Damage Restoration Process in Nellis AFB

We start with extraction, pulling standing water with truck-mount and portable units so the clock on mold growth stops as early as possible. From there we run moisture meters across slab, drywall, and framing to map exactly how far water traveled, since desert construction tends to push moisture laterally under slabs rather than straight down. Structural drying follows, set up per the IICRC S500 standard, using air movers and desiccant or refrigerant dehumidifiers depending on ambient humidity, which can swing widely here between a humid monsoon afternoon and a bone-dry desert evening. We document moisture readings daily until materials hit dry standard, then photograph and log everything for the insurance file.

One thing that trips up a lot of out-of-town restoration crews: in this part of the valley, a wet exterior wall doesn't necessarily mean a roof leak.
