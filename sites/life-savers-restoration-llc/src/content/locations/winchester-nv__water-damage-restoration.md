## Why Winchester Properties See Water Damage Issues

Winchester is an older, unincorporated pocket of the Las Vegas valley, and a lot of its housing stock dates back to the 1950s through 1970s, well before current plumbing codes. Galvanized supply lines and original cast iron drain stacks are still common, and both fail from the inside out, with no visible warning until a wall or ceiling is already wet. Add in the desert monsoon pattern from July through September, when a single storm cell can dump an hour's worth of rain on hardpan soil that barely absorbs it, and you get flash flooding that pushes water against foundations and under exterior doors fast. Slab-on-grade construction, standard across this part of Clark County, means a slab leak doesn't show up as a ceiling stain. It shows up as a warm spot on the floor, a spike in the water bill from Las Vegas Valley Water District, or carpet that stays damp no matter how many fans you point at it.

## Our Water Damage Restoration Process in Winchester

On arrival we isolate the water source, whether it is a failed supply line, a slab leak, or storm intrusion, and start pulling standing water with truck-mount extractors. Because so many Winchester properties have slab foundations, we check moisture content in the concrete itself, not just the carpet pad above it, since trapped moisture under flooring can re-wet a room weeks after it looks dry on the surface. Drywall gets moisture-mapped room by room, and we pull baseboards or drill small weep holes where wall cavities have trapped water, which is common behind kitchen and bathroom plumbing walls in homes built before 1980. Drying equipment is calibrated to the dry desert air outside, which sounds like it should help but can actually pull moisture out of materials unevenly if the HVAC and dehumidifiers aren't balanced correctly.

## Equipment & Methods We Use
