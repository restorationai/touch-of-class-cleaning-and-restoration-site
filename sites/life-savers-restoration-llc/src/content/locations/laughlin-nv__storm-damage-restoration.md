## Why Laughlin Properties See Storm Damage

Laughlin sits in a stretch of the Mojave where summer monsoon cells roll up from the river corridor fast and hard. The ground here doesn't drink water the way clay or loam soils do elsewhere, hardpan desert soil sheds rainfall almost immediately, which is why a short burst of rain can flash-flood a wash or parking area within minutes. A lot of the housing and resort-adjacent construction in the area relies on flat or low-slope roofing systems built for sun, not standing water, and those roofs struggle when a storm cell parks overhead longer than the drainage system was designed for. Manufactured and modular homes, common throughout the Laughlin area, are also more exposed to wind-driven water intrusion around seams and skirting than site-built homes with deeper foundations. Add in the high straight-line winds that can precede a monsoon cell off the river, and it's a combination that tends to hit roofs, carports, and exterior stucco all at once.

## Our Storm Damage Restoration Process in Laughlin

The first call is about stopping the damage from spreading, not just cleaning up what already happened. We tarp open roof sections, board broken windows, and extract standing water before it works into subfloor or insulation that's harder to dry once it's saturated. From there we pull moisture readings on drywall, baseboards, and flooring to map out exactly how far water traveled, because in a flat-roof structure a leak at one corner of the building can travel yards before it ever shows a stain. Drying follows the IICRC S500 standard, with air movers and dehumidification sized to the structure, not a one-size-fits-all setup. Downed tree limbs and wind debris get cleared as part of the same visit when they're blocking access or sitting against siding, since debris left against stucco or wood trim holds moisture and invites rot or pest issues long after the storm itself is gone.

## Local note

One thing property owners new to the area don't always expect: Laughlin's monsoon humidity spikes hard during a storm, even though the desert climate is bone-dry the rest of the year.
