## Why Enterprise Properties Face Distinct Fire Damage Challenges

The housing stock across Enterprise is unusually mixed for a single unincorporated community. Newer master-planned developments feature open floor plans with cathedral ceilings — layouts that allow smoke to stratify and deposit soot at different densities on different surfaces. Older ranch homes, by contrast, often have original attic insulation and single-pane windows that trap smoke residue in wall cavities long after the fire is out. The desert climate adds another layer of complexity: low humidity accelerates the bonding of smoke particles to porous materials like drywall, wood trim, and upholstery, which means the window for effective dry-soot removal is shorter here than in more humid regions. Waiting even a day or two before beginning cleanup allows acidic soot to etch metal fixtures, yellow painted surfaces, and permanently stain grout and tile.

Enterprise's proximity to the Las Vegas Valley also means many homes rely on forced-air HVAC systems that run year-round — and those systems become smoke-distribution networks during a fire. Soot and odor-carrying particles circulate through return vents and settle inside ductwork, making HVAC cleaning a non-optional step in any thorough post-fire restoration.

## Our Fire Damage Restoration Process in Enterprise

The process begins with a full structural assessment — not just the rooms that burned, but the adjacent spaces where smoke migrated. Technicians use thermal imaging to locate heat-affected areas behind walls and above ceilings that aren't visible to the naked eye. From there, the scope breaks into four parallel workstreams:

**1. Emergency stabilization.** Board-up, roof tarping if the structure is compromised, and temporary power coordination if utilities have been shut off by NV Energy.

**2. Soot and smoke removal.** Dry-chemical sponging for loose soot, followed by wet-cleaning with pH-appropriate agents matched to the surface type. Protein fire residue — common after kitchen fires — requires enzymatic cleaners and more aggressive surface prep than wood or paper-based soot.

**3. Odor neutralization.** Thermal fogging and hydroxyl generation reach into wall cavities, ductwork, and soft goods that surface cleaning cannot address. In Enterprise's sealed, climate-controlled homes, odor treatment often takes longer than the visible cleaning phase.

**4. Structural drying and reconstruction.** If firefighting water is present — and it almost always is — structural drying runs concurrently with smoke remediation. The team is IICRC-certified in both fire/smoke restoration and structural drying, so both workstreams are managed under one scope rather than handed off between contractors.
