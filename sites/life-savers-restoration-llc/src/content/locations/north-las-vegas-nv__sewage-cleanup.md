## Why North Las Vegas Properties See Sewage Backup Problems

The valley's extreme heat — routinely above 110°F in July and August — accelerates grease and bio-waste buildup inside sewer laterals, narrowing pipes faster than in cooler climates. When monsoon moisture arrives in late summer, the sudden ground saturation can shift that caliche layer just enough to crack older clay or cast-iron sewer lines. North Las Vegas also has a significant share of homes built during the rapid-growth decades of the 1980s and 1990s, when builder-grade PVC fittings and shallow lateral depths were common. Those fittings are now at or past their design lifespan, and root intrusion from desert landscaping — ironwood, mesquite, and invasive tamarisk — is a frequent culprit in line failures. Slab-on-grade construction, which dominates the housing stock here, means there is no crawl space buffer: a sewer backup reaches finished flooring almost immediately.

## Our Sewage Cleanup and Sanitization Process in North Las Vegas

Raw sewage is classified as Category 3 water — the most hazardous — and the cleanup protocol reflects that. The process begins with containment: affected rooms are isolated with poly barriers to prevent cross-contamination to unaffected areas of the home. Standing sewage is extracted using truck-mounted units capable of handling solids, not just water. All porous materials that absorbed contamination — drywall, carpet, pad, baseboards — are removed and bagged for regulated disposal per Nevada solid-waste handling requirements.

Once the structure is cleared to hard surfaces, technicians apply an EPA-registered disinfectant rated for Category 3 contamination, dwell time included. In North Las Vegas's dry heat, surfaces can appear dry within hours, but moisture meters and thermal imaging cameras confirm whether the slab or wall cavities still hold residual moisture that could feed mold growth within 24 to 48 hours of a loss. Structural drying with desiccant and refrigerant dehumidifiers follows, sized to the square footage and ambient conditions — desert air is dry but hot, and interior humidity climbs fast when wet materials are present. A final post-remediation verification confirms pathogen levels before any reconstruction begins.
