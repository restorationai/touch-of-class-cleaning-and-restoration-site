## Why Summerlin South Properties See Water Damage

The desert climate here cuts both ways. Low ambient humidity most of the year means surface water looks like it's drying on its own, which leads a lot of homeowners to assume a small leak solved itself. What's actually happening is the moisture is migrating into wall cavities and under cabinetry where airflow never reaches it. Clark County's caliche-heavy soil also compacts and shifts with seasonal moisture swings, which over time stresses slab plumbing and copper supply lines, a common source of the slow, hidden leaks we get called out for in this part of the valley. Add in the summer monsoon pattern, when a single storm can dump more rain in an hour than the ground can absorb, and flat-roofed or low-slope sections of Summerlin South homes occasionally take on water through roof penetrations and parapet flashing.

## Our Water Damage Restoration Process in Summerlin South

We start with moisture mapping, not guesswork. On slab construction, that means thermal imaging and penetrating moisture meters to find out how far water has traveled under tile, luxury vinyl plank, or hardwood before we pull a single plank. Extraction comes next, using truck-mounted or portable units depending on access, followed by structural drying set up to the IICRC S500 standard, which on a concrete slab usually means more air movers and longer dry times than you'd see on a raised wood-frame home back east. Our technicians hold IICRC WRT and ASD credentials, so the drying plan accounts for how slow concrete releases trapped moisture compared to a subfloor, and we document readings daily so the drying log holds up if your insurance adjuster asks questions.
