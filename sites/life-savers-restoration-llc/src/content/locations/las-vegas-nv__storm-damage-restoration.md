## Why Las Vegas Properties Are Vulnerable to Storm Damage

The Mojave's soil is largely caliche — a dense, calcium-carbonate-hardened layer that sits close to the surface across much of the Las Vegas Valley. During a heavy monsoon event, water cannot penetrate that hardpan fast enough to drain naturally. Instead, it sheets across driveways, pools against foundations, and finds every gap in a home's envelope: weep screeds, window frames, garage door seals, and roof penetrations that were never designed for sustained rainfall.

The valley's intense summer heat compounds the problem in a different way. Prolonged UV exposure causes roofing membranes, caulking around skylights, and EPDM flat-roof coatings to crack and shrink over time. A roof that looks intact in a dry April can admit water the moment a monsoon cell parks overhead in July. Stucco exteriors — the dominant finish on homes built here from the 1980s onward — can hide moisture intrusion for weeks before efflorescence or interior staining signals the problem.

High-wind events, including the occasional haboob that pushes through the valley ahead of a monsoon front, add a mechanical damage layer on top of the water intrusion: dislodged roof tiles, broken fascia, downed block-wall sections, and tree limbs through windows. Both damage types often arrive together, which means a storm restoration job in Las Vegas routinely involves water extraction, structural drying, and exterior repair in the same scope of work.

## Our Storm Damage Restoration Process in Las Vegas

When a call comes in, the first priority is stopping ongoing intrusion. If wind has opened the roof or broken a window, crews tarp and board before any drying equipment goes in — otherwise the equipment is fighting a losing battle. Moisture mapping with thermal imaging and pin-type meters follows immediately, because water in a stucco-and-concrete-block home travels differently than it does in wood-frame construction. It tends to wick laterally through block cores and collect behind baseboards well away from the visible entry point.

Extraction and drying are calibrated to Las Vegas's low ambient humidity. The desert air accelerates surface drying, which can be misleading — a wall cavity or subfloor can read dry at the surface while moisture is still trapped inside. The IICRC S500 standard guides drying protocols, and readings are logged daily so documentation is available for insurance adjusters.

Debris removal — broken tile, blown insulation, tree limbs — is staged and hauled before interior work begins. Once the structure is dry and verified with final moisture readings, reconstruction moves forward: drywall, insulation, stucco patching, roofing, and finish work handled under one contractor rather than coordinating multiple trades.
