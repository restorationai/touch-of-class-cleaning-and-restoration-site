**Smoke odor won't lift on its own in a sealed-up Paradise condo.** Between the high-rise towers near the Strip corridor and the garden-style apartment complexes that make up most of the housing stock here, odor problems in Paradise tend to be trapped odor problems: tight building envelopes, shared HVAC risers, and desert air that dries porous materials fast but locks volatile compounds into drywall, stucco, and carpet pad instead of letting them off-gas. Odor removal and deodorization work here means treating the structure, not just masking the room.

## Why Paradise Properties See Odor Problems That Linger

Paradise is mostly multifamily: high-rise condos stacked along the Strip corridor, mid-rise apartment buildings, and a scattering of 1970s-80s single-story homes with stucco exteriors and shared or adjacent HVAC runs. That construction pattern matters for odor work. Smoke, cooking grease, pet accidents, and mildew from a leaking evaporative cooler don't stay contained to one unit, they travel through return air ducts and party walls into neighboring units. Add Southern Nevada's low humidity and summer heat, which pulls odor-causing compounds deeper into stucco pores and unsealed drywall rather than letting them evaporate off the surface, and a smell that would fade in a wetter climate can sit for months in a Paradise building if it isn't treated at the source.

## Our Odor Removal and Deodorization Process

We start by identifying what's actually causing the odor, since masking a smoke smell or a pet odor without finding the source material just means it comes back once the deodorizer wears off. That's porous surfaces: carpet, pad, drywall, insulation, and sometimes HVAC ductwork shared between units in a condo building. Depending on the source and whether the space is occupied, we use hydroxyl generators, thermal fogging, or ozone treatment. Hydroxyl is our go-to for occupied buildings and multi-unit properties because it's safe to run while residents or neighboring tenants are present, which matters in a Strip-corridor high-rise where you can't just evacuate a floor. Ozone treatment is more aggressive and faster for vacant units or post-fire jobs, but it requires the space to be sealed and unoccupied for a set period, something we coordinate directly with property management in shared buildings. Thermal fogging recreates the conditions that drove the original odor, letting a deodorizing solution penetrate the same cracks and fabric fibers smoke did, which is often the only way to fully neutralize a heavy smoke event in upholstery, drapes, and soft goods.

## Paradise HOA and Property Management Coordination

A lot of odor removal jobs in Paradise happen inside condo associations and apartment complexes with their own notice and access rules. Ozone treatment in particular usually requires advance notice to adjacent units because the gas can migrate through shared vents or under doors if the space isn't properly sealed, so we work with building management to schedule treatment windows and confirm HVAC zones are isolated before we start. For insurance-involved losses, mainly fire and smoke damage, we document the affected materials and treatment steps so the file holds up with the carrier, and we coordinate directly with most major insurers on the restoration side of the claim.

## Equipment and Methods We Use

Beyond hydroxyl generators and ozone units, odor jobs in Paradise's apartment and condo stock often need targeted HVAC cleaning, since a shared duct system can keep recirculating an odor from one unit into the next even after the source material is removed. We also use sealing primers on drywall and subfloor after extraction, which blocks residual odor from off-gassing back into the room once standard deodorization is finished. For soft goods, fogging equipment gets the deodorizing agent into fabric weave in a way that surface spraying can't match.

We check adjoining units and shared ductwork as part of every job here, not just the unit that called it in.
