## Why Winchester Properties See Fire Damage Risk

Winchester is one of the older unincorporated communities tucked against the east side of the Las Vegas valley, and a good share of its housing stock dates to the postwar decades when ranch homes with swamp coolers and older electrical panels were the standard build. Those aging electrical systems, combined with window-unit and evaporative cooling equipment that pulls a heavy summer load, are a recurring source of the electrical fires we get called out for in this part of the valley. Low humidity and high summer temperatures also mean that smoke odor binds aggressively into stucco, block construction, and the porous materials common in mid-century Clark County homes, so odor that seems manageable on day one can resurface weeks later if it isn't treated at the source.

## Our Fire Damage Restoration Process in Winchester

We start with a structural safety check, since many Winchester properties have had electrical or HVAC work added on over the decades that isn't always up to current code, and we want to know what we're dealing with before crews go in. From there we board up and tarp exposed openings, remove charred and unsalvageable materials, and run HEPA air scrubbing before any wet cleaning begins, because disturbing soot too early just pushes particulate deeper into HVAC ducting and wall cavities. Smoke odor gets treated with thermal fogging or ozone, depending on the materials involved, and we document every step with photos for the insurance file as we go rather than after the fact. Homes built before 1978 get an added layer of care: our EPA Lead-Safe certification governs how we handle demolition dust and debris removal so disturbed lead paint doesn't become a secondary hazard during the rebuild.
