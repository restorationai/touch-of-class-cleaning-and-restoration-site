## Why Spring Valley Properties See Water Damage Differently

Spring Valley's position in the Las Vegas Valley means the soil is predominantly caliche — a dense, calcium-carbonate-hardened layer that doesn't absorb water well. When a slab leak or heavy monsoon rain saturates the ground near a foundation, that water has nowhere to go except laterally, often finding its way through expansion joints, utility penetrations, and any existing crack in the concrete. Homes built in the rapid-growth decades of the 1980s and 1990s — which make up a substantial portion of Spring Valley's housing stock — frequently used post-tension slab construction. Drilling or cutting into those slabs during repairs requires careful coordination to avoid tensioning cables, which affects how and where moisture meters and drying equipment can be placed.

Spring Valley also experiences the Mojave's monsoon season, typically running July through September. Afternoon storms can dump an inch or more of rain in under an hour onto streets and yards engineered for sun, not drainage. Flat or low-slope rooflines common in stucco ranch-style homes can pond water at parapet edges, and window weep holes — often clogged with years of desert dust — back up and push water into framing.

## Our Water Damage Restoration Process in Spring Valley

When we arrive, the first priority is stopping active water movement and protecting unaffected areas. We use thermal imaging cameras alongside calibrated moisture meters to map exactly where water has traveled — including behind tile backers, inside stucco walls, and under floating wood floors — before a single piece of material comes out. In the Las Vegas Valley's low-humidity environment, it can be tempting to open windows and assume the desert air will do the drying. It won't do it safely or evenly. Controlled drying with commercial-grade desiccant or refrigerant dehumidifiers and high-velocity air movers is the only way to bring structural materials down to acceptable moisture content without creating a secondary mold window.

Our process follows the IICRC S500 standard for water damage restoration. As an IICRC Certified Firm with technicians holding Water Restoration Technician (WRT) and Applied Structural Drying (ASD) credentials, we document moisture readings at every stage — data your insurance adjuster will need for a complete claim.
