## Why Spring Valley Properties See Mold Issues

The Mojave Desert climate feels dry, but that perception works against homeowners. HVAC systems run almost year-round in Spring Valley, and when a supply line drips inside a cabinet or a roof flashing fails during a monsoon storm, the warm, conditioned air traps moisture against building materials rather than evaporating it. Stucco exteriors — standard on homes built here from the 1980s through today — can absorb water at the weep screed and wick it into the wall cavity without showing any visible staining on the interior surface for weeks.

Flat and low-slope rooflines, popular throughout the master-planned communities in this area, are another recurring factor. Standing water on a flat roof after a July or August storm can find its way through aging membrane seams into the insulation and ceiling deck below. By the time a water stain appears on the drywall, mold may already be colonizing the paper facing on the back side of the board — the side you cannot see.

## Our Mold Remediation Process in Spring Valley

Every job starts with a thorough visual inspection and moisture mapping using thermal imaging and calibrated moisture meters. Because slab foundations are the norm here, we pay particular attention to flooring transitions — tile-to-carpet seams and baseboards are common places where moisture migrates laterally after a plumbing event and goes undetected.

Once affected areas are identified, we establish negative-air containment with 6-mil poly barriers and HEPA-filtered air scrubbers before any demolition begins. This prevents spores from being redistributed into living spaces during removal. Affected drywall, insulation, and porous materials are removed, bagged, and disposed of per EPA guidelines. Structural surfaces are then treated with an EPA-registered antimicrobial agent and dried to manufacturer-specified moisture content before any reconstruction begins. The entire process follows the IICRC S520 standard for mold remediation.

Mold claims in Nevada often hinge on whether the underlying water intrusion is considered a sudden and accidental event or the result of long-term seepage.
