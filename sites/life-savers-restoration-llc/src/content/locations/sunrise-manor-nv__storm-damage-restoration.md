## Why Sunrise Manor Properties See Distinctive Storm Damage

The housing stock across Sunrise Manor skews heavily toward construction from the 1970s through the early 1990s — an era when Las Vegas was expanding rapidly but before modern energy codes pushed builders toward tighter roof assemblies and impact-resistant materials. Flat and low-slope roofs were common on both residential and small commercial buildings of that period, and those roofs age poorly under the desert's UV load. By the time a monsoon-season microburst arrives, aging membrane seams and dried-out flashing are already compromised. A storm that would be a minor inconvenience on a newer home can mean full interior saturation on a 1980s build.

The desert soil beneath Sunrise Manor is also a factor most homeowners don't think about until water is already inside. Caliche — the hardpan calcium carbonate layer common throughout Clark County — sits close to the surface in many parts of this community. When monsoon rain falls faster than the caliche layer can absorb it, water sheets across yards and driveways and finds the lowest point of entry: garage door thresholds, sliding glass door tracks, and aging stucco cracks at the foundation line. That's a different water intrusion pattern than a roof leak, and it requires a different drying strategy.

## Our Storm Damage Restoration Process in Sunrise Manor

When we arrive at a Sunrise Manor property after a storm event, the first priority is a systematic exterior and interior assessment before any equipment goes in. Wind damage and water damage interact in ways that aren't always visible at first — a soffit breach that let wind-driven rain into the attic for 45 minutes can leave insulation saturated and roof decking wet while the ceiling below still looks fine. Thermal imaging helps locate those hidden moisture pockets before they have a chance to become a mold problem; in Nevada's summer heat, mold colonization in a wet attic space can begin within 24 to 48 hours.

Once the scope is documented, the process moves through debris removal, emergency tarping or board-up where the structure is still exposed, and then controlled drying using commercial-grade desiccant and refrigerant dehumidifiers calibrated for desert ambient conditions. Dry desert air sounds like an advantage, but it also means humidity gradients shift quickly — equipment settings that work in a coastal climate will over-dry surface materials here while leaving deep structural members still wet. Our IICRC ASD (Applied Structural Drying) certification covers exactly this kind of calibration.
