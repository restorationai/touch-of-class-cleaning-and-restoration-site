## Why Enterprise Properties See Repeated Storm Damage

The Las Vegas Valley's geography concentrates storm risk in ways that catch newer residents off guard. Enterprise, positioned at the valley's southwestern edge, receives drainage from a wide upstream watershed during monsoon events — typically July through September. Caliche soil, which underlies much of the area, is nearly impermeable when dry, meaning even a moderate rainfall event can produce rapid surface runoff that has nowhere to go except toward foundations, block walls, and low-lying slabs.

The housing stock here skews heavily toward construction from the late 1990s through the 2010s — stucco exteriors over wood-frame, flat or low-pitch tile roofs, and block perimeter walls. Stucco is durable in dry conditions but can wick moisture into the underlying sheathing when a storm drives rain at an angle or when hail chips the surface coating. Tile roofs shed water well when intact, but a single displaced tile during a high-wind event creates a direct path for water into the attic cavity. Block walls are sturdy but their footings can undermine if flash-flood water pools and saturates the soil around them repeatedly.

## Our Storm Damage Restoration Process in Enterprise

The first priority after any storm loss is stopping new damage from entering the structure. That means emergency tarping of compromised rooflines, boarding of broken windows or doors, and placing water barriers at threshold points before interior work begins. Once the structure is stabilized, the assessment phase starts — moisture mapping with thermal imaging and calibrated meters to locate water that has already migrated behind stucco, into wall cavities, or under flooring.

From there, the process follows a documented drying and repair sequence:

- **Water extraction and drying**: Standing water is extracted, then industrial air movers and desiccant or refrigerant dehumidifiers are deployed. Drying progress is logged daily against IICRC S500 standard benchmarks until affected materials reach acceptable moisture levels.
- **Debris and tree damage cleanup**: Wind-driven debris and downed vegetation are cleared and documented for insurance purposes before any structural repair begins.
- **Structural assessment and repair**: Damaged roofing materials, fascia, soffits, sheathing, and interior finishes are assessed. Repair scopes are documented with photos and line-item estimates compatible with major insurance carriers' estimating platforms.
- **Final inspection**: Moisture readings are verified before any new drywall, insulation, or stucco is closed in, preventing hidden mold conditions from developing after the job is complete.

Life Savers Restoration is an IICRC Certified Firm with technicians holding Water Restoration Technician (WRT) and Applied Structural Drying (ASD) credentials, and the team is EPA Lead-Safe Certified — relevant in any older Enterprise home where existing painted surfaces are disturbed during storm repair.
