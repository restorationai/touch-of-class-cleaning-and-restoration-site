## Restoration emergencies common in Jurupa Valley

Jurupa Valley sits in the western Inland Empire where the Santa Ana River corridor meets the foothills of the Jurupa Mountains. That geography shapes the plumbing and HVAC problems that show up here. Summer heat regularly pushes into the triple digits, which accelerates wear on water heater anode rods, stresses refrigerant lines, and causes PVC drain fittings to shift. The region also sees periodic heavy rain events, often after long dry stretches, that overwhelm drainage systems and push water into slabs, garages, and subfloor spaces faster than the soil can absorb it.

Much of the city's housing stock dates from the 1970s through the 1990s, a period when galvanized steel supply lines were still common in older builds and copper was the standard in newer ones. Galvanized pipe corrodes from the inside out, narrowing over decades until water pressure drops or a fitting fails entirely. Homes built in the 1980s may also carry polybutylene supply lines, a material known to become brittle and crack without warning. Add the expansive clay soils common across much of the western Inland Empire, which swell and shift with seasonal moisture changes, and slab leaks become a recurring issue rather than a rare one.
