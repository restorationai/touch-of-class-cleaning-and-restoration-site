## Restoration emergencies common in Ontario

Ontario sits in the eastern Inland Empire, where summer temperatures regularly push past 100°F and the Santa Ana winds arrive each fall dry and fast. That combination drives a specific set of plumbing and HVAC failures. Thermal expansion stresses older copper and galvanized supply lines during heat spikes. Evaporative coolers — still common in homes built before central air became standard — develop pan leaks and float-valve failures that quietly saturate ceilings and attic sheathing before a homeowner notices a stain. When the region does get rain, it tends to come hard and fast; flat or low-slope roofs on mid-century commercial buildings shed water faster than drains can carry it, and the dense clay soils in parts of the city slow percolation enough that water finds the path of least resistance — which is often a foundation wall or a garage slab joint. HVAC systems running year-round in this climate accumulate condensate drain clogs that, left uncleared, overflow into wall cavities and subfloor assemblies.

Most of Ontario's single-family residential neighborhoods were built between the 1950s and the 1990s, predominantly on slab-on-grade foundations — crawlspaces are uncommon here.
