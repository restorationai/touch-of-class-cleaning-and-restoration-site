## Restoration emergencies common in Chino

Chino sits in the western Inland Valley where summer temperatures routinely push past 100°F and the Santa Ana winds arrive each fall dry and fast. That combination does predictable damage: AC systems that run flat-out from May through September fail at the worst possible moment, water heaters that sat idle through a mild spring crack a fitting when demand spikes, and gas lines and fittings that expand and contract through wide daily temperature swings develop slow leaks that go unnoticed until a smell triggers a call.

Winter brings its own pattern. While hard freezes are rare in Chino proper, overnight lows in the upper 20s do occur — cold enough to split an exposed hose bib or freeze a supply line running through an uninsulated garage wall. The city's clay-heavy soils also shift seasonally as they wet and dry, which puts lateral sewer lines and underground supply lines under stress that accelerates at joints and couplings.

## Services we provide in Chino

### Plumbing

Chino's housing stock spans several decades, which means the plumbing materials vary widely by block. Galvanized steel supply lines in older homes corrode from the inside out, reducing flow to a trickle before they fail visibly. Copper systems from the 1970s and 1980s are generally reliable but show pinhole leaks where water chemistry has been aggressive. Slab leaks — where a supply or drain line fails beneath a concrete foundation — are a recurring issue across the city's large inventory of slab-on-grade construction. RT Olson handles leak detection, slab leak repair, drain clearing, water heater replacement, and full repiping.

### Heating

RT Olson services, repairs, and replaces gas furnaces and handles the gas line work that often accompanies an equipment swap.

## Building stock, site conditions, and permits in Chino

Chino's residential neighborhoods developed in several waves. Older blocks near the historic downtown core include homes built from the 1940s through the 1960s — wood-frame construction on slab foundations, with galvanized supply lines and cast-iron drain stacks that are now well past their design life. Chino's soils include expansive clay in many areas, which swells when wet and contracts when dry — a cycle that stresses underground lines and can crack slab foundations over time.
