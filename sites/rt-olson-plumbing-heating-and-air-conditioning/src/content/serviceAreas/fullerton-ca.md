## Restoration emergencies common in Fullerton

Fullerton sits in northern Orange County where the Santa Ana winds dry out the region for months at a time and then winter rain arrives fast and hard. That weather pattern — long dry spells followed by sudden heavy rainfall — puts real stress on plumbing and HVAC systems. Older homes baked through summer see caulk crack, pipe joints shift, and water heater anode rods corrode faster than the manufacturer's schedule suggests. When the rains come, flat or low-slope roofs common on postwar tract homes can push water toward foundations, and aging cast-iron or galvanized drain lines that have narrowed with decades of buildup can back up quickly.

Fullerton's housing stock skews heavily toward homes built between the late 1940s and the mid-1970s, which means a significant share of the city's properties still have original supply and drain materials that are well past their design life. Slab-on-grade construction is the norm in most of these neighborhoods, so a supply line failure under the floor isn't just a plumbing problem — it's a flooring, drywall, and potentially a mold-risk problem if water sits in the slab cavity. HVAC systems in these homes are often original or first-replacement units tucked into tight closets or attic spaces, and they work hard against Southern California's summer heat.

## Building stock, site conditions, and permits in Fullerton

Most of Fullerton's residential neighborhoods took shape between the late 1940s and early 1970s, producing a city dominated by single-story slab-on-grade homes with stucco exteriors and either flat or low-pitched roofs.
