## What tools do professional leak detectors actually use to find a hidden leak?

Professional leak detection relies on acoustic listening equipment, infrared thermal imaging, and occasionally tracer gas, all of which locate a leak within inches without opening walls or flooring first.

Acoustic equipment uses a sensitive microphone pressed to a floor, wall, or exposed pipe to pick up the distinct hissing or ticking sound of pressurized water escaping a line. A technician moves the listening disc across a grid pattern, and the sound peaks right over the leak. This is the primary method for slab leaks, since it doesn't require guessing where the pipe runs under the concrete.

Thermal imaging cameras read surface temperature differences. A hot water line leak shows up as a warm bloom on a cooler wall or floor; a cold line leak can show the opposite. It's especially useful for confirming what the acoustic equipment already suggested, or for scanning a larger area quickly before narrowing in with sound.

Tracer gas detection is reserved for trickier cases, usually when the leak is small or the pipe is in a location that muffles sound (deep under a slab, behind tile, inside a wall cavity with insulation). A technician pressurizes the empty line with a mix of nitrogen and a small percentage of hydrogen, then uses a handheld sensor to sniff out where the gas escapes through the slab or wall surface. ## How much does professional leak detection cost in Corona?

Leak detection in Corona typically runs $150 to $500 depending on the method used and how accessible the suspected leak area is, separate from whatever repair follows.

A straightforward acoustic scan on an accessible slab or wall cavity sits at the lower end. Add thermal imaging confirmation, or a larger search area with multiple suspect zones, and the cost climbs. Tracer gas detection, because it requires isolating and pressurizing a section of pipe, tends to be the most expensive detection method on its own, often $300 to $600.

The repair cost is a separate number entirely. A single slab leak repair with a localized access cut and reroute can run $1,500 to $4,000 depending on depth and flooring material. Whole-home repiping, when a slab has had repeat leak history on aging copper, is a bigger conversation but solves the problem permanently rather than chasing the next pinhole leak. Either way, insist on a written quote that separates the detection fee from the repair estimate, and ask whether the detection fee is credited toward the repair if you move forward with the same company. According to [EPA WaterSense](https://www.epa.gov/watersense), the average household's leaks waste nearly 10,000 gallons of water a year, which is a useful number to keep in mind if a leak has been running undetected for months.

## What should you do if you suspect a leak right now?

If you can hear running water with everything off, see a damp patch with no obvious source, or your water meter is spinning with no fixtures in use, shut off the main water valve and call a licensed company for diagnostic equipment, not a guess.

Finding the home's main shutoff ahead of time, usually near the street-facing side of the house or in the garage, means you're not scrambling during an active leak. If the leak is minor and the water bill spike is your main clue, you don't necessarily need to shut off the whole house, but it's worth getting a scan scheduled before a slow leak becomes a bigger repair.
