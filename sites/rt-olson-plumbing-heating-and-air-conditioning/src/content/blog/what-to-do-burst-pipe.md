If a pipe has just burst in your home, here's what matters right now: **shut off the water main, cut power to flooded areas, and get the water moving out before it moves into your walls and floors.** Every minute of standing water is another minute of absorption into drywall, subfloor, and insulation. The steps below are in order — follow them sequentially, and you'll limit the damage significantly before any professional arrives.

---

## Step-by-Step: The First 10 Minutes After a Pipe Bursts

### 1. Kill the water supply (do this first, before anything else)

Your main shutoff valve is almost always in one of three places in a Southern California home: near the front of the house by the meter box at the curb, in a utility closet, or along an exterior wall in the garage. In Corona and much of Riverside County, the street-side meter box is the most reliable falloff if you can't find the interior valve — you'll need a flathead screwdriver or a meter key to turn it.

Turn the valve clockwise until it stops. If it hasn't been touched in years, it may be stiff. Apply steady pressure rather than forcing it — older gate valves can break if you torque them hard.

Once the main is off, open a faucet on the lowest floor of the house. This bleeds pressure from the lines and slows the water still draining out of the burst section.

### 2. Cut the electricity to any affected rooms

Go to your breaker panel and switch off the circuits for any room where water is present or directly above. Water and live outlets are the most immediate physical danger in a burst-pipe situation — more urgent, in the first minutes, than the water damage itself.

If you're not sure which breakers control which rooms, and the panel isn't clearly labeled, shut off the main breaker. You can work in the dark with a flashlight; you cannot safely work in a room with standing water and live circuits.

### 3. Document before you touch anything

Before you grab towels or move furniture, spend 60 seconds photographing everything with your phone. Shoot the source of the burst if you can see it, the standing water, the ceiling stain or wall bubble, and any damaged belongings. This documentation matters enormously for an insurance claim. Adjusters work from evidence, and water damage can look deceptively minor once things dry out.

### 4. Start moving water out

Now you can act on the water itself:

- Use a wet/dry shop vac if you have one — it's far more effective than towels for standing water on hard floors.
- Layer old towels or blankets at doorways to stop water from migrating to adjacent rooms.
- If water is pooling on an upper floor, check the ceiling below for bulging drywall. A ceiling that's sagging and wet is holding hundreds of pounds of water — puncture it with a screwdriver in a controlled spot to let it drain into a bucket rather than collapse.
- Open windows if the outdoor humidity is lower than inside (in Corona's dry-summer climate, this is usually a safe bet from late spring through early fall).

### 5. Locate and assess the burst if you can do so safely

Common burst-pipe locations in Inland Southern California homes:

- **Under slab:** You won't see the pipe, but you'll notice warm or wet spots on the floor, a sudden spike in your water bill, or the sound of running water when everything is off. Slab leaks are a separate repair category entirely.
- **In the wall:** Look for a bubbling or soft section of drywall, discoloration, or peeling paint. The pipe behind it may have burst at a joint or a corroded section.
- **Under a sink or in a crawl space:** These are the most accessible and easiest to confirm visually.
- **At an irrigation or hose bib connection:** Especially relevant after a cold snap — Corona does see occasional freezing nights in December and January, and uninsulated hose bibs are a common failure point.

Knowing the approximate location helps the plumber you call arrive with the right materials.

## What NOT to Do

- **Don't use electrical appliances in or near the wet area** — hair dryers, fans plugged into wet-room outlets, anything with a cord running through standing water.
- **Don't assume the damage is contained to what you can see.** Water follows the path of least resistance, which often means it's already inside wall cavities or under flooring before it appears at the surface. What looks like a small ceiling stain can represent significant saturation behind it.
- **Don't use your HVAC system to try to dry the space.** Circulating air through a wet structure before the moisture source is controlled can push humid air into other parts of the house and accelerate mold conditions.
- **Don't pour bleach on wet drywall or framing** in an attempt to prevent mold. It doesn't penetrate porous materials effectively and can complicate remediation later.
- **Don't ignore it and wait until morning** if it happened overnight. The 24-to-48-hour window is when mold colonization begins in wet cellulose materials — drywall paper, wood framing, insulation. The faster the structure dries, the less likely you are to face a mold problem on top of the water damage.

Some burst-pipe situations you can stabilize yourself and schedule a repair during normal hours. Others need a plumber on-site fast:

- The burst is at or near the water heater, a gas line, or the main service entry.
- You suspect a slab leak (running-water sounds with everything off, warm floor, unexplained water pooling).
- The volume of water is beyond what you can manage with household tools.

## The Recovery Process After the Immediate Crisis

Once the water is off and the immediate danger is handled, you're entering a different phase — one that typically takes days, not hours.

Structural drying is the priority. Drywall, wood framing, and subfloor materials need to reach safe moisture levels before any repair or reconstruction begins. If you skip this step and patch over wet materials, you're sealing in the conditions mold needs to grow.

Your insurance company will likely send an adjuster to assess the damage. The photos you took in step 3 become your baseline. Keep receipts for anything you spend in the immediate response — shop vac rental, towels, fans — as these are often reimbursable as mitigation costs.

If a pipe has just burst in your home, do these four things right now: **shut off the main water supply, cut power to affected rooms, open a cold tap to drain pressure, and call a licensed plumber.** Everything else — the cleanup, the insurance call, the drywall repair — comes after those four steps. The next ten minutes will determine how much water soaks into your framing, insulation, and flooring, so move fast but move in order.

## Step-by-Step: The First 10 Minutes

Think of this as a countdown. Every minute of active flow adds gallons to the damage.

**1. Find and close the main shutoff valve (minutes 1–2)**

In most Southern California homes, the main shutoff is one of two places: at the water meter near the street (usually under a rectangular concrete lid in the parkway), or on the exterior wall of the house near the front hose bib. Turn it clockwise until it stops. If you have a ball valve — a lever handle rather than a round wheel — rotate it 90 degrees so it sits perpendicular to the pipe. Water should stop flowing within seconds.

If you have never located this valve, do it today, before anything goes wrong. Tape a note to your water heater with the location.

**2. Switch off electricity to wet areas (minute 2–3)**

Water and live circuits are a serious hazard. Go to your breaker panel and cut power to any room where water is actively flowing or pooling. If the panel itself is in a wet area, do not touch it — call 911 and stay out of the space.

**3. Open a cold faucet on the lowest floor (minute 3–4)**

With the main valve closed, there is still pressurized water sitting in the pipes. Opening a faucet — a bathtub tap or a utility sink works well — lets that remaining water drain down and out rather than continuing to seep through the break.

**4. Document everything before you touch it (minutes 4–6)**

Walk the affected area with your phone and shoot video. Pan slowly across the ceiling stain, the wet floor, the visible pipe. This footage is your insurance claim. Adjusters work from evidence, and a 90-second video taken before cleanup is worth more than photographs taken after you've moved furniture.

**5. Move what you can, fast (minutes 6–10)**

Rugs, upholstered furniture, electronics, and wood furniture absorb water quickly and can be total losses if left sitting. Lift rugs off wet floors entirely — a wet rug traps moisture against hardwood or subfloor and accelerates mold growth. Stack furniture on aluminum foil squares or plastic bags if you cannot move it out of the room.

## What NOT to Do

In a burst-pipe emergency, a few instincts will make things worse:

- **Don't use a shop vac or household vacuum on standing water if the power is still on in that room.** Cut the breaker first.
- **Don't assume the wet area is the only wet area.** Water follows framing cavities and can travel six feet horizontally inside a wall before it shows up as a stain. Press your palm along the drywall above and around the visible damage — if it feels cool and slightly soft, water is behind it.
- **Don't use a hair dryer or space heater to dry things out yourself.** Surface drying without addressing moisture inside the wall cavity creates the exact conditions mold needs: warmth and trapped humidity.
- **Don't pour bleach on wet drywall.** It does not prevent mold growth inside a water-saturated wall cavity, and it can mask odors that would otherwise alert you to a hidden moisture problem.
- **Don't wait to call your insurance company.** Most homeowner policies have a reporting window, and delayed notification can complicate your claim.

## How to Recognize Hidden Water Damage

Not every burst pipe announces itself with a waterfall. A pinhole failure in a supply line inside a wall may run for hours before you notice anything. Here is what to look for:

- **Ceiling bubbling or sagging:** Paint that blisters or drywall that looks pregnant with water means there is standing water above it. Do not stand under it.
- **Warm or soft spots on the floor:** In a slab-foundation home — common in Corona and the Inland Empire — a warm patch on a tile floor with no other explanation can indicate a hot-water line break beneath the concrete. That is a slab leak, and it requires specialized detection equipment.
- **A sudden spike in your water meter:** If you suspect a hidden leak, turn off every fixture in the house and watch the meter dial for two minutes. If it moves, water is going somewhere it should not.
- **Musty smell without visible mold:** Mold can colonize drywall paper within 24 to 48 hours of saturation. A smell without a stain usually means the damage is inside the wall.

## When to Call a Plumber (and What to Tell Them)

Call a licensed plumber as soon as the immediate hazards are controlled — meaning the water is off, the power is safe, and you have documented the scene. Do not wait until morning if water is still moving or if you cannot locate the shutoff.

When you call, tell them:

- Where in the house the break appears to be (kitchen, bathroom, exterior wall, under slab)
- Whether you have shut off the main supply
- Whether the water was hot or cold (this helps identify which line failed)
- How long the leak was active before you noticed it

That information lets a plumber arrive with the right materials and equipment rather than diagnosing blind. Burst-pipe repair in a finished wall is different work than a failed hose bib on an exterior wall — the repair scope, the access method, and the drying timeline all differ.

## The Recovery Process After the Repair

Fixing the pipe is the beginning, not the end. Here is what follows:

1. **Moisture mapping.** A plumber or water damage professional uses a moisture meter to find the full boundary of saturation — not just what looks wet, but what reads wet. In older stucco construction common across the Inland Empire, moisture can wick into the lath layer and stay there long after the surface feels dry.
2. **Drying.** Industrial air movers and dehumidifiers pull moisture out of structural materials. This typically takes several days, not hours, and the equipment needs to stay in place until readings confirm drywall and framing are back to acceptable moisture levels.
3. **Mold assessment.** If water was present for more than 24–48 hours before drying began, a mold assessment is worth the cost before you close the wall back up.
4. **Reconstruction.** Once materials are confirmed dry, drywall, insulation, flooring, and paint can be restored. This is usually coordinated with your insurance adjuster.
