## What Is a C-36 Plumbing Contractor License?

A C-36 is a specialty contractor license issued by the California Contractors State License Board that authorizes a contractor to install, alter, and repair plumbing systems in residential and commercial buildings. It covers water supply lines, drain and waste systems, gas piping, water heaters, and related fixtures. Without a C-36 (or a General Building contractor license that includes plumbing), a contractor cannot legally perform plumbing work valued over $500 in California.

The C-36 is not a journeyman plumber card. It is a business license for the contracting entity. To qualify, the applicant must pass a trade exam and a law-and-business exam, document four years of journey-level experience, and carry a $25,000 contractor's bond. The CSLB also requires workers' compensation insurance if the contractor has employees. That combination of testing, experience, and financial accountability is what separates a licensed contractor from someone who learned the trade informally.

California's C-36 does not automatically include HVAC work. A heating and air conditioning contractor holds a separate C-20 license. If a company offers both plumbing and HVAC, look for both license classifications, or a B General Building contractor license that covers both trades.

## How to Verify a Plumber's License in 60 Seconds

Go to the [CSLB license check page](https://www.cslb.ca.gov/OnlineServices/CheckLicenseII/CheckLicense.aspx) and enter the contractor's license number or business name. The result shows the license classification, current status (Active, Inactive, Suspended, or Expired), expiration date, bond information, and any workers' comp filings.

Here is the step-by-step:

1. Open [cslb.ca.gov/OnlineServices/CheckLicenseII/CheckLicense.aspx](https://www.cslb.ca.gov/OnlineServices/CheckLicenseII/CheckLicense.aspx) on your phone or computer.
2. Enter the license number the contractor gave you, or search by business name.
3. Confirm the status reads **Active**. Expired or Suspended means the contractor cannot legally work.
4. Check the **classifications** listed. You want to see C-36 for plumbing work.
5. Confirm the **bond** is in place. No bond means no financial backstop if the contractor walks off the job.
6. Check for any **disciplinary actions** listed at the bottom of the result.

The whole process takes under a minute. If a contractor hesitates when you ask for their license number, that hesitation is your answer.

## Which Plumbing Jobs Require a Permit?

A license and a permit are two different things. A license authorizes the contractor to do the work. A permit authorizes the specific job at a specific address, and it triggers an inspection by the local building department.

In Riverside County and the cities it encompasses, the following plumbing jobs typically require a permit:

- **Water heater replacement**: Any water heater swap, tank or tankless, requires a permit. The inspector checks the T&P relief valve, the discharge line routing, seismic straps, and gas connections. Skipping the permit means an uninspected gas connection and a potential insurance issue if the unit ever floods or fails.
- **Whole-house repipe**: Replacing copper or galvanized pipe with PEX or new copper is a significant structural alteration. A permit is required, and inspections happen at rough-in (before walls close) and final.
- **Gas line work**: New gas lines, extended runs, and pressure tests all require permits. Gas work also requires the contractor to perform a pressure test witnessed by the inspector.
- **Sewer line replacement or relining**: Any work on the building sewer from the house to the property line requires a permit. Trenchless relining is included.
- **New fixture rough-ins**: Adding a bathroom, relocating a sink, or adding a laundry connection requires a permit because it changes the drain-waste-vent system.

Minor repairs, like replacing a faucet, swapping a toilet, or clearing a clogged drain, generally do not require a permit. When in doubt, ask your contractor. A licensed contractor will know the local requirements and pull the permit on your behalf. The permit fee is typically included in a reputable contractor's quote. If a contractor tells you a permit is unnecessary for a water heater swap or a repipe, that is a red flag.

For more detail on what the CSLB expects from licensed contractors and what homeowners can do to protect themselves, see the [CSLB's Hire a Contractor guide](https://www.cslb.ca.gov/Consumers/Hire_A_Contractor/).

## What Are the Risks of Hiring an Unlicensed Plumber?

Hiring an unlicensed plumber exposes you to several concrete risks beyond just shoddy work.

**No bond protection.** A licensed contractor's $25,000 CSLB bond exists to compensate you if the contractor abandons the job or causes damage. An unlicensed contractor has no bond. If they disappear after taking a deposit, your recourse is small claims court, not the CSLB.

**No workers' comp coverage.** If an unlicensed worker is injured on your property, you may be liable for their medical bills and lost wages. Licensed contractors with employees are required to carry workers' compensation insurance. That coverage protects them and protects you.

**Permit problems.** Unpermitted work can surface when you sell the house. A buyer's inspector or the title company may flag it, and you may be required to open walls, bring the work up to code, and pay for the inspection retroactively. Unpermitted gas or water heater work can also void your homeowner's insurance coverage for related claims.

**Code violations.** The California Plumbing Code (based on the Uniform Plumbing Code, or UPC) sets minimum standards for pipe sizing, venting, water pressure, and fixture installation. A licensed contractor knows these standards and is accountable to them through the permit process. An unlicensed one is not.

## Does a Plumber Also Need to Be Bonded and Insured?

Yes, and the CSLB lookup shows you both. A licensed California plumbing contractor must carry a $25,000 contractor's license bond, which the CSLB verifies and displays in the license record. Contractors with employees must also carry workers' compensation insurance.

General liability insurance is separate from the bond and is not tracked by the CSLB. Ask the contractor for a certificate of insurance showing general liability coverage. A reputable contractor provides this without hesitation. The certificate names the insurance company, policy number, coverage limits, and expiration date. For a water heater installation or a repipe, you want to see at least $1 million in general liability coverage.

## What To Do Before Scheduling Any Major Plumbing Job

Before you book a water heater installation, a repipe, or any gas line work, take three minutes to do the following:

1. Ask for the contractor's CSLB license number before they arrive.
2. Look it up at cslb.ca.gov. Confirm Active status, C-36 classification, and bond.
3. Ask for a certificate of general liability insurance.
4. Confirm the contractor will pull the permit. If they say the job doesn't need one and you're not sure, call your city's building department to verify.
5. Get a written scope of work and price before any work starts. The written quote should list materials, labor, permit fees, and the estimated inspection timeline.
