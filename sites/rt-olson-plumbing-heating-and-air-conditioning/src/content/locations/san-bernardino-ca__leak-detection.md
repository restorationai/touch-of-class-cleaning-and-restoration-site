## Why San Bernardino Properties See More Hidden Leaks

The Inland Empire's climate puts plumbing through a punishing cycle. Summer temperatures routinely push past 105°F, causing supply lines and underground laterals to expand significantly during the day and contract overnight. Over years, that movement fatigues solder joints and compression fittings in ways that don't show up as an obvious drip, just a slow seep behind drywall or beneath a concrete slab.

San Bernardino's water supply also carries high mineral content. Hard water accelerates scale buildup inside pipes, narrowing the interior diameter and raising line pressure. That elevated pressure quietly stresses the weakest points in a plumbing system, often the older galvanized sections found in mid-century homes throughout the city's established residential corridors. When a pinhole finally forms in galvanized pipe, it can run for months before the ceiling or subfloor shows any visible staining.

Slab construction is common across San Bernardino, which means a significant share of supply and drain lines run directly through or beneath the concrete foundation. A slab leak won't puddle on the floor the way a supply line failure under a sink will. Instead, homeowners notice a warm patch underfoot, a floor that feels soft or damp through tile grout, or a water meter that keeps spinning after every fixture in the house is shut off.

## Our Leak Detection Process in San Bernardino

RT Olson technicians start every leak detection call with a meter test, shutting off all fixtures and watching the meter dial for movement. If the meter moves, the leak is confirmed on the supply side. From there, the process depends on where the evidence points.

For suspected slab leaks, we use electronic amplification equipment to listen through the concrete for the distinct frequency of pressurized water escaping a pipe. Acoustic leak detection isolates the loudest signal point, which gives us a precise target before any concrete is touched, minimizing the size of any access opening needed for repair.

For leaks behind walls or above ceilings, thermal imaging cameras detect temperature differentials that indicate moisture migration. Water moving through a wall cavity is measurably cooler than the surrounding dry material, and the camera maps that difference in real time. We combine thermal imaging with a moisture meter to confirm saturation depth before recommending any opening of finished surfaces.

For underground service lines between the meter and the house, a common failure point in San Bernardino's older neighborhoods, we use tracer gas or line pressurization to isolate the leak location along the buried run, so excavation is targeted rather than exploratory.

## Equipment & Methods We Use for Leak Detection

The right tool depends on the construction type and where the leak signal is strongest. Our van-stocked equipment typically includes:

- **Acoustic ground microphones**, amplify the sound of water escaping pressurized pipe through soil or concrete
- **Thermal imaging cameras**, map moisture migration behind finished surfaces without destructive opening
- **Electronic line locators**, trace buried pipe runs so we know exactly where to dig
- **Digital moisture meters**, confirm saturation levels in drywall, subfloor, and concrete before and after detection
- **Tracer gas equipment**, for underground leaks where acoustic signal is diffuse

Using multiple methods in sequence reduces false positives and keeps any necessary repair opening as small as possible.
