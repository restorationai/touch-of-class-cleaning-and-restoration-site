## Why San Jacinto Properties See Emergency Plumbing Problems

The clay-heavy soils throughout the San Jacinto Valley expand when the rains come and contract hard during the long dry summers. That seasonal movement puts constant lateral stress on underground supply lines and sewer laterals, especially on properties built before modern flexible-joint standards became common. Homes from the late 1980s and early 1990s, a significant portion of San Jacinto's residential stock, were often plumbed with polybutylene pipe, a material that degrades from the inside out and fails without much warning. The first sign is sometimes a slow drip behind drywall; the second is a ceiling collapse.

Hard water is another persistent factor. The Inland Empire's water supply carries high mineral content, and San Jacinto is no exception. Calcium and magnesium build up inside water heater tanks, pressure regulators, and supply valves over time, accelerating failure. A water heater that rumbles and pops when it fires up is telling you the sediment layer has gotten thick enough to overheat the tank floor, and a tank that overheats eventually cracks or blows a relief valve.
