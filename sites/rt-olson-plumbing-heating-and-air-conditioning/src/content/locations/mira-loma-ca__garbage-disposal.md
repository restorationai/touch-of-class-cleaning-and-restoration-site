## Why Mira Loma Properties See More Disposal Problems

Mira Loma's housing stock is a mix of older ranch-style homes built in the 1960s and 1970s and newer construction that went up during the development booms along the I-15 and SR-60 corridors. The older homes frequently have 3.5-inch drain lines under the sink rather than the 4-inch lines common in newer builds, a small difference that matters a lot when a disposal is grinding fibrous vegetable scraps or citrus rinds. A unit that would drain fine in a newer home can back up repeatedly in one of those original ranch kitchens because the narrower line simply can't move the slurry fast enough.

Hard water is the other persistent factor. The Inland Empire's water supply carries high mineral content, and calcium deposits gradually coat the inside of disposal chambers and drain connections. That buildup tightens clearances, makes splash guards stiff and brittle, and can cause a unit that was working fine to suddenly seize. If your disposal is humming but the plate won't turn, mineral-locked bearings are often the culprit, not a failed motor.

## Our Repair and Installation Process in Mira Loma

Every service call starts with a diagnosis, not a sales pitch. When a disposal is humming, the first step is a manual reset and a check of the flywheel with an Allen wrench, many jammed units free up in under five minutes without any parts. When the problem is a leak, the location tells the story: a leak at the sink flange usually means a failed putty seal, while a leak from the side port points to a cracked dishwasher drain connection. Bottom leaks typically mean the internal seal has failed and replacement is the right call.

For installations, we match the motor size to the household's actual usage and the drain line's capacity. A 1/2 HP unit is adequate for a single-person household with a wide drain line; a family kitchen with a narrower vintage drain benefits from a 3/4 HP or 1 HP unit that grinds finer and moves waste faster. We handle the full swap, disconnecting the old unit, cleaning the mounting flange, setting the new unit, reconnecting the dishwasher drain if present, and testing under load before we leave.
