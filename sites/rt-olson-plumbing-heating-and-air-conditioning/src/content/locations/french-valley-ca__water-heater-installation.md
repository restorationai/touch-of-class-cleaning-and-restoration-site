## Why French Valley Properties See Water Heater Problems

The Inland Empire's hard water is one of the most consistent factors shortening water heater life in this part of Riverside County. French Valley draws from water sources that carry elevated mineral content, and that calcium and magnesium settle as sediment inside tank units over time. You'll often hear it before you see it, a low rumbling or popping sound when the burner fires, which is the heating element working against a hardened crust on the tank floor. Left alone, sediment buildup reduces efficiency, drives up gas or electric bills, and accelerates corrosion that eventually causes a leak.

The area's newer residential developments, many built in the 2000s and 2010s on the expanding edge of the Temecula Valley, typically have 50-gallon natural gas tank water heaters installed as standard equipment. Those units are now reaching or approaching the end of their 10-to-12-year service window, which means a wave of replacements is underway across French Valley's residential streets. If your unit is more than a decade old and you're noticing lukewarm water, rusty discharge, or moisture around the base, it's worth having it assessed before it fails completely.

## Tank vs. Tankless: Choosing the Right System for French Valley Homes

The decision between a traditional tank water heater and a tankless (on-demand) unit comes down to household size, available space, and how you use hot water. In French Valley's newer tract homes, where garage space is often at a premium and families run multiple showers, dishwashers, and laundry cycles simultaneously, a tankless water heater can eliminate the "cold water sandwich" problem and reduce standby energy loss. Tankless units also handle the hard-water environment better when paired with a whole-home water softener, something worth discussing if your current system shows heavy scale.

That said, a 50-gallon tank replacement is often the faster and more cost-effective path for a household with predictable demand and an existing gas line already sized for a standard unit. RT Olson will assess your current setup, gas pressure, venting configuration, available clearances, before recommending a direction. The goal is the right system for your home, not the most expensive one.

## Our Water Heater Installation Process in French Valley

Every installation starts with a site evaluation: we check the existing gas supply line, flue or venting path, seismic strapping requirements (California requires water heaters to be strapped in two places to resist earthquake movement), and the condition of the shutoff valve and supply lines. If any of those components need upgrading to bring the installation up to current code, we'll walk you through what's required before work begins.

For tankless installations, we assess whether the existing gas meter and supply line can support the higher BTU draw of a condensing tankless unit, this is a step that gets skipped by less thorough installers and can result in inadequate hot water flow at the fixture. Once the new unit is in place, we test water temperature, verify proper venting, and confirm the pressure relief valve is functioning correctly before we consider the job complete.
