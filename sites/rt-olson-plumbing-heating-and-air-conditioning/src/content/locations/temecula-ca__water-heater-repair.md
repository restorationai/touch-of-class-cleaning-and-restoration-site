## Why Temecula Properties See Water Heater Problems

Temecula sits in a region where the water supply carries a notably high mineral content. Hard water accelerates sediment buildup inside tank-style water heaters, that low rumbling or popping sound you hear when the burner fires is often calcium and magnesium deposits baking onto the heating element or the tank floor. Left alone, sediment insulates the burner from the water, driving up energy use and shortening the unit's life. It also traps heat at the bottom of the tank, which can cause the glass lining to crack and lead to a slow, corrosive leak.

The temperature swings between Temecula's scorching summers and its cooler winter nights, temperatures can dip into the 30s in the valley, also affect the pressure relief valve and the expansion tank on closed plumbing systems. Thermal expansion cycles stress fittings and connections that might hold fine in a more temperate coastal climate. Homes in newer master-planned communities often have tankless units that require periodic descaling to handle the local water chemistry; older tract homes may still be running original tank heaters that are well past their expected service life.
