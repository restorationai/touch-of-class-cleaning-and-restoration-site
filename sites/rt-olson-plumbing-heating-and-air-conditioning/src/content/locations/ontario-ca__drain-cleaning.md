When summer temperatures push past 100°F and the soil contracts, sewer laterals shift — and when the first heavy rain arrives, roots that have been probing cracked joints all season suddenly push through. ## Why Ontario Properties See Drain Problems

The Inland Empire's clay-heavy soils expand and contract dramatically between wet winters and dry summers. That movement stresses underground drain lines — especially in older commercial corridors and residential tracts built in the 1970s and 1980s when ABS plastic pipe was the standard. Over time, joint gaps open just wide enough for tree roots from nearby eucalyptus and ficus plantings to find their way in. Once roots establish inside a four-inch sewer line, they catch grease, soap scum, and food debris like a net. What starts as a slow kitchen sink drain becomes a full backup.

Hard water is the other Ontario-specific factor. The region's water supply carries elevated mineral content, and calcium and magnesium deposits build up inside drain walls the same way they coat water heater elements. In commercial kitchens — particularly in the dense restaurant and warehouse district near the 60 and 15 interchange — that scale combines with cooking grease to create blockages that a standard drain snake barely dents.

## Our Drain Cleaning Process in Ontario

Every job starts with a drain assessment, not a guess. For recurring or severe clogs, we run a camera through the line to locate the blockage and identify what's causing it — root intrusion, grease accumulation, scale buildup, or a belly in the pipe from soil settlement. That footage also tells us whether the line can handle hydro jetting or needs a gentler approach first.

For most residential kitchen sink clogs and shower drain clogs, mechanical rooter service clears the immediate blockage quickly. For lines with significant root intrusion or heavy grease buildup — common in older commercial properties and multi-unit buildings — high-pressure hydro jetting is the more thorough solution. A hydro jet scours the pipe wall rather than just punching a hole through the clog, which means the drain stays clear longer. After jetting, we do a second camera pass to confirm the line is fully open before we leave.

## Local Note

One pattern we see consistently in Ontario's older residential tracts — particularly in neighborhoods built out during the 1970s and 1980s — is that the original sewer laterals were installed with minimal slope, right at the code-minimum grade. Decades of soil movement in this region's expansive clay can flatten that slope further or create a low spot, called a belly, where solids settle instead of flowing to the main. A drain snake will clear a belly temporarily, but the debris will collect again within weeks. A camera inspection is the only way to catch this before a homeowner has cleared the same drain three times in a single year.
