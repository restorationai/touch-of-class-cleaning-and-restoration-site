## Why Bloomington Properties See Drain Problems More Often Than You'd Expect

The geology under Bloomington is a significant factor that rarely comes up until something backs up. San Bernardino County's inland valley sits on alluvial soils with a high clay content. Clay shrinks during dry spells and swells after rain, and even a modest winter storm can shift that soil enough to nudge an older drain line slightly out of alignment. A misaligned joint doesn't fail overnight; it collects debris, narrows the flow path, and eventually produces the slow drain or full blockage that sends homeowners searching for rooter service.

Bloomington's water supply also carries elevated hardness levels typical of the region. Minerals deposit on pipe walls over time, narrowing the interior diameter and giving grease and soap scum a rough surface to cling to. Kitchen sink clogs here often aren't just grease, they're grease bonded to scale, which makes a simple plunger far less effective than a professional cable or hydro jetting service.

Older residential construction in the area adds another layer. Homes built in the 1960s and 1970s frequently used cast iron or Orangeburg drain lines that are now at or past their service life. These materials corrode from the inside, shed debris into the flow, and can collapse under root pressure from the large shade trees common on residential lots throughout Bloomington.

## Our Drain Cleaning Process in Bloomington

Every service call starts with a drain assessment, not just a plunger and a hope. We run a cable inspection or deploy a small camera into the line to identify what's actually causing the restriction before we choose a method. That step saves time and prevents the frustration of clearing a clog only to have it return because the root cause was never addressed.

For standard kitchen sink clogs and shower drain clogs, a motorized cable auger typically breaks through and removes the obstruction in a single visit. For lines with heavier scale buildup, root intrusion, or recurring blockages, hydro jetting is the more effective solution. A hydro jet pushes water at high pressure through the entire pipe diameter, stripping grease, mineral scale, and fine root hairs from the pipe wall rather than simply punching a hole through the blockage. The result is a line that flows the way it was designed to, not just well enough to get by.

After the line is cleared, we flush and re-inspect. If the camera reveals a structural issue, a cracked section, a belly in the line, or a collapsed segment, we document it and walk you through your options before any additional work begins.
