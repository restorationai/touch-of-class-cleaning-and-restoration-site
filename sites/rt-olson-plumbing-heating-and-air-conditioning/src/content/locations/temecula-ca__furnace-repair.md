## Why Temecula Homes See Furnace Problems in the First Place

The Temecula Valley's climate is genuinely unusual for Southern California. Elevation plays a role, the city sits roughly 1,000 feet above sea level, and cold air drains into the valley floor on clear nights, pushing temperatures well below what coastal communities see. That means furnaces here work harder during cold snaps than homeowners expect, and systems that are marginally maintained get pushed past their limits quickly.

Temecula's housing stock adds another layer. Large portions of the city were built during the 1990s and 2000s master-planned boom, and many of those homes have original furnaces that are now approaching or past the 20-year mark. Builders in that era commonly installed builder-grade equipment sized to minimum code, units that were adequate when new but are now showing their age in the form of worn inducer motors, degraded heat exchangers, and ignition systems that misfire on cold starts. Homes in the newer developments on the city's eastern and western edges tend to have younger equipment, but the trade-off is tighter duct systems that amplify airflow problems when a blower motor starts to weaken.

Duct leakage is also a persistent issue in the region. The clay-heavy soil under much of Temecula shifts seasonally, and that movement can stress duct connections in crawlspaces and attic runs over time, allowing conditioned air to escape before it reaches living spaces, which often gets misread as a furnace problem when the real issue is delivery.

## Our Furnace Repair Process in Temecula

When a technician arrives at your home, the first step is a systematic diagnostic rather than a parts swap. That means checking the thermostat signal, verifying gas pressure at the valve, inspecting the igniter and flame sensor, and running the inducer motor through a full startup cycle while monitoring for fault codes. Modern furnaces store error codes in their control boards, reading those codes takes minutes and often points directly to the failed component.

If the heat exchanger is involved, we inspect it carefully before proceeding. A cracked heat exchanger is a carbon monoxide pathway, and the right call in that situation is a clear explanation of your options, repair, replacement, or temporary shutdown, not a rushed fix. We carry common repair parts on our service vehicles so that straightforward repairs like igniter replacements, flame sensor cleanings, and capacitor swaps can typically be completed in a single visit.
