## Why Ontario Properties See More Garbage Disposal Problems

The Inland Empire's water supply consistently ranks among the harder municipal water sources in Southern California. Over time, mineral deposits coat the impellers and splash guard inside a disposal, reducing grinding efficiency and putting extra load on the motor. That's often the real reason a unit starts humming without spinning — the motor is trying, but the grinding plate is locked by scale or a lodged piece of debris.

Ontario's climate adds a second layer. Summer temperatures regularly push past 100°F, and that heat radiates through cabinet doors into the under-sink space. Disposal motors running in an already-warm environment cycle through heat stress more quickly than units in milder climates, which can accelerate seal wear and lead to the slow drip that shows up as a leak at the unit's base. If you've noticed a puddle under your sink that seems to appear only after heavy use, heat-fatigued seals are a common culprit here.

Ontario also has a substantial stock of tract homes built during the 1970s and 1980s construction boom that followed the city's incorporation and industrial growth. Many of those kitchens were plumbed with 1.5-inch drain lines and older P-trap configurations that weren't designed with today's higher-horsepower disposals in mind. Installing a modern ¾ HP or 1 HP unit in one of those homes without checking the drain line diameter and venting first can create a slow-drain problem that looks like a disposal issue but is actually a drain compatibility issue.

## Our Garbage Disposal Repair and Installation Process in Ontario

Every visit starts with a diagnosis, not a parts order. When a disposal is humming but not spinning, the fix is often as simple as using the reset button and a hex wrench to free a jammed grinding plate — no replacement needed. When the motor is genuinely burned out, or when the unit is leaking from the mounting ring rather than a supply connection, replacement is the cleaner call.

For installations, the process includes:

- **Drain line compatibility check** — confirming the existing 1.5-inch or 2-inch drain can handle the new unit's output without backing up into the dishwasher drain connection
- **Electrical verification** — Ontario homes from the 1970s–80s sometimes have the disposal wired to a shared circuit; we confirm the outlet under the sink is on a dedicated or appropriately rated circuit before installing a higher-draw motor
- **Mounting and seal inspection** — the sink flange and mounting assembly are replaced or reseated, not just reused, to prevent the slow leak-at-the-base that shows up weeks after a rushed install
- **Test run and leak check** — we run water and grind material before closing the cabinet, checking every connection point
