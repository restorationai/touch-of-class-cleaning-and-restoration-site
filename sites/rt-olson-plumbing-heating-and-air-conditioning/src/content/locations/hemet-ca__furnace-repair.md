## Why Hemet Homes See Furnace Problems When the Cold Arrives

The Inland Empire's desert-adjacent climate creates a heating pattern that's hard on equipment. Hemet furnaces may sit idle for six or seven months, then get called on to run continuously during a cold stretch, sometimes for days without a break. That long dormancy followed by sudden heavy use is one of the most common reasons a heat exchanger cracks, a flame sensor corrodes, or a draft inducer motor seizes.

Hemet's housing stock adds another layer of complexity. A significant portion of the city's single-family homes and manufactured housing communities were built in the 1970s through the 1990s, when 80% AFUE furnaces were standard and ductwork was often sized generously, or not sized carefully at all. Older gas furnaces in that era frequently used standing pilot assemblies rather than electronic ignition, and those pilots are now failing at higher rates as components age past their design life. If your furnace is blowing cold air or cycling on and off without reaching temperature, the root cause is often something specific to that generation of equipment rather than a generic breakdown.
