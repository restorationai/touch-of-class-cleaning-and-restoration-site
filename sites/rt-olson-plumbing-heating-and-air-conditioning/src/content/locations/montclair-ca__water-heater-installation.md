## Why Montclair Homes See Water Heaters Fail Sooner

The water supplied to Montclair through the Inland Empire Utilities Agency and local municipal sources is notably hard, high in dissolved calcium and magnesium. Over time, that mineral content builds up as a chalky sediment layer at the bottom of a tank-style heater. You may notice it first as a low rumbling or popping sound when the burner fires, which is the burner heating water trapped beneath that sediment crust. Left alone, the sediment acts as insulation, forcing the burner to run longer, raising energy bills, and eventually cracking the tank lining from the inside out.

Montclair's climate adds a second layer of stress. Garage-mounted water heaters, common in the city's post-1960s tract homes, spend summer months in spaces that can exceed 120°F. That ambient heat strains the anode rod, accelerates corrosion, and causes temperature-and-pressure relief valves to cycle more frequently than they should. A unit that might last 12 years in a cooler climate often shows signs of failure here in 8 to 10.

## Tankless vs. Tank: What Makes Sense in Montclair

Both tank and tankless water heaters have a place in Montclair homes, but the choice depends on your household size, gas service capacity, and how your home's plumbing is laid out.

**Tank-style units** (40- and 50-gallon are the most common sizes in Montclair's single-family homes) are straightforward to replace on existing connections and typically carry a lower upfront cost. If your current unit is a standard 50-gallon natural gas heater, a like-for-like swap can often be completed in a few hours.

**Tankless water heaters** deliver hot water on demand and eliminate standby heat loss, a real efficiency gain given how long Montclair summers last. The trade-off is upfront cost and, in some cases, a gas line upgrade. Many of the city's older homes were plumbed with half-inch gas supply lines that can't support the higher BTU draw of a condensing tankless unit without upsizing. We assess your existing gas line capacity before recommending a model, so there are no surprises on installation day.

Every water heater installation follows the same sequence, calibrated to local conditions:
