Pomona sits in one of the harder-water corridors in the San Gabriel Valley, where municipal supplies drawn from a mix of groundwater basins and imported Metropolitan Water District sources routinely deliver water with elevated calcium and magnesium levels. If you've noticed white crust building up around your faucets, soap that won't lather properly, or a water heater that's started rumbling earlier than it should, the mineral load in your supply is almost certainly the cause. RT Olson Plumbing, Heating and Air Conditioning installs water softeners, whole-house filtration systems, and reverse osmosis units sized for Pomona's specific water chemistry, not a one-size-fits-all catalog pick.

## Why Pomona Properties Deal with Hard Water Challenges

The Pomona Valley draws from a combination of local groundwater and blended imported water, and the result is a supply that can fluctuate in hardness depending on the season and how much MWD water is in the mix. During drier months when local groundwater makes up a larger share, hardness levels tend to climb. That mineral load doesn't just show up on your glassware, it accumulates inside pipe walls, shortens the service life of tankless water heaters, clogs aerators, and leaves a film on tile and fixtures that no amount of scrubbing fully removes.

Older housing stock in Pomona, particularly the craftsman bungalows and mid-century ranch homes that make up much of the city's residential fabric, often has galvanized or copper supply lines that have been quietly collecting scale for decades. Hard water accelerates that process, narrowing flow and raising pressure drop across the system. A properly sized water softener addresses the problem at the point of entry, protecting every fixture and appliance downstream.

## Our Water Softener and Filtration Process in Pomona

Every installation starts with a water quality assessment. We test for hardness, total dissolved solids, chloramines (the disinfectant byproduct Pomona residents commonly notice as a faint chemical taste or smell), and any secondary contaminants relevant to your neighborhood's supply. That test result drives equipment selection, grain capacity for the softener, filter media type, membrane rating for reverse osmosis if you're adding a drinking-water stage.

For whole-house softener installs, we locate the system at the main service entry before the water heater branch, standard practice, but the exact placement varies depending on whether your home has a dedicated bypass for outdoor irrigation (most Pomona lots with landscaping do, and softened water on turf is a waste of salt and resin capacity). We handle all connections, set the regeneration cycle to match your household's actual daily usage, and commission the system before we leave. Reverse osmosis units for kitchen drinking water are typically installed under the sink with a dedicated faucet and, where applicable, a line to the refrigerator ice maker.

## Equipment and Methods for Hard Water Treatment

For most Pomona households, a salt-based ion-exchange softener remains the most effective and cost-efficient solution for whole-house hardness reduction. We also install salt-free conditioners (template-assisted crystallization systems) for customers who prefer to avoid sodium addition or who have HOA restrictions on brine discharge, some Pomona-area HOAs and local sewer authority guidelines have begun flagging high-chloride discharge from regenerating softeners, so it's worth confirming your situation before choosing a system type. For drinking water quality, a multi-stage reverse osmosis system under the kitchen sink removes chloramines, nitrates, and residual TDS that a softener alone doesn't address.

## Local Note

Pomona homes built in the 1950s and 1960s, a significant share of the city's housing stock, were often plumbed with copper that has held up well structurally but has accumulated a layer of scale on the interior walls over sixty-plus years of hard water. When we install a new softener on one of these homes, we always advise homeowners to run cold water at every fixture for a few minutes over the first week. Softened water is slightly more aggressive toward existing scale deposits, and in older copper lines it can temporarily dislodge mineral buildup that then shows up at aerators and showerheads.
