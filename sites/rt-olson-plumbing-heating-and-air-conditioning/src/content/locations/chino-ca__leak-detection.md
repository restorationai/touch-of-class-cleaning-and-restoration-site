## Why Chino Properties See Leak Detection Issues

Chino sits on expansive clay soils common throughout the Inland Empire's western edge. When that clay dries out during a long summer, it contracts; when winter rains arrive, it swells. Copper and galvanized supply lines buried in or under a slab absorb that movement over years, developing micro-fractures at joints and bends. The leak that results rarely announces itself — water migrates along the path of least resistance, sometimes surfacing several feet from the actual break.

Chino also has a meaningful share of residential construction from the 1970s and 1980s, when galvanized steel pipe was still common in supply and drain lines. Galvanized corrodes from the inside out, narrowing over decades and eventually pinholing. By the time a homeowner notices reduced pressure or a damp spot on the garage floor, the pipe wall may have been failing for months. Slab foundations — standard in most of Chino's single-family tracts — make that kind of leak harder to localize without electronic equipment.

## Our Leak Detection Process in Chino

The process starts before any concrete is touched. We use electronic acoustic listening devices to isolate the sound signature of pressurized water escaping a pipe — different pipe materials and soil densities produce different acoustic profiles, and clay-dominant ground like Chino's tends to transmit sound differently than sandy or loamy soil. We also use thermal imaging to identify temperature differentials in slab surfaces and walls, and correlate those readings with pressure tests on isolated line segments.

Once we've triangulated the leak location to within a tight area, we document the findings — photographs, pressure readings, and a written location report — before any exploratory opening is made. That documentation matters for insurance claims and, in some cases, for HOA approval processes before work begins on common-area plumbing or shared infrastructure.
