## Why Hemet Properties See Emergency Plumbing Problems

Hemet sits in the San Jacinto Valley at roughly 1,600 feet elevation, surrounded by terrain that drains hard and fast when the rains do come. The valley's expansive clay soils swell when wet and contract sharply during the long dry season, a cycle that puts lateral sewer lines and main water service connections under constant low-grade stress. Over time, that movement causes joints to separate and pipes to crack in ways that show no warning until a toilet backs up into the shower or a wet spot appears on the ceiling.

The city's housing stock adds another layer of complexity. Hemet grew rapidly through several distinct eras, mid-century tract homes, 1970s and 1980s retirement-community builds, and more recent master-planned developments, which means the plumbing inside any given house might be galvanized steel, copper, CPVC, or PEX, sometimes in the same run. Galvanized lines in older homes corrode from the inside out, restricting flow for years before they finally split. CPVC, common in the retirement-era builds, becomes brittle in extreme heat and can shatter rather than bend when a fitting is stressed.
