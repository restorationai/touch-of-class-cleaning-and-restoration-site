## Why Montclair Homes See AC Problems More Often Than Coastal Communities

The Inland Empire's climate is the primary culprit. Montclair sits in a basin that traps heat radiating off the surrounding foothills, and the city's position along the I-10 corridor means afternoon temperatures can run 15–20 degrees warmer than coastal Los Angeles on the same day. That sustained heat load pushes residential HVAC equipment hard, compressors cycle more frequently, refrigerant pressures climb, and capacitors that might last a decade on the coast can fail in half that time here.

Montclair's housing stock adds another layer. A significant portion of the city's single-family homes were built during the tract-home boom of the 1960s and 1970s, when ductwork was often routed through unconditioned attic spaces with minimal insulation. Those attic temperatures can exceed 140°F on a July afternoon, degrading duct seals and forcing the air handler to work against significant heat gain before conditioned air ever reaches a living space. If your system runs constantly but the house never quite cools down, deteriorating duct connections are often the reason, not the unit itself.

## Our AC Repair Process in Montclair

Every repair call starts with a full system diagnostic rather than a quick parts swap. We check refrigerant charge and look for signs of a leak, an undercharged system is one of the most common reasons a Montclair home stops cooling effectively, and simply topping off refrigerant without finding the leak means you'll be calling again in a few months. We pressure-test the refrigerant circuit, inspect the condenser coil for debris and corrosion (outdoor units here accumulate a surprising amount of dust and cottonwood during spring), and test capacitors, contactors, and the blower motor under load.

If the diagnosis points to a refrigerant leak, we locate and repair it before recharging, not after. We also check static pressure in the duct system, because an undersized return or a collapsed flex duct can create symptoms that look exactly like a refrigerant problem but won't be fixed by touching the refrigerant circuit at all.

## Local Note

One pattern we see specifically in Montclair's older tract homes: the original builders often ran refrigerant line sets through exterior walls on the south or west face of the house, where they get direct afternoon sun for hours. Over years, that UV and heat exposure degrades the insulation jacket on the suction line, reducing its effectiveness and sometimes causing the copper itself to develop stress micro-cracks near fittings. If your system is more than 15 years old and you're seeing intermittent cooling loss that clears up overnight, it's worth having the line set inspected, not just the unit.
