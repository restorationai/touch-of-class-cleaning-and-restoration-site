## Why French Valley Properties See Drain Problems

The soil composition throughout the French Valley area, expansive clay mixed with decomposed granite, shifts noticeably with seasonal wet-dry cycles. When the ground swells after a rainy winter and contracts through a dry Inland Empire summer, underground drain pipes move with it. That movement stresses joints and creates low spots where grease, soap scum, and debris collect. Roots from the ornamental trees planted along subdivision greenbelts and parkways find those stressed joints quickly.

The housing stock here skews newer, much of French Valley was built out during the 2000s and 2010s, so you're less likely to encounter the cast-iron pipes common in older Southern California cities. PVC and ABS lines dominate, which is good news for longevity, but those materials still accumulate biofilm and grease scale, especially in kitchens where cooking oils go down the sink daily. High-density neighborhoods also mean shared lateral lines in some townhome communities, where one neighbor's grease buildup becomes everyone's slow drain.

## Our Drain Cleaning Process in French Valley

When we arrive, the first step is a drain assessment, not just running a snake and calling it done. We listen to what you're experiencing: is the backup isolated to one fixture, or are multiple drains sluggish at the same time? Multiple slow drains usually point to a main line issue rather than a localized clog.

For kitchen sink clogs and shower drain backups, cable auger service cuts through grease and hair blockages efficiently. For main line stoppages, especially where root intrusion or sediment buildup is suspected, hydro jetting is the more thorough solution. A hydro jet pushes water at high pressure through the line, scouring the pipe walls clean rather than just punching a hole through the clog. It's the difference between clearing a drain and actually cleaning it.

When the cause isn't obvious, we run a camera inspection through the line to see exactly what we're dealing with before recommending a method. That step saves you money, there's no reason to hydro jet a line that needs a simple cable clearing, and no reason to cable a line that has a root mass a snake won't touch.

## Local Note

One pattern we see consistently in French Valley's newer subdivisions: builders often run kitchen drain lines with minimal slope to keep them hidden in tight cabinet spaces. Code requires a minimum pitch, but the minimum is sometimes exactly what was installed, just enough to pass inspection, not enough to flush grease reliably over years of use. If your kitchen sink has been slow since the house was new, that low-slope drain run is often the culprit, and hydro jetting combined with a camera look at the pitch will tell you whether the line needs a slope correction or just a thorough cleaning.
