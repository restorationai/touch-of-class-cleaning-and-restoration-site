Anaheim's water infrastructure serves one of Orange County's densest urban cores — a city where aging residential blocks sit alongside sprawling resort-district commercial properties, and where Southern California's hard, mineral-rich water accelerates corrosion inside pipes that may not have been touched since the 1970s. When a slow, hidden leak starts behind a stucco wall or beneath a concrete slab, the first sign is often a water bill that climbs $40 or $80 without explanation, not a visible puddle. RT Olson Plumbing, Heating and Air Conditioning uses electronic and acoustic leak detection equipment to find those leaks without tearing open walls or breaking up slabs until we know exactly where the problem is.

## Why Anaheim Properties Develop Hidden Leaks

Anaheim's housing stock spans several distinct eras, and each era comes with its own leak profile. Tracts built in the 1950s and 1960s — common in neighborhoods west of the 57 Freeway — frequently still carry galvanized steel supply lines. Galvanized pipe corrodes from the inside out, narrowing over decades before it begins to weep at joints or develop pinhole failures mid-run. You may have full water pressure at the tap right up until a section fails.

Homes built in the 1970s and early 1980s often used copper, but the combination of Anaheim's hard municipal water and slightly acidic soil in parts of the city can pit copper from the outside when it runs underground. Slab foundations — standard across most of Anaheim's single-family residential stock — bury those copper runs beneath four to six inches of concrete, which means a leak can saturate the subsoil and wick up through flooring for months before it registers as damage you can see or smell.

The resort and commercial corridor near Harbor Boulevard adds another layer: large irrigation systems, high-volume supply lines, and older backflow assemblies that see heavy use and are often the first components to develop slow leaks that drive up water bills without triggering any visible sign of failure.

## Our Leak Detection Process in Anaheim

We start with a pressure test on your supply system. Isolating sections of the line tells us quickly whether the loss is on the supply side, the irrigation system, or somewhere in the drain and waste network. That narrows the search before we bring in equipment.

For suspected slab leaks — one of the most common calls we get in Anaheim — we use acoustic listening devices and electronic amplification to hear the frequency signature of water escaping under pressure through concrete. Different pipe materials and different slab thicknesses change what that signal sounds like, and experience with Southern California construction matters here. We also use thermal imaging in situations where a leak is warming or cooling a floor surface unevenly, which is often visible to an infrared camera before it is visible to the eye.

Once we have a precise location, we discuss access options with you before any work begins. In many slab-leak situations, a targeted spot repair through a small core opening is far less disruptive than opening a wall chase or rerouting the line through the attic — but the right answer depends on pipe age, leak location, and whether the line has shown prior repairs.

## Local Note

Anaheim sits within the service territory of the Anaheim Public Utilities Department for water, and the city's water is drawn partly from the Orange County groundwater basin — a supply that is naturally high in dissolved minerals. That mineral content leaves scale deposits inside water heaters and on valve seats, but it also interacts with older copper pipe in ways that can accelerate pitting corrosion at low points in the line where water sits. If your home was built before 1985 and has never had its under-slab copper inspected, a pressure test is a reasonable precaution, not just a response to an obvious problem.
