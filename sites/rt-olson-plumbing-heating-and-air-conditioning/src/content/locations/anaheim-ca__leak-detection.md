## Why Anaheim Properties See Hidden Leak Problems

Much of Anaheim's residential housing stock was built between the late 1940s and the early 1970s, a period when copper and galvanized steel were the standard pipe materials. Galvanized lines corrode from the inside out, narrowing over decades until pinhole leaks open up inside walls or under slabs. Copper fares better, but Anaheim's water — delivered through the Municipal Water District of Orange County and blended with Colorado River water — carries mineral content that accelerates pitting corrosion in older copper runs. The result is that many homes in the city are carrying pipes that look fine from the outside and are actively leaking inside.

Slab construction compounds the problem. A large share of Anaheim's single-family homes sit on post-tension or conventional concrete slabs poured directly on grade. When a supply line beneath that slab develops a pinhole, water migrates laterally through the sand bed before it finds a path upward — which means the warm spot you feel underfoot or the damp patch near a baseboard may be several feet from the actual break. Without electronic detection, a plumber guessing at the leak location can open multiple unnecessary trenches.

## Our Leak Detection Process in Anaheim

The goal of leak detection is to find the exact failure point before anything is cut, cored, or excavated. The process starts with a pressure test on the supply system to confirm a leak is present and to isolate whether it's on the hot side, cold side, or a drain line. From there, electronic amplification equipment listens through the slab or soil for the acoustic signature of pressurized water escaping a pipe — a sound that's distinct from normal flow even when it's buried under several inches of concrete.

For leaks that don't produce a strong acoustic signal — common in low-pressure situations or in homes with thick slabs — thermal imaging can map temperature differentials across the floor surface, revealing the migration path of escaping water. Ground-penetrating radar or tracer gas methods are available for cases where the pipe routing is unknown or the leak is in a pressurized irrigation line running through the yard. The combination of methods means we locate the leak with enough precision that most repairs require a single, targeted opening rather than a speculative trench.

## Local Note

Anaheim homes built in the 1950s and 1960s frequently have their original galvanized branch lines still intact even when the main supply has been repiped in copper or PEX. It's a partial repipe pattern that's common across older Orange County cities — the visible, accessible runs get replaced, but the lines stubbed into walls behind tile or under slabs get left in place. When a leak surfaces in one of these homes, the failure is often in that forgotten galvanized stub, not in the newer material. Knowing to look for that hybrid plumbing configuration saves time during diagnosis and prevents misidentifying the wrong section of pipe as the source.
