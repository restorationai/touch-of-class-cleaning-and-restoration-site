## Why Eastvale Homes See Fixture Problems Early

The Inland Empire sits in a region where water hardness regularly runs high, calcium and magnesium deposits accumulate inside valve seats, aerator screens, and cartridge housings faster than homeowners expect. In a city built mostly within the last twenty years, that means fixtures that should have a decade of life left are already showing scale buildup inside the body of the faucet, not just at the tip. You'll notice it as reduced flow, a faucet that drips within minutes of being shut off, or a toilet that runs in short bursts long after the tank should have settled. The hard water problem is compounded by the fact that many Eastvale subdivisions were built with cost-efficient plumbing packages, functional at installation, but not engineered for longevity under mineral-heavy conditions.

Shower valves are a particular pain point. Pressure-balancing cartridges in single-handle showers trap scale in the ceramic disc seats, causing the valve to stick, leak behind the wall, or lose its ability to hold a consistent temperature. Left alone, a slow weep behind a shower valve can saturate the backer board and framing before it ever shows on the surface.

## Local Note

One pattern that comes up repeatedly in Eastvale's newer subdivisions: the angle stop shutoff valves under sinks and behind toilets are often the original compression-style valves installed during construction. After ten or more years, these valves can seize in the open position, meaning if a supply line fails or a faucet needs replacement, there's no reliable way to shut off water to that fixture without going to the main.
