## Why Hemet Properties See Water Heater Problems Earlier Than Expected

The water delivered through Hemet's distribution system consistently tests on the harder end of the scale for Riverside County, total dissolved solids and hardness levels that, over a few years, deposit a thick layer of scale on tank elements and along the bottom of storage tanks. That scale acts as insulation between the burner and the water, forcing the unit to run longer cycles to hit temperature. Longer cycles mean more thermal stress on the tank lining, more wear on the thermocouple and gas valve, and higher gas bills before anything visibly fails.

Hemet's summer temperatures regularly push past 100°F, and attics and garages, the two most common water heater locations in the valley's single-family housing stock, can reach temperatures that stress pressure relief valves and accelerate anode rod depletion. A sacrificial anode that might last six years in a mild coastal climate can be significantly reduced in five or fewer years here. When the anode is gone, the tank wall is next.

## Our Water Heater Repair Process in Hemet

When a technician arrives, the first step is a pressure and temperature check at the unit and at the nearest fixture, low hot-water pressure at the tap often points to a failing dip tube or heavy scale at the inlet, not the heater itself. From there the diagnostic moves through the thermocouple or igniter, gas valve operation, element resistance on electric units, and a visual inspection of the tank exterior and the area around the base for early signs of a slow weep.

If sediment is the primary issue, a full flush and descale can restore efficiency and quiet that low rumbling sound that tends to get louder in the early morning when the tank is recovering after overnight use. Where a component has failed, a thermocouple that won't hold a flame signal, a pressure relief valve that drips continuously, a corroded anode, parts are replaced on the same visit when stock allows. One pattern that comes up repeatedly in Hemet's older neighborhoods, particularly in the housing tracts built during the valley's growth period in the 1970s and 1980s, is water heaters installed in tight garage alcoves with minimal clearance around the flue.
