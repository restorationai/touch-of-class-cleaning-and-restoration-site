## Why Norco Properties Are Prone to Hidden Leaks

Norco's housing stock skews toward single-story ranch homes built between the 1960s and 1980s — an era when copper supply lines were standard but slab construction was equally common. Copper holds up well, but decades of chlorinated municipal water, slight ground movement from Inland Empire seismic activity, and the expansion-contraction cycles that come with Southern California's wide seasonal temperature swings all add stress to joints and fittings over time.

The soil here compounds the problem. Expansive clay soils shift when they dry out in summer and swell when winter rains arrive. That movement can gradually stress the underground service lines running beneath driveways, corrals, and landscaped areas. Because Norco properties often sit on larger lots — some with detached barns, arenas, or guest quarters — the buried plumbing network is simply longer than on a standard suburban lot, which means more pipe exposed to ground movement and more opportunities for a pinhole leak to develop far from the main structure.

## Our Leak Detection Process in Norco

We don't start by opening walls or breaking concrete. Leak detection is a diagnostic process first, and the goal is to pinpoint the source before any restoration work begins.

The typical sequence looks like this:

**Pressure testing** — We isolate sections of the supply system and monitor pressure drop over time. A sustained drop tells us there's an active leak somewhere in that zone; a stable reading rules it out.

**Electronic leak detection** — Using acoustic listening equipment, we amplify the sound of water escaping under pressure through soil, concrete, or framing. On Norco's slab-on-grade homes, this step is especially valuable because it can locate a leak to within a few inches before we ever touch the floor.

**Thermal imaging** — A thermal camera reads temperature differentials in walls and floors. Evaporating water cools the surrounding material, which shows up as a distinct cold signature on the camera. This is particularly useful on stucco exteriors and interior drywall where moisture has migrated laterally from the original breach.

**Video pipe inspection** — For underground lines or drain laterals, a camera run through the line confirms the location and condition of the pipe, which matters when you're deciding between a spot repair and a full re-route.

Once we've confirmed the location, we give you a clear explanation of what we found and what the repair options are — before anything is opened up.
