## Why Claremont Properties See AC Repair Issues

The combination of Claremont's inland heat, low humidity, and the Santa Ana winds that funnel through the Cajon Pass creates conditions that accelerate wear on air conditioning equipment in ways that milder climates simply don't. Compressors cycle harder and longer from June through September. Condenser coils accumulate the fine dust and debris that blows in from the foothills, reducing airflow and forcing refrigerant pressures out of range. Capacitors, the components that give compressor motors their starting kick, fail at higher rates in extreme heat, and Claremont's summer temperatures are consistently among the highest in Los Angeles County.

Claremont's housing stock adds another layer of complexity. The city's older residential neighborhoods include homes built in the 1920s through the 1950s, many of which were retrofitted with central air conditioning decades after original construction. Ductwork in those retrofits is often undersized for today's higher-efficiency equipment, which means even a well-functioning unit can struggle to cool the house evenly. A refrigerant leak or a failing blower motor in one of these systems doesn't just mean discomfort, it means the whole retrofit is working against you.

## Our AC Repair Process in Claremont

Every repair call starts with a full system diagnostic, not a parts swap. A technician will check refrigerant pressures against manufacturer specifications, inspect the condenser and evaporator coils for fouling or damage, test capacitors and contactors under load, and measure airflow at supply registers throughout the home. That sequence matters because a low refrigerant reading, for example, is a symptom, the underlying refrigerant leak is the actual problem, and sealing it correctly requires locating the breach, not just topping off the charge.

For refrigerant work, technicians follow EPA Section 608 handling requirements regardless of refrigerant type. If your system still uses R-22, common in equipment installed before 2010, the repair conversation will also include an honest assessment of whether the unit's age and condition make repair the right economic call versus replacement. That's a conversation worth having before spending money on a refrigerant that is no longer manufactured.

Once the root cause is confirmed, repairs are completed in a single visit whenever parts are available on the truck. Claremont homeowners shouldn't have to schedule a second appointment just to get their system running again on a 105-degree afternoon.
