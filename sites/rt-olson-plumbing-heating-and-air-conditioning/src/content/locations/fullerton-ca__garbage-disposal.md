## Why Fullerton Homes See More Disposal Problems Than You'd Expect

Fullerton's housing stock spans a wide range of eras, and that variety matters when it comes to kitchen plumbing. Homes built in the mid-20th century — and there are many in Fullerton's established residential corridors — often have narrower drain lines and older slip-joint fittings under the sink. When a disposal is installed without accounting for those constraints, the connection points flex and loosen over time, producing the slow leak that shows up as a water stain on the cabinet floor rather than a dramatic flood.

Southern California's hard water is the other factor. The municipal supply serving Fullerton carries a high mineral load, and over months and years, calcium deposits build up inside the disposal's grinding chamber and along the discharge elbow. That buildup restricts flow, forces the motor to work harder, and accelerates wear on the impeller plate — which is why disposals in this region often fail earlier than their rated lifespan suggests.

## Common Signs Your Fullerton Disposal Needs Attention

A disposal that hums but won't grind has almost certainly tripped its thermal overload or has an object — a bottle cap, a small utensil — lodged between the impeller and the grinding ring. That's often a same-visit fix. A unit that trips the reset button repeatedly, even with nothing jammed, usually points to a failing motor that's drawing too much current. Leaks fall into two categories: a leak from the top flange means the sink mounting is loose or the plumber's putty has dried out; a leak from the side or bottom discharge port means a gasket or the housing itself has failed. Knowing which you have before calling saves time on the phone.
