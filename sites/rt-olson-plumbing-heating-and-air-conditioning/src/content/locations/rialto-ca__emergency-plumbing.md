## Why Rialto Properties See Emergency Plumbing Problems

The San Bernardino Valley's clay-heavy soil is the root cause of many of the emergency calls we take in Rialto. Clay expands when it absorbs winter rain and contracts sharply during the long dry summers. That constant movement works on buried pipe joints over time, particularly on older galvanized steel supply lines and cast-iron drain stacks found in homes built through the 1970s and 1980s, which make up a significant share of Rialto's residential stock. When a joint finally gives, the failure is rarely a slow drip; it tends to be a sudden, high-volume release that can flood a slab-on-grade foundation in minutes.

Hard water is the other persistent factor. Water delivered across San Bernardino County carries elevated mineral content, and that scale accumulates inside water heater tanks, pressure-reducing valves, and supply risers. A PRV that's been fighting scale buildup for a decade can fail without warning, spiking line pressure and stressing every fixture in the house simultaneously. If you've noticed your water pressure creeping up, that's worth addressing before it becomes a 2 a.m. emergency.

## Local Note

Homes built on Rialto's older residential streets, particularly those developed in the 1960s and early 1970s, frequently have galvanized steel supply lines that were never replaced. Galvanized pipe corrodes from the inside out, so the exterior can look intact while the interior bore has narrowed to a fraction of its original diameter. When one of these lines finally ruptures, the failure point is rarely the obvious one; pressure finds the weakest corroded section, which may be several feet away from where the water is actually appearing.
