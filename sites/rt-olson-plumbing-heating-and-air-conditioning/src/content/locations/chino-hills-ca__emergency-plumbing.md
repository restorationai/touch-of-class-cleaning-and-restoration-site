## Why Chino Hills Properties See Emergency Plumbing Issues

Most of Chino Hills developed rapidly through the late 1980s and 1990s, which means a large share of the housing stock is now pushing 30 to 40 years old, old enough for original polybutylene or early CPVC supply lines to become brittle and fail without warning. Those same tracts were built on graded hillside pads where engineered fill compacts unevenly over decades. As the ground settles, slab foundations shift slightly, and the rigid drain lines embedded in them can crack or separate at the joints. The result is a slow slab leak that warms a section of tile floor long before the water bill spikes.

Chino Hills also sits in a region that swings between dry Santa Ana wind events and occasional heavy winter rain. After a dry stretch, clay soils shrink and pull away from foundation edges; when the rains return, they swell back. That repeated cycle stresses outdoor irrigation connections and the main water service line where it enters the slab, two of the most common sources of emergency calls in this city.
