## Why Santa Ana Homes See More Disposal Problems

Mineral scale is the quiet culprit behind a large share of disposal calls in this part of Orange County. Hard water deposits build up on the impeller plate and flywheel over months of use, gradually reducing the unit's ability to break down food waste efficiently. The result: a disposal that hums and trips its reset button repeatedly, or one that drains slowly even when the grinding chamber sounds clear.

Santa Ana's housing stock adds another layer. The city has a substantial inventory of homes built in the 1950s through 1970s, many of which still have the original cast-iron drain lines under the kitchen. Those older pipes have rougher interior surfaces where food particles catch and accumulate, meaning even a well-functioning disposal can contribute to chronic slow drains if the downstream plumbing isn't sized or sloped correctly. When we assess a disposal problem in an older Santa Ana home, we're looking at the whole drain path — not just the unit mounted under the sink.

## What the Repair and Installation Process Looks Like

When a technician arrives, the first step is a quick diagnostic to separate a fixable repair from a unit that's past its useful life. A jammed disposal — where something hard got past the splash guard and locked the flywheel — is often resolved with a hex wrench at the reset port and a few minutes of hands-on clearing. A disposal that hums but won't turn is usually a seized motor or a flywheel frozen by scale buildup; sometimes freeing it is enough, sometimes the motor is gone.

Leaking disposals require pinpointing the source before anything else. A leak from the top of the unit (around the sink flange) is a different fix than a leak from the side discharge port or the bottom drain connection. We don't assume — we trace it.

When replacement makes more sense than repair, we carry a range of units suited to different household volumes and plumbing configurations. For older Santa Ana homes with narrower drain lines, motor size and grinding speed matter more than horsepower ratings alone — a high-torque, slower-grinding unit often handles legacy plumbing more reliably than a fast, high-HP model that overwhelms a 1.5-inch drain.
