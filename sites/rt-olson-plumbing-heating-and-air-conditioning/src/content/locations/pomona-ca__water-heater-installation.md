## Why Pomona's Water and Housing Stock Wear Out Water Heaters Faster

The water delivered across Pomona consistently runs on the harder end of the Southern California spectrum. High mineral content accelerates sediment accumulation at the bottom of tank-style heaters, forcing the burner to work harder to heat water through that insulating layer. The result is higher gas or electric bills, longer recovery times, and a shortened lifespan, a unit that might last 12 years in a softer-water market can start failing noticeably sooner here.

Pomona's housing stock adds another layer of complexity. The city has a substantial inventory of homes built between the 1940s and 1970s, many of which still have original or once-replaced water heater closets sized for older 30- or 40-gallon units. Installing a modern 50-gallon water heater, or making the switch to a tankless system, often means reworking the gas line sizing, upgrading the flue or venting configuration, and sometimes relocating the unit entirely to meet current California code clearance requirements. These aren't surprises if you know the local housing stock; they're just part of the job.

## Tankless vs. Tank: What Makes Sense in a Pomona Home

The tankless vs. tank question comes up on nearly every replacement call, and the honest answer depends on the home. Tankless water heaters eliminate standby heat loss and deliver continuous hot water, which appeals to larger households, but in Pomona's older homes, the gas supply line often needs to be upsized to support the higher BTU demand of a tankless unit. That upgrade adds cost and labor that a straight tank swap doesn't require.

For a typical single-family home with moderate hot water demand, a quality 50-gallon tank unit with a good first-hour rating is frequently the more cost-effective path. For households with high simultaneous demand, multiple bathrooms running at once, a large family, a properly sized tankless system pays back over time through lower operating costs. We walk through both options with you before any work begins, including realistic cost ranges for each, so the decision is yours to make with complete information.

## Our Installation Process in Pomona

Every water heater installation in Pomona starts with pulling the required permit through the City of Pomona's Building and Safety Division. California requires permits for water heater replacements, and inspections protect you, an unpermitted install can complicate a home sale or a homeowner's insurance claim down the road.

Once the permit is in hand, the process moves through disconnecting and safely removing the old unit, inspecting the existing gas line, water supply connections, and venting for code compliance, and then setting and connecting the new equipment. For tankless installs, that typically includes mounting the unit, running new gas supply if needed, and configuring the condensate drain on condensing models. We test the system fully before we leave and walk you through the new unit's settings and maintenance schedule, including how often to flush the heat exchanger given Pomona's water hardness.

## Local Note: Older Homes and Seismic Strapping Requirements

California requires water heaters to be double-strapped to the wall framing to resist seismic movement, a requirement that's been on the books for decades but is frequently missed in older Pomona homes where a unit was swapped informally without a permit. When we pull an existing heater in a pre-1970s home, we often find single-strap or no-strap installations that wouldn't pass today's inspection. We bring the strapping into compliance as part of every installation, which matters both for safety and for the permit sign-off. It's a small detail that becomes a significant one if the ground ever moves.
