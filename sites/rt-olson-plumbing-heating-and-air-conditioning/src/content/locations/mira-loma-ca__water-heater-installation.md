## Why Mira Loma Properties See Accelerated Water Heater Wear

The water delivered to Mira Loma homes and businesses carries a high mineral content that leaves scale on heating elements, coats the inside of tank walls, and forces burners to work harder to reach set temperature. A standard tank unit that might last 12 years in a coastal climate can show serious sediment buildup and efficiency loss in half that time here. You may notice it as a rumbling or popping sound when the burner fires, that's trapped water boiling beneath a crust of sediment at the tank floor.

The area's housing stock adds another layer of complexity. Much of Mira Loma developed rapidly through the 1970s and 1980s, and a large share of those homes still have their original gas lines, venting configurations, and water supply connections sized for equipment that no longer meets current California energy efficiency standards. Replacing a unit isn't always a straight swap, venting may need to be rerouted, seismic strapping must meet current code, and the gas line may require upsizing if you're moving to a higher-BTU tankless unit.

## Tankless vs. Tank: What Makes Sense in Mira Loma

The tankless vs. tank water heater question comes up on nearly every installation call, and the honest answer depends on your household's hot-water demand, your gas line capacity, and how long you plan to stay in the property.

Tankless water heaters heat water on demand rather than maintaining a stored supply, which eliminates standby heat loss, a meaningful saving when your unit is already working overtime against hard-water scale. However, tankless units require a larger gas supply line (typically ¾-inch minimum) and proper venting, and they benefit significantly from a whole-home water softener in Mira Loma's water conditions. Without one, the heat exchanger in a tankless unit can scale up and lose efficiency within a few years.

A 50-gallon tank unit remains the right call for many households, lower upfront cost, simpler installation in homes where the gas line can't be easily upsized, and straightforward replacement when the time comes. We'll walk through both options with you, including current equipment costs, so you can make the decision that fits your home and budget.
