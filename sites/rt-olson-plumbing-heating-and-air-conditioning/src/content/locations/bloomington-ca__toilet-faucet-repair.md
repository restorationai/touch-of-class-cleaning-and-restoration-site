## Why Bloomington Properties See Fixture Problems

The water delivered to Bloomington homes and commercial properties through the Inland Empire Utilities Agency service area consistently registers high on the hardness scale. Calcium and magnesium deposits accumulate inside faucet bodies, around toilet fill valves, and behind shower cartridges faster than homeowners in coastal climates typically expect. A faucet that develops a slow drip is often not a worn washer, it's a cartridge that has been gradually locked in place by mineral buildup until it can no longer seat cleanly.

Bloomington's housing stock adds another layer. Many properties in the area were built during the tract-home boom of the 1960s through the 1980s, when builder-grade brass fittings and single-handle shower valves were standard. Those valves are now 40 to 60 years old. Replacement parts exist, but some original valve bodies are corroded beyond the point where a cartridge swap alone will hold, the entire valve needs to come out. Knowing the difference before opening a wall saves time and money.

## Our Toilet, Faucet and Fixture Services Process in Bloomington

Every fixture call starts with a diagnosis, not an assumption. A running toilet, for example, can be caused by a worn flapper, a faulty fill valve, a float set too high, or a cracked overflow tube, and the fix is different in each case. Swapping a flapper when the fill valve is the actual problem just means a callback in two weeks.

For faucet and shower valve work, the process includes shutting off the appropriate supply stop, inspecting the valve body for corrosion before committing to a repair versus replacement, and sourcing parts that match the existing rough-in dimensions. In older Bloomington homes where the original valve is recessed into tile, we work carefully to avoid unnecessary tile damage, a small detail that matters a great deal when matching 1970s tile is no longer possible.

Toilet installations follow local code requirements for water-efficient fixtures. California's plumbing code mandates low-flow toilets in new installations and replacements, so every toilet we install in Bloomington meets current state efficiency standards without sacrificing flush performance.

## Local Note: Hard Water and Fixture Longevity in Bloomington

One thing that comes up repeatedly on fixture calls in the Bloomington area: homeowners are often surprised to find that a brand-new faucet starts dripping within a year or two of installation. In a high-hardness water environment like this part of San Bernardino County, ceramic disc cartridges, which are marketed as virtually maintenance-free, can still develop leaks when mineral deposits etch the disc surface. If you're replacing a faucet in Bloomington and want it to last, it's worth asking about models with replaceable cartridges rather than sealed ceramic disc assemblies, and considering a point-of-use filter at the supply line.
