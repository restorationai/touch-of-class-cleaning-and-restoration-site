## Why Yorba Linda Fixtures Wear the Way They Do

The water supplied to most of Yorba Linda carries elevated hardness levels — a natural result of groundwater drawn from inland basins and blended through regional treatment. Over time, calcium and magnesium deposits accumulate inside the components that see the most flow: toilet fill valves, ballcocks, faucet aerators, and the ceramic discs inside cartridge-style handles. A faucet that drips one drop per second wastes roughly 3,000 gallons a year, and in a community where summer temperatures routinely push past 95°F, conservation matters beyond the water bill.

Yorba Linda's housing stock adds another layer. Neighborhoods developed in the 1970s and 1980s — a significant portion of the city's single-family homes — were built with chrome-plated brass supply stops and angle valves that are now approaching or past their expected service life. When a toilet repair or faucet swap requires shutting off a supply stop that hasn't moved in decades, that valve can fail on the spot. Knowing this ahead of time is the difference between a planned fixture replacement and an unplanned emergency.

One thing that comes up repeatedly in Yorba Linda's established neighborhoods is the condition of the angle stops under sinks and behind toilets.
