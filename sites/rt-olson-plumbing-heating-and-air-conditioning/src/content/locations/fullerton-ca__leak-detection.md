## Why Fullerton Properties See More Hidden Leaks

Fullerton sits in the northern end of Orange County where expansive clay soils are the norm rather than the exception. When Southern California's dry seasons give way to wet winters, that clay swells and contracts — and the underground pipes running through it flex accordingly. Copper supply lines installed in homes built between the 1950s and 1970s are particularly vulnerable: the repeated movement work-hardens the pipe walls over decades, eventually producing pinhole leaks that lose only a few gallons per hour but can saturate a slab or undermine a foundation over months.

Slab-on-grade construction dominates a large portion of Fullerton's residential stock, which means those slow leaks have nowhere to drain visibly. Water migrates laterally under the concrete and surfaces wherever it finds a path — sometimes a bathroom floor on the opposite side of the house from the actual break. That's the scenario where electronic leak detection earns its keep: acoustic sensors and thermal imaging locate the leak without a single unnecessary hole in your floor.

Older galvanized water mains, still present in some of the city's pre-1960 neighborhoods, add another variable. Galvanized pipe corrodes from the inside out, and the rust buildup that narrows the bore also creates turbulence that accelerates wall thinning at fittings and elbows.

## Our Leak Detection Process in Fullerton

When we arrive at a Fullerton property, the first step is always a pressure test on the supply side to confirm whether the loss is on the pressurized line or the drain system — those require different equipment and different repair strategies. For pressurized leaks, we use electronic acoustic amplification to listen for the characteristic frequency signature of water escaping under pressure through a small opening. Concrete and soil attenuate sound, but modern ground microphones are sensitive enough to isolate a pinhole leak under a four-inch slab.

For leaks that don't produce a clear acoustic signal — a slow weep at a fitting buried in a wall cavity, for example — we bring in thermal imaging. Active leaks create temperature differentials in surrounding materials that a calibrated infrared camera resolves into a map of wet versus dry structure. We combine both methods when the situation warrants it, because the goal is a single, confirmed location before any concrete is cut or drywall is opened.

Once we've marked the leak location, we walk you through the repair options and, if your homeowner's policy covers sudden and accidental water loss, we document the findings in a format your adjuster can use directly.

## Local Note: Slab Leaks Near Fullerton's Older Neighborhoods

One thing worth knowing if you own an older home in Fullerton's established residential corridors: the original hot-water recirculation lines in some of these houses run directly under the slab with minimal insulation separation from the concrete. Hot water lines under a slab corrode faster than cold lines because thermal cycling accelerates the pinhole process — and because the line is always under pressure, even a small breach loses volume continuously. Homeowners in these areas sometimes notice warm spots on tile floors before they notice any moisture, which is actually a useful early warning. If your floor feels warm in a localized area and your water heater is running more than usual, that combination is worth a leak detection call before you have a full slab leak on your hands.
