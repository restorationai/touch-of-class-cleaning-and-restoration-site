## Why Fullerton Properties See More Hidden Leaks

Fullerton sits in the northern end of Orange County where expansive clay soils shift with seasonal wet-dry cycles. That ground movement puts lateral stress on buried supply and drain lines over decades, gradually opening joints and cracking older pipe materials. Homes built in the postwar building boom — and there are many of them throughout Fullerton — were plumbed with galvanized steel that corrodes from the inside out. By the time rust-colored water appears at a faucet or a water meter spins without explanation, the leak may have been seeping slowly for months.

Slab foundations are common across Fullerton, and slab leaks are particularly deceptive. A pressurized line running beneath a concrete slab can erode the soil underneath it long before moisture wicks up through the slab surface. The first sign is often a warm patch underfoot, a section of flooring that feels slightly soft, or a bathroom that smells faintly of mildew with no visible source.

## Our Leak Detection Process in Fullerton

Finding a leak without unnecessary demolition requires layered diagnostic tools, not guesswork. The process typically starts with a pressure test on the supply system to confirm a leak exists and to isolate which branch is losing pressure. From there, electronic amplification equipment picks up the acoustic signature of water escaping under pressure — a sound that travels through concrete and soil but is inaudible to the human ear without amplification.

For slab leaks, thermal imaging adds a second layer of confirmation. A pressurized line losing water changes the temperature of the concrete above it in a pattern that a thermal camera reads clearly, even when the surface looks completely normal. Correlating acoustic and thermal data lets us mark the leak location within inches before any cutting or excavation begins. In older Fullerton homes with original cast-iron drain lines, we also run camera inspection through the drain system to check for root intrusion or joint separation — a separate failure mode that a pressure test alone won't catch.

## Equipment and Methods We Use for Leak Detection

The tools matter because the wrong approach causes its own damage. Ground microphones and electronic correlators let us listen through concrete slabs and compacted soil. Tracer gas detection — introducing a safe, inert hydrogen-nitrogen mixture into a line and scanning the surface above — is particularly effective for underground irrigation and service line leaks where acoustic signals are weaker. Video pipe inspection with a push camera documents drain line condition and pinpoints the exact footage where a break or root intrusion occurs. None of these methods require opening a wall or cutting a slab until we know precisely where the problem is.
