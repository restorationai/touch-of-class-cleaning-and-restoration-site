## Why Winchester Properties See Fixture Problems

Much of Winchester's housing stock dates from the mid-2000s building boom, when production builders moved fast and installed builder-grade fixtures that were never meant to last two decades. Pressure-balance shower valves from that era, the cartridge-style units common in tract homes throughout this part of Riverside County, tend to stiffen or fail entirely as mineral deposits accumulate. Winchester's water supply carries a moderately high mineral load, and that hardness accelerates wear on ceramic disc faucet cartridges, flapper seats, and fill valve seals. A toilet that ran fine for ten years can suddenly cycle every few minutes once the flapper seat pits enough to let water seep past.

Newer construction along the Highway 79 corridor often uses low-flow fixtures to meet California's water-efficiency standards, which are among the stricter in the country. Those high-efficiency toilets perform well when properly installed, but they're sensitive to supply line pressure and fill valve calibration. A slightly misadjusted fill valve in a 1.28-gallon-per-flush toilet can mimic a running toilet without ever triggering a visible leak, and the water bill tells the story before anything else does.
