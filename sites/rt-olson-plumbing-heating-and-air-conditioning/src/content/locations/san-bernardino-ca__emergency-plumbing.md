## Why San Bernardino Properties See Emergency Plumbing Issues

The Inland Empire's climate swings are hard on plumbing in ways that milder coastal cities don't experience. San Bernardino sits in a basin where summer heat regularly exceeds 105°F and winter nights can dip below freezing, that thermal cycling causes pipe joints to expand and contract repeatedly, loosening connections that were perfectly sound when installed. Clay-heavy soils in lower-elevation parts of the city shift with seasonal moisture changes, which can torque buried sewer laterals and water mains until they crack or separate at the joint.

The city's housing stock adds another layer. A significant portion of San Bernardino's residential neighborhoods were built between the 1940s and 1970s, when galvanized steel supply lines and cast iron drain stacks were standard. Galvanized pipe corrodes from the inside out, flow restricts gradually until a weak spot blows, often without much warning. Cast iron drain lines from that era can hold up for decades, but root intrusion and corrosion eventually win. When those older systems fail, they tend to fail completely rather than leaking slowly.
