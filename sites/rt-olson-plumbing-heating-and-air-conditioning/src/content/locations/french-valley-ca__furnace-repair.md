## Why French Valley Homes See Furnace Problems When They Do

The Inland Valley climate is deceptive. French Valley homeowners run air conditioning hard through September, then flip to heat in November, sometimes the same week. Furnaces that sit dormant for six or seven months collect dust on burners and flame sensors, and the first cold call of the season is often when a neglected system shows its hand. Dirty flame sensors are one of the most common reasons a furnace blows cold air or short-cycles: the sensor can't confirm ignition, so the control board shuts the burner down as a safety measure.

The area's newer subdivisions, many built in the 2000s and 2010s during Temecula Valley's rapid northward expansion, tend to feature two-stage and variable-speed furnaces paired with zoned HVAC systems. These are more efficient than older single-stage units, but they also have more components that can fail: secondary heat exchangers, modulating gas valves, and communicating thermostats that require brand-specific diagnostic tools rather than a generic meter. A technician who works on these systems regularly will move through the diagnosis faster and avoid misreading a fault code.
