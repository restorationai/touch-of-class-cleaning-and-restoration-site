## Why Fontana Fixtures Wear Faster Than You'd Expect

The water coming through Fontana taps carries a high mineral load, hardness levels across the Inland Empire routinely stress fixture internals in ways that coastal California homes rarely see. Calcium and magnesium build up inside ballcock assemblies and cartridge valves, causing toilets to ghost-flush or run continuously and faucets to lose pressure even when the supply line is fully open. Shower valves are especially vulnerable: the pressure-balancing cartridge that keeps your water temperature stable can seize with mineral scale, making the valve stiff to turn or unable to hold a set temperature.

## What the Repair or Replacement Process Looks Like

Every fixture call starts with a clear diagnosis before any parts are ordered or work is quoted. For a running toilet, that means pulling the tank lid and watching the fill cycle, a worn flapper, a misadjusted float, or a cracked overflow tube each behaves differently and requires a different fix. For a dripping faucet, the cartridge or stem is inspected for mineral pitting and the seat is checked for scoring that would cause a new cartridge to fail just as fast.

When replacement makes more sense than repair, a toilet that has been rebuilt twice already, or a faucet body that's corroded through, the swap is handled start to finish in one visit when possible. That includes shutting off the supply, removing the old fixture, checking the rough-in dimensions or drain configuration, and setting the new unit level and leak-free before the water is restored. Shower valve replacements in single-handle or thermostatic configurations follow the same pattern: the cartridge is matched to the valve body manufacturer so the pressure-balancing function works correctly after the repair.

One pattern that shows up repeatedly in Inland Empire homes is premature fill valve failure caused by mineral buildup on the float mechanism.
