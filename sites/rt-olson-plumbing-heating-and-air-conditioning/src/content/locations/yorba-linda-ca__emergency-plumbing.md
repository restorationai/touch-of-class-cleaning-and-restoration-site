## Why Yorba Linda Properties See Emergency Plumbing Issues

Yorba Linda's residential landscape is a mix of ranch-style homes built in the 1960s and 1970s and newer hillside developments that went up during the building boom of the late 1990s and 2000s. Those older ranch homes often still carry galvanized steel supply lines that have been corroding quietly for decades — the first sign of trouble is usually a dramatic pressure drop or a pinhole burst behind drywall, not a slow drip you'd catch early. Slab foundations are common throughout the city, and the clay-heavy soil in the foothills expands and contracts with seasonal moisture changes, placing lateral stress on underground drain lines and slab penetrations over time.

The region's Santa Ana wind events compound the problem. Water heaters also take a beating: Yorba Linda's water supply carries relatively high mineral content, and sediment buildup accelerates in tank-style heaters that run hard through summer cooling seasons, shortening their effective life and increasing the risk of a sudden failure.
