## Why Riverside Kitchens See More Disposal Problems

Riverside's water hardness is measurably higher than the state average, and over months and years that mineral content coats the impeller plate and flyweights inside a disposal. The result is a unit that jams more easily — especially if fibrous scraps like artichoke leaves or celery get introduced to an already sluggish grind chamber. Add in the region's warm climate, which encourages year-round cooking and heavier kitchen use, and disposals here simply cycle through more wear than units in cooler, lower-use households.

Riverside's housing stock adds another layer. Neighborhoods with homes built in the 1950s through the 1970s often have 3-inch drain lines under the sink rather than the 4-inch lines common in newer construction. A disposal that's slightly oversized for that drain configuration will back up repeatedly, and no amount of resetting the unit will fix a fundamental drain-sizing mismatch. Identifying that before recommending a replacement unit is the kind of detail that separates a useful diagnosis from a wasted service call.

## Our Repair and Installation Process in Riverside

When we arrive, the first step is a hands-on assessment rather than an immediate swap. A disposal that hums but won't spin almost always has a jammed flyweight — something that can be cleared in minutes with the right hex key and without buying a new unit. A disposal that leaks from the bottom seal is a different story; that typically means the internal seal has failed, and repair cost versus replacement cost tips toward replacement. We walk you through that math before touching anything.

If installation is the right call, we match the unit's horsepower rating to your household's actual usage and your existing drain configuration. A ¾-horsepower unit is appropriate for most Riverside households; a full 1-horsepower model makes sense if you cook frequently or have a large family. We handle the mounting assembly, electrical connection, and drain plumbing, then run the unit through a full test cycle before we leave. Cleanup is part of the job — we're not done until the cabinet under your sink looks the way it did before we opened it.

## Local Note

Homes in Riverside built during the postwar boom — particularly in the older residential corridors closer to downtown — were frequently plumbed with galvanized steel drain lines that have narrowed significantly with decades of mineral accumulation. If your disposal drains slowly even after a repair, the problem may not be the unit at all; it may be the 2-inch galvanized stub-out connecting to the P-trap. We flag this when we see it because installing a new disposal onto a compromised drain line just moves the problem downstream. A quick camera look at the drain can save you a repeat service call.

If you're in one of Riverside's newer developments, the opposite issue sometimes appears: high-end kitchen remodels occasionally include deep farmhouse sinks with non-standard drain basket dimensions that require an adapter kit for proper disposal mounting. It's a small detail, but skipping it causes leaks at the flange within weeks.
