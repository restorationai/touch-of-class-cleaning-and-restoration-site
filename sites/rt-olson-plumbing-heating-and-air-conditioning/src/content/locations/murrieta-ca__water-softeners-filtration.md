Murrieta's tap water is some of the hardest in Southern California, drawn from a blend of Colorado River and State Water Project sources, it arrives at your faucet carrying calcium and magnesium levels that leave chalky rings in sinks, shorten water heater life, and make soap feel like it never quite rinses clean. If you've noticed scale building up on fixtures or a film on glassware coming out of the dishwasher, that's not a cleaning problem, it's a water chemistry problem. RT Olson Plumbing, Heating and Air Conditioning installs water softeners, whole-house filtration systems, and reverse osmosis units sized for Murrieta homes and the specific mineral load coming through your meter.

## Why Murrieta's Water Demands a Dedicated Treatment System

The Inland Valley's water supply has long carried high total dissolved solids (TDS), and Murrieta is no exception. The Metropolitan Water District blends imported surface water with local groundwater, and the result typically tests well above the threshold where scale damage to appliances and pipes becomes a real cost. Tankless water heaters, popular in newer Murrieta construction, are especially vulnerable: sediment and mineral buildup can void manufacturer warranties and trigger error codes within a few years of installation without a softener upstream. Whole-house carbon filtration also addresses the chloramine disinfectant that the local water district uses in place of chlorine, which affects taste and can degrade rubber seals in fixtures over time.

Murrieta's rapid growth over the past two decades means the city has a wide mix of housing vintages. Newer subdivisions built in the 2000s and 2010s often have PEX plumbing that handles hard water better than older copper, but the fixtures, appliances, and water heaters in those homes still accumulate scale at the same rate. Older homes on the western edges of the city may have copper supply lines where long-term mineral deposits narrow flow over time.

## Our Water Treatment Process in Murrieta

Every installation starts with a water test at your tap, not a generic regional estimate. We measure hardness, TDS, chloramine levels, and pH before recommending any equipment. That data drives the softener grain capacity and filter media selection, so you're not paying for more system than your household needs or under-sizing a unit that'll run regeneration cycles constantly.

For whole-house softener installation, we locate the system at the main supply line entry point, ahead of the water heater and all interior fixtures. We handle the bypass valve, drain line for regeneration discharge, and electrical connection if the unit requires it. Reverse osmosis systems are typically installed under the kitchen sink with a dedicated faucet, though we also run whole-house RO for clients who want filtered water at every outlet. Every system we install is sized with Murrieta's water hardness profile in mind, not a one-size-fits-all spec sheet.

## Equipment and Methods for Hard Water Treatment

For most Murrieta households, a salt-based ion-exchange softener remains the most effective solution for hardness, it's the technology that actually removes calcium and magnesium rather than simply conditioning or coating them. We also install salt-free conditioners for clients in HOA communities or homes where brine discharge is a concern, though we'll be straightforward with you about the difference in performance. Whole-house carbon block or catalytic carbon filters address taste, odor, and chloramine reduction. For drinking water specifically, a multi-stage reverse osmosis system under the kitchen sink delivers the cleanest result and is a common add-on in Murrieta homes where the family wants filtered water for cooking and drinking without buying bottled.

## Local Note

Some Murrieta HOA communities, particularly in master-planned developments, have CC&Rs that restrict visible exterior equipment or require that softener discharge be routed in a specific way to protect shared landscaping irrigation systems. Brine from softener regeneration can damage turf and plants if it reaches drip lines, and a few local HOAs have flagged this in their rules. Before installation, it's worth pulling your HOA documents or having us review the discharge routing plan with you so the system is compliant from day one rather than requiring a retrofit later.
