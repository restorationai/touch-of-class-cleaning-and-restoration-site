## Why Fullerton Homes See Furnace Problems When They Do

Southern California's climate creates a pattern that's easy to overlook: furnaces in Fullerton may run fewer total hours per year than units in colder climates, but that doesn't mean they age gracefully. Long idle periods allow igniter assemblies to become brittle, draft inducer motors to seize from lack of lubrication, and flame sensors to accumulate a layer of oxidation that prevents a clean light-off. When the thermostat finally calls for heat in November or December, a system that "worked fine last spring" can fail on the first cycle.

Fullerton's housing stock adds another layer of complexity. The city grew rapidly in the postwar decades, and a significant share of its single-family homes were built in the 1950s through 1970s — an era when forced-air systems were often installed in tight attic spaces or narrow utility closets not designed with future serviceability in mind. Accessing a heat exchanger or blower assembly in one of these configurations takes more time than a straightforward garage installation, and it's worth knowing that going in rather than discovering it mid-job.

## What We Check During a Furnace Repair Visit

A furnace that's blowing cold air, short-cycling, or simply not responding to the thermostat can fail for a dozen different reasons. Rather than swapping parts on a guess, the diagnostic process starts with the fundamentals:

- **Ignition system** — hot surface igniters crack with age; intermittent sparks from a failing igniter look identical to a gas supply issue until you measure resistance directly.
- **Flame sensor** — a thin layer of oxidation on the sensor rod is one of the most common causes of a furnace that lights, runs for a few seconds, and then shuts off. A careful cleaning often resolves it without any parts cost.
- **Heat exchanger** — cracks in the heat exchanger are a carbon monoxide concern. If the exchanger is compromised, we'll tell you clearly what that means for the unit's serviceability.
- **Blower motor and capacitor** — a weak start capacitor causes the blower to draw excess current and trip the limit switch, which reads as a no-heat call.
- **Flue and venting** — blocked or improperly pitched vent runs cause pressure switch faults that lock out the burner entirely.

Once the fault is isolated, we explain what we found, what the repair involves, and what it costs before any work begins.

## Local Note: Attic Furnaces and Fullerton's Postwar Tract Homes

One thing that comes up repeatedly in Fullerton's older neighborhoods is the attic-mounted furnace. Builders in the 1950s and 1960s routinely installed gas furnaces in attic spaces to preserve square footage below — a practical decision at the time that creates real challenges for modern service work. Attic temperatures in a Fullerton summer can exceed 140°F, which accelerates wear on electrical components, wiring insulation, and the furnace cabinet itself. If you have an attic-mounted unit and it's been more than a few years since anyone looked at the wiring connections or the flue collar, that's worth putting on the inspection list before heating season starts — not after the first cold snap.
