## Why Chino Homes Struggle with Indoor Air Quality

Chino's geography works against you in a few specific ways. The city sits in a basin that limits natural air circulation, and during inversion events — common from late fall through early spring — ground-level ozone and particulate matter have nowhere to go. That outdoor air finds its way inside through gaps around doors, windows, and HVAC return ducts, especially in tract homes built during Chino's rapid growth periods in the 1980s and 1990s. Those homes were constructed quickly, and their duct systems were sized for basic comfort, not filtration efficiency.

Chino also has a long agricultural and dairy history. While many of the dairy operations have relocated westward over the decades, the fine clay soils they left behind still get disturbed by grading, landscaping, and wind. That particulate load — combined with vehicle emissions from the nearby 60 and 71 freeways — means the air coming into your HVAC system carries a heavier burden than it might in a coastal community with regular marine airflow.

Finally, homes in Chino that were built before tighter energy codes took effect often have attic insulation and ductwork that has degraded or been disturbed by pest activity. Leaky ducts pull air from unconditioned attic spaces — air that can carry fiberglass particles, dust mite debris, and mold spores — directly into your living areas.

## Our Indoor Air Quality Services Process in Chino

Every engagement starts with an honest assessment of what's actually in your air and where it's coming from. We look at your existing HVAC filtration, measure airflow at registers, and inspect accessible ductwork for leaks or contamination. From there, the right solution depends on what we find.

**HVAC filtration upgrades** are often the first step. Many Chino homes are running 1-inch fiberglass filters that capture large debris but let fine particulates pass straight through. Upgrading to a properly fitted high-MERV media filter — or a whole-house electronic air cleaner installed in the air handler — captures the smaller particles that irritate airways without choking off airflow the way an ill-fitted thick filter can.

**UV air purifiers for HVAC systems** address a different problem: biological contaminants. UV-C lamps mounted in the air handler or duct system neutralize bacteria, mold spores, and viruses before they circulate through the home. In Chino's climate, where air conditioning runs heavily from May through October, the evaporator coil stays wet for long stretches — a condition that encourages mold growth on the coil itself. A UV system addresses that directly.

**Whole-home humidifier installation** matters more here than many homeowners expect. The Santa Ana wind events that hit Chino in fall and winter can drop indoor relative humidity into the teens, which dries out mucous membranes and makes respiratory irritation worse. A bypass or fan-powered humidifier tied into the HVAC system maintains a consistent 35–50% RH without the maintenance burden of portable units.

**Indoor air quality testing** gives you a baseline measurement of particulate levels, VOCs, carbon dioxide, and humidity before and after any equipment installation, so the improvement is documented rather than assumed.
