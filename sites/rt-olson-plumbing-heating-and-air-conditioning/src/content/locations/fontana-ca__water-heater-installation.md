## Why Fontana's Water Conditions Accelerate Water Heater Wear

The San Bernardino Valley's water supply carries elevated hardness levels, typically measured in grains per gallon well above what most water heater manufacturers consider ideal for long tank life. That mineral load attacks anode rods faster, shortens the effective lifespan of glass-lined tanks, and forces tankless heat exchangers to work harder between descaling services. Homes built during Fontana's rapid expansion periods, particularly the tract developments that spread west toward the 15 freeway corridor and east toward the foothills, were often plumbed with standard 40- or 50-gallon gas tanks sized for smaller households. Those units are now aging out, and many are undersized for the families living in them today.

Gas service in this part of San Bernardino County is delivered by SoCalGas, and proper BTU matching to your existing gas line size matters during any replacement. A tankless unit that demands more gas flow than the supply line can deliver will short-cycle and underperform, something that only shows up after installation if the line sizing isn't checked first. Every installation RT Olson performs includes a gas pressure and flow verification before the new unit is commissioned.

## Tank vs. Tankless: What Makes Sense for a Fontana Home

The tankless vs. tank decision comes down to a few Fontana-specific factors: household size, existing gas line capacity, available installation space, and how hard you want to work against the local water chemistry going forward.

**Tank water heaters**, the 40- and 50-gallon units most Fontana homes already have, are straightforward replacements with lower upfront cost. A 50-gallon gas water heater install typically fits within a morning's work, and the existing gas, water, and flue connections usually transfer cleanly. For households that aren't running multiple showers simultaneously, a properly sized tank unit with a quality anode rod is a reliable, cost-effective choice.

**Tankless water heaters** deliver hot water on demand and eliminate standby heat loss, which adds up in Fontana's warmer months when the ambient temperature in a garage-mounted unit's space is already elevated. The tradeoff is a higher installation cost, partly equipment, partly the labor involved in upgrading the gas line to the larger diameter most whole-home tankless units require, and sometimes adding a dedicated 120V outlet for the ignition system. Hard water also means committing to annual descaling; skipping that service shortens a tankless unit's life significantly in this region.

RT Olson carries both tank and tankless options and will walk through the numbers with you before any equipment is ordered.

## Our Water Heater Installation Process in Fontana

Installations follow a consistent sequence regardless of which unit you choose:

1. **Assessment**, We inspect the existing unit, measure the space, check gas line sizing, verify the flue or venting path (or plan for direct-vent or power-vent routing on a tankless), and confirm the cold water inlet pressure.
2. **Permit coordination**, San Bernardino County requires a permit for water heater replacements. RT Olson pulls the permit; you don't chase paperwork.
3. **Removal and installation**, The old unit is drained, disconnected, and hauled away. The new unit is set, connected, and leak-tested before we leave.
4. **Commissioning**, We light the pilot or power up the ignition, verify the temperature setting (120°F is the standard safe delivery temperature), and confirm hot water is reaching fixtures throughout the home.
5. **Inspection scheduling**, We coordinate the county inspection so the permit closes properly.

The full process for a like-for-like tank replacement typically runs a few hours. A tankless conversion takes longer, particularly if gas line work is involved.

## Local Note: Garage Installations and Fontana's Summer Heat

The majority of Fontana homes store their water heater in an attached garage, and garage temperatures in the summer regularly push well past 100°F in this part of the Inland Empire. That ambient heat affects tankless units differently than tanks: the combustion air intake pulls in hotter air, which can affect efficiency ratings that were calculated at standard temperatures. It also means the unit's electronics and venting components need to be rated for high-ambient environments. When we spec a tankless unit for a Fontana garage installation, we account for that thermal load rather than defaulting to whatever the manufacturer's standard recommendation is for a climate-controlled utility closet.
