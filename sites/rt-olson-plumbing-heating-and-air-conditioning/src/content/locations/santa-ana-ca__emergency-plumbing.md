## Why Santa Ana Properties See Emergency Plumbing Issues

Santa Ana's housing stock tells the story of Orange County's postwar boom. A large share of the city's single-family homes and duplexes were built between the late 1940s and early 1970s on concrete slab foundations — no crawl space, no basement, just a thin layer of concrete between your living room and the supply lines running underneath it. Galvanized steel pipe, standard in that era, corrodes from the inside out. You won't see it happening. The first sign is often a sudden drop in water pressure, a warm spot on the floor, or a water bill that jumps without explanation.

Orange County's soil also plays a role. Expansive clay soils common throughout the Santa Ana basin shift seasonally — contracting in the dry summer months and swelling when winter rains arrive. That movement stresses underground lines at joints and elbows, which is exactly where older pipe connections are already weakest. Add the region's hard water, which accelerates mineral buildup inside galvanized and copper supply lines, and you have a combination that makes emergency calls more likely the older the property is.
