## Why Norco Properties See Emergency Plumbing Issues

Norco sits in a transitional zone between the Santa Ana River basin and the drier inland foothills, and the soil conditions that come with that position are hard on underground plumbing. Clay-heavy soil expands when it absorbs water and contracts sharply during dry stretches — a cycle that stresses buried supply and drain lines year after year. Homes on larger parcels frequently have long lateral runs from the street to the structure, giving that movement more pipe to work on. Add the region's periodic freeze events — rare but real, especially in January when overnight temperatures in the inland valleys can dip into the mid-20s — and you have conditions that produce sudden failures in pipes that looked fine the season before.

The housing stock adds another layer. A significant portion of Norco's residential properties were built between the late 1950s and the early 1980s, when galvanized steel supply lines were standard. Galvanized pipe corrodes from the inside out, narrowing over decades until pressure spikes or a joint finally gives. When that happens, the failure is rarely at a convenient spot — it's often inside a wall, under a slab, or deep in a yard where the line runs to a detached structure like a barn or workshop.
