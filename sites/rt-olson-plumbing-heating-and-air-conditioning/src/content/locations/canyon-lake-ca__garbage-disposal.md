## Why Canyon Lake Homes See Garbage Disposal Problems

Canyon Lake's housing stock skews toward the 1970s and 1980s, the era when builders routinely undersized kitchen drain lines at ¾-inch or even 1½-inch diameter rather than the 2-inch lines that modern disposal manufacturers recommend. That mismatch means even a mid-range disposal can overwhelm the drain, backing up food waste into the sink basin and putting stress on the unit's motor every time it runs. Older flange gaskets in those homes have also had decades to harden and shrink, which is the most common reason a disposal develops a slow drip at the sink collar, the unit itself may be fine, but the seal between the disposal and the sink has simply given out.

The lake-adjacent climate adds another layer. Humidity near the water encourages mineral deposits inside the grinding chamber, and homeowners who run the disposal infrequently, a vacation home pattern that's common in Canyon Lake, often find the impeller plate has corroded or seized. A disposal that sits unused for weeks and then hums without grinding is almost always a seized turntable, not a failed motor.

## Our Garbage Disposal Repair and Installation Process in Canyon Lake

Every call starts with a diagnosis before any part is ordered or any unit is pulled. A humming disposal that won't spin usually just needs the reset button and a manual quarter-turn with an Allen wrench at the bottom of the unit, a five-minute fix that saves a replacement. A disposal that leaks from the bottom (not the collar) has a failed internal seal and is almost always more cost-effective to replace than repair. We walk you through the difference on-site so you're making an informed call, not guessing.

For replacements, we match the new unit to your actual drain line diameter and your household's usage pattern. A Canyon Lake home used as a primary residence with regular cooking warrants a ¾-horsepower or 1-horsepower unit with stainless grinding components. A seasonal or part-time residence can often run well on a quality ½-horsepower unit. We also check the electrical outlet under the sink, older Canyon Lake kitchens sometimes have a switched outlet that's wired incorrectly or a cord that doesn't reach the new unit's junction box, and we correct that before we leave.
