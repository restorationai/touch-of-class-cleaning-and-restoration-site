## Why Chino Properties Experience Emergency Plumbing Problems

Chino's water supply carries some of the highest mineral content in the Inland Empire. That hardness — measurable as calcium and magnesium carbonate — deposits scale inside supply lines, water heaters, and fixture valves over time. The buildup narrows the effective bore of older galvanized pipes until pressure spikes can crack them outright. Homes built during Chino's rapid residential expansion in the 1980s and 1990s often have original copper or galvanized supply lines that are now approaching or past their expected service life, and the clay-heavy soils common across the area shift seasonally as moisture levels change — placing lateral stress on underground lines that can open hairline fractures into full separations.

Slab construction is widespread in Chino, which means many supply and drain lines run beneath a concrete foundation rather than through an accessible crawl space. A slab leak doesn't announce itself loudly; it often shows up first as an unexplained spike on your Cucamonga Valley Water District bill, a warm spot on a tile floor, or the faint sound of running water when every fixture is closed. By the time water surfaces at a wall or baseboard, the damage beneath the slab can already be substantial.
