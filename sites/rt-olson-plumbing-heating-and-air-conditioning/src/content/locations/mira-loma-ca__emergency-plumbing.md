## Why Mira Loma Properties See Emergency Plumbing Issues

The Inland Empire's water is notoriously hard, calcium and magnesium deposits build up inside supply lines and water heater tanks over years, narrowing flow and stressing fittings until something finally gives. Mira Loma's older residential neighborhoods, many of which were developed in the 1960s and 1970s, still carry galvanized steel supply lines in places. Galvanized pipe corrodes from the inside out, so the exterior can look fine while the interior has scaled down to a fraction of its original diameter. When that pipe finally cracks, the failure is sudden and the water loss is immediate.

The area's expansive clay soils add another layer of risk for slab-on-grade homes. Clay swells when wet and contracts sharply during dry stretches, and Mira Loma's hot, dry summers followed by periodic heavy rain create exactly that cycle. Over time, that ground movement stresses the copper or PVC lines embedded in or running beneath the slab, producing pinhole leaks or outright breaks that show up as warm spots underfoot, unexplained spikes on a Riverside Public Utilities water bill, or soft patches in flooring.
