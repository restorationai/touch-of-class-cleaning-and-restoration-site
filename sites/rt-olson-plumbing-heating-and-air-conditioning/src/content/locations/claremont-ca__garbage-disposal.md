## Why Claremont Homes See Garbage Disposal Problems

Claremont sits at the foot of the San Gabriel Mountains, and the Inland Empire's hard water is a real factor here. The municipal supply carries elevated mineral content, and over time that calcium and magnesium buildup coats the inside of disposal grinding chambers, stiffens the impeller plate, and accelerates wear on the motor bearings. What starts as a slow grind becomes a full jam, or that telltale hum where the motor runs but nothing moves.

The city's housing stock adds another layer. Claremont's older neighborhoods, particularly the blocks close to the Claremont Colleges and the Village, include homes built in the mid-20th century with cast-iron drain lines that are narrower than modern PVC. A disposal upgrade that works fine in a newer build can overwhelm an older 1.5-inch branch drain if the unit's horsepower outpaces what the drain can carry. Getting the motor size right for the existing plumbing isn't optional; it's the difference between a clean install and a recurring clog.

## Our Garbage Disposal Repair and Installation Process in Claremont

When we arrive, the first step is a quick diagnosis before any parts leave the truck. A humming disposal that won't spin usually means a jammed impeller, often fixable on the spot with a hex key reset and some manual clearing of whatever locked it up. A disposal that's completely silent points to a tripped thermal overload or a failed switch, both of which we carry stock for. Leaks get traced to one of three places: the sink flange at the top, the side dishwasher inlet, or the discharge outlet at the bottom, each has a different fix and a different urgency level.

For installations, we match the unit's horsepower to your household's actual use and to the drain configuration under your sink. Homes with older plumbing get a closer look at the P-trap and branch drain before we commit to a motor size. We handle the full swap, disconnecting the old unit, resetting the mounting assembly if the flange has corroded, connecting the dishwasher drain knockout if needed, and testing under load before we leave.
