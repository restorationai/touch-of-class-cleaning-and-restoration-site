## Why Murrieta Properties See Emergency Plumbing Issues

Murrieta sits in the Temecula Valley, where expansive clay soils shift with the seasons. That ground movement works against underground supply lines and sewer laterals in ways that don't show up until a pipe fails. Homes built during the city's rapid growth period in the late 1980s and through the 1990s are now old enough that original galvanized steel supply lines are corroding from the inside out, the water pressure looks fine until a fitting gives way behind a wall. Newer master-planned communities often have longer interior runs of PEX or CPVC that can develop pinhole leaks at fittings when water hammer goes unaddressed for years.

Murrieta's hard water, typical of the region's imported and groundwater blend, accelerates mineral buildup inside water heaters and at shutoff valves, which means valves that haven't been exercised in years may not close fully when you need them most. That's a problem at 2 a.m. when you're trying to stop a leak and the main won't cooperate.
