## Why French Valley Properties See Emergency Plumbing Issues

French Valley's growth curve tells the story. The area expanded rapidly through the late 1990s and into the 2000s, which means a large share of the housing stock is now approaching or past the 20-to-25-year mark, the window when original builder-grade fixtures, flex supply lines, and water heater tanks start to fail in earnest. Polybutylene and early CPVC installations from that era are particularly prone to sudden failure after years of cycling through the Inland Valley's temperature swings.

The region's water also plays a role. Southwest Riverside County is served by water that carries high mineral content, and the scale buildup inside water heater tanks and supply valves accelerates wear. That rumbling sound a tank makes when sediment has layered up on the heating element isn't just noise, it's a warning that the tank wall is working harder than it should. Left unaddressed, the result is often a failed pressure-relief valve or a seam that gives way without much additional notice.

Slab-on-grade construction is common throughout French Valley, and slab leaks are among the more disruptive emergencies we handle here. ## Local Note

One thing that catches homeowners off guard in French Valley's newer subdivisions: many of these communities were built with individual pressure-reducing valves (PRVs) set at the builder's default, and those valves have a service life. When a PRV starts to fail, it can allow line pressure to creep up well above the 80 PSI threshold, and elevated pressure is one of the leading causes of supply line failures at the fixture connections under sinks and behind toilets. If you've had more than one supply line fail in the past few years, or if your fixtures seem to run with unusual force, it's worth having the PRV tested while we're already on site for the emergency repair.
