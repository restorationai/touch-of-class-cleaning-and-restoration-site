## Why Upland Properties See Hidden Leak Problems

Upland's housing stock spans several distinct eras, and each one carries its own leak profile. Homes built in the postwar decades, common throughout the older residential corridors closer to downtown, were typically plumbed with galvanized steel supply lines. Galvanized corrodes from the inside out, narrowing and eventually pinholing without any visible warning at the wall surface. Copper lines installed through the 1970s and 1980s can suffer from formicary corrosion, a pitting pattern linked to certain soil chemistries and trace organics in the water supply. Newer construction isn't immune either: PEX and CPVC fittings under slabs can loosen at joints over years of thermal cycling, especially during the wide temperature swings between Upland's cool winter nights and triple-digit summer afternoons.

Because the San Bernardino County water table and irrigation infrastructure vary block by block across Upland's grid, underground leaks don't always surface where you'd expect them. A break on a main supply lateral may migrate along a gravel bedding layer and surface ten or twenty feet from the actual failure point, which is exactly why visual inspection alone rarely finds the source.

## Our Leak Detection Process in Upland

The goal of leak detection is to locate the failure precisely before any wall, floor, or landscape is opened, minimizing damage and keeping repair costs predictable. The process typically moves through several stages:

**Pressure isolation** narrows the leak to a specific zone, main supply, a branch line, or the irrigation system, by shutting valves and watching pressure drop on a calibrated gauge. This alone tells us whether we're chasing a slab leak, an underground lateral, or a concealed wall leak.

**Electronic amplification** uses ground microphones and acoustic listening equipment to detect the frequency signature of water escaping under pressure. Concrete slabs and compacted soil transmit sound well, and trained ears with the right equipment can pinpoint a slab leak to within inches without cutting.

**Thermal imaging** identifies temperature differentials caused by moisture migration, particularly useful in Upland homes where a slab leak may have been running long enough to saturate the subfloor above it, or where a wall cavity leak has wicked into insulation.

**Tracer gas** is reserved for situations where acoustic methods are inconclusive, typically deep underground laterals or lines running beneath hardscape. A non-toxic hydrogen-nitrogen blend is introduced into the line; a surface sensor detects where it escapes.

We document findings with photos and a written report before any excavation or access is recommended, so you have a clear picture of what's happening and where.
