Pomona sits in the eastern San Gabriel Valley where winter nights can drop into the low 40s, cold enough that a furnace that's been coasting through mild falls suddenly gets pushed hard when temperatures actually bite. That gap between "barely used" and "running all night" is exactly when heat exchangers crack, igniters burn out, and blower motors that haven't been stressed in months decide to quit. When your heat stops working on one of those cold January nights, RT Olson Plumbing, Heating and Air Conditioning is available around the clock to diagnose and repair it.

## Why Pomona Homes See Furnace Problems

Pomona's housing stock spans a wide range, mid-century ranch homes built in the 1950s and 60s, older craftsman-era properties closer to downtown, and newer construction toward the city's edges. That means the furnaces in this city are equally varied: some are aging gas units that haven't been serviced in years, others are newer systems installed in homes that were flipped or remodeled. The common thread is that Southern California homeowners tend to run furnaces infrequently, which creates its own set of problems. Dust and debris accumulate in burner assemblies during the off-season. Pilot assemblies and flame sensors corrode from sitting idle. Condensate drains in high-efficiency units clog with algae because they don't flush regularly enough. When the first real cold snap arrives, all of that deferred wear shows up at once.

Pomona also sits in a basin prone to temperature inversions that trap particulates close to the ground. That air quality dynamic means furnace filters load up faster than in cleaner-air climates, and a clogged filter is one of the most common reasons a furnace blows cold air or shuts down on a limit switch.

## Our Furnace Repair Process in Pomona

Every repair call starts with a full system diagnostic rather than a guess. We check the thermostat signal, inspect the igniter and flame sensor, test the heat exchanger for cracks, verify the blower motor and capacitor, and confirm the gas valve is opening and closing correctly. We also pull the filter and check static pressure, because a furnace that's starved for airflow will trip its high-limit switch and shut off repeatedly, mimicking a more serious failure.

For older gas furnaces common in Pomona's mid-century neighborhoods, we pay particular attention to the heat exchanger. A cracked exchanger is a carbon monoxide risk, and California's Title 24 energy standards and local mechanical codes require that compromised exchangers be replaced, not patched. If we find a crack, we'll explain your options clearly: repair or replacement, with honest guidance on which makes financial sense given the unit's age.

Once we identify the fault, we carry a broad inventory of common replacement parts, igniters, flame sensors, capacitors, pressure switches, and control boards, so most repairs are completed in a single visit.

## Local Note

One pattern worth knowing: many of Pomona's older homes, particularly the craftsman and Spanish-revival properties built before the 1960s, were originally heated with floor or wall furnaces, and at some point had central forced-air systems retrofitted into attic or closet spaces. Those retrofit installations sometimes used undersized ductwork or non-standard flue configurations that don't meet current mechanical code. If your home has had any remodeling work done and the furnace is behaving erratically, it's worth having the duct system and venting inspected as part of the repair visit, a poorly vented furnace will short-cycle or trip safety switches no matter how good the burner assembly is.
