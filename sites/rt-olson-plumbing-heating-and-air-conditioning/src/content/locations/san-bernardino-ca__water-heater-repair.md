## Why San Bernardino Properties See Water Heater Problems Early

The water coming through San Bernardino's distribution system carries a high mineral load, calcium and magnesium that settle out of solution the moment water is heated. Inside a tank-style water heater, that sediment accumulates on the bottom, forcing the burner to work harder and longer just to reach the same temperature. The rumbling or popping sound you hear from a water heater that's been in service for several years is sediment cracking and shifting as the burner cycles, a sign the unit is under serious strain.

Older residential neighborhoods throughout the city, including areas with housing stock dating to the postwar boom of the late 1940s and 1950s, often have water heaters installed in tight utility closets or garages with minimal clearance. That can complicate access for repairs and, in some cases, means a replacement unit needs to be a different form factor than what was originally installed. San Bernardino's climate also plays a role: the inland heat means garage-installed water heaters work in ambient temperatures that swing dramatically between summer afternoons and winter nights, stressing the expansion tank and pressure relief valve more than coastal installations.

Every service call starts with a full diagnostic before any parts are ordered or quoted.
