## Why Chino Hills Properties See Drain Cleaning Issues

The expansive clay soils common throughout the Chino Hills hillsides are the root of many recurring drain problems, literally. Clay soil contracts in dry summers and swells after winter rains, and that constant ground movement can shift drain pipe joints just enough to let fine roots from ornamental trees and shrubs work their way inside. Once a root mass establishes itself in a line, it catches every passing wad of grease, soap scum, and food debris until the pipe is effectively sealed.

Chino Hills also developed rapidly through the 1980s and 1990s, meaning a large share of the housing stock is now 30 to 40 years old. The ABS plastic drain lines installed during that era are durable, but decades of use leave them prone to scale buildup and, in some cases, joint separation where the original glue has aged. Homes built on steeper lots, common throughout the city's hillside tracts, sometimes have longer, more complex drain runs with multiple bends that trap debris more readily than simpler layouts on flat ground.

## Our Drain Cleaning Process in Chino Hills

Every drain cleaning call starts with a conversation about what you're seeing: is it one fixture or several? Slow drain or complete backup? That triage tells us whether we're dealing with a localized clog or a main line obstruction before we pull a single tool from the truck.

For most kitchen sink clogs and shower drain backups, a professional cable auger (rooter) clears the immediate blockage and lets us feel what we're working through, grease, roots, or a foreign object each have a distinct resistance. When the clog is deep in the main line or keeps coming back, hydro jetting is the right call. A hydro jet delivers a high-pressure water stream that scours pipe walls clean rather than just punching a hole through the clog, which is why it's especially effective on the root intrusions and grease accumulation common in Chino Hills drain lines.

When the cause isn't obvious or a line has backed up more than once in the same season, we run a camera inspection so you can see exactly what's inside the pipe, root mass, offset joint, or buildup, before deciding on a repair approach. That step saves homeowners from paying for repeated service calls on a problem that needs a structural fix, not just another cleaning.
