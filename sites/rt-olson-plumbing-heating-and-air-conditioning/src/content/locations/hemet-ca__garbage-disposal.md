## Why Hemet Homes See More Disposal Problems

Hemet sits in the San Jacinto Valley at an elevation that brings real seasonal swings, hot, dry summers followed by cooler winters that can stress plumbing connections and gaskets. The city's water supply carries elevated hardness levels, and that mineral load gradually coats the inside of a disposal's grinding ring. The result is reduced grinding efficiency, more frequent jams, and seals that dry out and crack sooner than the manufacturer's rated lifespan suggests.

Hemet's housing stock adds another layer. A significant portion of the city's single-family homes were built during the 1970s and 1980s tract-home boom, when half-horsepower disposals were standard and kitchen drain lines were often run at shallower slopes than modern code prefers. Replacing an old unit in one of these homes sometimes reveals corroded mounting assemblies or undersized drain connections that need attention before a new disposal can be seated properly.

## Our Repair and Installation Process in Hemet

Every service call starts with a diagnosis, not a sales pitch. Once the unit is mounted and wired, we run a full test cycle and check the P-trap connection for any weeping before we pack up.

## Local Note

Hemet's water district draws from a blend of groundwater and imported sources, and the hardness level fluctuates more than homeowners expect, noticeably higher in late summer when groundwater reliance increases. That seasonal spike in mineral content is exactly when disposal seals and splash guards tend to fail, because the deposits accumulate faster during those months. If your unit starts leaking or grinding rough in August or September, the timing is rarely a coincidence. Running cold water through the disposal for a full 30 seconds after each use helps flush mineral-laden water through the drain line rather than letting it sit against the rubber components.
