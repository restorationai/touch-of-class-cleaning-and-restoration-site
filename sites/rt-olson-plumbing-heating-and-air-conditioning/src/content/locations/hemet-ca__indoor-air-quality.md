Hemet sits in the San Jacinto Valley at roughly 1,600 feet elevation, where summer temperatures regularly push past 105°F and the surrounding desert landscape kicks fine particulate dust into the air on a near-daily basis. That combination, extreme heat cycling, low humidity in summer, and airborne dust from the valley floor, puts unusual pressure on the air inside your home. If your family is sneezing more than they used to, noticing musty odors when the HVAC kicks on, or waking up with dry throats and scratchy eyes, the air inside your house may be working against you. RT Olson Plumbing, Heating and Air Conditioning provides indoor air quality services calibrated to what Hemet homes actually deal with.

## Why Hemet Homes Struggle with Indoor Air Quality

The San Jacinto Valley's geography creates a natural bowl effect that traps particulates during temperature inversions, a pattern familiar to anyone who has watched a hazy morning settle over the valley before the afternoon winds pick up. Hemet's housing stock includes a significant share of homes built in the 1970s and 1980s, when duct systems were sized and sealed to standards that don't hold up well today. Aged flex ductwork develops micro-tears and loose connections over time, pulling unconditioned attic air, loaded with insulation fibers, rodent dander, and accumulated dust, directly into your living spaces every time the blower runs.

The region also experiences a wide humidity swing across seasons. Summers are dry enough to crack wood and irritate airways; winter rains can push interior humidity up quickly in homes with inadequate vapor management. That seasonal seesaw creates conditions where dust mites thrive in winter and airborne particulates spike in summer. Neither extreme is comfortable, and neither is healthy over the long term.

## Our Indoor Air Quality Services Process in Hemet

Every engagement starts with indoor air quality testing, not a visual inspection, but actual measurement of particulate levels, humidity, and in applicable situations, VOC concentrations. That baseline tells us whether the problem is filtration, humidity control, biological contamination in the duct system, or some combination of the three.

From there, the path typically runs through one or more of the following:

- **HVAC filtration upgrades**: Swapping a standard 1-inch filter slot for a high-MERV media cabinet that captures fine dust and pollen without starving your system of airflow. Many Hemet homes with older air handlers need a static-pressure check before we upsize filtration, because undersized blower motors can't compensate for a restrictive filter.
- **UV air purifier for HVAC**: A germicidal UV lamp installed inside the air handler targets mold spores and bacteria that accumulate on the evaporator coil, a common issue in valley climates where coils cycle between wet and dry conditions repeatedly through the cooling season.
- **Whole house air purifier**: For homes with more severe particulate loads, a whole-home electronic air cleaner or polarized media system installed in the return plenum treats every cubic foot of air the system moves.
- **Whole home humidifier installation**: A bypass or fan-powered humidifier tied to the furnace keeps interior humidity in the 35–50% range through dry stretches, reducing static electricity, protecting wood floors and cabinetry, and making the air feel less harsh on airways.

After installation, we verify airflow and system operation before we leave. If your existing ductwork has obvious leakage points, we'll note them, because even the best air purifier can't overcome a duct system that's pulling in attic air.

## Equipment and Methods We Use

The equipment we specify for Hemet homes is chosen with the valley's dust load in mind. Standard 1-inch fiberglass filters are essentially useless against the fine clay-and-silica particulates that blow in from the surrounding desert terrain. We work with high-MERV pleated media systems and whole-house electronic air cleaners that are rated for the particle sizes actually present in this region.

For UV treatment, we install systems with dual-lamp configurations on the supply and return sides of the coil when the contamination history warrants it. Single-lamp installs are common and effective for maintenance; dual-lamp setups are better suited to systems that have gone multiple seasons without coil cleaning.

Humidification equipment is sized to your home's square footage and the typical outdoor dew point range for the San Jacinto Valley, not a generic national average. Oversized humidifiers in a valley climate can introduce their own problems, so the spec matters.

## Local Note

Hemet's older neighborhoods, particularly those developed in the 1970s near the city's original commercial core, frequently have homes where the original return-air system was a single central hallway grille pulling from one end of the house. That single-return design was code-compliant at the time but creates significant air quality problems today: rooms at the far end of the house get poor circulation, humidity stratifies, and the HVAC filter only sees a fraction of the air the system is supposed to condition. When we encounter this layout, we recommend a duct system assessment alongside any air quality upgrade, because adding a whole-house purifier to a poorly balanced system is like putting a high-end water filter on a pipe with a leak.
