## Why Bloomington Properties See AC Repair Issues

Bloomington's geography works against air conditioning equipment in ways that aren't obvious until something fails. The inland valley traps heat and dust, and the fine particulate matter that blows in from surrounding agricultural land and the nearby 215 and 10 freeway corridors loads up air filters and coil surfaces faster than coastal climates. A coil that might stay clean for a full season in a milder area can develop enough buildup in a Bloomington summer to choke airflow and trigger high-pressure lockouts within a few months.

The housing stock adds another layer. Much of Bloomington developed rapidly during the 1970s and 1980s, and the original ductwork in those homes, often flex duct stapled through attic trusses, degrades over decades of extreme heat cycling. Attic temperatures in this part of San Bernardino County can exceed 150°F in midsummer, which accelerates duct liner deterioration and causes joints to separate. When ducts leak into the attic instead of into the living space, the system runs constantly without ever cooling the rooms below, a pattern that looks like a refrigerant problem but is actually a distribution failure.

Refrigerant leaks are genuinely common here too. The thermal expansion and contraction that comes with swinging between cool desert nights and scorching afternoons stresses copper line sets at brazed joints and flare connections over time. If your system is low on refrigerant, adding more without finding and repairing the leak is a short-term fix that leaves the underlying problem in place.

## Our AC Repair Process in Bloomington

Every repair call starts with a full system diagnostic rather than a guess. The technician checks static pressure across the air handler, measures supply and return temperatures, inspects the evaporator and condenser coils, and reads refrigerant pressures against the manufacturer's specifications for the outdoor conditions at the time of the visit, because ambient temperature in Bloomington at 3 p.m. in July is a real variable that affects those readings.

If refrigerant is low, the leak gets located before anything is added. Electronic leak detection finds the source at line set connections, the evaporator coil, and the condenser coil. Once the leak is repaired and pressure-tested, the system is evacuated and recharged to the correct weight per the equipment nameplate, not estimated, not topped off.

For duct-related issues, the technician can perform a duct blaster test to quantify leakage before recommending repairs, so you know exactly what you're dealing with and what the fix will accomplish.
