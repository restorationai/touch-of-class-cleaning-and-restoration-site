San Bernardino sits in a basin where the Santa Ana winds push dry desert air through the Cajon Pass for weeks at a stretch, then pivot to trap wildfire smoke and particulate matter against the San Gabriel and San Bernardino mountains. That cycle, desiccating wind followed by stagnant, smoky air, puts indoor air quality in a different category here than it does in coastal Southern California. If your HVAC system is still running a standard one-inch filter, it was not designed for what the Inland Empire's air actually delivers.

## Why San Bernardino Properties See Air Quality Challenges

The geography that makes San Bernardino distinctive also makes it one of the harder places in California to maintain healthy indoor air. The South Coast Air Quality Management District (SCAQMD) regularly issues Spare the Air alerts for this region, and wildfire seasons have grown longer, meaning fine particulate matter (PM2.5) finds its way into homes even when windows stay closed. Older duct systems, common in the city's mid-century housing stock, develop gaps and flex-duct collapses over time that pull attic air, insulation fibers, and rodent dander directly into living spaces. On top of that, the region's low relative humidity during Santa Ana events can drop indoor moisture levels to the point where respiratory membranes dry out and airborne viruses travel more efficiently, a problem a whole home humidifier installation addresses directly.

## Our Indoor Air Quality Services Process in San Bernardino

Every engagement starts with indoor air quality testing, not a sales pitch. We use calibrated particle counters and humidity sensors to establish a baseline: what's actually in the air, at what concentration, and where in the home it's worst. That data drives the recommendation, whether that's an HVAC filtration upgrade to a MERV-13 or higher media filter, a UV air purifier for HVAC that targets biological contaminants at the air handler, or a whole house air purifier installed in the return plenum.

For homes with documented humidity swings, common in San Bernardino's desert-adjacent climate, we size and install whole home humidifier systems that integrate with the existing thermostat so humidity stays in the 35–50% range year-round without manual adjustment. Every piece of equipment we install is matched to the actual cubic footage and air-change rate of your system, not a generic "fits most homes" spec.

## Equipment and Methods We Use for Air Quality

The tools matter more than the brand names on the box. For particulate control, high-efficiency media filtration catches what standard fiberglass filters miss, including the fine particles generated by wildfire smoke. UV air purifiers mounted at the coil or in the supply plenum use germicidal ultraviolet light to neutralize mold spores, bacteria, and viruses before conditioned air reaches living spaces; the coil placement also keeps the evaporator cleaner between service visits, which matters in San Bernardino's dusty environment. For homes where duct leakage is the underlying problem, we can perform duct sealing before any filtration upgrade, otherwise you're cleaning air that's being diluted by whatever the attic is contributing.

## Local Note

San Bernardino's housing stock includes a significant number of homes built in the 1950s and 1960s with original forced-air systems that were sized for much lower insulation levels than current Title 24 standards require. When those homes get retrofitted with better insulation and tighter windows, the air exchange rate drops sharply, which is good for energy bills but can concentrate indoor pollutants that used to leak out. If your home has been upgraded for energy efficiency in the last decade, the indoor air quality picture may have changed more than you'd expect, and a fresh air quality test is the only way to know what the new baseline actually looks like.
