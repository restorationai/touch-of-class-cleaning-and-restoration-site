## Why Rialto Properties Experience Fixture Problems

The Inland Empire's water hardness is measurable and consequential. Calcium and magnesium deposits accumulate inside toilet fill valves and flapper seats, causing the slow, hissing leak you hear at 2 a.m. that adds 20 to 30 gallons of waste per day without ever showing a visible drip. The same mineral buildup restricts aerator flow in kitchen and bathroom faucets, making low water pressure feel like a supply problem when the fix is a five-minute aerator swap or cartridge replacement.

Rialto's housing stock adds a second layer of complexity. The city grew rapidly through the 1970s and 1980s, which means a significant share of homes still carry original builder-grade fixtures, chrome-plated brass faucets, single-flush toilets rated at 3.5 gallons per flush, and tub/shower valves that predate pressure-balancing requirements. California's current plumbing code requires pressure-balancing or thermostatic shower valves on any new installation or full replacement, so a shower valve repair that crosses into replacement territory triggers a code-compliant upgrade. We carry the parts and know the threshold.

## Our Fixture Services Process in Rialto

Every visit starts with a pressure and flow check at the fixture before any parts are ordered or quoted. Rialto's municipal water pressure can run on the higher end, fluctuations in the distribution system can stress older fixture bodies and supply lines, so we verify that a new faucet or toilet won't be fighting the same pressure that damaged the last one. If pressure is the root cause, we'll say so and discuss a whole-house pressure regulator before you spend money on a fixture that will fail again.

For toilet work, we distinguish between repair and replacement clearly. A running toilet is usually a fill valve, flapper, or flush valve seat, parts that cost little and take under an hour. A cracked tank, a rocking toilet with a failed wax ring, or a 3.5-gallon-per-flush unit that's pushing your water bill higher than it should be are all cases where a WaterSense-certified 1.28-gallon replacement makes more financial sense. We walk you through both options with straight pricing before we touch anything.

Faucet and shower valve work follows the same logic: identify the valve brand and generation, source the correct OEM cartridge or rebuild kit, and test under pressure before closing the wall or cabinet.
