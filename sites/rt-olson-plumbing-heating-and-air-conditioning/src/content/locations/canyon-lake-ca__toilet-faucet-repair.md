## Why Canyon Lake Fixtures Wear Out Faster

The water hardness in this part of Riverside County is the single biggest factor most homeowners don't account for. Hard water leaves scale deposits on flappers, inside fill valves, and around the seats of ball-type and cartridge faucets. A toilet that seems to run intermittently is often a flapper that can no longer form a clean seal because mineral buildup has roughened the seat surface. A kitchen faucet that drips even after you've replaced the cartridge twice is frequently suffering from the same problem, the seat itself is etched. Diagnosing the root cause rather than just swapping parts is the difference between a repair that holds and one that fails again in six months.

Canyon Lake's gated-community character also means a significant share of the housing stock was built in the 1970s and 1980s, when builders commonly used compression-style faucets and early ball-valve designs that are now decades past their service life. Replacing those fixtures with modern quarter-turn ceramic-disc valves or pressure-balanced shower cartridges is often more cost-effective than hunting for obsolete replacement parts.
