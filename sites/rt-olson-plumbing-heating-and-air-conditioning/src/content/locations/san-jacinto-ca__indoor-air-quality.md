## Why San Jacinto Homes See Elevated Indoor Air Quality Concerns

The geography here creates a particular set of challenges. The San Jacinto Valley funnels wind from multiple directions, and during dry months that wind carries dust, agricultural particulates from surrounding farmland, and wildfire smoke that can linger for days. Homes in San Jacinto tend to run their HVAC systems hard, long cooling seasons mean more hours of air recirculation, which means more opportunities for dust, mold spores, pet dander, and volatile organic compounds to accumulate in ductwork and living spaces.

Older housing stock in the area, particularly homes built before the mid-1990s that predate tighter building envelope standards, often has duct leakage that pulls unconditioned air from attics and crawl spaces directly into the living area. That attic air in a San Jacinto summer can carry insulation fibers, rodent dander, and concentrated particulates that no standard 1-inch filter is designed to capture. Even newer construction benefits from an IAQ assessment, because tighter envelopes trap pollutants rather than diluting them.

## Our Indoor Air Quality Services Process in San Jacinto

Every IAQ engagement starts with indoor air quality testing, not a sales conversation. We measure particulate levels, assess humidity, and evaluate your existing HVAC filtration before recommending anything. That baseline matters because the right solution for a 1970s stucco ranch with leaky ducts is different from what a 2010 tract home with a well-sealed envelope needs.

From there, the most common upgrades we install in San Jacinto homes fall into three categories:

- **HVAC filtration upgrades**, moving from a basic fiberglass filter to a properly sized high-MERV media filter that captures fine particulates without starving your system of airflow. Sizing matters: a filter that's too restrictive for your blower motor creates its own problems.
- **UV air purifiers for HVAC**, germicidal UV-C lamps installed in the air handler or duct system that neutralize mold spores, bacteria, and some viruses as air passes through. These work continuously without producing ozone, which is a meaningful distinction given that San Jacinto already sits in an air basin that regularly exceeds state ozone standards.
- **Whole home humidifier installation**, counterintuitive in a desert climate, but San Jacinto winters drop indoor relative humidity to levels that dry out mucous membranes, crack wood trim, and make respiratory irritation worse. A bypass or fan-powered humidifier integrated with your existing system keeps RH in the 35–50% range where most people feel and breathe better.
- **Whole house air purifiers**, standalone or integrated units that combine filtration, UV, and sometimes activated carbon to address a broader spectrum of pollutants, including the VOCs that off-gas from furniture, flooring, and cleaning products.

Something worth knowing if you're in San Jacinto: the area's combination of high heat and periodic high humidity during monsoon season, typically July through September, creates a window where mold growth inside ductwork and on evaporator coils accelerates faster than it would in a drier climate.
