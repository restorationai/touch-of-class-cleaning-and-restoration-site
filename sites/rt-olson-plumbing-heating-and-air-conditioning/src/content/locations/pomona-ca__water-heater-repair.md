## Why Pomona Homes See Water Heater Problems Earlier Than Expected

The Inland Empire's water supply travels a long way before it reaches your tap, picking up dissolved minerals along the route. In Pomona specifically, that hardness accelerates sediment accumulation inside tank-style heaters, you may hear it as a low popping or rumbling sound when the burner fires, which is actually steam escaping through a crust of calcified scale. Left alone, that layer insulates the burner from the water above it, forcing the unit to run longer, consume more gas or electricity, and ultimately overheat the tank lining.

Pomona's housing stock adds another layer of complexity. Much of the city's residential construction dates from the postwar boom through the 1970s, meaning a significant share of homes still have original or near-original water heater closets that were sized for 30- or 40-gallon tanks with minimal clearance. When a unit fails in one of these tight spaces, especially in older neighborhoods near downtown, replacement or repair requires careful attention to current California Title 24 energy standards and local seismic strapping requirements, both of which have been updated since those homes were built.

## Our Water Heater Repair Process in Pomona

When you call about no hot water or a visible leak, the first thing we do is listen, specifically for whether the problem sounds like a thermostat issue, a burner or igniter failure, a pressure relief valve discharge, or a tank breach. That triage over the phone helps us arrive with the right parts rather than making a second trip.

On-site, we start with a full visual inspection: checking the anode rod condition (often sacrificed by Pomona's hard water long before the tank itself fails), testing the thermostat settings, inspecting the dip tube for degradation, and pressure-testing the T&P valve. For gas units, we verify the thermocouple and pilot assembly before touching anything else, a weak thermocouple is one of the most common reasons a pilot won't stay lit, and it's a straightforward repair when caught early. For electric units, we test both heating elements and the high-limit switch.

If the tank itself is compromised, a pinhole leak at a weld seam, a cracked glass lining, or significant external corrosion, we'll give you an honest assessment of whether repair makes economic sense versus replacement, and we'll walk you through your options before any work begins.

## Local Note: What Pomona's Water Does to Anode Rods

Here's something that matters specifically in this part of the San Gabriel Valley: Pomona's elevated water hardness chews through magnesium anode rods significantly faster than the manufacturer's rated lifespan assumes. Most anode rods are rated for roughly six years under average water conditions, but in high-mineral environments like Pomona's, it's not unusual to find a rod that's been fully consumed in three to four years, leaving the tank wall completely unprotected. If your water heater is more than a few years old and you've never had the anode rod inspected, that's worth asking about when we're on-site. A rod swap is inexpensive; a tank replacement because the lining corroded through is not.
