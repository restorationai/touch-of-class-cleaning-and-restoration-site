## Why Montclair Properties Experience Hidden Leaks

Much of Montclair's residential stock was built between the late 1950s and the 1970s, when copper pipe was standard but joint soldering techniques and pipe sizing varied widely by crew. After fifty-plus years, those joints are subject to pinhole corrosion, especially where the water chemistry shifts seasonally through the Inland Empire's distribution system. The clay-dominant soils common throughout this part of San Bernardino County also expand and contract with moisture, and that ground movement puts lateral stress on underground supply and irrigation lines that rigid pipe simply wasn't designed to absorb indefinitely. A small underground leak can erode a void beneath a slab before it ever surfaces as a wet spot on your floor.

Homes with original cast-iron or galvanized drain lines add another layer of complexity: corrosion inside those pipes can cause slow seepage at joints that reads as a foundation moisture problem rather than a plumbing failure. Knowing which era a home was built in, and what materials were typical for that era in this part of Southern California, shapes how we approach the search.

## Our Leak Detection Process in Montclair

The process starts at the meter. If the meter dial is moving with every fixture closed, water is going somewhere it shouldn't. From there, we use acoustic listening equipment to amplify the sound of pressurized water escaping through a pinhole or joint, even through concrete, stucco, and packed soil. For underground lines, ground microphones and correlating amplifiers let us triangulate the leak location to within inches before any excavation begins.

For slab leaks specifically, we combine acoustic detection with thermal imaging when conditions allow: a pressurized hot-water line leaking under a slab will create a warm anomaly at the floor surface that a thermal camera can map. That combination, acoustic pinpointing plus thermal confirmation, dramatically narrows the area that needs to be opened, which matters in any home but especially in Montclair's older stucco-and-tile construction where cosmetic repair after a wide excavation adds real cost.

We also pressure-test isolated line segments to confirm the leak location before recommending any repair path, so you're not paying to open a slab based on guesswork.

## Local Note

Montclair's older neighborhoods contain a notable share of homes with original concrete-encased copper supply lines, a construction method common in inland Southern California tracts built before flexible sleeving became standard practice. When copper sits in direct contact with alkaline concrete over decades, the concrete slowly attacks the pipe exterior in a process called concrete-induced corrosion. The leak often starts as a pinhole that grows gradually, and because the pipe is fully encased, there's no visible drip until the water finds a path out through a crack in the slab. Homeowners sometimes notice the problem first as a warm area on a tile floor or an unexplained jump in their water bill, not as any visible water. Acoustic and thermal detection is particularly valuable in these situations because it locates the failure point without requiring the crew to open the entire slab to find it.
