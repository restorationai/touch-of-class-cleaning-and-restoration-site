## Why Murrieta Homes See AC Problems More Often Than You'd Expect

Murrieta sits in the Temecula Valley, where summer cooling loads are among the highest in Riverside County. That sustained heat, weeks of 95°F-plus days, not just a few spikes, puts residential AC systems under compressor stress that coastal climates simply don't replicate. Systems that would coast for another season in San Diego or Los Angeles are genuinely at their limits here.

The city's growth pattern compounds the issue. Large tracts of production homes built throughout the 2000s and early 2010s were fitted with builder-grade equipment that's now approaching the end of its design life. Capacitors fail first, usually showing up as a unit that hums but won't start. Refrigerant lines on those same systems, especially runs that travel through sun-exposed attic spaces, develop micro-leaks at fittings over time. When the system is low on refrigerant, it runs longer, works harder, and still can't pull the indoor temperature down to the thermostat setpoint. That's the "AC not cooling" call we get most often from Murrieta homeowners.

## What AC Repair Looks Like in Murrieta's Climate

Because the heat here is dry and persistent rather than humid and episodic, the failure modes we see most in Murrieta skew toward heat-related electrical faults and refrigerant issues rather than the drain-pan overflow and mold problems more common in humid climates.

Our diagnostic process starts at the thermostat and works outward: confirming the call for cooling is reaching the air handler, checking static pressure across the coil, measuring supply and return temperatures to calculate actual system delta-T, and then moving to the outdoor condenser to check refrigerant pressures and electrical components. In Murrieta's heat, we pay particular attention to condenser coil cleanliness, dust and debris from the valley floor accumulate on the fins and reduce heat rejection efficiency significantly. A condenser that's 30% blocked can behave like a system that's undercharged, and cleaning it before adding refrigerant saves homeowners money and prevents overcharging.

For refrigerant leak repairs, we locate the leak, recover the remaining charge, repair the fitting or line, pressure-test, and recharge to manufacturer specification, not just "top it off." A system that's been low on refrigerant for a season may also have a stressed compressor; we flag that honestly rather than just recharge and leave.
