## Why Ontario Ranch Properties See Emergency Plumbing Issues

Ontario Ranch sits on the western edge of the Chino Valley, a basin with expansive clay soils that shift measurably with seasonal wet-dry cycles. That ground movement puts stress on underground supply and drain lines in ways that aren't always obvious from inside the house. A slab leak might announce itself as a warm patch underfoot or a water bill that quietly doubles before you notice the floor is damp. New construction also tends to use PEX supply lines, which perform well but can fail at fittings if they were crimped under pressure or exposed to freeze events, and yes, Ontario Ranch does see overnight temperatures cold enough in January and February to stress uninsulated garage lines and hose bib connections.

The community's growth also means utility infrastructure is still maturing. Water pressure can run on the higher end in newer service areas, which accelerates wear on toilet fill valves, washing machine hoses, and angle stops, the small components most homeowners never think about until one of them lets go.
