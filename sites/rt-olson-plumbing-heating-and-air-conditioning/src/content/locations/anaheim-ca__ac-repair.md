## Our Air Conditioning Repair Process in Anaheim

Every repair call starts with a systematic diagnostic rather than a parts swap. A technician will check refrigerant pressure against manufacturer specifications, inspect the evaporator and condenser coils for fouling or freeze-up, test capacitors and contactors (two of the most common failure points in high-run-time climates), and verify that airflow through the air handler is actually reaching the rooms that need it.

Refrigerant work follows EPA Section 608 handling requirements — recovered refrigerant is not vented, and any system with a confirmed leak gets the leak addressed before a recharge, not after. Topping off a leaking system is a short-term fix that costs more over the season and accelerates compressor wear. If a refrigerant leak is the diagnosis, the repair includes locating the leak point, repairing or replacing the affected component, pressure-testing, and then recharging to the correct specification for your equipment.

For duct-related underperformance — common in Anaheim's older tract homes — the technician will identify whether the issue is a disconnected run, a collapsed flex duct section, or simply a register layout that predates the room additions many of these homes have accumulated over the decades.

## Local Note

One pattern that comes up repeatedly in Anaheim's older neighborhoods is the combination of a newer high-efficiency condenser paired with original 1960s air handler equipment and undersized return-air grilles. Homeowners often replace the outdoor unit when it fails but leave the indoor side untouched, which creates a mismatch — the new condenser can move refrigerant faster than the old air handler can push air across the coil. The result looks like an AC that "doesn't cool" even though the compressor is working fine. A technician who knows the local housing stock will check for this mismatch early in the diagnostic rather than chasing refrigerant or electrical faults that aren't there.
