Sitting in the San Jacinto Valley at roughly 1,600 feet elevation, the city bakes under inland heat that regularly pushes past 105°F from June through September, and an air conditioner that's struggling to keep up at noon can be completely overwhelmed by 3 p.m. When your system stops cooling or shuts down mid-afternoon, RT Olson Plumbing, Heating and Air Conditioning dispatches technicians around the clock to diagnose and repair the problem, whether it's a refrigerant leak, a failed capacitor, a frozen evaporator coil, or a condenser fan that's given out under the heat load.

## Why Hemet Properties See AC Repair Issues

The San Jacinto Valley's combination of intense dry heat, dust, and wide day-to-night temperature swings creates specific wear patterns that accelerate AC problems. Dust and fine particulate from the surrounding desert floor clog condenser coils faster than in coastal markets, a condenser running with restricted airflow works harder, runs hotter, and trips on high-pressure lockout more frequently during peak summer days.

Hemet also has a large share of single-story ranch homes and manufactured housing built during the 1970s and 1980s, many of which were fitted with original ductwork that was never sized for today's higher-SEER equipment. When a newer unit is installed in an older home without a duct audit, the system is often oversized for the existing duct network, leading to short-cycling, humidity problems, and compressor stress that shortens equipment life. Refrigerant leak repair is a common call in this housing stock, older copper line sets develop micro-cracks at flared fittings after decades of thermal expansion and contraction in the valley heat.

## Our Air Conditioning Repair Process in Hemet

Every repair visit starts with a full system diagnostic rather than a parts-swap guess. The technician checks static pressure across the air handler, measures supply and return temperatures, inspects the condenser coil and fan motor, and reads refrigerant pressures against the manufacturer's charging specification for the ambient outdoor temperature, a step that matters more in Hemet's extreme heat because charging tolerances shift significantly at 100°F+ outdoor conditions compared to the 70°F baseline many technicians default to.

If a refrigerant leak is confirmed, we locate the breach with an electronic leak detector before adding refrigerant. Simply topping off a leaking system is a short-term fix that leaves refrigerant escaping into the atmosphere and the root problem unresolved. Once the leak is repaired and pressure-tested, the system is recharged to the manufacturer's specification. Electrical components, capacitors, contactors, disconnect blocks, are inspected and replaced as needed while the system is already down, since a failing capacitor on a Hemet summer afternoon typically means a callback within days.

## Local Note

One pattern that comes up repeatedly in Hemet's older manufactured and modular homes: the condensate drain line is often routed through a belly pan that traps debris and restricts flow. When the drain backs up, the air handler's float switch shuts the system off, and the homeowner calls for an AC repair when what's actually needed is a drain flush and pan cleaning. It's a quick fix once identified, but easy to misread as a refrigerant or electrical problem if the technician doesn't check the drain pan first. In Hemet's dust-heavy environment, we treat condensate drain inspection as a standard step on every cooling system call, not an afterthought.
