## Why Montclair Homes See Furnace Problems When They Do

The Inland Empire's climate is deceptive. Summers are long and hot, so most homeowners are thinking about air conditioning from April through October. The furnace sits idle for months, sometimes the better part of a year, and that extended downtime is exactly when sediment builds up in gas valves, capacitors weaken, and flame sensors collect a film of oxidation that prevents reliable ignition. When a cold front drops overnight lows into the 30s, as they do during Santa Ana wind reversals in December and January, a furnace that hasn't been exercised in eight months is asked to run hard immediately.

Montclair's housing stock adds another layer. Many neighborhoods contain homes built in the 1960s and 1970s with original ductwork and furnaces that have been replaced once or twice but never fully upgraded. Older forced-air systems in these houses often have undersized return air paths, which causes the heat exchanger to overheat and cycle off on the high-limit switch, a symptom homeowners describe as the furnace "blowing cold air" because the blower keeps running after the burner shuts down. That's a repair call, not a thermostat issue.

## Our Furnace Repair Process in Montclair

When we arrive, the first step is a full system check before any parts are ordered or swapped.
