## Why Chino Hills Fixtures Wear Faster Than You'd Expect

The water delivered to Chino Hills homes carries a notably high mineral load, Southern California's imported water supply is well-documented for hardness, and the Inland Empire end of that system is no exception. Calcium and magnesium deposits build up inside faucet aerators, showerhead ports, and toilet fill valves over months, gradually choking flow and forcing internal components to work harder. A faucet that once poured at full pressure starts to trickle. A fill valve that once shut off cleanly starts hunting, cycling on and off at odd hours.

Beyond water chemistry, Chino Hills' housing stock matters. Much of the city developed rapidly through the late 1980s and 1990s, meaning a large share of homes are now 30-plus years old, old enough that original builder-grade fixtures are well past their design life. Compression-style faucets, older Fluidmaster fill valves, and first-generation low-flow toilets from that era are common service calls. They can be repaired in some cases, but when parts are discontinued or the fixture body itself has corroded, replacement is the smarter investment.
