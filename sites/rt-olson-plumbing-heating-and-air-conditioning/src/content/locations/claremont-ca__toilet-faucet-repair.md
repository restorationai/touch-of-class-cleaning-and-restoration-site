## Why Claremont Properties See Fixture Problems

The water delivered to Claremont homes carries a notably high mineral load, a characteristic of water drawn from the San Gabriel Basin and supplemented by imported sources. That hardness accelerates scale buildup inside supply lines, around valve stems, and on the ceramic discs inside cartridge faucets. A faucet that drips steadily isn't just annoying; at typical Claremont water rates, a slow drip from a single fixture can add measurably to a monthly bill.

Claremont's housing stock spans a wide range of eras. Older homes near the Village often have galvanized supply lines that were never replaced, and the threaded fittings on those lines can be corroded enough that a simple faucet swap becomes a supply-line repair as well. Homes built in the 1970s and 1980s frequently have plastic-body ball valves and early-generation pressure-balance shower cartridges that are now out of production, sourcing compatible parts or upgrading to a current-code cartridge is part of the job, not an afterthought.

## Our Fixture Services Process in Claremont

Every call starts with a diagnosis, not a parts list. When a toilet keeps running, the cause could be a worn flapper, a waterlogged float, a fill valve that's lost its seal, or, less obviously, a slow leak at the flush valve seat that only shows up under close inspection. We identify the actual failure before recommending a repair or replacement.

For faucet replacement, we assess the existing supply stops first. Stops that haven't moved in years can shear when turned, so we check valve condition before committing to a swap. If a stop needs replacement, we handle it in the same visit rather than leaving you with a shut-off at the meter. Shower valve repairs follow a similar logic: we identify whether the issue is the cartridge, the trim, or the valve body itself, and we carry a range of OEM and compatible cartridges in the service vehicle to avoid a second trip.

All fixture work in Claremont is performed under California Plumbing Code, which sets specific requirements for toilet flush volume and, in certain renovation contexts, for fixture efficiency ratings. We pull permits when the scope of work requires one.

## Local Note

Homes in Claremont's older neighborhoods, particularly those built before the 1960s, were often plumbed with 3/8-inch supply risers at a time when that was standard. Current faucet manufacturers design for 1/2-inch stops, so the connection requires an adapter or a riser swap. It's a small detail, but skipping it means the new faucet sits on an undersized supply and flow is restricted from day one. We stock the transition fittings and replace the risers as part of the installation, not as an add-on.

If you've noticed low flow from a recently installed faucet, or from one that's been in place for years, that mismatch is worth checking before assuming the fixture itself is the problem.
