## Why Montclair Properties See Elevated Water Treatment Needs

The Inland Empire's water supply is a blend of local groundwater and imported water, and the proportions shift seasonally. During dry stretches, utilities draw more heavily on groundwater from the Chino Basin, water that picks up dissolved minerals as it moves through the region's sedimentary geology. The result is hardness that fluctuates throughout the year rather than holding steady, which matters when sizing a softener's resin capacity and regeneration cycle.

Older homes in Montclair, many built during the postwar tract-housing boom of the 1950s and 1960s, often have galvanized steel supply lines still in service. Hard water accelerates mineral buildup inside galvanized pipe, progressively narrowing the interior diameter and reducing flow pressure. A water softener doesn't reverse existing scale, but it stops the accumulation that would otherwise force a full repiping job years sooner than necessary. If your home has galvanized supply lines, that context shapes which system we recommend and how we stage the installation.

## Our Water Softener and Filtration Installation Process in Montclair

Every installation starts with an on-site water test rather than relying on published utility averages. Hardness, total dissolved solids, pH, and chloramine levels all factor into which treatment train makes sense, a salt-based ion-exchange softener alone handles hardness, but if you're also concerned about taste, chloramines, or sediment, a whole-house carbon filter or a point-of-use reverse osmosis system at the kitchen sink may be the right pairing.

Once the equipment is selected, we identify the best entry point on your main supply line, typically near the water meter or where the line enters the structure, and confirm there's adequate space for the brine tank if you're going with a traditional softener. We pull any required permits through the City of Montclair's Building and Safety Division before work begins. After installation, we program the regeneration schedule based on your household's actual water usage and the tested hardness level, then walk you through bypass procedures and salt replenishment so you're not dependent on a service call for routine upkeep.

## Equipment and Methods We Use for Water Treatment

For hard water, we install salt-based ion-exchange softeners from manufacturers whose resin tanks are rated for the high-hardness conditions common in this part of San Bernardino County. For households with salt restrictions, a real consideration given regional water agency guidelines on brine discharge, we offer salt-free template-assisted crystallization (TAC) conditioners, which don't remove calcium and magnesium but change their structure so they don't adhere to pipe walls and fixtures.

Whole-house carbon filtration addresses chloramines, which the local water authority uses as a disinfectant in place of free chlorine. Chloramines require catalytic carbon rather than standard activated carbon, standard carbon media breaks down slowly under chloramine exposure and loses effectiveness faster than most homeowners realize. For drinking water quality at the tap, a reverse osmosis system with a remineralization stage produces clean, balanced water without the flat taste that a basic RO membrane can leave behind.

Montclair's water agency periodically adjusts its blend of groundwater and imported water in response to drought conditions and State Water Project allocations. During periods when imported water makes up a larger share of supply, total dissolved solids can actually drop noticeably, but hardness from local groundwater sources tends to spike during dry years when the basin is drawn down more aggressively.
