## Why Ontario Ranch Properties See Leak Detection Issues

Ontario Ranch sits on the eastern edge of the Chino Valley, where expansive clay soils shift seasonally with rainfall and drought cycles, a pattern that puts stress on underground supply lines and slab penetrations year after year. During Southern California's periodic atmospheric river events, the ground can absorb and then release significant moisture, causing subtle movement that stresses pipe joints. In drier stretches, that same clay contracts, which can pull fittings slightly out of alignment.

Because Ontario Ranch has been built out in large tracts over the past decade-plus, many homes share a construction vintage where PEX tubing and copper stub-outs meet in manifold systems tucked inside walls or utility closets. Manifold connections are generally reliable, but a fitting that was crimped under less-than-ideal site conditions can weep slowly for months before it shows up on a water bill. The community's relatively high baseline water pressure, common in newer developments served by regional transmission lines, can accelerate that weeping into a measurable loss.

## Our Leak Detection Process in Ontario Ranch

The goal is to find the leak without opening walls or trenching yards unnecessarily. The process starts with a pressure isolation test: shutting off fixtures zone by zone to confirm whether the loss is on the supply side, the irrigation system, or somewhere in the slab. That step alone narrows the search area considerably.

From there, electronic acoustic listening equipment picks up the frequency signature of water escaping under pressure, a sound distinct from normal pipe flow and from ambient noise. In Ontario Ranch's newer construction, where walls are typically drywall over wood framing, that signal travels cleanly and is easier to triangulate than in older stucco-and-plaster builds. For suspected slab leaks, thermal imaging can identify temperature differentials at the floor surface caused by water migrating through the concrete, giving us a second data point before any concrete is touched.

Once the leak is located with confidence, we walk the homeowner through exactly what was found, where it is, and what the repair options look like, including whether a targeted spot repair or a partial repipe makes more sense given the pipe's age and condition.

## Equipment & Methods We Use for Leak Detection

Electronic acoustic detection and thermal imaging handle the majority of cases. For underground supply lines where the pipe path isn't obvious from the manifold layout, we can introduce a tracer gas, a safe, non-toxic helium-hydrogen blend, that migrates through soil to the surface directly above the breach. A sensor wand then maps the escape point with precision that acoustic equipment alone can't always achieve in compacted or clay-heavy soil.
