## Why Sun City Fixtures Wear Out Faster Than You'd Expect

The water delivered to Sun City homes carries a notably high mineral load. That hardness doesn't just show up as white scale around faucet aerators, it builds inside valve bodies, around flapper seats, and along the small ports inside pressure-balancing shower cartridges. Over time, calcium deposits cause flappers to seat unevenly, producing the low hiss of a running toilet that can waste hundreds of gallons a week without ever showing a visible drip. Aerators clog and reduce flow to a trickle. Shower valves lose their ability to hold a consistent temperature because the cartridge can no longer move freely.

Sun City's housing stock also plays a role. A large share of the community was developed from the late 1950s through the 1970s as an active-adult retirement destination, meaning many homes are carrying original or near-original fixture rough-in configurations. Older compression-style faucets and early ball-valve designs were not engineered with today's water pressure norms in mind, and parts for some of those fixtures are increasingly difficult to source. Knowing when to repair versus when to replace, and which modern cartridge or valve body will fit an older rough-in, is where experience with this specific housing stock matters.
