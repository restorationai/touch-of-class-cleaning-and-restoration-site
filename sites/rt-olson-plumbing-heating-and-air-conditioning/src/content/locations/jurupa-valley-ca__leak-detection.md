## Why Jurupa Valley Properties Experience Hidden Leaks

Jurupa Valley is a relatively young city, incorporated in 2011, but its housing stock spans several decades. Neighborhoods developed in the 1970s and 1980s often have copper supply lines that are now reaching the age when pinhole corrosion becomes a real risk, especially in homes that have seen the hard water common throughout the Inland Empire. That mineral-heavy water slowly deposits scale inside pipes, and once a pinhole forms, the leak can run silently inside a wall or beneath a slab for weeks before it shows up on a water bill.

The region's hot, dry summers also stress irrigation lines and outdoor supply connections. Soil shrinks during drought conditions and expands again when winter rains arrive, that repeated movement puts mechanical stress on buried fittings and older PVC lateral lines. A high water bill that appears in late fall or early spring, after the first significant rain, is often the first sign that a fitting shifted and cracked during the dry months.

Jurupa Valley's mix of single-family homes on slab foundations and older ranch-style properties on raised foundations means the leak detection approach has to be calibrated to the structure. Slab leaks require acoustic listening equipment and thermal imaging to isolate the line before any concrete is opened. Raised-foundation homes allow visual inspection of exposed runs but can hide leaks inside insulated subfloor cavities.

## Our Leak Detection Process in Jurupa Valley

When RT Olson responds to a leak detection call in Jurupa Valley, the process starts with a pressure test on the supply system to confirm a leak is present and to estimate its severity. From there, we use electronic amplification equipment, acoustic correlators and ground microphones, to listen for the specific frequency signature that pressurized water makes as it escapes a pipe. This narrows the search area from a whole yard or a whole slab down to a few square feet.

For suspected slab leaks, we add thermal imaging to the acoustic work. A pressurized water line leaking beneath a concrete slab will create a temperature differential at the surface, often subtle, but visible with an infrared camera. Combining both methods dramatically reduces the number of exploratory cuts needed and protects your flooring and landscaping.

For underground irrigation or service line leaks, we trace the line with a pipe locator before applying acoustic equipment, so we know exactly where to listen. Jurupa Valley's sandy alluvial soils actually transmit the acoustic signal well, which improves detection accuracy compared to clay-heavy soils found in other parts of Riverside County.
