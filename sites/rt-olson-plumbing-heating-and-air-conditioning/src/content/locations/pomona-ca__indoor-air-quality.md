Pomona sits in the eastern San Gabriel Valley, where summer heat regularly pushes past 100°F and Santa Ana winds sweep through the Pomona Valley corridor in fall and winter, dropping humidity to single digits for days at a stretch. That combination, extreme heat cycling through your HVAC system, followed by bone-dry air events, creates indoor air conditions that are genuinely different from what homeowners in coastal Southern California deal with. If you've noticed more dust on surfaces than seems normal, worsening allergy symptoms, or air that feels thick and stale even with the AC running, the climate here is likely part of the explanation.

## Why Pomona Properties See Air Quality Challenges

The Pomona Valley's geography channels both heat and particulate matter in ways that show up inside homes. Wildfire smoke from the San Gabriel Mountains and Chino Hills drifts into the basin and gets pulled into HVAC systems through standard 1-inch fiberglass filters that were never designed to capture fine particles. During Santa Ana conditions, outdoor PM2.5 levels spike, and whatever your system is pulling in circulates through every room.

Pomona's housing stock adds another layer. The city has a significant number of homes built between the 1940s and 1970s, many with original duct systems that have never been sealed or upgraded. Older ductwork in these homes can leak conditioned air into attic spaces while simultaneously pulling in unconditioned, dusty attic air. The result is a system that's actively degrading your indoor air even while it's running. Homes in this era also frequently lack any mechanical ventilation strategy, so moisture from cooking, bathing, and occupancy has nowhere to go, leading to elevated indoor humidity levels that encourage dust mite populations and, in some cases, mold growth on window frames and in closets.

## Our Indoor Air Quality Services Process in Pomona

Every project starts with an assessment rather than a sales pitch. We look at what filtration is currently in place, measure airflow at registers, and ask about symptoms, headaches, dry throats, persistent coughing, that often point toward specific problems before any equipment is discussed.

From there, the path depends on what we find. For homes where particulate matter is the primary concern, an HVAC filtration upgrade to a high-MERV media filter or a whole-house air purifier is often the most cost-effective first step. For households with allergy or asthma concerns, a UV air purifier for the HVAC system can address biological contaminants, mold spores, bacteria, viruses, that pass through even quality mechanical filters. When the Pomona Valley's dry Santa Ana seasons leave the air inside uncomfortably arid, a whole home humidifier installation adds moisture at the source rather than relying on portable units that only treat one room at a time. We also offer indoor air quality testing for homeowners who want data before committing to equipment, particularly useful if symptoms are present but the source isn't obvious.

## Equipment and Methods We Use

The equipment we install is sized and selected for Pomona's specific climate demands, not just pulled from a standard catalog. A whole-house air purifier that works well in a mild coastal climate may be undersized for a home that runs its air handler eight months of the year. UV air purifiers are positioned within the air handler to treat air at the coil, the single wettest, most biologically active surface in your HVAC system and the one most likely to harbor mold growth in a climate with warm, humid monsoon periods.

For filtration upgrades, we assess whether your existing air handler can support a higher-MERV filter without restricting airflow. Many older systems in Pomona cannot, forcing a MERV-13 filter into a system designed for a MERV-4 can reduce airflow enough to damage the compressor over time. When that's the case, we'll discuss alternatives like media cabinets or electronic air cleaners that achieve better filtration without the static pressure penalty.

## Local Note

Pomona's older neighborhoods, particularly those with homes built before the 1970s, often have wall and ceiling cavities that were never insulated, which means the interior surfaces of exterior walls can get cold enough in winter to cause condensation. That condensation, invisible behind drywall or plaster, is one of the more common sources of musty odors that homeowners attribute to general "stuffiness" but that actually indicate localized moisture accumulation. If your home has that persistent damp smell in certain rooms during winter months, indoor air quality testing that includes moisture mapping is worth doing before investing in filtration equipment alone.
