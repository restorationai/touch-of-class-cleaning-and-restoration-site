## Why Claremont Properties See Emergency Plumbing Issues

Claremont sits at the base of the San Gabriel Mountains in the Pomona Valley, and that geography shapes how plumbing systems behave here. The soil in the foothills shifts seasonally, wetter winters followed by long dry stretches cause ground movement that stresses buried supply and drain lines. Homes built in the 1950s through the 1970s, which make up a significant share of Claremont's residential neighborhoods, often still carry original galvanized supply lines or cast-iron drain stacks that are well past their design life.

The Claremont Village area, with its older craftsman and Spanish Colonial homes, is especially prone to this pattern. Galvanized pipe corrodes from the inside out, narrowing the bore over decades until a pressure surge, a water hammer from a quickly closed valve, or a spike after a brief utility outage, causes a sudden failure. The result is water behind plaster walls, under hardwood floors, or pooling in a crawl space at 11 p.m. on a Tuesday.

The city's connection to the Foothill Municipal Water District means local water tends to run hard, with elevated mineral content. That hardness accelerates scale buildup inside water heaters and at valve seats, which is a common trigger for the kind of slow leak that turns into a flooded utility room when a corroded fitting finally gives way.
