## Why Fontana Properties See Emergency Plumbing Issues

Fontana sits on the western edge of San Bernardino County, where the soil composition shifts between sandy alluvial fill near the older flatlands and harder caliche-heavy ground as you move toward the foothills. Both create problems. Sandy soil allows water to migrate quickly when a slab leak or irrigation line fails, washing out support beneath foundations before the damage is visible inside the house. Caliche layers, on the other hand, resist drainage and can force pressurized water sideways along a pipe trench rather than downward, meaning a leak that starts near one wall can surface ten feet away.

Fontana's housing stock adds another layer of complexity. The city grew in distinct waves: older ranch-style homes built in the postwar boom through the 1960s, a wave of tract development through the 1980s and '90s, and then the large master-planned communities that came in the 2000s. Homes from the earlier eras often still have galvanized steel supply lines that have corroded from the inside out, they look intact at the shutoff valve and fail catastrophically at a fitting behind drywall. Newer tract homes are predominantly copper or PEX, but the sheer density of construction in some corridors means shared utility easements and tight lot lines that complicate access during an emergency.

## What to Do While You Wait

If you're dealing with an active leak, shut off the water at the main before anything else. If the leak is near an electrical panel, outlets, or appliances, don't enter standing water, cut power to that area at the breaker first.
