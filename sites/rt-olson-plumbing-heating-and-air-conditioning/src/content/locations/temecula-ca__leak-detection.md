## Why Temecula Properties Develop Hidden Leaks

Much of Temecula's residential construction came up quickly during the 1990s and early 2000s boom, meaning a large share of the housing stock is now 20–30 years old, old enough for copper supply lines to show pinhole corrosion and for original polybutylene or early CPVC fittings to fatigue. Slab-on-grade construction dominates across the city, so supply and drain lines run directly through or beneath the concrete. When a joint fails there, the water has nowhere obvious to go: it wicks through the slab, pools under flooring, and sometimes travels laterally several feet before surfacing, which is why the wet spot you see rarely marks the actual leak point.

The Inland Southern California climate adds another layer. Summer ground temperatures can push soil moisture levels extremely low, causing clay to shrink and pull away from pipe joints. Winter rains then re-saturate the clay rapidly, creating movement that stresses those same joints again the following season. Irrigation systems, common in Temecula's landscaped communities, add pressurized lateral lines that run close to foundations and are easy to nick during landscaping work.

## Our Leak Detection Process in Temecula

Accurate leak detection starts before any tool touches the ground. We pull the meter reading, check flow at the shutoff, and isolate zones, irrigation, interior supply, and any secondary meters, to confirm whether the loss is in a pressurized line or a drain. That isolation step matters in Temecula because many properties have both a domestic meter and a separate irrigation meter through Rancho California Water District, and a high bill doesn't always tell you which system is bleeding.

Once the system is isolated, we use electronic amplification equipment to listen for the characteristic hiss of pressurized water escaping through concrete or soil. For slab leaks, we combine acoustic listening with thermal imaging when floor coverings allow, warm water lines running under tile create a detectable temperature gradient at the surface. For underground yard lines, ground microphones let us walk the pipe path and pinpoint the signal peak, typically within a foot or two of the actual leak. We mark the location before any concrete is touched, so your plumber or contractor cuts once.
