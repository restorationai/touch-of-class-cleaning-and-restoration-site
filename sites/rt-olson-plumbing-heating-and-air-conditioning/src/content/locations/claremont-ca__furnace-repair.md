## Why Claremont Homes See Furnace Problems

Claremont's climate is deceptive. Most of the year, residents barely think about their heating systems, then a Santa Ana wind pattern shifts, temperatures fall into the low 40s overnight, and a furnace that sat idle for eight or nine months is suddenly expected to perform. That long dormancy is exactly when problems surface: pilot assemblies gum up, ignitors crack from thermal stress, and heat exchangers that developed hairline fractures over the summer make themselves known the moment the burner fires.

The city's older housing stock adds another layer. Many homes built in the 1950s and 1960s, particularly in the neighborhoods surrounding the Claremont Colleges, still run on original duct layouts that were never designed for modern high-efficiency units. Undersized returns, restrictive filter boxes, and flex duct added during remodels can starve a furnace of airflow, causing it to overheat, short-cycle, and eventually lock out on a safety fault. A furnace blowing cold air in one of these homes is often a symptom of a system-wide airflow problem, not just a bad part.

## Our Furnace Repair Process in Claremont

Every call starts with a full system diagnostic rather than a parts swap. The technician checks static pressure across the air handler, measures temperature rise at the heat exchanger, tests the ignition sequence, and inspects the flue for proper draft, steps that matter especially in Claremont homes where the furnace may share a utility closet with a water heater and combustion air is limited.

Once the root cause is confirmed, the repair is explained in plain terms before any work begins. Common repairs include replacing cracked ignitors, failed flame sensors coated with oxidation residue, faulty pressure switches on two-stage units, and draft inducer motors that seize after a long off-season. If the heat exchanger shows cracks, a safety concern that affects combustion gas containment, that finding is documented and the options are laid out honestly, including whether repair or replacement makes more financial sense given the unit's age.

After the repair, the system is run through a full heating cycle to confirm temperature rise is within the manufacturer's specified range and that all safety limits reset cleanly.

## Local Note

Homes built in Claremont during the postwar decades frequently have their furnaces installed in interior hallway closets rather than garages or attics, a layout choice that made sense for the climate but creates tight working conditions. Combustion air in these closets can be marginal, and when a homeowner adds weatherstripping or insulation to the closet door over the years, the furnace may begin starving for air and triggering high-limit shutoffs that look like an ignition failure. Technicians working in Claremont learn to check combustion air clearances early in the diagnostic, because a misdiagnosed "bad ignitor" in a sealed closet will fail again within a season if the underlying air supply issue isn't corrected.
