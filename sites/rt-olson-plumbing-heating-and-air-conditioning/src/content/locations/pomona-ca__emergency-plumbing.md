## Why Pomona Properties See Emergency Plumbing Failures

Pomona's housing stock spans a wide range of eras, and that range matters when something goes wrong. Homes built in the post-war boom of the late 1940s and 1950s, many of them still standing in the older residential corridors near downtown, were originally plumbed with galvanized steel pipe. Galvanized corrodes from the inside out, narrowing over decades until a pressure spike or a hot summer finally splits it. You may have had low water pressure for years without realizing the pipe wall was nearly gone.

Homes built in the 1970s and 1980s introduced copper, which holds up well but develops pinhole leaks at fittings when the water's mineral content is high, and the Inland Empire's water, sourced through the Metropolitan Water District, is notably hard. Polybutylene, installed in some tracts through the early 1990s before it was phased out, is another vulnerability: it degrades with chlorine exposure and can fail without warning.

Beyond the pipes themselves, Pomona sits in an area where expansive clay soils shift with the wet-dry cycle. That movement stresses underground drain lines and slab penetrations, which is why slab leaks, detectable as warm spots underfoot or an unexplained spike on your Southern California Gas or water bill, are a recurring call in this part of Los Angeles County.

## Local Note: What Pomona's Clay Soil Means for Your Drain Lines

One thing that comes up repeatedly in Pomona and the surrounding San Gabriel Valley foothills is the behavior of clay-heavy soil during the rainy season. When the ground saturates, especially after a dry stretch followed by a heavy winter storm, it expands and can shift drain lines enough to crack clay tile sewer laterals or offset ABS joints. Homeowners often notice the problem as slow drains or sewage odor in the yard, and assume it's a clog. Sometimes it is. But in a home where the lateral is original to a 1950s build, the real issue is a displaced joint that no amount of snaking will fix. A camera inspection during or after a wet season is the fastest way to know which problem you actually have.
