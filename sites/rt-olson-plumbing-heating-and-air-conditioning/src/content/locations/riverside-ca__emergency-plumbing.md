## Why Riverside Homes See More Plumbing Emergencies

Riverside's housing stock spans more than a century of construction, and that range creates a wide variety of failure points. Neighborhoods closer to the historic downtown core often have homes built in the 1920s through 1950s, many of which still carry original galvanized steel supply lines. Galvanized pipe corrodes from the inside out — water pressure drops gradually until a section finally ruptures, usually at a joint. Homeowners often don't notice until water is running behind a wall.

On the newer end, tract developments built in the 1980s and 1990s across the eastern and southern parts of the city used polybutylene or early-generation CPVC that has now aged past its reliable service life. The combination of Riverside's hard water — calcium and magnesium content is consistently high in water delivered through Eastern Municipal Water District and Western Municipal Water District service areas — accelerates scale buildup inside these older pipe materials, narrowing flow and raising the risk of a sudden failure.

Slab foundations, common throughout Riverside, mean that a slab leak can go undetected for weeks. The first sign is often a warm patch on a tile floor, a water bill that jumped without explanation, or the sound of water running when every fixture in the house is off.

## Local Note

Riverside's hard water is one of the most underappreciated contributors to plumbing emergencies in the area. The mineral content in water supplied through the region's distribution systems leaves scale deposits inside water heater tanks, on valve seats, and along the inside walls of supply lines. Over time, that buildup can cause a water heater pressure relief valve to stick open or a ball valve to seize in the closed position — which becomes a real problem when you need to shut off water fast during a leak. If your home's shutoff valves haven't been exercised in several years, it's worth having them checked before you're in a situation where a stuck valve turns a minor leak into a flooded room.
