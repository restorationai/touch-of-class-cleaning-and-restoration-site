## Why Riverside Properties See More Hidden Leaks

Riverside's housing stock spans a wide range of eras and materials, and each era comes with its own leak profile. Homes built in the postwar boom through the 1970s — common throughout older residential corridors in the city — were often plumbed with galvanized steel pipe that has long since reached the end of its useful life. Galvanized corrodes from the inside out, narrowing first and then failing at joints and elbows where mineral deposits concentrate. Riverside's water is notoriously hard, and that mineral load accelerates the process.

Beyond the pipes themselves, the expansive clay soils that underlie much of the Inland Empire shift seasonally. A dry summer followed by heavy winter rain causes the ground to heave and settle, and underground supply lines or irrigation laterals that cross those movement zones develop stress fractures over time. That kind of underground leak can run for months — the water simply migrates down through the soil rather than surfacing — while your meter keeps spinning.

## Our Leak Detection Process in Riverside

When a call comes in, the first step is a meter test: we isolate the home and watch the meter for movement with all fixtures off. That confirms there's an active leak and gives us a rough flow rate before any equipment comes out of the truck. From there, the approach depends on what the meter and a visual inspection suggest.

For suspected underground or slab leaks, we use acoustic listening equipment that picks up the specific frequency of water escaping under pressure — different from ambient noise, different from normal pipe flow. Correlating sensors let us triangulate the leak location along a run of pipe so the excavation or access point, if one is needed, is precise rather than exploratory. For leaks behind walls or above ceilings, thermal imaging cameras detect temperature differentials caused by evaporating moisture, often pinpointing a wet cavity before it's visible to the eye.

We document everything — meter readings, camera captures, acoustic data — so you have a clear record for your insurer or, if you're in an HOA community, for the association's maintenance coordinator.

## Local Note: Hard Water and Copper Fittings in Riverside

One pattern that comes up repeatedly in Riverside homes plumbed with copper in the 1980s and 1990s: the fittings fail before the straight pipe does. Riverside's water hardness causes scale to build at solder joints and compression fittings, and the resulting turbulence erodes the copper from the inside — a process called erosion corrosion. The leak often starts as a weep at an elbow or tee inside a wall cavity, not at an exposed fixture where you'd notice it immediately. If your water bill has crept up gradually over several months rather than spiking overnight, that slow-weep pattern at a concealed fitting is worth ruling out before assuming the meter is wrong.
