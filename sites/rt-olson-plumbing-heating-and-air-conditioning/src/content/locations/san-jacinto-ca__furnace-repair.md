## Why San Jacinto Homes See Furnace Problems

The Inland Empire's wide temperature range is the core issue. San Jacinto's furnaces often sit idle through eight or nine warm months, then get switched on hard when a cold front rolls through the San Jacinto Valley. That long dormancy is when problems surface, a cracked heat exchanger that went unnoticed in spring, a pilot assembly caked with dust from the dry summer air, or a blower motor that seized after months without use.

The housing stock here adds another layer. Many homes in San Jacinto were built during the 1970s and 1980s tract-home boom that spread across Riverside County, and the furnaces in those properties are often original or close to it. Aging ductwork, older gas valve designs, and thermostats that predate modern digital controls are common findings on service calls in this area. Homes built in that era also tend to use attic-mounted or closet-installed units, configurations that make access trickier and mean heat distribution problems can be misread as thermostat issues when the real cause is a failing inducer motor or a restricted flue.

## Our Furnace Repair Process in San Jacinto

When a technician arrives, the first step is a systematic diagnosis rather than a parts swap. That means checking the heat exchanger for cracks before anything else, a cracked exchanger can push combustion gases into living space, and that's a condition that gets addressed before the furnace is returned to service. From there, the tech works through the ignition system, flame sensor, pressure switches, and blower assembly in sequence.

For San Jacinto homes with older forced-air systems, we pay close attention to the duct connections at the air handler. The dry inland air accelerates the breakdown of duct tape and mastic over time, and a loose duct joint in an attic can mean the furnace is running hard while conditioned air bleeds into unconditioned space, which explains why some homeowners notice the unit cycling constantly without the house ever warming up. Sealing those joints is sometimes part of a furnace repair visit, not a separate job.

Once the repair is complete, we run the system through a full heating cycle, verify the temperature rise across the heat exchanger is within the manufacturer's specified range, and confirm the thermostat is reading and responding correctly before leaving the property.
