## Restoration emergencies common in Loomis

Loomis sits in the Sierra Nevada foothills at the edge of Placer County, where the climate swings hard enough to stress buildings year-round. Winter atmospheric rivers drop several inches of rain in a matter of days, overwhelming gutters, area drains, and the clay-heavy soils that shed water rather than absorb it. That runoff finds its way into crawlspaces, around stem walls, and through any gap in a foundation that has shifted over decades of wet-dry cycling. Summer brings the opposite problem: triple-digit heat and wildfire smoke from fires burning in the Tahoe National Forest and surrounding foothill terrain, leaving smoke residue and ash on surfaces both inside and outside homes. The transition seasons — when warm days and cold nights stress roofing materials and flashings — are when most roof-leak calls originate. Loomis's mix of older ranch homes and newer construction on larger lots means the exposure varies by neighborhood and decade of build.

## Services we provide in Loomis

### Water damage restoration

Water damage in Loomis frequently starts at the roof or the crawlspace rather than a burst pipe. Foothill rain events are intense enough to push water under improperly lapped roofing felt or through aging flashing around chimneys and skylights. Once water reaches the subfloor or wall cavities of a crawlspace-foundation home, it can read dry at the surface while the framing below stays saturated. We use thermal imaging and moisture meters to map the actual wet zone, then dry the structure to IICRC S500 standards before any rebuild begins.

### Fire and smoke damage restoration

Even when a fire is contained to one room or a detached structure, smoke migrates through HVAC systems and wall penetrations into every corner of the house. In Loomis, wildfire smoke events can also deposit fine particulate on interior surfaces when homes are not fully sealed — a slower version of the same contamination. Restoration involves controlled demolition of char, HEPA air scrubbing, thermal fogging or hydroxyl treatment where appropriate, and odor-neutralizing encapsulants before any finish work.

### Mold remediation

The combination of wet winters, crawlspace foundations, and homes built before modern vapor-barrier standards creates real mold risk in Loomis. Mold can colonize damp framing within days of a water intrusion event. Remediation means containing the affected area, removing compromised material, treating structural wood, and correcting the moisture source — without that last step, regrowth is a near certainty.

### Reconstruction

Because TDI Builders holds a general contractor license, we carry a water or fire loss all the way through to finished reconstruction — drywall, flooring, cabinetry, roofing, whatever the scope requires. One contractor managing both the mitigation and the rebuild reduces the documentation gaps that slow insurance claims.

## Building stock, site conditions, and permits in Loomis

Loomis developed in several distinct waves. Older parcels near the town center include mid-century ranch homes on raised foundations with pier-and-beam or continuous-perimeter stem walls — crawlspace construction that concentrates moisture risk below the floor deck. Newer subdivisions built from the 1980s onward tend toward slab-on-grade with stucco exteriors. Homes built before the mid-1980s may contain asbestos-containing materials in floor tile, duct insulation, or popcorn ceilings, and pre-1978 construction requires lead-paint awareness during any demolition scope. Placer County's clay-rich soils expand when wet and shrink when dry, a cycle that works on foundation perimeters and can open pathways for water intrusion over time. Structural repair and reconstruction in Loomis falls under Placer County Building Services for unincorporated parcels; the Town of Loomis handles permitting for properties within town limits. Either way, work that touches structural framing, electrical, or plumbing typically requires a permit and inspection before close-in.
