## Restoration emergencies common in Loomis

Loomis sits in the Sierra Nevada foothills at the edge of Placer County, where the climate swings between dry, fire-prone summers and wet, storm-driven winters. The rainy season — roughly November through March — delivers atmospheric river events that can drop several inches of rain in 24 hours. Foothill soils shed water quickly when they're saturated, and that runoff finds its way into crawlspaces, garages, and low-lying slabs. Older ranch-style homes on the western edge of town often have minimal roof overhangs and aging composition shingles that let driven rain work under flashing. Summer brings a different threat: wildfire smoke and ember cast from fires burning in the Tahoe National Forest corridor can leave smoke and soot residue inside homes even when the structure itself is never touched by flame. The combination of wet winters and dry, smoky summers means Loomis property owners deal with water intrusion and air-quality events in the same calendar year more often than homeowners in the Sacramento Valley floor.

## Services we provide in Loomis

### Water damage restoration

Water damage in Loomis frequently starts in crawlspaces. The foothills construction common here places wood framing close to grade, and when winter storms saturate the ground, moisture migrates upward into subfloor assemblies. Standing water under a house can read dry at the surface while the rim joists and floor sheathing stay wet for weeks — long enough for mold to colonize if the space isn't dried with commercial dehumidification equipment. TDI Builders extracts standing water, maps moisture with thermal imaging, and dries to the IICRC S500 standard before any reconstruction begins.

### Fire and smoke damage restoration

Foothill homes with wood-burning fireplaces and older HVAC ductwork are especially vulnerable to smoke migration. After a fire — whether it started in the structure or drifted in from a nearby wildland event — soot embeds in insulation, ductwork, and wall cavities in ways that aren't visible at first. The crew performs controlled demolition only where necessary, cleans affected surfaces with appropriate chemistry, and seals or replaces materials that cannot be fully deodorized.

### Mold remediation

Mold growth in Loomis most often follows undetected plumbing leaks or inadequately dried crawlspaces. Remediation scope is determined by the extent of colonization, not by a formula. TDI Builders contains the affected area, removes compromised materials, and treats surfaces before reconstruction closes the walls.

### Reconstruction

Because TDI Builders holds both a contractor's license and restoration certifications, the same company that mitigates the loss can pull the permit and rebuild — no handoff to a second contractor, no gap in accountability.

## Building stock, site conditions, and permits in Loomis

Most of Loomis's residential stock was built between the 1960s and the early 2000s, with a mix of single-story ranch homes and two-story tract construction. Crawlspace foundations are common in the older inventory; slab-on-grade became more prevalent in subdivisions built after the 1980s. Homes from the 1960s and 1970s may contain asbestos-containing materials in floor tile, duct insulation, and popcorn ceilings — a factor that must be evaluated before any demolition work begins. Copper supply plumbing is typical of that era, though some late-1980s construction used polybutylene, which has a known failure history. Foothill soils in this part of Placer County tend toward decomposed granite and clay loam, which drain unevenly and can allow seasonal moisture to accumulate against stem walls. Structural repair and reconstruction permits in Loomis are issued by Placer County, and work that affects the building envelope or structural members will typically require a permit and inspection before walls are closed.
