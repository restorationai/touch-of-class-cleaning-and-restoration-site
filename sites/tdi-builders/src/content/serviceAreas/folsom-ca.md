## Restoration emergencies common in Folsom

Folsom sits at the eastern edge of the Sacramento Valley where the valley floor gives way to Sierra Nevada foothills. That geography shapes the damage patterns here in specific ways. The region gets concentrated rainfall in the late fall and winter — often arriving in atmospheric river events that can drop several inches in 24 hours — after months of dry, fire-season heat. Homes that have been baking in 100°F summers with low humidity suddenly absorb heavy rain, and the transitions are hard on roofing, flashing, and window seals.

Folsom also carries a wide range of construction vintages. Older neighborhoods closer to the historic downtown core have homes built decades apart from the large master-planned communities that expanded along the Highway 50 corridor from the 1990s onward. That mix means restoration work here encounters everything from aging galvanized plumbing and original wood-frame assemblies to tract homes with engineered lumber, PEX supply lines, and stucco-over-OSB exteriors — each with its own failure points.

Wildfire smoke is a seasonal reality throughout the foothills corridor. Even when a fire burns miles away in El Dorado County, smoke and ash infiltrate HVAC systems and settle into attic insulation, requiring deodorization and sometimes duct cleaning before a home is comfortable again.

## Services we provide in Folsom

### Water damage restoration

Water intrusion in Folsom frequently follows one of two patterns: interior plumbing failures — supply line breaks, water heater leaks, dishwasher overflows — or exterior water entry after heavy rain pushes against foundations and window wells. In slab-on-grade homes, which make up much of the post-1990s housing stock, water has nowhere to drain once it gets under a door threshold or through a cracked slab penetration. Moisture meters and thermal imaging help locate saturation that looks dry at the surface.

### Fire and smoke damage restoration

House fires in the foothills often involve wood-frame construction with attic spaces that allow smoke to travel far from the point of ignition. Smoke residue — particularly the oily, wet-smoke residue from modern synthetic materials — bonds to drywall, framing, and HVAC ductwork. Restoration means more than repainting; it means sealing, deodorizing, and in many cases replacing materials that have absorbed odor permanently.

### Mold remediation

Folsom's dry summers can mask slow leaks that feed mold colonies behind walls and under flooring for months before the smell surfaces. After any water event, mold can begin colonizing porous materials within 24 to 48 hours under the right temperature and humidity conditions. Remediation involves containing the affected area, removing compromised materials, and addressing the moisture source — not just treating visible growth.

### Reconstruction

Because TDI Builders, Inc. is a licensed general contractor, we can carry a project from emergency mitigation through permitted reconstruction without handing the homeowner off to a separate rebuild contractor. That matters when a loss involves structural framing, drywall, flooring, cabinetry, or roofing that requires a building permit to replace.

## Building stock, site conditions, and permits in Folsom

Folsom's housing stock spans roughly a century of construction, though the largest share was built during the suburban expansion of the 1990s and 2000s. That era favored slab-on-grade foundations, wood-frame walls sheathed in OSB, and stucco exteriors — assemblies that perform well in dry conditions but are vulnerable to bulk water intrusion at penetrations and transitions. Homes from earlier decades may contain asbestos-containing materials in popcorn ceilings, floor tile mastic, or pipe insulation; any scope that disturbs those materials requires proper testing and handling protocols.

The soils in the Folsom area transition from valley clay to decomposed granite and rocky foothill terrain. Expansive clay soils, where present, can shift seasonally and stress slab perimeters and foundation walls. Drainage behavior varies significantly by neighborhood and lot grading.

Building permits for structural repair and reconstruction in Folsom are issued by the City of Folsom's Building and Safety Division. California's statewide building code framework applies, and Folsom falls within a seismically active zone — relevant when structural repairs require engineering review. Many of the master-planned communities also have active HOAs with their own exterior material and color approval requirements that run parallel to the city permit process.
