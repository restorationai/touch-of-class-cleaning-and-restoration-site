## Restoration emergencies common in Citrus Heights

Citrus Heights sits in the Sacramento Valley foothills, where the climate delivers wet, concentrated winters and long, dry summers. That seasonal swing is the engine behind most of the restoration calls here. Winter atmospheric rivers can push several inches of rain in 48 hours — faster than the clay-heavy soils common across Sacramento County can absorb it. Water backs up against foundations, finds its way through aging crawlspace vents, and pools in low-lying yards before it ever reaches a storm drain. When that same moisture gets inside a wall cavity and the heat of a Sacramento summer follows, mold colonization can begin within days.

Summer brings its own risks. Wildfire smoke from the Sierra Nevada foothills regularly blankets the area, leaving fine particulate residue on HVAC filters, ductwork, and porous surfaces — a cleanup scope that goes well beyond wiping down countertops. And because much of Citrus Heights was built during a period of rapid postwar and mid-century suburban expansion, the housing stock carries age-related vulnerabilities: supply lines that have cycled through decades of hot summers and cold winters, water heaters well past their service life, and roof assemblies that were not designed for the occasional atmospheric river that now arrives with more intensity.

## Services we provide in Citrus Heights

**Water damage restoration** — Water intrusion in Citrus Heights frequently involves more than a visible wet floor. Clay soils slow drainage and keep hydrostatic pressure against slab edges and crawlspace walls long after rain stops. Moisture meters and thermal imaging reveal saturation behind drywall and under subfloor sheathing that looks dry at the surface. Extraction, structural drying, and monitoring continue until readings confirm the assembly is genuinely dry — not just surface-dry.

**Mold remediation** — The combination of wet winters, warm summers, and older construction details creates conditions where mold can establish quickly after any unaddressed moisture event. Remediation involves containing the affected area, removing compromised materials, and treating structural components — followed by clearance testing to confirm the space is safe before reconstruction begins.

**Fire and smoke damage restoration** — Smoke residue from a kitchen fire or a wildfire-smoke intrusion event behaves differently depending on what burned and how hot. Protein smoke from cooking fires leaves an almost invisible film that carries a persistent odor; wood-based smoke from structure fires embeds in drywall, insulation, and framing. Scope development here requires identifying every affected material, not just the ones with visible char.

**Reconstruction** — Once mitigation is complete, TDI Builders, Inc. carries the project through permitted structural repair and finish work. That continuity — one contractor from demo through rebuild — simplifies insurance documentation and keeps the project on a single timeline.

## Building stock, site conditions, and permits in Citrus Heights

Citrus Heights developed rapidly from the late 1940s through the 1970s, and the majority of its single-family housing reflects that era. Most homes are slab-on-grade, though a portion of the older stock — particularly in the areas closer to the Arcade Creek corridor — sits on raised foundations with crawlspaces. Wall assemblies from this period commonly used wood lath and plaster before drywall became standard, and insulation was minimal by current standards. Plumbing in homes built before the mid-1980s may include galvanized steel supply lines, which corrode from the inside out and can fail with little warning. Homes of this era also warrant asbestos and lead-paint assessment before any invasive scope begins.

Sacramento County's clay-dominant soils expand when wet and shrink when dry, a cycle that stresses slab edges and crawlspace stem walls over time. Permits for structural repair and reconstruction in Citrus Heights are issued through the City of Citrus Heights Community Development Department. Work that involves structural framing, electrical, plumbing, or mechanical systems typically requires a permit; a restoration contractor should be pulling those permits, not asking the homeowner to do it.
