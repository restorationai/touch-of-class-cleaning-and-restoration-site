## Restoration emergencies common in Citrus Heights

Citrus Heights sits in the Sacramento Valley foothills transition zone, which gives it a climate that swings hard between seasons. Winters bring atmospheric river storms capable of dropping several inches of rain in 24 hours — the kind of event that overwhelms gutters, pushes water against foundations, and sends it under doors before a homeowner realizes what's happening. Summers are long and dry, which dries out roofing materials, caulk lines, and wood framing so that the first hard rain of fall finds every gap that opened over the summer.

The housing stock amplifies these risks. Most of Citrus Heights was developed between the late 1950s and the mid-1980s, meaning a large share of homes are on slab-on-grade foundations with original plumbing that is now 40 to 60 years old. Galvanized steel supply lines from that era corrode from the inside out; they can look intact at the wall and be paper-thin at the joints. When they let go — often during a pressure surge after a long dry period — the water runs under the slab or into wall cavities before it surfaces. The region also sees periodic wildfire smoke events, and homes without modern air sealing pull that smoke into attics and HVAC systems in ways that require more than airing out.

## Services we provide in Citrus Heights

**Water damage restoration** is the most common call we receive from Citrus Heights. The combination of aging supply lines, slab construction, and periodic heavy rain means water intrusion here often involves materials that read dry at the surface — tile, painted drywall — while the substrate underneath stays saturated for days. We use thermal imaging and penetrating moisture meters to map the actual wet zone, not just the visible stain, then dry to the IICRC S500 standard before any reconstruction begins.

**Mold remediation** follows water damage that wasn't caught quickly or wasn't dried completely. In a Sacramento Valley climate where indoor humidity can spike after a storm and then stay elevated in a closed house, mold can begin colonizing porous materials within 24 to 48 hours of a moisture event. Remediation involves containment, HEPA filtration, removal of affected materials, and clearance testing — not surface treatment.

**Fire and smoke damage restoration** in Citrus Heights often involves homes with original wood-frame construction and attic spaces that pull smoke deep into insulation and framing. Smoke residue is acidic and continues damaging surfaces after the fire is out; the timeline for cleaning and sealing matters as much as the physical work.

**Reconstruction and rebuild** follows any loss large enough to require permitted structural work — framing, drywall, roofing, or mechanical replacement. We handle the rebuild phase directly, so property owners are not coordinating between a separate restoration contractor and a separate general contractor.

**Biohazard and specialty cleanup** services are also available for Citrus Heights properties when the situation requires them.

## Building stock, site conditions, and permits in Citrus Heights

The majority of Citrus Heights residential construction dates from the post-war suburban expansion through the early 1980s. Ranch-style and split-level homes on slab-on-grade foundations dominate the housing stock; true basements are uncommon. Homes built before 1978 may contain lead-based paint, and those built before the mid-1980s may contain asbestos-containing materials in floor tile, pipe insulation, or textured ceiling finishes — both of which require testing and proper handling before a restoration scope can proceed with demolition.

The soils across this part of Sacramento County include expansive clay in many areas, which swells when wet and can exert lateral pressure on slab edges and foundation stems. Drainage is generally sheet-flow dependent; when downspouts discharge against the foundation or grading has settled toward the house, water finds the slab perimeter quickly.

Permits for structural repair, re-roofing, or mechanical replacement in Citrus Heights are issued through the City of Citrus Heights Community Development Department. California's statewide building code framework applies; any scope that involves structural framing, electrical, plumbing, or HVAC replacement will typically require a permit and inspection before walls are closed.
