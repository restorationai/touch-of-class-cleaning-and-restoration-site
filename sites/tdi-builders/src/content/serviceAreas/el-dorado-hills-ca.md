## Restoration emergencies common in El Dorado Hills

El Dorado Hills sits in the Sierra Nevada foothills at elevations that create a distinct seasonal pattern: wet, atmospheric-river winters followed by long, dry, fire-prone summers. That cycle drives most of the restoration work here. Winter rain events can overwhelm gutters and downspouts on the steeply pitched lots common throughout the area, pushing water into crawlspaces and against slab edges. The same storms that saturate the ground can topple oaks onto roofs, opening the building envelope to days of rain intrusion before repairs are complete.

Summer brings a different threat. The Caldor and Dixie fires in recent years underscored how quickly smoke and ash can penetrate homes in the wildland-urban interface zones that border El Dorado Hills to the east. Even homes that survive a fire structurally often carry odor-laden soot in HVAC systems, attics, and wall cavities — damage that looks minor but compounds if not addressed promptly. Plumbing failures, mold from delayed leak repairs, and post-fire reconstruction round out the typical loss picture here.

## Services we provide in El Dorado Hills

### Water damage restoration

Water losses in El Dorado Hills frequently involve the interaction between hillside drainage and slab foundations. When soil becomes saturated, hydrostatic pressure can push moisture through slab cracks or around stem walls, leaving flooring wet at the surface while the concrete substrate stays elevated in moisture content for weeks. We use thermal imaging and penetrating moisture meters to find what surface readings miss, then dry to the IICRC S500 standard before any reconstruction begins.

### Fire and smoke damage restoration

Smoke from a structure fire or a nearby wildland event doesn't stay in the room where the damage occurred. It migrates through HVAC returns, attic bypasses, and wall penetrations. Restoration involves controlled demolition of char, dry-ice or soda blasting of framing where appropriate, sealing of odor-bearing surfaces, and full duct cleaning — not just surface wiping.

### Mold remediation

El Dorado Hills homes that have experienced a slow roof leak or a plumbing drip behind cabinetry can develop mold colonies within days in warm weather. Remediation here follows EPA guidelines: containment, HEPA filtration, removal of affected material to clean wood, and post-clearance testing before reconstruction closes the wall.

### Reconstruction and rebuild

After mitigation, most losses require permitted reconstruction — framing, drywall, flooring, cabinetry, painting. As a licensed general contractor, TDI Builders, Inc. carries the work from emergency response through finished rebuild under one contract, which simplifies insurance documentation and eliminates the gap between mitigation and rebuild crews.

## Building stock, site conditions, and permits in El Dorado Hills

Most of El Dorado Hills' residential stock was built between the mid-1980s and the mid-2000s, with a significant wave of construction following the US-50 interchange improvements in the 1990s. Homes from the earlier part of that range may contain asbestos-containing materials in floor tile mastics, popcorn ceilings, and HVAC duct insulation — a scope consideration that requires testing before any demolition begins. Copper supply lines are typical in this era, though some late-1980s construction used polybutylene, which has a documented failure history.

Foundations are predominantly slab-on-grade, with some two-story homes on raised foundations over partial crawlspaces. The foothill soils in this area include expansive clays in lower elevations and decomposed granite higher up; both present drainage challenges, and clay-heavy soils can hold water against foundations long after a rain event ends.

Permits for structural repair and reconstruction in the unincorporated portions of El Dorado Hills are issued through El Dorado County; the incorporated community areas fall under county jurisdiction as well, since El Dorado Hills is an unincorporated community. California's Title 24 energy code and the current adopted edition of the California Building Code govern the work. HOA covenants are common throughout the master-planned communities here and can affect exterior material selections during reconstruction — worth confirming with your association before finalizing a scope.
