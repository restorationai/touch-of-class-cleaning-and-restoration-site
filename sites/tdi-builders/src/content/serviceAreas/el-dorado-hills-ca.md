## Restoration emergencies common in El Dorado Hills

El Dorado Hills sits at the transition between the Sacramento Valley floor and the Sierra Nevada foothills, and that geography shapes the damage patterns here. Winter atmospheric rivers drop heavy rain on ground that is often underlain by decomposed granite and clay — a combination that sheds water quickly and pushes it toward foundations and crawlspaces rather than absorbing it. Summer heat in the foothills regularly exceeds 100°F, accelerating mold colonization after any moisture intrusion; the window between a slow leak and an active mold colony can be shorter here than in cooler coastal climates. The community also sits within the wildland-urban interface, meaning smoke infiltration and ember-related ignition are realistic risks during fire season — not abstract ones. Homes with tile roofs and stucco exteriors common in planned developments can trap smoke odor in attic cavities and HVAC systems long after the visible fire is out.

## Services we provide in El Dorado Hills

### Water damage restoration

Water intrusion in El Dorado Hills frequently traces back to roof penetrations during winter storms, slab leaks in homes built on expansive soils, and irrigation or pool plumbing failures in the warm months. Because many homes here are slab-on-grade, water that migrates under a slab can wick upward through flooring for weeks before it becomes visible. TDI Builders uses moisture mapping and thermal imaging to locate saturation that surface readings miss, then dries to the IICRC S500 standard before any reconstruction begins.

### Fire and smoke damage restoration

Smoke behaves differently in foothill homes than in valley construction. Vaulted ceilings, open-beam great rooms, and whole-house fans — popular in El Dorado Hills for summer cooling — distribute smoke residue into every cavity. Odor that seems gone after airing out often resurfaces when temperatures rise. Restoration work here includes content cleaning, structural deodorization, and HVAC cleaning before any cosmetic repairs are made.

### Mold remediation

El Dorado Hills' hot, dry summers create a false sense of security around moisture. In reality, a slow supply line drip inside a wall cavity or a bathroom exhaust fan venting into an attic can sustain mold growth for months undetected. Remediation follows EPA guidelines for containment and removal; affected materials are documented and disposed of properly before reconstruction closes the wall.

## Building stock, site conditions, and permits in El Dorado Hills

Most of El Dorado Hills' residential stock was built between the mid-1980s and the 2010s, with significant tract development accelerating through the 1990s and early 2000s. The dominant construction is wood-frame on slab-on-grade foundations, with stucco exteriors and concrete tile roofs. Homes from the late 1980s through mid-1990s may contain polybutylene supply piping, a material prone to fitting failures that can release water inside walls without warning. Copper and PEX are more common in later builds. Because much of the community developed after California's asbestos and lead-paint phase-outs, ACM and lead paint are less prevalent than in Sacramento's older neighborhoods — but any home with pre-1980 components warrants testing before invasive work begins.
