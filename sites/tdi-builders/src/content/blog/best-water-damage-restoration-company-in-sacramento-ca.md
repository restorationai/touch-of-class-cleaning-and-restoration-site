## What Should You Look for in a Water Damage Restoration Company in Sacramento?

The right company for a water damage job in Sacramento can document a California contractor license, carries its own liability and workers' comp insurance, and can either perform or directly coordinate the full rebuild, not just the drying phase. Those three things separate a legitimate operation from a subcontracted patchwork.

Water damage restoration actually has two distinct phases. Mitigation is the emergency response: extracting standing water, setting up air movers and dehumidifiers, and testing moisture levels in walls and subflooring over several days. Reconstruction is what comes after: removing damaged drywall and flooring, addressing any mold growth behind the walls, and rebuilding the space to match (or improve on) what was there before. Some companies specialize only in the first phase and then refer you out for the rebuild. That's not wrong, but it means a second contract, a second insurance conversation, and a second set of people learning your project from scratch.

California licensing matters here specifically because rebuild work, drywall, flooring, cabinetry, electrical if it was affected, legally requires a licensed contractor once the job crosses certain cost thresholds. The [Contractors State License Board](https://www.cslb.ca.gov) lets you verify any company's license number and status in about 30 seconds before you sign anything.

## Water Extraction vs. Full Rebuild: What's the Difference and Why Does It Matter?

Water extraction removes standing water and dries out the structure; a full rebuild replaces everything the water damaged and restores the space to livable condition. Confusing the two is the most common reason Sacramento homeowners end up frustrated mid-project.

A typical mitigation invoice covers water removal, industrial fans and dehumidifiers running for 3-5 days, and moisture readings to confirm the structure is dry. It does not cover replacing the baseboards that swelled, the section of oak flooring that cupped, or the drywall that had to be cut out because water wicked up inside the wall cavity. Those are construction line items: material costs, permits if load-bearing work is involved, and labor from framers, drywall crews, and painters.

This is also where mold risk enters the picture. If drywall or subfloor stays damp for more than 24-48 hours, the [EPA](https://www.epa.gov/mold) notes that mold growth can begin. A rebuild contractor who understands both the construction and the moisture history of the space is in a better position to catch a hidden damp stud bay before it becomes a bigger problem. ## What Mistakes Do Homeowners Make When Hiring a Water Damage Company?

The most common mistake is signing with the first company that answers the phone without confirming who handles the rebuild, then discovering weeks later that the mitigation company's scope stopped at drying the walls. Ask that question before you sign anything.

A second mistake: assuming the mitigation company's estimate is the total repair cost. It usually only reflects the emergency response phase. Ask specifically whether the quote includes reconstruction or is limited to extraction and drying.

A third mistake is not verifying the company's license number against the CSLB database, especially for any company offering to also handle the rebuild. A cleanup crew without a general contractor license legally cannot perform the reconstruction work above minor thresholds, no matter how good their reviews are for the drying phase.

Finally, homeowners sometimes sign a contractor's insurance authorization without reading it closely.
