## How Should You Actually Compare These Commercial Contractors?

Compare commercial contractors on four things: active California license status, actual years operating in your specific market, the project types they can document, and how they handle change orders and insurance coordination. A flashy portfolio photo doesn't tell you whether a contractor can hit a permit deadline.

Start by pulling the contractor's license number and checking it directly against the [California Contractors State License Board](https://www.cslb.ca.gov) database. An active, unrestricted license with no recent disciplinary actions is non-negotiable for any commercial project over a few thousand dollars.

Next, ask how many years the firm has worked specifically in the Sacramento metro, not just California generally. Local experience means the contractor already knows which jurisdictions (City of Sacramento, Sacramento County, Elk Grove, Folsom, Roseville) move fast on commercial permits and which ones routinely ask for resubmittals. If you're weighing a general contractor against a specialty subcontractor for part of the scope, our [general contractor vs subcontractor breakdown](/blog/general-contractor-vs-subcontractor/) explains who should hold the prime contract on a commercial job.

## What Mistakes Do Businesses Make When Hiring a Commercial Contractor?

The most common mistake is hiring based on the lowest bid without confirming the contractor can self-perform or properly supervise the trades listed in that bid. A bid that's 20% below everyone else's usually means someone is cutting corners on supervision, materials, or insurance coverage.

A second common mistake is not clarifying who manages insurance-related rebuild work if a fire, water event, or storm damages the property mid-lease or mid-tenancy. Not every commercial contractor handles direct insurance coordination, and finding that out after a loss event costs weeks. Our [general contractor questions checklist](/blog/questions-to-ask-before-signing-contract/) is built for exactly this kind of pre-signing diligence, and applies just as much to a commercial scope as a residential one.

A third mistake is skipping the permit conversation. Commercial tenant improvements almost always trigger plan check and inspection requirements that residential remodels don't, especially around accessibility (ADA), fire-rated assemblies, and occupancy classification changes. Confirm early who is pulling permits and who owns the schedule risk if plan check takes longer than expected.
