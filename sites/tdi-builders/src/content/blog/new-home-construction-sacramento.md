## What does new home construction in Sacramento actually cost?

A new custom home in Sacramento runs $250 to $450 per square foot for construction costs alone, not counting the land. A 2,000-square-foot home lands between $500,000 and $900,000 in hard construction costs depending on your finish level. Tract-style production homes from a volume builder come in lower, but you're trading customization for speed and price.

Several factors push costs toward the high end in the Sacramento market:

- **Soil conditions.** Much of Sacramento sits on expansive clay soils. A geotechnical (geotech) report is often required by the city before permits are issued, and the findings may require a deeper or reinforced foundation, adding $15,000 to $40,000 to the foundation budget.
- **Energy code.** California's Title 24 energy efficiency standards are among the strictest in the country. Your plans need to show compliance before the city will issue a building permit. This typically means a blower-door test, specific insulation values, and often a solar-ready or solar-equipped electrical system.
- **Labor market.** The Sacramento subcontractor market is tight. Framing, electrical, and HVAC crews are booked 4 to 8 weeks out during peak season. A GC with established trade relationships gets you scheduled; an unknown builder may wait months for a crew slot.
- **Lot prep.** Grading, utility connections, and tree removal are separate from the construction contract. Budget $20,000 to $60,000 for site work on a typical infill lot.

## What does the new construction process look like, step by step?

New home construction in Sacramento follows a predictable sequence, though the timeline at each stage depends on city workload and your contractor's schedule.

**1. Design and engineering (2 to 5 months)**
You need a full set of architectural drawings and structural engineering before you can pull a permit. If your lot requires a geotech report, that happens here too. Expect to pay $15,000 to $40,000 for design and engineering on a custom home.

**2. Permit submittal and review (2 to 6 months)**
Sacramento's building department reviews residential permits, and the timeline varies by project complexity and current city workload. Simple single-family projects can move in 8 to 12 weeks; complex custom homes or projects in areas with additional review requirements (fire zones, floodplain, historic overlay) take longer. Plan for it.

**3. Site work and foundation (4 to 8 weeks)**
Once permits are in hand, grading and underground utilities go in first, then the foundation. Sacramento's clay soils mean the foundation inspection is a critical hold point, and the city inspector needs to sign off before you pour concrete.

**4. Framing (4 to 8 weeks)**
This is the most visually dramatic phase. The structure goes vertical, and you'll see your floor plan in three dimensions for the first time. California requires framing to meet the [California Building Code](https://www.dgs.ca.gov/BSC/Codes), which adopts the International Residential Code with state amendments.

**5. Rough mechanical, electrical, and plumbing (4 to 6 weeks)**
HVAC ducts, electrical wiring, and plumbing rough-in all happen before the walls close. Each trade gets its own inspection before insulation goes in.

**6. Insulation and drywall (3 to 5 weeks)**
Title 24 compliance is verified here. The insulation inspection confirms R-values match the energy calculations in your permitted plans.

**7. Finish work (8 to 16 weeks)**
Cabinets, flooring, tile, paint, trim, fixtures, and appliances. This phase takes the longest because it's the most labor-intensive and the most dependent on material lead times. Supply chain delays on windows, appliances, and custom cabinetry are the most common reason builds run long.

**8. Final inspections and certificate of occupancy**
The city conducts a final inspection covering structural, electrical, plumbing, mechanical, and energy compliance. When all inspections pass, you receive a certificate of occupancy and can move in.

## Do you need a permit for new home construction in Sacramento?

Yes, every new home requires a building permit from the City of Sacramento's Community Development Department, or from the county if the lot is in unincorporated Sacramento County. There is no legal path to build a new home without one, and unpermitted construction creates title problems and liability that follow the property forever.

For a detailed look at what triggers a permit requirement and how the process works, see our post on [Do You Need a Permit for That? Home Project Permits, Explained](/blog/do-you-need-a-permit/).

Beyond the building permit, new construction in Sacramento typically requires:

- **Grading permit** (if you're moving significant soil)
- **Encroachment permit** (if work affects the public right of way)
- **Utility connection approvals** (SMUD for electrical, Sacramento Regional County Sanitation for sewer)
- **Fire sprinkler permit** (required for all new single-family homes under California law since 2011)

Your general contractor should pull all permits. If a contractor asks you to pull permits yourself, that's a red flag worth taking seriously.

## How do you choose the right builder for a Sacramento new home?

The right builder for a Sacramento new home is a licensed California general contractor with documented experience in ground-up residential construction, not just remodeling, and with active relationships in the local subcontractor market.

A few things to verify before signing:

- **California contractor's license.** Check the [California Contractors State License Board (CSLB)](https://www.cslb.ca.gov/) to confirm the license is active, in the right classification (B-General Building for most residential work), and free of disciplinary actions. This takes two minutes and is non-negotiable.
- **Insurance.** Ask for a certificate of insurance showing general liability and workers' compensation. Confirm the coverage limits are adequate for your project size.
- **Local permit history.** A builder who has pulled permits with Sacramento's building department before knows the process, the inspectors, and the common hold points. Ask directly: how many new homes have you built in Sacramento in the last three years?
- **References from completed new builds.** Remodel references don't tell you much about a builder's ability to manage a ground-up project. Ask for references from homeowners who went through the full process.
- **A detailed written contract.** Scope, schedule, payment milestones, change-order process, and warranty terms should all be in writing before you pay a deposit.
