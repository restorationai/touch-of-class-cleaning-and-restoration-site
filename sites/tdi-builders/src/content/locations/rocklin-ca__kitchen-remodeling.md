## Why Rocklin Kitchens Are Ready for an Upgrade

Most of Rocklin's residential neighborhoods were platted and built during the city's growth boom between roughly 1990 and 2010. That era of construction favored speed over longevity — builder-grade cabinets with particleboard boxes, vinyl flooring glued directly to slab, and countertops that looked fine in a model home but weren't built for twenty years of daily use. By now, those kitchens are showing their age in very specific ways: drawer slides that have lost their glide, cabinet doors that won't hang level because the particleboard has swelled and settled, and tile grout that has cracked where the slab has shifted slightly with the soil beneath it.

Rocklin's clay-heavy soils — common throughout the Placer County foothills — expand and contract seasonally with rainfall and drought cycles. That movement is subtle, but over a decade or two it telegraphs through a slab foundation and into anything attached to it. Tile floors crack. Grout lines open up. Even cabinet toe kicks can pull away from the floor. A remodel that ignores this pattern and simply installs new materials over an unstable substrate will repeat the same problems within a few years.
