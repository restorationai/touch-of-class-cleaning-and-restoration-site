When that happens, the damage inside a home goes well beyond charred framing: smoke travels through HVAC systems, soot settles into every horizontal surface, and the chemical residue from burning composite materials and modern furnishings lingers in ways that plain cleaning cannot address. ## Why El Dorado Hills Properties Face Particular Fire Damage Challenges

The foothills climate plays a direct role in how fire damage behaves here. Summers in El Dorado Hills are long and dry, with low relative humidity that causes smoke odor compounds to penetrate deeper into porous materials — wood framing, drywall paper facing, and insulation — before the structure ever cools down. That same dry air means soot residue desiccates quickly into a fine powder that migrates further into ductwork and wall cavities than it would in a more humid climate.

The housing stock adds another layer of complexity. Many homes in El Dorado Hills were built during the rapid suburban expansion of the 1980s and 1990s, when open-concept floor plans and cathedral ceilings were standard. Those high-volume interior spaces allow smoke to stratify and deposit heavily on upper wall surfaces and roof sheathing — areas that are easy to overlook during a surface-level assessment but that will continue off-gassing if left untreated. Homes built in that era also commonly used paper-faced fiberglass batt insulation, which absorbs smoke odor and typically requires full replacement after a significant fire event.

## Our Fire Damage Restoration Process in El Dorado Hills

Every fire loss is different, but the sequence of work follows a disciplined order that protects both the structure and the occupants.

**Emergency stabilization** comes first — boarding windows and doors, tarping any compromised roof sections, and shutting off utilities if that hasn't already been done by the fire department. In El Dorado Hills, PG&E serves the area, and coordinating a safe reconnection timeline is part of our early communication with the property owner.

**Damage documentation** follows immediately. Our team photographs and catalogs every affected surface and system before any material is moved or removed. This documentation is the foundation of an accurate insurance claim and protects the homeowner if scope disputes arise later.

**Structural assessment** determines what can be cleaned and restored versus what must be removed. Fire-damaged framing that has lost structural integrity, insulation that has absorbed smoke, and drywall with deep soot penetration are typically removed rather than cleaned — attempting to restore them adds cost without solving the odor problem.

**Soot and smoke cleaning** uses dry chemical sponges, HEPA vacuuming, and appropriate chemical agents matched to the surface type. Wet smoke residue from smoldering fires requires different chemistry than the dry, powdery soot from a fast-burning fire — our technicians assess the residue type before selecting a method.

**Odor neutralization** is where many restoration projects fall short. Thermal fogging and hydroxyl generation address odor at the molecular level rather than masking it. In homes with forced-air HVAC systems — the standard in El Dorado Hills — duct cleaning is a required step, not an optional add-on.

**Reconstruction** closes the project. As a licensed general contractor with over 40 years of construction experience, TDI Builders handles both the restoration and the rebuild under one contract, which simplifies scheduling and eliminates the gap that often appears when a cleanup company hands off to a separate contractor.

## Local Note

Homes in El Dorado Hills built during the 1980s and early 1990s frequently used whole-house fan systems in addition to central HVAC — a popular feature in the foothills where summer nights cool down quickly. After a fire, these fans present a hidden complication: they draw air from the living space directly into the attic, and if the fan was running during or shortly after the fire event, smoke residue can be distributed throughout the attic insulation and roof sheathing even when the fire itself was confined to a single room. We inspect whole-house fan pathways as a standard part of our post-fire attic assessment on any El Dorado Hills property where the system is present.
