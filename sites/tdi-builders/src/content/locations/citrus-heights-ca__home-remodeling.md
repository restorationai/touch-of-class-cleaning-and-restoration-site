## Why Citrus Heights Homes Have Specific Remodeling Needs

The housing stock in Citrus Heights leans heavily on tract homes built between roughly 1950 and 1980. That era of construction brought single-pane aluminum windows, original galvanized plumbing, and electrical systems sized for a household with far fewer appliances than today's families run. When you open walls during a kitchen or bathroom remodel, it's common to find undersized wiring, original knob-and-tube remnants in older additions, or galvanized supply lines that have narrowed to a fraction of their original diameter from decades of mineral buildup, a natural consequence of Sacramento area water chemistry.

Citrus Heights also sits within Sacramento County's jurisdiction for most permitting purposes, which means remodeling projects that touch structural elements, electrical, plumbing, or HVAC require county-issued permits and inspections. ## Local Note

One thing that catches homeowners off guard in Citrus Heights remodels: the original slab foundations on many 1960s ranch homes were poured without a vapor barrier, or with a barrier that has long since degraded. When you're updating a master bathroom or adding a laundry room over slab, moisture wicking up through the concrete can compromise new flooring adhesives and cause tile failures within a year or two if it isn't addressed at the rough-in stage. We test slab moisture levels before specifying flooring systems, a step that adds a small amount of time upfront and prevents a much larger problem later.
