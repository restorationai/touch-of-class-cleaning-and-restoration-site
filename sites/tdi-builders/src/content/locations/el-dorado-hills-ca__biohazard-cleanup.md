## Why El Dorado Hills Properties Present Unique Biohazard Considerations

El Dorado Hills developed rapidly from the 1990s onward, meaning much of the housing stock is built on slab foundations with engineered wood flooring, open floor plans, and HVAC systems that circulate air across large square footage. That construction profile matters during biohazard remediation: porous materials like engineered hardwood, grout lines in tile, and textured drywall can harbor infectious material that is invisible to the naked eye. A surface that looks clean after a basic wipe-down may still require ATP testing or swab sampling to confirm it meets safe clearance standards.

The region's dry climate also creates a deceptive condition — biological material desiccates quickly on surfaces, which can make contamination appear contained when it has actually penetrated porous substrates. Our technicians account for this when scoping the affected area, testing beyond the visible boundary before any work begins.

## Our Biohazard Cleanup Process in El Dorado Hills

Every job begins with a thorough assessment of the affected space — not just the visible area, but adjacent surfaces, subfloor materials, and HVAC intake points if the situation warrants it.
