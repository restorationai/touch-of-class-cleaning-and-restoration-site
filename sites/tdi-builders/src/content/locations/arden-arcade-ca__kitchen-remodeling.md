## Why Arden-Arcade Kitchens Need Updating

A large share of Arden-Arcade's housing stock dates to the rapid suburban growth of the 1950s and 60s, when ranch-style tract homes went up fast and kitchens were treated as closed-off work rooms rather than gathering spaces. That means narrow footprints, limited electrical capacity for modern appliances, and in many cases a load-bearing wall separating the kitchen from the dining or living area. Add Sacramento Valley's long, dry summers, which push indoor temperatures up and strain older ventilation, and you end up with kitchens that are cramped, poorly vented, and running on wiring that was never designed for a double oven or an induction cooktop. Slab-on-grade foundations are also common in this part of the county, which affects how far we can relocate plumbing for an island or a sink without opening up the slab itself.

## Permits and County Coordination

Because Arden-Arcade doesn't have its own city hall, every electrical upgrade, plumbing relocation, or structural change to a load-bearing wall gets permitted through Sacramento County's building division. That's a different process than a remodel just a few miles away inside Sacramento city limits, with its own inspection scheduling and plan review turnaround. We handle that coordination directly, which matters most on remodels involving panel upgrades or wall removal, since those are the permits most likely to slow a project down if they're filed late or filed wrong the first time.
