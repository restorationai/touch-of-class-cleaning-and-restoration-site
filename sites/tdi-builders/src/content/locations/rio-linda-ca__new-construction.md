Building a new home in Rio Linda means building on some of the largest residential lots left in Sacramento County, and that changes the construction conversation before the first stake goes in the ground. Between septic feasibility, well water testing, and the semi-rural zoning that defines much of the area, new home construction here looks different from a standard infill build closer to downtown Sacramento. TDI Builders has been handling ground-up residential and commercial work across the region since 1985, and Rio Linda's acreage lots are part of what keeps that work interesting.

## Why Rio Linda Lots Build Differently

Rio Linda is unincorporated, which means Sacramento County, not a city planning department, handles permitting, zoning verification, and inspections. A lot of parcels here were platted decades ago for small farms and horse properties, so lot sizes, setbacks, and allowed uses vary block to block in a way that doesn't happen in a typical subdivision. Many properties still rely on septic systems and private wells rather than municipal sewer and water, which means a new build often starts with a septic feasibility study and well capacity check before the architectural plans are even finalized. Soil composition across the area also tends to run heavier in clay, which affects foundation design and drainage planning more than it would on a sandier Sacramento lot.

## Our New Home Construction Process in Rio Linda

We start with a site evaluation that covers soil conditions, septic or sewer access, well capacity if applicable, and setback requirements under the county's zoning for the parcel. From there, our team handles design coordination, county permitting, and scheduling of the inspections required at each phase, foundation, framing, rough mechanical, and final. Because many Rio Linda lots sit on an acre or more, we also plan for things that don't come up on a tighter in-town lot: longer utility runs, driveway and access road construction, and sometimes outbuildings or barn structures built alongside the primary residence. Every phase is documented so you have a clear paper trail for the county and for your own records once the home is finished.

## County Coordination and Permitting

Because Rio Linda falls under Sacramento County jurisdiction rather than a city building department, timelines and requirements can differ from what homeowners expect if they've built or renovated elsewhere in the metro area. Septic design approval, for instance, typically needs sign-off before county building permits move forward, and well permits run through a separate review. We coordinate these pieces in parallel where the county allows it, rather than waiting on one approval before starting the next, which keeps a project from stalling for weeks over paperwork that could have been submitted earlier.

## Local note

One detail that catches first-time Rio Linda builders off guard: a lot that looks flat and buildable can still require significant drainage engineering because of the clay-heavy soil common across the area, which doesn't drain the way sandy loam does elsewhere in the county. We factor this into grading and foundation plans early so it doesn't surface as a change order mid-build. It's a small thing, but it's the kind of detail that only shows up once you've worked a handful of lots in the area.
