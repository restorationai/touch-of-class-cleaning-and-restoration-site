## Why Fair Oaks Properties Face Distinct Board-Up and Tarping Risks

The housing stock throughout Fair Oaks skews older — many homes along the American River corridor were built in the 1960s and 1970s, when roofing materials, window framing, and exterior sheathing were installed to standards that have since been updated. Aged composition shingles and original single-pane aluminum-frame windows are more vulnerable to storm damage, fire exposure, and break-ins than modern construction. When those older assemblies fail, they tend to fail in larger sections rather than isolated spots, which means a tarping job here often covers more surface area than a comparable loss on a newer build.

Fair Oaks also sits in a zone where the dry season creates elevated wildfire exposure. A structure fire — or even a neighboring property fire carried by wind — can leave exterior walls and roof decking exposed to the elements within hours. Smoke and heat can compromise window glazing without fully shattering it, leaving gaps that aren't obvious from the street but allow water intrusion the moment rain arrives.

## Our Emergency Board-Up and Tarping Process in Fair Oaks

When you call, a crew is dispatched immediately — 24 hours a day, every day. On arrival, the first priority is a rapid exterior assessment: we identify every compromised opening, document it photographically for your insurance claim, and establish a sequenced work order so the highest-risk penetrations are sealed first.

For window and door board-up, we use structural-grade plywood cut and fitted to the opening rather than draped or stapled sheeting. Properly fitted boards resist wind loading and deter unauthorized entry — both real concerns in a community where a boarded property may sit unoccupied for days while insurance and restoration logistics are worked out.

For roof tarping, we use heavy-duty polyethylene tarps secured with batten strips and mechanical fasteners rather than simple sandbag weighting. On the pitched rooflines common to Fair Oaks ranch and split-level homes, a tarp that isn't mechanically anchored will migrate or lift in the afternoon delta breeze that pushes through the Sacramento Valley most evenings. We size tarps to extend well past the damaged area so runoff is directed away from the structure rather than pooling at the perimeter.

## Fair Oaks Insurance Coordination

Emergency board-up and tarping is a covered mitigation expense under most homeowner's insurance policies — but the documentation has to be done correctly from the start. We photograph every damaged opening before and after securing it, note materials used and square footage covered, and provide a written scope that matches the format most Sacramento-area adjusters expect. This record becomes part of your claim file and supports reimbursement for the mitigation work itself, not just the underlying damage.

Some Fair Oaks HOAs have specific rules about the color or finish of temporary protective materials visible from the street — we've navigated those conversations before and can work within those constraints while still getting the structure properly protected.

## Local Note

Older homes in Fair Oaks built before the 1980s frequently have roof overhangs and eave assemblies with original wood fascia and soffit boards that have never been replaced. When a tarp needs to be anchored near these areas, standard mechanical fastening can split or fracture deteriorated wood — creating a new penetration while trying to close an existing one. Our crews inspect fascia and soffit condition before anchoring and adjust the attachment method accordingly, using edge boards or standoff anchoring when the original wood won't hold a direct fastener safely.
