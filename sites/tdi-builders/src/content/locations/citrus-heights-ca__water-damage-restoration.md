## Why Citrus Heights Properties See Water Damage Issues

The Sacramento Valley's Mediterranean climate means months of dry heat followed by concentrated rainfall between November and March. That pattern puts stress on roofing, gutters, and exterior caulking that baked and contracted all summer. When the rain finally arrives, saturated soil around slab-on-grade foundations can push moisture through hairline cracks in the concrete — a slow intrusion that often goes unnoticed until flooring starts to buckle or a musty smell settles into a back bedroom.

Citrus Heights also has a significant share of homes built in the 1960s and 1970s, when galvanized steel supply lines were common. Galvanized pipe corrodes from the inside out; the first sign of a problem is often reduced water pressure, but a pinhole failure behind a wall can silently saturate insulation and framing for days before it appears on a ceiling or floor. Older homes in this era also frequently used particleboard subfloor — a material that swells and delaminates quickly when wet, making fast extraction critical.

## Our Water Damage Restoration Process in Citrus Heights

The first priority when our crew arrives is stopping active water intrusion and locating the full extent of the moisture — not just the visible wet area. We use thermal imaging cameras and calibrated moisture meters to map saturation behind walls and under flooring before a single piece of drywall is removed. Water behind plaster or beneath vinyl plank can read dry at the surface while the substrate underneath stays saturated; cutting corners on this step leads to mold colonization within 24 to 48 hours.

Once the scope is confirmed, we extract standing water with truck-mounted and portable extraction units, then deploy industrial air movers and low-grain refrigerant dehumidifiers sized to the affected area. Drying is documented daily against the IICRC S500 standard — the same protocol your insurance adjuster will expect to see in the claim file. We do not close out a job until moisture readings in structural materials match pre-loss baselines, not just until the floor feels dry underfoot.

If the loss involves contaminated water — an overflowing toilet, a sewage backup, or stormwater intrusion — affected porous materials are removed and properly disposed of rather than dried in place. This is not optional; Category 3 water carries pathogens that drying alone does not eliminate.

## Local Note

Homes in Citrus Heights built during the 1970s and early 1980s frequently have T-111 plywood siding on exterior walls — a material that wicks moisture aggressively along its vertical grooves when roof or gutter failures allow water to run down the facade. Once T-111 is saturated, it does not dry at the same rate as the interior wall cavity; exterior readings can normalize while the framing behind stays wet. If your home has this siding and you have had any roof or gutter overflow, it is worth having the wall assembly checked even if the interior looks fine.
