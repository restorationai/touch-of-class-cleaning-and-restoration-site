## Why Citrus Heights Properties Are Vulnerable to Water Damage

Citrus Heights was developed rapidly through the 1960s and 1970s, which means a large share of the commercial and residential building stock is now carrying original galvanized steel or early copper plumbing that has reached or exceeded its service life. Galvanized pipe corrodes from the inside out, pressure looks normal until a section fails suddenly, and by then water has often been seeping into wall cavities for hours. The region's clay-heavy soils don't drain quickly, so when Sacramento County gets a heavy rain event, surface water can pool against foundations and push through any crack or gap in a slab or stem wall. Flat or low-slope commercial roofs common in Citrus Heights retail corridors are also frequent failure points: a clogged drain or a seam that has dried and cracked over a long summer will let water migrate into ceiling assemblies well before a tenant notices a stain.

## Our Water Damage Restoration Process in Citrus Heights

Water damage restoration is not a single trade, it's a sequenced construction process, and the sequence matters. When we arrive at a Citrus Heights property, the first step is containment and documentation: we photograph affected areas, use thermal imaging to locate moisture that hasn't surfaced yet, and establish a moisture baseline with calibrated meters. That documentation matters for insurance claims and for making sure we don't miss saturated material behind intact finishes.

Extraction comes next, truck-mounted or portable units pull standing water from carpet, tile, and concrete. Then we set industrial air movers and desiccant or refrigerant dehumidifiers calibrated to the ambient conditions. In Citrus Heights's dry summer months, ambient humidity works in our favor; in January and February, when outdoor relative humidity climbs, we rely more heavily on desiccant equipment to pull moisture out of dense materials like engineered wood subfloor and older plaster.

Once structural drying is confirmed with final moisture readings, our crews move into the rebuild phase, drywall replacement, ceiling repair, flooring, painting, and any structural framing that absorbed too much water to remain sound. Because we are a full construction firm, not just a mitigation company, the restoration and rebuild happen under one contract, which simplifies scheduling and insurance billing.

One pattern we see consistently in Citrus Heights's older residential neighborhoods is that homes built in the late 1960s and early 1970s often have original acoustic "popcorn" ceilings over the main living areas.
