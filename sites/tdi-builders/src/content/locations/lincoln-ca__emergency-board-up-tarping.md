## Why Lincoln Properties Face Distinct Board-Up and Tarping Risks

Lincoln's growth over the past two decades has produced a wide mix of housing stock: older ranch-style homes on the west side of town built with wood-frame construction and composition shingles, and newer master-planned communities with tile roofs and stucco exteriors. Each responds differently to emergency damage. A tile roof that takes a hit from a fallen oak limb during a winter storm can look intact from the street while hiding a cracked underlayment that will let water pour into the attic the moment the next rain cell moves through. Stucco exteriors on newer homes can mask broken sheathing that leaves interior walls exposed. The dry, hot summers also mean that wildfire smoke and ember cast are real threats — a fire that starts in the foothill brush east of Lincoln can push embers into neighborhoods well ahead of the flame front, igniting eaves and attic vents before a homeowner realizes the danger is close.

Beyond fire, Lincoln's newer subdivisions often sit on expansive clay soils that shift seasonally, and that movement can stress window frames and door openings over time. A break-in or impact event on an already-stressed frame may leave gaps that standard plywood sizing doesn't cover cleanly — something our crews account for when they measure and cut on-site rather than arriving with pre-cut panels.

## Our Emergency Board-Up and Tarping Process in Lincoln

When you call (877) 688-0866, dispatch confirms your address and the nature of the opening — broken window, compromised door, missing roof section, or fire-damaged wall — so the crew arrives with the right materials loaded. On-site, the first step is a quick structural safety check: we don't send someone up a ladder onto a roof that may have hidden structural failure. Once the work zone is confirmed safe, we measure every opening, cut plywood to fit flush against the framing (not just over the opening), and secure it with construction screws rather than nails so the panels hold through wind events and can be removed cleanly when permanent repairs begin.

For roof tarping, we use heavy-duty polyethylene tarps with a minimum 6-mil thickness, weighted and fastened with cap nails and wood battens along the edges — not just draped over the peak. Lincoln's afternoon winds in spring and fall can lift an improperly secured tarp within hours, turning a temporary fix into a second damage event. We overlap the tarp at least two feet past every damaged edge and run the lower edge past the eave line so runoff sheds away from the structure rather than pooling against the fascia.

## Local Note

Lincoln's newer tile-roof subdivisions present a specific challenge that doesn't come up as often in older Sacramento neighborhoods: concrete tile is heavy, and when a section is damaged or displaced, the surrounding tiles often shift out of alignment without breaking. A tarp laid over misaligned tile can create a water channel that directs runoff straight into the gap rather than away from it. Our crews check the tile field around any damaged section before laying the tarp, reset loose tiles where it's safe to do so, and build up the tarp edge with battens that bridge the irregular surface. It's a small adjustment that makes a significant difference in how well the protection holds until a roofing contractor can complete the permanent repair.
