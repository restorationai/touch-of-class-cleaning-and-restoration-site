When a storm moves through, the damage isn't always obvious at first glance: a lifted ridge cap, a cracked fascia board, or siding that looks intact but has let water behind the sheathing. ## Why Modesto Properties Are Vulnerable to Storm Damage

The San Joaquin Valley's climate is deceptive. Summers are long and dry, which causes wood framing, trim, and caulking to contract and develop micro-gaps. When the wet season arrives, typically November through March, those gaps become entry points. A roof that looked fine in September can be admitting water by December.

Modesto's housing stock adds another layer of complexity. Much of the city's residential and light-commercial construction dates from the postwar boom through the 1970s, when roofing underlayment standards were less stringent and many homes were built with wood-frame walls that rely heavily on exterior cladding for weather resistance. When that cladding, whether original stucco, wood siding, or later vinyl, is breached by wind-driven debris or lifted by gusts, the underlying structure can absorb moisture quickly. Older stucco in particular can develop hairline fractures that are invisible until a hard rain reveals water staining on interior walls.

High-wind events in the valley also carry particulate, dust, debris, and small gravel, that abrades roofing surfaces over time, accelerating granule loss on asphalt shingles and creating conditions where a single storm does the damage that might take years in a calmer climate.

## Our Storm Damage Restoration Process in Modesto

Every job starts with a thorough exterior and interior assessment, not a quick walk-around, but a documented inspection that photographs every affected surface, measures moisture readings at wall cavities and subfloor assemblies, and identifies secondary damage that may not be visible yet. This documentation matters for insurance claims and for scoping the actual rebuild accurately.

Once the scope is established, the process typically moves through these stages:

- **Emergency stabilization**: tarping compromised roof sections, boarding damaged openings, and securing any structural elements at immediate risk. This stops the loss from growing while the full repair plan is finalized.
- **Structural and envelope repair**: roof decking, framing, sheathing, and underlayment are addressed before any finish work. For siding repairs, we match materials and profiles as closely as possible, a consideration that matters in older Modesto neighborhoods where original lap siding or stucco texture can be difficult to replicate.
- **Finish and weatherproofing**: new roofing, siding, trim, gutters, and flashing are installed and sealed. Final inspections confirm that the building envelope is restored to a watertight condition.

Because TDI USA handles both the restoration and the reconstruction under one roof, there's no handoff gap between the mitigation crew and the rebuild crew, the same licensed, insured team carries the project through to completion.

## Local Note

One pattern that shows up repeatedly in Modesto's older residential areas: homes built in the 1950s and 1960s often have original wood window and door headers that were never treated for moisture resistance. After a significant storm, these headers can absorb water through damaged trim or failed caulk lines and begin to swell or rot, but the problem doesn't show up on the interior for weeks. By the time a homeowner notices paint bubbling or a door that won't close properly, the header may need full replacement rather than a surface repair. During any post-storm inspection in these neighborhoods, we look specifically at header conditions around every exterior opening, not just the obvious damage points.
