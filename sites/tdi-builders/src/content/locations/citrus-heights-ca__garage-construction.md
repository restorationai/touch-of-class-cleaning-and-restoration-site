## Why Citrus Heights Properties Present Unique Garage Construction Challenges

The Sacramento Valley's clay-heavy soils expand when wet and contract sharply during the long dry season. For a garage slab or foundation, that cycle creates cumulative stress that shows up as cracking, heaving, or settlement if the footing design doesn't account for it. Citrus Heights was developed heavily from the 1950s through the 1980s, which means a large share of the housing stock sits on older foundations with varying degrees of soil prep underneath. Adding a new garage, attached or detached, next to one of those existing structures requires careful attention to how the new footings will behave relative to the old ones over time.

The city also falls under Sacramento County's building and zoning jurisdiction for most permitting matters, and setback requirements, lot coverage limits, and ADU conversion rules all factor into what's actually buildable on a given parcel.
