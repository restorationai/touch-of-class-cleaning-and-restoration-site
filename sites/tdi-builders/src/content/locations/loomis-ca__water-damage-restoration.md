Loomis sits in the western Sierra Nevada foothills where hot, dry summers give way to wet winters that can drop several inches of rain in a single storm — and when that moisture finds its way into a crawl space, a wall cavity, or under a hardwood floor, it moves fast. TDI Builders, Inc. has been responding to water losses across the Sacramento region since 1985, and the foothill communities along the I-80 corridor present their own specific challenges that flat-valley work simply doesn't prepare you for.

## Why Loomis Properties See Water Damage Issues

Loomis is largely a community of single-family homes on larger lots, many of them built in the 1970s through 1990s on pier-and-beam or raised foundations with unvented or partially vented crawl spaces. That foundation style is practical for the rolling terrain, but it creates a hidden reservoir problem: when a supply line fails or a roof penetration leaks during a Placer County winter storm, water pools in the crawl and wicks upward into subfloor sheathing and floor joists before anyone notices standing water above. By the time a homeowner smells something musty or feels a soft spot underfoot, the structural drying timeline has already stretched.

The clay-heavy soils common through this part of Placer County also complicate drainage. During heavy rain events, saturated ground can back-pressure perimeter drains and push water through foundation walls or low-lying slab transitions — a pattern that shows up repeatedly in older ranch-style homes on the east side of town near the Highway 193 corridor. Homes with mature landscaping and established irrigation systems add another variable: slow pinhole leaks in poly-butylene or galvanized supply lines often go undetected for weeks in wall cavities behind cabinetry.

## Our Water Damage Restoration Process in Loomis

When we arrive at a Loomis property, the first priority is stopping active water intrusion and mapping the full extent of moisture migration — not just the visible wet area. We use thermal imaging cameras and calibrated moisture meters to trace water movement through wall assemblies, across subfloor panels, and into adjacent rooms. Water follows framing cavities and gravity in ways that aren't obvious from the surface.

Once the scope is documented, water extraction begins with truck-mounted and portable extraction units suited to the floor type — carpet and pad pull differently than tongue-and-groove hardwood, and neither behaves like a concrete slab. Structural drying follows the IICRC S500 standard, using commercial-grade desiccant or refrigerant dehumidifiers and high-velocity air movers positioned to create a controlled drying chamber around affected materials. In Loomis homes with vaulted ceilings or exposed-beam construction — common in the custom builds throughout the area — we adjust equipment placement to account for the larger air volume.

Daily moisture readings track drying progress and determine when materials have reached acceptable equilibrium moisture content for the regional climate. We don't pull equipment early because the surface reads dry; we pull it when the readings confirm it throughout the assembly.

## Loomis Insurance Coordination

Most standard homeowner policies cover sudden and accidental water losses — a burst pipe, a washing machine supply line failure, an ice-maker line that lets go behind the refrigerator. What they don't cover is gradual leakage that a homeowner could reasonably have detected, which is why thorough documentation at the time of loss matters. We photograph affected materials, record moisture readings with date-stamped reports, and communicate directly with adjusters to support your claim. We work with the major carriers that write policies in Placer County and can explain the documentation process before we start work so there are no surprises on scope.

## Local Note

Loomis homes built in the 1980s and early 1990s frequently used polybutylene supply piping — a gray plastic material that was common across California foothill communities during that era and is now known to degrade and fail at fittings without warning. If your home was built during that period and hasn't had a plumbing inspection or repipe, a water loss event is often the first indication the pipe system is compromised. When we document a loss in one of these homes, we flag the pipe material in our report so your contractor and insurer have the full picture — because a second failure in a freshly dried wall is the worst outcome for everyone.
