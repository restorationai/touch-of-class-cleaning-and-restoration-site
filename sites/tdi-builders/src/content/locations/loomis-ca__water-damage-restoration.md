Loomis sits in the western Sierra Nevada foothills at an elevation where winter storms roll in hard off the mountains and summer thunderstorms can drop an inch of rain in under an hour. That combination, clay-heavy foothill soils that shed water quickly, aging ranch-style homes on large lots with long roof spans, and a water table that rises faster than most homeowners expect, means a slow roof leak or a burst supply line can saturate structural framing before the damage is visible at the surface. TDI USA has been working on exactly these kinds of losses since 1985, and the work here in Placer County is genuinely different from a flat-lot suburban job in the valley.

## Why Loomis Properties See Water Damage Differently Than Valley Towns

The foothill climate is the first factor. Loomis receives meaningfully more precipitation than Sacramento, and it arrives in concentrated bursts during atmospheric river events that hit the Sierra foothills hard. Homes here also tend to have crawl spaces rather than slab foundations, a smart choice for sloped lots, but one that creates a hidden reservoir when groundwater rises or a supply line weeps undetected. Moisture accumulates under the subfloor, wicks into floor joists, and can begin supporting mold colonization within 24 to 48 hours of saturation. By the time a homeowner notices a soft spot in the hardwood or a musty smell in a back bedroom, the structural damage has often been building for days.

Older ranch homes in Loomis, many built in the 1960s through the 1980s, frequently have original copper supply lines that have softened at the fittings, galvanized drain lines that corrode from the inside out, and plaster-over-lath wall assemblies in the older sections of the house. Plaster reads dry at the surface long before the lath behind it has released its moisture. Standard moisture meters can give a false-negative reading on plaster walls, which is why thermal imaging is part of the assessment on any Loomis job where the construction era is uncertain.

## Our Water Damage Restoration Process in Loomis

Every job starts with a full structural assessment before any drying equipment goes in. We use penetrating moisture meters, non-invasive thermal imaging, and, where needed, inspection ports cut into wall cavities to confirm saturation depth. That step matters more in foothill construction than in newer valley tract homes because the building assemblies are less predictable.

Once we know the full extent of the moisture intrusion, we establish a drying perimeter, sealing off affected areas, removing saturated materials that cannot be saved (insulation, compromised drywall, swollen subfloor panels), and positioning commercial-grade desiccant dehumidifiers and axial air movers calibrated to the actual cubic footage of the space. Drying logs are recorded daily and shared with your insurance adjuster so the claim file reflects real measured progress, not estimates.

When structural drying is complete and verified with final moisture readings, TDI USA handles the rebuild, drywall, ceiling repair, subfloor replacement, paint, and finish work, so you are not managing two separate contractors through a single loss event. That continuity matters when you are living in the house during restoration.

## Loomis Insurance Coordination

Most standard homeowners policies cover sudden and accidental water discharge, a burst pipe, a failed water heater, an appliance supply line failure. What they do not cover, and what adjusters will look for, is evidence of long-term seepage or deferred maintenance. Thorough documentation at the time of assessment protects your claim: we photograph every affected surface, log moisture readings by location, and produce a written scope of work that maps directly to the carrier's line-item format. We work with most major carriers and can communicate directly with your adjuster to keep the process moving.

## Local Note

One pattern that comes up repeatedly in Loomis and the surrounding foothill communities: homes on larger lots often have irrigation systems that run along the foundation perimeter, and the emitters nearest the house are frequently the last ones inspected. A slow drip from a cracked irrigation line against a stem wall can push moisture into a crawl space for an entire growing season without triggering any interior symptom. If the source of your water intrusion is not immediately obvious, no burst pipe, no roof damage after a storm, it is worth having the irrigation system pressure-checked before the drying phase closes out.
