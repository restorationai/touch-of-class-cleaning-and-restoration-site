## Why Rocklin Properties Experience Sewage Backup Issues

Rocklin's growth from a small granite-quarrying town into one of Placer County's fastest-growing cities means the housing stock spans several distinct eras — and each era carries its own plumbing vulnerabilities. Homes built in the 1970s and 1980s, particularly in established neighborhoods closer to Old Town Rocklin, often have clay or Orangeburg sewer laterals that have been slowly deteriorating for decades. Tree root intrusion is especially common in these older sections, where mature oak and pine root systems have had generations to find and infiltrate pipe joints.

Newer master-planned communities on Rocklin's eastern and northern edges tend to have PVC laterals in better condition, but they face a different challenge: the expansive clay subsoils common throughout the western Placer County foothills can shift laterals out of alignment during dry summers and wet winters, creating bellied pipes that collect solids and eventually back up. Rocklin's Mediterranean climate — dry, hot summers followed by concentrated winter rainfall — accelerates this cycle. A heavy January storm can saturate soil that has been bone-dry since May, and that sudden ground movement is often what tips an already-stressed lateral into failure.

## Our Sewage Cleanup and Sanitization Process in Rocklin

Raw sewage backup is a health emergency before it is a property damage claim. The moment our crew arrives, the priority is containment — stopping the spread of contaminated water to unaffected areas of the home before any extraction begins.

Here is what the process looks like from arrival to clearance:

- **Containment and safety assessment** — We identify the source, shut off contributing water supply if applicable, and establish physical containment barriers to protect clean areas.
- **Extraction** — Truck-mounted and portable extraction units remove standing sewage from flooring, subfloor cavities, and crawl spaces. In Rocklin homes with raised foundations — common in older sections of the city — crawl space extraction requires specialized equipment and protective protocols.
- **Removal of unsalvageable materials** — Porous materials that have absorbed Category 3 water (drywall, insulation, carpet, pad) are removed and bagged for disposal per Placer County waste handling guidelines.
- **Antimicrobial treatment and sanitization** — All affected structural surfaces are treated with EPA-registered disinfectants. This is not a one-step spray; it involves dwell time, mechanical agitation on porous surfaces, and a second application on high-contact areas.
- **Drying and monitoring** — Industrial air movers and dehumidifiers run until moisture readings in walls, subfloor, and framing return to pre-loss baselines. We monitor daily.
- **Documentation** — Every step is photographed and logged for your insurance carrier.
