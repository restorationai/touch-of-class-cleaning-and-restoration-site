## Why Fair Oaks Properties See Catastrophic Loss Events

The housing stock throughout Fair Oaks skews older — a significant portion of residential and light commercial buildings were constructed in the 1960s through the 1980s, when building envelopes, plumbing systems, and electrical panels were engineered to standards that have since been substantially revised. Galvanized steel supply lines common in that era are prone to sudden, high-volume failures rather than slow leaks, meaning a single pipe event can release thousands of gallons before a shutoff is reached. Sacramento County's clay-heavy soils also shift seasonally: wet winters cause expansion that stresses slab foundations, and the dry summers that follow cause contraction. Over decades, that cycle creates micro-fractures in slabs and utility penetrations that become catastrophic failure points during a major weather event or seismic tremor.

The region's fire exposure adds another layer. Fair Oaks borders wildland-urban interface zones, and while the community itself is largely suburban, wind-driven embers during a Diablo or Delta wind event can ignite multiple structures in a short window — exactly the scenario that overwhelms single-crew restoration operations and requires a coordinated large loss response.
