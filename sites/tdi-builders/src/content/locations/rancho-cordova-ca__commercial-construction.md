## Why Rancho Cordova's Commercial Building Stock Creates Unique Construction Challenges

Rancho Cordova's commercial inventory spans several distinct eras. Warehouse and light-industrial properties along the older sections of Mather Field Road and White Rock Road were built to mid-century standards, lower ceiling clearances, undersized electrical panels, and mechanical systems that predate modern HVAC load requirements. When a tenant signs a lease expecting open-plan offices or a healthcare buildout, those legacy conditions drive scope in ways that a landlord's base-building allowance rarely anticipates.

The city's Central Valley climate adds another layer. Summer temperatures routinely exceed 100°F, which means commercial HVAC design here isn't a checkbox, it's a core cost driver. A tenant improvement that looks straightforward on paper can require significant mechanical upgrades once a real load calculation is run against an aging rooftop unit. We factor that into preconstruction so cost surprises don't land mid-project.

Rancho Cordova also sits within Sacramento County's jurisdiction for most permitting, but the city processes its own building permits through the Community Development Department. Understanding which counter handles what, and how plan check cycles run for commercial tenant improvements versus ground-up construction, keeps projects moving instead of stalling in the queue.

## Local Note: Mather Field Road Corridor and Older Flex-Space Buildings

Contractors who haven't worked the older flex-space buildings along the Mather Field Road corridor sometimes underestimate what's inside the walls. Many of these structures were built or substantially modified in the 1970s and 1980s for light manufacturing or government use, and it's not uncommon to find non-standard framing, asbestos-containing materials in floor tile or ceiling texture, or electrical conduit runs that don't match any surviving as-built drawing. A thorough pre-demolition hazardous materials survey isn't optional on these properties, it's the step that keeps a tenant improvement from turning into an environmental remediation project mid-schedule. We build that survey into our preconstruction process on any building of that era.
