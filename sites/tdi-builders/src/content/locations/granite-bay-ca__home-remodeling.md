## Why Granite Bay Homes Present Unique Remodeling Considerations

Much of Granite Bay's residential stock was built during the late 1980s through the early 2000s, a construction era defined by tract-style framing, builder-grade fixtures, and interior layouts that prioritized square footage over livability. Homes from that period often have galley kitchens that feel undersized relative to the lot, master bathrooms with dated step-in tubs that no longer match how families actually use the space, and open-plan living areas that were roughed in but never finished.

The foothill climate adds a specific layer of complexity. Placer County's temperature swings — routinely exceeding 100°F in July and dipping below freezing on winter nights — stress tile adhesive, grout joints, and wood cabinetry in ways that coastal contractors don't always anticipate. Expansion and contraction cycles in concrete slab foundations are worth accounting for before any tile or hardwood floor installation begins. Getting that substrate assessment right at the planning stage prevents callbacks after the first summer.

HOA covenants are also a real factor in many Granite Bay communities. Exterior material changes, window replacements, and additions often require architectural review board approval before permits are pulled. Working through that process takes time, and skipping it creates problems at resale.
