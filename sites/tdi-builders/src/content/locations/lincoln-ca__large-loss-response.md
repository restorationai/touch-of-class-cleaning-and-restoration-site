TDI Builders, Inc. has been managing large-scale restoration and catastrophic response since 1985, and our Sacramento headquarters puts Lincoln well within our immediate response range around the clock.

## Why Lincoln Properties Face Elevated Large Loss Risk

Lincoln's explosive residential growth over the past two decades produced thousands of homes built in quick succession — tract construction with shared rooflines, common-area drainage systems, and interconnected utility runs. When one building envelope fails during a heavy atmospheric river event, water doesn't stop at a single property line. Slab-on-grade foundations common throughout Lincoln's newer developments can trap moisture beneath flooring systems, making what looks like a contained loss far larger once thermal imaging and moisture mapping are complete.

Lincoln also sits in Placer County's wildland-urban interface. Fire risk is real and seasonal, and a structure fire in a densely built community can involve multiple units, shared attic spaces, and HVAC systems that distribute smoke and soot across a building before the fire department clears the scene. That combination of fire, smoke, water from suppression, and potential structural compromise is exactly the scenario that demands a large loss team — not a standard crew with a few dehumidifiers.

## Our Large Loss and Catastrophic Response Process in Lincoln

When a call comes in from a Lincoln property — whether it's a commercial building, a multi-family complex, or a large single-family home — the first step is a rapid site assessment by a senior project manager, not a sales representative. We document the full scope using thermal imaging, moisture mapping, and structural evaluation before a single line item goes on paper. That documentation is what large loss adjusters need, and it's what protects the property owner from a claim that gets disputed or underpaid.

From there, the work moves in coordinated phases: emergency stabilization and board-up, water extraction and structural drying per IICRC standards, contents inventory and pack-out if the structure requires it, and then a full reconstruction scope developed in direct coordination with the carrier. Our IICRC certified technicians manage each phase, and our licensed and insured status means carriers aren't looking for a reason to delay approval.

For commercial losses — warehouses, retail centers, office buildings — we maintain the equipment inventory to run large-scale drying operations without subcontracting the mitigation to a third party. That single-source accountability matters when a business interruption clock is running.

## Lincoln Insurance and Large Loss Adjuster Coordination

Large loss claims in Placer County routinely involve multiple coverage layers — property, business interruption, and sometimes shared HOA master policies for common-area damage. We work directly with large loss adjusters and third-party administrators, providing the photo documentation, moisture logs, and scope narratives that support a clean claim. For commercial properties and multi-family owners, we can also provide preliminary scope estimates that help carriers assign the right adjuster tier from the start, which shortens the approval timeline considerably.

Lincoln's newer master-planned communities often carry HOA master policies that cover exterior and structural elements while individual unit owners carry interior coverage. ## Local Note

Lincoln's growth has outpaced some of its utility and drainage infrastructure in certain areas, and during heavy rain years, storm drain systems in newer subdivisions can back up in ways that push water toward garage slabs and first-floor living spaces. Properties built on the western slopes of the development areas tend to see this more than those on higher pads. If you're managing a loss in one of these lower-lying sections and the source isn't immediately obvious, the water may be migrating from a shared drainage easement rather than a failed interior component — something worth flagging to your adjuster before the scope is written.
