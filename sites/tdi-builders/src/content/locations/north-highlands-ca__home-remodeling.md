Most of the remodeling calls we get from North Highlands start the same way: a homeowner has outgrown a 1,100-square-foot ranch house built during the Sacramento area's postwar boom, and the galley kitchen, single bathroom, and closed-off floor plan just don't work for how the family actually lives now. Home remodeling here isn't about chasing trends, it's about reworking a slab-on-grade layout that was never designed for central air, modern wiring loads, or open living.

## Why North Highlands Homes Need a Different Remodeling Approach

A large share of North Highlands housing stock dates to the 1950s and 60s, built quickly and economically as Sacramento County's suburbs expanded. These homes typically sit on concrete slabs rather than raised foundations, which limits where plumbing and electrical can be rerouted without saw-cutting concrete. Many still carry original 100-amp electrical panels sized for window-unit air conditioning, not the central HVAC, tankless water heaters, or EV chargers homeowners want installed today. Central Valley summers push attic temperatures well past what older insulation and ductwork were built to handle, so a remodel that doesn't address insulation and ventilation alongside the visible finishes tends to leave homeowners with a beautiful kitchen and a house that still runs hot through July and August.

## Our Home Remodeling Process in North Highlands

We start with a walk-through that pays as much attention to the panel, the attic, and the slab as it does to cabinetry and flooring. For slab homes, that means mapping existing plumbing runs before we commit to a new kitchen or bathroom layout, since moving fixtures usually means core-drilling rather than a simple reroute. Once design and scope are locked, we sequence demolition, rough mechanical and electrical, inspections, and finish work so the house stays livable as long as possible during the project. On older North Highlands homes we also check panel capacity early, since a remodel that adds a dishwasher, microwave circuit, and new lighting load can push an aging 100-amp service past what code and safety allow, and that's a conversation better had in week one than week six.

## Local note

One thing we watch for in North Highlands specifically: a lot of the ranch-style homes built in this era have additions or garage conversions done without permits sometime in the past few decades. Before we price a remodel that touches those spaces, we confirm what's actually permitted on record with Sacramento County, because unpermitted square footage can affect both the scope of work and how the project gets inspected and closed out.
