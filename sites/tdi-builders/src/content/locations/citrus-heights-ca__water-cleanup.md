## Why Citrus Heights Properties See Water Damage Differently

Much of Citrus Heights was developed in the 1950s through the 1970s, when tract housing pushed east from Sacramento along the Auburn Boulevard and Sunrise Boulevard corridors. Homes from that era commonly have galvanized steel supply lines that have long since passed their service life — a slow pinhole leak behind a cabinet can saturate particleboard subfloor and wall framing for weeks before it surfaces. Older slab-on-grade construction is also common throughout the city, which means water that migrates under a slab has nowhere obvious to go; it wicks upward through the concrete and into flooring adhesives, hardwood, and carpet padding.

The region's Mediterranean climate adds another layer. Citrus Heights gets the majority of its rainfall between November and March, and when back-to-back atmospheric river events hit the Sacramento Valley, area storm drains and residential French drains can back up within hours. A garage or finished basement that stayed dry for twenty years can flood in a single night.

## Our Water Cleanup Process in Citrus Heights

When we arrive, the first priority is stopping the source — whether that means shutting the main at the street, isolating a failed appliance valve, or identifying where surface water is entering. From there, the process follows a documented sequence calibrated to what we actually find:

- **Moisture mapping** using thermal imaging and calibrated meters to locate water behind walls and under flooring before any material is moved
- **Standing water extraction** with truck-mounted and portable units capable of pulling hundreds of gallons from slab-level floors where gravity drainage is limited
- **Structural drying** with high-velocity air movers and refrigerant or desiccant dehumidifiers, placed according to psychrometric readings rather than guesswork
- **Daily monitoring** of moisture content in framing, subfloor, and wall cavities until readings reach the dry standard for the material type
- **Controlled demolition** only where necessary — wet insulation, saturated drywall, and compromised flooring are removed to allow drying of what's behind them, not as a default first step

Because slab foundations are so prevalent here, we pay particular attention to the concrete-to-flooring interface. Moisture trapped under luxury vinyl plank or tile can read dry at the surface while the adhesive layer beneath remains saturated — a condition that leads to buckling and mold growth weeks after a crew that didn't check has left.

## Citrus Heights Insurance Coordination

Most standard homeowners policies cover sudden and accidental water losses — a burst pipe, a washing machine supply line failure, an ice maker line that let go overnight. What they typically exclude is long-term seepage or gradual leaks, which is why thorough documentation at the time of loss matters. We photograph affected materials, log moisture readings, and produce a scope of work in a format carriers recognize. We bill major insurance companies directly and can communicate with your adjuster throughout the process so you're not translating between a restoration crew and a claims desk.

## Local Note

Citrus Heights homes built on expansive clay soils — common throughout Sacramento County — can develop hairline foundation cracks over decades of seasonal soil movement. During a water loss, those cracks become pathways. Water that appears to be coming from a plumbing failure inside the home is sometimes also drawing in from the exterior through the foundation perimeter. If moisture readings near exterior walls stay elevated after the interior source is addressed, it's worth probing the foundation line before closing out the drying phase. This is a detail that matters specifically in this region's soil conditions and is easy to miss if a crew is working from a generic protocol.
