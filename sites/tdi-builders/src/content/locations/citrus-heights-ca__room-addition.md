## Why Citrus Heights Homes Present Unique Addition Challenges

Much of Citrus Heights was built during the postwar suburban expansion of the 1950s through 1970s, which means the housing stock tends to share a few common traits: slab-on-grade foundations, single-story layouts, and older electrical panels that weren't sized for today's loads. When you add square footage to a home like this, the work rarely stops at framing. Expanding a slab foundation in Sacramento County's reactive clay soil requires careful geotechnical consideration — clay that swells when wet and shrinks when dry can stress footings in ways that show up years later as cracked tile or sticking doors if the foundation work isn't done right the first time.

Beyond the soil, California's Title 24 energy code applies to new conditioned space, meaning any addition needs to meet current insulation, glazing, and HVAC efficiency standards even if the rest of the house predates those requirements. For homeowners in Citrus Heights, that often means upgrading the main electrical panel and sometimes the HVAC system to serve the new square footage — costs worth understanding before the permit application goes in.

## Our Room Addition Process in Citrus Heights

Every project starts with a site visit, not a phone estimate. We walk the property, review the existing structure, and identify anything that could affect scope — an aging sub-panel, a setback that limits how far a bump-out can extend toward the property line, or a roof pitch that complicates tying in a new addition cleanly. From there, we handle design coordination, permit drawings, and submission to Sacramento County's Building Permits and Inspections division, which oversees permitting for Citrus Heights.

Once permits are issued, the sequence typically runs: foundation work, framing, rough mechanical (electrical, plumbing, HVAC), insulation and Title 24 compliance inspection, drywall, finish work, and final inspection. We keep homeowners informed at each phase change so there are no surprises when an inspector needs access or a material lead time pushes a milestone.

For bump-out additions — where the footprint extends just a few feet without a full foundation pour — we assess whether the existing slab edge can be extended or whether a new isolated footing is needed. In Citrus Heights's clay-soil conditions, that decision matters structurally, and we don't cut corners on it.

## Permits, HOAs, and Local Coordination

Citrus Heights is an incorporated city, which means building permits are issued through the City of Citrus Heights Community Development Department rather than the county — an important distinction if you've gotten quotes from contractors who assumed county jurisdiction. The city has its own plan check process and inspection schedule, and familiarity with that workflow helps keep projects moving rather than stalled waiting for resubmittals.

Some neighborhoods in Citrus Heights fall under homeowners association oversight, which can add an architectural review step before or alongside the city permit process. We flag HOA requirements early and help homeowners understand what documentation the association typically needs — site plans, exterior material samples, elevation drawings — so that review doesn't become a bottleneck after construction is already underway.
