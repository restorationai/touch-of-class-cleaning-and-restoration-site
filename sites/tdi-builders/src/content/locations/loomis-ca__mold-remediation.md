Loomis sits in the western Sierra Nevada foothills where warm, dry summers give way to wet winters fed by Pacific storm systems rolling up the I-80 corridor. That seasonal swing — months of near-drought followed by heavy rainfall soaking into clay-heavy soils — creates exactly the moisture cycling that lets mold take hold inside walls, under subfloors, and in crawl spaces before a homeowner notices anything more than a faint musty odor. If you're searching for mold remediation in Loomis, the conditions driving that search are real, and they're specific to this part of Placer County.

## Why Loomis Properties See Mold Issues

The foothills climate here is deceptive. During the dry season, homes ventilate well and surfaces feel bone-dry. Then the rains arrive — sometimes hard and fast — and water finds every gap in older rooflines, around window flashing, and through foundation stem walls that weren't sealed for modern moisture loads. Loomis has a meaningful share of homes built in the 1960s through 1980s, when vapor barriers in crawl spaces were inconsistently installed and bathroom ventilation often exhausted into attic cavities rather than outside. Add the region's clay-dense soils, which drain slowly and hold moisture against foundation walls long after a storm passes, and you have conditions that keep wood framing and insulation damp well into spring.

Mold doesn't need a flood to colonize. Persistent relative humidity above 60% for 48 to 72 hours is enough for spores already present in the environment to begin growing on drywall paper, OSB sheathing, and cellulose insulation. In a Loomis crawl space that stays shaded and poorly ventilated from November through March, that threshold is easy to exceed without a single visible leak.

## Our Mold Remediation Process in Loomis

Every remediation starts with a thorough assessment — not a visual scan, but moisture mapping with calibrated meters that read behind surfaces. Mold visible on a baseboard is often the trailing edge of a colony that has already moved into the wall cavity. Knowing the full moisture map before any demolition begins prevents incomplete remediation and failed post-clearance testing.

Once the scope is defined, physical containment goes up: heavy-gauge poly barriers, negative air pressure with HEPA-filtered air scrubbers exhausting to the exterior, and zipper-door access to keep spores from migrating into unaffected living areas. Affected materials — drywall, insulation, compromised framing — are removed, bagged, and disposed of per California Department of Public Health guidelines. Remaining structural surfaces are HEPA-vacuumed and treated with an EPA-registered antimicrobial agent.

Drying is not optional after remediation. Structural wood that tests above 19% moisture content by weight will re-colonize. Commercial desiccant or refrigerant dehumidifiers run until readings confirm the substrate is genuinely dry, not just surface-dry. Only then does reconstruction begin — and because TDI Builders, Inc. is a full construction and restoration firm with more than 40 years in the trade, remediation and rebuild happen under one roof rather than requiring a homeowner to coordinate two separate contractors.
