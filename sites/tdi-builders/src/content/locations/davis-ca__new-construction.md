Building a new home in Davis starts with a conversation most buyers do not expect: what's under the lot. Yolo County's valley clay shifts with the seasons, and a foundation plan that works fine in the Sacramento foothills can crack or heave here if it is not engineered for local soil. New home construction in Davis means starting with the ground, not the floor plan, and working outward from there.

## Why Davis Lots Need Different Groundwork

Much of Davis sits on expansive clay typical of the Central Valley floor. That soil swells when winter rain soaks in and shrinks hard during the dry, often triple-digit summer stretch. Homes built on a standard slab without that movement in mind tend to show stress cracks in stucco and drywall within a few years. Before we finalize a design, we look at soil reports and grading so the foundation, whether slab-on-grade or raised, is matched to how the lot actually behaves across a full year, not just how it looks on the day of the pour.

Davis's climate adds its own wrinkle. Summers run hot and dry, tempered some evenings by the Delta breeze pulling in from the bay, and winters bring stretches of tule fog that can sit over the valley for days. Framing schedules, concrete cure times, and even paint and stucco application windows get planned around those patterns rather than a generic build calendar.

## Our New Home Construction Process in Davis

Every ground-up build we take on in Davis follows the same backbone, adjusted for the lot:

- **Site and soil review** to confirm foundation type and drainage before design is locked
- **Plan development and engineering**, built around the lot's orientation and the valley's heat and sun exposure
- **Permitting coordination** with the city's building division, including energy code compliance
- **Foundation and framing**, sequenced around weather windows rather than a fixed calendar
- **Mechanical, electrical, and plumbing rough-in**, with attention to energy-efficient systems given Davis's long push toward low-carbon new construction
- **Finish work and final walkthrough**, with documentation handed over for inspections and warranty records

We treat the soil and permitting steps as the ones that actually determine whether the rest of the schedule holds, which is why they come first instead of being an afterthought between design and framing.

## Davis Permitting and Code Coordination

Davis has built a reputation as one of the more sustainability-forward cities in the region, and that shows up in new construction requirements that often ask for more than the statewide Title 24 baseline, particularly around solar readiness and energy performance. We coordinate directly with the city's building division on plan review and inspections so a design doesn't stall mid-permit over an energy or efficiency detail that could have been caught at the drafting stage. For infill lots near the university side of town, where setbacks and neighboring structures are tighter, we also factor in how a new build will sit relative to existing homes, since much of that older housing stock dates to the postwar decades and sits close to the street.

## Local Note

One thing only a crew working Yolo County lots regularly will flag: the same clay that causes foundation movement also drains slowly after a wet winter. We've learned not to schedule foundation pours right after a soaking rain even if the forecast looks clear, because the subgrade can hold moisture longer than it appears to on the surface. Waiting an extra few days for the pad to actually dry out, not just look dry, saves problems that would otherwise show up as settling cracks a year or two later.
