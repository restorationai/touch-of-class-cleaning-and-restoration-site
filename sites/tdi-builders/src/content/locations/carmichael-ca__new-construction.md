## What Makes New Home Construction in Carmichael Different

Carmichael developed heavily in the 1950s through the 1970s, which means most available infill lots sit in neighborhoods where the surrounding homes were built under code standards that look nothing like today's California Building Code requirements. When you tear down an older structure to build new, or develop one of the remaining vacant parcels, the project immediately involves current Title 24 energy compliance, Sacramento County's grading and drainage requirements, and — depending on the parcel — proximity to the American River Parkway corridor, which adds environmental review considerations that don't apply in newer master-planned communities farther east.

The valley clay soils common throughout Carmichael expand and contract seasonally with the wet-dry cycle that defines Sacramento's Mediterranean climate. A proper soils report and engineered foundation design aren't optional here — they're the difference between a slab that performs for decades and one that telegraphs every dry summer in hairline cracks. We coordinate geotechnical review early in the design phase so foundation specs reflect actual site conditions, not generic assumptions.

## Our New Home Construction Process in Carmichael

Every ground-up build starts with a site assessment and pre-design consultation. For Carmichael parcels, that means reviewing Sacramento County zoning (most of the area falls under county jurisdiction rather than an incorporated city), confirming setback requirements, and identifying any protected trees — Carmichael's mature valley oaks can trigger arborist review that affects where a foundation footprint can legally land.

From there, the process moves through design coordination, permit submittal to Sacramento County's Building Inspection Division, and a construction schedule that accounts for the region's weather windows. Framing and exterior work move fastest in the late spring and early fall; we sequence accordingly to avoid the dead heat of July and August for work that's sensitive to temperature and humidity, and to keep interior finishes from going in during the rainy season before the building envelope is fully closed.

Throughout construction, our licensed and insured crews handle structural, mechanical rough-in coordination, and finish work under a single contract — no handoff gaps where accountability gets lost between subs.

## Local Note

Carmichael has a higher concentration of protected native oaks than most Sacramento County communities of similar size, and the county's tree preservation ordinance has real teeth. On infill lots where a heritage oak sits near the proposed building envelope, the county may require a certified arborist to establish a protection zone that effectively moves the foundation footprint — sometimes by enough to change the floor plan. Experienced local builders know to get the arborist on-site before finalizing design, not after permit submittal. It saves weeks and avoids the frustration of redesigning a plan the owner already loves.
