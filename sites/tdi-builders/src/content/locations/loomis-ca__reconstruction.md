## Why Loomis Properties Face Distinct Reconstruction Challenges

Placer County's foothill corridor, including Loomis and the surrounding communities along the I-80 corridor, sits in a climate band that swings between dry, fire-prone summers and wet winters that can deliver significant rainfall in short windows. That combination is hard on structures. A roof compromised by ember cast during a wildfire event, left exposed through even a moderate rain season, can see secondary water intrusion that damages framing, insulation, and interior finishes well beyond the original fire perimeter.

The housing stock in Loomis also spans a wide range of construction eras. Older ranch-style homes on larger parcels — many built in the 1960s and 1970s — often used construction methods and materials that are no longer code-compliant. When reconstruction triggers a permit, Placer County building inspectors will apply current code to the rebuilt portions, which can mean upgrading electrical panels, adding seismic strapping, or bringing insulation values up to Title 24 standards. These aren't surprises if you've worked in the area; they're part of every scoping conversation.

## Our Reconstruction Process in Loomis

Reconstruction after a significant loss follows a sequence that has to be disciplined to protect both the property and the insurance claim. The process begins with a thorough structural assessment — not a visual walk-through, but a documented evaluation of load-bearing elements, moisture content in framing members, and the integrity of any systems (electrical, plumbing, HVAC) that survived the event.

From there, the work moves through demolition of unsalvageable materials, temporary weatherproofing if the structure is exposed, and then permitted reconstruction that aligns with Placer County's current building requirements. Because TDI Builders, Inc. has been doing this work since 1985, the team understands how to sequence trades so that inspections don't create bottlenecks — a framing inspection cleared before drywall goes up, rough mechanical signed off before insulation is installed. That sequencing discipline keeps a project moving and keeps the insurance carrier's timeline expectations realistic.

For commercial properties, the process also involves coordinating with business continuity needs where possible — phasing work so that portions of a building can remain operational, or accelerating specific scopes to reduce the duration of a full closure.
