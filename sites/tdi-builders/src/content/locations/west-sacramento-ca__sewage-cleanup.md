## Why West Sacramento Properties See Sewage Backup Issues

The city's geography creates a specific set of plumbing vulnerabilities that don't show up the same way across the river in Sacramento proper. Much of West Sacramento was developed on reclaimed floodplain, and the clay-dominant soils expand and contract with the wet and dry seasons — California's Central Valley swings hard between the two. That seasonal movement cracks and offsets older clay-tile sewer laterals, which were the standard installation material in homes built through the mid-twentieth century. Tree root intrusion accelerates the problem: mature street trees planted along residential corridors send roots toward any moisture source, and a hairline crack in a lateral is an open invitation.

The city also operates a combined municipal sewer system in older sections, meaning heavy winter rain events can overwhelm capacity and push sewage backward through residential connections. Homeowners in lower-elevation neighborhoods near the river corridor are especially exposed during high-flow periods on the Sacramento River, when the water table rises and reduces the gradient that gravity-fed lines depend on.

## Our Sewage Cleanup and Sanitization Process in West Sacramento

Raw sewage is classified as Category 3 water — the most contaminated type — and the cleanup protocol reflects that. When our crew arrives, the first priority is stopping ongoing exposure: we establish containment, ventilate the space to the exterior, and identify whether the source line has been cleared or is still active. We coordinate with West Sacramento's public works line if the backup traces to a municipal main rather than the private lateral.

Extraction comes next. Standing sewage is removed with truck-mounted extraction equipment, and affected porous materials — drywall, insulation, carpet, subfloor — are assessed for removal. In slab-on-grade construction, which is common throughout West Sacramento's postwar residential stock, sewage can migrate under the slab through expansion joints; we use moisture mapping and thermal imaging to trace the full extent before any drying equipment goes down.

Sanitization follows extraction. We apply EPA-registered antimicrobial agents to all affected structural surfaces, with dwell times calibrated to the contamination level. Odor control — including hydroxyl or ozone treatment depending on the space — runs concurrently with drying. Final clearance testing confirms that microbial levels have returned to background before we close out the job.
