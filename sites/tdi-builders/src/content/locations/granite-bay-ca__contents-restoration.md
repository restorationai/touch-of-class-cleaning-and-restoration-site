When a burst pipe soaks through the built-ins of a custom home off Douglas Boulevard, or wildfire smoke from the surrounding foothill corridors settles into upholstered furniture and cabinetry, the contents inside those homes need more than a wipe-down — they need a structured, documented restoration process. TDI Builders has been handling exactly that since 1985, coordinating pack-out, cleaning, storage, and return for Granite Bay homeowners navigating some of the most disruptive events of their lives.

## Why Granite Bay Homes Present Unique Contents Challenges

Granite Bay's housing stock skews toward larger, custom-built and semi-custom homes constructed from the late 1980s through the 2000s — a period when vaulted ceilings, open floor plans, and high-end finishes were standard. That means more square footage of contents to protect, more specialty materials (hardwood cabinetry, stone countertops, custom drapery, built-in electronics) that require careful handling, and more surface area for smoke odor and soot to penetrate.

The foothills climate adds another layer. Summer temperatures routinely climb well above 100°F, and the low humidity that follows a rain event can cause wood furniture and artwork to dry unevenly — warping frames, cracking veneer, and setting stains if contents aren't moved to a climate-controlled environment quickly. After a water loss, contents left in place during the drying phase are exposed to both residual moisture and rapid temperature swings that accelerate secondary damage. Pack-out isn't just a convenience here — it's often the difference between restoring a piece and replacing it.

Wildfire smoke is a recurring concern throughout Placer County. Even when a fire burns miles away, fine particulate and volatile organic compounds travel on the prevailing winds and infiltrate homes through HVAC systems, window gaps, and attic vents. Smoke odor embeds differently in natural fibers, leather, and porous wood than it does in synthetic materials, and the longer it sits, the deeper it migrates.

## Our Contents Restoration and Storage Process in Granite Bay

Every contents job starts with a detailed inventory. Before a single item leaves your home, our team photographs, barcodes, and logs it — furniture, clothing, electronics, art, documents, and valuables alike. That inventory becomes the foundation for your insurance claim and your peace of mind.

From there, contents are transported to our climate-controlled storage facility, where they're protected from the temperature extremes that Granite Bay's foothill climate can produce. Cleaning methods are matched to the material: ultrasonic cleaning for hard goods and electronics, ozone and hydroxyl treatment for smoke-affected textiles and upholstery, and dry-cleaning or wet-cleaning protocols for clothing and linens. Structural pieces like cabinetry components and built-ins are assessed on-site before any decision is made about removal.

Once your home has been restored and cleared, we coordinate the return and reinstallation of your belongings — placing items back in the rooms they came from, not just staging them in the garage. Our IICRC certified technicians document the condition of contents at every stage, which matters when your adjuster is reviewing the claim.

## Local Note

Granite Bay's proximity to the Sierra Nevada foothills means that even homes not directly threatened by wildfire can accumulate a meaningful smoke load during fire season. What many homeowners don't realize is that the type of smoke matters: wildfires burning through dry grass and chaparral produce a different residue chemistry than structure fires, and that residue can be more difficult to neutralize on porous surfaces like unfinished wood, natural stone, and raw linen. If your home took on smoke odor during a regional fire event — even without visible soot — it's worth having contents assessed before assuming the smell will air out on its own.
