## Why Rocklin Properties Are Exposed to Catastrophic Loss

Rocklin sits at the edge of the Sierra Nevada foothills in Placer County, and that geography shapes the risk profile for large-loss events in ways that aren't always obvious from a standard property inspection. The region's Mediterranean climate means long, dry summers followed by concentrated wet seasons — a pattern that drives both wildfire exposure and sudden, high-volume water intrusion when the rains finally arrive. Flat commercial roofs that performed fine through October can fail under a December atmospheric river event, sending thousands of gallons into tenant spaces before a building engineer is even on site.

The construction boom that reshaped Rocklin through the 1990s and 2000s left a significant amount of commercial and light-industrial square footage built to code standards that predate current fire-suppression and moisture-barrier requirements. Older tilt-up concrete buildings common in Rocklin's business parks can trap moisture inside wall cavities for weeks after a water event, because the concrete itself reads dry at the surface while framing and insulation behind it remain saturated. That hidden saturation is exactly the kind of condition that turns a manageable loss into a multi-million-dollar remediation if it isn't caught early with thermal imaging and moisture mapping.
