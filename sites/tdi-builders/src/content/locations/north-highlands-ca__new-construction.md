Ground-up construction in North Highlands comes with a different set of variables than building in the newer subdivisions ringing Sacramento. Between expansive clay soils, a Central Valley climate that swings from triple-digit summers to wet winters, and a building stock that mixes mid-century ranch homes with newer infill lots, a new home here needs a foundation plan and a crew that already understands the ground they're pouring into.

## Why New Construction Looks Different in North Highlands

Much of North Highlands sits on Sacramento County's heavier clay soils, the kind that expand when wet and contract hard during the dry months. That movement is the reason foundation design matters more here than in sandier parts of the region: a slab poured without the right reinforcement or moisture barrier can develop stress cracks within a few seasons. The area's post-war growth also means a lot of surrounding lots back up to older ranch-style homes on smaller parcels, which affects setbacks, easements, and how a new structure needs to be sited relative to existing property lines. We factor all of that into the site plan before a shovel goes in the ground, not after.

## Our New Home Construction Process in North Highlands

We start with a soils report and a site walk, because guessing at clay content on an unincorporated Sacramento County lot is how foundations fail early. From there the sequence is straightforward but disciplined: grading and compaction suited to the soil type, footing and foundation work with reinforcement sized to local conditions, framing, rough mechanical and electrical, and exterior envelope work timed around the valley's rain windows so framing isn't sitting exposed through a wet stretch. Inspections are scheduled around Sacramento County's permitting process, and we build in buffer time for re-inspections rather than promising a date we can't hold. Clients get a realistic schedule up front, with the honest caveat that weather and inspection queues can shift it by a week or two either direction.

## North Highlands Permitting & Site Coordination

Because North Highlands is unincorporated, permitting runs through Sacramento County rather than a city building department, which means the review process, fee structure, and inspection scheduling follow county timelines. We handle that submission and coordination directly, including utility connections, septic or sewer verification where applicable, and any drainage requirements tied to the clay soil on a given lot. If a property backs onto an older subdivision with its own easement or access quirks, we identify that during the design phase so it doesn't surface as a surprise during framing.

## Local note

One thing we watch closely on North Highlands lots: shallow groundwater after a wet winter can sit higher than homeowners expect, especially on parcels near low-lying drainage paths. A foundation design that looks fine on paper in August can need adjusted footing depth or additional drainage if the soil was tested during a dry spell. We prefer to pull soil samples at more than one point on the lot, and at more than one time of year when the build schedule allows it, so the foundation plan reflects how the ground actually behaves rather than how it behaved on the day someone drilled a test hole.
