## Why Citrus Heights Homes Present Unique Renovation Challenges

The Central Valley's climate is not gentle on building envelopes. Summers push well past 100°F for weeks at a stretch, and the thermal cycling between those scorching days and cool valley nights causes wood framing, stucco exteriors, and concrete slabs to expand and contract in ways that accelerate wear. Homes built before the mid-1980s, a large share of the Citrus Heights housing stock, were constructed before California's Title 24 energy code had teeth, meaning insulation levels, window performance, and HVAC sizing are frequently inadequate by current standards.

Beyond energy performance, the region's expansive clay soils shift seasonally with wet winters and dry summers. That movement telegraphs into foundations, door frames, and tile work over time. A bathroom remodel that seems straightforward can reveal cracked backer board, failed mortar beds, or subfloor deflection that needs to be addressed before new tile goes down. Understanding that sequence, and budgeting for it honestly, is part of what separates a general contractor with real regional experience from one who is learning on your project.

Sacramento County also requires permits for most structural, electrical, plumbing, and mechanical work. Pulling permits correctly, scheduling inspections, and keeping a project on track through that process is a core part of TDI Builders' work in Citrus Heights, not an afterthought.

## Local Note

One thing worth knowing if you own a home built in Citrus Heights during the tract-development boom of the late 1960s through early 1980s: many of these houses were built on post-tension concrete slabs, a foundation method that was common in the Sacramento Valley because of those expansive clay soils. Post-tension slabs require specific care during any work that involves cutting or coring concrete, for plumbing reroutes, floor drain additions, or in-slab radiant heat. Cutting a post-tension cable without knowing where it runs can cause serious structural damage. A general contractor who knows the local housing stock will pull the slab plans or use a cable locator before any concrete work begins. It is a step that adds a little time and saves a great deal of trouble.
