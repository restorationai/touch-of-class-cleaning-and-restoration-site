Citrus Heights sits in Sacramento County's inland valley, where hot, dry summers bake structures and occasional winter storms push water into foundations and wall cavities — a cycle that accelerates deterioration in ways that don't always show up until a fire, flood, or roof failure finally exposes what's been building underneath. When that moment arrives, the reconstruction work that follows isn't just cosmetic. It's structural, it's code-driven, and in a city where much of the housing stock was built during the postwar and 1960s–70s tract-home boom, it often uncovers deferred issues that a straight repair estimate never anticipated. TDI Builders, Inc. has been navigating exactly this kind of complexity since 1985.

## Why Citrus Heights Properties Face Distinct Reconstruction Challenges

The Sacramento Valley's climate puts Citrus Heights buildings through a punishing seasonal rhythm. Summer temperatures routinely push past 100°F, causing wood framing to expand and contract in ways that open gaps at sheathing joints and around window rough openings. Winter rains — especially in years following drought, when hardpan soil has lost its absorption capacity — send surface water into crawl spaces and against stem walls that weren't designed for prolonged saturation.

The city's predominant housing stock compounds this. Ranch-style and split-level homes built in the 1960s and 1970s frequently used original-growth lumber that is denser and more resistant than modern dimensional lumber, but those same structures often have galvanized plumbing approaching the end of its service life, original aluminum wiring in some units, and insulation that doesn't meet current Title 24 energy standards. When a loss event — fire, water intrusion, storm damage — triggers a reconstruction permit, Sacramento County's building department requires that the rebuilt portions comply with current code, which can mean upgrading electrical panels, adding seismic strapping, or improving attic insulation even when those systems weren't directly damaged.

## Our Reconstruction Process in Citrus Heights

Every reconstruction project starts with a scope document, not a ballpark. Before a single wall is framed or a subfloor is replaced, our IICRC certified technicians and licensed construction team walk the structure together to separate what's genuinely damaged from what only appears damaged — a distinction that matters enormously when you're working with an insurance adjuster's line-item estimate.

From there, the process moves in a deliberate sequence:

- **Structural stabilization** — temporary shoring, tarping, or board-up if the structure is exposed, followed by demolition of only the material that cannot be restored.
- **Rough-in trades** — framing, mechanical, electrical, and plumbing rough-ins scheduled to minimize the number of open-wall inspections required by the county.
- **Inspections and approvals** — Sacramento County building inspections are coordinated around your timeline, not ours. We pull permits, schedule inspections, and track correction notices so you're not managing a bureaucratic backlog on top of a damaged property.
- **Finish work** — drywall, texture, paint, flooring, cabinetry, and exterior finishes matched to existing materials where possible.
- **Final walkthrough** — a documented punch-list review before we close the permit.

For commercial properties in Citrus Heights — retail, office, or light industrial — we also coordinate with Sacramento County's commercial plan check process, which runs on a separate track from residential and typically requires stamped engineering drawings for structural work.

## Citrus Heights Insurance Coordination

Most post-damage reconstruction in Citrus Heights runs through a homeowner's or commercial property policy, and the gap between what an adjuster initially estimates and what the work actually costs is one of the most consistent friction points we see. Scope creep isn't a contractor problem — it's a physics problem. Water migrates. Fire char travels further into framing than it looks. Smoke residue settles into HVAC systems that weren't in the original estimate.

We document losses with photographs, moisture readings, and written scope narratives that adjusters can work from directly. We're licensed and insured, and we communicate with carriers in the language they use — line items, depreciation schedules, code-upgrade supplements — so that disputes get resolved at the desk rather than after work has already started.

## Local Note

One pattern that shows up repeatedly in Citrus Heights reconstruction projects is the interaction between original post-tension slab foundations — common in tracts built through the 1970s — and the repair work that follows a water or fire loss. Post-tension slabs contain tensioned steel cables that cannot be cut without engineering review, which means any penetration for new plumbing or electrical conduit requires a structural engineer's sign-off before a saw touches the concrete. Contractors unfamiliar with this construction type sometimes discover the cables mid-cut. We identify slab type before demolition begins and bring in the engineering review as part of the permit package, not as an afterthought.
