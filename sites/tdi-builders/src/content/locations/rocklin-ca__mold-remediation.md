## Why Rocklin Properties See Mold Issues

Rocklin sits at the edge of the Sierra Nevada foothills, where the soil transitions from the flat Sacramento Valley clay to decomposed granite. That granite-heavy soil drains quickly on the surface but can channel subsurface water toward foundations during heavy rain events — especially in neighborhoods built on graded hillside pads. Homes constructed during Rocklin's rapid growth periods of the late 1980s through the 2000s often feature slab-on-grade foundations with minimal vapor barriers by today's standards. When irrigation systems, roof drainage, or plumbing failures introduce water at or near the slab edge, moisture wicks into wall framing and insulation quietly, sometimes for weeks before a visible stain or musty odor signals a problem.

Added to that, Rocklin's HOA-heavy planned communities — many of which share common-wall construction or attached garages — mean that a neighbor's plumbing failure can become your mold problem. Shared rooflines and party walls conduct moisture in ways that single-family detached homes don't, and the source of the intrusion isn't always on your property.

## Our Mold Remediation Process in Rocklin

Every remediation starts with containment and assessment, not demolition. Before any material is removed, we use moisture meters and thermal imaging to map exactly where moisture is elevated — because what looks dry at the drywall surface can read very differently at the wall cavity or subfloor beneath it. Cutting out only what is actually affected keeps reconstruction costs down and avoids disturbing building materials unnecessarily.

Once the scope is confirmed, we establish negative air pressure containment using HEPA-filtered air scrubbers to prevent spores from migrating to unaffected areas of the home. Affected materials — drywall, insulation, wood framing where necessary — are removed, bagged, and disposed of per California Department of Public Health guidelines. Structural surfaces are treated and dried to verified moisture levels before any rebuild begins.

Because TDI Builders is a licensed construction firm, not just a remediation-only contractor, we handle the rebuild in the same project. There is no handoff to a second company, no scheduling gap while your walls sit open, and no question about who is responsible for matching finishes or pulling permits.

## Rocklin Insurance & HOA Coordination

Mold claims in Placer County often hinge on documenting the originating water event — when it started, how long it went undetected, and whether it qualifies as sudden and accidental versus long-term seepage. We photograph and document conditions at the time of our assessment, which gives your adjuster a clear picture of scope before any work begins. That documentation also matters when an HOA is involved, because many Rocklin HOA governing documents draw a hard line between what the association's master policy covers and what falls to the unit owner.

If your HOA requires a post-remediation clearance test from an independent industrial hygienist before approving repairs to common-wall areas, we coordinate that step — we do not self-certify clearance on jobs where a third-party test is required or requested.

## Local Note

Rocklin's newer master-planned communities — particularly those built after roughly 2005 — frequently used engineered lumber and OSB (oriented strand board) sheathing rather than solid-wood framing. OSB is more sensitive to prolonged moisture exposure than dimensional lumber; it can delaminate and lose structural integrity before surface mold becomes visible. When we assess homes in these developments, we probe sheathing and subfloor panels specifically, because a wall that passes a visual inspection can still require structural replacement at the sheathing layer. Homeowners in these areas should not assume that no visible mold means no structural concern after a water event.
