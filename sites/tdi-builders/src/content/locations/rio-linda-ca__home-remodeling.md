Rio Linda still carries the feel of the rural subdivision it was platted as back in the 1910s: half-acre and full-acre lots, horse setups in the backyard, and a housing stock that runs heavy on 1950s and '60s ranch construction. Remodeling here isn't the same job as a gut renovation in a dense Sacramento infill neighborhood. The lots are bigger, the systems underneath the house are often older and non-standard, and a kitchen or addition project has to work around realities a tract-home crew downtown never sees.

## Why Rio Linda Homes Need a Different Remodeling Approach

A good share of Rio Linda's housing was built before modern slab and plumbing codes were standardized, and because the community sits unincorporated in Sacramento County, many properties still run on septic systems and private wells rather than municipal sewer and water. That changes how we plan a remodel. Moving a bathroom, adding square footage, or reconfiguring a kitchen means confirming where the septic tank, leach field, and well casing actually sit before anyone breaks ground, not after. Older ranch homes in the area also tend to have additions stacked on over the decades, sometimes with mismatched foundations or wiring that was never permitted. Our first walkthrough treats that as the baseline assumption rather than a surprise.

## Our Home Remodeling Process in Rio Linda

We start with a site visit that documents the existing structure, locates septic and well infrastructure where applicable, and checks what's actually behind the walls in homes that have seen multiple past updates. From there we handle design and scope, pull permits through Sacramento County (since Rio Linda is unincorporated, county planning and building handles approvals rather than a city office), and sequence the work so load-bearing and systems changes happen before cosmetic finishes. For whole-home remodels we phase the project so kitchens, bathrooms, and living spaces aren't all torn open at once, which matters more on properties where the only access road is a single gravel or narrow paved driveway shared with outbuildings or animal enclosures. Final walkthroughs confirm fixtures, trim, and finish work match the approved plans before we close out permits.

## Reaching Rio Linda from Sacramento

Our crews run out of Sacramento and work Rio Linda regularly, typically routing in via I-5 and Elkhorn Boulevard or through Elverta Road depending on the job site. Because the community is spread across larger parcels rather than dense blocks, we build extra time into scheduling for material deliveries and equipment staging, especially on properties set back from the main road or behind gated fencing. We're available 24/7 for coordination and scheduling calls, so getting a project started doesn't mean waiting for a weekday window.

## Permitting and Site Coordination

Rio Linda doesn't have the HOA density you'd find in newer Sacramento-area developments, but that doesn't mean remodeling is unregulated. Sacramento County building and planning departments review additions, structural changes, and anything touching electrical or plumbing systems, and properties with septic systems may need additional review if a remodel adds bedrooms or significantly changes water usage. We coordinate those approvals directly so homeowners aren't the ones tracking down inspectors or re-submitting plans that got bounced for a missing setback calculation.

## Local Note

One thing that trips up remodels in Rio Linda more than almost anywhere else in the Sacramento area: well casings and septic leach fields aren't always where the original property survey says they are, especially on lots that changed hands multiple times since the 1960s. Before we finalize an addition or room reconfiguration footprint, we confirm actual locations in the field rather than relying on old paperwork. It's added a week to planning on more than one project, but it's cheaper than redesigning a foundation pour after hitting a leach line.
