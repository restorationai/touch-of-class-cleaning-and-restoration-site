## Why Loomis Properties Face Particular Fire Damage Challenges

Loomis is part of Placer County's wildland-urban interface, and that geography shapes how fire damage behaves here. The town's residential character leans heavily toward single-story ranch homes and larger parcels with wood fencing, detached garages, and mature vegetation — all of which extend the burn path and give smoke more surfaces to penetrate before suppression. Attic spaces in homes built during the 1970s and 1980s, which make up a significant portion of Loomis's housing stock, often contain kraft-faced insulation and older sheathing that absorbs smoke odor deeply and resists surface cleaning alone.

The foothills climate adds another layer. Summer relative humidity regularly drops into the single digits during wind events, which means charred structural materials dry unevenly and smoke residue — particularly the oily, wet-smoke type common in slow-burning household fires — can migrate into wall cavities and HVAC ductwork faster than in the more humid Sacramento valley floor. That migration is invisible until air quality testing or a thermal camera reveals it.

## Our Fire Damage Restoration Process in Loomis

Every fire loss is different, but the sequence that protects your property follows a consistent logic. We begin with a full structural and contents assessment — documenting damage with photos and moisture readings before anything is moved or discarded. That documentation matters for your insurance claim and for Placer County permit applications if structural repairs are required.

From there, the work moves in overlapping phases:

- **Emergency board-up and tarping** to secure openings and prevent weather intrusion, especially important during Loomis's brief but heavy winter rain season.
- **Debris removal and controlled demolition** of unsalvageable materials, with care taken to identify load-bearing elements in older ranch-style framing before any walls are opened.
- **Smoke and soot cleaning** using dry chemical sponges, HEPA vacuuming, and thermal fogging or hydroxyl generation for odor neutralization — the method depends on the smoke type and the porosity of affected surfaces.
- **Structural drying** if suppression water is present, using commercial desiccant or refrigerant dehumidifiers calibrated to the low ambient humidity typical in the foothills.
- **Reconstruction** handled in-house, so there is no handoff gap between the mitigation phase and the rebuild.

Being licensed and insured and holding 40+ years of construction and restoration experience means we can take a fire-damaged Loomis home from emergency response through permitted reconstruction under a single contract.

## Local Note

Homes in the Loomis area that were built in the late 1970s through the mid-1980s frequently used T1-11 plywood siding and exposed rafter tails — an aesthetic common in foothills construction of that era. Both materials are highly porous and trap smoke odor in the wood grain in a way that painted or fiber-cement siding does not. Surface cleaning alone will not resolve the odor; the wood typically needs encapsulation with a shellac-based primer or, in severe cases, replacement. If your home has this profile and you are getting conflicting advice about whether siding can be saved, ask specifically about odor testing after cleaning and before any encapsulant is applied.
