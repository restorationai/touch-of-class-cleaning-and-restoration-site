Folsom's commercial corridor has expanded rapidly over the past two decades, and that growth brings a specific set of construction challenges: mixed-vintage building stock ranging from early retail pads along East Bidwell Street to newer Class A office shells in the Palladio and Iron Point districts, a hot Central Valley climate that compresses exterior work windows in summer, and a permitting environment through the City of Folsom's Community Development Department that rewards contractors who already know the local review cycle. TDI USA, Inc. has been navigating exactly these conditions since 1985, delivering commercial construction and tenant improvements for businesses that need the work done right the first time, without schedule surprises that cost them rent.

## Why Folsom's Commercial Market Has Its Own Rhythm

Folsom is not a single-speed market. The older retail centers along Sutter Street and the historic district operate under different design-review considerations than the high-growth corridors near Highway 50 and Blue Ravine Road. Newer shell buildings in those growth zones are often delivered by developers as cold, dark shells, concrete tilt-up or steel-frame structures with no interior partitions, no HVAC distribution, and no electrical beyond a main panel. Tenants moving into these spaces need a full buildout from slab to ceiling grid before they can open doors. At the same time, businesses taking over second-generation space in older centers face a different problem: existing improvements that may not meet current Title 24 energy code, ADA path-of-travel requirements triggered by the scope of renovation, or mechanical systems sized for a previous tenant's load that won't serve a new one. Understanding which category a project falls into before the first drawing is produced is what separates a smooth permit submittal from a three-round correction cycle.

Folsom's climate adds a layer of planning discipline that contractors from cooler regions sometimes underestimate. Summer temperatures routinely exceed 100°F, which affects concrete cure schedules, adhesive performance on LVT and VCT flooring, and the comfort, and therefore productivity, of crews working in unfinished, un-air-conditioned shells. Sequencing HVAC rough-in and temporary cooling early in the schedule is not optional on a Folsom summer buildout; it is a budget and timeline decision made at preconstruction.

## Our Commercial Construction and Tenant Improvement Process in Folsom

Every project begins with a site walk that goes beyond measuring square footage. We review the existing as-built conditions, confirm utility service capacity with PG&E and the local jurisdiction, identify any deferred maintenance the landlord needs to address before tenant work begins, and flag ADA compliance gaps that the scope of work will legally require the owner to correct. That upfront investment in information prevents the change orders that blow timelines and budgets mid-project.

From there, the process moves through design coordination, permit submittal to the City of Folsom, subcontractor procurement, and phased construction. For occupied buildings, a retail center where neighboring tenants are still open, or an office suite being renovated around a partial workforce, we build a phasing plan that isolates dust, noise, and utility interruptions to agreed windows. Inspections are scheduled proactively rather than reactively; Folsom's building inspection calendar fills, and a crew sitting idle waiting for a framing inspection is a cost that falls on the project.

Final close-out includes punch-list resolution, certificate of occupancy coordination, and a documented as-built package that the tenant and landlord both receive, useful when the next improvement cycle comes around.

## Local Note: Title 24 and the Folsom Permit Cycle

One thing that catches out-of-area contractors working in Folsom for the first time is the interaction between California's Title 24 energy compliance requirements and the City's plan-check process. Folsom's reviewers are thorough on mechanical and lighting compliance documentation, a T24 report that was acceptable in a neighboring jurisdiction may come back with correction comments here. We build the energy compliance package in parallel with the architectural drawings rather than treating it as an afterthought, which typically keeps first-submittal correction lists short and avoids the two- to three-week delay that a resubmittal adds to a schedule. For tenants with a lease commencement date already on the calendar, that discipline is the difference between opening on time and paying rent on an empty space.
