Granite Bay sits at the edge of the Sierra Nevada foothills, where the soil shifts from the heavy clay of the Sacramento Valley floor to decomposed granite and loam as you climb toward Folsom Lake. That transition matters enormously when you're breaking ground on a new home — foundation design, drainage planning, and grading all behave differently here than they do on flat valley lots, and a builder who doesn't account for those variables will hand you problems that show up years after move-in. TDI Builders, Inc. has been doing ground-up residential construction since 1985, and the foothill terrain around Granite Bay is terrain we know well.

## Building on Granite Bay's Foothill Terrain

The decomposed granite that gives this community its name is excellent bearing material in dry conditions, but it drains quickly and can shift subtly during the wet season when the water table fluctuates near the lake corridor. Homes built on steeper lots — common throughout the hillside parcels that define much of Granite Bay's residential character — require engineered retaining systems, careful cut-and-fill grading, and drainage swales designed to handle the concentrated runoff that comes off a roof and hardscape during a Sierra foothill storm. We work with licensed geotechnical engineers from the start of every project to make sure the foundation design matches what's actually in the ground, not just what a standard plan assumes.

Placer County's building department governs permitting for Granite Bay, and the county has specific requirements around grading permits, stormwater pollution prevention plans (SWPPPs), and fire-hazard zone compliance that add steps to the pre-construction process that builders unfamiliar with the jurisdiction often underestimate. We've navigated that process many times and build the permit timeline into the project schedule from day one so clients aren't surprised by delays.

## Our New Home Construction Process in Granite Bay

Every custom home we build starts with a site assessment — not a generic checklist, but a walk of the actual parcel with attention to slope, drainage patterns, existing vegetation, and utility access. Granite Bay parcels served by PCWA (Placer County Water Agency) have specific connection and backflow requirements, and PG&E service laterals in the foothill zones sometimes require longer runs than comparable valley lots, which affects both cost and scheduling.

From site assessment we move into design coordination, permitting, and then a phased construction sequence:

- **Site preparation and grading** — engineered to the specific slope and soil profile of your lot
- **Foundation work** — slab, raised, or pier-and-beam depending on soil conditions and design
- **Framing and structural systems** — built to California's Title 24 energy code, which is among the most demanding in the country
- **Mechanical, electrical, and plumbing rough-in** — coordinated with Placer County inspections at each required stage
- **Insulation, drywall, and finish work** — sequenced to avoid moisture entrapment before the building envelope is fully closed
- **Final inspections and certificate of occupancy**

We are licensed and insured, and we carry the coverage required for residential construction in California throughout every phase.

## HOA and Design Review Coordination

Many Granite Bay neighborhoods are governed by homeowners associations with architectural review committees that have authority over exterior materials, roof pitch, color palettes, and setbacks beyond what county code requires. Submitting a plan that satisfies Placer County but conflicts with an HOA's CC&Rs can cost months of revision time. We review the applicable CC&Rs before finalizing exterior design specifications and coordinate directly with architectural review boards when submissions are required — keeping the project moving rather than stalling at a bureaucratic checkpoint.

## Local Note

Granite Bay parcels near the Folsom Lake shoreline corridor fall within a zone where the county requires additional review for projects that could affect riparian buffers or viewsheds. If your lot is within roughly a quarter mile of the lake's high-water mark, expect an extra layer of environmental review before grading permits are issued. It's not a dealbreaker — it's a known step — but builders who don't flag it early have handed clients unexpected schedule gaps mid-project. We identify that exposure at the site assessment stage so the timeline reflects reality from the beginning.
