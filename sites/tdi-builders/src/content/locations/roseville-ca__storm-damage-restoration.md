## Why Roseville Properties See Distinctive Storm Damage

Roseville's building stock spans a wide range of construction eras, and that matters when you're assessing storm damage. Neighborhoods developed in the 1970s and 1980s, many of them in the western and central parts of the city, commonly feature composition shingle roofs that are now approaching or past their rated lifespan. When an atmospheric river drops two or three inches of rain in 24 hours, aging tab shingles lift, crease, and crack in ways that newer architectural shingles resist. The result is water intrusion that tracks along rafters before it ever shows up as a ceiling stain.

Newer master-planned communities built in the 2000s and later present a different challenge: tile roofs. Concrete and clay tile holds up well in normal rain, but wind-driven debris, broken branches, detached fence panels, airborne gravel, can crack individual tiles without visibly displacing them. A cracked tile passes a casual visual inspection and fails spectacularly during the next storm. Our inspection process uses close-contact assessment rather than binocular surveys from the ground, because that's the only way to catch hairline fractures before they become interior water damage.

Siding damage follows a similar pattern. Stucco exteriors common in Roseville's mid-century and Spanish-revival-style homes develop micro-cracks over time; wind-driven rain exploits those cracks and saturates the weather-resistant barrier beneath. Fiber-cement and vinyl siding on newer builds can delaminate or buckle when debris impact combines with prolonged moisture exposure. Either way, the exterior damage you can see is rarely the full picture.

## Our Storm Damage Restoration Process in Roseville

When we arrive at a Roseville property after a storm event, the first priority is stabilization, not paperwork. That means emergency tarping of breached roof sections, boarding compromised windows, and identifying any structural concerns that make the building unsafe to enter. We document everything with photographs and moisture readings before a single piece of material is removed, because that documentation is what your insurance adjuster needs to process the claim accurately.

Once the structure is stabilized, we move into full damage assessment. For roofing, that includes checking decking for delamination and soft spots, inspecting flashing at chimneys, skylights, and valleys, and evaluating the condition of ridge vents and gutters. For siding, we probe for moisture behind the cladding using non-invasive meters and, where readings are elevated, open targeted sections to inspect the sheathing and framing beneath.

Rebuild scope is written to current California Building Code requirements, which in Placer County include specific wind-load ratings for roofing fasteners and underlayment. We pull the necessary permits, coordinate inspections, and close out the job with documentation that satisfies both the building department and your insurance carrier.
