## Why Citrus Heights Homes See Duct Buildup Faster Than You'd Expect

Citrus Heights sits in Sacramento County's inland valley, where the combination of dry summers, wildfire smoke seasons, and minimal coastal airflow creates a particulate load that coastal California homeowners rarely deal with. During the late summer and fall, smoke from Sierra Nevada and foothills fires regularly settles into the valley — and every time your system pulls in outside air, that smoke residue enters the duct network. Over a season or two, the interior walls of flex duct and sheet metal trunk lines accumulate a layer of fine ash and carbon particles that no filter fully stops.

The housing stock adds another layer. A significant portion of Citrus Heights was built during the postwar suburban expansion of the 1950s through 1970s, with duct systems that were sized and routed for the equipment of that era. Many of those original layouts use unconditioned attic space for the main trunk lines — attics that hit 140–150°F in July. That thermal cycling causes flex duct to expand and contract repeatedly, loosening connections and creating small gaps where conditioned air leaks out and attic dust infiltrates in. Homes that have never had their ductwork inspected since original construction often show debris accumulation at every branch takeoff.

## Our Air Duct Cleaning Process in Citrus Heights

We start with a visual inspection of the supply and return sides before any equipment is connected. In older Citrus Heights homes with original ductwork, this step matters — we want to confirm duct integrity before applying negative pressure, because a compromised flex duct section can collapse under vacuum if it isn't identified first.

Once the system is mapped, we use truck-mounted or high-powered portable negative air equipment to place the duct system under continuous negative pressure, then work register-by-register with rotary brush and compressed air tools to dislodge and capture debris. The vacuum draws all loosened material toward the collection point rather than redistributing it through the house. Supply registers, return grilles, the air handler cabinet, and the blower assembly are all addressed — not just the main trunk. We finish with an antimicrobial sanitization application to the interior duct surfaces for clients who request it, which is particularly relevant after wildfire smoke events or if any moisture intrusion has occurred near the air handler.

Dryer vent cleaning is available as an add-on and is worth scheduling at the same time — Citrus Heights fire departments respond to dryer vent fires every year, and a lint-packed vent is a straightforward hazard to eliminate.
