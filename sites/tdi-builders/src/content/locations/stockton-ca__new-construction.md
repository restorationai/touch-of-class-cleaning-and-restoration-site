## Why Stockton Lots Need a Different Foundation Approach

Much of Stockton sits on Central Valley alluvial and clay soils that expand when wet and shrink hard when dry. That swing is a real factor for slab-on-grade construction, especially on lots near the Delta where the water table runs higher for part of the year. A soil report that just says "clay" isn't enough detail to build on. We pull or review geotechnical data for the specific parcel and adjust footing depth, rebar placement, and slab design accordingly, rather than defaulting to a generic valley spec that was written for a different lot.

## Stockton Permitting and Build Coordination

New construction in Stockton typically moves through multiple inspection points, from foundation and framing to rough mechanical, electrical, and plumbing, before a final occupancy sign-off. ## Local note

One detail that catches first-time Stockton builders off guard: a soil report that looks fine in February can describe ground that behaves very differently by August, because the expansive clay common across the valley floor shrinks as it dries out over a hot, rainless summer. A foundation designed only from wet-season data can end up undersized for the cracking and heave that shows up once the clay dries. We account for that seasonal swing in the footing design rather than building to a single snapshot of the soil.
