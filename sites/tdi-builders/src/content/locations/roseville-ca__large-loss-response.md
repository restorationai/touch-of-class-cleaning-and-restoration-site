Roseville sits at the convergence of several risk factors that make large-loss events more likely than in many comparable California cities. The Sierra Nevada foothills to the east funnel cold air drainage and occasional wildfire smoke and ember cast toward the valley floor; the 2020 and 2021 fire seasons showed how quickly ember ignition can jump to rooflines in communities that border open space. Meanwhile, the city's aggressive growth over the past three decades means that a single commercial corridor or HOA community can include dozens of structures sharing the same utility infrastructure — a single main-line failure or electrical fault can cascade across multiple addresses simultaneously.

## Our Large Loss and Catastrophic Response Process in Roseville

A catastrophic loss requires a different operational tempo than a single-room water damage call. When TDI Builders receives a large-loss dispatch in Roseville, the first step is a rapid site assessment by a senior project manager — not a technician — who can make real-time decisions about structural safety, utility isolation, and the scope of equipment needed before a single piece of drying gear is placed.

From there, the process moves through clearly defined phases:

- **Stabilization**: Emergency board-up, tarping, temporary power, and water extraction to stop active damage.
- **Documentation**: Systematic photographic and thermal imaging documentation calibrated for large-loss adjuster review and carrier reporting — the kind of package that supports a multi-million dollar claim without revision requests.
- **Drying and Demolition**: Industrial-grade desiccant dehumidification and high-velocity air movement scaled to the structure's actual square footage and material composition, with selective demolition of non-salvageable assemblies.
- **Reconstruction**: TDI's in-house construction capability means the rebuild phase begins without a handoff gap — the same company that documented and dried the loss executes the structural and finish reconstruction.

Our IICRC-certified technicians follow established drying science standards throughout, and the project manager maintains a daily log that both the property owner and the assigned adjuster can track.

## Local Note

Roseville's newer master-planned communities — particularly those developed in the 2000s and 2010s in the western and northwestern portions of the city — were built with stucco-over-foam exterior insulation systems (EIFS-adjacent assemblies) that trap moisture differently than traditional wood-framed stucco.
