El Dorado Hills sits in the Sierra Nevada foothills at an elevation where summer temperatures routinely push past 100°F and the dry season stretches well into autumn. That combination — intense heat, low humidity, and the construction boom that has reshaped this community over the past two decades — creates post-build cleanup conditions that differ meaningfully from a flatland Sacramento suburb. Fine silica-rich dust from graded hillside lots works its way into HVAC registers, cabinet interiors, and window tracks long before a final walk-through, and it behaves differently than the heavier clay dust common closer to the valley floor.

## Why El Dorado Hills Construction Sites Need More Than a Standard Broom-Out

The foothill terrain that makes El Dorado Hills attractive to builders also complicates cleanup. Grading for new pads and additions kicks up decomposed granite and fine mineral dust that becomes airborne at the slightest air movement. Once HVAC systems are energized — even briefly during a final inspection — that particulate circulates through ductwork and settles on freshly painted surfaces, polished fixtures, and appliance interiors. In active developments across El Dorado Hills, it is common to find a visible haze on cabinet shelving installed just days earlier.

Beyond dust, the region's low relative humidity during summer and fall means that adhesive residues from tape, protective films, and caulk dry harder and bond more aggressively to tile, glass, and stone than they would in a more humid climate. Removing those residues without scratching surfaces requires the right solvents and timing — not just elbow grease.

El Dorado Hills also has a significant share of newer construction governed by HOA covenants that specify how and when exterior cleaning, dumpster placement, and debris removal can occur. Coordinating cleanup within those windows matters for final-occupancy sign-off.

## Our Post-Construction Cleaning Process in El Dorado Hills

A thorough post-construction clean happens in phases, and the sequence matters as much as the effort.

**Rough clean** removes the bulk: drywall scraps, lumber offcuts, packaging, and the initial layer of construction dust from floors and horizontal surfaces. In foothill builds, this phase often requires HEPA-filtered vacuuming of ductwork and air-return cavities before any other surface work begins — otherwise the first time the system runs, it re-deposits debris across everything cleaned below.

**Detail clean** follows once trades have finished and the rough debris is gone. This covers window frames and glass (including the adhesive residue from protective film), grout joints, fixture interiors, cabinet boxes and drawer slides, appliance cavities, and any surface that will be visible at move-in. Hard-water mineral deposits from construction-phase water testing are common on fixtures and need targeted treatment.

**Final clean** is the white-glove pass done within 24–48 hours of occupancy. Surfaces are wiped down again, floors are polished or buffed depending on material, and the space is left ready for a walk-through. For larger projects, this phase can also include specialty services such as pressure washing of driveways and exterior hardscape, window cleaning, and construction stain removal from concrete.

With 40+ years of construction and restoration experience, the TDI Builders team understands how a site reads at each phase — what's genuinely clean versus what looks clean until the light shifts.

Rules around dumpster placement, debris haul-out hours, and pressure-washing runoff vary by development, and some require documentation that hazardous materials (adhesives, solvents, paint residue) were disposed of properly before issuing a certificate of completion.

## Local Note

One pattern that comes up repeatedly on El Dorado Hills hillside builds: stucco exteriors and concrete flatwork on west-facing elevations accumulate a reddish-brown iron oxide stain during construction from the decomposed granite disturbed during grading. It looks like rust but originates in the soil, not the structure. Standard pressure washing alone rarely removes it cleanly — it requires a pH-adjusted treatment applied at the right dwell time before rinsing. Catching this during the specialty cleaning phase, before a homeowner or buyer sees it, saves a difficult conversation at the walk-through.
