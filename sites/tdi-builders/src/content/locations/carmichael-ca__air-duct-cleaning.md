## Why Carmichael Properties See Duct Issues

Carmichael sits in the Sacramento Valley's inland heat pocket, where summer temperatures routinely climb well above 100°F. That means HVAC systems run hard — sometimes eight to ten hours a day from June through September. Heavy runtime accelerates the rate at which dust, pet dander, and insulation fibers accumulate inside ductwork. The region also sits in a high-pollen corridor; valley oak, ash, and grass pollens are dense in spring and early summer, and a significant share of that airborne load ends up in return ducts before filters can catch it.

Housing stock adds another layer. A large portion of Carmichael's residential neighborhoods were built between the late 1950s and the early 1980s, when flex duct was installed loosely and duct board was common. Older flex duct develops small tears and sags over decades, creating low spots where debris collects and moisture can pool during the brief, humid stretches of a Sacramento winter. Homes of that era also frequently have duct systems that were never redesigned after room additions — meaning airflow is uneven and some branch runs see almost no velocity, which lets particulates settle rather than pass through.

## Our Air Duct Cleaning Process in Carmichael

The process starts with a visual inspection — we pull register covers and use a camera or mirror to assess debris load and duct condition before any equipment goes in. This step matters in older Carmichael homes because damaged flex duct can be made worse by high-powered negative pressure if the liner is already compromised. If we see a section that needs repair before cleaning, we'll tell you.

Once the system is cleared for cleaning, we attach a truck-mounted or portable HEPA-filtered vacuum to the main trunk line and work each branch run with agitation tools — rotary brushes or compressed-air whips depending on duct material. Supply and return sides are cleaned separately. Register boots and air handler compartments are wiped down. If sanitization is requested, we apply an EPA-registered antimicrobial to the interior duct surfaces after the mechanical cleaning is complete — not before, which would just seal debris in place.

Dryer vent cleaning is often scheduled at the same time. Sacramento Valley lint fires are a real risk; dryer vents in homes where the laundry room was added during a remodel sometimes have long, kinked runs that accumulate lint quickly.

## Local Note

One thing that comes up repeatedly in Carmichael's older ranch-style homes: the original duct systems were often run through unconditioned attic space with minimal insulation wrap. During Sacramento's hot summers, attic temperatures can exceed 150°F, which degrades the outer jacket of flex duct over time and causes the inner liner to pull away from fittings. When that happens, you're not just dealing with dirty ducts — you're losing conditioned air into the attic and potentially pulling attic air (and whatever is living up there) back into the living space. A thorough cleaning inspection will catch this; a simple duct cleaning without a visual check will not.

If your home was built before 1985 and hasn't had ductwork evaluated in the last several years, it's worth asking specifically about duct integrity when you call — not just about cleaning.
