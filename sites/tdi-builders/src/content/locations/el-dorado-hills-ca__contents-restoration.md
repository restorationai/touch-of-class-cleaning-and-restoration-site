When a burst pipe, chimney fire, or storm intrusion damages your belongings, the contents inside your home often suffer as much as the structure itself — and in a community where many residences carry custom furnishings, built-in cabinetry, and heirloom pieces, getting those items properly cleaned, restored, and stored matters as much as drying the walls.

## Why El Dorado Hills Properties See Elevated Contents Damage

The foothill climate creates a specific pattern of loss that affects personal property in ways that differ from the Sacramento Valley floor. Summer wildfire smoke — a recurring reality in El Dorado County — infiltrates homes through HVAC systems and window gaps, depositing a fine, oily soot on upholstered furniture, artwork, clothing, and electronics long before a flame ever touches the structure. That smoke residue bonds to fabric fibers and porous wood surfaces within hours; the longer it sits, the deeper it migrates.

Winter rains present a different challenge. Hillside lots throughout El Dorado Hills drain quickly on the surface but can channel water into garages and lower-level living spaces with surprising speed. Homes built on cut-and-fill pads — a common grading approach for the terrain here — sometimes experience water intrusion at the foundation that saturates stored items, flooring underlayment, and the lower shelves of built-in units before a homeowner notices anything is wrong.

## Our Contents Restoration and Storage Process in El Dorado Hills

The first step is a thorough contents inventory — every affected item is photographed, catalogued, and assessed before anything leaves your home. That documentation matters both for your insurance claim and for ensuring nothing gets lost or misattributed during the restoration process.

Items that can be safely cleaned on-site are treated there. Upholstered furniture, area rugs, and drapery often benefit from ultrasonic cleaning or dry-ice blasting for smoke and soot removal. Electronics and documents require controlled-environment drying and specialized treatment that cannot happen in a water-damaged room. Those items are packed out in climate-controlled vehicles and moved to a secure storage facility where temperature and humidity are actively managed — critical for preventing secondary mold growth on items that absorbed moisture during a loss event.

Once your home has been dried, repaired, and cleared for re-occupancy, contents are returned, unwrapped, and placed back in their original positions. The full chain of custody is documented at every handoff. IICRC certified technicians oversee the process from pack-out through final return, and TDI Builders has been doing this work since 1985 — the institutional knowledge of what different materials need shows in the details.

## Local Note

El Dorado Hills has a significant share of homes built during the 1990s and early 2000s boom, many of which used engineered wood products — laminate flooring, OSB subfloor panels, composite cabinetry — that respond to moisture very differently than solid wood. Engineered wood swells and delaminates quickly when wet, but it also off-gasses formaldehyde at elevated levels when it dries under heat. During contents pack-out from a water-damaged room with these materials, the team takes care to ventilate and monitor air quality before crews spend extended time working inside — a precaution that matters more in a tightly built foothill home than in an older, draftier structure.
