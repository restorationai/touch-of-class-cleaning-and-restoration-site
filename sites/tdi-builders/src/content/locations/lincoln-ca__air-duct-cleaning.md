Lincoln's position in Placer County puts it squarely in Sacramento Valley's inland heat corridor — summers that push past 100°F for weeks at a stretch, followed by wet winters that pull cold, damp air through crawl spaces and attic returns. That thermal swing is hard on ductwork. If your system hasn't been cleaned in the last few years, you're likely circulating more than conditioned air.

## Why Lincoln Properties See Duct Issues

Lincoln has grown fast. The city's master-planned communities — many of them built in the mid-2000s housing boom — used flexible duct runs that were fine on day one but are now old enough to show wear. Flex duct is prone to kinking at elbows and at the collar connections near registers; those low points collect fine particulate, pet dander, and the fine clay dust that blows in from the surrounding foothills during dry, windy stretches. Older homes on the city's west side, built in the 1970s and 1980s, often have sheet-metal trunk lines that weren't sealed at the joints — meaning conditioned air has been leaking into wall cavities for decades, and the return side has been pulling in unconditioned air (and whatever is living in that wall cavity) ever since.

The region also sits in an area with elevated wildfire smoke risk. During active fire seasons, fine particulate matter infiltrates return air systems faster than most homeowners expect, and a single bad smoke week can leave a visible residue on supply registers and inside the first few feet of duct runs.

## Our Air Duct Cleaning Process in Lincoln

The process starts before any equipment goes into the duct. A technician walks the system — locating every supply and return, checking for disconnected flex runs, and noting any visible mold or moisture at registers before the cleaning begins. This inspection step matters more than it sounds: a disconnected duct in an attic that gets vacuumed without being reconnected first just redistributes debris into the attic instead of removing it.

Once the system is mapped, we use negative-pressure source removal — a high-powered vacuum creates suction at the main trunk while technicians work each branch with agitation tools, driving debris toward the collection point rather than into the living space. Supply and return sides are cleaned separately. Registers and grilles are removed, cleaned, and reinstalled. If sanitization is requested, an EPA-registered antimicrobial is applied to the interior duct surfaces after the mechanical cleaning is complete — not before, which would just trap contaminants under the coating.

Dryer vent cleaning follows a separate protocol: the vent is disconnected at the dryer, cleared from the exterior cap back to the machine, and the cap itself is inspected for bird nests or lint buildup that can restrict airflow and create a fire hazard.
