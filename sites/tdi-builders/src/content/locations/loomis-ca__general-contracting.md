## Why Loomis Properties Present Unique Renovation Challenges

The housing stock in Loomis spans a wide range of eras and construction styles. Ranch-style homes built in the 1960s and early 1970s frequently feature post-and-beam framing, low-slope rooflines, and original single-pane windows, all of which complicate modern remodels in ways that don't show up until walls open. Older additions often lack proper shear walls or were framed without permits, meaning a kitchen expansion or master bath remodel can trigger a retroactive code compliance review through Placer County Building Services.

The climate adds another layer. Loomis sits in a Mediterranean foothill zone where summers push well past 100°F and winters bring periodic hard freezes and significant rainfall. That freeze-thaw cycle stresses concrete slabs and exposed plumbing in ways that flat-valley homes don't experience to the same degree. Crawl spaces in older Loomis homes, particularly those on sloped lots, tend to accumulate moisture during wet winters, which can mean rotted sill plates or subfloor damage hiding beneath perfectly presentable flooring. Any serious remodel starts with understanding what's underneath.

One pattern we've observed consistently in Loomis's older hillside properties: original septic systems and their leach fields are often closer to the home's foundation than current setback standards would allow.
