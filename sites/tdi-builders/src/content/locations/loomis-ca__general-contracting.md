## Why Loomis Properties Present Unique Remodeling Conditions

The housing stock in Loomis spans a wide range — from mid-century ranch homes on larger lots to newer construction in planned communities that crept in during the 1990s and 2000s boom. That mix matters when you're remodeling. Older homes often have original cast-iron drain lines, single-pane windows, and undersized electrical panels that weren't designed for modern kitchen appliances or a master bath with radiant floor heat. Opening a wall to expand a kitchen can surface knob-and-tube wiring or subfloor framing that's undersized by today's code — discoveries that need a licensed general contractor on-site, not a specialty-only trade.

The foothill climate adds its own layer. Summer heat in Loomis regularly climbs past 100°F, which accelerates the off-gassing of adhesives and coatings and means scheduling finish work for early mornings matters more here than it does in coastal California. Winter moisture — especially in years with heavy Sierra snowpack and runoff — can expose flashing failures and foundation drainage issues that a remodel is the right moment to address permanently.

## Our Renovation and General Contracting Process in Loomis

Every project starts with a scoped walkthrough, not a ballpark estimate over the phone. For remodels in Loomis, that walkthrough includes a look at the existing mechanical systems — HVAC, plumbing, electrical — because foothill homes built before the 1980s frequently have systems that are functional but not code-compliant for the scope of work being proposed. We pull permits through Placer County Building Services, coordinate inspections, and keep the permit record clean so your title isn't clouded when you sell.

For post-damage rebuilds — fire, water, or structural — we work from the insurance scope and flag line items that are missing or undervalued before demo begins. That front-end documentation protects you from out-of-pocket surprises mid-project.

The build sequence we follow:

1. **Scope and permit application** — drawings, material specs, Placer County submittal

4. **Finish trades** — tile, cabinetry, paint, and trim sequenced to protect completed work from trade damage
5. **Final inspection and punch list** — permit sign-off before project closeout
