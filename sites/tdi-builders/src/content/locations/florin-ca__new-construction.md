Building a new home on a Florin lot starts with a conversation most buyers in other parts of Sacramento County never have to have: what's actually under the dirt. Florin's history as farmland, much of it planted in strawberries and row crops well into the twentieth century, left behind soil that's been worked, irrigated, and compacted in ways a fresh subdivision survey doesn't always capture. New home construction here means planning the foundation around that history, not just the architect's plans.

## Why Florin Lots Need a Different Foundation Approach

Sacramento Valley clay is notorious for expanding when wet and contracting hard in the dry months, and Florin's parcels, many converted from agricultural use over the past few decades, carry that same soil profile. A slab poured without accounting for seasonal swell can crack within a few years. We pull soil samples and work with a geotechnical engineer before finalizing footing depth, and on parcels near older irrigation routes we often adjust the foundation plan once excavation starts and the soil tells a different story than the boring log did.

The Central Valley climate adds its own pressure. Summer heat routinely pushes past 95 degrees for weeks at a stretch, which affects concrete cure times and when we schedule framing and roofing work. Winter brings tule fog and periodic heavy rain that can leave an open foundation trench sitting in standing water if the schedule isn't managed tightly. We sequence excavation, forming, and pour around the forecast rather than a fixed calendar.

## Our New Home Construction Process in Florin

Ground-up construction in Florin runs through Sacramento County's permitting office rather than a city building department, since Florin is unincorporated. That means setback rules, utility connection requirements, and inspection scheduling follow county code, and we coordinate directly with county inspectors at each phase: foundation, framing, rough mechanical, and final. On parcels that were previously agricultural, we also check for old well casings, septic remnants, or buried irrigation lines before breaking ground, since these show up more often on Florin lots than on tract subdivisions built on raw ground.

Once the soil and permitting picture is settled, the build follows a standard sequence: site prep and grading, foundation forming and pour, framing, rough-in for plumbing and electrical, insulation, drywall, and finish work. What changes job to job in Florin is the front end, the weeks spent confirming soil conditions and utility access, because that's where a Central Valley infill lot differs most from a new subdivision pad.

## Sacramento County Permitting & Utility Coordination

Utility hookups in Florin often involve coordinating between SMUD for power and Sacramento County Water Agency or a private well depending on the parcel's history, especially on lots that were on well and septic systems before being subdivided for residential use. We handle the utility coordination as part of the build schedule so it doesn't become a bottleneck after the foundation is already poured. For larger custom builds, we also manage the county's setback and lot coverage requirements, which can be stricter on older agricultural parcels than on newly platted subdivision lots.

## Local note

On more than one Florin build, excavation has turned up remnants of old irrigation infrastructure, cast iron piping or compacted berms from row-crop irrigation, that never showed up on the initial soil report. It doesn't happen on every lot, but it happens often enough in this part of the county that we build a contingency window into the schedule for excavation rather than assuming the dirt will behave like a brand-new subdivision pad.
