## Why Folsom's Housing Stock and Climate Shape Every Remodel

Folsom sits in a climate zone where summer temperatures routinely push past 100°F, and that heat does real work on a home over time. Attic insulation compresses, HVAC systems strain against undersized ductwork, and west-facing walls absorb heat loads that older construction never accounted for. When homeowners here open up walls for a kitchen expansion or a primary suite addition, it's common to find insulation values well below current California Title 24 energy code requirements — which means a remodel becomes an opportunity to bring the whole envelope up to standard, not just update the finishes.

Folsom also has a significant share of homes built during the 1980s and 1990s boom years, when certain framing and plumbing conventions were standard that are now either outdated or flagged during permit review. Polybutylene supply lines, for instance, were common in that era. A bathroom remodel that might seem straightforward on the surface can reveal plumbing that needs full replacement before the finish work begins. Our team scopes for these conditions before we price a project, so there are no mid-project surprises that derail a budget or a timeline.

## HOA Coordination in Folsom's Planned Communities

A significant portion of Folsom's residential neighborhoods fall within HOA jurisdictions, and many of those associations have architectural review committees with real teeth — submission requirements, approval timelines, and material restrictions that can add weeks to a project's pre-construction phase if they're not handled proactively. We've worked within these frameworks long enough to know that submitting incomplete documentation to an ARC is the single most common cause of avoidable project delays.

Our team prepares HOA submittal packages as part of the pre-construction process — elevation drawings, material callouts, color specifications — so that the review committee has everything they need to approve on the first pass. If a community has specific requirements around exterior materials or roofline modifications, we build those constraints into the design before the homeowner falls in love with a detail that won't clear review.

## Local Note

Folsom's older neighborhoods closer to the historic downtown corridor often feature homes with original hardwood floors running beneath decades of layered flooring — vinyl over carpet over the original oak. When renovation work opens up those layers, homeowners frequently discover hardwood that's in salvageable condition, which changes the scope and the budget in a positive direction. It's worth having a flooring assessment built into any gut renovation in that part of the city before committing to new floor materials. We flag this routinely during our initial walkthroughs because it's the kind of detail that saves money and preserves character that can't be replicated with new material.
