When that water finds a crack in a foundation, a failing roof flashing, or a supply line that has been stressed by the region's temperature swings, it moves fast — and the clay-heavy soils common across Placer County don't absorb runoff the way sandy ground does, which means water pools against slabs and crawl spaces longer than homeowners expect. TDI Builders, Inc. has been responding to exactly these situations since 1985, and our IICRC certified technicians are available around the clock.

## Why Granite Bay Properties See Water Damage

The housing stock here tells part of the story. Granite Bay grew rapidly through the 1980s and 1990s, which means a large share of homes were built with polybutylene supply lines — a pipe material that degrades over time and fails without warning, often inside walls where the leak goes undetected for days. Homes built during that same era frequently used OSB sheathing and paper-faced drywall that can begin to delaminate within 24 to 48 hours of saturation. Older ranch-style homes on the western edges of the community sometimes have raised wood-frame floors over unconditioned crawl spaces, where standing water after a storm or plumbing failure can sit against joists and subfloor for extended periods before anyone notices the soft spot underfoot.

Winter storms also create a pattern specific to foothill communities: roofs shed heavy rainfall faster than gutters can carry it, and when gutters are blocked by the valley oak and pine debris common throughout Granite Bay, water backs up at the fascia and works its way under the roofline. That kind of intrusion is slow and diffuse — it rarely announces itself with a visible flood, but it saturates insulation and ceiling drywall over weeks.

## Our Water Damage Restoration Process in Granite Bay

Every job starts with a thorough moisture assessment using thermal imaging cameras and calibrated moisture meters, not just a visual inspection. Water behind plaster or inside a wall cavity can read dry at the surface while the substrate stays saturated — a reading that looks fine on a pin meter but tells a different story with a non-invasive sensor. We map the full moisture boundary before any drying equipment is placed.

From there, the process follows the IICRC S500 standard for water damage restoration:

- **Extraction** — truck-mounted and portable extractors remove standing water and pull moisture from carpet, pad, and hard-surface flooring before structural drying begins.
- **Structural drying** — commercial-grade desiccant and refrigerant dehumidifiers, paired with high-velocity air movers, are positioned to create a directed airflow pattern calibrated to the room's cubic footage and the material's drying class.
- **Daily monitoring** — moisture readings are logged at each check so drying progress is documented and equipment is repositioned as conditions change.
- **Controlled demolition when necessary** — if drywall or flooring has absorbed beyond the restorable threshold, we remove only what cannot be saved, preserving as much of the original structure as possible before reconstruction begins.

Because TDI Builders handles both mitigation and reconstruction, there is no handoff gap between the team that dried the structure and the team that rebuilds it.

## Local Note

Granite Bay's mature tree canopy — the valley oaks and ponderosa pines that make the neighborhood visually distinctive — creates a specific drainage hazard that out-of-area contractors sometimes miss. Root systems from large, established trees can displace or crack older clay sewer laterals, and a slow lateral leak beneath a slab can saturate the subgrade for months before it surfaces as a wet floor or a musty smell in a ground-floor room. If a moisture reading shows elevated levels at the slab without an obvious plumbing source above grade, we scope the lateral before closing out the drying phase — because treating the symptom without identifying that source means the problem returns.
