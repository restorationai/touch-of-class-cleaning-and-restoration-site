Adding a room in Mather means building on ground that was farmland and airfield before it was a neighborhood, and that history still shows up in the soil and the permitting process. Whether you're planning a bump out addition for a larger kitchen or a full second-story home addition, the slab foundations and expansive clay common to this part of Sacramento County change how the job gets engineered from the footing up.

## Why Mather Properties Need a Different Approach to Additions

Most of Mather's housing stock went up after Mather Air Force Base closed and the land was redeveloped through the 1990s and early 2000s, which means you're mostly working with slab-on-grade construction rather than raised foundations. That's good news for keeping a new room level with the existing floor, but it also means any addition needs a soils-aware foundation plan. Sacramento County's clay-heavy valley soil expands when wet and contracts hard in the dry months, and a room addition tied into an existing slab without the right footing depth and reinforcement will eventually show stress cracks where old meets new. We also see a fair number of homes here with tile or concrete-shingle roofing from that same build era, which affects how we match rooflines on a second-story or roof-tie-in addition so the new section doesn't look bolted on.

## Our Room Addition Process in Mather

We start with a site visit that looks at the existing foundation, the grading around the home, and how the new footprint will tie into plumbing and electrical runs that are often original to the 1990s build. From there we handle the permit set through Sacramento County, since Mather is unincorporated and falls under county building and planning review rather than a city hall. Framing and foundation work come next, matched to the soil report rather than a standard spec, and we sequence utility rough-in before closing walls so inspections aren't delayed waiting on a second trade. Exterior finishes, from stucco texture to roofing material, are matched to the original structure so the addition reads as part of the house rather than an obvious add-on.

## Mather Permitting and HOA Coordination

Because Mather sits in unincorporated Sacramento County, plan review and inspections run through the county building department rather than a municipal one, and timelines can differ from what homeowners in nearby incorporated cities expect. Some of the newer planned developments built on former base land carry HOA design standards covering exterior materials, rooflines, and setbacks, so we check those covenants early in the design phase rather than after drawings are finished. For additions that extend toward a property line or increase the structure's footprint significantly, we also confirm county setback and lot-coverage requirements before submitting plans, since a redesign after rejection costs more time than a short conversation up front.

## Local note

Because Mather grew up around a former Air Force base that's now Sacramento Mather Airport, parts of the community fall inside airport land use compatibility review areas that can affect height, use, and in some cases construction type for additions built close to flight paths. It's not something every contractor checks, but we confirm a property's location relative to those compatibility zones before finalizing a second-story addition, since a change there can affect both design and approval timeline.
