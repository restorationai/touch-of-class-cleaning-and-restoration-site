## Why Carmichael Properties See Water Damage Issues

The Sacramento region's Mediterranean climate means long dry summers followed by concentrated winter rainfall — and Carmichael's older neighborhoods feel that cycle acutely. Expansive clay soils shift with the wet-dry seasons, putting stress on slab foundations and the supply lines that run beneath them. Slab leaks are a recurring problem here, and because the water migrates laterally under the concrete before surfacing, homeowners often notice a warm spot on the floor or a spike in their SMUD bill before they see any visible moisture.

The housing stock adds another layer. Homes built in the 1950s and 1960s frequently have galvanized steel supply lines that are now well past their service life. Galvanized pipe corrodes from the inside out, narrowing over decades until a section finally fails — sometimes at a joint hidden inside a wall cavity. When that happens in a home with original plaster-and-lath construction, the wall face can read dry to the touch while the lath and framing behind it stay saturated for days.

## Our Water Damage Restoration Process in Carmichael

Every water loss starts with a thorough assessment before any equipment is staged. Our technicians use thermal imaging cameras and calibrated moisture meters to map exactly where water has traveled — including under flooring, behind baseboards, and into wall cavities that show no surface staining. In Carmichael's older homes, this step is especially important because plaster walls and hardwood subfloors can hold moisture in ways that standard visual inspection misses entirely.

Once the scope is documented, we move into water extraction. Industrial truck-mounted and portable extraction units pull standing water and saturated material quickly. Structural drying follows: commercial-grade desiccant and refrigerant dehumidifiers, along with high-velocity air movers, are positioned according to a drying plan calibrated to the affected materials and square footage. We monitor moisture readings daily and adjust equipment placement until readings confirm the structure has returned to acceptable levels — a process governed by the IICRC S500 standard for water damage restoration.

If building materials — drywall, insulation, flooring — are saturated beyond recovery, controlled demolition removes only what cannot be saved. Documentation of every affected area, every reading, and every removed material goes directly into the claim file for your insurance carrier.

## Carmichael Insurance Coordination

Most homeowners' policies cover sudden and accidental water losses — a burst pipe, a failed appliance supply line, an overflow — but the documentation has to be thorough for the claim to move smoothly. We photograph affected areas before, during, and after mitigation, log moisture readings at every monitoring visit, and produce a scope of loss that meets the format most carriers and their third-party adjusters expect. We bill carriers directly where assignment of benefits is in place, which keeps the financial burden off you while the claim is being processed.

## Local Note

In Carmichael's older ranch-style homes — the kind built on concrete slabs in the late 1950s through the 1970s — original hardwood floors are often glued directly to the slab with cutback adhesive, a tar-based product that was standard before modern flooring adhesives existed. When a slab leak saturates that assembly, the hardwood, the adhesive layer, and the concrete surface all have to be dried as a system, not just the wood on top. Rushing the process or pulling the floor before the slab itself has dried leads to re-wetting and failed reinstallation. We account for slab drying time in every drying plan on these properties — it adds days to the timeline, but it's the only way the repair holds.
