## Why El Dorado Hills Properties Face Distinct Board-Up and Tarping Needs

The foothill climate here is not the same as Sacramento's valley floor. Summer temperatures regularly push past 100°F, and the Diablo winds that sweep through El Dorado County in late summer and fall can accelerate fire spread and drive embers into attic vents or through broken glazing. That same wind load that cracks a window during a fire can also peel back roofing material on a home that was never touched by flames — leaving the structure exposed before a homeowner even realizes there's a problem.

The housing stock in El Dorado Hills skews toward planned communities built from the late 1980s through the 2000s, with a significant share of homes featuring tile roofs over wood decking, vaulted ceilings, and large expanses of glass. Tile roofs present a specific challenge: the tiles themselves often survive a fire or storm event intact, but the underlayment beneath them can be compromised or the decking cracked, making the roof look fine from the street while water moves freely into the attic. A proper tarp installation accounts for this by covering the full affected field rather than just the visible break.

## Our Emergency Board-Up and Tarping Process in El Dorado Hills

When a call comes in, the first priority is a quick visual assessment of every opening — not just the obvious one. A fire that vented through a second-story window may have also warped a garage door frame or cracked a skylight. We document each breach with photographs before any material goes up, which matters when your adjuster reviews the claim.

For board-up work, we use exterior-grade plywood cut and fastened to protect against both weather and unauthorized entry. On stucco-clad homes common throughout El Dorado Hills, we use non-penetrating anchor methods where the finish allows, to avoid creating new damage at the fastener points. For roof tarping, we secure heavy-duty poly tarps with weighted edges and cap nails driven into undamaged decking — never into tile — so the tarp holds through wind without cracking surviving roofing material.

We also coordinate directly with your insurance carrier. We document dimensions, materials, and labor so the scope is clear when your adjuster arrives, reducing back-and-forth and keeping the claim moving.

## Working with El Dorado Hills HOAs and Local Requirements

A significant portion of El Dorado Hills falls within homeowner association jurisdictions, and many of those HOAs have aesthetic standards that apply even to emergency repairs. We've learned that some associations require notification — and occasionally approval — before exterior materials like plywood or visible tarps are installed, even in an emergency. We recommend homeowners contact their HOA management as soon as possible after an event; we can provide documentation of the emergency nature of the work to support any variance request. El Dorado County Building also has requirements around permitted reconstruction work that follows board-up, and we're familiar with how the handoff from emergency stabilization to permitted repair typically works in this jurisdiction.

## Local Note

Homes in the hillside sections of El Dorado Hills are often built on sloped lots with second-story rooflines that are difficult to access from grade. Tile roofs on steep pitches require fall-protection rigging before any crew member sets foot on the surface — this is non-negotiable on our jobs. It also means our crews arrive with the right equipment for this terrain rather than improvising on-site. If your home has a 6:12 or steeper pitch, plan for the tarp installation to take somewhat longer than a flat or low-slope roof; the additional time is the safety margin, not inefficiency.
