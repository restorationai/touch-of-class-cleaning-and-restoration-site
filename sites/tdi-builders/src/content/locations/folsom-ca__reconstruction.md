## Why Folsom Properties Face Distinct Reconstruction Challenges

Folsom sits at the edge of the Sierra Nevada foothills, which gives the city a climate pattern that sets it apart from the flat Sacramento basin. Hot, dry summers push interior moisture levels low — wood framing and engineered lumber can shrink and shift over years, creating gaps at sheathing seams and roof penetrations that become entry points the next time a storm arrives. When the rainy season does hit, it can arrive fast and hard. Expansive clay soils common throughout the region respond to that moisture cycle by swelling and settling, which puts pressure on slab foundations and can crack stem walls in ways that aren't obvious until a contractor opens a wall cavity.

Older western Folsom neighborhoods built in the 1970s and 1980s often feature wood-frame construction with original plywood sheathing, single-pane windows, and rooflines that weren't engineered to current California Building Code wind-uplift requirements. When fire or water damage forces a partial rebuild in those areas, bringing the repaired sections into current code compliance — not just back to original condition — adds scope that homeowners sometimes don't anticipate. TDI's team walks through that code-compliance picture before any work begins so there are no surprises mid-project.

## Our Reconstruction Process in Folsom

Every reconstruction project starts with a detailed scope-of-loss assessment. We document structural damage with photos, moisture readings, and written measurements — the kind of documentation that holds up with insurance adjusters and building inspectors alike. From there, the process moves through these stages:

**Stabilization and securing the structure.** Before any rebuild begins, the property needs to be safe and weather-tight. That may mean temporary boarding, tarping a damaged roof section, or shoring a compromised load-bearing wall.

**Permit acquisition through Sacramento County.** Folsom reconstruction projects that touch structural elements, electrical, plumbing, or mechanical systems require permits through Sacramento County's building department. Our team prepares the documentation, submits the applications, and coordinates inspections so the project stays on a legal and inspectable footing from day one.

**Framing, sheathing, and systems rough-in.** We rebuild to current California Building Code standards, which in fire-prone Folsom often means upgraded ignition-resistant materials at eaves and vents — requirements that apply in State Responsibility Areas and many local fire hazard severity zones that touch the city's eastern edges.

**Finish work and final inspection.** Drywall, insulation, flooring, cabinetry, paint, and exterior finishes are matched as closely as possible to pre-loss conditions, then signed off through the required county inspection before the job closes.

## Folsom HOA and Insurance Coordination

Many of Folsom's newer planned communities — particularly those developed in the 2000s and 2010s — are governed by HOAs with architectural review requirements. A reconstruction project that changes roofing materials, siding color, or window style may need HOA approval before work begins, and some associations require a licensed contractor's documentation as part of that submission. TDI coordinates directly with HOA management companies to get the right paperwork in front of the right people early, so approval doesn't become a bottleneck after permits are already in hand.

On the insurance side, we work with most major carriers and provide the line-item documentation — Xactimate-compatible estimates, photo logs, and material specifications — that adjusters need to process claims efficiently. We communicate directly with your adjuster throughout the project so you're not caught in the middle translating between your contractor and your insurance company.

## Local Note

Folsom's position in a designated High Fire Hazard Severity Zone in parts of its eastern footprint has a direct effect on reconstruction scope. When fire damage triggers a rebuild in those areas, California's building code requires that the repaired structure meet current Chapter 7A standards — ignition-resistant construction at eaves, vents screened with fine-mesh ember-resistant material, and in some cases upgraded exterior wall assemblies. Homeowners who received permits for their original construction under older code editions are sometimes surprised to learn that a fire-damage rebuild triggers these current requirements. We flag this at the assessment stage so the insurance scope and the permit scope align before demolition begins.
