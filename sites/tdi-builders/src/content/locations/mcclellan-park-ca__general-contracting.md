Renovating a property in McClellan Park means working inside a footprint most contractors have never touched: a former Air Force base turned mixed-use business and residential park, where hangar-era construction sits next to newer infill built under Sacramento County jurisdiction rather than a city building department. Whether you're converting commercial square footage, updating a residence built during the base's redevelopment years, or planning a full design-build remodel, the permitting path and the bones of the building are different from a typical Sacramento neighborhood job.

## Why McClellan Park Properties See Different Renovation Challenges

McClellan Park's building stock is unusual for the region. Many structures date to the base's active military years and were later adapted for civilian commercial or residential use, which means renovation work often uncovers construction methods, electrical layouts, or structural systems that predate current residential code entirely. Add to that the heavy clay soil common across this part of Sacramento County, which expands and contracts with the valley's wet winters and dry summers, and slab foundations in the area can show more movement than you'd expect in a newer subdivision. A remodel that looks straightforward on paper can turn into a conversation about footing repair or slab leveling once we're inside the walls.

Because McClellan Park sits in unincorporated Sacramento County rather than inside Sacramento city limits, permitting runs through the county building department. That's a meaningful difference for general contracting work: inspection scheduling, plan review timelines, and occupancy classifications for adapted base buildings all follow county procedure, not city code. A contractor who only works inside Sacramento proper can misjudge the approval sequence and stall a project before framing even starts.

## Our Renovations, Remodels and General Contracting Process in McClellan Park

We start with a walkthrough that's part design conversation and part forensic assessment, especially on any structure that predates the park's civilian redevelopment. That means checking for outdated electrical panels, verifying what's actually load-bearing versus what was added later as a partition, and confirming foundation condition before we finalize scope. From there we pull permits through Sacramento County, sequence demolition and structural work first, and bring in our own trade crews for framing, electrical, plumbing, and finish work so there's one point of accountability from design through final inspection rather than a chain of subcontractors you have to manage yourself.

On occupied properties we phase the work so you're not displaced for the full project, closing off work zones and coordinating utility shutoffs in advance. For commercial or mixed-use buildings in the park, we also account for the county's inspection cadence, scheduling rough-in inspections early enough that a delay doesn't push the whole job past a planned reopening date.

## McClellan Park Permitting & County Coordination

Because the park falls under county jurisdiction, we handle plan submittal, permit tracking, and inspection scheduling directly with Sacramento County rather than assuming city rules apply. For adapted base buildings, this sometimes means confirming the building's current occupancy classification before we can finalize a remodel scope, particularly if a space has shifted from its original military use to commercial or residential over the years. We handle that coordination as part of the project rather than leaving it for the property owner to untangle.

## Local note

A detail that trips up contractors unfamiliar with the area: several McClellan Park buildings have concrete slab construction poured decades ago under military specifications, which is denser and thicker than standard residential slab. Core drilling for new plumbing or electrical runs takes longer and dulls standard bits faster, so we budget extra time and bring heavier-duty equipment for any remodel that requires cutting into the original floor.
