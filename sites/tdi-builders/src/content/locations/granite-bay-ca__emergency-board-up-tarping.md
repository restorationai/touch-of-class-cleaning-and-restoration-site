Granite Bay sits in the Sierra Nevada foothills east of Sacramento, where long, dry summers give way to winters that can bring atmospheric river storms capable of stripping shingles, snapping oak limbs, and driving rain through any gap in a structure's envelope. When a wildfire ember lands on a wood-shake roof, a wind-driven branch punches through a window, or a kitchen fire leaves an exterior wall exposed, the window between the damage event and secondary loss — water intrusion, theft, smoke migration — can be measured in hours. TDI Builders, Inc. has been responding to exactly these moments since 1985, and our IICRC-certified, licensed and insured crews are available around the clock to board up, tarp, and secure Granite Bay properties before the next weather system arrives.

## Why Granite Bay Properties Face Distinct Board-Up and Tarping Challenges

Granite Bay's housing stock skews toward large custom homes and planned communities built from the late 1980s through the 2000s — many featuring steeply pitched tile or wood-shake roofs, expansive glazing, and multi-car garages with wide door openings. That architectural profile creates specific vulnerabilities. A tile roof that takes fire or impact damage can shed large sections quickly, leaving underlayment exposed to rain. Wide garage openings and floor-to-ceiling windows, common in the area's upscale builds, require oversized plywood panels or heavy-gauge poly sheeting to span properly — standard 4×8 sheets often need sister-framing to cover the full opening without sagging.

The surrounding oak woodland and chaparral also mean wildfire smoke and ember intrusion are real seasonal threats, not theoretical ones. After a fire event — whether the structure itself burned or a nearby wildland fire drove smoke and embers — board-up work isn't just about keeping rain out. It's about sealing the envelope so that smoke odor doesn't continue migrating into unaffected rooms while restoration planning is underway.

## Our Emergency Board-Up and Tarping Process in Granite Bay

When you call, a crew is dispatched from our Sacramento base with materials staged for the call — plywood, dimensional lumber for framing wide spans, heavy-duty poly tarps rated for extended outdoor exposure, and fastening systems appropriate for tile, composition, and wood-shake roofs. On arrival, we assess the full perimeter before touching anything: which openings are actively exposed to weather, which are at risk if wind direction shifts, and whether the roof damage has a secondary leak path that a single tarp won't cover.

Board-up follows a priority sequence — largest or most weather-exposed openings first, then secondary vulnerabilities. For roof tarping, we use a ridge-anchor method rather than simply draping poly over the peak, which keeps the tarp from lifting in the Diablo wind events that periodically push through the foothills. Every fastener placement accounts for the roofing material beneath: tile and slate require different anchor points than composition shingles to avoid creating new penetrations that outlast the tarp.

Documentation runs parallel to the physical work. We photograph every opening before and after boarding, record material dimensions, and note pre-existing conditions — all of which feeds directly into the insurance claim file.

## Local Note

Granite Bay's oak woodland setting means that after a wind event or wildfire, debris — branches, bark, and in fire situations, partially burned material — frequently lands on roofs and in gutters before a crew arrives. That debris can mask secondary damage: a branch that looks like it's resting on intact tile may have cracked the tile beneath it and created a leak path that won't show up until the next rain. Our crews clear the debris zone around every tarp and board-up area before finalizing the scope, because a tarp installed over hidden damage just moves the discovery date — it doesn't prevent the loss.
