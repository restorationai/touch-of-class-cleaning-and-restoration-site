## Why Loomis Properties See Sewage Backup Issues

Loomis is a community built largely around its rural and semi-rural character — horse properties, larger lots, and a housing stock that spans from mid-century ranch homes to newer construction along the Highway 50 corridor. That mix creates a specific set of plumbing vulnerabilities.

Many properties in and around Loomis rely on private septic systems rather than municipal sewer connections. Septic tanks that haven't been pumped on schedule, drain fields that have become saturated after a wet winter, or distribution lines compromised by tree roots can all cause raw sewage to surface in a yard or back up through floor drains and toilets inside the home. The Placer County foothills also experience expansive clay soils that swell with winter moisture and contract in summer heat — a cycle that stresses older sewer laterals and can crack pipe joints over time, letting root intrusion accelerate the damage.

Even properties connected to municipal sewer service can experience backups when the main line becomes overwhelmed during heavy rainfall events, which are not uncommon in the foothills between November and March. When that happens, sewage doesn't just sit in a drain — it spreads across flooring, wicks into subfloor materials, and begins generating harmful pathogens within hours.

## Our Sewage Cleanup and Sanitization Process in Loomis

Raw sewage is classified as Category 3 water — the most contaminated type — and it requires a structured removal and sanitization protocol, not just a mop and a shop vac.

When our crew arrives, the first priority is containment: isolating the affected area to prevent cross-contamination to unaffected rooms. We extract standing sewage using truck-mounted and portable extraction equipment, then remove any porous materials — carpet, pad, drywall, insulation — that have absorbed contaminated water and cannot be safely restored. Structural materials that can be dried and retained are treated with EPA-registered antimicrobial agents designed to address the bacterial and viral load associated with raw sewage.

Drying equipment — industrial dehumidifiers and air movers — runs continuously after extraction, and moisture readings are taken at each visit to confirm that subfloor assemblies, wall cavities, and crawl spaces are reaching acceptable levels. In Loomis homes with pier-and-beam or raised foundations, crawl space contamination is a particular concern: sewage that reaches the crawl can saturate vapor barriers, soil, and wood framing in ways that aren't visible from above. We inspect below the floor before closing out any job.

Final sanitization includes a thorough application of antimicrobial treatment to all affected surfaces, followed by documentation that supports your insurance claim.

## Loomis Insurance Coordination

Sewage backup coverage is often a separate endorsement on a standard homeowner's policy, and many Loomis homeowners discover they have — or don't have — that rider only after a loss occurs. Whether your claim falls under that endorsement, a general water damage provision, or a commercial property policy, we document the loss thoroughly: photographs of affected materials before removal, moisture readings, scope of work, and itemized invoices formatted for adjuster review. For properties on private septic systems, homeowner's insurance typically does not cover septic system repair itself — but the resulting interior damage often is covered. We can help clarify the scope of what's claimable so you're not leaving money on the table.
