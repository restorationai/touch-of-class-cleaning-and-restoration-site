## Why Loomis Homes Call for a Thoughtful Kitchen Approach

The housing stock in Loomis is unusually varied for a town its size. You'll find mid-century ranch homes with original galley kitchens that were designed around a single cook and a much smaller refrigerator, sitting a few streets away from hillside custom homes built in the 1990s with vaulted ceilings and island layouts that have simply aged out of style. That mix matters when planning a remodel because the structural assumptions, ceiling height, load-bearing walls, plumbing rough-in locations, electrical panel capacity, differ significantly between eras.

Placer County's dry summers also affect material selection in ways that homeowners moving from coastal California sometimes underestimate. Wood cabinetry and hardwood flooring expand and contract more aggressively in low-humidity winters and high-heat summers than they do near the coast. Solid wood drawer boxes and properly finished cabinet interiors handle that seasonal movement better than budget-grade MDF alternatives, which can swell at joints after a few years of Loomis summers.

## Cabinet Installation and Material Selection for the Foothills Climate

Cabinet installation is where a kitchen remodel either holds together for thirty years or starts showing problems in five. In the Loomis area, we pay particular attention to the space between the cabinet box and exterior walls, which can see significant temperature swings. Proper shimming, leveling on floors that have settled over decades, and securing to studs rather than just drywall are baseline requirements, but so is leaving appropriate expansion gaps on runs of cabinetry that span a full wall.

For countertops, quartz has become the dominant choice in Placer County foothills homes because it handles heat, resists staining from the hard well water common in rural Loomis properties, and doesn't require the annual sealing that natural granite does. That said, granite and quartzite remain strong options for homeowners who want the natural variation, and we work with local fabricators who can template and turn around slabs without the extended lead times of big-box sourcing.

## Local Note

One thing that catches homeowners off guard in older Loomis properties, particularly ranch homes built before the mid-1970s, is the presence of original cast-iron drain lines under the kitchen slab or crawl space. When a remodel involves moving the sink or adding a dishwasher drain, those lines need to be inspected before the design is finalized. Cast iron at that age can be partially collapsed or heavily scaled, and discovering that during rough-in rather than during design saves both time and money. It's a quick camera inspection, but it's the kind of thing that separates a remodel that goes smoothly from one that stalls mid-project.
