Arden-Arcade's housing stock is overwhelmingly postwar: ranch-style homes built during Sacramento's suburban boom of the 1950s and 1960s, many sitting on lots that were generous for their era but cramped by today's standards. When a growing family in one of these homes needs a fourth bedroom, a larger primary suite, or a home office that isn't also the dining table, the usual options are a bump out addition along an existing wall or a full room addition that ties into the original roofline. TDI Builders has been building and rebuilding Sacramento-area homes since 1985, and additions on these older ranch footprints come with their own set of structural and permitting questions that a newer subdivision simply doesn't raise.

## Why Arden-Arcade Homes Need Thoughtful Addition Planning

Most of the ranch homes in this area were built with slab-on-grade foundations and roof framing sized for the original footprint, not for a future addition. Tying a new room into that structure means matching roof pitch, verifying the existing foundation can carry new point loads, and often discovering that electrical panels from the 1950s and 60s need upgrading before they can support a new bedroom or bathroom circuit. Lot sizes here also vary more than people expect: some Arden-Arcade parcels were platted with wide frontages and shallow backyards, which pushes a room addition toward a side or rear bump out rather than a straight back extension.

## Permitting Through Sacramento County

Arden-Arcade is unincorporated, which means addition permits don't go through a city planning department, they route through Sacramento County. That distinction matters for setback requirements, lot coverage limits, and how quickly plan review moves. We handle the application package, from site plans to structural calculations, and coordinate directly with the county so the project doesn't stall waiting on a resubmittal. For homeowners who've only dealt with city permitting before, this is often the first surprise of planning a house addition here, and it's one we walk through at the first site visit.

## Our Room Addition Process in Arden-Arcade

We start with a site visit to document the existing foundation type, roof framing, and utility runs, since all three drive what kind of addition is realistic on a given lot. From there we develop a design that respects the home's original roofline rather than bolting on something visibly mismatched, which also helps with county review and with resale value down the line. Once permits are approved, we sequence foundation work, framing, and roof tie-in before moving to electrical, insulation, and finish work, keeping the footprint of disruption to the existing living space as small as possible. Throughout, we're licensed and insured for residential work, and we bill progress draws clearly so there are no surprises on where the budget stands.

## Local note

Because much of Arden-Arcade sits in the American River corridor, soil moisture and clay content can shift noticeably from one block to the next, even within the same older subdivision. We don't assume a footing spec that worked on one street will work two blocks over; we pull soil and grading information specific to the lot before finalizing footing depth on a room addition or bump out, which avoids the cracking and settling issues that show up a few years later when that step gets skipped.
