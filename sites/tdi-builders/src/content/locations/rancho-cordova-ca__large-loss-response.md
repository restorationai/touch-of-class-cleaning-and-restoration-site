## Why Rancho Cordova Properties Face Elevated Catastrophic Loss Risk

Rancho Cordova's commercial landscape is unusually dense for a Sacramento suburb. The Aerojet Road and White Rock Road corridors are home to aerospace contractors, light manufacturing, large-format warehouses, and multi-tenant office parks — building types where a single loss event can affect multiple tenants, trigger business-interruption claims across several policies, and require simultaneous coordination with a property manager, a carrier, and a public adjuster.

The region's climate adds its own pressure. Sacramento Valley summers push temperatures well above 100°F, and that heat accelerates secondary damage after a fire or water event: drywall wicks moisture faster, adhesives fail sooner, and mold colonization can begin within 24 to 48 hours in a building that loses HVAC. Conversely, the wet season — typically November through March — can overwhelm older drainage infrastructure, particularly in the lower-lying commercial pockets near the American River. When a storm event coincides with a structural failure, the resulting loss is rarely contained to a single system.

## Our Large-Loss Response Process in Rancho Cordova

Large-loss response is not a scaled-up version of a residential water call. It is a managed project from the first hour.

**Rapid stabilization** comes first: emergency board-up, temporary roofing, water extraction with industrial-grade truck-mount and desiccant dehumidification equipment, and utility isolation where needed. Because we operate 24/7, that first call at 2 a.m. reaches a live coordinator, not a voicemail.

**Scope and documentation** run parallel to mitigation. Our IICRC-certified technicians photograph and log affected materials room by room, generating the kind of line-item documentation that large-loss adjusters and carrier catastrophe teams require before they will authorize a scope of work. Skipping this step — or doing it loosely — is the most common reason large claims stall.

**Reconstruction planning** begins before mitigation is complete. For a commercial property in Rancho Cordova, that means pulling Sacramento County permits, coordinating with the local building department on inspection scheduling, and sequencing trades so the building returns to occupancy as fast as the code process allows. With 40-plus years of construction and restoration experience, our project managers understand how to run mitigation and rebuild as a single workflow rather than two separate handoffs.

## Coordinating with Large-Loss Adjusters and Carriers

Catastrophic commercial losses in Rancho Cordova routinely involve dedicated large-loss adjusting units — specialists who work differently from residential claims handlers. They expect detailed Xactimate or Symbility estimates, moisture mapping with calibrated readings, and a restoration contractor who can defend scope line by line.

TDI Builders works directly with carrier catastrophe teams and public adjusters. We provide the documentation package they need upfront: photo logs, drying reports, equipment placement records, and a preliminary reconstruction estimate. This reduces back-and-forth and keeps the project moving. We are licensed and insured, which is a baseline requirement for most commercial carriers before they will authorize a large-loss contractor.

For multi-tenant properties, we also help property managers communicate with individual tenants about timelines and access — a coordination layer that matters in Rancho Cordova's busy mixed-use and flex-industrial parks, where a tenant's business interruption clock starts the moment the building is closed.

## Local Note

Rancho Cordova's commercial building stock along the Sunrise Boulevard and Mather Field Road areas includes a significant number of tilt-up concrete structures built in the 1980s and 1990s. Tilt-up panels create a specific challenge after fire events: the panels themselves are often structurally sound while the interior steel connections, ledger bolts, and roof deck above are compromised. A visual inspection from the parking lot can look deceptively clean. Our crews know to call for a structural engineer review before any interior mitigation work begins in tilt-up buildings — not because it is always required, but because the consequences of missing a compromised connection in a building that size are not recoverable.
