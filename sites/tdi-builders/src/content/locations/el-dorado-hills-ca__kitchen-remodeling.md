## Why El Dorado Hills Kitchens Have Specific Remodeling Considerations

The housing stock in El Dorado Hills skews toward homes built in the 1980s through the 2000s — a period when tract builders favored builder-grade laminate cabinets, tile countertops with wide grout joints, and soffit-enclosed upper cabinet runs that eat ceiling height. Those soffits are almost always the first thing homeowners want gone, and removing them correctly means verifying whether electrical runs or HVAC ducts are tucked inside before a single nail is pulled. The foothills climate adds another layer: the low humidity in summer can cause solid-wood cabinet doors to gap and warp if they were not finished on all six faces originally, which is common in production-built homes of that era. A remodel is the right moment to upgrade to cabinet boxes and doors that are properly sealed and dimensionally stable for the elevation.

El Dorado Hills also sits within El Dorado County's jurisdiction for building permits, and the county requires permits for structural changes, electrical panel upgrades, and plumbing relocations — all common elements of a full kitchen renovation. Pulling permits is not optional, and it matters at resale: unpermitted kitchen work in El Dorado County can complicate title transfer. TDI Builders handles permit coordination as a standard part of the project, not an add-on.

## HOA Considerations in El Dorado Hills Communities

A significant portion of El Dorado Hills is governed by homeowners associations, and some of those HOAs extend their review authority to exterior changes that accompany a kitchen remodel — a new range hood exhaust penetration through an exterior wall, for instance, or a window enlargement to bring more light into a north-facing kitchen. Interior-only work typically does not require HOA approval, but any change that alters the exterior envelope or roofline can trigger an architectural review process. TDI Builders flags these situations early so that HOA submittals and county permits move in parallel rather than sequentially, which can otherwise add weeks to a project timeline.

El Dorado Hills homes built in the late 1980s and 1990s frequently have kitchen soffits that were framed to conceal the original 6-inch HVAC supply runs feeding the great room.
