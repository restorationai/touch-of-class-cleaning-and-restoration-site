## Why Citrus Heights Construction Projects Leave a Specific Kind of Mess

The Sacramento Valley's dry heat accelerates construction timelines, which means trades often overlap — drywall finishers, painters, and flooring crews working in close succession. ## Our Post-Construction and Specialty Cleaning Process in Citrus Heights

A final clean after construction isn't a single pass with a mop. The process moves in stages, and sequence matters.

The first phase is rough-out: removing bulk debris, pulling protective film off appliances and fixtures, clearing construction materials left by trades. This stage often reveals what's underneath — adhesive residue on floors, overspray on trim, silicone smears on glass.

The second phase is detail cleaning. This is where the work separates a professional crew from a general cleaning service. Outlets and switch plates come off so the boxes behind them can be vacuumed. Window tracks get cleaned down to the frame corners. Cabinet interiors are wiped before hardware is inspected. HVAC registers are removed and cleaned before the system runs, because circulating construction dust through new ductwork defeats the purpose of everything else.

The final phase is inspection and specialty work — polishing fixtures, buffing surfaces, addressing any staining or residue the earlier phases surfaced. For renovation projects on older Citrus Heights homes, this sometimes includes cleaning original hardwood floors that were exposed during the project, which requires different products than engineered or luxury vinyl plank.

## Local Note

One thing that comes up on renovation projects in Citrus Heights's older ranch-style homes: the original forced-air systems in these houses often have return-air ducts that pull directly from hallways or living areas without a filter grille — just an open return behind a flat register cover. During construction, those returns draw in drywall dust and insulation fibers and deposit them throughout the duct system. A surface-only clean leaves that contamination in place. On projects in this housing stock, the cleaning scope should always include register removal and duct-mouth vacuuming before the HVAC system is turned on for the first time post-renovation.
