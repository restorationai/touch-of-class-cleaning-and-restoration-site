## Why Loomis Properties See Contents Damage Differently

The housing stock in Loomis skews toward larger single-family homes on acreage, many built between the 1970s and early 2000s, with a mix of wood-frame construction, vaulted ceilings, and finished basements or bonus rooms that collect water when drainage fails. Homes on larger lots often have detached garages, workshops, and outbuildings where tools, seasonal gear, and stored furniture are just as vulnerable to smoke infiltration or moisture as the main living space — and those secondary structures are easy to overlook in the initial panic after a loss.

The foothills climate adds a layer of complexity that flat-valley restoration doesn't always account for. Summer temperatures in Loomis regularly push past 95°F, which accelerates mold colonization on damp contents — the window between a water loss and active mold growth on porous materials can be shorter here than in cooler coastal climates. In winter, back-to-back atmospheric river events can keep relative humidity elevated for days, meaning items that seem dry at the surface may still be holding moisture in dense wood, leather, or layered textiles.

## Our Contents Restoration and Storage Process in Loomis

Every contents job begins with a room-by-room inventory before a single item leaves the property. Each piece is photographed, logged, and assigned a condition rating — this documentation matters both for your insurance claim and for ensuring nothing gets lost or misattributed during the cleaning and storage phase.

Pack-out from Loomis properties follows a deliberate sequence. Fragile items, electronics, and documents are separated and packed first. Furniture and large textiles come next, wrapped and padded for transport. Once contents reach our facility, the cleaning method is matched to the material: ultrasonic cleaning for hard goods and small valuables, dry-cleaning solvents for smoke-affected textiles, ozone or hydroxyl treatment for persistent odor in upholstered pieces, and HEPA-filtered air scrubbing for electronics and documents. Items are stored in a climate-controlled environment until your home is ready to receive them — not in a standard warehouse where summer heat could undo the restoration work.

Return delivery is coordinated with your reconstruction timeline so contents go back into finished, dried, and cleaned spaces rather than being staged in a partially repaired home.

## Loomis Insurance Coordination

Contents restoration and storage is a covered line item under most standard homeowner policies, but the documentation requirements are specific. Carriers want itemized inventories, before-and-after photographs, cleaning method records, and storage receipts — not a summary estimate. TDI Builders has worked with insurance claims on residential losses since 1985, and the documentation package we produce is built to satisfy adjuster review without requiring you to chase down records after the fact.

If your policy includes a contents coverage sublimit or a depreciation schedule for older furniture, we can walk through what that means for your specific inventory before work begins so there are no surprises at settlement.

## Local Note

Loomis and the surrounding Placer County foothill communities see a meaningful number of homes with whole-house fans and open-rafter construction that were popular in 1970s and 1980s builds. These ventilation designs are great for summer cooling, but during a wildfire smoke event they can pull fine particulate matter deep into wall cavities, attic insulation, and the HVAC system — which means smoke odor in contents isn't just surface contamination. Upholstered furniture, bedding, and clothing stored in rooms with whole-house fans active during a smoke event often require more intensive odor treatment than the same items in a tightly sealed modern home. Knowing this upfront changes how we prioritize the cleaning sequence.
