## Why Lincoln Properties Face Distinct Reconstruction Demands

Lincoln sits in Placer County's western foothills, where the climate swings hard — triple-digit dry heat in summer, ground-saturating rain events in winter, and occasional Tule fog that traps moisture against building surfaces for days. That thermal cycling is hard on wood framing, stucco exteriors, and the fiber-cement siding common in the planned communities that expanded through the 2000s. When a loss event opens a structure to those elements, the reconstruction window matters: exposed framing that bakes in July heat can check and warp before a rebuild crew even pulls permits.

The city's housing stock layers two very different eras. Closer to the historic downtown, you find older wood-frame construction with plaster walls and pier-and-beam or slab foundations that predate modern seismic and energy codes. Out toward the newer master-planned areas, you encounter tract homes built to California's Title 24 energy standards, with engineered lumber, spray-foam insulation, and integrated HVAC systems that require careful sequencing during reconstruction so mechanical work doesn't have to be torn out twice. Understanding which era you're working in shapes every phase of the project.

## Our Reconstruction Process in Lincoln

Every reconstruction project starts with a thorough structural assessment — not just what's visibly damaged, but what the damage event did to adjacent systems. Fire reconstruction, for example, requires evaluating smoke and heat penetration into wall cavities, attic insulation, and HVAC ductwork before framing work begins. Water-loss reconstruction means confirming that all moisture has been mitigated before new drywall goes up, because enclosing wet framing in Lincoln's warm summers creates ideal conditions for mold colonization within days.

From there, the process moves through a documented sequence:

- **Structural stabilization** — shoring, temporary weatherproofing, and board-up if the structure is open to the elements
- **Demolition and debris removal** — selective demo that preserves salvageable framing and finishes wherever code and condition allow
- **Rough trades coordination** — framing, electrical, plumbing, and HVAC rough-in, sequenced to pass Placer County inspections without unnecessary delays
- **Insulation and drywall** — installed to current California energy and fire codes, which affect both material selection and installation method
- **Finish work and final inspections** — flooring, cabinetry, paint, and fixtures, followed by county sign-off

Because TDI Builders handles both the mitigation and reconstruction sides of a loss, there's no handoff gap — the crew that documented the damage is the same team managing the rebuild.

## Local Note

One pattern worth knowing in Lincoln: homes built during the city's major growth period in the mid-2000s frequently used OSB sheathing on exterior walls rather than plywood. OSB holds up well when dry, but once it absorbs water from a roof breach or a plumbing failure, it swells and delaminates faster than plywood — and it doesn't always look compromised until you're mid-demo. On reconstruction projects in those neighborhoods, we budget for sheathing replacement as a likely line item rather than a contingency, which keeps the project scope honest from the start and avoids change-order surprises during framing.
