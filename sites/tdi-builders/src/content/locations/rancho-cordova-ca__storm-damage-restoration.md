Rancho Cordova sits in the Sacramento Valley's storm corridor, where atmospheric river events can drop several inches of rain in hours, and the region's notorious Diablo winds push downed trees and torn roofing into structures that were never designed to handle that kind of lateral load. When a storm rolls through and leaves a gaping hole in your roof, standing water in your garage, or a 60-foot oak across your fence line, the clock starts immediately — saturated framing and insulation can begin to degrade within the first day, and secondary mold colonization follows close behind. TDI Builders, Inc. has been handling structural storm damage since 1985, and our IICRC-certified technicians are available around the clock to respond.

## Why Rancho Cordova Properties See Elevated Storm Damage Risk

The Sacramento Valley's wet season runs roughly November through March, but the storms that cause the most damage here aren't always the slow, soaking ones — they're the fast-moving systems that arrive with little warning and combine heavy rainfall with wind gusts strong enough to strip shingles and snap mature trees. Rancho Cordova's position east of Sacramento along the American River corridor means it sits in a natural drainage path. When upstream runoff combines with local rainfall, water tables rise quickly, and properties with shallow foundations or aging drainage infrastructure can see water intrusion from below as well as above.

A large portion of Rancho Cordova's residential housing stock was built during the postwar boom of the 1950s through the 1970s. Homes from that era commonly feature wood-framed roofs with original sheathing, single-pane windows, and composition shingles that are well past their rated service life. When a storm event stresses these older assemblies — whether through wind uplift, impact from debris, or prolonged saturation — the damage tends to be more extensive than it would be on newer construction. Stucco exteriors common to the area can also mask water intrusion for weeks, allowing moisture to migrate into wall cavities before any visible sign appears inside.

## Our Storm Damage Restoration Process in Rancho Cordova

The first priority after any storm loss is emergency stabilization — stopping active water intrusion before it compounds the structural damage. That means emergency tarping and board-up on compromised roofs and openings, followed by a systematic moisture assessment using thermal imaging and calibrated meters to find water that has already migrated behind walls or under flooring.

Once the structure is stabilized, the restoration process moves through debris removal, structural drying with commercial-grade desiccant and refrigerant dehumidifiers, and documentation of every affected material for insurance purposes. For tree impact damage — one of the most common storm loss types in this part of Sacramento County — we assess the full load path of the impact, not just the visible entry point. A falling tree transfers enormous force through rafters and wall plates, and damage often extends well beyond the obvious breach.

Final scope includes structural repairs, roofing, siding, interior finishes, and any code-required upgrades triggered by the repair permit. Because TDI Builders holds both construction and restoration capabilities in-house, there's no handoff gap between the mitigation crew and the rebuild crew.

## Rancho Cordova Insurance Coordination

Storm damage claims in California are typically covered under the dwelling protection portion of a standard homeowners policy, but the documentation requirements matter. Insurers want a clear separation between pre-existing wear and storm-caused damage — a distinction that's especially important on older Rancho Cordova homes where deferred maintenance and storm damage can look similar to an adjuster reviewing photos.

Our team photographs and documents affected materials before any work begins, prepares a detailed scope of loss, and communicates directly with your carrier's adjuster throughout the process. We work with most major insurance carriers and can help you understand what your policy covers before you commit to a repair scope.

## Local Note

One pattern that comes up repeatedly in Rancho Cordova storm work involves stucco-clad homes built in the 1960s and early 1970s. The original stucco on these houses was applied over wood lath, and the moisture barriers behind it — where they exist at all — are long past their functional life. After a wind-driven rain event, water finds its way through hairline cracks in the stucco face and sits against the wood lath and sheathing, which reads completely dry at the surface. Homeowners sometimes close out a claim after patching the visible crack, then find rot and mold in the wall cavity two or three years later. A proper post-storm assessment on these homes needs to go behind the surface, not just across it.
