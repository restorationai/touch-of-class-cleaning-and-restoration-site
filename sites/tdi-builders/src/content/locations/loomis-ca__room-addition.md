## Why Loomis Homes Are Well-Suited — and Well-Challenged — for Additions

Loomis is largely single-story, ranch-era construction built on expansive clay soils that swell in the wet season and crack in the long, dry summers. That soil behavior matters the moment a foundation for a new room addition touches the ground. Footings need to be sized and reinforced for the shrink-swell cycle, and slabs poured in late summer can behave very differently by the following February when the first heavy rains arrive. Placer County's grading and drainage requirements reflect this reality, and any reputable home addition contractor working in the area will factor soil conditions into the structural design before a single form is set.

The older housing stock — many homes date to the 1960s and 1970s — also means that electrical panels are frequently undersized for the load a new addition creates. Adding a room to a house that still runs on a 100-amp service often triggers a panel upgrade as part of the permit scope, something homeowners don't always anticipate until the design phase is underway.
