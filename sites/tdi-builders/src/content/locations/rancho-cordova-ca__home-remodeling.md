## Why Rancho Cordova's Housing Stock Shapes Every Remodel

The Sacramento Valley's climate is a real factor in how homes age here. Summers routinely push past 100°F, and the delta breezes that cool the region at night create significant temperature swings that stress building materials over decades. In homes built during the postwar boom along the Folsom Boulevard corridor, that cycle of heat expansion and contraction has often worked caulk loose, cracked tile substrates, and shifted door frames out of square. Before a single cabinet goes in or a floor gets laid, a thorough remodel in Rancho Cordova has to account for what forty or fifty years of valley summers have done to the underlying structure.

Crawl spaces are common in the older ranch-style homes throughout the area, and they frequently show signs of moisture intrusion from the clay-heavy soils that dominate much of Sacramento County. Clay soil doesn't drain the way sandy or loamy soil does, it holds water against foundation stems and subfloor framing longer than homeowners expect. A kitchen or bathroom remodel that skips a crawl space inspection before closing up walls is a remodel that may need to be reopened.

## Our Home Remodeling Process in Rancho Cordova

Every project starts with a walkthrough that goes beyond the rooms you want changed. ## Local Note

Homes built in Rancho Cordova during the late 1950s and 1960s frequently used a fiber-reinforced drywall compound that was common in California tract construction of that era. It looks like standard drywall mud, but it sands differently and doesn't accept primer the same way modern joint compound does.
