Carmichael's tree-lined streets and mix of mid-century ranch homes, post-war bungalows, and newer infill construction make it one of the more interesting places in Sacramento County to remodel — and one of the more demanding. Homes built in the 1950s and 1960s that define much of the community's character often carry original plumbing, knob-and-tube wiring tucked inside walls, and floor plans that weren't designed with open-concept living in mind. Bringing those spaces into the present without losing what makes them worth keeping is exactly the kind of work TDI Builders, Inc. has been doing since 1985.

## Why Carmichael's Housing Stock Shapes Every Remodel

Carmichael sits in an unincorporated pocket of Sacramento County, which means permits are issued through the county rather than a city building department — a distinction that catches some homeowners off guard when they're comparing contractor bids. County review timelines and inspection scheduling can run differently than what neighbors in Citrus Heights or the city of Sacramento experience, and a contractor unfamiliar with that process can add weeks to a project before a single wall comes down.

Beyond permitting, the housing stock itself sets the terms. Ranch-style homes built between the late 1940s and early 1970s — the dominant form across much of Carmichael — frequently have low-slope rooflines that complicate attic access during bathroom or kitchen vent rerouting. Original hardwood floors under decades of carpet are common, and original single-pane aluminum windows are still in place in a surprising number of homes. Each of those conditions affects scope, timeline, and material selection before the design conversation even begins.

The Sacramento Valley's climate adds its own layer. Summers regularly push past 100°F, which means whole-home remodels that touch the HVAC system need to account for cooling load changes — especially when walls are opened, square footage is added, or a garage conversion brings new conditioned space online. Insulation upgrades and window replacements that work in coastal climates don't always translate directly to a valley home that bakes from June through September.

## Our Home Remodeling Process in Carmichael

Every project starts with a walkthrough that treats the existing structure as a document — what the walls, floors, and ceilings tell us about how the home was built informs every decision about how it should be changed. For older Carmichael homes, that often means opening a small area before finalizing scope, because what's behind the drywall or plaster isn't always what the original plans suggest.

From there, the process moves through design coordination, material selection, permit application with Sacramento County, and a phased construction schedule built around how you actually live in the space. Kitchen remodels and bathroom remodels get sequenced to minimize the time a household is without a functioning sink or shower. Whole-home remodels are staged so that one wing of the house remains livable while work progresses through the other.

Throughout construction, licensed and insured crews handle every trade phase — framing, electrical, plumbing, tile, cabinetry, and finish carpentry — under a single project manager rather than a rotating cast of subcontractors you'd have to coordinate yourself. That continuity matters most on complex projects where a decision made during rough plumbing affects finish selections made three weeks later.

## Local Note

One pattern that shows up repeatedly in Carmichael's older ranch homes: the original interior walls are often a plaster-over-lath system rather than drywall, particularly in homes built before the mid-1960s. Plaster walls are harder and more sound-deadening than drywall — qualities worth preserving where possible — but they require a different approach when cutting for electrical or plumbing rough-in. A standard drywall saw tears through plaster unpredictably, and patching plaster to match the original texture is a skill fewer finish crews carry. Knowing that going in changes how we plan the demo phase and which finishers we schedule for the back end of the job.
