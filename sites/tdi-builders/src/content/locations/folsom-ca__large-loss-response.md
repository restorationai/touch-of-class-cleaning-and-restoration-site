## Why Folsom Properties Experience Large-Scale Losses

Folsom's growth over the past three decades means a significant portion of its commercial and residential building stock was constructed during the 1990s and early 2000s — an era when fire-suppression systems were standard but when CPVC plumbing was also widely installed. CPVC is brittle as it ages, and a single fitting failure in a pressurized sprinkler or domestic water line can discharge hundreds of gallons before a shutoff is reached. In multi-story commercial buildings along the East Bidwell Street corridor, that water migrates through suspended ceilings, elevator shafts, and electrical rooms with little resistance.

Folsom also sits in a fire-hazard severity zone transition area. Properties near the open space preserves and the American River Parkway are not immune to ember cast during regional fire events, and the dry Diablo winds that push through the Sacramento Valley in fall can carry embers well into developed neighborhoods. A fire that starts at the roofline of a wood-frame structure can reach full structural involvement before a suppression system activates — and the resulting loss is rarely limited to one unit or one floor.

## Our Large Loss and Catastrophic Response Process in Folsom

Large loss work is categorically different from a standard residential water or fire claim. The scope requires a dedicated project manager on-site, not just a crew lead. From the moment TDI Builders, Inc. is engaged, the process runs on parallel tracks: emergency stabilization, forensic documentation, and carrier coordination happen simultaneously rather than sequentially.

Stabilization comes first — board-up, temporary roofing, structural shoring if needed, and industrial extraction and drying equipment deployed at a scale that matches the affected square footage. Documentation runs alongside it: thermal imaging, moisture mapping, air quality readings, and photographic inventories that large loss adjusters and independent appraisers can work from directly. Our IICRC certified technicians dry to the S500 and S520 standards, which matters when a carrier's large loss specialist is reviewing the claim file.

From there, the rebuild phase is scoped, permitted through Sacramento County or the City of Folsom depending on jurisdiction, and sequenced to get the property back to pre-loss condition — or better — on a timeline that accounts for material lead times, subcontractor scheduling, and occupancy requirements.
