Building a new home in Foothill Farms means working with Sacramento County's permitting process rather than a city planning department, since Foothill Farms is unincorporated. That single fact changes the timeline, the inspection schedule, and even how the lot gets surveyed before a shovel goes in the ground. TDI Builders has handled ground-up construction across the greater Sacramento area for more than 40 years, and we plan every Foothill Farms project around county review cycles from day one instead of discovering them midway through framing.

## Why Foothill Farms Lots Need a Different Approach

Much of Foothill Farms sits on tract parcels developed in the postwar decades, which means a lot of the surrounding housing stock is mid-century ranch construction on slab or raised foundations. When a new build goes in next to or in place of one of those older homes, the soil underneath often hasn't been engineered to today's standards. Sacramento Valley clay is known for expanding and contracting with seasonal moisture swings, hot dry summers followed by wet winters, and that movement is exactly what cracks a poorly designed slab five years after move-in. Before we finalize a foundation plan, we look at how the parcel has drained historically and whether the soil report calls for a post-tension slab or deeper footings than the neighboring homes were built with.

## Our New Home Construction Process in Foothill Farms

We start with the Sacramento County permit package: site plan, soil and grading report, and utility connection applications, since Foothill Farms properties typically tie into county water and sewer infrastructure rather than a city system. From there, our sequence looks like most ground-up builds, but we schedule concrete pours and curing windows around the Central Valley's summer heat, when slab temperatures can affect cure time and cracking risk. Framing, rough mechanical, and electrical follow, each stamped by county inspection before we close up walls. We keep clients looped in at every inspection milestone rather than batching updates, because a failed rough-in inspection on a county timeline can add real weeks if it's not caught early.

## Foothill Farms Permitting and Lot Considerations

A lot of the confusion we see on new builds in this area comes from older parcel lines that don't match current county setback requirements, especially on lots that were subdivided decades ago from larger agricultural parcels. We verify easements and setbacks against current county records before design work starts, not after, since a redesign mid-permit costs more time than a careful survey up front. If the property backs onto a drainage easement, which is common in parts of Foothill Farms given the area's flood-control history tied to the American River watershed, we coordinate grading plans that keep runoff moving the way the county expects.

## Local note

One thing that trips up out-of-area builders in Foothill Farms: a surprising number of lots have legacy septic or well infrastructure left over from before the area was fully built out, even though most of the neighborhood now runs on county water and sewer. We always pull utility history on a parcel before finalizing a site plan, because capping an old well incorrectly or missing a buried septic tank during grading is the kind of thing that stalls a project for weeks once county inspectors find it.
