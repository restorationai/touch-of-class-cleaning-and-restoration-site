Folsom summers are no joke — triple-digit heat bakes the Sacramento foothills from June through September, and when a house fire, burst pipe, or smoke event hits during that stretch, the contents inside face a second wave of damage the moment they're exposed to that dry, superheated air. Soot embeds faster into porous fabrics, smoke odor polymerizes into wood grain, and moisture from firefighting efforts can begin promoting mold growth within 48 hours. TDI Builders, Inc. has been handling contents restoration and storage since 1985, and the way heat and low humidity interact with damaged belongings here is something the process has to account for from the first hour on-site.

## Why Folsom Properties See Contents Damage Differently

Folsom sits at the edge of the Sierra Nevada foothills, and the housing stock reflects that geography. Neighborhoods closer to the older core of the city include homes built in the 1970s and 1980s with wood-paneled interiors, particleboard cabinetry, and older upholstered furniture — materials that absorb smoke odor deeply and can off-gas for weeks if not treated promptly. Newer master-planned communities on the eastern edge of the city feature tighter construction with more synthetic materials, which trap smoke particulates differently and often require ultrasonic cleaning for electronics, collectibles, and hard-surface décor.

Folsom's climate also swings hard in the other direction: winter storms rolling off the Sierra can bring sudden, heavy rainfall that overwhelms drainage and leads to interior flooding. Contents damaged by Category 2 or Category 3 water — from a sewage backup or a roof breach during a storm — require a completely different cleaning and decontamination protocol than fire-damaged items. Knowing which scenario you're dealing with determines everything about how items are handled, packaged, and stored.

## Our Contents Pack-Out and Restoration Process in Folsom

The process begins with a room-by-room inventory before a single item leaves the property. Every piece — from furniture to framed photographs to kitchen appliances — is photographed, catalogued, and assigned a condition code. That documentation matters for your insurance claim and gives you a clear chain of custody throughout the job.

From there, contents are packed out using materials appropriate to the damage type: sealed poly bags for smoke-affected soft goods, padded crating for artwork and electronics, climate-controlled transport for anything sensitive to temperature swings. Given Folsom's summer heat, we move quickly once items are packed — sitting in a hot vehicle is its own form of secondary damage.

Cleaning methods are matched to the item and the loss type. Ultrasonic cleaning removes soot and residue from hard surfaces and electronics without abrasion. Ozone and hydroxyl treatments address odor at the molecular level in soft goods and upholstery. Furniture restoration after fire or water damage may involve refinishing, re-upholstering, or structural repair depending on the extent of involvement. Items that cannot be restored are documented for replacement value — that documentation goes directly to your adjuster.

Storage is climate-controlled and secured. Items remain inventoried and accessible throughout the restoration process, and are returned only when the structure itself has been cleared for reoccupancy.

## Folsom Insurance Coordination for Contents Claims

Contents claims are often the most disputed portion of a property loss because adjusters and policyholders frequently disagree on whether an item is restorable or a total loss. Our documentation process — room-by-room photography, itemized condition reports, and written cleaning assessments — is built to support your claim, not just our workflow. We communicate directly with your carrier and can provide line-item estimates in formats most major insurers accept. If your policy includes Additional Living Expenses (ALE) coverage, we can also help coordinate temporary storage costs as part of the overall claim.
