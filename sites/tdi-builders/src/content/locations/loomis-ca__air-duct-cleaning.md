Loomis sits in the Sierra Nevada foothills at an elevation where summer heat routinely pushes past 100°F and winter valley fog rolls in from the Sacramento basin — a combination that cycles your HVAC system hard year-round. That constant on-off demand pulls dust, pollen, and the fine particulate that blows off the surrounding oak woodland and horse properties straight into your ductwork. Over time, those deposits don't just reduce airflow; they bake onto duct walls during summer cooling runs and release a stale, musty odor the moment you switch back to heat in October.

## Why Loomis Properties See Duct Buildup Faster Than Valley Homes

The foothills climate is drier than coastal California but wetter than the Central Valley, and that swing matters inside your ducts. Loomis properties — many of them built on larger parcels with detached garages, barn structures, or converted outbuildings — often run longer duct runs than a comparable suburban tract home. Longer runs mean more surface area for dust to settle, and more joints where unconditioned attic air can infiltrate if the duct sealing has aged.

The surrounding landscape adds its own load. Oak pollen season in the foothills is aggressive in spring, and the unpaved driveways and horse pastures common across the rural parcels west of town push fine mineral dust into outdoor air intakes at levels a standard filter isn't designed to handle alone. If your home sits on a property with any exposed soil or gravel around the foundation, that dust finds its way in.

Older homes in Loomis — particularly those built in the 1970s and 1980s during the area's first wave of rural residential development — frequently have original fiberglass duct board or flex duct that has never been professionally cleaned. That material holds debris differently than modern sheet metal, and it requires a lower-pressure cleaning approach to avoid liner damage.

## Our Air Duct Cleaning Process in Loomis

The process starts with a visual inspection of every accessible register, return, and main trunk line before any equipment runs. In foothills homes, that inspection often turns up pest intrusion debris — insect casings, rodent material, or nesting fragments that have worked into the duct system from the attic or crawlspace. Knowing what's in the ducts before cleaning determines the right method and whether sanitization is warranted.

Cleaning uses a truck-mounted negative air system that puts the entire duct network under continuous negative pressure while rotating brush heads dislodge debris from duct walls. Every register is sealed during the process so loosened material travels toward the vacuum collection unit rather than back into your living space. Supply and return sides are cleaned separately, which matters in the longer duct runs typical of Loomis properties.

When debris levels or visible biological growth warrant it, we follow mechanical cleaning with an EPA-registered duct sanitizer applied as a fine mist — not a fogger that leaves residue on surfaces outside the duct. Dryer vent cleaning is handled as a separate line item and uses a rotary brush sized to the vent diameter; the foothills fire risk makes lint-clogged dryer vents a more serious concern here than in many other parts of Placer County.
