## Why Lincoln Properties See Sewage Backup Issues

Lincoln sits in a transitional zone where older, established neighborhoods with aging clay or cast-iron lateral lines sit alongside master-planned communities built on expansive clay soils — and both conditions create distinct sewage risk profiles.

In the city's earlier residential pockets, sewer laterals laid decades ago are increasingly vulnerable to root intrusion from mature trees and to joint separation caused by ground movement. Placer County's hot, dry summers followed by heavy winter rains cause the clay-heavy soil to shrink and swell seasonally, which stresses buried pipe joints over time. A lateral that holds through a dry October can fracture or offset just enough during the first significant December storm to allow a full blockage.

In newer master-planned developments, the problem often isn't the pipes — it's volume. When a large tract of homes all drain into a shared lift station or a recently commissioned sewer main, a single pump failure or grease-line blockage can push sewage back through the lowest fixtures in multiple homes simultaneously. Ground-floor bathrooms and finished basements are the first to flood.

Septic systems are also still in use on larger parcels at Lincoln's rural edges. A saturated drain field after a wet winter can cause the tank to back up into the home before the homeowner realizes the field has failed.

## Our Sewage Cleanup and Sanitization Process in Lincoln

Sewage cleanup is not the same as water damage cleanup. The contamination classification changes the entire protocol.

**Containment first.** Before any removal begins, the affected area is isolated to prevent cross-contamination to unaffected rooms. HEPA-filtered negative air machines are set up to capture airborne particulates while work is underway.

**Extraction and removal.** Standing sewage is extracted with truck-mounted or portable units rated for Category 3 water. Porous materials — carpet, pad, drywall below the flood line, insulation — that have contacted raw sewage are removed and bagged for disposal per Placer County waste handling guidelines. There is no safe way to dry and restore materials that have absorbed black water contamination.

**Structural cleaning and sanitization.** Exposed framing, concrete slabs, and hard surfaces are cleaned with EPA-registered antimicrobial solutions. In homes with crawl spaces — common in Lincoln's older neighborhoods — the crawl space is inspected and treated separately, since sewage can migrate under the structure through soil and vapor.

**Drying and verification.** Once contaminated materials are out and surfaces are sanitized, commercial drying equipment is deployed. Moisture readings are logged daily and drying is not closed out until structural readings return to acceptable baselines per the IICRC S500 standard.
