## Why Citrus Heights Properties See Storm Damage Differently

Citrus Heights developed rapidly through the 1950s, 1960s, and 1970s, and a large share of the housing stock reflects that era: wood-frame construction with composition shingle roofs, single-pane windows, and older attic ventilation systems that were never designed to shed the kind of sustained rainfall an atmospheric river delivers. Roof decking from that period is often thinner than current code requires, which means a single large branch — or even a sustained wind load — can crack or puncture it without leaving obvious exterior evidence. Water then travels along rafters and wall cavities before it ever shows up as a ceiling stain.

The soil throughout much of Citrus Heights also presents a challenge. The clay-heavy subsoil common to this part of Sacramento County absorbs moisture slowly and holds it longer than sandy or loamy ground. After a heavy storm, that saturation can push hydrostatic pressure against slab foundations and crawl space walls, forcing water in through gaps that were dry all summer. Homeowners who have never had a water intrusion problem can find standing water in a crawl space within 24 hours of a major rain event.

## Our Storm Damage Restoration Process in Citrus Heights

When a call comes in, the first priority is a thorough damage assessment — not just the obvious breach point, but the full moisture map. Technicians use thermal imaging cameras and calibrated moisture meters to trace where water has traveled inside wall assemblies and subfloor systems. In older Citrus Heights homes with plaster-over-lath construction, this step is especially important: the plaster face can read nearly dry while the lath and framing behind it hold significant moisture.

Once the scope is documented, the process moves in sequence:

1. **Emergency tarping and board-up** — securing the envelope against additional weather intrusion before any interior work begins.
2. **Water extraction** — removing standing water from affected rooms, crawl spaces, and garages using truck-mounted and portable extraction equipment.
3. **Structural drying** — deploying commercial-grade desiccant and refrigerant dehumidifiers alongside high-velocity air movers, with drying logs maintained throughout.
4. **Debris removal and damage documentation** — cataloging affected materials with photos and moisture readings for the insurance claim file.
5. **Reconstruction** — repairing or replacing roofing, siding, framing, drywall, insulation, and finish materials to pre-loss condition.

With 40+ years of construction and restoration experience, the team understands that drying targets and rebuild specifications are not interchangeable from job to job — a 1962 ranch home in Citrus Heights needs a different approach than a 2010 townhouse.
