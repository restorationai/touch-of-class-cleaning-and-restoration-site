## Why Granite Bay Properties Face Elevated Large Loss Risk

The housing stock along the Granite Bay corridor skews toward large custom homes — many built in the late 1990s through the 2000s boom — with complex roof lines, multi-zone HVAC systems, and finished basements or bonus rooms that sit partially below grade. When a catastrophic water loss occurs in a home like this, water doesn't travel in predictable straight lines. It follows framing cavities, pools behind engineered hardwood, and saturates spray-foam insulation in ways that a moisture meter at the surface won't detect. Fire losses in this area carry an added layer of complexity: the dry oak woodland surrounding many neighborhoods means that wildfire smoke and ash can infiltrate attic spaces and ductwork even when the structure itself isn't directly involved in a burn.

Placer County's proximity to Folsom Lake also means that during heavy rainfall years, drainage patterns across the rolling terrain can overwhelm residential drainage systems quickly, sending water into crawl spaces and garages faster than sump pumps can manage. Large loss response in this environment requires industrial-grade extraction, thermal imaging to locate hidden saturation, and a project management structure that can coordinate multiple trade crews simultaneously.

## Our Large Loss and Catastrophic Response Process in Granite Bay

Catastrophic losses require a different operational model than a standard water or fire claim. When TDI Builders responds to a large loss in Granite Bay, the first step is a structured scope assessment — not a quick walkthrough, but a systematic documentation process that captures every affected system: structural, mechanical, electrical, and finish. IICRC-certified technicians use thermal imaging, moisture mapping, and air quality sampling to establish a baseline that holds up to carrier scrutiny.

From there, the project moves into simultaneous workstreams. Drying and dehumidification equipment is deployed at scale — commercial desiccant dehumidifiers and high-CFM air movers calibrated to the square footage and construction type of the specific structure. Structural drying in a 5,000-square-foot custom home with concrete tile roofing and stucco cladding behaves differently than drying a wood-frame production home, and the drying plan reflects that. Reconstruction planning begins in parallel so that material lead times, permit applications with Placer County, and subcontractor scheduling don't create gaps that extend the project timeline unnecessarily.

## Local Note

Granite Bay's custom homes frequently feature tile or slate roofing over skip-sheathing rather than solid plywood decking — a construction method common in California's foothill climate zones that allows for ventilation but creates a more complex water intrusion path when a roof is compromised. Scoping a large loss accurately in this construction type requires opening the ceiling in multiple locations rather than relying on the obvious damage point alone.
