Bathroom remodeling in McClellan Park comes with a wrinkle most Sacramento contractors never think about: a large share of the housing stock here started life as military family housing when McClellan Air Force Base was still active, then got converted and resold as the base redeveloped into a business park and residential community. That history shows up in the bathrooms first, in old pipe runs, undersized vent stacks, and tile jobs that were never meant to last seventy years.

## Why McClellan Park Bathrooms Show Their Age

Homes and buildings tied to the former base conversion often share a construction era and a builder's shorthand: slab-on-grade foundations, galvanized or early copper supply lines, and cast iron drain stacks that have been in the ground since before most current owners were born. When we open a wall in one of these bathrooms we typically find plumbing that was adequate for 1950s fixture counts but struggles with modern dual-flush toilets, rain showerheads, or a second sink. Sacramento Valley's clay-heavy soil adds its own pressure, swelling and contracting with the region's wet winters and bone-dry summers, which over decades can shift slab foundations just enough to stress tile and grout lines in older bathrooms. None of this means a remodel has to be a demolition project, but it does mean we plan differently for a McClellan Park bath than we would for a newer Sacramento subdivision home.

## Our Bathroom Remodeling Process in McClellan Park

We start with a walkthrough that includes a look at what's actually behind the tile, not just what the finishes suggest. For slab-foundation homes that's often a camera check on the drain line before we commit to a layout, since moving a toilet or shower drain on a slab is a different cost conversation than it is on a raised foundation. From there we handle design, material selection, and the permit process through Sacramento County, scheduling demolition, rough plumbing and electrical, waterproofing, tile or surround installation, and final fixtures and trim. Tub-to-shower conversions and walk-in shower builds are common requests here, partly because older tubs in base-era homes were sized for a different era of fixtures and partly because aging owners are planning ahead for accessibility. We sequence the trades so the bathroom is out of commission for the shortest reasonable stretch, which matters in homes with only one full bath.

## Local note

One thing we check early on almost every McClellan Park bathroom job: whether the home still has its original cast iron drain stack. These were standard in base-era construction and they're durable, but after seventy-plus years they can develop scale buildup or hairline cracks that won't show up until a remodel opens the wall and the new fixtures put more water through the line. Catching it during demo instead of after tile is set saves a homeowner from redoing finish work twice.
