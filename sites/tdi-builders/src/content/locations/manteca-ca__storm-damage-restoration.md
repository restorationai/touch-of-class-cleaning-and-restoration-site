When those systems roll through San Joaquin County, they can drop several inches of rain in under 48 hours, push sustained winds that strip roofing materials, and leave standing water against foundations that were never designed to shed that kind of volume. TDI USA, Inc. has been handling storm damage restoration since 1985, and the pattern of loss in communities like Manteca is specific enough to warrant a different approach than what works in the foothills or the coast.

## Why Manteca Properties See Concentrated Storm Damage

Manteca's growth has happened in waves. Older neighborhoods closer to the downtown core carry housing stock from the mid-20th century, when roof decking was often 1x6 skip sheathing rather than plywood, and fascia boards were undersized by today's standards. When a line of storms pushes through, those older assemblies can fail at the connection points first: lifted shingles, separated drip edges, and water intrusion behind the siding before the storm has even passed.

Newer subdivisions on the city's expanding edges bring their own vulnerabilities. Stucco-over-foam cladding systems, common in Central Valley tract construction from the 1990s onward, can develop hidden moisture pathways when wind-driven rain finds a crack at a window corner or a penetration that wasn't properly flashed. The damage reads dry from the outside and wet from the inside, and it often isn't discovered until mold has already started colonizing the wall cavity.

The valley's clay-heavy soils also play a role. When the ground is already saturated from an early-season storm, subsequent rain has nowhere to go. Perimeter drainage systems that function fine in a normal year can back up against foundations, sending water into crawl spaces and slab edges.

## Our Storm Damage Restoration Process in Manteca

Every storm damage job starts with a structured assessment before any work order is written. That means walking the roof, probing the decking, inspecting the attic for daylight or moisture staining, and using moisture meters at the wall base and around every window and door opening. What looks like a shingle repair from the street is sometimes a full deck replacement once the layers come off.

From there, the work is sequenced to protect the structure first: emergency tarping or board-up to stop active water entry, then controlled demolition of compromised materials, drying of any wet assemblies, and finally the rebuild. For siding storm damage repair, that means matching the existing cladding profile as closely as possible, which on older Manteca homes can require sourcing materials that are no longer stocked at regional suppliers.

Wind damage repair on commercial properties follows a similar sequence but adds a coordination layer for tenant notification, ADA access maintenance during construction, and documentation that satisfies commercial property insurers.
