Citrus Heights sits in a stretch of Sacramento County where commercial corridors built in the 1970s and 1980s are actively turning over — strip centers are being subdivided, single-tenant retail boxes are being carved into multi-suite professional suites, and second-generation office parks are getting full gut renovations to attract modern tenants. That kind of work demands a general contractor who understands both the structural realities of that era's construction and the permit workflow through Sacramento County's planning and building departments, which govern most commercial projects in the city. TDI Builders has been doing exactly this work since 1985.

## Why Citrus Heights Commercial Properties Present Unique Build Challenges

Much of Citrus Heights's commercial building stock was constructed during a period when tilt-up concrete panels, single-pane storefronts, and drop-ceiling grid systems were standard. When a tenant improvement scope touches those original systems — upgrading electrical panels to meet current load demands, replacing HVAC equipment sized for a prior use, or opening up a ceiling plenum for modern ductwork — the work quickly moves beyond cosmetic renovation into structural and mechanical coordination.

The Sacramento Valley's climate adds another layer. Summer temperatures in Citrus Heights regularly push above 100°F, which means HVAC system sizing is not a back-of-napkin calculation. An office buildout that underspecifies cooling capacity will fail tenants within the first season. Conversely, the region's wet winters — with rain concentrated between November and March — require careful attention to exterior penetrations, storefront thresholds, and any rooftop mechanical work scheduled during the buildout phase. Sequencing matters here in ways it simply does not in more temperate markets.

## Our Commercial Construction and Tenant Improvement Process in Citrus Heights

Every project begins with a site walkthrough that goes beyond measuring square footage. We assess the existing electrical service, plumbing rough-in locations, structural grid, and any deferred maintenance that a landlord's shell condition may be obscuring. That information shapes the construction documents and, critically, the permit submittal package.

Permit timelines through Sacramento County's Department of Community Development can vary meaningfully depending on project complexity and current review queue depth. We build realistic permit lead times into every project schedule from day one — not as a contingency, but as a planned phase. Tenants and property owners who have worked with contractors who promise fast starts and then stall at plan check understand why this matters.

Once permits are in hand, our crews work in a sequenced trade order: demolition and rough framing, rough mechanical and electrical, inspections, insulation and drywall, finish trades, and final inspections. For occupied multi-tenant buildings — a common scenario in Citrus Heights's older retail centers — we coordinate work hours and staging to minimize disruption to neighboring tenants.

## Local Note

Citrus Heights's older commercial corridors — particularly properties along Sunrise Boulevard and Greenback Lane — frequently have electrical panels that were sized for the original tenant type and haven't been upgraded since. A restaurant converting to office use, or a large retail box being subdivided into smaller suites, will almost always require a panel upgrade or a new sub-panel installation. This is not a surprise item we surface mid-project: it's something we look for in the initial site assessment and price into the bid so the number you approve is the number you pay.
