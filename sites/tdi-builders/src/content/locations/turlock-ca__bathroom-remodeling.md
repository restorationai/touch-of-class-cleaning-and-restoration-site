## Why Turlock Homes Often Need More Than a Cosmetic Bathroom Update

The housing stock across much of Turlock reflects a specific era of California tract construction: slab-on-grade foundations, fiberglass tub surrounds that were standard in the 1970s and 1980s, and bathroom exhaust fans that vented into attic cavities rather than through the roof. That combination creates predictable problems. Grout cracks as the slab shifts slightly through wet winters and dry summers. Fiberglass surrounds develop stress fractures at the corners. Moisture that has no clear exit path collects in wall cavities behind tile, softening the substrate long before you see any surface staining.

A remodel that only addresses the visible surface, new tile over compromised backer board, a fresh coat of paint over a poorly vented space, will not hold up in this climate. The work has to go deeper, and that means inspecting what is behind the walls before anything new goes in.

## Local Note: Hard Water and Turlock Bathroom Fixtures

Turlock's municipal water supply draws from groundwater sources that carry a notably high mineral content. Homeowners who have lived here for more than a few years know the white calcium scale that builds up on showerheads and faucet aerators. What is less obvious is how that same mineral content affects grout over time: repeated wetting and drying with hard water accelerates efflorescence and can cause grout to become brittle faster than it would in areas with softer water. When we specify grout for Turlock projects, we favor epoxy-based formulations in wet zones specifically because they resist mineral penetration better than standard cement grout. It is a small material choice that makes a measurable difference in how long the finished bathroom holds up.
