## Why Citrus Heights Properties Face Elevated Large-Loss Risk

The Sacramento Valley's climate creates a narrow but punishing window for catastrophic loss events. Extended dry seasons leave wood-framed structures — common throughout Citrus Heights's post-war residential and commercial building stock — with low moisture content and high fire vulnerability. Then, when winter storms arrive, aging flat-roof commercial buildings and older residential properties with original plumbing can fail suddenly and dramatically. A single supply-line failure in a multi-tenant commercial strip can saturate multiple units within minutes; a structure fire in a densely built neighborhood can spread to adjacent buildings before the first engine arrives.

Citrus Heights also sits on expansive clay soils that shift seasonally with wet-dry cycles. That movement stresses foundations and utility penetrations over time, and when a pipe finally fails or a sewer lateral backs up, the damage path often follows hidden channels behind walls and under slabs — making the visible loss a fraction of the actual affected area.

## Our Large Loss and Catastrophic Response Process in Citrus Heights

Large-loss response is a different discipline than standard restoration. The first priority is life-safety stabilization: shoring compromised structural elements, boarding and tarping to prevent secondary weather intrusion, and isolating utilities. TDI Builders dispatches a senior project manager alongside the initial crew — not after the fact — so scope decisions are made on-site from the first hour.

From there, the process moves through systematic moisture mapping or fire-damage assessment across every affected zone, detailed photo and thermal documentation formatted for large-loss adjusters, and a phased scope of work that separates emergency mitigation from reconstruction. Our IICRC certified technicians apply drying and decontamination protocols calibrated to the actual materials present, whether that's the stucco-over-wood-frame construction typical of Citrus Heights commercial corridors or the slab-on-grade foundations common in the city's mid-century residential neighborhoods.
