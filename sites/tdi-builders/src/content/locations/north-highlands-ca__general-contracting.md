Renovation work in North Highlands almost always runs into the same obstacle before the first wall comes down: the house is older than the permit records make it look. Sacramento County handles permitting for this unincorporated stretch of the county, and a remodel plan that would sail through in a newer subdivision can stall here over a 1960s electrical panel or a foundation that was never engineered for the addition someone built in 1985. Renovations, remodels, and general contracting in North Highlands means planning for what's actually inside the walls, not just what's on the lot survey.

## Why North Highlands Properties See Renovation Complications

A large share of North Highlands housing stock dates to the postwar growth tied to the former McClellan Air Force Base, when tract builders put up modest single-story homes quickly and on tight budgets. That means slab-on-grade foundations, galvanized plumbing that's well past its service life, and electrical systems sized for a 1950s household, not a kitchen remodel with a double oven and an EV charger. Add the Sacramento Valley's clay-heavy soil, which expands and contracts hard between the wet winters and the dry 100-degree summers, and you get slab cracking and door-frame shifting that shows up mid-project if it wasn't accounted for at the estimate stage. A general contractor working here has to budget time for discovery, not just demolition.

## Our Renovations, Remodels and General Contracting Process in North Highlands

We start with a walkthrough that assumes the house has surprises, because in this part of the county it usually does. That means checking panel capacity before we finalize electrical scope, probing slab condition before flooring selections are locked in, and pulling the county permit history so we know what's actually been inspected versus what was done without a sign-off. From there we sequence the work: structural and systems first, then envelope and finishes, with county inspections scheduled at each hold point rather than bunched at the end. Design-build clients get drawings and material selections finalized before a crew mobilizes, which keeps the Sacramento County permit review from becoming the bottleneck it often is on reactive, change-order-heavy projects.

## North Highlands Permitting and Inspection Coordination

Because North Highlands is unincorporated, every structural, electrical, and plumbing permit routes through Sacramento County rather than a city building department, and the review queue moves on county timelines, not city ones. We handle the submittal package, respond to plan check comments, and schedule the rough and final inspections so homeowners aren't the ones chasing down an inspector's callback. On additions or room conversions, we also flag early whether the parcel's setback and lot coverage will support the scope, since some of the older North Highlands lots were platted before current county zoning minimums and an addition that looks fine on paper can trigger a variance conversation nobody budgeted for.

## Local note

One pattern we watch for specifically in North Highlands: homes built on the original postwar slabs often have plumbing runs that were never re-routed when later additions went on, so a kitchen or bath remodel can uncover a drain line running under a room that's now someone's bedroom. We scope a camera inspection of the under-slab plumbing before finalizing layout changes on any home from that era, because moving a wall is cheap compared to discovering mid-project that the water line has to come up through a finished floor.
