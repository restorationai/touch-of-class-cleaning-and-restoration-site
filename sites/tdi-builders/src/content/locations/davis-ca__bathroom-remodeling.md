Bathroom remodeling in Davis runs into a problem most homeowners don't expect until demo day: the house was built on a slab, and the plumbing underneath has been sitting in Central Valley clay for fifty-plus years. Add Davis's famously hard, mineral-heavy groundwater and you get corroded supply lines, scaled-up valves, and grout lines that fail years before they should. A remodel here isn't just picking tile, it's planning around what's actually running through the walls and floor.

## Why Davis Bathrooms Show Their Age

Much of Davis's housing stock went up during the UC Davis growth decades of the 1950s through 1970s, and a large share of it sits on concrete slab foundations rather than raised crawlspaces. That means original bathroom plumbing is often embedded in the slab itself, which makes a simple fixture swap riskier than it looks and makes full re-piping during a remodel the smarter long-term move rather than working around old galvanized or cast iron lines. Layer on Davis's groundwater, which has historically run high in dissolved minerals, and you'll often find shower valves, drain assemblies, and even old cast-iron tubs more corroded than their age alone would suggest. Hot, dry Central Valley summers also mean grout and caulk take a beating from UV exposure near west-facing bathroom windows, which is why we see premature cracking in homes that otherwise look well maintained.

## Our Bathroom Remodeling Process in Davis

We start with a walk-through that includes checking the foundation type, because a slab-on-grade bathroom changes how we approach moving a toilet flange or relocating a shower drain, sometimes requiring slab cutting and patching that wouldn't come up in a raised-foundation home. From there we handle permitting through the City of Davis building division, which is a required step any time plumbing or electrical gets altered during a remodel, not just a formality to skip. For a tub-to-shower conversion or a full master bath gut, we rough in new supply and drain lines rated for the local water chemistry, set waterproofing membranes before tile goes down, and sequence inspections so the job doesn't stall waiting on sign-off. Framing, electrical, and finish carpentry are handled by the same crew start to finish, which keeps a bathroom remodel from turning into the kind of multi-contractor project where nobody owns the mistakes.

## Local note

If your Davis home still has its original tub or shower valve, don't be surprised when we find more mineral buildup inside the cartridge than you'd see in a similar-age house in the Bay Area. Davis's water supply has historically carried more dissolved minerals than nearby cities, which shortens the working life of standard washerless valves and accelerates hard-water staining on glass shower enclosures. We factor that into fixture selection, often steering toward valve bodies and finishes built to tolerate mineral content rather than the builder-grade parts that came standard in the original construction.
