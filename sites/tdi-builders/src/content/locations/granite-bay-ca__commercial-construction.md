## Why Granite Bay's Commercial Market Has Its Own Rhythm

Placer County's planning and building departments operate on timelines and submittal requirements that differ meaningfully from Sacramento County. Commercial projects in Granite Bay fall under Placer County jurisdiction, which means plan check cycles, fire-marshal coordination, and grading reviews all route through Auburn, not Sacramento. For a tenant improvement in a multi-tenant professional building, that distinction affects your schedule from the first day of design. Contractors who only work the Sacramento city core sometimes discover this mid-project.

The area's commercial inventory skews toward Class B and Class C office product built in the late 1980s through early 2000s, the same growth wave that filled out the Douglas Boulevard corridor and the professional park clusters near Eureka Road. Buildings from that era often have HVAC systems sized for original floor plans, electrical panels that predate modern data and AV loads, and drop-ceiling grids that complicate sprinkler modifications. A thorough pre-construction walkthrough catches those conditions before they become change orders.

Granite Bay's soil profile, decomposed granite over clay hardpan in many areas, also influences foundation work and underground utility routing on ground-up commercial projects. Drainage design has to account for the way water moves through that layered profile, particularly on sloped parcels that are common in this part of Placer County.

## Our Commercial Construction and Tenant Improvements Process in Granite Bay

Every project starts with a scope-of-work meeting on site. For ground-up commercial builds, it means reviewing the parcel's entitlements, utility service points, and any conditions of approval attached to the project.

From there, the process moves through design coordination, permit submittal, subcontractor scheduling, and phased construction, with regular communication so tenants, landlords, and lenders stay informed. TDI USA is licensed and insured for commercial, industrial, and residential work, which matters when a project involves mixed occupancy or a building that straddles use categories.

For tenant improvements specifically, we work to minimize disruption to adjacent suites. In occupied multi-tenant buildings, that means scheduling noisy demolition during agreed windows, maintaining dust barriers that meet fire-code requirements, and coordinating temporary utility shutoffs with the property manager rather than surprising neighboring tenants.
