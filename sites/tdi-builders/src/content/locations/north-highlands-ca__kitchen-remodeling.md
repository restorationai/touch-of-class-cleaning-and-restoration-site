North Highlands kitchens tend to fall into one of two camps: the original 1950s and '60s tract layout with a closed-off galley kitchen, or a later addition that opened things up but never quite solved the ventilation and electrical load for modern appliances. Either way, a kitchen remodel here has to account for the slab foundations and mid-century framing that are common across this part of Sacramento County, not just swap in new cabinets and call it done.

## Why North Highlands Kitchens Need a Different Approach

Much of the housing stock in North Highlands went up during the postwar growth years, when Sacramento Valley builders favored slab-on-grade foundations and compact kitchen footprints built around a single exterior wall for plumbing and venting. That means moving a sink or range almost always means rerouting drain lines through a slab rather than an accessible crawlspace, which changes both the cost and the sequencing of a remodel. The long, dry Central Valley summers also take a toll on original wood cabinetry and trim: decades of heat cycling leave doors warped and frames shrunk away from the wall, which is often the first thing we find once demo starts, even on kitchens that looked fine from the outside.

## Our Kitchen Remodeling Process in North Highlands

We start with an on-site walkthrough to confirm what's behind the walls before we price anything, because slab plumbing and older electrical panels are the two things that most often turn a straightforward kitchen remodel into a bigger job. From there we handle demo, rough framing changes, electrical and plumbing coordination, cabinet installation, countertop templating, and finish work as a single sequenced project rather than handing it off between separate trades. For homes still running on a 1970s-era panel, we flag early whether the new appliance load will need a service upgrade, since that's a common surprise in this area once a kitchen goes from one outlet circuit to several dedicated ones. Custom cabinet installation is built to the actual wall, not a showroom template, which matters in houses where decades of settling have left corners slightly out of square.

## Permits and Sacramento County Coordination

Kitchen remodels that involve moving plumbing, adding electrical circuits, or altering load-bearing walls require permitting through the county, and inspection scheduling can add time to a project if it isn't planned for up front. We pull the permits, coordinate inspection windows around our own work schedule, and keep the paperwork organized so a homeowner isn't the one chasing down a reschedule. On older North Highlands homes, this step also tends to surface outdated plumbing or wiring that technically needs to be brought up to current code as part of the remodel, something worth knowing before a budget is finalized rather than after walls are already open.

## Local note

A detail that catches a lot of homeowners off guard: because so many North Highlands kitchens were built against a single plumbing wall, relocating an island sink or a wet bar almost always means breaking into the slab, and Sacramento County inspectors want to see that trenching done to code before it's covered back up. We plan for that inspection window in the schedule from day one instead of treating it as a delay once it happens, which keeps a kitchen remodel moving instead of stalling mid-project.
