Rosemont's kitchens tell the story of when the community was built: a wave of postwar ranch and split-level homes that still define much of Sacramento County's residential footprint. If your kitchen still has the original cabinet boxes, laminate counters, or a galley layout designed around a 1960s appliance lineup, you're not alone, and a remodel here usually means working around that original bone structure rather than against it. TDI Builders has been doing exactly that kind of work since 1985.

## Why Rosemont Kitchens Need a Different Approach

Much of Rosemont's housing stock predates open-concept living by decades. Load-bearing walls between kitchen and dining room were standard, which means a "simple" cabinet swap often turns into a structural conversation the moment a homeowner asks for a bigger island or a wall removed. Sacramento County's Central Valley climate adds its own wrinkle: long, dry summers followed by wet winter stretches put real stress on older wood cabinetry and subfloor, especially in kitchens that back up to original single-pane windows or uninsulated exterior walls. We see swelling at cabinet toe-kicks and warped lower drawer boxes more often in these homes than in newer Sacramento-area construction, which changes what we spec for materials before a single cabinet comes out.

## Our Kitchen Remodeling Process in Rosemont

We start with an on-site walkthrough that treats the house's age as a variable, not an afterthought: checking whether walls are load-bearing, what's actually behind the drywall for electrical and plumbing, and whether the slab or crawlspace foundation common to this part of the county will affect cabinet leveling. From there we move through demo, rough-in for any relocated plumbing or electrical, cabinet and counter installation, and finish work, with inspections scheduled around Sacramento County's permitting timelines rather than assumed. For a full renovation, that sequence typically runs several weeks; a cabinet-and-counter refresh without structural changes moves faster. We walk every homeowner through which phase they're in and what's next, because an open timeline is the number one source of remodel anxiety.

## Reaching Rosemont from Our Sacramento Base

Operating out of Sacramento puts us a short drive from Rosemont for initial consultations, material deliveries, and the multiple site visits a kitchen remodel requires between demo and final walkthrough. That proximity matters more for remodeling than people expect: cabinet and countertop installs often need a second or third site visit for templating, delivery, and punch-list items, and a contractor based across the region adds scheduling friction every time. Being close means we can adjust for weather delays or permit holds without losing weeks on travel logistics.

## HOA and Permit Coordination

Several Rosemont neighborhoods fall under homeowners associations with architectural review requirements, particularly for anything visible from the street or affecting shared structural elements in attached or clustered housing. Even for interior kitchen work, a load-bearing wall removal or window relocation typically needs a county building permit, and HOA sign-off can run on its own separate timeline from the permit office. We handle the paperwork for both where applicable, submitting drawings and specs so homeowners aren't the ones chasing approvals mid-project.

## Local note

One detail that catches first-time remodelers off guard: a lot of Rosemont's original kitchens were plumbed with galvanized supply lines that are now decades past their practical service life. When we open a wall for a sink relocation or island plumbing, we routinely find corrosion that wasn't visible from inside the cabinet, which means the plumbing scope on an "aesthetic" remodel sometimes grows once we're inside the wall. We flag this possibility during the estimate phase so it's not a surprise invoice mid-project.
