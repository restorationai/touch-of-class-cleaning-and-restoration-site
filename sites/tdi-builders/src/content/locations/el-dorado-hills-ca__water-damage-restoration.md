El Dorado Hills sits in the Sierra Nevada foothills where the transition from valley heat to foothill elevation creates its own moisture dynamic — summer thunderstorms roll in fast, clay-heavy soils shed water rather than absorb it, and the hillside grading that makes the views so appealing can funnel runoff straight toward a foundation or garage. When a pipe bursts, a roof seam fails during a storm, or a water heater lets go, the damage moves quickly. TDI Builders, Inc. has been responding to exactly these situations since 1985, with IICRC certified technicians available around the clock.

## Why El Dorado Hills Properties See Water Damage Issues

The foothill terrain here does things to water that flat Sacramento neighborhoods simply don't experience. Lots are graded for drainage, but when clay soil becomes saturated — common during the wet season between November and March — it loses permeability fast. Water that has nowhere to go finds the path of least resistance: window wells, crawl space vents, garage slabs, and the gap between a stem wall and the mudsill.

The housing stock adds another layer. El Dorado Hills grew substantially through the 1980s and 1990s, and homes from that era commonly feature slab-on-grade construction with copper supply lines that have now been in service for 30-plus years. Pinhole leaks in copper are slow and silent — they can saturate the subfloor insulation beneath a slab for weeks before a homeowner notices the floor feels soft or a water bill spikes. Homes built in later phases of the community, particularly those in planned developments with shared landscaping, can also see irrigation system failures that saturate exterior walls from grade level up.

## Our Water Damage Restoration Process in El Dorado Hills

The first step on any water loss is stopping the spread. Our technicians locate the source, isolate it, and begin extracting standing water using truck-mounted and portable extraction units capable of pulling water from carpet, hardwood, and concrete surfaces. In foothill homes where crawl spaces are common, we deploy equipment into the crawl to remove pooled water and assess vapor barrier integrity before it becomes a mold concern.

After extraction, the work shifts to structural drying. We place industrial air movers and desiccant or refrigerant dehumidifiers in a calculated pattern based on the square footage, material types, and ambient conditions. El Dorado Hills summers are hot and dry, which actually accelerates evaporation — but that same dry heat can mask residual moisture inside wall cavities and under flooring. We use thermal imaging and calibrated moisture meters to track drying progress daily, not just by feel. Drying is documented against the IICRC S500 standard so your insurance adjuster has a clear record.

Once structural moisture readings return to baseline, we assess what can be saved and what needs to go. Wet drywall, saturated insulation, and compromised flooring are removed, bagged, and disposed of properly. Framing and sheathing are treated and dried in place when salvageable. The rebuild phase — the part many restoration companies hand off to a separate contractor — stays with our crew because TDI Builders is both a licensed restoration and construction firm.

## Local Note

Something worth knowing about foothill properties in this part of El Dorado County: homes built on cut-and-fill lots — where part of the pad is native soil and part is compacted fill — can develop differential settlement over time. That settlement creates small but persistent gaps at foundation transitions, exactly where water finds entry during heavy rain. If your water intrusion appears only during storms and only in one corner of the house, the issue may be a grade-level gap rather than a plumbing failure. We inspect for both before assuming the source, because treating the wrong entry point means the problem returns next season.
