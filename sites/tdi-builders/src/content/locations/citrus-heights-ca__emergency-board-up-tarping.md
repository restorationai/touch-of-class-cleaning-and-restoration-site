## Why Citrus Heights Properties Face Distinctive Board-Up and Tarping Needs

Citrus Heights developed rapidly through the 1950s, 60s, and 70s, leaving the city with a large share of tract homes built on slab foundations with wood-frame construction and composition shingle roofing. That roofing stock ages, and when a fire, fallen tree limb, or severe wind event breaches it, the underlayment beneath often has little remaining integrity to resist even a single night of rain. Sacramento County's wet season — roughly November through March — means a roof opening left unprotected even briefly can allow water intrusion that turns a fire or impact claim into a secondary water damage claim as well.

The city's street grid also includes a mix of older residential blocks with mature trees, and those trees are a recurring factor in storm-related damage. A single atmospheric river event can bring enough wind and saturated soil to topple trees onto rooflines, punching through sheathing and leaving an opening that needs tarping before the next rain band arrives. For commercial properties along the Sunrise Boulevard and Greenback Lane corridors, broken storefront glazing from accidents or vandalism creates both a security and a liability exposure that calls for same-day board-up.

## Our Emergency Board-Up and Tarping Process in Citrus Heights

When you call, a crew is dispatched immediately — 24 hours a day, seven days a week. The first step on arrival is a quick structural walk to identify all breach points: broken windows, compromised doors, roof openings, and any sections of wall or soffit that are structurally unsound. We photograph every opening before we cover it, because that documentation becomes part of your insurance claim file.

For window and door board-up, we use exterior-grade plywood cut and fitted to the opening, secured with screws rather than nails so the panels can be removed cleanly when permanent repairs begin. For roof tarping, we use heavy-duty polyethylene tarps — minimum 6-mil, typically heavier for larger spans — weighted and secured with lumber battens and roofing screws driven into sound decking, not just draped over the peak. A tarp that lifts in the next windstorm is not a secured property; the attachment method matters as much as the material.

If the damage involves fire residue or hazardous debris near the opening, we work in coordination with your adjuster's timeline so that evidence is photographed and documented before any debris is moved to access the work area.

Emergency board-up and tarping is almost universally covered as a mitigation expense under homeowners and commercial property policies — it is the step that prevents a covered loss from becoming a larger one. ## Local Note

One pattern worth knowing about Citrus Heights specifically: a number of the city's 1960s-era homes were built with low-slope or nearly flat rear porch roof extensions added to original construction. These secondary roof sections are often the first to fail in a wind or impact event, and their shallow pitch means a standard ridge-anchored tarp won't shed water effectively. When we encounter these configurations, we frame the tarp with additional lumber to create positive drainage slope rather than simply laying it flat — otherwise pooling water adds load and can worsen the structural situation before permanent repairs are made.
