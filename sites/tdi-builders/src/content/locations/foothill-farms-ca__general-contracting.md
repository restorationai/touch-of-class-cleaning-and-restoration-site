Foothill Farms grew up fast in the 1950s and 60s as Sacramento County filled in its unincorporated edges with affordable ranch-style tract housing, and that building boom is exactly why renovation work here looks different than it does in newer subdivisions. Original slab foundations, single-pane windows, and galvanized plumbing are still common under these roofs, which means a kitchen remodel or room addition often uncovers structural or electrical surprises that a general contractor needs to plan for before the first wall comes down.

## Why Foothill Farms Homes Need Thoughtful Renovation Planning

Most of the housing stock in Foothill Farms predates 1970, and a lot of it was built to the code standards of that era, not today's. Slab-on-grade foundations are common, which limits where plumbing can be rerouted without trenching concrete. Many homes still carry original aluminum or early copper wiring that wasn't sized for modern appliance loads, and attic insulation is frequently thin or absent given how the Sacramento Valley's summer heat and winter fog cycles stress a roofline over decades. Any serious remodel, whether it's a kitchen, bathroom, or full addition, needs to account for what's actually behind the walls rather than what the original blueprints assumed.
