## Why McClellan Park Properties See Water Damage Issues

McClellan Park's redevelopment from a decommissioned Air Force base into a mixed commercial, industrial, and residential district means the building stock isn't uniform. Some structures started life as military facilities with concrete slab construction and older plumbing runs that were never designed for today's tenant improvements. Others are newer builds that still sit on the same flat valley terrain and clay-heavy soil common across this part of Sacramento County. That soil doesn't drain quickly, so when winter storms roll through the Central Valley, water that can't absorb fast enough backs up against foundations, finds gaps in slab penetrations, and works its way into wall cavities from the bottom up rather than the top down. Add a burst supply line or a failed water heater inside one of the area's repurposed buildings, and the damage pattern is often wider and slower to show than homeowners expect.

## Our Water Damage Restoration Process in McClellan Park

We start with moisture mapping, not guesswork. A moisture meter and thermal camera tell us where water traveled, which matters more in a converted commercial structure where a leak on one side of a demising wall can migrate well past where the visible stain stops. Extraction comes next, followed by drying equipment set to the specific material: drywall and ceiling water damage repair calls for different airflow and dehumidification targets than concrete or exposed block construction common in the base-era buildings. Once materials test dry per the IICRC S500 standard, we move into reconstruction, which on this campus often means matching older construction details to code-compliant repairs rather than a simple drywall patch.
