## Why Roseville Properties Face Sewage Backup Risks

Roseville's growth has been rapid and layered. Neighborhoods built in the 1970s and 1980s sit alongside master-planned communities from the 2000s and newer infill development — and the underground plumbing tells that whole story. In older sections of the city, cast-iron and Orangeburg sewer laterals have reached or exceeded their service life. Orangeburg pipe, a compressed-fiber product common in postwar construction, softens and deforms over decades, creating the low spots and partial collapses where grease, roots, and solids accumulate until a backup forces the issue.

The regional climate adds pressure. Roseville receives most of its annual rainfall between November and March, and the clay-dominant soils that characterize much of Placer County absorb water slowly. During heavy storms, groundwater infiltration into aging sewer lines increases dramatically, surcharging the system and pushing sewage back through the lowest fixture in the house — typically a basement floor drain, ground-floor toilet, or utility sink. Septic systems on larger parcels at the city's edges face a related problem: saturated soil loses its ability to absorb effluent, and a system that functioned fine in summer can overflow in January.

## Our Sewage Cleanup and Sanitization Process in Roseville

Raw sewage is classified as Category 3 water — the most contaminated category under IICRC standards — which means the response protocol is more involved than a standard water loss. The process begins with containment: sealing off the affected area to prevent cross-contamination to clean portions of the home, and establishing negative air pressure where the loss is enclosed. Technicians document the extent of contamination with moisture mapping and photography before any material is moved.

Porous materials that have absorbed sewage — drywall, insulation, carpet, subflooring — are removed and bagged for disposal rather than dried in place. No extraction equipment reverses Category 3 contamination in porous substrates. Once affected materials are out, all hard surfaces receive an EPA-registered disinfectant application, followed by a dwell period long enough for the chemistry to work. A second pass with a different disinfectant class addresses cross-resistance concerns. Structural drying of the remaining framing and subfloor assembly follows, monitored with moisture meters until readings return to regional equilibrium levels appropriate for Roseville's dry-summer climate.

Because Roseville sits within Placer County's jurisdiction for waste disposal, bagged sewage-contaminated materials must be handled and transported in compliance with county requirements. Our team coordinates that disposal step so property owners are not left managing hazardous waste logistics on their own.
