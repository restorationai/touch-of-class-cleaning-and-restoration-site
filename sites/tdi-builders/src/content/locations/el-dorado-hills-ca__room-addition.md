El Dorado Hills sits on the western slope of the Sierra Nevada foothills, where long, dry summers, clay-heavy soils, and a housing stock that expanded rapidly through the 1990s and 2000s create a particular set of conditions for homeowners thinking about adding space. Whether you need a ground-floor family room bump-out, a second-story primary suite, or a full in-law addition to accommodate multigenerational living, TDI Builders, Inc. has been navigating the planning, permitting, and construction side of home additions in the Sacramento region since 1985.

## Why El Dorado Hills Homes Are Well-Suited — and Sometimes Tricky — for Additions

Most of the residential development in El Dorado Hills happened in waves: early master-planned communities in the late 1980s and early 1990s, then a second surge through the mid-2000s. That means the majority of homes here are single-story or story-and-a-half tract builds on slab foundations, with standard wood-frame construction and relatively predictable structural systems — a good baseline for additions. The complication is that El Dorado Hills falls within El Dorado County's jurisdiction for unincorporated parcels, and the county's building department has its own permit review timelines, setback requirements, and grading ordinances that differ from neighboring Sacramento County. Lot coverage limits and side-yard setbacks vary by zoning district, so the first thing we do is pull the parcel data and confirm exactly how much buildable envelope you have before any design conversation begins.

The clay-dominant soil throughout much of the foothills also matters for ground-level additions. Expansive soils shift seasonally — swelling with winter rain and shrinking in the dry heat of summer — which is why a proper soils assessment and appropriately engineered footings are not optional extras here. Skipping that step is how additions develop foundation cracks within a few years of completion.

## Our Room Addition and Home Addition Process in El Dorado Hills

Every project starts with a site visit and a frank conversation about what the county will and won't allow on your specific lot. From there, the process moves through design development, permit submittal to El Dorado County Building and Safety, and then construction — but the sequencing matters as much as the steps themselves.

**Design and feasibility:** We review your lot's zoning, setbacks, and any applicable HOA covenants before a single line gets drawn. Many El Dorado Hills communities have active HOAs with architectural review committees that require separate approval from county permits — both tracks run in parallel, not sequentially, to avoid delays.

**Permitting:** El Dorado County's permit review process for residential additions typically involves plan check by the Building Department and, depending on scope, review by the Environmental Management Department if grading or drainage is affected. We prepare and submit the permit package and manage the back-and-forth with the county so you're not chasing inspectors.

**Construction:** Our crews handle framing, roofing tie-ins, exterior finishes, insulation, drywall, and finish carpentry. We coordinate licensed subcontractors for electrical, plumbing, and HVAC — all pulled under the same permit set so there's one point of accountability.

**Final inspection:** El Dorado County requires a final inspection sign-off before the addition can be legally occupied. We schedule and walk all inspections through to a recorded final.

## HOA Coordination in El Dorado Hills Communities

A meaningful portion of El Dorado Hills is governed by homeowners associations, some of which have detailed design guidelines covering exterior materials, roof pitch, window proportions, and paint palettes. An addition that clears county permitting can still be rejected by an architectural review committee if it doesn't match the community's standards. We've worked through this dual-approval process enough times to know that submitting to both bodies simultaneously — rather than waiting for county approval before approaching the HOA — is the move that keeps projects on schedule. If your community requires stamped architectural drawings for HOA submission, we coordinate that with our design partners early.

## Local Note

El Dorado Hills sits in a high fire hazard severity zone designated by CAL FIRE, which has a direct impact on addition projects. Any new construction or significant addition in these zones must comply with Chapter 7A of the California Building Code — the wildland-urban interface construction standards. In practical terms, that means ignition-resistant exterior materials, ember-resistant vents, and in some cases multi-pane windows. Homeowners are sometimes surprised to learn that a simple room addition triggers a full 7A compliance review on the new work. We flag this at the feasibility stage so there are no material surprises mid-project.
