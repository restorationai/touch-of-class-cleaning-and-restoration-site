## Why Citrus Heights Homes Are Particularly Vulnerable to Fire Damage

Citrus Heights developed rapidly through the 1950s, 1960s, and 1970s, and a large share of the housing stock reflects the construction standards of that era. Homes built during those decades often feature wood-frame construction with minimal fire-blocking between wall cavities, which allows smoke and heat to travel vertically through walls long after the visible flames are out. Older attic insulation — particularly the blown cellulose and fiberglass batts common in mid-century Sacramento-area builds — absorbs smoke compounds deeply, making surface cleaning alone insufficient.

The region's hot, dry summers also mean that wood framing tends to be lower in moisture content than in wetter climates, which affects how char penetrates structural members and how quickly secondary oxidation damage spreads after a fire is suppressed. This is not a minor detail: structural members that look intact on the surface can have compromised load capacity that only a thorough inspection will reveal.

## Our Fire Damage Restoration Process in Citrus Heights

Every fire loss is different, but the sequence of work follows a disciplined order that protects both the structure and the people inside it.

**Emergency stabilization** comes first — boarding windows, tarping compromised roof sections, and securing the structure against weather and unauthorized entry. Citrus Heights sits inland, and even in the cooler months an unsecured structure can take on additional moisture from overnight fog or light rain that complicates the restoration.

**Damage assessment and documentation** follows immediately. Our team photographs and catalogs every affected surface and system — walls, ceilings, HVAC ductwork, cabinetry, flooring — in a format that is legible to insurance adjusters and supports a clean claim submission.

**Soot and smoke removal** is the most labor-intensive phase. Dry soot from fast-burning fires behaves differently than the wet, oily residue left by slow smolders or synthetic materials. We match cleaning chemistry and technique to the residue type rather than applying a single method to every surface.

**Structural drying and odor neutralization** close out the remediation phase before any reconstruction begins. Thermal fogging and hydroxyl treatment are used where appropriate to address smoke odor embedded in framing and insulation that cannot be physically removed.

**Reconstruction** — our differentiator as a licensed general contractor — means you work with one company from emergency response through finished repairs, rather than handing off to a separate rebuild crew mid-project.

## Local Note

Citrus Heights homes built in the 1960s and early 1970s frequently used aluminum wiring in branch circuits — a code-compliant practice at the time that is now known to carry elevated fire risk at connection points. After a fire in a home of that era, it is worth confirming with your electrician whether aluminum branch wiring is present before reconstruction begins, because the restoration scope may need to include connection upgrades at outlets and fixtures. This is not a universal requirement, but it is a conversation worth having before walls are closed back up.
