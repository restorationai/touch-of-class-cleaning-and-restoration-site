## Our Post-Construction and Specialty Cleaning Process in Loomis

Every project starts with a walkthrough to document what trades have been on-site and what surfaces were affected. Construction cleaning is sequenced — rough clean first, detail clean second, final polish third — because skipping steps means redistributing debris rather than removing it.

**Rough clean** removes bulk debris: scrap lumber, fasteners, packaging, drywall offcuts, and the heaviest accumulations of dust. This phase includes HEPA-filtered vacuuming of all framed cavities that remain accessible before flooring or trim is installed.

**Detail clean** addresses the surfaces that show. Cabinet interiors are wiped down before hardware is installed. Window frames and sills are cleared of overspray and sticker adhesive. Tile grout haze — a common byproduct of tile setting in Loomis bathroom and kitchen renovations — is treated with appropriate cleaning agents that lift the haze without etching the tile surface.

**Final polish** is what the homeowner or property manager actually sees at walkthrough. Floors are cleaned to a move-in standard, fixtures are wiped free of fingerprints and installation residue, and glass surfaces are cleared of paint overspray and construction film.

For specialty cleaning scopes — pressure washing of exterior concrete, cleaning of exposed beam ceilings, or degreasing of garage slabs — the process is scoped separately and scheduled to avoid recontaminating surfaces that have already been detailed.

## Local Note

One thing that comes up regularly on Loomis custom home builds: the combination of exposed wood beam ceilings and open-concept great rooms means that construction dust doesn't just settle on horizontal surfaces — it embeds in the grain of rough-sawn or hand-hewn beams. Standard wiping smears the dust deeper into the wood rather than lifting it. The correct approach is dry brushing with a natural-bristle brush followed by low-pressure vacuuming before any finish oil or wax is applied. Getting this sequence wrong means the homeowner sees a hazy, dulled beam ceiling on move-in day that requires refinishing to correct.
