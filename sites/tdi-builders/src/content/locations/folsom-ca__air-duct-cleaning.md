## Why Folsom Properties See Duct Buildup Faster Than You'd Expect

Folsom sits at the edge of the Sierra Nevada foothills, where prevailing winds carry fine clay dust from the surrounding grasslands and, in recent years, increasingly heavy wildfire smoke from fires burning to the east and north. That particulate is small enough to pass through many standard HVAC filters and settle deep inside supply and return ducts. Homes built during Folsom's major growth periods — the 1990s through the mid-2000s, when large master-planned communities expanded rapidly across the city — often have long duct runs with multiple bends, which are natural collection points for debris. Flex duct, common in that era of construction, develops small sags over time that trap moisture and particles together, creating conditions where biological growth can take hold. Add the fact that many Folsom households run their systems nearly year-round — cooling from May through October, heating from November through March — and duct surfaces accumulate contaminants faster than in milder climates.

## Our Air Duct Cleaning Process in Folsom

The work starts before any equipment enters the house. A technician walks the system — locating every supply register, return grille, and access point — and notes any visible flex duct sag, disconnected joints, or signs of past moisture intrusion. This inspection step matters more in Folsom than in areas with older rigid-duct construction, because flex duct problems that go unaddressed during cleaning can reintroduce debris into a freshly cleaned system within weeks.

Once the system is mapped, we use high-powered negative-pressure equipment to place the entire duct network under vacuum, then work register by register with rotary brush and compressed-air agitation tools to dislodge debris toward the collection point. Supply and return sides are cleaned separately so cross-contamination doesn't undo the work. If sanitization is requested — common after wildfire smoke events or when biological growth is present — an EPA-registered antimicrobial agent is applied to interior duct surfaces after cleaning, not before, so it contacts clean metal rather than a layer of dust. Dryer vent cleaning follows the same visit when requested; Folsom's newer construction often routes dryer vents through long horizontal runs inside finished walls, which accumulate lint faster than short exterior-wall installations.
