Building a new home in Arden-Arcade starts with a question most buyers don't expect: whose permit desk are you dealing with? As an unincorporated community, Arden-Arcade runs its building approvals through Sacramento County rather than a city hall, and that single distinction shapes everything from site plan review to final inspection scheduling. TDI Builders has handled ground-up construction across Sacramento County long enough to know the county's process, not just the city's, and that familiarity keeps a project moving instead of stalling in the queue.

## Why Arden-Arcade Lots Present Unique Construction Challenges

Arden-Arcade sits in the Central Valley's clay-heavy soil belt, which means expansive soil behavior is a real design input, not an afterthought. Clay soil swells when wet and contracts hard during the area's long dry summers, and a foundation engineered without that movement in mind will show cracking within a few seasons. Add in the mix of older ranch-style lots carved out decades ago alongside newer infill parcels, and every site brings a different combination of setback rules, existing utility tie-ins, and soil compaction history that has to be checked before a shovel goes in the ground.

The climate itself is a second factor worth planning around early. Summer heat in this part of Sacramento County runs hot and dry for months at a stretch, which affects everything from concrete cure scheduling to how we size HVAC and insulation packages so the home performs once it's occupied, not just when it passes inspection.

## Our New Home Construction Process in Arden-Arcade

We start with a soils report and a review of the parcel's permitting history with Sacramento County, since unincorporated lots sometimes carry conditions from prior approvals that affect what can be built today. From there, foundation design is matched to the site's actual clay content rather than a generic slab spec, framing and systems rough-in follow standard California building code sequencing, and we schedule county inspections at each phase so nothing holds up the next trade. For custom builds, we also walk the owner through material and layout decisions before framing locks those choices in, which matters most on narrower or irregular Arden-Arcade lots where square footage has to be used efficiently.

## Arden-Arcade HOA and County Coordination

Not every Arden-Arcade subdivision has an HOA, but where one exists, especially in the area's older established pockets, architectural review can run parallel to county permitting rather than after it. We submit to both tracks at the same time when a project calls for it, which keeps an HOA's exterior material or setback requirements from surfacing late and forcing a redesign. On straight county-permitted lots without an association, the process is more direct, but we still confirm zoning and setback specifics before design work is finalized, since Sacramento County's unincorporated zoning can vary parcel to parcel in ways a city-wide code would not.

## Local note

One detail that catches new-construction clients off guard in this part of the county: older Arden-Arcade parcels were sometimes platted before current utility easement standards, which means a lot that looks straightforward on a plat map can have a water or sewer line running through where a garage or addition was planned. We pull utility records as a standard early step specifically because of this pattern, not as an extra charge after the fact.
