## Why Rancho Cordova Properties See Water Damage Issues

The soil composition across much of Rancho Cordova is a mix of expansive clay and sandy loam left behind by ancient river deposits. That combination does two things that matter to homeowners: it drains poorly during heavy Sacramento Valley rain events, which means water pools against foundations and crawl space vents rather than running off, and it shifts seasonally as it wets and dries, stressing slab foundations and the plumbing lines embedded in them. The result is a higher-than-average rate of slab leaks and foundation seepage in this part of Sacramento County compared to newer developments built on engineered fill.

The housing stock compounds the risk. A large share of Rancho Cordova's single-family homes were built between 1955 and 1980, when copper supply lines were standard but galvanized steel drain pipes were still common. Those galvanized pipes corrode from the inside out — the exterior looks fine until a section fails completely. Homeowners often don't discover the problem until water has already spread under flooring or into wall cavities behind the kitchen or bathroom.

## Our Water Damage Restoration Process in Rancho Cordova

Every water damage call starts with a thorough moisture assessment — not just the visible wet area, but the surrounding materials that may have absorbed water silently. We use thermal imaging cameras and calibrated moisture meters to map the full extent of saturation before any drying equipment is placed. In Rancho Cordova's older ranch-style homes, that often means checking beneath vinyl and linoleum flooring that was glued directly to the concrete slab, a construction method that traps moisture and hides it from a visual inspection.

Once we understand the scope, water extraction begins immediately using truck-mounted and portable extraction units. Structural drying follows with industrial air movers and desiccant or refrigerant dehumidifiers sized to the affected area — not a one-size-fits-all setup. We monitor moisture readings daily and adjust equipment placement until materials reach their target drying goals per the IICRC S500 standard. Documentation is generated throughout: photos, moisture logs, and equipment records that your insurance adjuster will need.

## Local Note

In Rancho Cordova's tract homes built during the 1960s and early 1970s, original bathroom and kitchen walls were often finished with a layer of plaster over metal lath rather than the paper-faced drywall used in later construction. Plaster absorbs water more slowly than drywall, which means it can feel firm and intact at the surface while the lath and framing behind it remain saturated for days. Standard drying timelines don't apply — these assemblies need extended monitoring and sometimes targeted airflow directed into wall cavities before readings stabilize. Skipping that step is one of the most common reasons secondary mold problems develop in this city's older housing stock.
