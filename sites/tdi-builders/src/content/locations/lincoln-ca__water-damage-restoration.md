## Why Lincoln Properties See Water Damage Issues

Lincoln's growth over the past two decades brought thousands of production homes built on expansive clay-heavy soils common to the foothills of Placer County. Clay soil holds moisture and shifts seasonally, which puts pressure on slab foundations and the plumbing that runs through them. Slab leaks are a recurring problem here — water migrates silently beneath concrete floors for weeks before a homeowner notices a warm spot underfoot or an unexplained spike in the water bill. Older homes in the more established parts of the city often have galvanized supply lines that corrode from the inside out, narrowing until they fail without warning.

Winter atmospheric river events — the kind that deliver several inches of rain over 48 to 72 hours — regularly overwhelm area drainage. When gutters back up or grading channels runoff toward a foundation rather than away from it, water finds its way into crawl spaces and garages. Once it's under a structure, the humidity it generates can begin feeding mold colonization within 24 to 48 hours of the initial intrusion.

## Our Water Damage Restoration Process in Lincoln

Every job starts with a thorough moisture assessment before any equipment is staged. We use thermal imaging cameras and calibrated moisture meters to map saturation behind walls and under flooring — not just the visible wet area. In Lincoln homes built on slab foundations, this step is especially important because water wicks laterally through concrete and can surface several feet from the actual source.

Once the scope is documented, we extract standing water using truck-mounted and portable extraction units, then set commercial-grade desiccant dehumidifiers and high-velocity air movers calibrated to the room volume and material types present. Drying is monitored daily against psychrometric targets — we don't pull equipment because a surface feels dry; we pull it when the moisture readings confirm structural materials have returned to acceptable levels. If flooring, drywall, or insulation is too saturated to dry in place, we remove it cleanly and document everything for your insurance claim.

## Local Note

Lincoln's newer master-planned communities often feature stucco exteriors over wood-frame construction. Stucco is deceptive after a water event — it can look and feel dry at the surface while the OSB sheathing and wall cavity behind it stay saturated for weeks. Standard surface moisture readings miss this entirely. When we respond to water losses in these neighborhoods, we probe through weep screeds and use thermal imaging to check the cavity before closing out the drying phase. Skipping that step is one of the most common reasons mold shows up months after a "completed" restoration.
