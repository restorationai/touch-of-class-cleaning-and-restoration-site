## Why Lincoln Properties See Water Damage Issues

Lincoln's growth over the past two decades has layered multiple eras of construction on top of each other. Established neighborhoods closer to the historic downtown core include homes built on pier-and-beam foundations with older galvanized or early copper plumbing, the kind of supply lines that develop pinhole leaks quietly, sometimes for weeks before a ceiling stain or soft floor gives them away. Farther out, the master-planned communities that expanded Lincoln through the 2000s and into the 2010s brought slab-on-grade construction with PEX runs and tile roofing, a different risk profile but not an immune one: slab leaks in those homes are notoriously difficult to detect at the surface while they silently saturate the concrete and the framing above it.

Lincoln's clay-dominant soils also drain poorly after sustained rain. When gutters overflow or downspouts discharge too close to a foundation, that standing water migrates toward crawl spaces and slab edges rather than percolating away. The result is moisture intrusion that reads dry on the surface but registers on a thermal imaging camera or a deep-probe moisture meter as elevated, sometimes significantly.

## Our Water Damage Restoration Process in Lincoln

The first priority on any water loss is stopping the source and containing the spread. Once the water is off, the assessment phase begins: thermal imaging to map moisture migration behind walls and under flooring, moisture readings logged by material type and location, and a written scope that documents what needs to come out versus what can be dried in place.

Drying in Lincoln requires accounting for the region's seasonal humidity swings. During the wet season, ambient outdoor air is too moisture-laden to assist drying, so the process relies on closed-system desiccant or refrigerant dehumidifiers working in tandem with high-velocity air movers. Readings are taken on a documented schedule, typically daily, until affected materials reach the dry standard. Structural materials that cannot reach that standard through drying alone are removed before they create a secondary problem.

Once the structure is dry and verified, TDI USA handles the rebuild: drywall replacement and finishing, ceiling repair, flooring reinstallation, paint, and any millwork or cabinetry affected by the loss. Keeping restoration and reconstruction under one contractor avoids the handoff gaps where scope items fall through and timelines slip.
