## Why West Sacramento Properties Face Particular Board-Up and Tarping Risks

The housing stock in West Sacramento spans a wide range of eras and construction types. The older neighborhoods closer to the riverfront include mid-century wood-frame homes built on slab foundations, where a broken window or compromised roof deck exposes interior framing to the elements almost immediately. Newer infill development and the mixed-use corridors near the Bridge District sit alongside light industrial and commercial properties — each with different envelope vulnerabilities.

The climate compounds the risk. Sacramento Valley summers regularly push above 100°F, and an unprotected roof opening after a fire can allow heat to bake smoke residue deeper into framing within hours. In winter, tule fog keeps relative humidity elevated for days at a stretch, and any exposed structural opening becomes an entry point for moisture that can begin supporting mold colonization within 24 to 48 hours of a loss event. A proper emergency tarp — secured with battens, not just weighted at the edges — is the difference between a contained loss and one that doubles in scope before your adjuster arrives.

## Our Emergency Board-Up and Tarping Process in West Sacramento

When we receive a call from a West Sacramento property owner, the first thing we assess is the nature of the opening and the weather window. A fire-damaged roof in July and a fire-damaged roof in January require different tarp weights and fastening approaches — the summer concern is UV degradation and heat expansion, while the winter concern is standing water pooling under a poorly tensioned tarp during a valley rain event.

For board-up work — broken windows, kicked-in doors, vehicle impact openings — we use plywood cut and fitted to the rough opening, secured in a way that meets the minimum security standard without causing additional damage to the surrounding frame or trim. On commercial storefronts along the West Sacramento retail corridors, we work with property managers to ensure the board-up is clean enough to satisfy any landlord or city code compliance requirement while the permanent repair is scheduled.

Our process follows a consistent sequence: site safety assessment, photograph and document all openings before any material is placed, install tarping or board materials, and provide the property owner with a written scope of what was installed and where. That documentation matters when you're working with an insurance carrier.
