A kitchen remodel in Davis has to account for something most California contractors don't think about twice: the Central Valley heat cycle that bakes this town for four or five months a year, then gives way to damp winter mornings that roll in off the Yolo Bypass. That swing matters when you're choosing cabinet materials, slab seating, and finishes that won't warp, gap, or discolor two summers in. TDI Builders has been building and rebuilding Northern California kitchens since 1985, and Davis jobs get planned around that climate reality from the first design conversation.

## Why Davis Kitchens Need a Different Approach

Much of Davis's housing stock dates to the ranch-style tract boom of the 1960s and 70s, with a second wave of construction tied to UC Davis's growth in the decades after. Those older homes typically came with compact galley kitchens, slab-on-grade foundations, and plumbing runs that were never designed for a modern island or a second sink. Moving a drain line a few feet in a slab-foundation kitchen means saw-cutting concrete, which changes both the cost and the sequencing of a remodel compared to a home with a crawlspace.

Water quality is the other factor that shows up in almost every Davis kitchen project. For decades the city relied heavily on groundwater with a reputation for high mineral content, which left a legacy of scale buildup in older fixtures, faucets, and dishwasher lines. Even with the regional surface water improvements of recent years, we still see plenty of Davis kitchens where swapping in a filtration line or a different faucet finish is worth discussing during the design phase, especially for homeowners who've already noticed spotting on stainless or glass.

## Our Kitchen Remodeling Process in Davis

We start with an on-site walkthrough to confirm what's actually behind the walls and under the slab, not just what the original builder's plans say, since permit records for Davis's older tract homes don't always match what's really there after decades of owner updates. From there we handle design, cabinet selection, electrical and plumbing coordination, and finish work as one continuous project rather than handing it off between subcontractors. For slab-foundation homes we scope any necessary cutting and re-pour early, so it doesn't become a surprise mid-project. Because we carry both residential and commercial licensing, the same team that frames a custom kitchen addition can also pull in structural or electrical expertise if a wall turns out to be load-bearing or a panel needs upgrading to support new appliances.

## Local note

One detail that catches Davis homeowners off guard: window placement in a lot of mid-century kitchens here was designed around natural cross-ventilation rather than air conditioning, since many of these homes predate widespread residential AC in the Central Valley. When we open up a wall for a more modern layout, we pay close attention to how that affects summer airflow through the kitchen, because losing the original cross-breeze without planning for it can turn a beautifully remodeled kitchen into the hottest room in the house by July.
