Arden-Arcade's mid-century ranch homes, many built in the 1950s and 1960s on concrete slab foundations, were fitted with bathrooms sized for a different era: small single-vanity layouts, cast iron tubs, and galvanized supply lines that are now well past their intended service life. A bathroom remodel here isn't just cosmetic. It's often the point where a homeowner finally addresses scale buildup from Sacramento's hard water, a tub that's become a fall risk, or plumbing runs hidden in a slab that make even simple fixture swaps more involved than they'd be in a newer build.

## Why Arden-Arcade Bathrooms Need Updating

Slab-on-grade construction is the norm across the unincorporated neighborhoods of Arden-Arcade, which means supply and drain lines run beneath the concrete rather than through an accessible crawlspace. When those original galvanized pipes start to corrode from the inside, a remodel is frequently the first time anyone gets eyes on the actual condition of the plumbing. Add in Sacramento's naturally hard water, which leaves mineral scale on shower heads, faucet aerators, and tile grout faster than it would in a softer-water region, and a lot of these original bathrooms are showing their age in the fixtures long before the layout itself feels dated. Homes closer to the American River Parkway also tend to have slightly higher ambient humidity during the summer, which accelerates grout and caulk breakdown around original tile tubs.

## Our Bathroom Remodeling Process in Arden-Arcade

Because Arden-Arcade is unincorporated, permitting for remodel work runs through Sacramento County rather than a city building department, and we handle that submittal as part of the project rather than leaving it to the homeowner. On slab-foundation homes, we start with a plumbing assessment before any demo begins, since relocating a drain or supply line means cutting and patching concrete, not just reframing a wall. From there the sequence is demo, rough plumbing and electrical, waterproofing (critical for tub-to-shower conversions, where the old tub surround is removed and a full shower pan and membrane system goes in its place), tile and fixture installation, and final county inspection before we close out the permit. Master bath remodels in the larger ranch floor plans common to the area often involve reworking the layout entirely, since original primary bathrooms here were rarely built larger than a powder room by today's standards.

## Local note

One detail that catches homeowners off guard: because so many Arden-Arcade bathrooms still have their original cast iron tubs, removing one for a tub-to-shower conversion is a two-person job just to get it out of the house, and the subfloor underneath is almost always in rougher shape than expected after decades of a cast iron tub distributing weight unevenly across the slab transition. We budget for subfloor repair as a likely line item on these conversions rather than an unpleasant surprise mid-project.
