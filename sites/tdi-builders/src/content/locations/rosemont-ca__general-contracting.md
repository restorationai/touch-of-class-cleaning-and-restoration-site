## Why Rosemont Homes Need a Different Renovation Approach

Rosemont is unincorporated, which means permits for additions, structural changes, and most remodels route through Sacramento County rather than a city building department. That changes the timeline and the inspection sequence compared to projects inside Sacramento's city limits, and a general contractor who pulls permits regularly in the county knows which inspections to schedule together to avoid losing a week waiting on a callback. The housing stock itself adds another layer: many Rosemont homes from the postwar boom were built on slab foundations with minimal rebar by modern standards, so a kitchen remodel that involves moving plumbing or load-bearing walls often needs a structural assessment before demo starts, not after a crew finds a surprise.

## Local Note: Mid-Century Plumbing and Panel Upgrades

A detail that catches homeowners off guard in Rosemont's older sections: many houses from the 1950s and 60s still run on cast iron or galvanized steel drain lines, even if the supply side was updated at some point. When we open a wall for a bathroom remodel in one of these homes, we check the drain material before finalizing the fixture layout, because cast iron that's corroding internally can look fine from the outside but fail within a year of a remodel if it's not addressed during the same project. Pairing a drain line inspection with the remodel scope, rather than treating plumbing as a separate call later, saves homeowners from reopening finished tile work.

Because Rosemont falls under Sacramento County rather than a municipal building department, the permit and inspection cadence differs from projects just a few miles away inside city limits.
