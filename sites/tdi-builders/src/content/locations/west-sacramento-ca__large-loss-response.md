## Why West Sacramento Properties Face Elevated Catastrophic Loss Risk

The geography here works against property owners in ways that aren't always obvious until a loss occurs. Much of West Sacramento sits on reclaimed floodplain and river delta soils — expansive clays and silty fill that shift seasonally and complicate structural assessments after a major event. Buildings that look stable on the surface can show differential settlement damage that only becomes visible once walls are opened during restoration.

The city's building stock spans a wide range of eras. The older residential neighborhoods near the riverfront include homes built before modern seismic and waterproofing codes, with crawl space foundations and original cast-iron or galvanized plumbing that can fail catastrophically rather than gradually. The newer mixed-use and commercial developments along the waterfront corridor bring their own challenges: complex mechanical systems, multi-tenant occupancy, and HOA or property management structures that add layers to the coordination process.

Sacramento Valley's climate adds pressure. The region's wet winters — with concentrated rainfall events that can dump several inches in 24 hours — regularly overwhelm aging storm infrastructure. When drainage backs up across a commercial property or a residential complex, the resulting loss can easily cross into multi-million-dollar territory before the water even recedes.

## Our Large Loss and Catastrophic Response Process in West Sacramento

Large loss work is not accelerated small-loss work. The process is structurally different, and the differences matter from the first hour on-site.

When TDI Builders mobilizes for a catastrophic loss in West Sacramento, the initial assessment is led by a senior project manager — not a crew lead — whose job is to map the full scope before any demolition or extraction begins. That means thermal imaging across affected assemblies, moisture mapping that accounts for the clay-heavy soils that can wick ground moisture into slab-on-grade foundations, and a documented chain of custody for any materials that may be subject to insurance dispute.

From there, the work is staged. Structural stabilization and weatherproofing come first if the building envelope is compromised. Water extraction and drying run concurrently with hazardous material identification — older West Sacramento commercial buildings often contain asbestos-containing floor tiles or pipe insulation that must be addressed before full demolition can proceed. Our IICRC certified technicians manage drying to the S500 standard, with daily moisture readings logged and available to adjusters.

Throughout the project, a dedicated point of contact handles communication with the property owner, the insurance carrier, and any public agency that has jurisdiction — including Yolo County permitting when structural work triggers a permit requirement.

## West Sacramento Insurance and Large Loss Adjuster Coordination

Catastrophic losses in West Sacramento frequently involve commercial carriers, surplus lines policies, and independent adjusters who may be managing multiple simultaneous claims after a regional weather event. TDI Builders has worked alongside large loss adjusters for decades. We provide the documentation format they need: scope of loss reports, Xactimate-compatible line items, daily drying logs, and photographic records organized by trade and timeline.

For multi-tenant commercial properties, we coordinate with property managers to document individual unit impacts separately — a requirement for carriers writing separate policies on the same building. We do not tell you what your policy covers; that's your adjuster's role. What we do is make sure the documentation never becomes the reason a claim stalls.
