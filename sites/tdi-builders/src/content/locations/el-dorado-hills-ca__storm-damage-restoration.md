## Why El Dorado Hills Properties See Storm Damage Differently

The foothill topography that makes El Dorado Hills attractive — the oak-studded lots, the grade changes, the mature tree canopy — is also what makes storm losses here more complex than a flat suburban claim. Blue oak and valley oak root systems are shallow relative to canopy spread, so a sustained wind event can topple trees that looked perfectly healthy the week before. When a 60-foot oak comes down on a composition shingle roof, it rarely stops at the decking; it compresses rafters, shears fascia, and opens a cavity that funnels rain directly onto insulation and ceiling drywall.

The housing stock adds another layer. Much of El Dorado Hills was built in phases from the late 1970s through the early 2000s, meaning you'll find a mix of T-111 siding, OSB sheathing, and older felt underlayments — materials that absorb moisture quickly once the weather barrier is breached. Stucco exteriors common in the area can look intact while concealing water migration behind the scratch coat for weeks. That hidden saturation is exactly the kind of problem that turns a storm event into a mold remediation job if it isn't caught early.

## Our Storm Damage Restoration Process in El Dorado Hills

Every job starts with a full exterior and interior assessment before any debris is moved. Photographing the damage in place — roof penetrations, soffit intrusions, window frame failures — creates the documentation chain that matters most when an insurance adjuster reviews the claim. From there, the process follows a clear sequence:

**Immediate stabilization** — tarping and board-up to stop active water intrusion. In a foothill storm, secondary rain events often follow within 24 to 48 hours; an unsealed opening compounds the loss quickly.

**Structural debris removal** — downed limbs and displaced roofing materials are removed and documented by category (structural vs. cosmetic) because carriers treat them differently.

**Moisture mapping** — thermal imaging and calibrated moisture meters identify where water has traveled beyond the obvious impact zone. Water behind stucco or inside wall cavities reads dry at the surface; the meters tell the real story.

**Drying and dehumidification** — commercial-grade desiccant and refrigerant dehumidifiers, combined with high-velocity air movers, bring structural materials to target moisture content before any reconstruction begins. IICRC-certified technicians monitor readings daily.

**Reconstruction** — roofing, framing, siding, drywall, and finish work are handled in-house. One contractor managing the full scope means the timeline doesn't stall between trades.

## Local Note

El Dorado Hills properties built on the steeper grade sections — particularly lots that back up to natural drainage swales — face a compounding problem after major storms: surface runoff that was designed to sheet away from the foundation can reverse course when storm drains are overwhelmed with debris. Mulch, oak leaf litter, and broken branches clog area drains fast. When that happens, water ponds against stem walls and finds its way into crawl spaces and slab perimeters through cracks that were never an issue in a normal rain year. It's worth having those drainage paths inspected as part of any post-storm assessment, not just the roof.
