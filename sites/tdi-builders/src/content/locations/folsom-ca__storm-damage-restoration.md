Folsom sits at the edge of the Sierra Nevada foothills, where late-season atmospheric rivers can drop several inches of rain in a matter of hours, and where oak and pine canopy — dense in many of the city's established neighborhoods — turns into a liability the moment sustained winds arrive. When a storm peels back roofing, drops a limb through a fence, or drives water under a door threshold, the damage compounds fast: saturated insulation, swelling framing, and the beginnings of mold colonization can all be underway before the skies clear. TDI Builders, Inc. has been handling exactly this kind of loss since 1985, and our IICRC-certified technicians are available around the clock.

## Why Folsom Properties See Storm Damage the Way They Do

Folsom's position along the western slope of the foothills creates a specific weather profile that differs meaningfully from the flat Sacramento Valley floor to the west. Winter and early-spring storms that have already dropped snow at higher elevations arrive here as cold, heavy rain — sometimes with embedded wind gusts that exceed what the valley sees. That combination of volume and velocity is hard on roofing systems, particularly the concrete tile roofs common in Folsom's planned communities, where cracked or displaced tiles can let water migrate into the underlayment and down into attic framing before a homeowner notices a stain on the ceiling.

Folsom also sits in a zone where mature oak trees are protected under city ordinance, which means they tend to grow large and close to structures. During a significant storm event, a limb failure over a roof or fence line is one of the more common calls we receive from this area. The soil in the foothills transitions between clay-heavy and decomposed granite depending on the parcel, and that variability affects drainage: clay-rich yards can sheet water toward foundations when storm drains are overwhelmed, while decomposed granite areas drain quickly but can erode and undercut hardscape.

## Our Storm Damage Restoration Process in Folsom

The first priority after any storm loss is stopping ongoing intrusion. That means emergency tarping of compromised roof sections, boarding of broken windows or doors, and — where water has already entered — extracting standing water before it wicks into subfloor assemblies. We document every affected area with photographs and moisture readings before we touch anything, because that documentation is what your insurance adjuster will rely on when evaluating the claim.

Once the structure is stabilized, the scope of work becomes clearer. Tree impact damage often involves structural assessment: rafters, ridge boards, and sheathing may need replacement rather than repair. Water intrusion from roof breaches or overwhelmed gutters tends to travel farther than it looks — water behind plaster or stucco can read dry at the surface while the framing behind it stays saturated. We use thermal imaging and calibrated moisture meters to map the actual wet zone, then dry to the IICRC S500 standard before any rebuild begins.

Rebuild scope in Folsom frequently involves matching concrete tile, stucco finishes, and the specific paint colors that HOAs in many of the city's master-planned communities require. We coordinate with HOA architectural review processes and pull the appropriate building permits through the City of Folsom's Community Development Department so the finished repair closes out cleanly.

## Folsom Insurance & HOA Coordination

Most homeowner policies cover sudden storm damage — wind, hail, falling trees — but the documentation requirements vary by carrier. We photograph and measure every affected surface, produce a written scope of loss, and communicate directly with adjusters so you are not caught between competing estimates. Supplemental claims for hidden damage found during the repair phase (wet insulation behind intact drywall, for example) are common on storm jobs, and we handle those conversations with the carrier as part of the project.

For properties governed by an HOA — and a significant share of Folsom's housing stock falls under one — exterior repairs typically require architectural approval before work begins. We are familiar with that process and can provide the documentation packages most HOA management companies request.

## Local Note

Folsom's protected oak canopy is one of the city's most valued features, but it creates a specific complication after windstorms: because property owners cannot simply remove a protected oak without city authorization, a limb that has fallen onto a structure may still be attached to a standing protected tree. That affects how debris removal is staged and sequenced. We coordinate with the property owner on the city notification process before we move material that is still connected to a protected tree, which avoids creating a separate code issue on top of the storm loss itself.
