## Why Fair Oaks Properties See Water Damage Issues

The Sacramento foothills climate delivers dry, hot summers followed by concentrated winter rain events — the kind that overwhelm gutters, push water against foundations, and expose any weakness in aging plumbing. Fair Oaks was developed heavily through the 1950s, 1960s, and 1970s, and many homes in the area still carry their original galvanized steel supply lines. Those pipes corrode from the inside out, and by the time a pinhole leak becomes a visible problem, the wall cavity behind it has often been damp for weeks.

Clay soils common throughout Sacramento County don't drain quickly. When rain saturates the ground around a slab foundation or crawl space, hydrostatic pressure builds and water finds its way in through foundation cracks, weep screed gaps, and utility penetrations. Crawl space flooding is especially common in older Fair Oaks homes that were built before modern vapor barriers were standard.

The American River corridor also means that properties in lower-lying pockets of the area can experience rising groundwater during prolonged rain seasons, compounding what might otherwise be a straightforward interior plumbing loss.

## Our Water Damage Restoration Process in Fair Oaks

Every water loss is different, but the sequence of work follows a proven structure. When a call comes in, the first priority is stopping the source — whether that means coordinating with a plumber or shutting off the main supply. From there, the process moves through extraction, drying, and verification.

**Water extraction** removes standing water using truck-mounted and portable extraction units. In Fair Oaks homes with hardwood floors over a crawl space, this step requires attention to what's happening below the surface — water migrates through subfloor seams and can pool under the structure even when the finished floor looks dry.

**Structural drying** uses calibrated air movers and commercial dehumidifiers placed according to psychrometric readings, not guesswork. Drying is documented with daily moisture readings so there's a clear record for your insurance adjuster.

**Moisture mapping** with thermal imaging and pin-type meters identifies saturation inside walls and under flooring that isn't visible to the eye. This step is especially important in older Fair Oaks homes where plaster walls and dense wood framing hold moisture differently than modern lightweight construction.

TDI Builders' IICRC certified technicians follow the IICRC S500 standard throughout — a requirement most insurance carriers expect to see documented before they approve a claim.

## Local Note

Older Fair Oaks homes built in the 1950s and 1960s frequently used lath-and-plaster wall systems rather than drywall. Plaster is dense and slow to release moisture — surface readings can show acceptable numbers while the wood lath behind the plaster is still holding significant water content. Crews working in these homes need to extend drying timelines and place equipment differently than they would in a home with standard half-inch drywall. Pulling a moisture reading from the plaster face and calling the wall dry is one of the more common mistakes in this type of loss. The right approach is to monitor the lath layer directly, which sometimes means creating small access points to get an accurate reading.
