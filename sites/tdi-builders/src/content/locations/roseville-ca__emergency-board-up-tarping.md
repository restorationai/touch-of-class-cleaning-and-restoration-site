## Why Roseville Properties Face Distinct Board-Up and Tarping Risks

Roseville sits at the edge of California's Central Valley foothills, which gives it a climate that swings harder than most of the Sacramento metro. Summer temperatures routinely push past 100°F, drying out wood framing, aging caulk lines, and roofing felt faster than coastal communities see. That brittleness means a minor impact — a falling oak limb, a hailstone from a rare spring cell, a chimney cap failure — can open a roof deck that looks intact from the street but is already letting heat and UV into the attic. Come winter, the same dried-out structure is the first to absorb moisture when the Sierra Nevada storms push through.

Roseville's housing stock adds another layer. The city has grown in distinct waves: post-war tract homes near the older downtown core, 1980s and 1990s subdivisions that expanded rapidly as Sacramento commuters moved outward, and the large planned communities built through the 2000s. Homes from the earlier waves often have original single-pane windows and aging wood siding that shatter or splinter more completely in a storm or fire event, creating larger openings that require more board footage and more careful fastening to avoid damaging already-stressed framing. Newer stucco-clad homes in the master-planned communities present a different challenge: stucco is unforgiving of improper anchor points, and a crew that drills without thinking about the substrate can create water intrusion paths that outlast the original damage.

## Our Emergency Board-Up and Tarping Process in Roseville

When you call (877) 688-0866, the first question we ask is what type of opening you have — broken glass, missing roof section, breached wall — because the materials and approach differ. For window and door openings, we use structural-grade plywood cut and fastened to the exterior framing, not just taped over the frame. For larger wall breaches common after vehicle impacts or fire department forcible entry, we may use a combination of plywood and temporary framing to restore the structural envelope.

Roof tarping in Roseville requires attention to pitch and material. Many homes here have low-to-moderate pitch roofs with composition shingles; a tarp that isn't weighted and secured at the ridge and eaves will catch Roseville's afternoon delta breezes and peel away before morning. We use ballasted cap boards along tarp edges rather than nailing directly through the tarp into the sheathing — a method that protects the underlying material and makes the eventual roof repair cleaner. For tile roofs, which appear frequently in the newer western subdivisions, we work carefully around broken field tiles to avoid enlarging the damaged area during the tarping process.

Every job is documented with timestamped photographs before and after securing — both for your insurance carrier and for the roofing or reconstruction crew that follows us.

## Local Note

One pattern we see repeatedly in Roseville's 1990s-era subdivisions: the original roofing felt on homes from that period has often reached or exceeded its rated service life, which means a storm event that only damages a few field shingles may have actually compromised the felt layer across a wider area. When we tarp these roofs, we extend coverage well beyond the visible damage zone rather than tarping to the edge of the broken shingles — a tighter tarp on a failed felt layer can funnel water laterally under the sheathing rather than stopping it. It's a detail that costs a few extra square feet of tarp material and saves thousands in secondary water damage.
