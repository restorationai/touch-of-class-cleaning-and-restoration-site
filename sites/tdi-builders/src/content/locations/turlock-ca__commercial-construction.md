## Why Turlock's Building Stock Shapes Every Tenant Improvement Project

A large share of Turlock's commercial inventory was built during the agricultural boom years of the 1970s and 1980s, when construction standards and energy codes looked very different than they do today. Strip centers and light-industrial buildings from that era frequently have undersized electrical panels, single-pane storefront glazing, and HVAC systems that were never designed to meet California's current Title 24 energy requirements. When a new tenant signs a lease in one of these spaces, the improvement scope almost always expands beyond what the initial budget anticipated, because bringing the base building into compliance is part of the permit package.

The Central Valley climate adds another layer. Turlock summers regularly push past 100 degrees Fahrenheit, which means mechanical systems sized for a previous tenant's load may be completely wrong for a restaurant, a medical office, or a call center. Properly sizing HVAC during a tenant improvement is not optional here; it affects both occupant comfort and your energy costs from day one.

## Local Note: Title 24 and Older Turlock Commercial Shells

One thing that catches out-of-area contractors in Turlock is the combination of older building envelopes and California's current Title 24 energy code. A landlord may hand you a lease for a 1982 retail shell and assume the tenant improvement only covers interior finishes, but the moment you pull a permit for new lighting or HVAC, the entire affected system must be brought into Title 24 compliance. In practice, that often means LED lighting throughout, a new programmable thermostat with economizer controls, and sometimes additional roof insulation. Budgeting for that compliance work upfront, rather than discovering it mid-permit, is the difference between a project that closes on time and one that stalls at plan check. We flag this in the very first conversation with every Turlock client.
