## Why Folsom Properties Experience Sewage Backups

Folsom's growth pattern tells the story of two very different housing stocks. The city's older core — developed in the mid-20th century — sits on clay-heavy soils that shift seasonally as moisture content changes. That movement stresses original cast-iron and Orangeburg sewer laterals, causing cracks and root intrusion that eventually produce full blockages. Newer master-planned communities built on the city's eastern edges are served by modern PVC lines, but even those systems face pressure when heavy winter rain events overwhelm municipal infrastructure and push sewage back through floor drains and toilets.

Folsom's proximity to the American River also matters. Properties in lower-lying areas near the river corridor can experience elevated groundwater tables after significant storm events, which increases hydrostatic pressure on sewer lines and septic tanks alike. Homes on acreage with private septic systems — common in the rural-residential pockets outside the city's urban core — face a different risk: septic tank overflow when the drain field becomes saturated and can no longer absorb effluent.

## Our Sewage Cleanup and Sanitization Process in Folsom

Sewage is classified as Category 3 water — the most contaminated type — and the cleanup protocol reflects that. When our crew arrives, the first priority is containment: isolating the affected area to prevent cross-contamination to adjacent rooms or HVAC systems. We photograph and document everything before extraction begins, which matters for insurance claims and for Sacramento County environmental compliance.

Extraction of standing sewage is followed by removal of all porous materials that cannot be adequately sanitized — drywall, insulation, and flooring that have absorbed contaminated water. In Folsom homes with hardwood floors over a concrete slab, that assessment is especially important: sewage can wick beneath the wood and pool against the slab edge without being visible at the surface. After material removal, affected structural surfaces are treated with EPA-registered antimicrobial agents. Drying equipment — industrial air movers and dehumidifiers — runs until moisture readings confirm the structure has returned to normal levels. A final sanitization pass addresses odor-causing bacteria that survive the initial treatment.
