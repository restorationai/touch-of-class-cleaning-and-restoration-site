## Why Citrus Heights Bathrooms Age the Way They Do

The valley's climate is the silent partner in most bathroom remodels here. Prolonged dry heat causes caulk and grout to shrink and crack faster than in cooler, more humid regions — which means water intrusion at the tub surround or shower pan often starts long before a homeowner notices a soft spot in the floor. Citrus Heights also sits on clay-heavy soils common throughout the Sacramento Valley floor. Clay expands when wet and contracts when dry, and that seasonal movement telegraphs into slab foundations, shifting tile layouts and cracking grout lines in ways that look cosmetic but can signal underlying movement.

Many homes in Citrus Heights were built during the suburban expansion of the late 1950s through the 1970s, when three-piece fiberglass tub-shower combos were standard and bathroom footprints were kept small. The plumbing in those homes — often galvanized supply lines that have long since approached the end of their service life — can complicate a remodel if it hasn't been addressed. Catching corroded supply lines or outdated drain configurations during a bathroom renovation is far less disruptive than discovering them later.

## Our Bathroom Remodeling Process in Citrus Heights

Every project starts with a thorough assessment of what's behind the walls and under the floor, not just what's visible. In Citrus Heights's older ranch-style homes, that means checking for water damage at the subfloor, confirming drain slope and venting before any new shower or tub is set, and identifying whether existing framing can support a heavier tile installation or a freestanding soaking tub.
