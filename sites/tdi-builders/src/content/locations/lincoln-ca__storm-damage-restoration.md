## Why Lincoln Properties See Storm Damage Differently Than the Valley Floor

Lincoln is not Sacramento. The city sits several hundred feet higher in elevation, which means storms that arrive as rain downtown can arrive here as freezing rain or wind-driven sleet that coats rooflines and clogs gutters before the bulk of the precipitation even falls. The newer master-planned communities on Lincoln's western edge were built with concrete tile roofing that handles weight well but is brittle under point-impact from falling branches. Older neighborhoods closer to the historic downtown core — some homes dating to the early twentieth century — often have wood-framed roofs with original skip sheathing underneath, where a single compromised tile or missing shingle can allow water to track laterally across the decking before it ever shows up as a ceiling stain inside.

The region's expansive clay soils compound the problem. When saturation is reached, water has nowhere to go except along foundation walls and into crawl spaces. Homes with older perimeter drainage or no vapor barrier in the crawl space are particularly vulnerable to post-storm moisture intrusion that doesn't announce itself immediately.

## Our Storm Damage Restoration Process in Lincoln

Every storm job begins with a thorough exterior and interior inspection before any materials are moved or discarded. Roof penetrations, soffit damage, and compromised flashing are documented with photographs and moisture readings — both because insurance carriers require it and because visible damage rarely tells the whole story. Water that enters through a cracked fascia board on a Saturday can be wicking through wall cavities by Monday morning.

Once the scope is clear, the sequence is: emergency tarping or board-up to stop active intrusion, structural drying of any wet assemblies using commercial desiccant and refrigerant dehumidifiers, debris removal (including tree limbs and displaced roofing material), and then reconstruction. IICRC-certified technicians oversee the drying phase, following the S500 and S520 standards to determine when materials are genuinely dry rather than surface-dry. Reconstruction — framing, roofing, siding, drywall, paint — is handled in-house, which means one contractor carries the job from emergency response through finished repair.
