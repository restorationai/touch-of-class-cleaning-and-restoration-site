Loomis sits in that particular band of the Sierra Nevada foothills where hot, dry summers push interior temperatures past 100°F and winter storms roll in off the mountains with enough moisture to work into wood framing, stucco, and older foundations. Remodeling a home here isn't the same calculation as remodeling in the flatlands of the Sacramento Valley — the climate swings are wider, the housing stock is a mix of mid-century ranch homes and newer custom builds on larger lots, and the rural-suburban character of the community shapes everything from permit timelines to material choices. TDI Builders, Inc. has been navigating exactly these kinds of conditions since 1985.

## Why Loomis Homes Present Distinct Remodeling Conditions

Placer County's foothill climate is the defining factor for almost every remodeling decision made in Loomis. Summer heat in the 100–110°F range accelerates the aging of roofing underlayment, exterior paint, and wood trim — so when homeowners open up walls for a kitchen or bathroom renovation, it's common to find sun-driven moisture intrusion at west- and south-facing assemblies that looks nothing like the water damage you'd expect from a flat-valley home. Conversely, the area's wet winters — fed by atmospheric rivers that can drop several inches of rain in 48 hours — put real pressure on older drainage grades and crawl spaces.

The housing stock adds another layer. Many homes in the Loomis area were built in the 1970s and 1980s on generous parcels, which means original construction often used materials and techniques that are now a generation out of code: single-pane aluminum windows, undersized electrical panels, and plumbing that predates modern water-efficient fixtures. A whole home remodel here frequently involves bringing those systems up to current California Title 24 energy standards at the same time as the cosmetic and layout work — which affects both budget planning and permit scope.

## Our Home Remodeling Process in Loomis

Every project starts with a thorough walkthrough of the existing structure. In foothill homes, that means paying close attention to the crawl space or raised foundation (common in this area), the condition of the roof deck, and the state of the electrical service — especially if the home was built before the mid-1990s and hasn't had a panel upgrade. We document existing conditions before a single wall comes down, which matters both for permitting with Placer County and for managing the project scope accurately.

From there, the process moves through design coordination, permit submission, demolition, rough trades (framing, electrical, plumbing, HVAC), inspections, and finish work. Placer County's building department serves the unincorporated areas around Loomis, and permit review timelines can vary by season and project complexity. We factor that into the schedule from day one so the inspection sequence doesn't stall the finish trades.

Material selection is also climate-informed. For exterior work, we lean toward fiber cement siding, metal roofing, and composite decking over wood alternatives — not because wood is wrong, but because the UV exposure and temperature cycling in this part of the foothills shortens maintenance cycles significantly. For interior remodels, we pay attention to HVAC zoning, since a home that was built as a single open floor plan often needs ductwork redesigned when walls move.

## Local Note

One thing that catches homeowners off guard in the Loomis area: many of the larger-lot properties along the rural roads northeast of downtown were built with septic systems rather than municipal sewer connections, and those systems are often located in spots that weren't mapped with future additions in mind. Before any remodel that adds square footage, a bathroom, or a laundry room, it's worth verifying the septic capacity and drain field location — because a permitted addition that overloads an undersized system creates a far more expensive problem than the remodel itself. We flag this in the early walkthrough so it doesn't surface as a surprise mid-project.
