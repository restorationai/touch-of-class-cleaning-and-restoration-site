## Why Loomis Homes Present Distinct Remodeling Conditions

The housing stock in Loomis skews older than many people expect. A significant share of the single-family homes in town were built before modern energy codes, which means original kitchens and bathrooms often sit behind walls with little or no insulation, galvanized supply lines that have long since narrowed with mineral buildup, and subfloor systems that weren't designed for the tile or stone a homeowner now wants to install. When you open a wall in a 1968 ranch home here, you're likely to find surprises — not because the original builders did poor work, but because four or five decades of Placer County summers and winters leave their mark.

The climate matters too. Loomis gets hotter and drier than the Sacramento valley floor in summer, and the temperature swings between seasons are wider than coastal California. Wood framing and trim expand and contract noticeably, which is why remodels that don't account for proper expansion gaps in hardwood flooring or exterior trim often show problems within a few years. Clay soils in the area can also shift seasonally, and on sloped lots that's worth evaluating before any addition or structural change goes on the drawing board.

## Our Home Remodeling Process in Loomis

Every project starts with a walk-through that's specific to the property, not a checklist pulled from a generic template. For kitchen remodels, that means looking at how the existing layout interacts with the home's structural walls — in many Loomis ranch homes, the kitchen sits adjacent to a load-bearing wall that runs parallel to the roofline, and opening that space up requires proper beam work, not just removing drywall. For bathroom remodels, it means assessing the existing drain slope and whether the subfloor can support a tile installation without flex.

From there, the process moves through design and material selection, permitting with Placer County, rough-in work, and finish. Our team is licensed and insured, and we pull the permits — homeowners who skip permits on remodel work in Placer County can face complications at resale when title companies flag unpermitted improvements. We coordinate inspections and keep the project moving so that permit delays don't stretch a three-week bathroom remodel into three months.

For whole-home remodels, we phase the work so that the home remains livable where possible, and we're direct about timelines. A full interior remodel on a 1,800-square-foot Loomis home is a multi-month project; we'd rather set that expectation clearly upfront than manage frustration later.
