## What renovations, remodels, and general contracting actually involve

Home renovation services cover a wide spectrum — from a focused bathroom remodel that replaces fixtures, tile, and plumbing rough-in, to a full gut-and-rebuild that strips a room to the studs and rebuilds it to current code. Kitchen remodels typically involve cabinet layout, countertop fabrication, appliance placement, updated electrical circuits for modern loads, and plumbing repositioning if the layout is changing. Bathroom remodels add waterproofing membranes, backer board, and exhaust ventilation to the mix.

Post-damage rebuild work — reconstruction after a water loss, fire, or structural failure — adds another layer: coordinating with insurance adjusters, matching existing finishes, and sequencing trades so drywall doesn't go up before moisture readings confirm the framing is dry. With over 40 years of construction and restoration experience, TDI Builders has handled both sides of that equation.

Timelines vary with scope. A bathroom remodel in a Sacramento-area tract home typically runs two to four weeks once materials are on-site. A full kitchen remodel with layout changes runs four to eight weeks. Larger whole-home renovations or post-damage rebuilds are scoped individually — permit timelines at the local building department factor in, and Sacramento County and city jurisdictions each have their own inspection cadence.

## Our process

1. **Scope and design consultation.** Before a single wall opens, we walk the space with you, document existing conditions, and produce a written scope of work. For post-damage rebuilds, this step includes coordinating with your insurance adjuster and aligning the scope to the claim.

2. **Permitting and pre-construction.** Structural changes, electrical panel upgrades, plumbing relocations, and additions require permits. We pull them, schedule inspections, and keep the project on the right side of Sacramento's building codes — protecting your home's resale value and your homeowner's insurance coverage.

3. **Demolition and rough-in.** Selective demo exposes what's behind the walls: plumbing condition, wiring vintage, insulation gaps, and any hidden moisture or structural issues. Rough-in for electrical, plumbing, and HVAC happens before walls close, with inspections signed off before proceeding.

4. **Finish work and installation.** Drywall, tile, cabinetry, countertops, fixtures, flooring, and paint are sequenced to protect finished surfaces. Subcontractors are scheduled so trades don't back up against each other — a common source of delays on self-managed projects.

5. **Final walkthrough and punch list.** Before we consider a project complete, we walk every item on the scope with you. Anything that doesn't meet the agreed standard gets corrected before final payment and permit close-out.

## What separates a good remodel from a bad one

The most common failures in home remodeling aren't visible on day one — they show up two years later. Tile that cracks because the substrate flexes. A kitchen backsplash that separates because the wall wasn't properly prepared. Cabinets that rack because the floor wasn't leveled before installation.

In post-damage rebuild work, the stakes are higher. Drywall installed over framing that hasn't fully dried creates a mold problem inside the wall cavity. Paint applied over smoke-affected surfaces without proper sealing allows odor to bleed back through. Less-experienced operators close walls to hit a timeline; experienced ones close walls when the conditions are right.

Insurance adjusters reviewing a post-damage rebuild scope look for line-item specificity: materials called out by type and grade, labor separated from materials, and documentation that matches the original loss report. Vague scopes get reduced or disputed. A detailed written scope protects the homeowner's settlement.

Licensed and insured general contractors also carry the liability coverage that protects you if a subcontractor is injured on your property — something unlicensed operators cannot offer.

## Seasonal and regional considerations

Sacramento's dry summers and wet winters affect remodel scheduling in practical ways. Exterior work — siding, roofing, additions — is best sequenced before the November-through-March rain window. Interior projects that open exterior walls, even briefly, need weather protection staged in advance. The Sacramento Valley's clay-heavy soils also mean foundation and drainage conditions should be assessed before any work that affects grade or drainage patterns around the structure.
