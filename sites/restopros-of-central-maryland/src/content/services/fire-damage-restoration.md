## What Fire Damage Restoration actually involves

Fire cleanup is not the same job as a general cleaning service with heavier equipment. Soot behaves differently depending on what burned: a grease fire in the kitchen leaves a different residue than a slow-smoldering electrical fire in a wall cavity, and each type calls for a different cleaning agent and technique. A thorough post-fire restoration covers the visible char and smoke staining, plus the soot that migrated into the HVAC system, behind outlet covers, and into fabric and upholstery throughout the house, even in rooms the fire never reached. Odor removal typically relies on thermal fogging, ozone treatment, or hydroxyl generators depending on how deeply the smoke penetrated porous materials. Most structural fire damage jobs run anywhere from a few days for a single-room smoke event to several weeks when framing, subfloor, or roof structure needs to be opened up and dried or rebuilt.

## Our process

1. **Safety and structural check.** Before anyone starts cleaning, we confirm the structure is safe to enter, check for compromised framing or electrical hazards, and board up any openings left by the fire department.
2. **Soot characterization and pretesting.** We identify whether the residue is dry smoke, wet smoke, or protein residue from a cooking fire, and test cleaning agents on a small area before treating the whole surface, since the wrong chemical can set a stain permanently.
3. **Contents pack-out and inventory.** Salvageable furniture, textiles, and belongings are documented, packed out, and taken off-site for specialized cleaning, which also gets them out of the active work zone.
4. **Structural cleaning and odor treatment.** Walls, ceilings, framing, and ductwork are cleaned with soot-specific agents, then treated with thermal fogging or ozone to break down odor at the molecular level rather than masking it.
5. **Final detail pass and air clearance.** Surfaces are wiped down a final time, HVAC filters are replaced, and the space is checked for lingering odor before contents come back in.

## What separates a good Fire Damage Restoration response from a bad one

The most common mistake is treating fire damage like a cleaning job instead of a chemistry problem. Protein soot from a kitchen fire is nearly invisible but intensely odorous, and wiping it with an all-purpose cleaner often smears it deeper into the surface instead of lifting it. A careful operator pretests cleaning agents on different materials, because what works on painted drywall can discolor wood trim or stain grout. Insurance adjusters pay close attention to whether HVAC ductwork was inspected and cleaned, since recirculated smoke odor is one of the most common reasons a homeowner calls a claim back open after the job is supposedly finished. Acidic soot residue also needs to be neutralized quickly on metal surfaces, hinges, and fixtures, or corrosion sets in within days and turns a cleaning job into a replacement cost.
