**Raw sewage backing up through a drain or toilet in Baldwin?** Call now, we answer 24/7. Sewage contact in a home isn't a cleanup job you wait on. Category 3 water carries bacteria, viruses, and parasites that start posing a real health risk the moment it touches flooring, drywall, or anything porous. Whether it's a sewer line backup from tree roots, a septic overflow after heavy rain, or a failed pump pushing waste back into a basement, the material has to be removed, the area disinfected, and the structure dried before anyone should be walking through it barefoot.

## What Sewage Cleanup and Sanitization actually involves

Sewage cleanup is a different discipline from standard water damage work because everything the water touched is treated as contaminated, not just wet. That means porous materials like carpet padding, drywall below the flood line, and insulation are typically removed rather than dried in place, since they can't be sanitized to a safe standard once saturated with blackwater. Our technicians work in PPE (gloves, respirators, boot covers) and use pumps or wet vacs rated for solids-laden water, followed by EPA-registered disinfectants applied to every surface the water contacted. Depending on the volume of the backup and how long it sat before discovery, a job can run from a single day for a contained bathroom overflow to several days when sewage has traveled under flooring or into wall cavities.

## Our process

1. **Contain and assess.** We identify the source (sewer line, septic, or fixture backup), shut off the path of re-entry where possible, and map how far the water traveled, including under flooring and behind baseboards.
2. **Raw sewage removal.** Standing waste is extracted first using pumps and extraction equipment built for solids, not standard water-damage extractors.
3. **Remove contaminated materials.** Carpet, pad, and any drywall or insulation that absorbed blackwater is cut out and bagged for disposal per local waste handling guidelines, since Category 3 material generally can't be cleaned and reused.
4. **Clean and disinfect.** All remaining surfaces, framing, subfloor, and fixtures are treated with hospital-grade or EPA-registered disinfectants, often in multiple passes.
5. **Dry and verify.** Structural drying equipment runs until moisture readings in the remaining materials confirm the space is dry, with monitoring documented throughout.

## What separates a good sewage response from a bad one

The most common mistake in sewage jobs is treating them like a standard water loss: drying materials that should have been removed, or skipping disinfection steps because the water "looks" cleared. Blackwater contamination isn't visible to the eye, so sanitizing based on what looks clean instead of following Category 3 protocols leaves bacterial residue behind, which can surface later as odor or a health complaint. Insurance adjusters reviewing a sewage claim typically look for documented moisture readings, photos of removed materials before disposal, and a clear disinfection log, not just a before-and-after photo pair. A thorough operator also checks for secondary damage, water that traveled into wall cavities or under cabinetry isn't always obvious from the surface and gets missed if the inspection stops at visible flooring.
