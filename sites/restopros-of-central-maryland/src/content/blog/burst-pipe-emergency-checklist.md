A burst pipe doesn't wait for a convenient time. The first move is always the same: find the main water shutoff valve and turn it off before you do anything else. That stops the flooding at the source. After that, the priority shifts to protecting the structure, your belongings, and anyone in the house, and documenting what happened before you start moving things around. Here's the order of operations, step by step, so you're not guessing while water is spreading across your floor.

## What's Actually Happening When a Pipe Bursts

Most residential pipe bursts come from one of three causes: a freeze crack in an unheated space (crawlspace, attic, exterior wall), age-related corrosion in old galvanized or copper lines, or sudden pressure spikes from a water hammer event. In Baldwin and the surrounding parts of Baltimore County, a lot of the housing stock dates to the mid-century ranch and split-level boom, which means original plumbing runs through crawlspaces and unconditioned basement corners that can drop below freezing during a hard cold snap. A pipe that cracks from freezing often doesn't leak right away either. It's the thaw, hours or days later, that produces the burst.

If you catch the pipe already spraying or pooling, you're not dealing with a slow drip. A fully burst supply line under municipal pressure can push out dozens of gallons a minute, which means the window between "I noticed water" and "it's reached the drywall and flooring" is sometimes measured in minutes, not hours.

## Step-by-Step: What to Do in the First Hour

1. **Shut off the main water valve.** This is usually near where the water line enters the house, in a basement, utility closet, or crawlspace access. Turning off a single fixture's valve won't help if the burst is upstream of it.
2. **Kill the electricity to the affected area** at the breaker panel if water is anywhere near outlets, light fixtures, or electrical panels. Don't do this while standing in water.
3. **Move anything you can lift** (furniture, rugs, electronics, boxes) out of the affected room or up onto blocks.
4. **Start removing standing water** with towels, a wet/dry shop vac, or a mop and bucket. The goal isn't to fully dry the space yourself, it's to stop water from sitting on flooring and wicking into baseboards and subfloor any longer than necessary.
5. **Photograph everything** before you clean up: the source of the break, the water line on the walls, soaked carpet or flooring, and any damaged belongings. This documentation matters if you file an insurance claim.
6. **Open windows or run a fan** if weather allows, to get air moving through the space while you figure out next steps.
7. **Call a plumber** to repair the broken pipe itself. A restoration company handles the water damage; a licensed plumber handles the pipe repair, and you typically need both.

If the burst happened near a washing machine, dishwasher, or water heater supply line rather than inside a wall, the response is the same, but it's worth having that connection inspected afterward. Appliance supply lines fail the same way main lines do, through age, corrosion, or a bad connector, and a slow leak behind a washing machine can go unnoticed for weeks before it's a soaked subfloor.

## What Not to Do

- **Don't run a space heater or heat lamp on wet materials** to "speed up drying." It can create a humid microclimate that actually feeds mold growth in wall cavities instead of drying them.
- **Don't cut into drywall yourself** unless you already know where the shutoff is and the water is off. Opening a wall with water still pressurized behind it turns a bad day into a worse one.
- **Don't wait to see if it dries on its own.** Drywall and framing lumber can read dry on the surface while insulation and the back side of the wallboard stay saturated. That's exactly the condition mold needs, and colonization can begin within 24 to 48 hours in warm, humid conditions.
- **Don't assume carpet padding is salvageable.** Padding acts like a sponge and rarely dries fully in place; in most cases it needs to come out even if the carpet itself can be saved.
- **Don't throw away damaged items before photographing them.** Insurance adjusters generally want documentation of what was lost, not just a verbal description.

## When It's Time to Call a Professional

If the water reached flooring, drywall, baseboards, or insulation, drying it with fans and towels alone usually isn't enough. Moisture can migrate under flooring, behind cabinetry, and into wall cavities where it's invisible from the room. A water damage restoration team uses moisture meters and thermal imaging to find exactly where water traveled, not just where it pooled, then sets up commercial dehumidifiers and air movers to dry the structure to a stable moisture level rather than just a surface-dry appearance.

It's also worth calling in a professional if:

- Water reached an electrical outlet, panel, or appliance
- The burst happened in a finished basement or behind cabinetry where you can't see the full extent of the spread
- More than a few hours passed before you noticed the leak
- You're planning to file an insurance claim and want documented drying and moisture readings as part of that record

## The Recovery Timeline After the Water Is Off

Once the pipe is repaired and standing water is removed, the drying phase typically takes a few days with proper equipment in place, monitored with moisture readings rather than guesswork, since visibly dry doesn't mean structurally dry. Baseboards and drywall near the floor are often removed in strips to let air reach the wall cavity directly rather than trying to dry through intact material. Flooring is the wildcard: hardwood that's cupped or buckled may need to come up entirely, while tile and most vinyl plank can often stay if the subfloor beneath them dries out in time.

After drying is confirmed complete, repairs move to rebuilding: new drywall, baseboard, flooring, and paint as needed. This is also the point where most insurance claims get finalized, since carrier documentation generally wants both the damage record and proof that the space was dried to an acceptable standard before reconstruction began.

A burst pipe is manageable if you move through the steps in order: water off, power checked, water removed, documentation taken, professional help called in before hidden moisture turns into a mold problem.
