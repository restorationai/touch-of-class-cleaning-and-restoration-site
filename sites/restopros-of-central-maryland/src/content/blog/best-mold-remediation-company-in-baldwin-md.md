## What to Ask Before Hiring Any Mold Remediation Company

Whichever company you call, a few questions separate a real remediation plan from a quick bleach-and-go job.

**Will you contain the work area?** Proper mold remediation isolates the affected area with poly sheeting and negative air pressure so spores don't spread into clean rooms during demolition. This is standard practice under the [IICRC S520 Standard for Professional Mold Remediation](https://www.iicrc.org).

**Do you test before and after?** A reputable company can explain the difference between a visual assessment and lab-verified clearance testing. If you're not sure what's actually growing on that wall, see [how to test for mold](/blog/how-to-test-for-mold/) before you pay for remediation you may not need.

**What's actually hidden?** Visible mold is often the smallest part of the problem. If you're seeing isolated spots but suspect more, [7 Signs You Have Hidden Mold](/blog/signs-of-hidden-mold/) covers what's usually growing behind walls and under flooring.

**Is it actually mold, or mildew?** Not every dark spot is toxic black mold. [Black Mold vs Regular Mold](/blog/black-mold-vs-regular-mold/) explains how to tell the difference before you panic or underreact.

The [EPA's mold guidance](https://www.epa.gov/mold) is a good baseline for what remediation should involve, regardless of which company does the work.

Baldwin's older homes and finished basements near Gunpowder Falls hold onto humidity longer than homes on higher, drier ground, which is part of why mold calls here tend to involve basements and crawl spaces more than attics. Whoever you hire, get the remediation plan in writing, including containment, equipment, and post-job clearance testing, before work starts.
