## Restoration emergencies common in Towson

Towson sits inland from the Chesapeake but still catches the remnants of tropical systems that move up through the Mid-Atlantic, along with the heavy convective thunderstorms that roll through Baltimore County most summers. Older sewer and storm infrastructure in parts of the county can struggle to keep up with a fast, high-volume rain event, which is where basement backups and window-well seepage tend to start. Winter brings its own version of the same problem: a hard freeze followed by a quick thaw is a reliable way to find out a supply line has already been cracked for weeks. Between the rain and the freeze-thaw cycle, water intrusion is the call we field most often from this part of the county.

## Building stock, site conditions, and permits in Towson

Towson's housing stock spans a wide range, from early-to-mid-20th-century brick colonials and Cape Cods near the county seat to newer construction and multifamily buildings further out. Basements are common in the older stock, which means groundwater and foundation seepage are a recurring concern during heavy rain, while plumbing in homes from that era is a mix of original galvanized or cast iron and later copper or PEX retrofits, each with its own failure pattern.

Homes built before the 1980s may also contain asbestos-containing materials in older flooring, insulation, or pipe wrap, and pre-1978 construction carries the standard lead-paint considerations any restoration scope has to plan around.
