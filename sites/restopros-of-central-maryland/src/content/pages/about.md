Water finds the weak point in a house, a cracked foundation seam, a failed sump pump, a supply line behind a washing machine, and it doesn't wait for business hours to do it. RestoPros of Central Maryland was built around that reality: a team that answers the phone when a pipe bursts at 2 a.m. or a basement floods during a nor'easter, and that knows the difference between a surface-dry floor and a subfloor that's still holding water three days later.

We're not a general contractor who also does water cleanup on the side. Restoration, specifically water damage response, is the focus, and that focus shapes how the days actually run here.

## What we do

Most calls start the same way: a homeowner who just found standing water, or a property manager staring at a stained ceiling, trying to figure out how bad it is and what happens next. Our job is to answer those two questions fast, then get equipment in place before the damage spreads into drywall, trim, and subfloor that can't be salvaged.

Because we operate 24/7, that often means arriving at odd hours, running moisture readings behind baseboards, and explaining in plain language what's wet, what's salvageable, and what has to come out. A large part of the work happens alongside insurance claims, documenting affected materials, logging drying progress, and communicating with adjusters so the homeowner isn't the one translating technical readings into claim language while they're also trying to keep their household running.

## Our certifications and licensure

Our technicians carry IICRC Water Damage Restoration Technician (WRT) training, the industry standard for how water losses are supposed to be assessed, categorized, and dried. That standard exists because drying a house wrong, pulling equipment too early, missing moisture trapped behind a wall, is how small leaks turn into mold problems months later. The WRT designation means the drying plan behind your job follows a documented, repeatable process rather than guesswork.

## Where we work

We're based in Baldwin, Maryland, and work across the surrounding Central Maryland communities in Baltimore and Harford counties. It's a region of older farmhouses, mid-century ranch homes, and newer subdivisions built on clay-heavy Piedmont soil, which means drainage around foundations varies a lot from one street to the next. Basements here run the gamut from unfinished utility space to fully finished living areas, and that distinction changes how fast a water event needs to be addressed and what's at stake if it isn't.
