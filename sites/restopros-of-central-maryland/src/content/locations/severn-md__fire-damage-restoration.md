## Why Severn Homes Face Unique Fire Damage Challenges

A lot of Severn's housing stock dates to the ranch and split-level boom of the 1960s through 1980s, with slab foundations and centralized HVAC systems that pull soot particles through the ductwork long after the fire is out. Newer homes built closer to the BWI employment corridor tend to use more synthetic materials, laminate flooring, vinyl trim, polyester carpet, which burn hotter and release denser, more corrosive soot than the wood and plaster common in older construction. Combine that with Maryland's humid continental climate, where summer dew points regularly sit in the 60s and 70s, and you get a window where leftover moisture from fire suppression starts feeding mold colonies within 24 to 48 hours if the structure isn't dried properly. We treat both problems on the same visit instead of letting one follow the other.

## Our Fire Damage Restoration Process in Severn

Every call starts with a structural safety check, confirming the roof, load-bearing walls, and electrical systems are stable enough for crews to work. From there we board up compromised openings, isolate the HVAC system to stop soot recirculation, and begin HEPA air scrubbing to pull particulates out of the air before they settle deeper into drywall and insulation. Any water used during firefighting gets extracted and dried following the IICRC S500 drying standard, since trapped moisture behind baseboards and under flooring is often what turns a fire job into a secondary mold job. Odor removal typically means thermal fogging and sealing porous materials that can't be fully deodorized, and when framing, drywall, or subfloor needs to come out entirely, we coordinate directly with rebuild contractors so the homeowner isn't managing two separate crews.
