## Why Columbia Properties See Fire Damage Issues

Columbia's early village construction, built mostly from the late 1960s through the 1970s, used wiring and electrical panel configurations that have since fallen out of code. Homes from that era are more prone to slow-developing electrical fires in attics and wall cavities, the kind that smolder before anyone smells smoke. Newer construction in Columbia isn't immune either, especially in townhome rows and condo buildings where a kitchen fire in one unit can push smoke through shared attic space or a common wall into a neighbor's living space. That shared-wall reality changes how a restoration crew has to scope the job from day one.

## Our Fire Damage Restoration Process in Columbia

We start with a walk-through to separate what's structurally sound from what isn't, then board up or tarp any exposed openings to stop weather and animals from adding a second loss on top of the first. Soot and smoke residue get tested before cleaning begins, since protein smoke from a kitchen fire behaves differently on surfaces than the synthetic smoke from a burned sofa or electrical fire. Structural drying and odor work follow IICRC S500 and S700 guidance, with air scrubbers and thermal fogging brought in once the space is stable. Content cleaning happens off-site when porous materials like upholstery or insulation have absorbed smoke deep enough that in-place cleaning won't fully resolve the odor.

## Columbia Insurance & HOA Coordination

Many Columbia neighborhoods fall under the Columbia Association or an individual village's architectural guidelines, which means exterior repairs, like replacing siding, roofing, or a damaged deck after a fire, often need design review approval before work starts. We document the loss with photos and moisture readings an adjuster can use, and we flag early when a repair is going to need HOA sign-off so that step doesn't stall the job later. On attached townhomes, we also coordinate with neighboring unit owners when smoke or firefighting water has crossed a shared wall, since that often means two separate claims moving on the same timeline.

## Local note

One pattern we watch for in Columbia: townhome rows built with shared attic trusses let smoke migrate two or three units down from the original fire, even when there's no visible damage on the exterior. A homeowner two doors down can have a real smoke odor problem with no scorch marks anywhere in their unit. We check adjoining attic space on any attached-home fire call, not just the unit where the fire started, because skipping that step is how odor complaints come back weeks after a job is marked closed.
