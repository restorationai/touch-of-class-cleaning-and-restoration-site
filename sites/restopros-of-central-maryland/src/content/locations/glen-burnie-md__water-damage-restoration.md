## Why Glen Burnie Properties See Water Damage Issues

Glen Burnie sits in the part of Anne Arundel County where Piedmont clay gives way to the sandier soils of the coastal plain, and that transition matters when it rains hard. Clay-heavy yards drain slowly, so water pools against foundations instead of moving away from the house, and that pressure finds its way into crawlspaces and basement walls through hairline cracks that look harmless until a storm sits over the area for a few hours. A lot of the housing stock here dates to the postwar growth of the 1950s and 60s, which means cast iron and galvanized supply lines that are well past their expected service life. When those pipes finally let go inside a wall or under a slab, the water often tracks along floor joists for yards before it ever shows up as a stain on the ceiling below, so the visible damage is rarely the whole story.

## Our Water Damage Restoration Process in Glen Burnie

We start by finding the water, not just the puddle. Moisture meters and thermal imaging let us trace where water has traveled behind drywall, under flooring, and into subfloor cavities before we pull a single piece of equipment off the truck. Extraction comes next, pulling standing water out of basements, crawlspaces, and carpet pad so structural drying can actually work instead of fighting against a wet slab underneath. For Glen Burnie's slab-on-grade homes, that often means specialized drying mats to pull moisture up through concrete, since a slab can read dry on the surface while it's still holding water several inches down. Dehumidifiers and air movers are placed and logged daily against the IICRC S500 drying standard until moisture content in the affected materials matches the rest of the structure, not just until things look dry to the eye.
