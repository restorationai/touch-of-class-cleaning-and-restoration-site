## Why Gaithersburg Properties See Water Damage Issues

Gaithersburg sits along the I-270 technology corridor, and the housing stock reflects decades of growth: brick colonials and split-levels from the 1960s and 70s sit alongside newer townhome and condo developments built out through the 1990s and 2000s. Older homes in the area often have cast iron or galvanized supply lines that are well past their service life, and basement finishes from different decades mean we frequently find layered flooring (carpet over vinyl over original slab) that traps water instead of letting it evaporate. Montgomery County's clay-heavy soil also swells and contracts with seasonal moisture, which puts stress on foundation walls and is a common contributor to basement seepage after heavy rain. Add in Mid-Atlantic summer storms that can dump an inch of rain in under an hour, and sump pump failures or window well backups become a recurring call type for us in this part of the county.

## Our Water Damage Restoration Process in Gaithersburg

The approach starts the same way on every job: stop the source, extract standing water, and get accurate moisture readings on every affected material before deciding what stays and what comes out. In Gaithersburg's older homes, that often means checking subfloor moisture under multiple flooring layers rather than assuming the surface reading tells the whole story. For newer townhomes with engineered flooring over concrete slab, drying timelines can actually run longer because the vapor barrier under the flooring slows evaporation even after visible water is gone. We dry structures per the IICRC S500 standard, which means documented moisture mapping, not guesswork, and we don't pull equipment until readings confirm the materials are back to a dry standard, not just dry to the touch.

## Gaithersburg Insurance & HOA Coordination

Many Gaithersburg properties, particularly townhomes and condos, fall under an HOA or condo association with its own rules about common-area water damage, shared wall access, and contractor insurance documentation. We document the loss with photos and moisture readings as we go, which supports both your homeowner's claim and any paperwork an association management company requests separately. If the source of the water involves a shared pipe or a neighboring unit, we'll flag that early since it can affect which policy ends up covering what.

## Local note

One thing that catches homeowners off guard in Gaithersburg's townhome communities: shared party walls mean water from one unit's bathroom or laundry leak can migrate laterally into the neighboring unit's framing before it ever shows as a visible stain. We check adjoining walls with moisture meters on these calls even when the visible damage looks contained to one unit, because catching it early avoids a second claim a few weeks later.
