## Why Towson Properties See Mold Growth

Baltimore County sits on heavy clay soil that drains slowly, so basements and crawlspaces built before modern vapor barriers became standard tend to hold groundwater moisture against foundation walls long after a storm has passed. In Towson specifically, a lot of the housing stock near the historic core was built with stone or masonry foundations that were never meant to be fully sealed, which lets humidity migrate inward even without a visible leak. Add in central air systems that cycle on and off instead of running continuously through a Mid-Atlantic summer, and you get the classic combination of a cool surface and warm, humid air: condensation on ductwork, joists, and the back side of finished basement walls. Bathrooms added to older homes without proper exhaust venting are another common culprit we see throughout the area.

## Our Mold Remediation Process in Towson

We start with a moisture map of the affected area, not just the visible mold, because in a lot of Towson's older homes the surface growth is the smallest part of the problem compared to what's trapped in the wall cavity or subfloor. From there we isolate the space with physical containment and negative air pressure so spores don't migrate into the rest of the house while work is underway, then remove and bag porous materials that can't be salvaged, HEPA-vacuum the remaining surfaces, and treat with an EPA-registered antimicrobial. Drying follows the IICRC S520 mold remediation standard, which means we verify moisture content with meters before closing anything back up rather than guessing based on how a surface looks or feels. For homes with older plaster or masonry, that drying phase often takes longer than drywall construction because the material releases moisture more slowly, and we account for that in the schedule instead of pulling equipment early.
