## Why Dundalk Properties See Water Damage Issues

Dundalk was built out quickly in the 1910s and 1920s as housing for shipyard and steelworkers, and a lot of that original housing stock is still standing. Older cast iron and galvanized plumbing in these homes corrodes from the inside out, and a slow leak behind a wall can go unnoticed until flooring starts to cup or a ceiling stain appears below. The area's proximity to the Patapsco River and the Chesapeake Bay also means higher water tables in parts of the community, so basements and below-grade additions take on groundwater during heavy rain even when the roof and gutters are in good shape. Add in Baltimore County's clay-heavy soil, which drains slowly and holds moisture against foundations longer than sandier ground, and you get a community where water intrusion is rarely a one-time event unless the underlying cause gets addressed.

## Our Water Damage Restoration Process in Dundalk

The first thing we do on arrival is find the water, not just the puddle on the floor. In Dundalk's older homes, that often means checking under original hardwood or layered flooring where a previous owner installed laminate or tile directly over the original subfloor, trapping moisture between layers. We extract standing water with truck-mounted and portable pumps, then set containment and air movers based on how the structure actually dries, which is different for solid-wood lath-and-plaster walls than it is for a 1980s addition with drywall and batt insulation. Moisture readings are logged daily and compared against dry standards from the IICRC S500 water damage standard, and we don't pull equipment until the readings confirm the structure, not just the surface, has dried. For homes with finished basements near the river side of town, we pay extra attention to wall cavities and baseboards where wicking can continue well after the floor looks dry.
