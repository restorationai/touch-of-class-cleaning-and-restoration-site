## Why Aspen Hill Homes See Water Damage

Aspen Hill's housing stock is mostly post-war: ramblers, split-levels, and colonials built from the 1950s through the 1970s on Montgomery County's heavy clay soil. That clay doesn't drain the way sandy or loamy soil does, so water from a hard summer storm or a failed WSSC main lingers against foundations longer than it would in newer, better-graded subdivisions. Many of these homes have additions tacked onto original slabs or crawl spaces, which means plumbing runs through at least two different eras of construction, and a supply line failure in one section can soak framing that's nearly impossible to inspect without pulling flooring or drywall. Basements with finished rec rooms, a common feature in this part of the county, trap moisture against carpet pad and baseboards where it's not always visible until the smell shows up.

## Our Water Damage Restoration Process in Aspen Hill

We start with extraction, pulling standing water with truck-mounted or portable pumps depending on how deep it is and whether power is still live. From there we map moisture with meters and, where needed, thermal imaging, because in these older Aspen Hill homes water tracks sideways under slab additions and settles in spots you wouldn't guess from the surface stain. Structural drying follows, with air movers and dehumidifiers set up per the IICRC S500 drying standard, and we recheck moisture content daily rather than guessing when the job's done. If carpet pad or insulation has absorbed water it can't release fast enough, we remove and replace those materials rather than drying around them, since trapped moisture under clay-heavy soil conditions tends to wick back up if it's not fully addressed.
