## Why Wheaton Properties See Water Damage Issues

Wheaton sits in Montgomery County's older inner-suburban ring, where much of the housing stock went up during the 1950s and 60s building boom. Many of these ramblers, split-levels, and colonials were built with partial basements or crawl spaces rather than full slab foundations, and the plumbing and drainage systems installed at the time are now well past their intended service life. Add in the region's expansive clay soil, which swells when saturated and puts lateral pressure on foundation walls, and you get a pattern we see constantly: basement seepage after heavy rain, sewer line backups in older sections of the county, and slow pinhole leaks in copper supply lines that have quietly been losing integrity for years. Humid Mid-Atlantic summers don't help either. Once water gets into wall cavities or subfloor, the same humidity that makes August miserable outside also slows natural evaporation indoors, giving mold a head start if drying isn't handled aggressively and fast.

## Our Water Damage Restoration Process in Wheaton

The first visit is about stopping the loss and understanding where the water actually went, not just where it's visible. We extract standing water with truck-mounted or portable extraction units, then move to moisture mapping with penetrating meters and thermal imaging, because in older Wheaton homes water almost always travels further under flooring and behind baseboards than it appears on the surface. From there we set structural drying equipment (air movers and dehumidifiers) calibrated to the room's size and material type, since plaster, hardwood subfloor, and engineered flooring all release moisture at different rates. We follow IICRC S500 drying standards and log moisture readings daily until materials hit dry targets, not just until things look dry. For homes with finished basements, a common configuration in this part of the county, we also check insulation and sill plates behind drywall before clearing a room, since trapped moisture there is what eventually turns a water job into a mold job.

## Local note

A detail that matters more in Wheaton than in newer parts of the county: many of the crawl space foundations under older homes here were never vapor-barriered when built. When a water loss originates in or near the crawl space, standard surface drying isn't enough, because the exposed soil underneath keeps releasing moisture back into the structure for days after the visible water is gone. We check crawl space humidity levels before closing out a job, not just the living space above it, because skipping that step is one of the most common reasons a "dried" Wheaton basement develops a musty smell a few weeks later.
