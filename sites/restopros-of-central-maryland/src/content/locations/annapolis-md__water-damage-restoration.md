## Why Annapolis Properties See Water Damage Issues

A lot of it comes down to age and elevation. Older homes near downtown were built before modern vapor barriers and modern drainage grading were standard, so water that would run off a newer lot instead pools against a foundation or seeps through old mortar joints. Add Maryland's humid summers and nor'easter-driven winter storms, and a roof or window leak that goes unnoticed for a week has a real head start on both structural saturation and mold growth. Homes closer to tidal water also deal with sewer backup risk during heavy rain events, when storm surge pushes water higher in the system than pipes were sized to handle.

## Our Water Damage Restoration Process in Annapolis

We start with extraction, pulling standing water out with truck-mounted or portable pumps depending on how the property is laid out, since some of the older downtown lots don't give us driveway access for bigger equipment. From there we move to moisture mapping: a moisture meter and thermal imaging tell us what's wet behind the walls, not just what's visibly wet on the surface, which matters a lot in homes with plaster or old lath construction that holds moisture longer than modern drywall. Structural drying follows, using air movers and dehumidifiers set per the IICRC S500 standard, and we track moisture content daily until materials are back to a dry standard rather than guessing on a fixed timeline. The last step is reconstruction scoping, documenting anything that needs to come out versus what can be dried in place.

## Annapolis Insurance & HOA Coordination

Many Annapolis properties, especially condos and townhomes in managed communities, carry HOA requirements around who can perform work and what documentation the association needs before repairs are approved. We photograph and document the loss as we go so that packet is ready whether it's going to your insurance carrier, your property manager, or both. For homes near the water, it's also worth checking whether your loss falls under a standard homeowners policy or a separate flood policy, since the source of the water (storm surge versus a burst supply line) changes which coverage applies, and that distinction affects how fast a claim moves.

## Local note

One thing we watch for in Annapolis specifically: homes built before the mid-1900s near downtown often used horsehair plaster over wood lath instead of drywall, and that plaster can look dry to the touch within a day while the lath behind it is still fully saturated. If drying equipment comes down too early in these houses, mold can start colonizing the hidden wood within 24 to 48 hours even though the wall surface reads fine on a cursory check. We run moisture checks behind the surface layer in these homes rather than relying on surface readings alone, which is part of why drying timelines in the historic district tend to run a little longer than in newer construction.
