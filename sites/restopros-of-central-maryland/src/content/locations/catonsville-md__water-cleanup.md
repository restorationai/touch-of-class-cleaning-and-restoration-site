Runoff doesn't soak into clay the way it does into sandier soil further out in the county, so it pools against foundations, backs up through window wells, and pushes into basements that were built decades before modern waterproofing codes existed. Add a sudden summer downpour or a failed sump pump during a winter thaw, and a dry basement can be standing in an inch of water within the hour.

## Why Catonsville Homes See Water Damage

A lot of the housing stock here, from the bungalows and foursquares near Frederick Road to the mid-century ramblers further out toward UMBC, was built with stone or parged masonry foundations that were never designed to handle today's storm volumes. Those foundations weep and seep rather than leak in one obvious spot, which means water damage often shows up gradually: a musty smell, a soft baseboard, carpet that stays damp two days after a storm has passed. Older cast iron and clay sewer laterals common in this part of Baltimore County are also more prone to root intrusion and collapse, which is a different kind of emergency water cleanup call than a simple appliance or pipe failure, because the water often carries contamination and has to be handled to a different standard.

## Our Emergency Water Cleanup Process in Catonsville

We start by identifying the water category and extracting standing water with truck-mounted or portable extraction units, whichever gets the floor dry fastest given the access at the property. In Catonsville's older homes we pay close attention to plaster and lath walls, which hold moisture far longer than drywall and can read deceptively dry at the surface while the cavity behind them stays saturated. Our technicians use moisture meters and thermal imaging to map what's actually wet, not just what looks wet, then set air movers and dehumidifiers calibrated to the room's size and material mix. Drying is monitored and logged daily against the IICRC S500 standard until materials return to a dry standard, not just until the floor feels dry underfoot.
