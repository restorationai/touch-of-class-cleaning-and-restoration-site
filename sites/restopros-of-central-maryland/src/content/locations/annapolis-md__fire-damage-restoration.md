Annapolis pairs humid, salt-laden air off the Chesapeake with a housing stock that ranges from colonial-era brick townhomes downtown to mid-century ramblers further out toward Parole, and both age brackets complicate a fire recovery in different ways. ## Why Annapolis Properties See Fire Damage Issues

A lot of Annapolis's older housing predates modern electrical code by decades, and knob-and-tube or early romex wiring tucked behind plaster and lath is still common in the downtown core and the neighborhoods immediately around it. Add window-unit AC load in summer and space heater use in a damp winter, and electrical fires show up more than people expect in homes that otherwise look well kept. Wood-burning fireplaces and older chimneys, common in the city's historic brick homes, bring their own slow-burn chimney fire risk, especially after a stretch of damp weather when flue liners and mortar joints have had time to deteriorate.

## Our Fire Damage Restoration Process in Annapolis

We start with a walkthrough to separate structural fire and heat damage from smoke and soot damage, since insurance scoping and repair sequencing depend on that distinction. In older plaster-and-lath construction, which is still common in Annapolis, soot clings differently than it does to drywall, so we adjust cleaning agents and dwell time rather than running the same protocol we'd use on a newer build. Water used in firefighting also needs attention fast: wet plaster in a humid coastal climate stays damp longer than drywall, which means we bring in air movers and dehumidification sized for that extended dry time, not a generic rental-grade setup. Odor control is its own phase, handled with thermal fogging or hydroxyl treatment once materials are dry enough to hold the treatment rather than off-gas it.

## Annapolis Insurance & HOA Coordination

Homes inside or near Annapolis's historic district often carry exterior and sometimes interior restrictions tied to the home's designation, which can affect what materials are approved for rebuild after a fire, particularly on street-facing facades, trim, and roofing. We photograph and document damage with that in mind so the insurance adjuster and any historic review step aren't working from two different stories. Anne Arundel County issues the building permits that apply here, and a fire repair that touches structural framing or electrical will usually need one before reconstruction starts, which we factor into the timeline up front rather than discovering it mid-job.

## Local note

Brick exteriors common in Annapolis's older homes hold heat from a fire longer than vinyl or wood siding, and that retained heat keeps interior humidity elevated for days afterward, which is part of why odor seems to "come back" in these homes even after an initial cleaning. We factor that heat retention into how long we run drying equipment before calling a job closed, rather than pulling equipment on a fixed schedule that works fine for a newer frame house but falls short in a 19th-century brick one.
