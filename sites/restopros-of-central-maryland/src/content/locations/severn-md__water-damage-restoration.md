## Why Severn Properties See Water Damage Issues

A good share of homes in this part of the county were built between the 1960s and 1980s, which means copper and galvanized plumbing that's now past its expected service life. Add in the area's proximity to the Fort Meade / BWI corridor, where development pushed a lot of housing stock up quickly during that same window, and you get a pattern we see often: slow pinhole leaks in copper supply lines that go unnoticed inside a wall cavity until the drywall starts to bubble. Newer townhouse communities closer to Route 32 and Route 175 tend to have better plumbing but often sit on grading that channels storm runoff toward foundations instead of away from them, which shows up as water intrusion at the lowest level of the home, finished basements included.

## Our Water Damage Restoration Process in Severn

The first visit is about stopping the loss and figuring out where the water actually went, not just where it's visible. We shut off the source where possible, extract standing water with truck-mounted or portable extraction units depending on access, and then map moisture behind walls and under flooring with meters rather than guessing from surface appearance. Severn's clay-based soil holds moisture longer after a storm than sandier ground closer to the Bay, so crawlspaces and basement slabs here often read wet on a moisture meter well after the yard looks dry. That changes how long we leave air movers and dehumidifiers running, drying per the IICRC S500 standard means tracking actual moisture content, not a calendar guess, before we call a space dry.

## Local note

One thing that catches a lot of Severn homeowners off guard: basements finished in the 1990s and early 2000s in this area were often built with carpet directly over a concrete slab with no vapor barrier. When groundwater pushes moisture up through that slab after a heavy rain, the carpet pad can wick water laterally for several feet beyond where the floor actually looks wet, which is why we always check moisture readings well past the visible waterline before deciding what stays and what comes out.
