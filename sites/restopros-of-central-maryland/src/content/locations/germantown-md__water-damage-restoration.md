## Why Germantown Properties See Water Damage Issues

Montgomery County sits in Maryland's Piedmont region, where the soil underneath most subdivisions is dense, clay-heavy, and slow to drain. After a hard summer thunderstorm or a slow winter thaw, that soil holds water against foundation walls for days instead of hours, and it finds its way through cove joints, window wells, and sump pit seams in older basements. Germantown's housing stock skews toward split-levels, colonials, and townhomes from the area's main development decades, many with finished basements that were never designed with today's storm volumes in mind. Add in the area's share of aging supply lines and water heaters original to a home's construction era, and you get two very different water damage calls on the same street: a slow failure behind a wall versus a fast basement flood.

## Our Water Damage Restoration Process in Germantown

We start with extraction, pulling standing water with truck-mounted and portable pumps before it has a chance to wick further into drywall, baseboards, and subfloor. From there we moisture-map the structure, checking not just carpet and pad but the framing and slab underneath, because a basement that reads dry at the surface can still be saturated below grade. Structural drying follows the IICRC S500 standard, with air movers and dehumidifiers placed based on the actual moisture readings rather than a guess at square footage. In homes with finished basements, a common Germantown layout, we pay close attention to the wall cavities behind drywall that was installed directly against the foundation, since those cavities trap moisture and are the most common reason a drying job gets extended.

## Local note

One thing that trips up a lot of Germantown homeowners: a finished basement with carpet over a slab will often look dry to the touch within a day of a flood, but the slab itself stays damp for much longer because it has nowhere to release that moisture except straight up through the flooring. We've learned to run moisture meters on the slab itself, not just the carpet pad, before calling a basement dry, because skipping that step is how mold shows up three weeks after everyone thought the job was finished.
