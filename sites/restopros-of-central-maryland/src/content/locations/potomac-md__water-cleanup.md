## Why Potomac Properties See Water Damage

Potomac's housing stock skews toward large colonial and split-level homes built from the 1960s through the 1990s, many with finished basements that double as rec rooms, offices, or guest suites. Those basements are often the first place water shows up, whether from a failed sump pump, a cracked foundation seam, or groundwater pushing in after a heavy rain event. The area's clay-heavy soil doesn't drain quickly, so a saturated yard can keep pressure on foundation walls for days after a storm has passed. Add in the number of properties on well water and septic systems outside the public sewer grid, and a water event here can involve more moving parts than a typical rowhouse flood: a tripped well pump, a failed sump backup battery, or a septic system backing up under sustained rainfall all complicate the cleanup in ways a straightforward municipal-water leak doesn't.

Mature tree cover, one of the things that makes Potomac desirable, also means root intrusion into aging clay or cast iron sewer laterals is a real and recurring cause of backups, especially on properties with mature oaks and tulip poplars planted close to the house.

## Our Emergency Water Cleanup Process

We start with extraction, pulling standing water out with truck-mounted or portable pumps before it has a chance to wick further into drywall, subfloor, and framing. From there we moisture-map the affected rooms, because what looks dry on the surface of a finished basement wall can still be saturated inside the stud cavity. Drying is set up according to the IICRC S500 standard, with air movers and dehumidifiers sized to the room and the materials involved, not a one-size-fits-all setup. On larger Potomac homes with multiple finished levels or a walkout basement built into a sloped lot, that often means staging equipment across more square footage and running longer drying cycles simply because there's more material to dry and less open-air exposure to help it along. We check back daily until moisture readings confirm the structure is dry, not just dry to the touch.

## Local note

One thing we watch for on Potomac calls: homes with septic systems that haven't had a recent pump-out can show symptoms that look identical to a plumbing leak, slow drains, a damp smell near the basement floor drain, even standing water near the cleanout, when the real problem is a septic system that's reached capacity after days of heavy rain. We check for that early, because treating a septic backup like a simple water leak means missing the actual source and seeing the same water damage again within weeks.
