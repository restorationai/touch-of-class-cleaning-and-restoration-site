## When Is It a Restoration Call, and When Is It a Plumber Call?

Call a restoration company the moment you see standing water, a soaked subfloor, or a stained ceiling, because every hour that water sits is an hour it spreads into wall cavities, subfloor, and insulation where you can't see it. Call a plumber first only if water is still actively flowing from a fixture or supply line and nobody has shut off the source yet.

In practice, most Thonotosassa calls need both, just in a specific order. If a supply line under a sink or a washing machine hose has split, shut off the water at the fixture valve or the home's main shutoff, then call the restoration team to start extraction while a plumber schedules the actual repair. If the leak has already stopped (a water heater that failed overnight, an AC condensate line that backed up and overflowed) skip the plumber step entirely for now and go straight to extraction and drying.

Two situations change the order completely. If water is anywhere near an electrical panel, outlet, or light fixture, cut power at the breaker, or call the utility company first if you can't reach the panel safely. If the water is sewage or backed-up from a drain line rather than clean supply water, that's a Category 3 loss under the [IICRC S500](https://www.iicrc.org) standard, also called black water, and it calls for [sewage cleanup](/services/sewage-cleanup/) protocols rather than standard extraction. Don't wade into it or run a shop vac through it.

Many properties around Lake Thonotosassa and the surrounding unincorporated parts of Hillsborough County sit on slab foundations with well and septic systems, which makes groundwater intrusion and septic backups more common here than in sewer-connected neighborhoods closer to Tampa. If you're not sure which type of water you're dealing with, say so on the phone. A good dispatcher will ask the right questions to figure it out before a truck ever leaves the yard.

## What Happens When You Call for Water Damage Restoration?

The first call is mostly information gathering: your address, what caused the water, how long it's been standing, and whether there's any electrical risk. That five-minute conversation determines what equipment loads onto the truck before anyone drives out.

On arrival, a technician walks the property with a moisture meter and documents every affected surface before moving anything, because that documentation is what your insurance adjuster will ask for later. From there the standard sequence is extraction of standing water, placement of air movers and a dehumidifier sized to the affected square footage, and daily moisture readings until the structure hits a normal dry standard. The [EPA](https://www.epa.gov/mold) notes mold can begin colonizing damp material within 24 to 48 hours, which is the real reason restoration crews push to start drying the same day water appears rather than waiting for a scheduled slot.

## Should You Call Your Insurance Company Before or After the Restoration Crew?

Call the restoration crew first, then your insurer once mitigation is underway, because most homeowner policies require you to take reasonable steps to prevent further damage and will expect that work to have already started. Waiting on a claim number before drying anything out usually just adds a day of unnecessary spread.

Having an IICRC Certified Firm on-site from the start also matters for the paperwork side. Moisture logs, photos, and equipment run-time records are exactly what adjusters want to see to approve mitigation costs without a fight.
