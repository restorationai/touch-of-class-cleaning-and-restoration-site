When a pipe bursts, the first two minutes matter more than the next two hours. Shut off your main water supply valve immediately, don't wait to find the source of the break. Then cut power to any rooms where water is pooling near outlets or panels. Those two steps alone limit the majority of secondary damage. Everything after that is about documentation, drying, and deciding what you can handle yourself versus what requires professional equipment.

## Step-by-Step: What to Do Right Now

Work through this list in order. Speed matters, but sequence matters more, doing step 5 before step 2 can make things worse.

1. **Shut off the main water supply.** In most Florida homes, the shutoff is at the meter box near the street or at a valve on the exterior wall near the water heater. Turn it fully clockwise. If you don't know where yours is, find it before you need it.
2. **Cut power to affected rooms.** Water and live circuits are an immediate hazard. Go to your breaker panel and switch off any circuits serving rooms where water is present. If the panel itself is wet or in a flooded area, do not touch it, call your utility provider.
3. **Open a cold-water tap elsewhere in the house.** This relieves residual pressure in the lines and slows any continued dripping from the break point.
4. **Document everything before you touch it.** Walk through with your phone and record video of every wet surface, every visible stain, every buckled floorboard. Your insurance adjuster will want this. Photograph the water meter reading too, it can help establish how much water was lost.
5. **Move valuables and furniture off wet flooring.** Upholstered furniture left sitting in water will wick moisture up through the frame and become a mold source within 24–48 hours. Get it elevated or moved to a dry room.
6. **Begin removing standing water.** Wet-dry vacuums, mops, and towels work for surface water. If you have more than a half-inch of standing water across a large area, a shop vac will take a long time, that's when extraction equipment becomes the practical choice.
7. **Ventilate the space.** Open windows if outdoor humidity is lower than indoor humidity (in Thonotosassa, that's less reliable in summer months, check the outdoor dewpoint before opening up). Run fans across wet surfaces to promote evaporation.
8. **Call your insurance company.** Most homeowner policies cover sudden and accidental pipe bursts. Notify them early so the claim timeline starts. They may send their own adjuster or ask you to use a preferred vendor.

## What NOT to Do After a Pipe Bursts

Some common instincts make the situation worse.

- **Don't use a standard household vacuum on standing water.** It isn't designed for liquid extraction and creates an electrocution risk.
- **Don't run your HVAC system to dry things out** if the air handler or ductwork passed through the flooded area, you risk spreading contaminated moisture or mold spores through the whole house.
- **Don't assume the floor is dry because the surface feels dry.** Water behind plaster or drywall, or beneath tile set on a concrete slab, can read dry at the surface while the substrate stays saturated for days. Moisture meters tell the real story; your hand does not.
- **Don't discard damaged materials before the adjuster documents them.** Pulling up wet carpet and throwing it out before the claim is documented can reduce your payout. Photograph everything, then move it to a garage or exterior area where it can be inspected.
- **Don't wait on mold.** In Florida's humidity, mold colonization can begin in as little as 24–48 hours on wet organic materials like drywall paper, wood framing, and carpet backing. Waiting a few days to "see how things dry" is one of the most common reasons a manageable water loss turns into a remediation project.

## How to Tell When This Is Beyond a DIY Response

You can handle a lot of the immediate steps above on your own. What you generally cannot do without professional equipment:

- **Measure actual moisture content** in walls, subfloor, and framing. Thermal imaging and calibrated moisture meters reveal saturation that looks and feels dry.
- **Extract water from beneath flooring systems.** Water under hardwood, laminate, or tile migrates laterally and pools in low spots, often far from where the pipe broke. Drying mats and floor drying systems pull that moisture out without demolition in many cases.
- **Dry wall cavities.** If water got into a wall, drying the surface does almost nothing for the insulation and framing inside. Structural drying requires injecting conditioned air into the cavity or opening the wall.
- **Establish a drying log for insurance.** Restoration professionals document daily moisture readings across mapped monitoring points. This record is what proves to your carrier that drying was completed to standard, not just that it looked dry.

If the break affected more than one room, if water reached wall cavities, or if you have any reason to believe the pipe was supplying a washing machine, dishwasher, or refrigerator line (which can run for hours undetected), those are situations where professional water damage restoration equipment makes a measurable difference in outcome.

## The Longer Recovery: What Happens After the Water Is Gone

Removing standing water is the beginning, not the end. Structural drying typically takes three to five days with professional equipment running continuously, longer if materials are dense (concrete slab, multiple wall layers) or if the loss was large. During that window:

- **Daily moisture readings** track whether materials are drying on curve. If a reading isn't dropping, the drying plan is adjusted, more airflow, a dehumidifier repositioned, or a wall cavity opened.
- **Antimicrobial treatment** may be applied to surfaces that were wet long enough to be at risk, particularly if the water source was a supply line that ran through a crawlspace or wall where organic debris was present.
- **Reconstruction** begins only after materials reach target moisture content. Painting over wet drywall, re-installing flooring on a wet slab, or closing up a wall before the framing is dry are the shortcuts that produce callbacks six months later.
