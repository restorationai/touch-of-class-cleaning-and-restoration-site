## Restoration emergencies common in Wesley Chapel

Wesley Chapel sits in Pasco County's fastest-growing corridor, and that growth has layered decades of construction side by side: older slab-on-grade homes from the 1980s and 1990s share the same zip codes with brand-new townhomes and large planned communities built in the 2010s and beyond. That mix matters when something goes wrong. Older polybutylene supply lines, common in homes built before the mid-1990s, can fail suddenly and without warning, releasing significant water inside walls before a homeowner notices anything on the surface. Newer construction, while built to more current Florida Building Code standards, often features tightly sealed building envelopes that trap moisture and accelerate mold colonization once a slow leak goes undetected.

Florida's subtropical climate compounds every one of these risks. Afternoon thunderstorms during the June-through-September wet season regularly overwhelm residential drainage and push water through low-lying foundation perimeters. Hurricane-season wind events can compromise roof assemblies, leaving attic insulation and sheathing saturated for days before interior damage becomes visible. The sandy, low-lying soils across much of this part of Pasco County drain poorly in sustained rainfall, and the regional water table rises quickly, sometimes fast enough to force water up through slab cracks during major storm events.
