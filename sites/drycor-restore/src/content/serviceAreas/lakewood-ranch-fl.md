## Restoration emergencies common in Lakewood Ranch

Lakewood Ranch sits in Manatee and Sarasota counties, and the climate here drives a predictable pattern of property damage. The wet season runs roughly June through September, bringing afternoon thunderstorms that can drop several inches of rain in under an hour. That kind of volume overwhelms gutters, overland drainage, and the relatively flat topography that characterizes most of the Ranch's planned communities. Stormwater that finds a gap in a roof flashing, a cracked window seal, or a door threshold can saturate interior assemblies faster than the humidity allows them to dry on their own.

Beyond storm intrusion, the high water table across much of the Sarasota-Manatee corridor means that slab-on-grade foundations sit close to saturated soil for much of the year. A plumbing supply failure or a slow slab leak does not just wet the flooring above it; moisture wicks laterally through the concrete and into adjacent wall cavities before a homeowner notices a soft spot underfoot. Hurricane season adds wind-driven rain to the equation, and post-storm mold colonization can begin within 24 to 48 hours in Florida's heat and humidity if wet materials are not addressed promptly.
