## Restoration emergencies common in Dover

Dover sits in the heart of Hillsborough County's agricultural and suburban transition zone, where flat terrain, clay-heavy soils, and a shallow water table create conditions that amplify water intrusion events. Florida's rainy season, roughly June through September, delivers intense, fast-moving thunderstorms that can drop several inches of rain in under an hour. When that water hits ground that is already saturated, it has nowhere to go except toward the lowest point of a structure: a garage slab, a laundry room, a screened lanai that has been enclosed over the years.

The housing stock in Dover skews toward single-family slab-on-grade construction built from the 1970s through the 2000s, with a meaningful share of manufactured and modular homes on the community's rural edges. Slab foundations eliminate crawlspace flooding but create their own problem: when a supply line fails under the slab, water migrates laterally through the concrete and wicks up into flooring, baseboards, and wall cavities before a homeowner notices anything. That hidden migration is exactly why surface moisture readings alone are not enough, thermal imaging and penetrating moisture meters are standard on every water loss call here.

Hurricane season also brings the threat of wind-driven rain through roof penetrations, soffit damage, and compromised window seals, which can introduce water into wall assemblies that look perfectly intact from the inside.
