## Why Gibsonton Properties See Water Damage Issues

The housing stock along this corridor is a mix of older manufactured homes, mid-century block construction, and newer subdivisions built on fill soil near the bay. Each presents its own challenge. Block construction common in homes built through the 1970s and 1980s wicks moisture into the wall cavity without showing surface staining right away, which means a slow leak behind a bathroom wall can go undetected for weeks. Manufactured homes, which remain common throughout the Gibsonton area, often have vapor barriers and subfloor assemblies that trap moisture once water gets underneath the unit. The combination of high ambient humidity, warm temperatures year-round, and the area's proximity to tidal influence along the Alafia River means that mold colonization can begin within 24 to 48 hours of a water intrusion event. There is no slow season for water damage here.

Flood risk is not uniform across the community. Properties closer to the river's floodplain face different exposure than those on slightly elevated ground further inland, but even the latter can experience water intrusion from storm surge, overwhelmed stormwater infrastructure, or plumbing failures that have nothing to do with weather. The Hillsborough County stormwater system in this area has historically struggled to keep pace with development, and that pattern affects how quickly groundwater recedes after heavy rain events.

## Our Water Damage Restoration Process in Gibsonton

Every job begins with a moisture inspection using thermal imaging and calibrated moisture meters. In Gibsonton's block and manufactured homes, this step is especially important because surface readings can be misleading. A wall that reads dry at the face may still have saturated block cores or compromised subfloor sheathing underneath.

Once the scope is mapped, water extraction comes first. High-capacity truck-mounted extractors remove standing water from flooring, and portable units reach areas where truck access is limited. Structural drying follows using industrial air movers and refrigerant or desiccant dehumidifiers calibrated to the ambient conditions. In Gibsonton's climate, where outdoor dew points run high for most of the year, desiccant dehumidification is often the more effective choice for achieving the vapor pressure differential needed to pull moisture out of dense materials. Drying is documented daily with moisture logs, and equipment is adjusted until readings reach the dry standard required by the IICRC S500 protocol.

For homes with EPA Lead-Safe concerns, which applies to any structure built before 1978, our EPA Lead-Safe Certified team follows the required containment and work practices before any material removal begins. That credential matters in a community where a meaningful share of the housing was built during or before that era.
