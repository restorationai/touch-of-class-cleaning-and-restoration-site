## Our Water Cleanup Process in Temple Terrace

Water cleanup done correctly follows a documented sequence, and that sequence matters more in Temple Terrace's climate than in drier regions where you have more time before secondary damage sets in.

**Inspection and moisture mapping** comes first. Technicians use thermal imaging and calibrated moisture meters to locate water migration behind walls, under tile, and along slab edges, not just the visible wet areas. In concrete block construction common throughout Temple Terrace, this step is especially important because block cavities can channel water horizontally away from the original intrusion point.

**Standing water removal** follows immediately. Truck-mounted and portable extraction units pull bulk water from flooring, crawl spaces, and low-lying areas. The faster standing water is removed, the narrower the window for microbial growth, in Florida's heat and humidity, that window is measured in hours, not days.

**Structural drying** uses industrial air movers and dehumidifiers positioned according to psychrometric calculations, not guesswork. Drying logs are maintained throughout the process so there is a documented record, useful for insurance claims and for confirming the structure is genuinely dry before any reconstruction begins.

**Final verification** confirms moisture readings have returned to acceptable baselines for the specific materials present. No job is closed out on a visual inspection alone.

The work is performed by an IICRC Certified Firm with technicians holding IICRC WRT (Water Restoration Technician) certification, and the company is EPA Lead-Safe Certified, relevant in Temple Terrace's older housing stock where disturbing building materials during water cleanup can expose lead-containing paint.

## Local Note

One thing that catches homeowners off guard in Temple Terrace's mid-century concrete block homes: the terrazzo flooring that's common in that era is nearly impermeable on its surface, which sounds like good news during a water event. It isn't. Water that gets under terrazzo has nowhere to evaporate upward, it migrates laterally under the slab finish and can stay trapped for weeks, keeping the subfloor environment saturated long after the terrazzo surface reads dry underfoot. Proper drying in these homes requires floor drying mats or the controlled injection of dry air beneath the surface, not just air movers pointed at the floor. Skipping this step is one of the most common reasons Temple Terrace homeowners end up with mold problems weeks after a water loss that seemed minor at the time.
