Siesta Key sits on a narrow barrier island off Sarasota's coast, and that geography makes storm damage here a different animal than what inland Florida properties face. When a hurricane or severe tropical system pushes ashore, the island absorbs wind from multiple directions simultaneously, salt-laden air accelerates corrosion of exposed fasteners and flashing within hours, and storm surge can push water under doors and through weep screed before a homeowner has finished moving valuables to higher ground. DRYCOR RESTORE responds 24/7 and holds IICRC certification, so when the storm clears and you can finally get back across the bridge, a trained crew is ready to meet you.

## Why Siesta Key Properties Are Especially Vulnerable to Storm Damage

Barrier island construction in this part of Sarasota County follows a logic shaped by decades of hurricane code evolution, but that means the housing stock is genuinely mixed. Older cottages and mid-century concrete block homes built before modern wind-load requirements sit alongside newer elevated structures engineered to current Florida Building Code standards. The older block homes often have flat or low-slope roofs that shed water poorly under sustained wind-driven rain, and their original single-pane jalousie or aluminum-frame windows offer far less resistance to debris impact than contemporary impact glass.

Beyond the structures themselves, the island's sandy, low-elevation soil drains slowly when saturated. After a significant storm, groundwater can wick upward into slab foundations, and pooled water in carports and enclosed lower levels can remain for days. Salt air compounds every surface it touches: roof decking, fascia boards, and exposed metal connectors begin oxidizing almost immediately after storm exposure, which means secondary damage accumulates faster here than in inland Sarasota neighborhoods.

## Our Storm Damage Restoration Process in Siesta Key

The first priority after any storm event is a systematic exterior and interior assessment before any debris is moved or materials are discarded. Roof penetrations, soffit separations, and window failures are documented photographically because insurance carriers and Florida's Citizens Property Insurance claims process both require detailed pre-mitigation evidence. Once documentation is complete, emergency tarping and board-up protect the structure from additional rain intrusion while the interior assessment begins.

Inside, moisture mapping with thermal imaging and pin-type meters identifies where water has traveled beyond the obvious wet areas. In Siesta Key's older concrete block homes, water that enters through a compromised roof or window often tracks down interior CMU walls and pools in the floor assembly before it becomes visible. Drying equipment, including high-capacity dehumidifiers and directional air movers, is positioned based on that moisture map rather than on guesswork. Affected materials are evaluated against IICRC S500 and S520 standards to determine what can be dried in place and what requires removal.

Tree damage cleanup is coordinated alongside structural work. Fallen palms and oak limbs that have punctured roofing or damaged soffits need to be removed in sequence with tarping so the opening is never left unprotected between steps.

## Siesta Key Insurance and HOA Coordination

Many properties on Siesta Key carry Citizens Property Insurance or a private wind carrier alongside a separate flood policy through the National Flood Insurance Program. Storm damage claims here frequently involve both policies simultaneously, which means the scope of work has to be clearly divided between wind-driven damage and flood-related damage from the start. DRYCOR RESTORE documents losses in a format that supports that dual-claim structure, photographs materials before removal, and provides the moisture readings and drying logs that adjusters require.

Condominium associations along the island's Gulf-front corridors typically have master policies that cover the building envelope, while unit owners carry HO-6 policies for interior finishes. Knowing which policy responds to which scope of damage before work begins prevents disputes at closeout. The crew communicates with association property managers as well as individual owners when the job involves a shared structure.
