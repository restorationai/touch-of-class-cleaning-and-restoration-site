## Why Nokomis Properties Present Unique Renovation Challenges

The housing stock in and around Nokomis skews older, with a significant portion of single-family homes built between the late 1960s and early 1990s. That era of Florida construction came with its own standards: terrazzo floors poured over concrete slabs, jalousie windows long since replaced (or not), and plumbing that may have been updated piecemeal over the years. Beneath the surface, the sandy, low-lying soil common to this part of Sarasota County drains poorly during heavy rain events, and slab foundations in flood-prone areas can shift subtly over time, creating gaps at wall bases that complicate both waterproofing and finish work.

Salt air accelerates corrosion on fasteners, flashing, and metal fixtures in ways that contractors from inland markets don't always anticipate. A bathroom remodel that looks straightforward on paper can reveal corroded supply lines and rotted subfloor once the old tile comes up. Kitchens in homes this age often have original cast-iron drain stacks that need assessment before any cabinet or flooring work begins. These aren't surprises to a team that has worked Sarasota County for years.

## Local Note

One thing worth knowing about Nokomis-area homes built on or near the coastal ridge: the original construction often used concrete block with a stucco exterior finish, and that stucco has frequently been repainted multiple times over the decades. When renovation work requires cutting into exterior walls, those layers can conceal moisture intrusion that has been slowly wicking into the block cores. A visual inspection from the outside won't catch it. Probing with a moisture meter at the base of walls and around window frames before finalizing a scope is standard practice here, not an upsell. It prevents a remodel from uncovering a remediation problem mid-project.
