## Why Lake Wales Properties See Water Damage Issues

The geography of Lake Wales works against homeowners when it rains. The city sits along the Lake Wales Ridge, a sandy, well-drained spine of Central Florida, but that drainage advantage disappears quickly in low-lying areas near the chain of lakes that defines the region. During the June-through-September rainy season, daily rainfall totals can exceed two inches in a single storm, and older drainage infrastructure in established residential areas can back up fast.

The local housing stock adds another layer of complexity. Many homes in and around Lake Wales were built in the mid-twentieth century, when slab-on-grade construction was common but vapor barriers and modern waterproofing were not. Concrete slabs in this era often lack adequate moisture protection underneath, which means groundwater and storm runoff can wick upward through the slab itself after a prolonged wet period. Stucco exteriors, common throughout the area, can trap moisture behind the finish coat when window seals or roof flashing fail, hiding saturation that only shows up on a moisture meter.

Central Florida's humidity also means that even a slow plumbing leak, a supply line under a bathroom vanity, a failing wax ring, a pinhole in copper pipe, can fuel mold colonization within 24 to 48 hours of the initial wetting.

## Our Water Damage Restoration Process in Lake Wales

Every job begins with a full moisture assessment. Our IICRC-certified technicians use thermal imaging cameras and calibrated moisture meters to map saturation behind walls, under flooring, and inside cavities that look dry to the eye. In Lake Wales homes with older stucco or block construction, this step is especially important because surface readings can be misleading.

Once we know the full scope, water removal comes first. We use truck-mounted and portable extraction units to pull standing water from flooring, subfloor assemblies, and low-lying areas. After extraction, we set a drying system calibrated to the structure: high-capacity dehumidifiers to pull moisture from the air and structural cavities, and air movers positioned to accelerate evaporation from surfaces. Drying is monitored with daily readings and adjusted until materials reach their target moisture content, documented per the IICRC S500 standard.

For homes with hardwood floors, tile over mortar beds, or older interior plaster, we adjust dwell times and equipment placement accordingly. These materials release moisture more slowly than modern drywall, and rushing the drying phase leads to callbacks.
