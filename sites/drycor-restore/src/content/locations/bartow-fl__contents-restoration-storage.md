Bartow sits in the heart of Polk County, where summer humidity rarely drops below oppressive and afternoon thunderstorms can push standing water into a home faster than a sump pump can react. When a flood, fire, or mold event hits a property here, the damage rarely stops at the walls. Furniture, documents, family photographs, electronics, and clothing absorb smoke odor and moisture just as readily as drywall does. Contents restoration and storage is the part of recovery most homeowners don't think about until they're standing in a saturated living room wondering what can actually be saved.

## Why Bartow Properties Face Particular Contents Damage Risks

Polk County's climate creates a specific set of conditions that accelerate secondary damage to personal property. The combination of high ambient humidity, intense UV exposure, and the region's clay-heavy soils, which drain slowly and push groundwater toward slab foundations during heavy rain events, means that contents sitting in a compromised space don't just get wet once. They stay wet. Mold can begin colonizing porous materials like upholstered furniture, cardboard boxes, and clothing within 24 to 48 hours of initial exposure, and in Bartow's summer heat, that window closes fast.

Older homes throughout the city, many built during Bartow's growth periods in the mid-20th century, often feature original hardwood floors and plaster walls that hold moisture differently than modern construction. When water migrates through those materials, it reaches contents stored in closets and lower cabinets without any visible surface sign. By the time a homeowner notices the smell, the damage to contents has already progressed well beyond what a simple wipe-down can address.

## Our Contents Restoration Process, Calibrated for Bartow Conditions

The process begins with a detailed inventory before anything leaves the property. Every item is photographed, catalogued, and assigned a condition rating. This documentation matters for insurance claims and gives homeowners a clear record of what was removed and where it went.

From there, contents travel to a climate-controlled facility where the real work happens. Upholstered furniture and textiles go through ozone treatment and thermal fogging to neutralize smoke and microbial odors, not just mask them. Electronics are assessed for corrosion and moisture intrusion before any power is applied. Documents and photographs, particularly fragile in Bartow's humidity, receive specialized drying and stabilization rather than standard airflow methods that can cause paper to warp or ink to bleed.

Structured drying equipment, including industrial dehumidifiers calibrated to the vapor pressure conditions common in Central Florida, runs continuously in the storage environment. Items are not returned to a property until the structure itself has been cleared and verified dry. Returning contents to a space that still holds elevated moisture simply restarts the damage cycle.

Because DRYCOR RESTORE operates 24/7 and is IICRC certified, the same standards that govern structural drying apply to how contents are handled and documented throughout the process.

Contents claims are often the most contested part of a property loss settlement.
