## Our Contents Restoration & Storage Process

The first step on any contents job is a room-by-room inventory. Every item is photographed, catalogued, and assessed for restorability before anything leaves the home. This documentation matters for insurance claims and gives homeowners a clear record of what was affected.

Items that can be restored are packed out and transported to a climate-controlled storage facility. There, depending on the type of loss, contents may go through ultrasonic cleaning (effective for soot and residue on hard surfaces), thermal fogging or ozone treatment for smoke odor, or controlled drying for water-saturated items. Soft goods, upholstery, area rugs, clothing, are assessed individually; some respond well to professional laundering and deodorization, while others require specialized dry-cleaning referral.

Documents and photos that have been wet are among the most time-sensitive items. Freeze-drying is the most reliable method for stabilizing water-damaged paper before restoration, and timing matters: paper that air-dries without intervention often warps and fuses permanently within hours. Electronics are set aside for professional assessment before any power is applied, energizing a water-damaged device before it is fully dried can cause irreversible damage.

Once the home itself has been remediated and cleared, contents are returned, unwrapped, and placed according to the homeowner's direction.
