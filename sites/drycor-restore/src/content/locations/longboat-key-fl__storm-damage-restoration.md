Longboat Key sits on a narrow barrier island between Sarasota Bay and the Gulf of Mexico, which means every named storm and severe weather event hits from multiple directions at once. Wind-driven rain finds its way under roof tiles, through aging window seals, and into the concrete block construction that defines much of the island's mid-century housing stock. When a storm moves through, the damage is rarely just cosmetic. DRYCOR RESTORE responds 24/7 to storm damage calls on Longboat Key, working to stop secondary damage before the subtropical humidity turns a structural problem into a mold problem.

## Why Longboat Key Properties Face Distinct Storm Damage Challenges

Barrier island geography concentrates storm risk in ways that mainland Sarasota County properties simply do not experience. Storm surge can push water inland from both the Gulf and the bay side simultaneously, leaving low-lying lots with standing water that has nowhere to drain quickly. The island's sandy soil drains reasonably well under normal conditions, but a sustained tropical system overwhelms it, and water backs up against foundations and into garages.

The housing stock adds another layer of complexity. A large share of Longboat Key's residential and condominium buildings date from the 1960s through the 1980s. Concrete block construction from that era is durable, but the original stucco coatings and window glazing were not designed to meet current Florida Building Code wind-load standards. Hurricane-force gusts can breach those older envelope details and introduce water into wall cavities that are difficult to access and slow to dry. Flat or low-slope roofing common on mid-century Gulf-front properties is particularly vulnerable to ponding after a storm strips or displaces roofing membrane.

## Our Storm Damage Restoration Process on Longboat Key

The first priority after a storm is stopping ongoing intrusion. That means emergency tarping of compromised roof sections, boarding damaged windows and doors, and extracting any standing water before it migrates into wall assemblies or under tile flooring. Longboat Key's high ambient humidity means that even a small amount of trapped moisture can support mold colonization within 24 to 48 hours of a weather event, so drying timelines here are tighter than in drier climates.

Once the structure is stabilized, the process moves to systematic moisture mapping. Our IICRC-certified technicians use thermal imaging and calibrated moisture meters to locate water that has wicked behind stucco, under slab tile, or into the concrete block itself. Drying equipment is positioned based on that mapping, not on a generic layout, because the dense wall assemblies common on the island require directed airflow to be effective. Structural repairs, debris removal, and coordination with roofing and glass subcontractors follow once drying readings confirm the structure is within acceptable limits.
