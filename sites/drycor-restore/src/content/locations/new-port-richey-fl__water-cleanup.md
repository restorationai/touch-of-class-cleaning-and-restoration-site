## Why New Port Richey Properties See Water Damage Differently

Pasco County's coastal geography puts New Port Richey in a particularly vulnerable position. ## Our Water Cleanup Process in New Port Richey

When a crew arrives, the first priority is understanding where the water actually is, not just where it's visible. Moisture meters and thermal imaging cameras map the extent of saturation behind walls and under flooring before a single piece of equipment is placed. That step matters here because Gulf Coast humidity means ambient moisture levels are already elevated; a reading that looks borderline in a dry-climate state can represent active intrusion in a New Port Richey home.

Extraction comes next. High-capacity truck-mounted and portable extractors pull standing water from hard surfaces and draw moisture from carpet padding and subfloor materials. Once bulk water is removed, drying equipment, industrial air movers and low-grain refrigerant dehumidifiers, is positioned to create a controlled drying environment calibrated to the outdoor dew point, which in summer can exceed 70°F in this region. Crews return daily to log moisture readings and adjust equipment placement until structural materials reach target dryness levels per the IICRC S500 standard.

Throughout the process, photo documentation and moisture logs are compiled in a format that supports insurance claims, room-by-room, date-stamped, and tied to equipment placement records.

## Local Note

Homes built near the Pithlachascotee River corridor and in the older sections of New Port Richey closest to downtown often have terrazzo flooring, a mid-century staple in Florida construction. Terrazzo is poured directly over the slab with no air gap, which means water that gets beneath it has nowhere to evaporate upward. Drying terrazzo correctly requires specialty drying mats placed directly on the surface to draw moisture through the stone rather than relying on airflow alone. Skipping that step and running standard air movers overhead can leave a slab reading elevated for weeks, creating conditions where mold can establish in wall cavities even after the visible surface looks dry.
