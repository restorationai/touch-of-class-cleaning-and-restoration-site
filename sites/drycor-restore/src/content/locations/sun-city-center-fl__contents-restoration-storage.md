## Why Sun City Center Homes Face Elevated Contents Risk

Sun City Center sits in Hillsborough County's low-lying coastal plain, where the water table runs shallow and afternoon thunderstorms between June and October can drop several inches of rain in under an hour. Homes built in the 1970s and 1980s, which make up a large portion of the housing stock here, often have slab foundations with minimal elevation above grade. That means floodwater doesn't have far to travel before it's inside a living room, soaking the base of a china cabinet or wicking up through carpet into the bottom shelves of a bookcase.

The climate adds another layer of urgency. At Sun City Center's typical summer humidity levels, mold can begin colonizing porous materials within 24 to 48 hours of a water event. Upholstered furniture, paper documents, and wooden cabinetry are all vulnerable. The same warm, moist air that makes the community comfortable for residents makes it aggressive on water-damaged belongings.

## Our Contents Restoration Process in Sun City Center

The first step is an on-site inventory. Every affected item is photographed, logged, and assessed before anything leaves the home. That documentation matters for insurance claims and gives residents a clear record of what was removed and where it went.

Items that can be restored are packed and transported to a climate-controlled facility for professional cleaning, deodorization, and drying. Smoke-damaged items go through thermal fogging and ozone treatment to neutralize odor at the molecular level rather than masking it. Water-damaged documents and photographs receive specialized drying techniques, freeze-drying for severely saturated papers when appropriate, because those items are often the ones residents are most worried about.

During the home restoration phase, contents are stored securely in our controlled environment. Once the structure is cleared, items are returned, and the final inventory is reconciled with the homeowner item by item.
