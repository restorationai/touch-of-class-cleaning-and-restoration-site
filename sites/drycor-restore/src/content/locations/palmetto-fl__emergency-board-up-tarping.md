## Why Palmetto Properties Face Elevated Board-Up and Tarping Risks

The Gulf Coast climate is the obvious factor, hurricane season runs June through November, and Manatee County sits squarely in the cone for storms tracking up from the Gulf or across the peninsula. But the risk isn't limited to named storms. Palmetto's position along the Manatee River corridor means that fast-moving squall lines can produce localized wind damage intense enough to strip shingles, crack soffits, or push a large oak limb through a roof deck before any formal storm warning is issued.

The local housing stock adds another layer of complexity. Much of Palmetto's established residential fabric, particularly in older subdivisions closer to the river and the downtown core, includes homes built with wood-frame construction and original single-pane windows that offer less resistance to wind pressure than modern impact-rated systems. A fire in one of these homes can leave exterior walls structurally sound but with every window opening exposed. A break-in on a vacant rental property can leave a sliding door or jalousie window frame shattered and unsecured. In each scenario, the opening needs to be covered before weather, pests, or unauthorized entry cause further damage.

Fire damage board-up in Palmetto also carries a specific consideration: older wood-frame homes can have irregular stud spacing and non-standard window dimensions that require on-site cutting and fitting rather than pre-cut panel installation. A crew that shows up with a one-size approach will leave gaps.

## Our Emergency Board-Up and Tarping Process in Palmetto

When a call comes in, the first question is what type of breach we're dealing with, roof, window, door, or a combination, because that determines what materials and crew size we dispatch. For roof tarping, we use heavy-duty polyethylene tarps weighted and fastened with lumber battens rather than just bungee-strapped over the ridge, which is the method most likely to fail when the next wind event arrives. The tarp is secured to undamaged decking, not to damaged or softened sheathing, and it's extended past the ridge line to prevent water from wicking underneath.

For window and door board-up, we cut OSB panels to fit each opening, secure them with structural screws into the framing (not just the siding or trim), and mark each panel with DRYCOR RESTORE contact information and the date of installation, a detail that matters for insurance documentation and for any subsequent contractor who needs to know when the opening was secured. On fire-damaged structures, we assess load-bearing conditions before sending anyone onto the roof or into the interior, and we coordinate with the fire marshal's office if the scene hasn't been formally released.

Every job is photographed before, during, and after installation. That photo set becomes part of the claim file.

## Insurance and Documentation in Palmetto

Most homeowners' policies in Florida cover emergency protective measures, board-up and tarping are typically classified as "reasonable steps to protect property from further damage" and are reimbursable under the dwelling coverage portion of a standard HO-3 policy. The documentation we provide (timestamped photos, material invoices, a written scope of work) is formatted to support a carrier's adjuster review rather than create additional back-and-forth. We bill major carriers directly and can communicate with your adjuster on the scope of the emergency work so you're not playing intermediary.

If your property is in a Palmetto HOA community, some associations have aesthetic rules about how long temporary board-up materials can remain visible from the street. We note the installation date on every panel precisely because HOA compliance timelines often start from that date, and your association management company may need it.

## Local Note

Palmetto's older neighborhoods near the Manatee River waterfront include a number of homes with original jalousie windows, the louvered glass-slat style common in Florida construction from the 1950s through the 1970s. When a jalousie frame is damaged, the individual glass slats fall out rather than breaking in place, leaving a large irregular opening that doesn't accept a standard rectangular OSB panel cleanly. Our crews carry aluminum flashing and foam backer rod specifically to seal the frame perimeter on these openings before the panel goes up, preventing wind-driven rain from entering around the edges. It's a small detail that makes a real difference in how dry the interior stays until permanent repairs begin.
