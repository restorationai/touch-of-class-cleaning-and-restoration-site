Longboat Key sits on a barrier island where salt air, high humidity, and the ever-present threat of storm surge shape every building decision. Renovating or remodeling a home here is not the same project it would be two miles inland on the mainland. Whether you are updating a Gulf-front kitchen, converting a screened lanai into conditioned living space, or rebuilding after wind or water damage, the materials you choose, the permits you pull, and the sequencing of every trade have to account for a coastal environment that does not forgive shortcuts. ## Why Longboat Key Renovations Require a Different Approach

Barrier island construction sits in a category of its own under Florida Building Code. Properties on Longboat Key fall within the High-Velocity Hurricane Zone (HVHZ) or are subject to coastal construction setback requirements enforced by Sarasota County, depending on the parcel. That means window and door replacements must meet impact-resistance specifications, roofing systems require specific fastening patterns, and any project that touches the building envelope triggers a more detailed permit review than you would see on a standard inland remodel.

Beyond code, the salt-laden air accelerates corrosion on fasteners, flashing, and HVAC components. Older condo buildings and single-family homes built in the 1970s and 1980s, which make up a significant portion of the island's housing stock, often have aluminum wiring, galvanized plumbing, and original single-pane windows that complicate renovation scopes. Opening a wall to update a bathroom can reveal conditions that require coordination with a licensed electrician or plumber before finish work can proceed. A general contractor who has not worked in this environment will underestimate the scope almost every time.

## Our Renovation and Remodeling Process on Longboat Key

Every project starts with a detailed walkthrough and written scope. For post-damage rebuilds, that scope is built directly from the drying and remediation documentation so nothing falls through the gap between mitigation and reconstruction. For elective remodels, kitchen renovations, bathroom updates, and structural changes, we develop a phased plan that accounts for permit lead times with the Town of Longboat Key's Building Department before a single subcontractor is scheduled.

Permit coordination on the island takes planning. The Town of Longboat Key has its own building department, and inspection scheduling on a barrier island with limited access points means that delays compound quickly if the permit is not in hand before materials are ordered. We manage the application, the plan review correspondence, and the inspection schedule so the project moves in sequence rather than stalling between trades.

Once permits are active, we coordinate licensed subcontractors for electrical, plumbing, HVAC, and specialty finishes, and we maintain a single point of contact for the homeowner or property manager throughout. For occupied properties, we schedule trades to minimize disruption, particularly during the busy season when many owners are in residence from November through April.

Our crews travel to Longboat Key via the most direct route available: south on I-75 to the Sarasota area, then west across the Cortez Bridge or the Longboat Pass Bridge depending on which end of the island the project is located. For projects in the mid-island or southern sections, the Cortez Bridge or the Ringling Causeway connection through St. Armands Circle is the standard route. ## Local Note: What Island Contractors Know About Slab and Stucco

Many of Longboat Key's mid-century and 1970s-era homes were built on concrete slab with exterior stucco finishes that have been repainted and patched over decades. When a renovation opens an exterior wall or a bathroom tile is removed, it is common to find that moisture has been wicking into the stucco assembly for years, sometimes without any visible interior sign. Our team is EPA Lead-Safe Certified and IICRC certified, so when a remodel scope intersects with older materials or hidden moisture, we can assess and address those conditions under the same project umbrella rather than stopping work and handing the homeowner a separate contractor to find. That continuity keeps the project moving and prevents the scope gaps that drive cost overruns on island renovations.
