## Why Brandon Properties Experience Sewage Backups

Brandon's residential growth accelerated through the 1970s and 1980s, which means a significant portion of the housing stock sits on sewer laterals that are now four to five decades old. Clay and Orangeburg pipe, common in homes built before the mid-1980s, deteriorates, cracks, and root-infiltrates over time. When roots from the mature oaks and laurel figs that shade older Brandon streets find a joint in an aging lateral, they can obstruct flow gradually until a heavy rain event overwhelms the system entirely.

Brandon also sits within a region where the water table can be surprisingly shallow, particularly in lower-elevation pockets. During Hillsborough County's wet season, roughly June through September, saturated soil reduces the absorption capacity around septic drain fields, contributing to septic overflow events that push effluent back toward the structure. Homes on septic in the outer edges of Brandon's unincorporated areas are especially susceptible after back-to-back storm days.

Flat topography compounds the issue. Unlike coastal areas with natural drainage gradients, much of Brandon relies on engineered retention and drainage infrastructure. When that infrastructure is overwhelmed, stormwater can infiltrate sewer cleanouts and push contaminated water into lower-level spaces.

## Our Sewage Cleanup and Sanitization Process in Brandon

Sewage is classified as Category 3, the most contaminated water type, and it requires a different response than a clean water pipe burst. The process we follow is calibrated to that reality:

**Containment first.** Before extraction begins, affected areas are isolated to prevent cross-contamination into unaffected rooms. In Brandon's ranch-style homes, where open floor plans mean a single bathroom backup can threaten adjacent hallways and living areas, this step matters more than it might in a multi-story home with natural compartmentalization.

**Extraction and solid removal.** Standing sewage and solid waste are removed using truck-mounted extraction and containment protocols that comply with Hillsborough County's requirements for biohazardous waste disposal.

**Structural assessment.** Porous materials, drywall, subfloor, insulation, carpet, that have been contacted by Category 3 water are evaluated for removal. In Brandon homes with wood subfloor over a crawl space or slab, we use moisture meters and thermal imaging to map contamination that isn't visible at the surface.

**Antimicrobial treatment and sanitization.** After affected materials are removed, all surfaces are treated with EPA-registered disinfectants. This step addresses bacteria, viruses, and pathogens present in raw sewage. We do not release a space for reoccupation until surface testing confirms the sanitization is complete.

**Drying and documentation.** Any residual moisture in structural cavities is addressed with commercial drying equipment. Full photo documentation is produced throughout, critical for insurance claims.
