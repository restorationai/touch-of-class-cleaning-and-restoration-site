## Why Brandon Properties See Mold Issues

Brandon sits in one of the most mold-hospitable climates in the continental United States. Summer relative humidity routinely climbs above 90 percent, and the area's flat topography means stormwater drains slowly. Homes built on concrete slabs, the dominant foundation type throughout Brandon's residential neighborhoods, are particularly vulnerable when the vapor barrier beneath the slab degrades or was never installed to modern standards. Moisture wicks upward through the concrete, saturates flooring adhesive, and provides exactly the dark, damp substrate that Cladosporium, Penicillium, and Stachybotrys (commonly called black mold) need to establish.

Brandon also experienced significant residential growth in the 1970s through the early 1990s, and homes from that era frequently have original ductwork that runs through unconditioned attic space. When the air handler cycles off on a humid afternoon, warm moist air can condense inside those ducts and drip into ceiling cavities, a pattern that often goes undetected until a homeowner notices discoloration on a ceiling tile or a persistent musty odor in a back bedroom.

## Our Mold Remediation Process in Brandon

Mold remediation is not the same as mold removal. Scrubbing a surface without addressing the underlying moisture source leaves the problem intact; the colony returns, often faster the second time because the spore load in the building has already been disturbed.

Our process follows the IICRC S520 Standard for Professional Mold Remediation and is calibrated for Brandon's climate conditions:

1. **Moisture mapping and source identification.** We use thermal imaging and calibrated moisture meters to locate water intrusion points, not just the visible growth. In Brandon's slab homes, this often means checking the perimeter of the slab, the base of exterior walls, and the area around sliding glass doors, which are common condensation points in Florida construction.
2. **Containment.** Affected areas are isolated with negative air pressure and polyethylene barriers to prevent cross-contamination to unaffected rooms. Given how open-plan most Brandon homes are, proper containment is non-negotiable.
3. **HEPA filtration and air scrubbing.** Commercial-grade HEPA air scrubbers run continuously throughout remediation, capturing airborne spores before they can resettle.
4. **Controlled removal of affected materials.** Porous materials, drywall, insulation, carpet, that have been colonized are removed and bagged for disposal per EPA guidelines. We are EPA Lead-Safe Certified, which matters in any Brandon home built before 1978, where disturbing walls can expose lead-based paint.
5. **Antimicrobial treatment and drying.** Structural cavities are treated and then dried to IICRC-compliant moisture levels before any reconstruction begins.
6. **Clearance testing coordination.** We work with independent industrial hygienists for post-remediation air quality testing when required by your insurance carrier or HOA, a step that protects you, not just us.

## Brandon Insurance & HOA Coordination

Mold claims in Florida carry some nuance. Homeowners' policies in the state often include a mold sublimit, a cap that is separate from and lower than your dwelling coverage limit, so documentation from the first hour of response matters. ## Local Note

Brandon homes built in the 1980s with original single-pane jalousie or aluminum-frame windows are worth a specific mention. Those window assemblies are notorious for allowing humid outdoor air to bypass the seal and condense on interior walls, especially on north-facing exterior walls that stay shaded and cool. The mold growth that results is often misread as a roof leak because it appears near the ceiling line, but the actual moisture pathway is the window frame, not the roof. If you're seeing discoloration in that location, ask your remediator to check the window perimeter before anyone starts cutting into the ceiling.
