## Why Polk City Properties See Sewage Backup Issues

Most of the residential development around Polk City grew up in two distinct waves: older rural-style homes on private septic systems, and newer subdivisions that tie into Polk County's municipal sewer network. Both have vulnerabilities. Septic tanks in this area sit in soil with a high sand content, which drains quickly under normal conditions but offers almost no filtration barrier when a tank overflows or a drain field fails. The wet season, roughly June through September, pushes the water table up and reduces the absorption capacity of the soil just when afternoon storms are adding the most volume to the system.

On the municipal side, the shallow utility corridors common in flat Central Florida terrain mean tree root intrusion is a persistent problem. Laurel oak and water oak roots, both common in Polk County landscaping, are aggressive enough to crack older clay or cast-iron lateral lines. A single root mass can partially block a line for months before a full backup occurs, and by then the blockage is usually severe.

## Our Sewage Cleanup and Sanitization Process in Polk City

Sewage is classified as Category 3 water, the most contaminated classification in the IICRC standard, and every step of the process is built around that reality. When we arrive, the first priority is containment: sealing off affected areas to prevent cross-contamination to clean spaces, and setting up negative air pressure where the layout allows. Standing sewage and saturated materials are extracted using truck-mounted equipment, not portable shop vacs.

After bulk removal, all porous materials that absorbed sewage, including drywall, insulation, and carpet padding, are removed and bagged for proper disposal. Hard surfaces are cleaned with EPA-registered disinfectants appropriate for Category 3 contamination. Drying equipment, including commercial dehumidifiers and air movers, is then positioned and monitored with moisture meters until readings confirm the structure has returned to normal levels. Because Polk City's humidity stays high well into the fall, drying timelines here often run longer than they would in a drier climate. We account for that in how we set and check equipment.
