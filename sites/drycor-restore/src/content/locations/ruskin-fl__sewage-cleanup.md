## Why Ruskin Properties Are Prone to Sewage Backups

Much of Ruskin was developed on low-lying land adjacent to the Little Manatee River and Tampa Bay's eastern shoreline. That geography creates two persistent problems. First, the water table in many parts of the area is shallow enough that heavy rainfall, common during Florida's June-through-September storm season, can saturate the soil around buried sewer laterals and septic drain fields, reducing their ability to accept effluent. When the ground is already holding as much water as it can, sewage has nowhere to go but back up through floor drains, toilets, and tub drains.

Second, a significant portion of Ruskin's residential stock includes older manufactured and site-built homes with cast-iron or clay-tile sewer laterals that have had decades to corrode, crack, and collect root intrusion from the area's aggressive subtropical vegetation. A line that drains slowly in the dry season can fail completely when storm surge or heavy rain raises the water table. Septic systems on larger lots, still common in Ruskin's more rural pockets, face their own version of this: drain fields that were sized for a smaller household load or installed before current Hillsborough County setback standards can saturate quickly and push effluent back toward the structure.

## Our Sewage Cleanup and Sanitization Process in Ruskin

Raw sewage is classified as Category 3 water, the most contaminated category recognized by the IICRC S500 standard, and it requires a different response than a burst supply line. The process starts with containment. Before extraction begins, affected areas are isolated to prevent cross-contamination to unaffected rooms, and anyone on-site is protected with appropriate PPE.

Extraction comes next. Standing sewage is removed with truck-mounted or portable extraction units capable of handling solids and debris, not just water. Affected porous materials, drywall, insulation, carpet, pad, and in some cases subfloor sheathing, are removed and bagged for disposal per Hillsborough County solid waste requirements, because porous materials that have contacted Category 3 water cannot be reliably sanitized and must go.

Once the structure is cleared of contaminated material, every affected hard surface is treated with EPA-registered antimicrobial and disinfectant solutions, applied with the dwell times those products require to be effective. Structural drying follows, using calibrated air movers and dehumidifiers. In Ruskin's humid climate, ambient moisture levels during the rainy season can slow drying significantly, so equipment placement and monitoring schedules are adjusted accordingly. Moisture readings are logged throughout the process so there's a documented record for your insurance carrier.
