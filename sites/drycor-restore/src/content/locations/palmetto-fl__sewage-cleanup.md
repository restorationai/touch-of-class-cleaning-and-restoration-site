## Why Palmetto Properties Are Vulnerable to Sewage Backups

Maricopa County is not Palmetto's frame of reference, Manatee County's low-lying coastal plain is. Much of Palmetto sits at or near sea level, and during the wet season (June through September) the water table rises close enough to the surface that septic drain fields lose their absorption capacity. When the ground is already saturated, a slow leak in an aging lateral line or a single heavy rain event can push sewage backward through floor drains, toilets, and washing machine standpipes.

The city's older residential areas include homes built in the 1950s through 1970s with cast-iron drain lines that have had decades to corrode, scale, and develop root intrusion points. Cast iron doesn't fail all at once, it weeps, and that slow contamination can go unnoticed behind walls or beneath slab until the smell makes it undeniable. Newer subdivisions closer to US-41 and the Terra Ceia Bay corridor have their own challenge: PVC systems that are properly installed but connected to municipal lines that experience surge pressure during major storm events.

## Our Sewage Cleanup and Sanitization Process in Palmetto

Sewage is classified as Category 3 water, the highest contamination level, which means every material it contacts is treated as potentially harboring pathogens until testing or removal confirms otherwise. The process doesn't skip steps regardless of how small the backup looks.

**Containment first.** Before extraction begins, affected areas are isolated to prevent cross-contamination into clean zones. In slab-on-grade homes common throughout Palmetto, this often means sealing HVAC registers and doorways before any equipment runs.

**Extraction and removal.** Standing sewage and contaminated water are extracted using truck-mounted units. Porous materials, drywall, insulation, carpet, and in some cases subfloor sheathing, that absorbed Category 3 water are removed rather than dried in place. There is no safe way to dry contaminated porous material back to a usable condition.

**Disinfection and deodorization.** After removal, all affected structural surfaces are treated with EPA-registered disinfectants. This isn't a single wipe-down, it's a multi-stage process that addresses both bacteria and the volatile organic compounds responsible for sewage odor. In Palmetto's heat and humidity, odor compounds off-gas quickly, so this step is time-sensitive.

**Drying and documentation.** Industrial air movers and dehumidifiers bring remaining structural materials to target moisture levels verified by calibrated meters. Every reading is logged, and the documentation package, moisture maps, photos, equipment placement records, goes directly to your insurance adjuster.

The crew is IICRC certified and OSHA trained, and DRYCOR RESTORE holds EPA Lead-Safe certification, which matters in Palmetto's older homes where disturbing building materials during demo can expose lead-containing paint.
