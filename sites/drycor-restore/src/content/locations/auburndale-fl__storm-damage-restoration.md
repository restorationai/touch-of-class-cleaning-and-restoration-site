## Why Auburndale Properties Take Storm Damage Hard

Polk County occupies a broad, low-relief plateau in Central Florida, and Auburndale's position among a chain of lakes amplifies storm behavior in ways that catch homeowners off guard. Lake breezes can accelerate wind gusts during afternoon thunderstorms, and the same water bodies that make the area scenic create elevated humidity that slows structural drying after a storm passes.

The local housing stock adds another layer of complexity. Much of Auburndale's residential construction dates from the postwar boom through the 1970s and 1980s, when building codes were less prescriptive about wind uplift resistance. Older concrete block homes, a common construction type in this part of Polk County, handle wind loads differently than modern wood-frame builds, but their flat or low-slope roofs are especially vulnerable to ponding water after heavy rain. When a roof membrane fails or a ridge cap blows off, water can enter wall cavities and reach the slab before anyone realizes the exterior is compromised. Florida's warm temperatures mean mold colonization can begin within 24 to 48 hours of that kind of intrusion.
