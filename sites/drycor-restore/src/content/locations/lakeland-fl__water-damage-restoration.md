## Why Lakeland Properties See Water Damage Differently

Lakeland's building stock tells the story of Polk County's postwar growth. Homes built from the 1950s through the 1980s, a large share of the city's residential inventory, were commonly constructed with concrete block walls and terrazzo or slab-on-grade floors. That construction style is durable in many ways, but it creates specific challenges after a water loss. Concrete block is porous: water that enters a wall cavity can migrate laterally through the block cores and surface on the opposite side of a room, far from the original breach. Moisture meters that read only the surface paint can show "dry" while the block itself stays saturated for days.

Lakeland's subtropical climate compounds the problem. High ambient humidity, regularly above 70% even on clear days, slows evaporative drying and accelerates mold colonization. Under the right temperature and humidity conditions, mold can begin to establish on wet organic material within 24 to 48 hours. That window is not a scare tactic; it is the reason extraction and drying equipment needs to be running quickly, not scheduled for the next business day.

## Our Water Damage Restoration Process in Lakeland

Every job begins with a moisture mapping survey, thermal imaging and calibrated meters used across walls, ceilings, and floors to find water that has migrated beyond the visible wet area. In Lakeland's older concrete block homes, this step routinely reveals saturation in adjacent rooms that look and feel dry.

Once the scope is documented, extraction comes first. Truck-mounted extraction units pull standing water from carpet, tile grout lines, and low points in the structure. After bulk water is removed, the drying phase begins with a calculated placement of commercial-grade desiccant or refrigerant dehumidifiers and high-velocity air movers. Equipment placement follows the IICRC S500 standard, which accounts for the material type, ambient conditions, and the volume of water involved, not a generic "one dehumidifier per room" approach.

Moisture readings are logged at each visit so drying progress is documented and available for your insurance carrier. When structural materials reach acceptable moisture content, equipment is removed and a final inspection confirms the structure is ready for any needed repairs.

## Lakeland Insurance Coordination

Most standard homeowner policies in Florida cover sudden and accidental water losses, a burst supply line, an appliance failure, storm-driven water entry through a damaged roof. What they typically do not cover is long-term seepage or maintenance-related deterioration, which is why the documentation gathered during the moisture mapping phase matters so much. Clear photographs, moisture logs, and a written scope of loss give your adjuster the information needed to process the claim accurately.

DRYCOR works directly with insurance carriers and can communicate with your assigned adjuster throughout the mitigation process. We do not determine coverage, that is your carrier's role, but we make sure the documentation supports an accurate and complete claim.

## Local Note

In Lakeland's older neighborhoods, many homes were built with a finished terrazzo floor over a concrete slab. Terrazzo is non-porous and does not absorb water the way wood subfloor does, but the grout lines between terrazzo chips and any cracks in the slab can allow water to migrate under the surface and pool beneath the flooring system. After a significant water loss, we check slab moisture with a tramex-style meter before declaring a floor dry, a step that matters specifically in homes of this era and construction type, and one that gets skipped when crews are not familiar with Polk County's housing stock.
