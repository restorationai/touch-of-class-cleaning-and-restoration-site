## Why Siesta Key Properties Face Distinct Fire Damage Challenges

The island's building stock ranges from mid-century concrete-block cottages to newer elevated wood-frame construction built to post-Andrew wind codes. Both present unique complications after a fire. Concrete block absorbs smoke odor deeply into its pores and can hold heat long after flames are out, making surface cleaning alone ineffective. Elevated wood-frame homes, built on pilings to meet FEMA flood requirements, have open crawl spaces beneath the structure where smoke and soot migrate freely, depositing residue on framing members that are already exposed to salt air.

Siesta Key's subtropical climate adds another layer. High ambient humidity, common for most of the year, means that soot particles bond with moisture quickly, forming acidic deposits that begin etching metal fixtures, appliances, and window frames within hours of a fire. The window for limiting secondary damage is short, which is why the timing of the first response matters as much as the quality of the work.

## Our Fire Damage Restoration Process on Siesta Key

The first priority on arrival is a thorough structural assessment. Coastal construction often conceals damage behind stucco cladding or within cavity walls, and thermal imaging helps locate heat-affected zones that aren't visible from the surface. Once the scope is documented with photographs and moisture readings, the team moves to controlled demolition of unsalvageable materials, careful separation of debris for disposal, and board-up or tarping of any compromised openings.

Smoke and odor removal on the island requires more than ozone treatment or simple airing out. Marine-saturated building materials hold odor compounds tenaciously. The process typically involves dry-ice blasting or chemical sponge cleaning on masonry surfaces, hydroxyl generation for occupied or contents-heavy spaces, and thermal fogging to reach cavities where smoke traveled. Affected HVAC ductwork is cleaned separately, because recirculated air will redeposit odor throughout the home if duct contamination is overlooked. Structural repairs follow once the environment tests clean, coordinated with licensed subcontractors under DRYCOR RESTORE's general contractor license (#CBC1253966).

## Local Note

One detail that catches out-of-area contractors on Siesta Key: the island's older stucco homes often have a three-coat lime-based plaster finish rather than modern synthetic stucco. Lime plaster is porous and absorbs smoke odor at a different rate than cement-based coatings, and it responds poorly to aggressive alkaline cleaners that work fine on modern exteriors. Cleaning those surfaces requires pH-neutral solutions and more dwell time, but it preserves the finish and avoids a costly re-stucco that some crews inadvertently create by using the wrong chemistry.
