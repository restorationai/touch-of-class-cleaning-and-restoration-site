## Why Sarasota Properties Face Particular Storm Damage Challenges

Sarasota's building stock spans several distinct eras, and each era carries its own storm vulnerability. Homes built in the 1950s and 1960s throughout the city's older inland neighborhoods frequently feature original wood-frame construction with horizontal lap siding that was never designed to withstand the wind-load standards adopted after Hurricane Andrew. Tile roofs common on mid-century and later Mediterranean-style homes can shed individual tiles during high-wind events, leaving the underlayment exposed to hours of rainfall before a tarp can be installed.

The geography compounds the problem. Sarasota County's flat topography and high water table mean that even moderate rainfall accumulates quickly at grade. When storm surge or sheet flooding follows wind damage, water enters structures that are already compromised. Stucco exteriors, prevalent throughout the area, can absorb significant moisture behind the surface while appearing intact, masking damage that a surface inspection alone will miss.

Salt-laden air from the Gulf also degrades caulking around windows and doors faster than it would in inland markets. A small gap that held through a mild season can fail catastrophically under the lateral pressure of tropical-force winds.

## Our Storm Damage Restoration Process in Sarasota

When we arrive at a Sarasota property after a storm event, the first priority is stabilization. That means emergency tarping of any breached roof areas, board-up of broken windows or doors, and a full exterior perimeter walk to identify hidden entry points before interior assessment begins.

Inside, we use thermal imaging cameras and calibrated moisture meters to map water intrusion behind walls, under flooring, and inside ceiling assemblies. Stucco and concrete block construction, common throughout Sarasota, can trap moisture in ways that standard visual inspection misses entirely. Readings guide extraction and drying placement rather than assumption.

Structural drying follows the IICRC S500 standard for water damage and the S520 protocol where mold risk is present. Desiccant and refrigerant dehumidifiers, combined with high-velocity air movers, are positioned based on psychrometric readings taken at regular intervals. We document conditions at each check so your insurance adjuster has a complete drying log, not just a summary.

Debris removal, including downed tree limbs and displaced roofing material, is coordinated alongside the restoration work rather than treated as a separate phase. Keeping the site clear accelerates access for equipment and reduces the risk of additional damage during the drying period.
