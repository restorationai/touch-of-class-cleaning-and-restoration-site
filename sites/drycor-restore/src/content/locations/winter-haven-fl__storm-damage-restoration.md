## Why Winter Haven Properties See Repeated Storm Damage

Central Florida's storm season runs longer than most homeowners expect, named storms and tropical systems can arrive from June through November, but the daily afternoon thunderstorms that build over the lakes from May onward do their own quiet damage. Repeated wind and rain cycles lift and re-seat shingles, work moisture into fascia boards, and saturate attic insulation before a single leak appears on the ceiling below.

Winter Haven's housing stock adds another layer of complexity. The city has substantial neighborhoods of mid-century block construction, concrete masonry units that handle wind loads well but are prone to water infiltration at mortar joints and around window frames, especially on older homes where original sealants have long since failed. Once water gets behind a block wall, it can migrate laterally before showing up inside, making the entry point hard to locate without moisture mapping equipment.

The area's high water table, common throughout low-lying Polk County, means that storm surge and heavy rainfall don't always drain quickly. Standing water around foundations can work its way into slab seams and through weep screed, particularly in homes built before current drainage standards were common practice.

## Our Storm Damage Restoration Process in Winter Haven

The first thing a crew does on arrival is a full exterior and interior assessment before any debris is moved. Photographing damage in place matters for insurance documentation, and disturbing a compromised roof section or shifting a fallen tree limb can change the loss picture in ways that complicate a claim later.

From there, the process follows a clear sequence:

**Emergency stabilization**, tarping breached roof sections, boarding windows, and securing any structural openings to stop ongoing water intrusion. In Winter Haven's climate, an unprotected opening during storm season is an invitation for a secondary rain event to compound the original damage within hours.

**Water extraction and moisture mapping**, any water that entered during the storm is located using thermal imaging and moisture meters, then extracted and dried using commercial drying equipment. Water that reads dry at the surface of a stucco or block wall can still be saturating the substrate behind it; drying decisions are based on readings, not appearance.

**Debris removal and structural assessment**, tree limbs, damaged roofing materials, and compromised framing are removed and documented. Our team is IICRC certified, EPA Lead-Safe certified, and OSHA trained, which matters on older Winter Haven homes where disturbing painted surfaces during storm repairs can trigger lead-safe work practice requirements.

**Reconstruction coordination**, once the structure is stabilized and dried, we move into repairs under our general contractor license (#CBC1253966), coordinating trades so homeowners aren't managing multiple separate contractors through an already stressful process.
