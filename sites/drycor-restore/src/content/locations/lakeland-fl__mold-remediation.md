## Why Lakeland Properties See Mold Problems

Polk County sits in one of the most mold-hospitable climates in the continental United States. Relative humidity in Lakeland regularly climbs above 80% during summer months, and the city's position between two major lake systems means ambient moisture levels stay elevated even between rain events. That matters because mold only needs 24 to 48 hours of sustained moisture and a surface to feed on, drywall paper, wood framing, ceiling tile, carpet backing, to begin colonizing.

Lakeland's housing stock adds another layer of complexity. The city has a significant number of homes built in the 1950s through 1970s, a construction era that predates modern vapor barriers and often relied on single-pane windows, minimal wall insulation, and building envelopes that breathe in ways modern homes don't. Older homes in established neighborhoods throughout the city frequently have original plaster walls or early-generation drywall that absorbs and holds moisture longer than contemporary materials. Slab foundations, extremely common in Central Florida, eliminate crawl spaces but create a direct pathway for ground moisture to wick upward into flooring systems, particularly in low-lying areas prone to standing water after heavy rain.

Air conditioning systems are both a solution and a source. In Lakeland's heat, HVAC units run hard for most of the year, and a clogged condensate drain line or an undersized system that short-cycles will leave indoor humidity elevated enough to sustain mold growth even when there's been no visible water event.

## Our Mold Remediation Process in Lakeland

Remediation starts with a thorough assessment, not just a visual scan, but moisture mapping with calibrated meters to find wet material behind surfaces that look dry. In Central Florida homes with stucco exteriors and concrete block construction, moisture can travel laterally through walls in ways that aren't obvious without instrumentation.

Once the scope is established, the affected area is contained using physical barriers and negative air pressure to prevent spores from migrating to clean areas of the home during work. This step is non-negotiable in Lakeland's climate: opening up a moldy wall cavity without proper containment in a humid environment can spread viable spores faster than in drier regions.

Contaminated materials, drywall, insulation, flooring, framing if affected, are removed, bagged, and disposed of properly. Remaining structural surfaces are cleaned and treated using EPA-registered antimicrobial agents. Drying equipment, commercial dehumidifiers and air movers, runs until moisture readings confirm the structure is within acceptable ranges. The NAERMC-(MOLD) certification our team carries reflects training in exactly this protocol sequence, and the process aligns with IICRC standards for applied microbial remediation.

For homes with lead-based paint, a real consideration in Lakeland properties built before 1978, our EPA Lead-Safe Certified status means remediation work that disturbs painted surfaces is handled under the appropriate containment and cleanup protocols.
