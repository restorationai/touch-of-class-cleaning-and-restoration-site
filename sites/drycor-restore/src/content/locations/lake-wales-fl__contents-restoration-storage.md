## Why Lake Wales Properties See Contents Damage More Often Than You'd Think

Polk County's ridge geography gives Lake Wales slightly better drainage than the low-lying coastal areas of Florida, but the trade-off is a sandy soil profile that doesn't buffer moisture the way clay does. Water that enters a structure moves fast, wicking into wood furniture, upholstery, and cardboard boxes before a crew can arrive. Homes in the older sections of Lake Wales, particularly those built in the mid-20th century with real-wood cabinetry and hardwood flooring, are especially vulnerable: solid wood swells and warps quickly, but it also responds well to professional drying and dehumidification when treatment starts early.

The region's storm season, which runs reliably from June through October, means that roof intrusions and window seal failures are a recurring source of interior water damage. A slow roof leak during a multi-day rain event can saturate attic insulation, drip onto stored boxes, and ruin irreplaceable items before anyone notices the ceiling stain. That pattern repeats across Lake Wales every summer.

## Our Contents Restoration and Storage Process in Lake Wales

The process begins with a room-by-room inventory. Every affected item is photographed, catalogued, and assigned a condition rating before anything moves. That documentation matters for insurance claims and gives you a clear record of what was recovered and what could not be saved.

Items that can be dried on-site are treated with industrial air movers and desiccant dehumidifiers calibrated to Florida's ambient humidity levels. Items that need more intensive treatment, or that simply cannot be safely dried in a home that is still being remediated, are packed out and transported to a climate-controlled storage facility. Textiles go through ozone or thermal treatment to address smoke or mold odors. Electronics are assessed by a qualified technician before any power is applied. Documents and photographs are stabilized through freeze-drying or air-drying protocols depending on the extent of saturation.

When the property is ready, contents are returned, unpacked, and placed back in their original positions. You receive a final inventory reconciling every item that left and every item that came back.

## Local Note

Lake Wales has a notable stock of homes built during the citrus boom era of the 1920s through the 1950s, many featuring original Dade County pine framing and plaster-over-lath interior walls. That construction holds up remarkably well structurally, but plaster walls behave differently from modern drywall when wet: the surface can feel dry to the touch while the lath behind it stays saturated for days. Contents stored against those walls, including bookshelves, wooden furniture, and fabric bins, can absorb moisture from the wall itself even after visible water is gone. Knowing to check wall-adjacent items carefully is something that only becomes obvious after working in this region's older housing stock.
