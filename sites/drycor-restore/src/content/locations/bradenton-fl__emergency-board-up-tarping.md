## Why Bradenton Properties Face Repeated Board-Up and Tarping Needs

Manatee County sits squarely in Florida's most active hurricane corridor. Even storms that make landfall well south or north of Bradenton routinely produce the wind shear and rain bands that peel back roofing, blow out garage doors, and compromise older window glazing. The problem compounds quickly here because of the region's humidity baseline, once an exterior envelope is breached, interior relative humidity can spike fast enough to support mold growth within 24 to 48 hours.

Bradenton's housing stock adds another layer of vulnerability. The city's older residential neighborhoods include a significant share of mid-century concrete block construction, a building type that handles wind reasonably well but is notoriously difficult to seal after window or soffit damage because the openings don't follow standard lumber dimensions. Tarping and boarding those structures requires custom-cut materials and mechanical fastening into masonry rather than wood framing, which takes longer and demands the right equipment on the truck.

Fire damage is the other major driver of emergency board-up calls in the area. After a residential fire, Manatee County fire inspectors will clear the scene, but the property owner is responsible for securing the structure against weather and unauthorized entry. Leaving that gap open, even overnight, can void portions of a homeowner's insurance claim if the carrier documents additional damage that occurred post-fire.

## Our Emergency Board-Up and Tarping Process in Bradenton

When a call comes in, the crew loads for the specific loss type before leaving Thonotosassa. A roof breach after a storm gets heavy-gauge polyethylene tarping material, ridge caps, and weighted edge battens designed to hold through sustained wind rather than the lightweight blue tarps that lift in the next gust. A fire or vandalism board-up gets pre-cut plywood panels, drill-set lag screws for masonry anchoring where needed, and temporary door hardware if an entry point has been compromised.

On arrival, the first priority is a rapid exterior assessment, identifying every breach point, not just the obvious one. Wind events in Bradenton frequently cause damage in multiple locations simultaneously: a missing ridge cap here, a blown soffit vent there, a cracked jalousie window panel on the side elevation. Addressing only the visible damage and missing a secondary opening is how interiors get flooded by the next rain band.

After securing the structure, the crew documents every covered opening with photographs and measurements. That documentation goes directly to the property owner and, when requested, to the insurance adjuster, it establishes what was open, when it was closed, and what materials were used, which matters when the carrier is evaluating the claim.

## Local Note: Jalousie Windows and Masonry Block Homes

One thing that catches out-of-area board-up crews in Bradenton: the prevalence of jalousie windows in mid-century Florida construction. Those louvered glass panels, common in homes built from the 1950s through the 1970s, don't accept standard plywood boarding the way a double-hung window does. The frame opening is often irregular, the surrounding masonry block doesn't offer wood nailers, and broken jalousie glass leaves sharp horizontal shards that have to be cleared before any panel can be seated safely. Crews that haven't worked this housing stock regularly show up with the wrong fasteners and the wrong panel dimensions.
