## Why Ellenton Properties Face Particular Board-Up and Tarping Risks

Maratee County's position between Tampa Bay and the open Gulf makes Ellenton one of the first inland communities to absorb wind-driven rain when systems push northeast after landfall. That geography matters for board-up and tarping work in a specific way: storms here rarely arrive and leave cleanly. A named storm or even a fast tropical depression can stall, circle, and deliver multiple rain bands over 12 to 36 hours. A property that takes structural damage in the first band has to survive the second and third without any protection unless someone secures it quickly.

The housing stock in this part of Manatee County adds another layer of complexity. Many homes in the Ellenton area were built during Florida's rapid-growth decades of the 1970s through the 1990s, before current wind-load standards tightened under Florida's post-Andrew building code reforms. Roof-to-wall connections on homes from that era can be more vulnerable to uplift, meaning a partial roof failure is a realistic outcome even in a Category 1 event. Older soffit and fascia systems on these homes also tend to peel before the roof deck itself is compromised, which creates an entry point for water that is easy to miss until interior ceilings show staining.

## Our Emergency Board-Up and Tarping Process in Ellenton

When a call comes in from the Ellenton area, the first question is what type of opening needs to be secured and what conditions the crew will be working in. The process differs meaningfully depending on whether the damage is a broken window from a projectile, a compromised entry door, or a section of roof deck that has lifted or separated.

For window and door board-up, the crew uses exterior-grade plywood cut and fastened to the structural framing around the opening, not just the trim. Fastening into trim is a shortcut that fails under continued wind pressure. For roof tarping, the standard approach uses heavy-duty polyethylene tarps rated for UV exposure and wind, secured with cap sheets and weighted or fastened at the ridge and eaves rather than just draped and tied. A tarp that lifts in the next rain band is not protection.

The crew photographs every opening before and after securing it, documents the materials used, and notes conditions that may affect the insurance claim, including visible pre-existing damage versus storm-caused damage. That documentation travels with the job file.
