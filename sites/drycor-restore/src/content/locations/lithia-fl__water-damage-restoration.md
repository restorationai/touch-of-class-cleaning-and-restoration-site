## Why Lithia Properties See Water Damage Issues

Hillsborough County's eastern edge, where Lithia sits, is underlaid by expansive clay soils that shift with moisture. During the wet season, that soil stays saturated for weeks at a stretch, which raises the effective water table around slab foundations and puts persistent hydrostatic pressure on stem walls and slab penetrations. A slow leak that would drain harmlessly in sandier soil can instead back up against a foundation and wick into framing.

Lithia's newer subdivisions, many built in the 2000s and 2010s, use concrete block construction with stucco exteriors. That combination is durable in dry conditions, but stucco that develops even a hairline crack around a window or roofline can channel water directly into the block cavity and then into interior wall assemblies. Because the exterior looks intact, homeowners often don't realize moisture has been accumulating inside the wall until they notice a musty odor or soft drywall. The IICRC S500 standard that guides our drying process accounts for exactly this scenario: surface readings can show dry while cavity moisture remains elevated, which is why we use thermal imaging and penetrating moisture meters, not just surface probes.

## Our Water Damage Restoration Process in Lithia

When we arrive at a Lithia property, the first step is containment and assessment, not immediate extraction. We map the moisture migration with thermal cameras and calibrated meters to understand where water has traveled, including into subfloor assemblies, wall cavities, and cabinet toe-kicks. That map drives every decision that follows.

Extraction comes next. Truck-mounted and portable extractors pull standing water from hard surfaces and carpet systems. For water that has reached the subfloor, we use weighted extraction tools designed to pull moisture from the material rather than just the surface above it.

Structural drying follows a calculated drying plan: industrial desiccant or refrigerant dehumidifiers, high-velocity air movers positioned to create directed airflow across wet assemblies, and daily monitoring logs that track temperature, relative humidity, and material moisture content. We adjust equipment placement as readings change rather than leaving a static setup and hoping for the best. Affected materials that cannot be dried in place, such as saturated insulation or buckled flooring, are removed and documented before drying begins.
