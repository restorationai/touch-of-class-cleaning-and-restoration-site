## Why Mulberry Properties Are Prone to Sewage Backups

The geology beneath Mulberry is not kind to underground pipes. The same limestone karst formations that made Polk County a center of phosphate extraction also create unpredictable soil movement, sinkholes, and subsidence that can shift and crack sewer laterals over time. Homes built in the mid-twentieth century, when Mulberry was growing alongside the mining industry, frequently have cast-iron or clay-tile drain lines that have had decades to corrode, root-intrude, and offset at the joints.

Florida's wet season compounds the problem. From June through September, heavy rainfall saturates the ground quickly, raising the local water table and increasing hydrostatic pressure on septic tanks and drain fields. A septic system that functions adequately in February can be overwhelmed by a sustained summer storm, pushing partially treated effluent back through floor drains and low-lying fixtures. Properties on the outer edges of Mulberry that still rely on private septic rather than municipal sewer are especially exposed to this pattern.

## Our Sewage Cleanup and Sanitization Process in Mulberry

Every sewage job starts with containment before cleanup. Sewage-contaminated water is classified as Category 3 by the IICRC, meaning it carries pathogens, bacteria, and potential chemical contaminants that require full personal protective equipment and controlled work zones. The crew establishes a barrier between affected and unaffected areas of the home before any material is moved.

Once contained, standing waste water is extracted using truck-mounted and portable extraction units capable of handling the debris load that comes with raw sewage. Affected porous materials, including flooring, baseboards, drywall, and insulation, are assessed and, where contamination has penetrated, removed and bagged for proper disposal per Florida Department of Environmental Protection guidelines for biohazardous waste. Hard surfaces are then treated with EPA-registered antimicrobial agents formulated for Category 3 losses, applied in multiple passes to reach grout lines, subfloor seams, and wall cavities.

Drying and dehumidification follow sanitization, not the other way around. Introducing airflow before surfaces are treated can aerosolize contaminants. Once the space reads clean, commercial-grade desiccant dehumidifiers and air movers are set to bring structural moisture levels back to normal range, with readings logged throughout the drying cycle.

Sewage backup claims can be complicated.
