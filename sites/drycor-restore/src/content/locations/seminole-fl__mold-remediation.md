## Why Seminole Properties See Mold Issues

The housing stock throughout Seminole skews older, and that matters for mold. Homes built before the mid-1980s often used paper-faced drywall and cellulose-based insulation that absorb moisture readily and give mold a ready food source. Flat or low-slope rooflines common in the ranch-style construction popular in this part of Pinellas County can allow water to pond and infiltrate at fascia and soffit joints, sending moisture into wall cavities without obvious interior signs.

Seminole's position between two bodies of water, the Gulf to the west and Tampa Bay to the east, means the air rarely fully dries out. Even without a direct water intrusion event, sustained indoor relative humidity above 60 percent is enough to feed surface mold on grout, wood framing, and HVAC ductwork. Homes with older central air systems that run longer cycles to compensate for aging efficiency lose their dehumidification advantage, and condensation on supply registers and inside air handlers becomes a recurring mold source.

## Our Mold Remediation Process in Seminole

Every remediation starts with a thorough inspection, not a visual pass. Moisture meters and thermal imaging cameras reveal saturation behind walls and under flooring that looks dry to the eye. Once the scope is mapped, containment goes up: negative air pressure barriers isolate the affected area so spores cannot migrate to clean rooms during removal.

Contaminated materials are removed following IICRC remediation guidelines and EPA protocols. Because DRYCOR RESTORE holds NAERMC mold certification and is an IICRC Certified Firm, the work follows a defined standard rather than a contractor's best guess. After physical removal, affected surfaces are HEPA-vacuumed and treated with an EPA-registered antimicrobial agent. Industrial-grade air scrubbers with HEPA filtration run continuously through the work period to capture airborne spores.

Final clearance involves post-remediation verification, typically a third-party air quality test, to confirm spore counts have returned to normal background levels before containment comes down. In Seminole's climate, skipping that step is a risk: ambient outdoor spore counts are already elevated compared to drier regions, so the bar for "normal" has to be measured, not assumed.

## Local Note

Older Seminole homes with terrazzo floors, a common feature in Pinellas County construction from the 1950s through the 1970s, present a specific challenge during mold remediation. Terrazzo sits directly on the slab with little or no vapor barrier beneath it in many of these builds. When a plumbing leak or appliance overflow saturates the slab, moisture wicks upward through the terrazzo and into the base of adjacent walls. Surface readings on the terrazzo itself can look acceptable while the wall framing directly above holds significant moisture. Thermal imaging at the wall-floor junction is the step that catches this pattern before a remediation is closed out prematurely.
