## Why Dade City Properties Are Especially Vulnerable After a Loss

Pasco County's climate is not gentle on buildings that lose their envelope. Dade City averages more than 50 inches of rainfall per year, and the rainy season runs from June through September with daily convective storms that can drop several inches in under an hour. A roof opening left unprotected overnight is not a minor inconvenience here, it is a fast path to saturated framing, ceiling collapse, and mold colonization that can begin within 24 to 48 hours in Florida's humidity.

Dade City's older residential neighborhoods include a meaningful share of homes built in the 1940s through 1960s, when construction relied on board-and-batten siding, single-wythe masonry block, and wood-frame rooflines that are less forgiving of water intrusion than modern construction. Block homes in particular can absorb moisture into the CMU itself, making a quick board-up and proper tarping critical to keeping a manageable repair from turning into a structural remediation project.

The area also sits along State Road 52 and US-98 corridors where commercial properties, agricultural storage buildings, and older downtown storefronts each present their own boarding challenges, irregular window dimensions, flat or low-slope roofs, and facades that require specialized anchoring to avoid causing additional damage during the securing process.

## Our Emergency Board-Up and Tarping Process in Dade City

When a call comes in, a DRYCOR RESTORE crew mobilizes from Thonotosassa with the materials needed to address the most common loss scenarios: plywood sheeting in standard and custom-cut dimensions, heavy-duty polyethylene tarps rated for Florida wind exposure, fastening systems that anchor to structural members rather than finish surfaces, and the ladders and lift equipment needed to work safely on steep or damaged rooflines.

On arrival, the crew assesses the full perimeter of the structure before touching anything. Openings that are not immediately visible, a soffit blown free, a gable vent dislodged, a side window cracked by heat, are documented and secured along with the primary breach. Every opening gets photographed before and after boarding or tarping, creating a record that supports the insurance claim from the first hour.

For roof tarping, the crew anchors to the ridge or to undamaged decking using battens and screws rather than staples, which pull free in wind. Tarps are overlapped and weighted or tied at the eaves. For board-up work on windows and doors, plywood is cut to fit and fastened to the structural framing, not just the trim, so the covering holds through subsequent weather events while repairs are scheduled.
