## Why Seminole Properties See Sewage Backup More Often Than You Might Expect

Seminole's housing stock spans several decades, and the era a home was built matters a great deal when it comes to sewer line reliability. Homes constructed in the 1960s and 1970s, which make up a significant share of the city's residential neighborhoods, were often plumbed with cast iron or orangeburg pipe. Both materials degrade over time: cast iron corrodes from the inside out, and orangeburg, a compressed tar-paper product, softens and collapses under soil pressure. When those pipes fail, the first sign is usually a slow drain, a gurgling toilet, or the unmistakable sulfur odor rising from a floor drain.

The area's flat terrain compounds the problem. Without meaningful elevation change, gravity drainage is marginal, and any partial blockage can push sewage backward into the lowest fixture in the home. Homes near tidal areas are also subject to infiltration during heavy rain events, when the municipal system absorbs more volume than the pipes were designed to handle. A storm that drops several inches in a short window can push wastewater back through connections that are otherwise functioning normally.

## Our Sewage Cleanup and Sanitization Process in Seminole

Raw sewage is classified as Category 3 water, the most contaminated category recognized by the IICRC. That classification drives every decision about how materials are handled, what protective equipment the crew wears, and what can be dried versus what must be removed.

The process begins with containment. Affected areas are isolated to prevent cross-contamination to unaffected rooms. Porous materials that have absorbed sewage, including carpet, pad, and in many cases the bottom courses of drywall, are removed and bagged for disposal rather than dried in place. Attempting to dry contaminated porous materials does not render them safe; it concentrates the pathogens and accelerates odor.

After extraction and removal, all affected surfaces are treated with EPA-registered disinfectants formulated for Category 3 losses. Structural cavities, including the space beneath subfloor and inside wall bays, are treated and then dried using commercial air movers and dehumidifiers. Moisture readings are documented throughout so there is a clear record showing the structure returned to a dry, safe baseline before any reconstruction begins. That documentation also matters when an insurance claim is involved.

## Local Note

Seminole's older concrete-block homes, common throughout the city's mid-century neighborhoods, present a specific challenge during sewage cleanup that is easy to overlook. Concrete block is porous, and when sewage contacts an uncoated block wall, it absorbs into the masonry itself rather than staying on the surface. Standard surface disinfection is not sufficient in those cases. The block needs to be treated at depth, and moisture readings taken at the surface can read lower than what is actually present inside the wall. Crews working in these homes need to account for that lag and extend the drying and treatment timeline accordingly.
