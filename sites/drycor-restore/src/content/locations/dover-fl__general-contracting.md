## Why Dover Homes Have Distinct Renovation Needs

Much of Dover's residential stock reflects the building trends of the mid-to-late 20th century, slab-on-grade construction, block and stucco exteriors, and original plumbing and electrical systems that weren't designed with today's appliance loads or code expectations in mind. Hillsborough County's clay soils shift seasonally as they absorb and release moisture, and that movement shows up inside homes as cracked tile grout, sticking doors, and hairline fractures in drywall that homeowners often mistake for purely cosmetic issues. Before any remodel begins, it's worth understanding whether those signs are surface-level or structural.

Dover also sits in a flood-prone corridor. Properties near low-lying areas along the county's drainage network frequently see water intrusion that compromises subfloor integrity, wall cavities, and insulation, damage that needs to be properly remediated before new finishes go in. Skipping that step means a beautiful new kitchen floor laid over a compromised subfloor, which rarely ends well.

## Local Note

One thing that catches out-of-area contractors working in Dover: the combination of clay soil movement and slab foundations means tile and hardwood installations need a proper moisture vapor barrier and, in many cases, a self-leveling underlayment before new flooring goes down. Skipping the leveling step on a slab that's shifted even slightly produces lippage, uneven tile edges, that becomes a warranty and liability issue. Contractors who do most of their work in newer, sandy-soil subdivisions further west sometimes underestimate how much prep a Dover slab needs before finish materials go in.
