## Why Dunedin Properties See Distinct Fire Damage Challenges

Dunedin's housing stock skews older. Many of the homes in the city's historic core were built in the mid-20th century, wood-frame construction with original pine subfloors, plaster-and-lath interior walls, and attic framing that predates modern fire-blocking requirements. When fire moves through that kind of structure, it travels differently than in a newer CBS (concrete block and stucco) home. Char penetrates deeper into old-growth pine. Plaster ceilings can hide smoke migration into the attic cavity that a surface inspection won't catch.

The salt-laden Gulf air compounds the problem. Metal fasteners, HVAC components, and electrical boxes in coastal Pinellas County homes corrode faster than their inland counterparts. After a fire, that corrosion accelerates when smoke acids combine with ambient humidity, which means the window for effective restoration is shorter here than in a drier climate. Waiting even a few days to begin post-fire restoration allows soot to etch metal fixtures and permanently stain porous surfaces.

## Our Fire and Smoke Restoration Process in Dunedin

Every fire damage job starts with a structured assessment, not a quick walk-through, but a systematic inspection that maps char depth, smoke migration paths, and moisture content in structural members. In Dunedin's older wood-frame homes, that means probing wall cavities and checking attic spaces where smoke stratifies and cools, depositing a residue layer that causes odor problems for months if it's missed.

From there, the process moves in a deliberate sequence:

- **Emergency board-up and tarping** to secure the structure and protect it from Dunedin's afternoon thunderstorms, which can arrive with little warning and turn a fire-damaged roof into a water damage event.
- **Controlled demolition** of unsalvageable materials, charred framing, compromised insulation, smoke-saturated drywall, documented thoroughly for your insurance adjuster.
- **Dry ice blasting or HEPA-filtered soot removal** on structural surfaces that can be saved, followed by chemical sponge cleaning on smoke-affected finishes.
- **Thermal fogging and hydroxyl treatment** to neutralize smoke odor at the molecular level, not just mask it. In a humid coastal environment, odor compounds bond to surfaces more aggressively, so this step gets extra attention.
- **Structural drying** of any water introduced by suppression efforts, documented to IICRC S500 standards.
- **Reconstruction** scoped to return the home to pre-loss condition, coordinated with your insurance carrier.

DRYCOR RESTORE is an IICRC Certified Firm and EPA Lead-Safe Certified, relevant in Dunedin because homes built before 1978 are common, and fire debris from those structures requires lead-safe work practices during demolition.
