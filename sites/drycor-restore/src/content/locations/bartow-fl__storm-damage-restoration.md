## Why Bartow Properties See Significant Storm Damage

Polk County's inland position does not insulate it from hurricane-force winds. When a storm tracks across the peninsula, Bartow often sits in the right-front quadrant, the zone that historically produces the strongest gusts and heaviest rain bands. That rainfall lands on soils with a high clay content, which means water moves laterally rather than percolating down. Yards flood, driveways become channels, and water finds its way under door thresholds and through foundation vents faster than most homeowners expect.

Older neighborhoods in Bartow feature homes with wood-shake or aging asphalt shingles, single-pane windows, and soffit systems that were not engineered to the current Florida Building Code wind-speed requirements. When a fast-moving squall or a tornado-warned cell passes through, those homes lose shingles, fascia boards, and sometimes entire roof sections in ways that newer construction typically does not. Once the roof deck is exposed, interior water intrusion can begin within the first hour of rainfall.

## Our Storm Damage Restoration Process in Bartow

The first priority after any storm event is stopping ongoing water intrusion. That means emergency tarping of damaged roof areas, boarding compromised windows, and identifying every entry point before interior drying work begins. Skipping that step and moving straight to extraction is one of the most common mistakes in storm response, because new water keeps arriving until the envelope is sealed.

Once the structure is secured, the process moves to documentation: moisture mapping with thermal imaging and calibrated meters, photo documentation of all affected materials, and a written scope that your insurance adjuster can work from directly. Structural drying follows, using commercial-grade dehumidifiers and air movers calibrated to the actual square footage and material types in the affected rooms. Polk County's summer humidity levels, which regularly exceed 85 percent, mean that equipment sizing matters considerably. Undersized rental equipment from a hardware store will run continuously and still not achieve the vapor pressure differential needed to pull moisture out of wall cavities and subfloor assemblies.

If a tree has come down on the structure, debris removal is coordinated alongside the tarping work rather than sequenced after it, because leaving a tree on a roof while waiting for a separate crew extends the window of water intrusion significantly.

## Local Note

Bartow's older wood-frame homes, particularly those built in the mid-twentieth century, frequently have original tongue-and-groove pine subfloors beneath later layers of vinyl or tile. When storm water enters through a compromised roof or a failed window seal and reaches that subfloor assembly, the pine holds moisture far longer than oriented strand board or plywood, and it can read nearly dry at the surface while remaining saturated in the lower layers. Moisture meters set to the wrong wood species calibration will miss it entirely.
