## Why Brandon Properties Face Distinct Fire Damage Challenges

Brandon sits in eastern Hillsborough County, a corridor that saw explosive residential growth from the 1970s through the 2000s. That means the housing stock is a patchwork: ranch-style homes from the late 1970s with original aluminum wiring, wood-frame construction from the 1980s and 1990s that used oriented strand board (OSB) sheathing, and newer stucco-clad subdivisions built in the 2000s. Each era presents a different fire behavior and a different restoration challenge.

OSB sheathing, common in homes built between roughly 1985 and 2005, absorbs smoke and moisture simultaneously after a fire event. Unlike solid-sawn lumber, it delaminates when wet and can lose structural integrity faster than it appears to from the outside. A wall that looks salvageable from the street may need full replacement once the stucco or siding comes off. Older homes with aluminum wiring require licensed electrical assessment before any restoration work can safely proceed, since heat events can compromise connections that were already at elevated risk.

## Our Fire Damage Restoration Process in Brandon

Fire restoration is not a single trade, it's a sequenced series of decisions, each one affecting what comes next. The process typically moves through these phases:

**Emergency stabilization** comes first: boarding windows and doors, tarping roof penetrations, and shutting off utilities where the fire or suppression water has compromised them. Brandon's afternoon thunderstorm season means a compromised roof can take on significant water within hours of a fire if it isn't covered, that secondary water intrusion adds mold risk and complicates the insurance claim.

**Damage documentation** follows immediately. Every affected surface, material, and structural element is photographed and catalogued before anything is moved or removed. This documentation is the foundation of the insurance claim and protects the homeowner if scope disputes arise later.

**Demolition and cleaning** removes char, smoke-saturated materials, and anything that cannot be restored. Soot is acidic, it continues to etch glass, corrode metal fixtures, and discolor surfaces the longer it sits. Thermal fogging and hydroxyl generation are used to neutralize odor molecules embedded in wall cavities, HVAC ducts, and soft goods that survived the fire visually but absorbed smoke deeply.

**Structural drying** addresses suppression water. Fire hoses deliver hundreds of gallons into a structure; that water migrates into subfloors, wall cavities, and insulation. Drying is conducted to IICRC S500 standards using commercial-grade dehumidifiers and air movers.

**Reconstruction** is the final phase, framing, drywall, insulation, roofing, and finishes, permitted and inspected through Hillsborough County.
