## Why Palmetto Properties Present Unique Renovation Challenges

The Gulf Coast climate isn't just a backdrop, it's an active participant in how buildings age here. Salt-laden air accelerates corrosion on fasteners, flashing, and HVAC components. Homes built in the 1960s and 1970s along Palmetto's older residential streets frequently used single-wythe concrete block construction, which is durable but offers almost no thermal or moisture buffer on its own. When those walls are opened during a remodel, contractors regularly find that decades of condensation cycling have degraded furring strips, window buck framing, and any wood blocking used for cabinetry anchors.

Soil conditions in low-lying areas near the river and bay can shift seasonally, which shows up as hairline cracks in slab foundations and tile floors that look cosmetic but sometimes signal minor movement underneath. A renovation that doesn't account for that movement, by using the wrong grout, skipping expansion joints, or anchoring new cabinetry to a wall that isn't plumb, will show problems within a year or two. Getting the substrate right before the finish work starts is the part of general contracting that separates a lasting remodel from one that needs to be redone.

## Local Note: Concrete Block Walls and Hidden Moisture

One thing that comes up repeatedly in Palmetto renovation work is the behavior of older concrete block walls when they're finally opened. The exterior paint on these homes has often been recoated multiple times over the decades, and that layering can trap moisture against the block rather than letting it breathe. When a kitchen or bathroom remodel requires cutting into an exterior wall, to relocate a window, add an outlet, or run new plumbing, it's not uncommon to find the interior face of the block damp even when the room itself feels dry. Because DRYCOR RESTORE carries IICRC certification for water damage work alongside our general contracting license, we can assess and address that moisture before closing the wall back up, rather than discovering it later as a mold or odor problem.
