## Why Palmetto Properties Take Storm Hits Differently

Most of Palmetto's residential neighborhoods were built during Florida's mid-century expansion, when building codes had not yet caught up with the realities of Gulf Coast wind loads. Homes constructed before the 1990s often feature original wood-frame construction with minimal hurricane strapping, meaning roof decks can lift or shift under sustained winds that newer CBS (concrete block and stucco) construction would shrug off. That same era of construction commonly used single-pane jalousie or aluminum-framed windows that offer little resistance to wind-driven debris.

The Manatee River's proximity also means that storm surge and tidal backflow are genuine threats during named storms and even strong tropical disturbances. Low-lying areas near the river can accumulate standing water quickly, and the clay-heavy soil common to this part of Manatee County drains slowly, water that pools against a foundation after a storm has time to wick into slab seams and block walls before it ever evaporates on its own.

## Our Storm Damage Restoration Process in Palmetto

The first priority after a storm is stopping the bleeding. That means emergency tarping and board-up to close any breach in the building envelope, roof openings, broken windows, compromised doors, so that the next rain event doesn't multiply the damage already done. Once the structure is secured, the work shifts to a systematic moisture assessment.

We use thermal imaging cameras and calibrated moisture meters to map water intrusion behind walls and under flooring, not just the wet surfaces you can see. In older Palmetto homes with plaster-over-wood-lath interiors, surface readings can look acceptable while the lath behind holds significant moisture, a condition that creates ideal mold colonization conditions within 24 to 48 hours if left unaddressed. Industrial air movers and desiccant dehumidifiers are positioned based on the moisture map, not just placed in the middle of a room.

Debris removal, downed trees, damaged roofing material, shattered glass, runs parallel to the drying process. For tree-impact damage specifically, structural assessment happens before any interior work begins, because a compromised rafter or header changes what's safe to work around.

Storm damage claims in Florida carry specific documentation requirements that differ from a standard water loss.
