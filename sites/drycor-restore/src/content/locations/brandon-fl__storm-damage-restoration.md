## Why Brandon Properties See Repeated Storm Damage

Brandon's growth over the past four decades produced a wide range of housing stock, from 1970s and 1980s ranch homes with original roof decking and single-pane jalousie windows to newer stucco-and-tile construction built under post-2002 Florida Building Code wind standards. Older homes in the area often have roof sheathing fastened with smooth-shank nails rather than ring-shank nails, which can let entire roof sections lift under hurricane-force uplift even when the shingles themselves look intact from the street. The region's sandy-clay soil drains poorly after saturation, so standing water in yards and crawl spaces lingers long after the storm passes, wicking into slab perimeters and garage walls. Brandon also sits in a zone where afternoon thunderstorms between June and September can produce localized tornado-strength straight-line winds with almost no warning, the kind of event that snaps a large pine at the base and drops it across a roof in seconds.

## Our Storm Damage Restoration Process in Brandon

Every job starts with a full exterior and interior assessment before a single piece of debris is moved. Roof penetrations get tarped and boarded immediately to stop the water intrusion that causes the most secondary damage, saturated insulation, swelling OSB decking, and mold colonization that can begin in as little as 24 to 48 hours in Florida's humidity. Our IICRC-certified crews then document all structural and content damage with calibrated moisture meters and thermal imaging, creating a detailed scope that insurance adjusters can work from directly. Tree removal and debris clearing happen in coordination with the structural work, not as a separate contractor callout, so the site stays organized and safe. Interior drying follows IICRC S500 and S520 standards using commercial-grade desiccant and refrigerant dehumidifiers sized to the actual cubic footage of the affected space, not residential units borrowed from a hardware store. Once the structure reads dry, reconstruction begins: framing, sheathing, roofing, drywall, and finish work handled under our contractor license (#CBC1253966), so there is no handoff gap between mitigation and rebuild.

## Brandon Insurance and HOA Coordination

Most homeowners' policies in Florida cover sudden and accidental storm damage, but the documentation burden falls on the policyholder to prove the loss originated with the weather event rather than pre-existing deterioration. ## Local Note

Brandon's older subdivisions contain a significant number of homes with barrel-tile roofs installed over single-layer felt underlayment, a combination that was standard practice before synthetic underlayments became code-required. After a storm, tile displacement or cracking is visible and obvious, but the felt beneath is often already compromised from years of Florida UV exposure. Water that gets under lifted tile can travel several feet horizontally along the felt before dropping into the attic, meaning the interior wet spot on the ceiling may be nowhere near the damaged tile on the exterior. Chasing that leak without thermal imaging leads to incomplete repairs and callbacks. Our crews scan the attic deck before patching anything on the roof surface.
