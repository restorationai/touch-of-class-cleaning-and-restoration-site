## Why Anna Maria Properties Face Acute Board-Up and Tarping Risks

Anna Maria Island's geography creates conditions that accelerate damage the moment a building envelope is breached. The island sits low and narrow, fully exposed to Gulf-side storm surge from the west and bay-side flooding from the east. Even a tropical system that weakens before landfall can push enough wind and rain to lift roofing material, shatter impact-resistant windows, or drive water horizontally through any gap left by fire damage or a structural failure.

The housing stock compounds the risk. Much of Anna Maria's residential construction dates to the mid-twentieth century, when homes were built on wood-frame piers or shallow slabs without the hurricane strapping and reinforced sheathing required by Florida's current building code. Older roof decks, single-pane jalousie windows still found on some cottages, and wood siding that has cycled through decades of salt air and UV exposure mean that once a breach opens, the interior is exposed to conditions it was never engineered to handle. Vacation rental properties add another layer of urgency: a home sitting empty between guests with an open roof or a boarded window left unsecured can sustain damage for days before anyone notices.
