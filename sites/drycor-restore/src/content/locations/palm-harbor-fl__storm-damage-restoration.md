## Why Palm Harbor Properties See Disproportionate Storm Damage

Palm Harbor's geography puts it at the intersection of two persistent hazards: Gulf moisture and the shallow, sandy soils that dominate coastal Pinellas County. Saturated ground has almost no capacity to absorb additional runoff once a storm stalls overhead, so water backs up against foundations and presses through slab seams and crawl space vents. The housing stock adds another layer of risk, a significant portion of Palm Harbor's residential neighborhoods were built in the 1970s and 1980s, when single-ply roofing membranes and aluminum-frame windows were standard. Those materials have aged past their design life, and a Category 1 wind event that would barely stress a newer build can breach them entirely. Vinyl siding common to that era can also buckle under impact from wind-driven debris, leaving sheathing and insulation exposed to rain for days if cleanup is delayed.

## Our Storm Damage Restoration Process in Palm Harbor

The first priority on any storm call is stopping active water intrusion. That means emergency tarping of breached roofs and boarding of broken windows or doors before any drying equipment is staged, wet materials keep getting wetter until the envelope is sealed. Once the structure is secured, the crew performs a full moisture mapping survey using thermal imaging cameras and pin-type meters to locate water that has migrated behind walls or beneath flooring. Readings are documented in writing, which matters later when an adjuster reviews the scope.

Drying in coastal Florida is not the same as drying in a drier climate. Outdoor dew points along the Gulf Coast regularly exceed 70°F during storm season, which means bringing in outside air to dry a structure actually introduces more moisture than it removes. Drycor's crews run refrigerant dehumidifiers calibrated to pull moisture from the air inside the building, paired with high-velocity air movers positioned to create a drying chamber rather than simply circulating humid air. Readings are logged daily and compared against IICRC S500 drying goals so the crew knows when materials have genuinely reached equilibrium, not just when they feel dry to the touch.

Debris removal, fallen tree limbs, displaced roofing materials, shattered glass, is handled concurrently with structural drying rather than as a separate phase, because leaving debris in contact with wet surfaces accelerates mold colonization. In Florida's warm, humid conditions, mold can begin to establish within 24 to 48 hours of a moisture event.
