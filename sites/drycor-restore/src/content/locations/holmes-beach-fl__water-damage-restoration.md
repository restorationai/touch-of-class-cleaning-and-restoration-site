## Why Holmes Beach Properties Face Elevated Water Damage Risk

Anna Maria Island's position in the Gulf means Holmes Beach absorbs weather from two directions. Tropical systems and afternoon thunderstorms drive rain horizontally into door frames and window seals that face west toward open water. At the same time, the island's elevation is low enough that even moderate storm surge can overwhelm the drainage capacity of residential streets, leaving water pressed against exterior walls and pooling in garages and ground-level entries.

The housing stock adds another layer of complexity. A significant portion of Holmes Beach homes were built in the 1960s and 1970s, when construction standards for moisture management were far less rigorous than they are today. Concrete block construction, common throughout the island, can wick moisture through the block itself long after surface water is removed. Older plumbing in these homes, often copper or galvanized steel, is also more prone to pinhole leaks and joint failures that go undetected inside block cavities until a stain appears on drywall or tile grout starts to lift.

Short-term rental properties, which are widespread in Holmes Beach, face an additional risk: water losses that start during a guest stay may not be reported promptly, giving moisture extra hours to migrate into subfloor assemblies and wall cavities before a property manager is even notified.

## Our Water Damage Restoration Process in Holmes Beach

The first priority on any water loss is stopping further migration. Our crew arrives with truck-mounted extraction equipment capable of pulling standing water from tile, carpet, and subfloor assemblies quickly. Once bulk water is removed, we use thermal imaging cameras to locate moisture that has traveled behind walls or beneath flooring, because on a barrier island with high ambient humidity, what reads dry at the surface is often still saturated an inch deeper.

Drying in Holmes Beach requires more aggressive dehumidification than an inland job of the same size. Outdoor dew points near the Gulf are frequently high enough that simply opening windows would introduce more moisture than it removes. We run commercial-grade desiccant or refrigerant dehumidifiers calibrated to the actual conditions inside the structure, not a one-size setting. Drying logs are recorded daily and mapped against the IICRC S500 standard so the drying goal is documented and defensible for your insurance carrier.

For concrete block homes, drying timelines are longer than they would be for wood-frame construction. We set that expectation upfront rather than pulling equipment prematurely and leaving residual moisture that feeds mold growth in the weeks after a job closes.

## Local Note

Concrete block homes built along Holmes Beach's canal-front streets in the 1960s and 1970s often have a construction detail that surprises homeowners during a water loss: the interior face of the exterior block wall is furred out with wood framing and drywall, leaving a narrow cavity between the drywall and the block. When water enters through a window seal or a roof penetration, it can travel down inside that cavity and pool at the base of the wall on the slab, completely hidden from view. Moisture meters read the drywall surface as normal while the bottom plate and slab edge stay wet for days. We probe those cavities specifically on island homes because missing that pocket of moisture is one of the most common reasons a Holmes Beach water loss leads to a mold call three weeks later.
