## Why Lakeland Properties Experience Sewage Backups

Lakeland sits atop sandy, porous soils that drain well in some areas but shift and settle around buried utility lines over time. That ground movement stresses older clay or cast-iron sewer laterals, the pipes connecting your home to the municipal main, causing cracks, offsets, and root intrusion that restrict flow. During Lakeland's heavy summer storm seasons, the municipal sewer system can become hydraulically overloaded, pushing wastewater back up through the lowest drain in a home, typically a floor drain, toilet, or tub in a ground-level bathroom.

Septic systems add another layer of risk. A significant portion of Lakeland properties, particularly those on larger lots outside the city's sewer service area, rely on septic tanks and drain fields. Florida's high water table, especially after prolonged wet-season rainfall, can saturate the soil around a drain field and cause the tank to back up into the home. Polk County Environmental Regulations govern septic system maintenance and repair timelines, and any sewage cleanup that involves a septic overflow must account for those requirements before the drain field is disturbed or repaired.

## Our Sewage Cleanup and Sanitization Process in Lakeland

Every sewage job begins with containment. Before extraction equipment rolls in, the affected area is isolated to prevent cross-contamination to clean zones, a step that matters especially in Lakeland's older concrete-block homes, where porous block walls can wick contaminated moisture laterally if the perimeter isn't controlled.

Extraction comes next. Truck-mounted and portable wet-vac units remove standing sewage and solids. Porous materials, drywall, insulation, carpet, pad, and subflooring, that have absorbed Category 3 water are removed and bagged for disposal per EPA and local waste guidelines. No amount of drying saves contaminated porous material; it has to come out.

After physical removal, the space is treated with EPA-registered hospital-grade disinfectants applied by OSHA-trained technicians. Surfaces are cleaned, disinfected, and where required, treated with antimicrobial agents to inhibit mold colonization, which in Lakeland's humidity can begin within 24 to 48 hours of initial exposure. Drying equipment (commercial dehumidifiers and air movers) runs until moisture readings in walls, floors, and framing return to normal baselines, verified with calibrated meters. As an IICRC Certified Firm, the process follows the IICRC S500 and S520 standards for water and microbial remediation.

## Local Note

Lakeland's concrete-block construction, common in homes built from the 1950s through the 1980s across many of the city's established residential areas, behaves differently during sewage events than wood-frame construction.
