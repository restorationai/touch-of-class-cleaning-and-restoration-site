## Why Gibsonton Properties See Mold Issues

Gibsonton's geography works against homeowners in a few specific ways. Much of the area was developed on fill and low-lying land adjacent to the river and its tributaries, which means crawl spaces and slab perimeters stay damp well into the dry season. Mobile and manufactured homes, which make up a meaningful share of Gibsonton's housing stock, often have vapor barriers that degrade over time, allowing ground moisture to migrate upward into subfloor cavities where mold colonizes out of sight.

Conventional block-construction homes in the area, many built between the 1970s and early 1990s, frequently used paper-faced drywall in bathrooms and utility rooms. That paper facing is a preferred food source for Stachybotrys chartarum and Cladosporium species once moisture is present. Florida's year-round warmth means there is no cold season to slow spore activity the way northern climates provide a natural pause.

Hillsborough County's storm season also matters here. When Gibsonton receives several inches of rain in a short window, water intrusion through window frames, sliding glass door tracks, and roof penetrations is common. Homeowners often dry visible surfaces and assume the problem is resolved, not realizing that insulation inside wall cavities can hold moisture for weeks.

## Our Mold Remediation Process in Gibsonton

Remediation begins with containment, not cleaning. Before any physical work starts, the affected area is isolated with polyethylene sheeting and negative air pressure is established using HEPA-filtered air scrubbers. This keeps disturbed spores from migrating to unaffected rooms, which is especially important in smaller floor plans common in this part of Gibsonton.

Once containment is in place, the scope of affected material is mapped using moisture meters and thermal imaging. In Gibsonton's climate, readings at the surface of drywall or wood can look acceptable while the material behind it remains saturated. The process follows IICRC S520 standards for mold remediation, which means removal decisions are based on moisture data and visible growth extent, not guesswork.

Contaminated porous materials, including drywall sections, insulation, and affected wood framing, are removed, bagged, and disposed of properly. Structural surfaces that can be salvaged are treated with an EPA-registered antimicrobial agent. Drying equipment, including commercial-grade dehumidifiers calibrated for Florida's high ambient humidity, runs until clearance readings confirm the area has returned to normal moisture levels. A final air quality test by an independent industrial hygienist can be arranged before the space is closed out.

## Local Note

Gibsonton's manufactured and mobile home communities present a specific remediation consideration that does not come up as often in conventional construction. Belly board, the fabric or plastic sheeting that seals the underside of a manufactured home's floor system, frequently tears or separates at seams over time. Once that barrier is compromised, ground moisture enters the floor cavity and the subfloor decking, often particleboard or OSB, absorbs it readily. By the time a homeowner notices soft spots underfoot or a persistent musty smell from floor registers, the mold growth underneath can be extensive. Inspecting the belly board condition is a standard part of any Gibsonton assessment for homes on that type of foundation.
