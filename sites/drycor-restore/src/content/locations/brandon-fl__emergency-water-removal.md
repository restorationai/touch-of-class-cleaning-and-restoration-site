## Why Brandon Properties Need Emergency Water Removal

The soil profile across much of Brandon and the broader eastern Hillsborough area drains poorly. During heavy rain events, common from June through September, water tables rise quickly, and homes on low-lying lots can see intrusion through foundation cracks, garage slabs, or sliding door thresholds before the storm even passes. Brandon's residential growth also spans several decades of construction, which matters for emergency water removal: homes built in the 1970s and 1980s often have galvanized supply lines that are well past their service life, and those pipes don't fail gradually, they fail suddenly, sometimes releasing hundreds of gallons before the shutoff is located. Newer subdivisions built on former agricultural land can have grading issues that direct stormwater toward foundations rather than away from them. Whatever the source, standing water in a Brandon home isn't a "dry it out with fans" situation, mold colonization can begin within 24 to 48 hours in Florida's humidity, which means the window for effective water removal is short.

## Our Water Removal & Cleanup Process in Brandon

The first thing a DRYCOR crew does on arrival is identify the moisture boundary, not just where the floor is wet, but how far water has wicked into walls, cabinetry, and subfloor material. Thermal imaging and calibrated moisture meters give a picture the eye can't. From there, the process moves through extraction, standing water removal, and cleanup:

**Extraction**, Truck-mounted and portable extractors pull standing water and saturated material from carpet, pad, and hard flooring. In Brandon homes with tile over concrete slab (a very common finish here), water often migrates under the tile and sits against the concrete for days if not properly removed.

**Pumping & Removal**, High-capacity dehumidifiers and air movers are positioned according to a plan calibrated to the affected square footage and material types, once bulk standing water has been pumped out and removed. Florida's ambient humidity makes passive drying essentially useless; equipment does the work.

**Monitoring**, Moisture readings are logged at each visit. Water removal is not complete when the surface feels dry, it's complete when readings in walls and structural materials return to acceptable baselines per the IICRC S500 standard.

**Documentation**, Every reading, every photo, and every equipment placement is recorded. That documentation matters when you're filing a claim with your carrier.
