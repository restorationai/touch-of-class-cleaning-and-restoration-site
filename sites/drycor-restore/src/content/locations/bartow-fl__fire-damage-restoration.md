## Why Bartow Properties Face Distinct Fire Damage Challenges

Bartow's residential building stock skews older. Many homes in the city's established neighborhoods were built in the mid-twentieth century with heart pine framing, original wood lathe, and plaster walls. That construction is beautiful and durable under normal conditions, but it behaves differently after a fire. Heart pine is dense and resinous, which means it chars deeply and holds smoke odor in ways that engineered lumber does not. Plaster walls trap smoke residue in their porous surface layers and can continue off-gassing for weeks if not properly treated.

Polk County's climate adds another layer. The region averages more lightning strikes per square mile than almost anywhere in the continental United States, and a direct strike to an attic or electrical panel can ignite materials before a homeowner even smells smoke. Dry winters and early springs, when humidity drops and vegetation desiccates, raise the risk further. Fires that start in attic spaces are particularly damaging in older Bartow homes because the original roof decking and rafter systems absorb heat and smoke throughout the structure before the fire is contained.

## Our Fire Damage Restoration Process in Bartow

Every fire loss is different, but the sequence of work follows a disciplined order regardless of the size of the damage.

The first step is a thorough structural assessment. We identify load-bearing elements that have been compromised by heat, document all affected materials with photographs and moisture readings, and establish a safe perimeter for the work. This documentation also forms the foundation of your insurance claim file.

Once the structure is confirmed safe for entry, fire cleanup begins with controlled demolition of materials that cannot be restored: charred framing, melted insulation, smoke-saturated drywall. In older Bartow homes, we test suspect materials for lead paint before any demolition begins, as required by EPA protocols. Our team holds EPA Lead-Safe certification, which matters on pre-1978 construction that is common throughout the area.

Smoke and soot removal follows. Dry soot from fast-burning fires behaves differently than the oily residue left by slow, smoldering combustion. We match the cleaning chemistry to the residue type rather than applying a single method across the entire structure. HEPA filtration runs continuously during this phase to capture airborne particulates.

Odor elimination comes next. Thermal fogging and hydroxyl treatment penetrate into wall cavities, subflooring, and HVAC ductwork where smoke molecules have migrated. Skipping this step is the most common reason a restored home still smells like a fire six months later.

Reconstruction closes out the project, returning the structure to pre-loss condition or better, coordinated with your insurance adjuster throughout.
