## Why Venice Properties Present Unique Renovation Challenges

The Gulf Coast climate is the constant variable in any Venice remodel. Salt-laden air accelerates corrosion on fasteners, flashing, and HVAC components. Homes built in the 1960s and 1970s, which make up a significant portion of the housing stock in Venice proper, often have aluminum wiring, single-pane jalousie windows, and original cast-iron or galvanized plumbing that intersects with modern renovation plans in complicated ways. Disturbing walls in these homes can surface surprises: asbestos-containing texture coatings, knob-and-tube remnants, or insulation that hasn't been touched since the Carter administration.

Because DRYCOR RESTORE is EPA Lead-Safe Certified, work in pre-1978 homes follows federal renovation, repair, and painting protocols. That matters in Venice, where a large share of the housing stock falls into that category and where resale buyers increasingly ask for documentation of compliant work.

Sandy, low-bearing soils also affect structural decisions. Additions and room conversions that require new footings need engineering review specific to Sarasota County's soil conditions, and permits flow through the county's building department for unincorporated areas or through the City of Venice depending on the parcel. Knowing which jurisdiction governs your address before demo begins saves weeks.

## Our Renovation and General Contracting Process in Venice

Every project starts with a scope walkthrough that accounts for what's behind the walls, not just what's visible. For post-damage rebuilds, that means reviewing the remediation documentation before framing begins so no moisture-compromised material gets enclosed. For elective remodels, it means probing for the surprises common in older Gulf Coast construction before the permit application is submitted.

From there, the process follows a clear sequence:

- **Permitting and plan review**: We prepare or coordinate drawings, submit to the appropriate jurisdiction, and track the review cycle. Venice's building department processes can run several weeks for larger scopes, and we build that into the project schedule upfront.
- **Demolition and rough-in**: Selective demo is done with containment in place where lead or other regulated materials are present. Rough framing, electrical, plumbing, and HVAC rough-ins are scheduled to align with inspection windows.
- **Finishes and close-out**: Tile, cabinetry, flooring, paint, and fixture installation follow inspections. Final inspection and certificate of completion close the permit.

Kitchen remodels and bathroom remodels in Venice often involve rerouting plumbing that was originally run under slab, which requires saw-cutting and patching. We coordinate the concrete work, the plumbing rough-in, and the slab repair as a single sequenced scope rather than handing it between separate trades.

## Local Note: What Venice's Coastal Construction Zone Means for Your Remodel

Properties within Venice's coastal construction control line, which runs through much of the island and some mainland parcels near Roberts Bay, are subject to Florida Department of Environmental Protection review for certain structural improvements. This layer of review sits on top of local building permits and can affect timelines for additions, elevated decks, and any work that changes the footprint of a structure near the shoreline. Contractors unfamiliar with this layer sometimes submit permit applications that stall because the CCCL review wasn't anticipated. It's one of the first questions we ask when a Venice homeowner describes an exterior addition or a project that touches the seaward side of the structure.
