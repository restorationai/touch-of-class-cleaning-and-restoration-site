## Why Dundee Properties Face Particular Challenges After a Fire

Florida's Gulf-side humidity does not pause after a fire is extinguished. Once firefighters clear a structure and the windows are open for ventilation, outdoor air carrying 70 to 90 percent relative humidity moves into a heat-stressed building. Soot particles, which are already chemically active, absorb that moisture and begin etching into painted surfaces, cabinetry, and soft furnishings faster than they would in a drier climate.

Dundee's housing stock adds another layer of complexity. Many homes in the city were built during Florida's mid-century expansion, when construction relied on concrete block exteriors paired with wood-framed interior walls. Concrete block holds heat longer than wood-frame construction, which means interior temperatures stay elevated well after the fire is out. That sustained heat drives smoke deeper into wall cavities and insulation, making surface cleaning alone insufficient. A thorough restoration requires controlled negative-pressure containment and HEPA filtration to pull embedded particulates out of the structure rather than simply off it.

## Our Fire Damage Restoration Process in Dundee

Every fire loss is assessed before any cleaning begins. The crew documents the full scope of damage with moisture readings, thermal imaging, and photographic evidence, both to guide the restoration plan and to support the insurance claim. That documentation matters in Polk County, where carrier adjusters often request detailed line-item inventories before approving structural work.

From there, the process moves through four core stages:

**Stabilization.** Board-up and roof tarping protect the structure from Florida's afternoon rain pattern, which can push water into a fire-compromised roof within a single storm cycle.

**Soot and smoke removal.** Dry-chemical sponging lifts loose soot from walls and ceilings before any wet cleaning is applied. Applying water or cleaning agents to dry soot first smears it deeper into the substrate.

**Odor neutralization.** Thermal fogging and hydroxyl generation reach into wall cavities and HVAC pathways where smoke odor compounds settle. In Dundee's older concrete-block homes, this step often requires extended dwell time because block is more porous than it appears.

**Structural drying and reconstruction coordination.** Any materials that absorbed firefighting water are dried to IICRC S500 standards before reconstruction begins, preventing a secondary mold event in a climate where mold colonization can begin within 24 to 48 hours of moisture exposure.
