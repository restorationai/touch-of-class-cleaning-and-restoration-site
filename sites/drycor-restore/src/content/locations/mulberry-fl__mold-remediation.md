## Why Mulberry Properties See Mold Issues

Mulberry's history as a phosphate-mining town shaped the way the city grew, and that history shows up inside its homes. Much of the residential stock in and around the city center dates to the mid-20th century, when construction relied on wood-framed walls with minimal vapor barriers and crawl spaces that sat close to grade. Polk County's high water table, which shifts noticeably during the wet season from June through September, means crawl spaces and slab perimeters in Mulberry can stay damp for weeks after a heavy rain event even when the surface looks dry.

Crawl-space mold in this region tends to colonize the underside of floor joists and subfloor sheathing before a homeowner notices any odor or discoloration inside the living space. By the time a musty smell reaches the kitchen or a bedroom, the colony is often well established. Older plumbing in mid-century homes, including galvanized supply lines and cast-iron drain stacks, is also prone to slow pinhole leaks inside wall cavities, the kind that feed mold for months without triggering a visible water stain.

## Our Mold Remediation Process in Mulberry

Every job begins with a thorough inspection that goes beyond what is visible. Moisture meters and thermal imaging cameras read conditions inside wall assemblies and beneath flooring, giving the crew a map of where moisture is actually present rather than where it appears to be. That step matters especially in Mulberry's older homes, where plaster walls and tongue-and-groove subfloors can hold moisture at depth while reading dry at the surface.

Once the scope is confirmed, the affected area is isolated with negative-air containment barriers so that spores disturbed during removal do not migrate to clean areas of the home. Contaminated materials are removed, bagged, and disposed of per EPA guidelines. Structural surfaces that can be saved are treated and dried to IICRC S520 standard. Air scrubbers with HEPA filtration run continuously throughout the project. The job is not closed until post-remediation air sampling confirms spore counts have returned to acceptable levels, a step that protects both the homeowner and the integrity of any insurance claim.
