## Our Water Cleanup Process in Riverview

Every water cleanup job begins with a full moisture assessment before a drop of water is extracted. Technicians use thermal imaging and penetrating moisture meters to map the actual boundaries of the wet zone, which almost always extends further than the visible damage suggests. This matters in Riverview's newer construction because water migrates quickly through the gaps in engineered flooring systems and into wall cavities before any surface discoloration appears.

Once the scope is confirmed, the process moves through extraction, structural drying, and monitoring:

- **Extraction**: Truck-mounted and portable extractors remove standing water and pull residual moisture from carpet, pad, and subfloor materials.
- **Structural drying**: High-capacity air movers and industrial dehumidifiers are positioned according to the IICRC S500 standard, creating airflow patterns that draw moisture out of wall cavities and floor assemblies rather than just drying the surface.
- **Daily monitoring**: Moisture readings are logged at each visit. Equipment is adjusted or repositioned based on actual drying progress, not a fixed schedule.
- **Documentation**: Every reading, photo, and equipment placement is recorded, a complete file that supports your insurance claim from day one.

## Local Note

Riverview's newer subdivisions frequently use open-web floor trusses rather than solid-joist framing, a design that improves HVAC efficiency but creates a network of interconnected cavities beneath the subfloor. When water enters this system, it travels laterally through the truss bays faster than it would in a traditional floor system, spreading the wet zone well beyond the point of entry. Technicians familiar with this construction type know to probe the full truss bay grid, not just the area directly below the source, before setting drying equipment.
