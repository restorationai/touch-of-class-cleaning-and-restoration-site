## Our Storm Damage Restoration Process in Temple Terrace

The first priority after any storm event is stopping additional damage. That means emergency tarping of breached rooflines, board-up of broken windows or doors, and rapid extraction of any standing water before it migrates into subfloor assemblies or wall systems. Water that sits for more than 24 to 48 hours in a warm, humid Florida environment creates conditions where mold can begin to colonize, a timeline that's compressed further in Temple Terrace's summer heat.

Once the structure is stabilized, the assessment phase begins. Moisture mapping with thermal imaging and calibrated meters identifies where water has traveled beyond the visible damage zone, inside wall cavities, beneath tile, under baseboards. This step matters because storm-driven water doesn't follow gravity the way a plumbing leak does; wind pressure forces it laterally into places that look dry on the surface.

Drying, debris removal, and structural repairs are sequenced based on what the moisture readings show, not on what looks bad from the doorway. The team holds IICRC certification and follows the S500 standard for water damage and the S110 standard for fire and smoke where applicable, the same protocols insurers expect to see documented in a claim file.
