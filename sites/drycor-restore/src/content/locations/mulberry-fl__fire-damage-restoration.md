## Why Mulberry Properties See Distinctive Fire Damage

The housing stock in Mulberry tells the story of the phosphate industry. Homes built in the 1940s through the 1960s often feature tongue-and-groove pine interiors, original hardwood floors, and jalousie windows, all of which behave very differently under fire and smoke exposure than modern construction. Pine absorbs smoke compounds deeply into the grain; jalousie glass frames warp and seal shut, trapping soot-laden air inside rooms that look untouched from the outside. Concrete block construction, common throughout central Polk County from that same era, absorbs heat into its core, which means surface temperatures can stay elevated for hours and smoke odor can leach out of block walls for days after cleanup appears complete.

Florida's high humidity compounds the problem. After a fire, moisture from firefighting water mixes with soot and ash to form a corrosive paste that accelerates rust on metal fixtures, stains porous tile grout, and begins breaking down drywall paper within hours. In Mulberry's climate, that window between fire suppression and the start of professional cleanup is short.

## Our Fire Damage Restoration Process in Mulberry

The first step on any fire loss is a thorough scope: we document structural damage, map smoke migration through the HVAC system, and identify which materials can be restored versus which need to be removed. Smoke travels further than most homeowners expect, finding its way through wall cavities, attic spaces, and ductwork into rooms that never saw a flame. In homes with older central air systems common to Mulberry's mid-century neighborhoods, duct cleaning is almost always part of the scope.

From there, the process moves through controlled demolition of unsalvageable materials, dry-ice or soda blasting of smoke-stained framing where the structure is sound, application of professional-grade sealants to lock in residual odor, and thermal fogging or hydroxyl treatment to neutralize airborne smoke compounds. Structural repairs, including framing, drywall, insulation, and finish work, follow once the environment is clean and dry. ## Local Note

Homes in Mulberry that were built to house phosphate-industry workers in the mid-twentieth century frequently have interior walls finished with a sand-textured plaster rather than modern drywall. That plaster is porous in a way that standard drywall is not: smoke compounds penetrate below the surface layer, which means wiping the wall clean does not eliminate the odor source. Encapsulation with an odor-blocking primer is often necessary before any repainting, and in severe cases the plaster itself needs to be removed. Knowing this before a restoration bid is written saves homeowners from a second round of work after the smell returns with summer humidity.
