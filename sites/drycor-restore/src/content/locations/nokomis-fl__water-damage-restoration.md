## Why Nokomis Properties Face Elevated Water Damage Risk

The geography here works against homeowners when water gets inside. Nokomis and the surrounding communities along U.S. 41 and Albee Road sit at low elevation, and the sandy coastal soils that make the area beautiful also drain poorly once they reach saturation during storm season. Slab-on-grade construction, which is common throughout Sarasota County, means there is no crawl space to ventilate, and moisture trapped beneath a slab or along its perimeter can wick upward into flooring and baseboards for weeks without visible surface signs.

Older concrete block homes, a common building type in this part of the county, absorb water through their masonry walls differently than wood-frame construction. The block itself can hold significant moisture even after surface readings look acceptable, which means a standard drying timeline is often not enough. Homes with terrazzo floors, another regional characteristic, require different moisture mapping techniques than vinyl plank or carpet, because terrazzo does not telegraph subsurface moisture the way softer flooring materials do.

Hurricane and tropical storm activity adds another layer of risk. Even storms that make landfall north or south of Sarasota County can push enough rain and storm surge to overwhelm drainage in low-lying coastal communities. The combination of wind-driven rain, compromised roof seals, and saturated ground creates multi-point intrusion events that are more complex to dry than a single-source leak.

## Our Water Damage Restoration Process in Nokomis

Every job starts with a thorough moisture assessment before any equipment is placed. Thermal imaging cameras and calibrated moisture meters map the full extent of saturation, including areas behind walls and under flooring that look undamaged from the surface. That mapping determines the drying plan, the equipment layout, and the documentation package that goes to your insurance carrier.

Water extraction comes first: truck-mounted and portable extraction units pull standing water from the structure before drying equipment is staged. Once extraction is complete, industrial air movers and low-grain refrigerant dehumidifiers are positioned based on the moisture map, not placed generically around the room. Drying targets follow the IICRC S500 standard, and readings are logged daily so progress is documented and the drying plan can be adjusted if conditions change.

In Nokomis homes with concrete block walls or terrazzo floors, drying often requires desiccant dehumidification or specialized floor mat systems to pull moisture from dense materials that refrigerant units alone cannot reach efficiently. The process takes longer in these structures, and we communicate that timeline clearly from the first day rather than giving an optimistic estimate that the building's material reality will not support.

## Local Note

Concrete block homes along the coastal sections of Nokomis present a specific drying challenge that does not show up on standard moisture meters the same way wood-frame construction does. Block walls can register as dry at the surface while the interior of the block remains saturated, and that trapped moisture will continue migrating toward interior finishes long after the visible water event is over. Technicians working in this area need to probe at multiple depths and use extended monitoring periods before confirming a structure is dry.
