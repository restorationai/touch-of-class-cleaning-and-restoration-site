## Why Lake Wales Properties Are Particularly Vulnerable to Storm Damage

The ridge country around Lake Wales is beautiful, but it creates real exposure. Properties on elevated lots take the full force of wind that has nothing to block it, while lower-lying areas near the chain of lakes deal with sheet-flow flooding when storm drains and swales reach capacity quickly. Polk County's soils shift between well-drained sandy ridges and poorly drained flatwoods clay, and a home's drainage behavior during a storm depends heavily on which soil type sits under its yard.

The local housing stock adds another layer of complexity. Lake Wales has a significant number of homes built between the 1940s and 1970s, when concrete block construction was the regional standard. CBS (concrete block and stucco) walls hold up well to wind but are highly susceptible to moisture intrusion once the stucco surface is cracked or breached by storm debris. Water that enters a CBS wall can wick laterally through the block cores and show up as staining or efflorescence far from the original entry point, making the source harder to trace without moisture mapping equipment.

Citrus groves and large canopy oaks are common throughout the area, and both become hazards in a strong storm. Falling limbs and uprooted trees cause roof punctures, broken windows, and compromised soffits that let wind-driven rain enter attic spaces and travel down interior wall assemblies.

## Our Storm Damage Restoration Process in Lake Wales

The first step on any storm loss is a thorough damage assessment before any material is moved or removed. We photograph and document every affected surface, which matters both for structural decision-making and for the insurance claim file. Thermal imaging and calibrated moisture meters let us map water migration inside walls and under flooring without unnecessary demolition.

Once the scope is clear, the work moves in a logical sequence:

- **Emergency stabilization**: tarping compromised roof sections, boarding broken windows, and securing any structural elements at risk of further movement.
- **Water extraction and drying**: standing water comes out first, followed by the placement of industrial air movers and low-grain refrigerant dehumidifiers calibrated to Florida's high ambient humidity. Drying in Central Florida's climate requires more dehumidification capacity than drier regions because the outdoor air trying to equalize with the structure carries significant moisture load of its own.
- **Debris and damaged material removal**: storm-damaged insulation, compromised drywall or plaster, and saturated flooring are removed to the extent necessary to allow structural components to dry and to prevent secondary mold colonization, which can begin within 24 to 48 hours of a water intrusion event in Florida's heat.
- **Structural drying verification**: we do not close a job on visual inspection alone. Moisture readings are logged daily and compared against baseline readings for the material type until all assemblies reach acceptable levels.
- **Reconstruction coordination**: once the structure is dry and stable, we coordinate or perform the repairs needed to return the home to pre-loss condition, working under license #CBC1253966.
