Roof decking gets exposed mid-storm, rain drives into wall cavities before the wind even stops, and the region's sandy loam soil, saturated after the first hour of heavy rain, stops absorbing runoff entirely, pushing water toward foundations and garage slabs. DRYCOR RESTORE responds 24/7 from Thonotosasa, and our IICRC-certified crews are equipped to handle everything from fallen-tree structural breaches to the hidden moisture that lingers long after the skies clear.

## Why Plant City Properties See Elevated Storm Damage Risk

Plant City's position along the I-4 corridor places it in one of Florida's most active thunderstorm zones, the region routinely ranks among the highest in the country for lightning strikes per square mile, and the convective storms that build over the Gulf can intensify rapidly as they push east toward Hillsborough County. That meteorological reality combines with a local housing stock that spans several eras: older ranch-style and block construction homes built before modern wind-load codes sit alongside newer subdivisions, and each type responds differently under storm stress.

Older concrete-block homes common throughout Plant City handle wind pressure reasonably well, but their flat or low-pitch roofs are vulnerable to ponding water when gutters back up or downspouts get blocked by debris. Soffit and fascia damage on these homes often goes unnoticed until moisture has already wicked into the attic insulation. Newer wood-frame construction, meanwhile, can sustain sheathing damage that looks minor from the street but allows driven rain to saturate wall cavities within minutes. In either case, the clock matters: mold colonization in Florida's humidity can begin within 24 to 48 hours of a moisture intrusion event.

## Our Storm Damage Restoration Process in Plant City

Every job starts with a systematic damage assessment before any materials are moved or covered. We photograph and document structural breaches, water intrusion points, and affected building materials, documentation that matters both for the repair scope and for your insurance carrier's adjuster. Moisture meters and thermal imaging cameras map the full extent of water migration, including areas that look dry at the surface but are holding saturation behind drywall or under flooring.

Once the scope is established, we prioritize emergency stabilization: tarping and boarding compromised roof sections, removing standing water, and beginning structural drying with commercial-grade desiccant and refrigerant dehumidifiers calibrated for Florida's ambient humidity levels. Debris removal, downed tree limbs, displaced roofing materials, damaged siding, is coordinated alongside drying so the two phases don't work against each other. Throughout the process, drying logs are maintained to IICRC S500 and S520 standards, giving your adjuster a verifiable record of the remediation timeline.
