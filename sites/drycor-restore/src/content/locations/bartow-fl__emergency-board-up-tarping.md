Bartow sits in the heart of Polk County, where late-summer storms can stall over the city for hours, hurricane-season wind gusts routinely peel back aging rooflines, and a single afternoon of heavy rain can push water through a freshly broken window or compromised soffit before a homeowner even realizes the storm has passed. When that happens, the window between exposure and serious interior damage is measured in hours, not days. DRYCOR RESTORE responds around the clock to board up openings and install heavy-duty roof tarps so that what starts as storm damage stays storm damage, rather than becoming a mold or structural problem on top of it.

## Why Bartow Properties Face Repeated Board-Up and Tarping Needs

Bartow's housing stock tells a story in wood and masonry. A significant share of homes in the city date to the mid-twentieth century, when construction relied on single-pane windows, wood-frame walls with little sheathing, and rooflines that were never engineered for the kind of sustained wind loads that a Polk County tropical system delivers. Older fascia boards rot quietly behind intact paint, and a gust that would leave a newer home untouched can send that fascia, along with a section of soffit or a few courses of shingles, into the yard.

The local climate compounds the problem. Bartow averages well over fifty inches of rain annually, and the rainy season stretches from June through September. A roof breach that occurs during that window has almost no dry margin for error. Standing water on attic decking can begin supporting mold colonization within 24 to 48 hours in Florida's humidity, which means a tarp installed the same night as the damage is not a precaution, it is a structural and health decision.

Fire damage is the other common driver. A kitchen fire or electrical event that vents through a window or burns through a wall leaves an opening that invites both weather and unauthorized entry. Boarding those openings quickly is typically a condition of the homeowner's insurance policy, and it protects the claim from being complicated by secondary damage that occurred after the fire was out.

## Our Emergency Board-Up and Tarping Process in Bartow

When a call comes in, the crew assesses the scope before arriving so the right materials are on the truck: plywood cut to standard window and door sizes, heavy-gauge polyethylene tarps rated for wind uplift, cap nails, furring strips, and the fastening hardware needed to anchor into tile, metal, or asphalt shingle rooflines without voiding a future repair.

On arrival, the priority is securing the roof first if there is active weather, then addressing wall and window openings. Tarps are lapped and fastened with furring strips rather than stapled loosely, which matters in Bartow's wind environment. A tarp that lifts and flaps in a 40-mph gust does almost no good. Plywood panels are cut to fit, not approximate, because a gap at the edge of a board-up is an entry point for both water and pests.

The crew photographs every opening before and after, documents materials used, and provides a written scope that your insurance adjuster can work directly from. DRYCOR RESTORE is an IICRC Certified Firm, and the documentation standards that apply to water and mold work carry over to board-up and tarping so the paper trail supports your claim from the first hour.

## Local Note

Bartow is the Polk County seat, and a number of its older residential blocks sit close to the historic downtown core where homes were built on pier-and-beam or block foundations rather than modern slabs. Those foundation types allow air movement beneath the floor system, which is generally good for ventilation but means that when a roof breach or broken window lets rain in, water can migrate under the house as well as through it. When tarping a home in one of these older blocks, the crew checks for signs of water intrusion at the base of exterior walls and documents anything that suggests the breach has been open longer than the homeowner realized.
