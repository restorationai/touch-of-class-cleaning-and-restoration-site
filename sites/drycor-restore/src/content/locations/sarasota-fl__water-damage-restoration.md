## Why Sarasota Properties See Water Damage Differently

Sarasota sits on a narrow coastal peninsula with a water table that rises dramatically during the rainy season, which typically runs from June through September. That high water table means that even a modest plumbing failure can saturate the soil beneath a slab foundation before any water appears at the surface inside the home. Concrete block construction, common across Sarasota's mid-century neighborhoods, is dense enough to trap moisture inside the masonry itself, where it can persist long after the visible surface reads dry on a basic moisture meter. Standard pin meters miss this entirely. Thermal imaging and deep-wall probes are the only reliable way to confirm drying progress in these walls.

Sarasota also sits in a high-wind zone, and hurricane-season roof damage is a frequent secondary cause of water intrusion. A compromised ridge vent or a cracked tile on a barrel-roof home lets wind-driven rain into the attic, where it soaks insulation and drips onto ceiling drywall for days before the homeowner notices a stain. By the time that stain appears, the framing above it may already be holding significant moisture.
