## Why Dunedin Properties See Mold Issues

Dunedin's housing stock tells most of the story. A large share of the city's residential properties were built between the 1950s and 1980s, when concrete block construction was standard across Pinellas County. Block walls hold moisture differently than wood-framed construction, surface readings can look dry while the interior of a wall cavity stays damp for weeks after a plumbing event or roof intrusion. Add to that the city's salt-air exposure along the waterfront corridor and the high ambient humidity that rarely dips low enough to allow passive drying, and you have a combination that keeps mold remediation contractors busy year-round.

Dunedin also sits in a region that receives some of Florida's heaviest summer rainfall. Afternoon thunderstorms drive water under sliding glass doors, through aging window seals, and into attic spaces where fiberglass insulation batts can hold moisture invisibly. Homes near the causeway and along Edgewater Drive, where older construction meets direct coastal exposure, tend to show recurring mold patterns in attic decking and soffit areas that are easy to miss on a casual inspection.

## Our Mold Remediation Process in Dunedin

Every remediation starts with a thorough moisture investigation, not just a visual scan. We use thermal imaging cameras and calibrated moisture meters to map hidden saturation inside block walls, under tile floors, and behind built-in cabinetry, the places where mold establishes itself before it's visible. That moisture map drives the scope of work, so containment barriers go up in the right places and no affected material is left behind.

Containment is built to IICRC S520 standard: negative air pressure, poly barriers, and HEPA-filtered air scrubbers running continuously throughout the work area. Affected porous materials, drywall, insulation, wood framing, are removed and bagged for disposal. Hard surfaces are treated with EPA-registered antimicrobial agents. Once remediation is complete, we coordinate independent post-clearance air sampling before the work area is reopened, so you have a third-party record that the space is clean, not just our word for it.

As an IICRC Certified Firm with NAERMC mold certification and EPA Lead-Safe certification, the crew working in your home is trained specifically for mold abatement, including the lead-paint protocols that matter in Dunedin's older housing stock.

## Local Note

Dunedin's concrete block homes from the 1960s and 1970s were often built with minimal vapor barriers between the block and interior finishes. When a plumbing leak or roof intrusion wets the interior face of a block wall, the block itself can wick and store moisture for an extended period, long after the drywall on the interior side has been removed and the space looks dry. Remediation in these homes requires extended post-demolition drying cycles and moisture verification inside the block cores before any rebuild begins. Skipping that step is one of the most common reasons mold returns in Dunedin's older properties.
