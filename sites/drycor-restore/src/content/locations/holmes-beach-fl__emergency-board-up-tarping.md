Holmes Beach sits on Anna Maria Island, a narrow barrier island where Gulf-front exposure, salt air, and the threat of named storms make property damage a recurring reality rather than a rare event. When a hurricane shutter fails, a roof section lifts, or a fire leaves a home open to the elements, every hour of delay invites the next problem: wind-driven rain, opportunistic intrusion, and the kind of structural deterioration that turns a manageable loss into a major rebuild. DRYCOR RESTORE responds around the clock to secure properties on the island before secondary damage compounds the original loss.

## Why Holmes Beach Properties Face Distinct Board-Up and Tarping Challenges

Anna Maria Island's geography shapes the damage patterns here in ways that don't apply to inland Manatee County. The island is narrow enough that storm surge and wind can arrive from multiple directions simultaneously, meaning a single weather event can compromise a roof, shatter a window on the leeward side, and push water under a door threshold all at once. Homes along the Gulf side of Holmes Beach are especially exposed during tropical systems, but even bay-side properties on the island's eastern edge see wind-driven rain channel through any unprotected opening.

The housing stock adds another layer of complexity. A significant portion of Holmes Beach homes were built in the 1950s through the 1970s, before current Florida Building Code wind-load requirements took effect. Older wood-frame construction with original roofing systems is more susceptible to partial roof loss during high-wind events than newer concrete block homes built to post-2002 standards. When a section of aged decking lifts, the opening is often irregular and larger than a standard panel size, which means tarping must be anchored with greater care to account for the structural variation underneath.

Salt air accelerates corrosion on fasteners and flashing, which can cause roofing components to fail at lower wind speeds than the same materials would inland. That failure mode shows up repeatedly in board-up and tarping calls after Gulf storms that wouldn't have caused the same damage in Tampa or Brandon.

## Our Emergency Board-Up and Tarping Process in Holmes Beach

When a call comes in from Holmes Beach, the first step is a rapid assessment of what's exposed and what's at immediate risk. That means identifying every unprotected opening, including windows, doors, damaged soffits, and any roof breach, before prioritizing the sequence of work. A compromised roof takes priority over a broken window when rain is in the forecast, but both get addressed on the same visit.

For window and door board-up, we use exterior-grade plywood cut to fit and fastened in a way that meets the practical requirements of Florida's wind zone without damaging the surrounding frame beyond what the original event already caused. On older Holmes Beach homes with wood window casings, that distinction matters: improper fastening can split aged trim and create additional repair work.

Roof tarping on barrier island properties requires heavy-duty polyethylene sheeting rated for the conditions, secured with weighted ballast and mechanical anchoring rather than relying solely on adhesive or lightweight tie-downs that Gulf winds can defeat. We extend tarps past the damaged area to account for the directional variability of island wind patterns, and we document every step with photographs for your insurance claim.

## Local Note

Homes on the Gulf side of Holmes Beach that have installed hurricane shutters sometimes present an unexpected complication after storm damage: the shutter track itself can warp or the panels can jam in a partially open position, leaving a gap that standard board-up panels don't fit cleanly. When we encounter this, we work around the existing shutter hardware rather than forcing removal, which can cause additional frame damage and complicate the shutter repair that follows. It's a detail that matters on an island where replacement parts for older shutter systems sometimes have to be ordered from off-island suppliers.
