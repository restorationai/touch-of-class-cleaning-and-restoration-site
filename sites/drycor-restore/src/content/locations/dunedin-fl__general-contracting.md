## Why Dunedin Homes Present Distinct Renovation Challenges

Dunedin's housing stock skews older, a significant share of the city's single-family homes were built between the 1940s and the 1970s, when construction norms looked very different. Slab foundations from that era often lack the vapor barriers now required by code, which means moisture migrates upward into subfloor assemblies and can compromise new flooring before it's even finished. Older electrical panels, 60-amp or 100-amp services with fuse blocks, frequently can't support a modern kitchen or primary bath remodel without a panel upgrade, and that work has to be permitted through Pinellas County before framing begins.

The salt-air environment accelerates corrosion on fasteners, flashing, and any exposed metal in exterior assemblies. A remodel that opens up an exterior wall often reveals rusted hurricane straps, corroded joist hangers, or deteriorated window pan flashing that wasn't visible from inside. Budgeting for that discovery phase, and having a licensed general contractor who can coordinate the structural repair alongside the finish work, is what separates a smooth Dunedin renovation from one that stalls at inspection.

## Our Renovation and General Contracting Process in Dunedin

Every project starts with a scope walk that treats the building like it has something to hide, because older Dunedin homes usually do. We document existing conditions before demolition begins: moisture readings in subfloor assemblies, visible signs of prior water intrusion at soffits and fascia, and the condition of any load-bearing walls that the design touches. That documentation protects you if a hidden defect surfaces mid-project and affects the budget.

Permitting runs through the City of Dunedin Building Division for work within city limits. We prepare the permit application, coordinate inspections, and keep the project on the inspection schedule so trades aren't waiting on a rough-in sign-off before the next phase can begin. For post-damage rebuilds, fire, water, or mold remediation that transitions into reconstruction, we carry the work from the remediation phase through the finished rebuild under a single point of contact, which simplifies both the timeline and the insurance documentation.

On the finish side, material selections account for the climate. Solid hardwood flooring expands and contracts aggressively in Dunedin's humidity swings; engineered hardwood or large-format porcelain is often the more durable choice for ground-level living spaces. Exterior paint and caulk specs are chosen for UV resistance and flexibility, not just color.

## Local Note

Dunedin's older concrete block homes, CBS construction, common throughout the city from the 1950s onward, behave differently in a remodel than wood-frame houses. Cutting new door or window openings in a CBS wall requires a lintel pour and typically a structural engineer's sign-off before the permit is issued. Contractors unfamiliar with CBS work sometimes underbid this phase or sequence it incorrectly, which stalls the project at framing inspection. It's a detail that comes up often enough in Dunedin that we build the engineering coordination into the project schedule from the start rather than treating it as a change order.
