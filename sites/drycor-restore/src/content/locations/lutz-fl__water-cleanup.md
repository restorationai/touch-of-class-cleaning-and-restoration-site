## Why Lutz Properties See Water Damage More Often Than You'd Expect

The geography here works against homeowners. Lutz occupies a transition zone where the Hillsborough River watershed meets a network of retention ponds and low-lying subdivisions. During the rainy season, roughly June through September, the water table rises close enough to the surface that even a modest roof leak or a slow supply-line failure can saturate a slab or crawl space before it shows up on a ceiling. Block homes built in the 1970s and early 1980s, common throughout older Lutz neighborhoods, have concrete masonry walls that wick moisture laterally; the inside surface can feel dry while the block cores hold water for days. Newer stucco-clad frame homes present a different problem: water that breaches the exterior envelope often travels behind the sheathing before it ever reaches the drywall, making the source harder to trace without thermal imaging.

Flood water cleanup after heavy rain is a recurring call for this area. ## Our Water Cleanup Process in Lutz

Every job starts with a full moisture assessment before a single pump is placed. Thermal imaging cameras and calibrated moisture meters map where water has traveled, not just where it's visible. In block construction, that means probing wall cavities and checking the interior face of exterior walls, where condensation and intrusion can hide behind paint for weeks. In newer frame homes, it means checking the wall assembly from the top plate down.

Extraction comes next. Truck-mounted and portable extraction units pull standing water from flooring, subfloor, and carpet padding. Flooring decisions, whether to save hardwood, tile, or laminate, are made based on moisture readings, not appearance alone. Wet materials that look intact can be harboring enough moisture to support mold colonization within 24 to 48 hours under Florida's humidity conditions.

Drying is calibrated to the structure. DRYCOR RESTORE's crews set industrial desiccant and refrigerant dehumidifiers alongside high-velocity air movers, and daily moisture readings track whether the drying curve is progressing correctly. The IICRC S500 standard guides the drying protocol, readings are logged and documented throughout, which matters when an insurance carrier asks for a drying record.
