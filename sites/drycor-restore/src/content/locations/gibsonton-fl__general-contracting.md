## Why Gibsonton Properties Present Unique Renovation Challenges

Hillsborough County's low-elevation Gulf Coast fringe, including the communities along U.S. 41 south of Riverview, carries a specific set of building conditions that affect every phase of a remodel. Slab foundations in this area are often poured directly on fill material that can compress unevenly over time, which means a kitchen remodel that looks like a straightforward cabinet-and-countertop swap can reveal out-of-level floors that need addressing before any finish work begins. Older homes in the area, particularly those built before modern moisture barriers became standard, frequently show evidence of long-term humidity intrusion inside wall cavities, even without a discrete water-damage event. Opening those walls during a bathroom remodel or an addition is when that history becomes visible.

The climate adds another layer. Gibsonton averages more than 50 inches of rain per year, much of it concentrated in the June-through-September window. Any exterior work, roofline changes, window replacements, or structural additions requires tight scheduling around that pattern. A project that misses the dry-season window can sit exposed during afternoon storm season, and the difference between a properly sequenced job and a rushed one shows up in moisture readings months later.

One of the more common calls DRYCOR RESTORE receives in this area follows a water loss or fire event where the insurance carrier has approved a rebuild scope and the homeowner needs a single contractor to carry the work from dried-out structure to finished living space.
