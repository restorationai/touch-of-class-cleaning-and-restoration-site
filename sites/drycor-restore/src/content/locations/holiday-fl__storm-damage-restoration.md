## Why Holiday Properties Face Distinct Storm Damage Risks

Holiday's housing stock tells a specific story. A large share of the community was built during the 1960s through the 1980s, when concrete block construction was standard but modern hurricane strapping and impact-rated windows were not. That means older roofs may be attached with methods that predate current Florida Building Code requirements, and when a storm pushes wind speeds high enough, the failure points are predictable: lifted roof decking, blown soffit panels, and water intrusion along the fascia line.

The area's low elevation and proximity to the Gulf also mean that even storms that don't make direct landfall nearby can push enough surge and rainfall to overwhelm drainage. Flat lots shed water slowly, and when soil saturation reaches its limit, that water finds the path of least resistance, which is often a foundation crack, a garage door seal, or an older sliding glass door track. Tree canopy throughout Holiday's residential streets adds another layer of risk: mature oaks and pines that haven't been trimmed create significant projectile and fall hazards during high-wind events.

## Our Storm Damage Restoration Process in Holiday

When a storm damage call comes in from Holiday, the first priority is a thorough damage assessment before any materials are moved or covered. Wind damage and water intrusion interact in ways that aren't always visible at first: water that entered through a roof breach may travel along ceiling joists and pool inside a wall cavity, reading dry at the surface while the framing behind it stays saturated.

The process moves through these stages:

- **Emergency stabilization**: tarping compromised roof sections, boarding breached windows or doors, and containing any active water intrusion.
- **Water extraction and moisture mapping**: using thermal imaging and calibrated moisture meters to locate hidden saturation, then extracting standing water and placing drying equipment.
- **Structural assessment**: identifying materials that can be dried in place versus those that require controlled demolition to prevent mold colonization. The IICRC S500 and S520 standards guide these decisions.
- **Debris and tree damage cleanup**: removing fallen limbs, displaced roofing materials, and storm debris from the property before reconstruction begins.
- **Documentation**: photographing every affected area, logging moisture readings across multiple days, and preparing a detailed scope of loss for your insurance carrier.

DRYCOR RESTORE is an IICRC Certified Firm with technicians holding WRT credentials, and the team is EPA Lead-Safe Certified, which matters in Holiday's older housing stock where lead-based paint can be disturbed during storm-related demolition.
