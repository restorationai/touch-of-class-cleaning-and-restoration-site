## Why Largo Properties See Water Damage Differently Than Most Florida Cities

Largo's housing stock is older than many people expect. Significant portions of the city were built out in the 1950s through 1970s, which means a large share of homes have original cast-iron or galvanized-steel drain lines that corrode from the inside out over decades. A slow pinhole leak behind a vanity or under a terrazzo floor can saturate the subfloor and wall cavities for weeks before it surfaces as a stain or a musty odor. Terrazzo itself, common in mid-century Largo homes, is dense and non-porous on top, but the sand-and-cement bed beneath it acts like a sponge once water finds a seam.

The city's low elevation and high water table compound the problem. During heavy rainfall events, which Pinellas County sees regularly from June through October, stormwater can back up through floor drains in older homes that lack modern backflow prevention. That water is a different category of contamination than a clean supply-line break, and it requires a different extraction and sanitation protocol.

## Our Water Damage Restoration Process in Largo

The first step on every job is containment and assessment, not guesswork. Technicians use thermal imaging cameras and calibrated moisture meters to map exactly where water has traveled, including inside wall cavities and beneath flooring, before a single piece of material is removed. In Largo's older concrete-block construction, water can wick laterally through the block itself, appearing dry at the surface while the interior stays saturated.

Once the moisture map is complete, the process moves through extraction, evaporation, and dehumidification in sequence:

- **Extraction**, truck-mounted and portable extractors pull standing water and water trapped in carpet, pad, and subfloor materials.
- **Structural drying**, high-velocity air movers and commercial-grade desiccant or refrigerant dehumidifiers are positioned according to the moisture map, not placed generically around the room.
- **Monitoring**, readings are logged at each visit until materials reach the dry standard defined by the IICRC S500, the industry protocol for water damage response, so drying decisions are data-driven, not visual.
- **Controlled demolition, if needed**, when wall cavities or flooring systems cannot be dried in place, affected materials are removed to allow airflow to reach the structure behind them.

All work is documented with photographs and moisture logs, the same records your insurance adjuster will need to process the claim.
