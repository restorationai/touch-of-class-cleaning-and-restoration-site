## Why Bartow Properties See Water Damage Issues

Polk County's sandy, high-permeability soils drain quickly in some spots and hold water at the surface in others, depending on how close a property sits to one of the area's many lakes or low-lying drainage corridors. Bartow itself, as the county seat, has a dense core of older government and commercial buildings alongside residential neighborhoods where homes were built before modern moisture barriers and vapor retarders were standard practice. Concrete block construction, common throughout central Florida from the 1950s onward, is deceptively porous. A slow roof leak or a plumbing failure inside a CBS (concrete block structure) wall can saturate the block itself, the interior furring, and any insulation behind it before a single water stain appears on the drywall surface.

Bartow also sits in a region where afternoon convective storms can drop several inches of rain in under an hour. When gutters and downspouts are undersized for that kind of volume, water finds its way into soffit vents, around window frames, and through foundation weep screed openings. Seasonal flooding along low-lying streets near the city's older drainage infrastructure is a recurring issue, particularly after consecutive storm days when the ground is already saturated.

## Our Water Damage Restoration Process in Bartow

The first step on any Bartow job is a thorough moisture assessment using thermal imaging and calibrated moisture meters. In older homes, this matters especially because surface readings can be misleading. A concrete block wall may feel dry to the touch while the block cores and interior framing register well above the acceptable moisture threshold. We map every affected area before any equipment is placed.

Once the scope is documented, standing water is removed using truck-mounted and portable extraction units. Structural drying follows, with industrial air movers and low-grain refrigerant dehumidifiers positioned according to the IICRC S500 standard for psychrometric drying. In Bartow's humid subtropical climate, ambient outdoor humidity frequently runs above 70 percent, which means equipment selection and placement have to account for the moisture load the building envelope itself is pulling in. We monitor drying progress with daily readings and adjust equipment as materials dry at different rates.

If building materials cannot be dried in place within acceptable timelines, controlled demolition removes only what cannot be saved. Baseboards, sections of drywall, or flooring are documented before removal so that scope of loss records are complete for your insurance carrier.

## Local Note

One thing that catches property owners off guard in Bartow's older neighborhoods is the behavior of original terrazzo flooring during a water loss. Terrazzo is dense and non-absorbent at the surface, which can make a room look like it dried quickly. The problem is underneath: the mud bed and sand-set layer beneath terrazzo hold moisture for a long time, and if the subfloor or slab below is not dried properly, that trapped moisture migrates into adjacent wall cavities and framing. We always check beneath terrazzo surfaces with penetrating moisture probes rather than relying on surface readings alone, because what reads dry on top can still be driving mold-growth conditions below.
