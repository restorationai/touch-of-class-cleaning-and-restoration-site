## Why Trinity Properties See Mold Issues

Pasco County's coastal proximity and flat topography mean that moisture has few places to go after heavy rain. Trinity's newer master-planned communities often feature concrete block construction with spray-foam or batt insulation, and when an HVAC system runs continuously to fight the heat, condensation can collect inside wall cavities and above drop ceilings without any visible leak. Older sections of the area closer to State Road 54 tend to have slab-on-grade foundations with minimal vapor barriers, which allows ground moisture to wick upward into flooring adhesives and subfloor materials. Even a slow drip from a supply line inside a cabinet can saturate particleboard within 24 to 48 hours, and mold colonization can begin in as little as 48 to 72 hours under Trinity's typical indoor humidity levels. By the time discoloration appears, the colony is usually well established behind the surface.

## Local Note

Trinity's newer construction, particularly the large-format homes built in the mid-2000s through the 2010s, frequently used engineered lumber and OSB sheathing in roof and wall assemblies. OSB is significantly more vulnerable to mold than traditional plywood because its compressed wood fibers absorb moisture rapidly and are harder to dry once saturated. When a roof leak or HVAC condensate line overflow soaks into OSB sheathing, surface drying can read normal on a pin-type moisture meter while the core of the panel remains wet. Technicians working in Trinity's housing stock use both pin and pinless meters, and sometimes destructive testing, to confirm that sheathing is genuinely dry before containment comes down.
