## Why Seminole Properties See Repeated Storm Damage

Pinellas County's geography puts Seminole in a position where storm tracks from the Gulf and the bay can converge, producing wind-driven rain that behaves differently than a simple downpour. The county sits at low elevation with a high water table, which means saturated soil has nowhere to drain during extended rainfall. Flat lots shed water slowly, and even a few inches of standing water in a yard can push under slab foundations or find its way through weep screeds into block-wall construction.

Much of Seminole's residential housing stock was built between the 1960s and 1980s, a period when Florida building codes were less demanding than current hurricane standards. Older single-story CBS (concrete block stucco) homes in this era often have original roof-to-wall connections and aging fascia that are more vulnerable to uplift than homes built after the 2002 Florida Building Code reforms. Tile roofs from this period can lose individual tiles in winds that newer installations would handle without issue, and once a tile is gone, the underlayment beneath it can saturate in a single storm event.

## Our Storm Damage Restoration Process in Seminole

The first priority after a storm is stopping ongoing water intrusion. That means emergency tarping of compromised roof sections, boarding of broken windows or doors, and a rapid moisture assessment of any interior space that was exposed. We use thermal imaging cameras and calibrated moisture meters to map where water has traveled inside wall cavities and under flooring, because surface readings alone miss saturation that is already migrating toward framing.

Once the structure is stabilized and documented, the restoration sequence follows the IICRC S500 and S520 standards for water and structural drying. Industrial desiccant and refrigerant dehumidifiers, high-velocity air movers, and negative air pressure containment are deployed based on the specific material types affected. CBS construction holds moisture differently than wood-frame, and Seminole's humidity levels during storm season mean ambient conditions work against drying, so equipment placement and runtime are calculated, not estimated.

Debris removal, structural repairs, and coordination with roofing and window contractors happen in parallel with the drying phase. As an IICRC Certified Firm with license #CBC1253966, DRYCOR RESTORE can carry the project from emergency mitigation through reconstruction under a single point of contact.

## Local Note

Seminole's older CBS homes frequently have flat or low-slope roof sections over additions or Florida rooms added in the 1970s and 1980s. These sections use built-up or modified bitumen roofing rather than tile, and they are among the first areas to fail in high-wind events. When a flat roof section is breached, water often travels horizontally along the ceiling plane before dropping, which means the visible ceiling stain may be several feet from the actual entry point. Checking only the area directly below a water stain in this type of construction will miss the source every time.
