## Why Gibsonton Properties See Serious Storm Damage

Hillsborough County's southern lowlands, including the Gibsonton area, sit at elevations that leave little buffer when tropical systems push a storm surge up the bay or when heavy rainfall overwhelms drainage infrastructure. The soil profile here is sandy over clay, which sheds surface water slowly and keeps ground saturation high well after a storm passes. That persistent moisture underneath a structure is what turns a one-day wind event into a two-week drying project.

The housing stock adds another layer of complexity. Gibsonton has a significant share of manufactured and modular homes, many of which have skirting, belly board insulation, and HVAC ductwork running beneath the floor deck. When wind-driven rain or flooding gets under the unit, those components absorb moisture in ways that standard drywall-and-stud construction does not. Roof damage on a manufactured home also tends to expose a larger percentage of the living area per square foot of opening than a comparable breach on a site-built house.

For newer site-built homes in the area, the concern shifts to wind uplift on roof sheathing and the integrity of window and door flashing after impact. Florida's building code requires hurricane strapping on newer construction, but homes built before those standards were widely enforced carry a different risk profile in a high-wind event.

## Our Storm Damage Restoration Process in Gibsonton

The first thing a crew does on arrival is a safety and scope assessment: structural stability, active water intrusion, electrical hazards, and gas line integrity before any equipment goes inside. In Gibsonton, that assessment almost always includes checking the crawl space or undercarriage on manufactured units, where wind-driven water accumulates invisibly.

Once the structure is safe to enter, the process moves in this order:

- **Emergency tarping and board-up** to stop ongoing weather intrusion through roof breaches, broken windows, or damaged doors.
- **Water extraction and moisture mapping** using thermal imaging and calibrated moisture meters to locate saturation behind walls and under flooring, not just at the surface.
- **Controlled drying** with industrial air movers and dehumidifiers sized to the affected volume, documented daily against IICRC S500 drying standards.
- **Debris removal and structural assessment**, including any tree or limb that has contacted the structure.
- **Reconstruction scoping**, coordinated with your insurance adjuster, covering everything from roof decking and sheathing to interior finishes.

DRYCOR RESTORE is an IICRC Certified Firm with technicians holding IICRC Water Restoration Technician credentials, and the company is EPA Lead-Safe Certified, which matters on older Gibsonton properties where disturbing painted surfaces is part of the repair scope.

Most homeowner policies covering storm damage require documented proof of the cause of loss, the scope of damage, and the mitigation steps taken before permanent repairs begin.
