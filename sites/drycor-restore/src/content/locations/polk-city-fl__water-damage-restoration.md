## Why Polk City Properties See Water Damage Issues

The geography here works against homeowners from the start. Polk City is flanked by Lake Arietta and Lake Hatchineha to the south and sits on sandy, high-porosity soils that drain quickly in some spots and hold water stubbornly in others depending on the depth of the clay layer beneath. That inconsistency means crawl spaces and slab edges can stay damp long after the surface appears dry, especially after the extended rain events that push through Central Florida from June through September.

The housing stock adds another layer of complexity. A significant portion of Polk City's residential properties were built during the 1970s and 1980s, when CBS (concrete block and stucco) construction was standard throughout Polk County. Block walls absorb moisture differently than wood-frame construction. Water migrates laterally through the block cores, and standard moisture meters calibrated for drywall can give misleading readings on block surfaces. Accurate drying requires thermal imaging and calibrated penetrating meters to confirm what's actually happening inside the wall assembly, not just at the face.

## Our Water Damage Restoration Process in Polk City

Every water damage call starts with a thorough inspection before any equipment is placed. We identify the water source category (clean supply line, gray water, or sewage), map the affected area with moisture meters and thermal imaging, and document everything photographically for your insurance carrier.

Extraction comes first. High-capacity truck-mounted and portable extractors pull standing water from flooring, carpeting, and subfloor assemblies. For slab-on-grade homes common throughout Polk City, we pay particular attention to the seam between the slab and the base of block walls, where water consistently migrates and hides. After extraction, we set a calculated drying system: commercial-grade LGR dehumidifiers, axial and centrifugal air movers positioned to create a directed airflow pattern, and desiccant units when ambient humidity makes standard refrigerant dehumidifiers less effective.

Drying progress is monitored with daily readings logged against the psychrometric targets established by the IICRC S500 standard. We don't pull equipment based on a calendar. We pull it when the readings confirm the structure has returned to acceptable moisture levels.

## Local Note

CBS construction throughout Polk City creates a drying challenge that catches some restoration crews off guard. The hollow cores in concrete block act as vertical channels, and water introduced at the base of a wall, whether from a slab leak or a storm intrusion at the footer, can travel upward through those cores several feet above the visible water line. Drying the face of the wall to a passing moisture reading doesn't mean the cores are dry. We drill weep holes at the base of affected block runs when necessary to allow trapped moisture to drain and airflow to reach the interior of the wall assembly. It adds a step, but it's the difference between a genuinely dry wall and one that grows mold inside the block six weeks after the crew leaves.
