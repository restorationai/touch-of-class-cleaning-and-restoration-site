## Why Plant City Properties See Mold Issues

Plant City sits in one of Florida's wetter inland corridors. Summer afternoons regularly bring heavy rain, and the soil in much of this area drains slowly, meaning moisture doesn't just fall from the sky, it also wicks upward through slab foundations and into crawl spaces. Homes built in the 1950s through the 1970s, which make up a significant portion of the housing stock in older residential sections of the city, were often constructed before modern vapor barriers became standard. That means concrete block walls and terrazzo floors can hold and transfer ground moisture in ways that newer construction doesn't.

The city's agricultural surroundings add another layer: fields that flood seasonally and irrigation runoff can raise the local water table, pushing moisture toward foundations even during dry spells. Inside the home, older ductwork that sweats in summer or HVAC systems undersized for Florida's humidity load can create persistent condensation on walls and ceilings, conditions that feed mold long before a visible stain appears.

## Our Mold Remediation Process in Plant City

Mold remediation isn't a single step, it's a sequence that has to be done in order, or the problem comes back. The process starts with a thorough moisture investigation using thermal imaging and pin-type moisture meters to find water that isn't visible at the surface. Concrete block construction, common throughout Plant City, can read dry on the face while holding significant moisture behind the block, so surface readings alone aren't enough.

Once the moisture source is confirmed and addressed, the affected area is contained using negative air pressure and physical barriers so that disturbing the mold doesn't spread spores to unaffected rooms. DRYCOR RESTORE holds NAERMC mold certification and operates as an IICRC Certified Firm, so the containment and removal steps follow recognized industry protocols. Affected porous materials, drywall, insulation, wood framing, are removed and bagged for disposal. Structural surfaces are HEPA-vacuumed and treated with an EPA-registered antimicrobial agent. Industrial air scrubbers run throughout and after the work to capture airborne particulates before containment is broken.
