## Why Englewood Properties See Concentrated Storm Damage

Englewood's geography puts it in a vulnerable position. The barrier island exposure along Manasota Key means that even storms that make landfall miles away can push storm surge and wind-driven rain into ground-floor living spaces. Farther inland, the mix of canal-front lots and low-lying streets means that drainage can back up quickly once rainfall rates exceed what the stormwater system can move.

The local housing stock adds another layer of complexity. A significant portion of Englewood's residential properties were built in the 1960s through the 1980s, before modern hurricane-resistant construction codes took hold in Florida. Older concrete block homes in this era often have flat or low-slope roofs that are more vulnerable to uplift damage, and their original windows and doors were not rated for the wind loads that current Florida Building Code requires. When a storm strips flashing or breaks a seal on one of these roofs, water follows the path of least resistance into the attic, down interior walls, and into floor assemblies before anyone realizes the extent of the intrusion.

## Our Storm Damage Restoration Process in Englewood

The first priority after any storm is stopping new water from entering the structure. Our crews carry tarping materials and board-up supplies to secure breached roofs, broken windows, and damaged entry points before any interior work begins. That step is not optional in Englewood's climate: an unsecured opening during a wet season afternoon thunderstorm can undo hours of drying progress.

Once the envelope is secured, the process moves to moisture mapping. We use thermal imaging and calibrated moisture meters to trace water migration through walls, ceilings, and subfloor assemblies. Water that enters through a roof breach does not stay where it lands; it travels along framing members, pools above ceiling drywall, and wicks into insulation before showing any visible staining. Knowing where the moisture actually is determines where equipment gets placed and how long the drying phase needs to run.

Structural drying follows IICRC S500 and S520 standards. Industrial air movers and dehumidifiers are positioned based on the moisture map, not by square footage alone. Daily readings track the drying curve and confirm that materials are returning to acceptable moisture content before any reconstruction begins. This documentation also becomes part of the insurance file.
