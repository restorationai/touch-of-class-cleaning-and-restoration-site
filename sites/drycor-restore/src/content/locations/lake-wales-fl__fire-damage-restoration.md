## Why Lake Wales Properties Face Distinct Fire Damage Challenges

Polk County's ridge communities, including Lake Wales, have a higher concentration of homes built between the 1940s and 1980s than many parts of the Tampa Bay region. Those older structures frequently have original wood-framed interiors, tongue-and-groove pine subfloors, and knob-and-tube or aluminum branch wiring that was common before modern electrical codes caught up. Aluminum wiring in particular is a documented fire-risk factor in homes of that era, and when it does cause a fire, the resulting char often penetrates deeper into framing members than it would in newer construction. Smoke from a slow-burning electrical fire also behaves differently than smoke from a fast kitchen grease fire: it carries more soot proteins, deposits a thicker oily film on surfaces, and embeds into porous materials like plaster and original hardwood at a molecular level that surface cleaning alone cannot address.

The local climate adds another layer. Lake Wales averages over 50 inches of rain per year, and the months immediately following the dry-season fire window bring intense humidity. A fire-damaged home with compromised roof decking or broken windows can absorb enough ambient moisture in 48 to 72 hours to create conditions where mold colonization begins, even before the smoke odor has been fully addressed. That overlap between fire damage and moisture intrusion is something the restoration plan has to account for from day one.

## Our Fire Damage Restoration Process in Lake Wales

The work begins with stabilization: boarding openings, tarping the roof, and securing the structure so no additional weather or intrusion damage compounds the loss. From there, the process follows a documented sequence.

First, a room-by-room assessment identifies the burn zone, the smoke migration path, and any water damage left by suppression efforts. Sprinkler systems and fire hose discharge leave standing water that needs extraction before any drying or cleaning can begin. Next, structural materials are evaluated for salvageability. Char-damaged framing is documented for the insurance scope; materials that can be cleaned and restored are separated from those that require removal.

Smoke and soot cleaning is the most labor-intensive phase. Dry sponge methods remove loose soot before any wet cleaning begins, because introducing moisture to a heavy soot layer drives it deeper into porous surfaces. Chemical sponges, alkaline cleaners, and HEPA-filtered equipment are used in sequence depending on the surface type. Odor control follows, using thermal fogging and hydroxyl generation to neutralize smoke compounds in the air and inside structural cavities, not just on surfaces. The IICRC-certified team at DRYCOR RESTORE applies these standards to every fire loss regardless of size.

Finally, reconstruction begins once the structure is clean, dry, and documented. That can mean anything from replacing drywall in a single room to rebuilding a roof assembly, and the scope is coordinated directly with your insurance adjuster.

## Local Note

Homes in the Lake Wales area that were built before the 1980s often have original plaster-over-wood-lath wall systems rather than drywall. After a fire, these walls present a specific challenge: the plaster itself may look intact and cleanable, but smoke and heat can travel through the lath gaps and deposit soot and odor compounds inside the wall cavity where no surface cleaning tool can reach. In those situations, selective demolition of the plaster is sometimes the only way to fully neutralize the odor and verify that the framing behind it is structurally sound. It is a more involved process than cutting out drywall, but skipping it typically means the smoke smell returns within weeks once the home is reoccupied and the HVAC system begins circulating air through those cavities again.
