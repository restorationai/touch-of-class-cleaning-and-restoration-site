## Why Dundee Properties See Mold Issues

Polk County's ridge-and-lake geography means Dundee homes often sit on sandy soils with limited natural drainage. After heavy rain, groundwater can wick upward through slab foundations or pool against block-wall crawl spaces, keeping structural materials damp for days longer than homeowners expect. Older concrete block construction, common throughout this part of central Florida, is porous enough to absorb moisture without showing obvious surface staining, which means mold can colonize interior wall cavities well before a musty odor becomes noticeable.

Central Florida's mold season does not have an off switch the way it might in a northern climate. Relative humidity in Dundee regularly climbs above 70 percent even in winter months, and HVAC systems running continuously to manage indoor temperatures can develop condensation in ductwork and air handler cabinets. When those systems are not serviced regularly, they become a distribution network for mold spores throughout the home.

## Our Mold Remediation Process in Dundee

Every project begins with a thorough inspection using moisture meters and thermal imaging to map hidden saturation in walls, ceilings, and subfloors. Surface mold is rarely the whole story, and we do not treat it as if it were.

Once the scope is clear, the affected area is sealed with polyethylene containment barriers and placed under negative air pressure using HEPA-filtered air scrubbers. This keeps spores from migrating to unaffected rooms during removal. Contaminated drywall, insulation, and porous materials are removed, bagged, and disposed of according to Florida Department of Environmental Protection guidelines. Remaining structural surfaces are HEPA-vacuumed, treated with an EPA-registered antimicrobial solution, and dried to verified moisture content before any reconstruction begins.

Because Dundee homes often feature block construction with interior furring strips and older fiberglass batt insulation, our crews are accustomed to removing and replacing these assemblies rather than attempting surface-only treatment, which rarely resolves the underlying saturation in that building type.
