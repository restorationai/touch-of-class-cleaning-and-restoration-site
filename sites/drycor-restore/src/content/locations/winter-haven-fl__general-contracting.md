## Why Winter Haven's Building Stock Shapes Every Remodel

A significant portion of Winter Haven's residential inventory was built between the 1950s and 1980s, a period when concrete block construction was the dominant method across Polk County. CBS (concrete block and stucco) homes are durable, but they present specific challenges during renovation: running new electrical or plumbing often means cutting through masonry rather than simply routing through a stud bay, and original windows in that era were frequently single-pane jalousie or aluminum-frame units that no longer meet current Florida Building Code energy requirements. Replacing them isn't optional on a permitted remodel, it's required.

Adding to that complexity, lakefront and low-lying properties throughout the area can have slab moisture issues that only become visible once flooring is pulled. A kitchen or bathroom remodel that looks straightforward on the surface sometimes reveals moisture intrusion at the slab edge or deteriorated subfloor framing, problems that need to be addressed before any finish work begins, not patched over afterward.

## Local Note: Slab Moisture and Tile Remodels Near the Lakes

In Winter Haven's lakefront and low-elevation neighborhoods, slab-on-grade homes can experience what's sometimes called "slab sweating", moisture vapor migrating upward through the concrete, especially during the transition between Florida's dry and wet seasons. This matters most in kitchen and bathroom remodels where new tile or luxury vinyl plank is going down. Installing flooring directly over a slab with elevated moisture vapor emission without a proper vapor barrier or moisture-tolerant adhesive system is a common shortcut that leads to adhesive failure and grout cracking within a year or two. We test slab moisture before any flooring goes down, it adds a step, but it's the step that keeps the finished floor from failing before the warranty expires.
