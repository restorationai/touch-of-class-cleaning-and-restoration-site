## Why Plant City Properties Face Elevated Board-Up and Tarping Needs

Plant City's climate is the defining factor. The city sits in one of the most lightning-active corridors in the United States, and the same convective storms that produce that lightning regularly generate localized wind gusts strong enough to cause structural damage. A compromised roof or broken window left open overnight doesn't just let in rain, it creates the moisture conditions that allow mold to begin colonizing porous materials within 24 to 48 hours.

The local housing stock adds another layer of complexity. Significant portions of Plant City's residential neighborhoods include homes built in the mid-twentieth century, when construction methods relied on wood-framed walls, single-pane windows, and roofing systems that are now well past their designed service life. Older rooflines with worn flashing and aging shingles are more likely to sustain damage in a storm, and more likely to need immediate tarping to prevent water infiltration from compounding a fire or wind loss.

The agricultural character of the surrounding area also matters. Properties on the outskirts of the city, including those near the strawberry fields and rural corridors that define much of eastern Hillsborough County, often have detached structures, pole barns, and outbuildings that sustain damage alongside the main residence. Board-up and tarping needs here can be more extensive than a single-family suburban loss.

## Our Emergency Board-Up and Tarping Process in Plant City

When a call comes in, the first priority is a rapid site assessment to identify every opening, broken windows, compromised doors, missing roof sections, and any structural gaps that weren't immediately obvious. In fire damage situations, that assessment also accounts for areas weakened by heat exposure that may not look damaged but could fail under the weight of a tarp or boarding material.

For window and door openings, we use exterior-grade plywood cut and secured to protect against both weather intrusion and unauthorized entry. For roof damage, heavy-duty polyethylene tarps rated for wind and UV exposure are anchored with batten boards and fastened to undamaged decking, not just draped over the peak. The method matters: a tarp that lifts in the next storm does nothing to protect the interior.

On properties where fire damage is involved, we coordinate the board-up sequence with the structure's stability. Charred framing and heat-compromised walls affect where and how fasteners can be set, and our OSHA-trained crew accounts for those conditions before anyone works at elevation.

## Plant City Insurance Coordination

Most homeowner policies cover emergency protective measures, board-up and tarping are generally classified as mitigation services, meaning the cost is often included in the same claim as the underlying loss. DRYCOR documents every opening, photographs conditions before and after boarding, and provides the written scope that carriers and adjusters need to process that portion of the claim. Keeping thorough documentation from the first hour on-site reduces disputes about what was damaged and what was done to prevent further loss.

## Local Note

Plant City's older bungalow-style homes, particularly those built in the 1940s and 1950s in the blocks surrounding the historic downtown core, frequently have roof structures with lower pitches and wider overhangs than modern construction. That geometry affects how tarps must be anchored: a standard ridge-and-drape approach doesn't seal the edges effectively on a low-slope roof, and water will track under the tarp along the overhang line. Crews familiar with this housing pattern adjust anchor points and tarp overlap accordingly, which makes a meaningful difference in how much water stays out before permanent repairs begin.
