## Why Mulberry Properties See Storm Damage Differently

The landscape around Mulberry is shaped by decades of phosphate mining, which left behind reclaimed land, shallow water tables, and soil profiles that drain unevenly. When a severe storm drops two or three inches of rain in under an hour, that water has nowhere to go fast. It pools against foundations, backs up through low-lying yard grades, and finds its way into crawl spaces and slab perimeters. Homes built in the 1960s and 1970s, which make up a significant portion of Mulberry's housing stock, often have original single-pane windows and aging roof decking that offers less resistance to wind-driven rain than newer construction.

Tornado activity is not rare in this part of Polk County. The same atmospheric conditions that fuel afternoon thunderstorms can spin up brief but violent tornadoes that take down fences, carports, and trees with root systems loosened by saturated soil. Tree damage cleanup in these situations is rarely just about the tree: a fallen oak can puncture a roof deck, shear gutters, and leave a jagged opening that a tarp alone cannot fully seal against the next afternoon storm.

## Our Storm Damage Restoration Process in Mulberry

The first priority when we arrive is stopping active water intrusion. That means emergency board-up and tarping of roof breaches, temporary covering of broken windows, and a full exterior walk to identify every point where water has entered or is likely to enter. From there, the process moves inside.

Moisture mapping comes next. We use thermal imaging and calibrated moisture meters to trace where water has traveled through wall cavities, under flooring, and into structural framing. In Mulberry's older ranch-style homes, water that enters through a roof breach can wick laterally through blown-in insulation and show up in a wall twelve feet from the original opening. Identifying that spread early is what separates a contained repair from a months-long mold remediation project.

Once the scope is documented, we extract standing water, remove saturated materials that cannot be dried in place, and deploy drying equipment calibrated to the ambient humidity conditions. Florida's baseline humidity means drying targets here are not the same as they would be in a drier climate, and we adjust our equipment placement and monitoring schedules accordingly. Our team holds IICRC certification and follows the S500 and S700 drying standards throughout the process.
