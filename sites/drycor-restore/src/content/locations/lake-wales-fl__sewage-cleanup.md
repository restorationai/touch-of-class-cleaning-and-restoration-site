## Why Lake Wales Properties See Sewage Backup Issues

The geology beneath Lake Wales is part of the answer. The region's sandy, high-porosity soils drain surface water quickly but offer little resistance when the water table rises after heavy rain, which in central Florida can happen repeatedly between June and October. That rising table puts hydrostatic pressure on septic tanks, drain fields, and older clay sewer laterals, pushing effluent back toward the lowest fixture in the house.

The local housing stock adds another layer. Municipal sewer capacity in older sections of the city can also be a factor during peak storm events, when inflow and infiltration overwhelm collection lines and force sewage back through residential connections. Homes on septic in the more rural stretches of the Lake Wales area face a different but related risk: drain fields that have been stressed by years of high water table conditions can fail without warning, sending untreated effluent to the surface or back into the structure.

## Our Sewage Cleanup and Sanitization Process in Lake Wales

Raw sewage is classified as Category 3 water, the most contaminated class under IICRC standards, and the cleanup protocol reflects that. The process begins with containment: affected areas are isolated to prevent cross-contamination to unaffected rooms, and the crew establishes negative air pressure where needed to control odor and airborne particulates.

Extraction comes next. Standing sewage is removed using truck-mounted or portable extraction equipment, depending on access. In Lake Wales's slab-on-grade homes, which are common throughout Polk County, sewage can wick into the concrete and into the base of drywall before it's visible at the surface. Moisture mapping with thermal imaging and calibrated meters identifies the full extent of saturation before any material decisions are made.

Contaminated porous materials, including drywall, insulation, and flooring, are removed and bagged for proper disposal. Hard surfaces are then cleaned, disinfected, and treated with EPA-registered antimicrobials. The IICRC S500 standard guides drying protocols throughout, and the structure is not cleared until post-remediation testing confirms contamination levels are within acceptable limits.
