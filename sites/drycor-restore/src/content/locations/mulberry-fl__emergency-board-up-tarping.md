## Why Mulberry Properties Are Especially Vulnerable After a Loss

Mulberry's history as a phosphate-mining town shaped the way the city grew, and that history shows up in the housing stock. Many homes in and around the older residential corridors near downtown date to the mid-20th century, when frame construction with wood-lap siding was standard. That siding and the underlying sheathing absorb moisture quickly once a wall or roof is breached. Florida's humidity rarely drops below 60 percent even on clear days, so an unprotected opening does not just let in rain. It lets in warm, saturated air that begins feeding mold growth on wood framing within 24 to 48 hours.

The local soil profile, influenced by decades of phosphate extraction in the broader Polk County area, also means drainage patterns can be unpredictable. Water that enters through a damaged roof or broken window does not always travel where you expect. Pooling under flooring or behind walls can go undetected until a tarp and board-up crew documents the full extent of the opening during their initial assessment.

## Local Note

Mulberry and the surrounding Polk County corridor see a specific pattern worth knowing: homes built during the phosphate-boom decades often have low-pitched roofs that were designed for a drier climate assumption than Florida actually delivers. When a low-pitch roof loses shingles, water does not shed the way it would on a steeper slope. It ponds at the breach point and wicks laterally under the remaining shingles before it ever reaches the interior. A tarp that only covers the visible damage zone on a low-pitch roof can still allow water intrusion two or three feet to either side. DRYCOR RESTORE extends tarp coverage well beyond the visible damage boundary on these roof profiles, which is a detail that makes a real difference on Mulberry's older housing stock.
