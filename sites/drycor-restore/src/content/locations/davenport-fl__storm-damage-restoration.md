## Why Davenport Properties Are Especially Vulnerable to Storm Damage

Davenport's rapid residential growth over the past two decades means the area has a wide mix of construction vintages sitting side by side. Newer master-planned communities built in the 2000s and 2010s often feature concrete block construction with tile roofs, which handle wind reasonably well but are susceptible to flying debris cracking tiles and allowing water to wick into the underlayment. Older ranch-style homes on the city's western edges may still carry original 3-tab shingles with limited wind ratings, making them more likely to lose field shingles in a 60-mph gust than a newer structure would.

The sandy, well-drained soils that make Polk County attractive for development also mean that when storm runoff overwhelms surface drainage, it tends to pool against foundations and find any gap in slab or stem-wall construction. Florida's flat topography gives water nowhere to go quickly, so a property that looks fine from the street may have standing water under a raised section or inside wall cavities within hours of a major storm.

Davenport also sits close enough to the tourist corridor along US-192 that commercial properties, short-term rental units, and HOA-governed communities make up a significant share of the local building stock. Storm damage in those settings carries revenue and compliance pressure that a single-family loss does not.

## Our Storm Damage Restoration Process in Davenport

The first step on any storm loss is a thorough exterior and interior assessment before any materials are moved or discarded. Roof penetrations, window failures, and soffit breaches each create different moisture pathways, and misreading the entry point leads to incomplete drying. Our IICRC-certified technicians use thermal imaging and moisture meters to map the actual wet zone, not just the visually obvious damage.

Once the scope is documented, emergency tarping or board-up secures the structure against additional weather exposure. In Davenport's active storm season, a property left open overnight can sustain a second water intrusion before the first one is even measured. After the structure is stabilized, debris removal begins, followed by controlled drying using commercial-grade dehumidifiers and air movers calibrated to the humidity levels typical of Central Florida summers, where ambient moisture in the air actively works against the drying process.

Structural repairs, including roof decking, framing, drywall, and flooring, follow once moisture readings confirm the assembly has reached its drying goal. Because DRYCOR RESTORE holds a contractor license (#CBC1253966), the same team that handled mitigation can carry the job through reconstruction without a handoff to a separate contractor.
