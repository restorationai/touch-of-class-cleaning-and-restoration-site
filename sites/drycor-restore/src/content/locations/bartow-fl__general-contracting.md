## Local Note: Plaster Walls and Renovation Sequencing in Bartow's Older Homes

Bartow has a meaningful number of homes built between the 1920s and 1950s that retain original plaster-over-wood-lath wall systems. Unlike drywall, plaster is dense and rigid, which makes it durable under normal conditions, but it fractures rather than flexes when a structure settles or when a contractor cuts into it carelessly. When a renovation plan calls for opening plaster walls, the sequencing matters: score the cut lines deliberately, remove sections in manageable panels, and inspect the lath condition before deciding whether to replaster or transition to drywall. Replastering preserves the original wall mass and finish character, which matters in historically significant areas of older Bartow neighborhoods. Switching to drywall is faster but changes the acoustic and thermal feel of the room. Knowing which approach fits the project and the homeowner's goals is something that only comes from working in this specific housing stock.
