## Why Lake Wales Properties Are Particularly Vulnerable After a Loss

Lake Wales is one of the higher-elevation points on the Lake Wales Ridge, a spine of ancient sand scrub running through central Polk County. That elevation means the area catches strong convective storms that build quickly over the peninsula and stall. Roof systems here take repeated beatings from hail, high-wind events, and the occasional tornado spin-down that moves through the ridge corridor. Many homes in Lake Wales were built in the mid-20th century, and their original roof decking, often board sheathing rather than modern plywood panels, can delaminate or split under impact in ways that leave larger gaps than a newer OSB deck would. Fire damage is another common trigger: a kitchen fire or electrical fire can blow out windows and compromise soffits, leaving an attic wide open to rain before the smoke has cleared.

Beyond the structure itself, Lake Wales's sandy, well-drained soils mean that rainwater driven through an unprotected opening moves fast. There is little clay to slow lateral moisture migration, so water that enters through a broken window or a torn roof section can reach interior wall cavities and subfloor assemblies quickly.

Once the site is confirmed safe, we work in a defined sequence:

## Local Note

One thing that comes up specifically in Lake Wales and the surrounding ridge communities: many of the older concrete block homes built here in the 1950s and 1960s have jalousie windows, the louvered glass style common throughout central Florida in that era. When a storm event breaks jalousie panes, the opening is not a single clean rectangle but a series of narrow horizontal slots across a wide frame. Standard plywood board-up requires a different fastening approach on these frames than on a conventional double-hung window, and cutting corners here means the board shifts in the next wind event. If your home has jalousie windows and you have taken storm damage, mention it when you call so the crew brings the right materials and hardware.
