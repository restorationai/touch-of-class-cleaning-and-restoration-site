## Why Land O' Lakes Properties See Sewage Backup Issues

Pasco County's flat, sandy terrain drains poorly after heavy rain, and Land O' Lakes receives some of the highest annual rainfall totals in the Tampa Bay region. That combination puts consistent hydraulic pressure on both private septic systems and the aging sewer laterals that connect older homes to municipal lines. When the ground becomes saturated, septic drain fields lose their ability to absorb effluent, and the waste has nowhere to go except back up through floor drains, toilets, and tub drains.

Many homes in the area were built during the rapid growth of the 1980s and 1990s, when septic systems were the default for lots outside established utility districts. Tanks installed during that era are now well past their typical service life. Root intrusion from the region's mature oak and pine canopy compounds the problem, roots seek moisture, and a hairline crack in a clay or PVC lateral is an open invitation. The result is that sewage backups here often involve both a mechanical failure and a saturated soil condition at the same time, which means cleanup cannot stop at the visible mess.

## Our Sewage Cleanup and Sanitization Process in Land O' Lakes

Raw sewage is classified as Category 3 water, the most hazardous contamination category under IICRC standards, and it requires a structured response, not just a mop and bleach. The process DRYCOR RESTORE follows in Land O' Lakes homes reflects both that classification and the specific conditions of this area.

**Containment first.** Before extraction begins, the affected area is isolated to prevent cross-contamination to unaffected rooms. In homes with open floor plans or central HVAC systems, this step matters more than most homeowners expect, airborne pathogens can travel through ductwork.

**Extraction and removal.** Standing sewage is extracted using truck-mounted equipment. Porous materials, drywall, insulation, carpet, subflooring, that have direct contact with Category 3 water are removed rather than dried in place. There is no safe way to dry contaminated porous material to a sanitary condition.

**Antimicrobial treatment.** After removal, all affected structural surfaces are treated with EPA-registered antimicrobial agents. This step addresses what you cannot see: bacteria, viruses, and fungi that remain on concrete slabs, wall framing, and subfloor joists after the visible waste is gone.

**Drying and documentation.** Once the area is sanitized, drying equipment is deployed and moisture readings are logged daily. In Land O' Lakes, ambient humidity during summer months can slow structural drying significantly, equipment runtimes here typically run longer than in drier climates, and that affects the drying timeline your adjuster should expect.
