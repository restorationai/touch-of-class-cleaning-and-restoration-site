## Why Lakeland Properties See Water Damage Differently

Lakeland's housing stock spans nearly a century of construction styles, and each era carries its own water vulnerability. Concrete block homes built in the 1950s and 1960s, common throughout older residential corridors, have minimal vapor barriers by today's standards, and CMU walls can wick groundwater laterally during sustained rainfall. Homes built in the 1980s and 1990s often used polybutylene supply lines, a material prone to stress fractures that can fail quietly inside a wall for days before the damage surfaces. Newer construction in master-planned subdivisions tends to feature slab foundations with in-slab plumbing, where a pinhole leak can saturate the subfloor from below with no visible sign above.

The region's high water table compounds all of this. After a heavy storm, the ground around a foundation can become saturated faster than drainage systems can clear it, pushing water toward crawl spaces, garage slabs, and any low-lying entry point. Lakeland's chain of lakes is a defining feature of the city's geography, and that same proximity to open water means the surrounding soil holds moisture longer than drier inland areas, slowing the natural drying process once water enters a structure.

## Our Water Cleanup Process in Lakeland

When the call comes in, the first priority is stopping any active source, whether that's shutting off a supply valve, coordinating with Duke Energy if electrical panels are at risk, or identifying where stormwater is still entering the structure. From there, the process follows a defined sequence calibrated to what water actually does in Central Florida's climate.

**Extraction first.** Standing water removal with truck-mounted and portable extractors pulls the bulk volume before it migrates further. Water under flooring, behind baseboards, and inside wall cavities requires targeted extraction tools, not just surface mopping.

**Moisture mapping.** Thermal imaging cameras and calibrated moisture meters identify hidden saturation that looks dry to the eye. In Lakeland's older concrete block homes, this step is especially important because CMU absorbs and holds water at a rate that surface readings can underestimate.

**Drying to standard.** Commercial-grade desiccant and refrigerant dehumidifiers, combined with high-velocity air movers, are set to dry affected materials to the IICRC S500 standard, the industry benchmark for structural drying. Readings are logged daily so the drying curve is documented, not estimated.

**Antimicrobial treatment.** Given the ambient humidity and warm temperatures that persist year-round in Central Florida, antimicrobial application to affected surfaces is a standard step, not an optional add-on.
