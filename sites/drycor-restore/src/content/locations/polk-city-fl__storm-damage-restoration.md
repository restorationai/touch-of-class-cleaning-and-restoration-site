## Why Polk City Properties Are Vulnerable to Storm Damage

Polk County's interior position doesn't insulate it from severe weather. The county sits in one of Florida's most active lightning and convective storm zones, and the flat terrain around Polk City offers little natural windbreak. Homes built on the sandy, well-drained soils common to this part of the county can shift subtly over time, creating small gaps at roof-to-wall transitions and around window frames that become entry points the moment wind-driven rain arrives.

The housing stock in and around Polk City includes a meaningful number of older concrete block homes built in the 1960s through 1980s. While CBS construction handles wind load well, the original roofing systems on those homes, and the aged caulking around their jalousie or single-pane windows, are frequently the first things to fail in a storm. Manufactured and modular homes are also common in the area, and their roof-to-wall connections require a different inspection approach after high-wind events.

Lake Hancock and the smaller water bodies nearby raise the local water table in wet season, which means saturated ground has limited capacity to absorb additional runoff. When a storm dumps rain faster than the soil can accept it, water backs up against foundations and finds its way into crawl spaces and slabs through cracks that were never a problem in drier months.

## Our Storm Damage Restoration Process in Polk City

The first priority on any storm call is stopping active water intrusion. That means emergency tarping of compromised roof sections, boarding damaged windows and doors, and extracting standing water before it migrates into wall cavities or under flooring. We use thermal imaging cameras to locate moisture that isn't visible at the surface, which matters especially in older CBS homes where water can travel along the block cores before appearing as a stain on an interior wall.

Once the structure is stabilized and water is extracted, the drying phase begins. Industrial air movers and dehumidifiers are positioned based on moisture readings, not guesswork, and we monitor daily to confirm drying is progressing. Structural materials that cannot be adequately dried, including saturated insulation, swollen OSB sheathing, or compromised drywall, are removed and documented before reconstruction begins. Our team is IICRC certified and follows the S500 and S110 standards for water and storm damage drying.

Tree impact calls add a structural assessment layer. When a trunk or large limb has breached a roof, we work alongside licensed contractors to evaluate rafter and truss integrity before any interior work proceeds. Our contractor license (#CBC1253966) allows us to carry restoration and limited reconstruction work through a single point of contact, which simplifies the process for property owners managing an insurance claim.
