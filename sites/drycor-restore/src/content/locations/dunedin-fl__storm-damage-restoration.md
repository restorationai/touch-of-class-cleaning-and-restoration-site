## Why Dunedin Properties Take Storm Hits Differently

Dunedin's housing stock skews older. Much of the city was built out in the 1950s through 1970s, when construction standards for wind uplift were far below what Florida's current building code requires. Roof decks in that era were often nailed with smooth-shank nails rather than ring-shanks, meaning the deck can separate from the trusses under sustained gusts well before the shingles themselves fail. Once the deck lifts even slightly, rain infiltrates at a rate that drywall and insulation cannot absorb without immediate intervention.

The city's position on the Pinellas Peninsula also means drainage has nowhere to go quickly. During a slow-moving tropical system, streets flood before storm drains can clear, and that surface water finds its way into garages, crawl spaces, and the lowest courses of block-construction walls. Salt air accelerates corrosion on any exposed metal, flashing, hurricane straps, HVAC condenser units, so storm damage here often reveals pre-existing deterioration that a drier inland climate would have hidden for years longer.

## Our Storm Damage Restoration Process in Dunedin

The first priority after any storm is stopping additional water from entering. That means emergency tarping and board-up before a full damage assessment, not after. Once the building envelope is secured, the team documents every affected surface with moisture meters and thermal imaging, because water that migrates behind stucco or into the cavity of a concrete block wall will not show up on a visual inspection alone.

Structural drying follows IICRC S500 and S520 protocols, with commercial desiccant dehumidifiers and high-velocity air movers positioned to create a controlled drying environment. In Dunedin's climate, ambient outdoor humidity during and after a storm routinely exceeds 90 percent, which means open-window drying is counterproductive, sealed, equipment-driven drying is the only method that reliably brings structural moisture content to acceptable levels. Progress readings are logged daily so the insurance adjuster has a defensible drying record.

Debris removal, downed trees, torn roofing, shattered glass, is coordinated in parallel rather than sequenced after drying, because leaving organic material on or against the structure accelerates mold colonization. In Florida's heat, mold can begin colonizing wet cellulose within 24 to 48 hours of initial wetting.
