## Why Longboat Key Properties Face Distinct Board-Up and Tarping Challenges

Longboat Key's geography creates conditions that set it apart from inland Florida communities. The island is essentially a sandbar, with the Gulf of Mexico on one side and Sarasota Bay on the other, meaning storm surge, wind-driven rain, and blowing debris arrive from multiple directions simultaneously. Many of the condominiums and single-family residences here feature large-format impact windows and sliding glass doors that, when breached, expose entire open-plan living areas to the elements in seconds.

Older construction along the island's mid-section often used concrete block with stucco exteriors, which holds up well structurally but presents unique anchoring challenges when attaching temporary plywood panels or roof tarps. The substrate is hard, fasteners require masonry bits, and improper attachment can leave cosmetic damage that HOA boards and property managers notice immediately. Roof profiles on the island also tend toward low-pitch designs built for aesthetics and wind resistance, but a low-pitch roof that loses even a small section of membrane or tile becomes a funnel for rainwater rather than a barrier against it.
