## Our Emergency Board-Up and Tarping Process in Parrish

When a call comes in, the crew loads material for the most common Parrish loss scenarios: pre-cut plywood panels in standard window dimensions common to production builders active in this area, heavy-duty polyethylene tarps rated for Florida UV exposure, and the fasteners appropriate for tile roofs, which are the dominant roof type in newer Parrish subdivisions.

On arrival, the priority sequence is:

1. **Assess structural stability** before any crew member goes onto a roof or into a fire-damaged structure.
2. **Identify all breach points**, including soffit vents, gable ends, and HVAC penetrations that storm debris may have compromised but that are easy to overlook.
3. **Tarp the roof** by running material past the ridge and weighting or fastening the upslope edge so wind cannot create a sail effect that lifts the tarp off.
4. **Board windows and doors** with plywood anchored into framing members, not just casing, so the panel holds under continued wind load.
5. **Document every opening** with photographs and measurements for the insurance claim file.

## Local Note

Tile roofs are common across Parrish's newer subdivisions, and they create a specific tarping challenge that flat or shingle roofs do not. Concrete and clay tiles are brittle under foot traffic, and walking a tile roof to set a tarp incorrectly can crack field tiles and create new leak points while trying to seal an existing one. The DRYCOR crew uses foam knee pads, walks the batten lines where possible, and stages the tarp from a ladder at the eave rather than dragging material across the field of the roof. It takes a few extra minutes, but it keeps the tile intact and avoids a secondary damage claim.
