## Why Pinellas Park's Housing Stock Complicates Fire Damage

The same construction era that gives Pinellas Park neighborhoods their character creates specific challenges after a fire. Homes built in the 1950s through 1970s frequently used horizontal wood lath beneath plaster walls, a material that chars unevenly, holds smoke odor deep in its grain, and can conceal hot spots that smolder long after surface flames are out. Older electrical panels, some still wired with aluminum branch circuits common in that era, may need evaluation before any restoration work begins.

Florida's climate adds another layer. Pinellas County's high ambient humidity, routinely above 70%, accelerates the bonding of smoke residue to surfaces. Soot that might stay loose and removable for 48 hours in a drier climate can begin etching into paint, metal fixtures, and glass within 24 hours here. That timeline matters for how quickly contents need to be inventoried and moved, and for how aggressively dehumidification runs alongside fire cleanup.

## Our Fire Damage Restoration Process in Pinellas Park

Every fire loss is different, but the sequence of decisions follows a consistent logic. When we arrive, the first priority is safety assessment, checking structural integrity, identifying any remaining electrical hazards, and confirming the building is safe to enter before any crew member steps inside. From there, the work moves through four overlapping phases.

**Board-up and tarping** secures the structure against weather intrusion and unauthorized entry. In Pinellas Park, where afternoon thunderstorms can arrive with little warning from June through September, this step is not optional, an open roof after a fire can turn a contained loss into a flood.

**Soot and smoke removal** is the most labor-intensive phase. Dry soot from fast-burning fires behaves differently than the wet, oily residue left by slow smoldering synthetic materials. We use dry chemical sponges, HEPA vacuuming, and thermal fogging calibrated to the specific residue type, not a single method applied uniformly.

**Structural drying** runs concurrently, because suppression water trapped in wall cavities or subfloor assemblies will grow mold within days in this climate. Our IICRC-certified technicians monitor moisture readings at multiple depths, not just surface readings, to confirm drying is complete before any reconstruction begins.

**Odor neutralization** closes the process. Smoke odor embeds in porous materials, insulation, wood framing, HVAC ductwork, and surface cleaning alone won't eliminate it. Hydroxyl generation and ozone treatment are deployed based on occupancy status and material sensitivity.
