## Why Riverview Properties See Mold Issues

The housing stock across Riverview skews toward wood-frame construction with OSB sheathing and paper-faced drywall, materials that absorb and hold moisture readily. Unlike older concrete-block homes common in parts of Tampa proper, these newer builds rely heavily on HVAC systems to manage interior humidity. When an air handler develops a slow condensate leak, a supply-line fitting drips behind a cabinet, or a roof flashing fails after a storm, moisture can accumulate inside wall cavities for weeks before it becomes visible. By then, mold colonies are often well established on the paper facing of drywall and the wood framing behind it.

Hillsborough County's clay-dominant soils also slow surface drainage, meaning water that pools against a foundation after heavy rain can wick into slab edges or crawl-space framing. Homes in lower-lying sections of Riverview, areas that sit close to the Little Manatee River watershed and its tributary drainage, are particularly susceptible to elevated ground moisture after prolonged wet seasons.

## Our Mold Remediation Process in Riverview

Remediation is not the same as painting over a stain or spraying a surface with a store-bought product. The process starts with containment: plastic sheeting and negative air pressure isolate the affected area so disturbed spores don't migrate to clean rooms. HEPA-filtered air scrubbers run continuously throughout the project.

Once containment is established, the scope is confirmed through moisture mapping with calibrated meters and, where indicated, thermal imaging to locate hidden saturation behind finishes. Affected porous materials, drywall, insulation, compromised wood, are removed, bagged, and disposed of per Florida Department of Environmental Protection guidelines. Structural framing that can be preserved is treated and encapsulated. The work is documented with photographs at each stage, which matters when an insurance carrier or a future buyer's inspector asks for evidence of proper remediation.

Post-remediation, a final air-quality clearance test, performed by an independent party, confirms spore counts have returned to acceptable levels before containment is removed and reconstruction begins. Drycor Restore holds NAERMC mold certification and operates as an IICRC Certified Firm, which means the process follows a defined standard rather than a technician's best guess.

## Local Note

Riverview's newer construction often uses engineered lumber (LVL beams, I-joists) in floor systems, and those materials behave differently than solid-sawn lumber when they get wet. I-joist webs are made from OSB, which swells and delaminates faster than dimensional lumber and can lose structural integrity before the surface looks seriously damaged. When a water loss involves a second-floor bathroom or a leaking supply line above a floor system with I-joists, the assessment has to account for that, moisture readings at the surface of finished flooring won't tell the whole story.
