## Our Water Damage Restoration Process in Brandon

When a crew arrives, the first priority is stopping any active source, shutting off the water supply, isolating the affected circuit if electrical proximity is a concern, and documenting the extent of saturation with thermal imaging and calibrated moisture meters before a single piece of material is moved. That documentation matters for insurance claims and for building an accurate drying plan.

Extraction comes next. Standing water is removed with truck-mounted and portable extraction units, including weighted extraction tools for carpet and pad. Once visible water is out, the work shifts to structural drying, a process governed by the IICRC S500 standard, which defines the moisture baselines materials need to reach before a structure is considered dry. In Brandon's ambient humidity, achieving those targets requires a calculated balance of industrial air movers and low-grain refrigerant dehumidifiers. Running equipment without monitoring daily psychrometric readings wastes time and misses hidden moisture pockets.

Moisture readings are logged at every visit. Affected cavities, wall bays, subfloor assemblies, ceiling plenum spaces, are checked with penetrating and non-penetrating meters to confirm drying progress rather than assume it. When materials cannot be dried in place within an acceptable timeframe, controlled demolition removes only what is necessary, preserving as much original structure as possible for reconstruction.

As an IICRC Certified Firm with Water Restoration Technician certification and EPA Lead-Safe certification, the team follows established protocols for material handling, a detail that matters in Brandon's older subdivisions where pre-1978 construction may involve lead-based paint in the materials being disturbed.
