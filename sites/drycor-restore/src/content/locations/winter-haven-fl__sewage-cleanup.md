## Why Winter Haven Properties See Sewage Backup More Often Than You'd Expect

The chain-of-lakes geography that makes Winter Haven distinctive also creates real challenges for residential sewer and septic systems. Much of the city sits on sandy, porous soil with a water table that rises significantly during Florida's wet season, roughly June through September. When the ground is saturated, septic drain fields lose their ability to absorb effluent, and older municipal sewer laterals can experience infiltration that overwhelms the line and pushes sewage back toward the lowest fixture in the house.

Homes built in the mid-twentieth century, and there are many throughout the older residential corridors near downtown and the lakefront, often have clay or cast-iron sewer laterals that have been slowly deteriorating for decades. Root intrusion from the large live oaks and cypress trees common across Polk County is a leading cause of line blockages. A slow-moving partial clog can go unnoticed until a heavy rain event or a high-use day tips it into a full backup.

Properties on septic systems face a related but distinct risk. When a drain field floods or a tank lid seal fails during a storm, the overflow doesn't just stay in the yard, it can migrate toward the foundation and enter the structure through floor penetrations or low-lying doorways.

## Our Sewage Cleanup and Sanitization Process in Winter Haven

Raw sewage is classified as Category 3 water, the most contaminated classification in the IICRC S500 standard, and it requires a structured response, not just a mop and bleach. The process DRYCOR RESTORE follows is calibrated to the specific conditions found in Central Florida homes.

**Containment first.** The crew establishes a containment perimeter using plastic sheeting and negative air pressure where needed to prevent cross-contamination to unaffected areas of the home. In Winter Haven's older homes with open floor plans or jalousie windows that don't seal tightly, this step requires extra attention.

**Extraction and removal.** Standing sewage is extracted using truck-mounted equipment. Porous materials, drywall, insulation, carpet, subflooring, that have been in contact with Category 3 water are removed and bagged for disposal per Florida Department of Health guidelines. There is no safe way to dry and retain saturated porous materials after sewage contact.

**Antimicrobial treatment.** After extraction, all affected hard surfaces are treated with EPA-registered disinfectants. Concrete slabs, block walls, and tile, common in Florida slab-on-grade construction, are scrubbed, treated, and allowed to dwell before a final rinse.

**Drying and documentation.** Moisture readings are taken throughout the affected area using thermal imaging and pin-type meters. Drying equipment runs until the structure returns to acceptable moisture levels. All readings are logged for your insurance claim.
