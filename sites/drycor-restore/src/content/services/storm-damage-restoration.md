A storm doesn't announce when it's done damaging your home. The wind stops, the rain moves on, and then the real problems begin. Shingles torn off at 2 a.m. let water into the attic before you've had a chance to call anyone. A tree limb through the roof opens the structure to hours of rainfall. The damage you can see from the driveway is rarely the full picture, and the damage you can't see, saturated insulation, compromised roof decking, water tracking down interior wall cavities, is what turns a manageable repair into a months-long ordeal.

## What storm damage restoration actually involves

Storm damage restoration is not a single trade. A severe weather event can combine structural breach, water intrusion, wind-driven debris impact, and electrical exposure in the same loss. Addressing it correctly means working across all of those simultaneously rather than sequentially.

On a typical storm loss, the first priority is **emergency stabilization**, tarping and board-up to stop the weather from continuing to enter the structure. From there, the work shifts to **water extraction and structural drying**, because any opening in the envelope that occurred during rain means water has entered. Depending on how long the structure was exposed, that moisture may have already begun migrating into wall assemblies, subfloor systems, and insulation bays.

Equipment deployed on a storm loss typically includes industrial air movers, low-grain refrigerant (LGR) dehumidifiers, moisture meters, and thermal imaging cameras. Thermal imaging is particularly useful after storm events because it reveals wet insulation and moisture behind intact drywall that reads dry at the surface, exactly the kind of hidden saturation that causes mold colonization within 24 to 72 hours of a loss if not addressed.

After drying is confirmed through documentation, the structural repair phase begins: roof decking, framing, drywall, insulation, and finish work. DRYCOR RESTORE holds general contractor license #CBC1253966, which means the remediation and the rebuild happen under one roof rather than requiring a separate contractor handoff.

## Our process

**1. Emergency stabilization and site safety**
Before any assessment begins, the structure is made safe and weather-tight. Tarps are secured over breached roof sections, broken windows and doors are boarded, and any active electrical hazards are identified and flagged. This step stops the loss from growing.

**2. Damage documentation and scope development**
Every affected surface is photographed, measured, and logged. Moisture readings are taken at multiple points across the structure, not just in the obvious wet areas. This documentation becomes the foundation of the insurance claim and the written scope of work.

**3. Water extraction and structural drying**
Any water that entered during the storm is extracted and the drying process begins. Drying equipment is placed based on a calculated drying plan, not by feel. Daily moisture readings track progress and equipment is adjusted until the structure reaches documented dry standard.

**4. Debris removal and structural repair**
Damaged materials, shingles, decking, framing members, drywall, insulation, are removed and disposed of. Structural repairs are completed to restore the building envelope before interior finish work begins.

**5. Interior restoration and final inspection**
Drywall, insulation, paint, trim, and any other interior finishes damaged by the storm or the water intrusion are restored. A final walkthrough confirms the structure is fully closed and dry before the job is closed out.

## What separates a good storm damage response from a bad one

The most common failure in storm damage work is treating the visible damage as the complete damage. A crew that patches the roof without checking for water intrusion behind the fascia, inside the attic, or along the top plates of exterior walls is leaving a mold problem behind the drywall.

A second common gap is inadequate moisture documentation. Insurance adjusters, and increasingly, AI-assisted claims platforms, require moisture readings with timestamps, equipment placement logs, and daily drying records. A job documented on a napkin or not at all creates disputes at settlement and delays your payout.

Experienced operators also know that wind-driven rain behaves differently than plumbing leaks. It enters at angles, follows roof lines into wall cavities, and can appear in rooms that seem far removed from the obvious breach. Thermal imaging during the initial assessment catches this. Skipping it misses it.

Finally, storm losses frequently involve older roofing materials, lead-based paint in pre-1978 homes, and asbestos-containing materials in certain construction eras. Disturbing those materials without proper protocol creates liability. DRYCOR RESTORE is EPA Lead-Safe Certified and OSHA trained, pre-demolition testing and safe work practices are part of the scope, not an afterthought.

## What does storm damage restoration cost?

Typical costs for storm damage restoration vary significantly based on the size of the breach, how long the structure was exposed to weather, and how much water entered. Most homeowners pay somewhere in the ranges below, these are industry-typical figures, not quotes.

| Scenario | Typical range |
|---|---|
| Minor roof breach, limited water intrusion, single room | $2,500 – $6,000 |
| Moderate roof damage, attic and ceiling water intrusion | $6,000 – $15,000 |
| Tree impact with structural framing damage | $12,000 – $35,000 |
| Multiple roof sections breached, widespread interior water | $20,000 – $60,000+ |
| Full reconstruction after major structural loss | $60,000 – $150,000+ |

Every loss is different, and DRYCOR RESTORE provides a written scope before any work begins so you know exactly what is being done and why. Homeowners insurance typically covers sudden and accidental storm damage, wind, hail, falling trees, though coverage for pre-existing deterioration or deferred maintenance is often excluded; your adjuster or policy documents will clarify your specific limits.

## Seasonal and regional considerations

Thonotosassa and the broader Hillsborough County area sit squarely in Florida's Atlantic hurricane corridor. The named storm season runs June through November, with the statistical peak in September, but severe convective storms, tornadoes, and damaging straight-line winds occur year-round in Central Florida. The region's high humidity baseline (often above 70% relative humidity even in dry months) means that any moisture introduced into a structure during a storm event has an accelerated path toward mold colonization compared to drier climates. Prompt response is not a sales pitch here, it is a function of the local environment.

Older housing stock in the area, including homes built before modern hurricane strapping requirements, can experience more widespread structural movement during high-wind events than newer construction, which sometimes means damage extends beyond the obvious point of impact.
