## Why Sharpsville Properties Face Particular Fire Damage Challenges

The housing stock throughout Sharpsville and the broader Mercer County area skews older, with a significant share of homes built before 1960. Balloon-frame construction, which was standard before platform framing took over, lacks the fire-blocking between floors that modern code requires. In a fire event, this means flames and superheated gases can move from a basement or first-floor room into the attic in minutes, turning what looks like a contained kitchen fire into a structural emergency.

Beyond framing, older homes in this region commonly feature original plaster-and-lath walls, cast-iron or galvanized plumbing, and knob-and-tube wiring in the oldest sections. Smoke and soot penetrate plaster more deeply than modern drywall, which means surface cleaning isn't enough. Effective fire and smoke restoration here requires getting into wall cavities, assessing insulation, and testing for hidden char before any reconstruction begins.

Western Pennsylvania's cold, damp winters add another layer: smoke residue that soaks into porous materials during a winter fire can reactivate as odor when the heat comes back on in spring. Addressing that residue thoroughly during the initial restoration prevents a homeowner from discovering the smell months later.

## Our Fire Damage Restoration Process in Sharpsville

Every fire loss is different, but the sequence of decisions matters. The process begins with a full structural and content assessment, documenting damage with photos and moisture readings before a single item is moved. This documentation is the foundation of the insurance claim and protects the homeowner throughout the process.

Once the scope is clear, the work moves in a deliberate order: board-up and tarping to secure the structure, removal of unsalvageable materials (char, compromised insulation, damaged framing), and then the detailed cleaning phase. Soot is acidic and continues etching surfaces the longer it sits, so speed in this phase matters. DISS uses dry chemical sponges on porous surfaces, HEPA-filtered vacuuming throughout, and thermal fogging or hydroxyl generation for odor neutralization, depending on the material types present.

For homes with plaster walls, which are common in Sharpsville's older neighborhoods, the cleaning approach is adjusted. Plaster is more alkaline than drywall, which affects which cleaning agents are appropriate, and its density means smoke odor can be trapped well beneath the surface layer. The team tests and re-tests until readings confirm the odor source has been addressed, not just masked.

EPA Lead-Safe certification is part of DISS's credential set, which matters directly in Sharpsville: pre-1978 homes disturbed during fire restoration work require lead-safe work practices under federal rules, and a significant portion of the local housing stock falls into that category.
