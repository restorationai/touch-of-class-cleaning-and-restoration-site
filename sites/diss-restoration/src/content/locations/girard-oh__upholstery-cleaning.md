## Why Girard Homes See Upholstery Buildup

Girard's housing stock skews older, a lot of it built during the city's run as a steel town in the early and mid-1900s, and that means plenty of homes still have their original wood-frame construction with limited ventilation in living rooms and dens. Combine that with Trumbull County's humid summers and damp winters, and fabric upholstery in these homes absorbs ambient moisture even when nothing has spilled on it. Leather fares a little better structurally but dries out faster in homes that run forced-air heat all winter, which cracks the finish and pulls dust and oils deeper into the grain. We see the same pattern in couches that sit near older radiators or baseboard units: one side fades and stiffens while the rest of the cushion stays soft.

## Our Upholstery Cleaning Process for Girard Fabrics

We start every job with a fiber and dye test on an inconspicuous area, which matters more in Girard than it might elsewhere because so much of the furniture we see is inherited or secondhand, with fabric tags long gone or unreadable. From there, we vacuum with a HEPA-filtered upholstery attachment to lift the dry soil that's worked into the weave, since skipping this step just turns dirt into mud once moisture hits the fabric. Steam cleaning follows for most synthetic and cotton-blend fabrics, using low-moisture extraction so cushions aren't left saturated in a house without great airflow. Leather pieces get a pH-balanced cleaner and conditioner instead, since steam and standard upholstery shampoos strip the finish faster than normal use ever would. We finish by setting fans or a dehumidifier near the piece for a few hours, which matters more here than in drier climates because ambient humidity slows evaporation from the padding underneath the fabric.

## Local Note

One thing we watch for in Girard specifically: homes near the Mahoning River floodplain sometimes have furniture that's been through a minor basement or crawlspace flood at some point, even if the owner doesn't mention it upfront. That kind of moisture exposure can leave upholstery looking fine on the surface while the padding underneath holds onto odor and, occasionally, early mold growth.
