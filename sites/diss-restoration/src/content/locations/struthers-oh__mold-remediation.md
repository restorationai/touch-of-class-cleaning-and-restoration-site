## Why Struthers Properties See Mold Issues

The housing stock throughout Struthers skews older, and older homes carry mold risk in ways newer construction doesn't. Basement walls poured in the 1940s and 1950s were not waterproofed to modern standards — they rely on drainage tile that can crack, shift, or simply fill with the heavy clay soil common to this part of northeastern Ohio. When that tile fails, water seeps through the foundation wall and wicks into floor joists, subflooring, and the bottom plates of interior walls. Because the moisture source is below grade, the surface can feel dry to the touch while the structural wood behind it is already colonized.

Struthers also sits close enough to the Mahoning River corridor that the water table fluctuates noticeably after significant rain events. A basement that stays dry through a mild winter can take on water in March or April when snowmelt and rain arrive together. That seasonal pattern means mold problems here often appear in spring — homeowners open a basement door after months of the space sitting closed and find growth that has been quietly spreading since January.

Older plumbing is another factor. Galvanized supply lines and cast-iron drain stacks, common in homes built before the 1970s, corrode from the inside out. Pinhole leaks inside wall cavities go undetected for months, and by the time a stain appears on drywall or plaster, the mold behind it has already spread beyond what the visible damage suggests.

## Our Mold Remediation Process in Struthers

Every job starts with a thorough inspection — not just the area you can see, but the cavities behind it. In Struthers homes with plaster-and-lath construction, that means using moisture meters and thermal imaging to map where water has traveled, because plaster holds moisture differently than modern drywall and can test dry at the surface while the wood lath behind it remains saturated.

Once the scope is established, the affected area is isolated with physical containment barriers and placed under negative air pressure using HEPA-filtered air scrubbers. This prevents spores from migrating to unaffected rooms during removal — a step that matters especially in the compact floor plans typical of postwar Struthers bungalows, where a basement stairwell opens directly into a living area.

Contaminated materials are removed, bagged, and disposed of according to Ohio EPA guidelines. Structural surfaces that can be salvaged are treated with an EPA-registered antimicrobial agent and dried to documented moisture levels. The drying phase in older Struthers homes often takes longer than industry averages because thick plaster, masonry block, and dense wood framing release moisture slowly — rushing this step is how mold comes back.

Final clearance is based on post-remediation verification, confirming that airborne spore counts and surface conditions meet remediation standards before containment comes down.
