## Why McDonald Properties See Water Damage Issues

McDonald grew up around its steel mill in the early 1900s, and a lot of the village's housing stock dates to that era: narrow lots, full basements, and plumbing that's been patched and repiped more than once over the decades. Galvanized supply lines in homes built before the 1960s corrode from the inside out, and a pinhole leak behind a wall can run for weeks before anyone notices the water stain or the soft spot in the flooring. Add in Trumbull County's hard winters, where overnight temperatures swing well below freezing and then climb back above it within a day or two, and you get the classic Ohio freeze-thaw burst: a pipe splits in an unheated crawlspace or exterior wall cavity, then nobody finds it until the thaw sends water pouring through the ceiling below. Sump pump failures during spring snowmelt are another common call, especially in basements set into McDonald's clay-heavy soil, which drains slowly and pushes groundwater against foundation walls after a hard rain.

## Our Water Damage Restoration Process in McDonald

The first visit is about stopping the loss and reading the structure, not just running fans. We shut off or isolate the water source, extract standing water with truck-mounted or portable extraction equipment depending on how much volume we're dealing with, and use moisture meters to map how far water has traveled under flooring and inside wall cavities, since surface-dry drywall over an old plaster-and-lath wall often hides saturation that a quick glance would miss. Drying follows the IICRC S500 standard, with air movers and dehumidifiers placed and adjusted based on daily moisture readings, not a fixed schedule. In homes with original plaster walls, which are common in McDonald's older sections, we expect drying to take longer than it would with modern drywall, because plaster releases absorbed moisture more slowly even after the surface reads dry. We document every reading along the way, which matters when a claim ends up in front of an adjuster.

## McDonald Insurance Coordination

Most water damage claims in McDonald's older homes involve some combination of sudden pipe failure and the gradual damage that follows once water sits under flooring or inside a wall for days before discovery. Insurance carriers distinguish sharply between the two, and sudden, accidental discharge is typically covered while long-term seepage often isn't, so the documentation we gather during extraction and drying becomes the evidence that supports your claim. We photograph affected materials, log moisture readings throughout the drying process, and provide the kind of paper trail adjusters expect, which helps avoid disputes over what was damaged and when.
