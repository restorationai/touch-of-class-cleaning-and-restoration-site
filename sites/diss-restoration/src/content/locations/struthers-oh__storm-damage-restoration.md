## Why Struthers Properties Are Especially Vulnerable to Storm Damage

Mahoning County sits in a corridor that funnels lake-effect moisture south from Lake Erie, and Struthers — tucked along the Mahoning River — gets the added complication of low-lying terrain that slows drainage after heavy rain events. That combination of wind, saturated ground, and older tree canopy creates conditions where storm damage rarely stops at the roof.

The housing stock here skews heavily toward mid-century construction — brick veneer, plaster-and-lath interiors, and original wood-framed windows that were built to code for their era but weren't engineered for the wind uplift values that modern roofing systems account for. When a severe thunderstorm or a tornado-warned system pushes through, those older roof-to-wall connections are often the first thing to show stress. Mortar joints in brick veneer can crack under wind pressure, letting water migrate into the wall assembly in ways that don't show up on the surface for days.

Flat and low-slope garage roofs — common on the ranch-style homes throughout the city — pond water quickly when gutters are overwhelmed, and even a small breach in the membrane can saturate the roof deck before the storm has cleared.

## Our Storm Damage Restoration Process in Struthers

The first priority on any storm call is stopping the bleeding — boarding windows, tarping roof sections, and removing any hanging debris that poses an immediate hazard to the structure or the people inside. That emergency stabilization work happens before any formal scope is written, because waiting costs more than acting.

Once the structure is secured, the team conducts a full moisture survey using thermal imaging and calibrated moisture meters. Wind-driven rain is deceptive: it follows the path of least resistance through soffits, ridge vents, and deteriorated caulk lines, and it can travel several feet horizontally inside a wall cavity before pooling. In plaster-wall homes common throughout Struthers, the plaster face may read dry at the surface while the wood lath behind it holds significant moisture — a reading-only inspection misses that entirely.

Structural drying is set up per the IICRC S500 and S520 standards, with desiccant or refrigerant dehumidification and high-velocity airflow calibrated to the actual cubic footage of the affected space, not a one-size estimate. Readings are logged daily so the drying curve is documented — documentation that matters when your insurance carrier asks for proof of scope.

Tree damage cleanup, debris removal, and coordination with roofing and siding trades for the rebuild phase are handled as part of the same project rather than handed off, so you're not managing three separate contractors while your home is still open to the weather.
