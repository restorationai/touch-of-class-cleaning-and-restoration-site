## Why Sharon Properties See Storm Damage Issues

Severe thunderstorms that track east off Lake Erie tend to intensify as they funnel through the Shenango Valley, and Sharon's mix of flat river-bottom lots and steeper hillside streets means wind and water behave differently block to block. A lot of the housing stock here dates to the steel boom years of the early-to-mid 1900s, so roofs are often original slate or aging asphalt layered over older decking that doesn't hold up well to hail or sustained wind uplift. Mature oak and maple trees planted along residential streets during that same era now have the root mass and canopy size to bring down power lines or punch through a roofline in a single gust. Add the Shenango River and Little Neshannock Creek running close to several neighborhoods, and even a moderate rain event can push surface water into foundations that were never built with modern waterproofing in mind.

## Our Storm Damage Restoration Process in Sharon

The first call is usually about a tree on the roof, a blown-out window, or water coming in somewhere it shouldn't. We tarp and board the exposed areas first to stop ongoing intrusion, then move to extraction if standing water has gotten into the structure. Drying follows the IICRC S500 standard, with moisture mapping to track how deep the water traveled into plaster, subflooring, or the stone foundations common in Sharon's older homes. Once the structure reads dry at depth, not just on the surface, we document everything for the insurance file and coordinate any tree removal or roofing repair the loss requires.

## Sharon Insurance Coordination

Most homeowners' policies in Pennsylvania cover wind and hail damage but draw a harder line on flood water from rising creeks or rivers, which usually falls under a separate flood policy. We photograph and document the loss in enough detail to show the adjuster what caused the damage, water intrusion from a roof breach versus river overflow, since that distinction often decides which policy pays. For storm events that affect multiple Sharon blocks at once, we've found it helps to get documentation started before debris gets moved or a dumpster shows up.

## Local note

Quite a few homes close to the Shenango River floodplain in Sharon still sit on original fieldstone or rubble foundations.
