## Why Hubbard Properties See Mold Issues

Hubbard's housing stock skews older — a significant portion of the city's residential neighborhoods were built before 1970, when vapor barriers were minimal or absent and basement walls were poured concrete or block without waterproofing membranes. Those foundations wick groundwater laterally, especially during the freeze-thaw cycles that hit Trumbull County hard between December and March. When that moisture migrates into unfinished basement framing or sits behind drywall added during a later renovation, mold can establish itself in wall cavities that look and feel dry at the surface.

The region's clay-dominant soil compounds the problem. Clay doesn't drain — it holds water against foundation walls long after a rainstorm passes, maintaining elevated moisture pressure for days. Homes in lower-lying parts of Hubbard near the Shenango River watershed are particularly susceptible to this kind of persistent ground saturation, which translates directly into elevated indoor humidity and mold-favorable conditions in basements and crawl spaces.

Older plumbing is another factor. Homes built in the 1950s and 1960s often still carry original galvanized supply lines or cast-iron drain stacks. Pinhole leaks in galvanized pipe can run undetected inside wall cavities for months, feeding a mold colony that only becomes visible when it reaches the drywall face — by which point remediation involves more than surface cleaning.

## Our Mold Remediation Process in Hubbard

Every remediation starts with a thorough moisture assessment, not just a visual inspection. Mold visible on a surface is almost always the trailing edge of a moisture problem that extends further into the structure. The team uses thermal imaging and calibrated moisture meters to map wet zones behind walls and under flooring before any containment goes up.

Once the scope is defined, physical containment — negative air pressure chambers with HEPA-filtered exhaust — isolates the affected area from the rest of the living space. This step matters especially in Hubbard's older homes, where open floor plans were uncommon and original construction used plaster-and-lath walls that can trap spores in gaps that modern drywall wouldn't have.

Remediation follows the IICRC S520 standard: affected porous materials are removed and bagged, structural surfaces are HEPA-vacuumed and treated with EPA-registered antimicrobial agents, and the area is dried to documented moisture levels before any reconstruction begins. The team is EPA Lead-Safe Certified, which matters in pre-1978 homes — disturbing painted surfaces during mold work requires lead-safe work practices, and much of Hubbard's housing falls into that category.

## Hubbard Insurance Coordination

Mold remediation coverage varies significantly by policy. Some homeowner's policies cover mold only when it results directly from a covered peril — a burst pipe, for example — while others exclude mold entirely or cap remediation costs. The DISS team documents the loss thoroughly: moisture readings, thermal images, affected material inventories, and photo documentation at each stage of the work. That documentation package is what adjusters need to process a claim, and providing it reduces back-and-forth that delays the project.

If your carrier requires a scope of work before authorizing remediation, the team can prepare that documentation during the initial assessment visit.

## Local Note

In Hubbard's older neighborhoods, many homes were built with unvented crawl spaces — a construction practice that was standard for decades but creates a persistent mold risk. Ground moisture evaporates upward into the crawl space, condenses on the underside of the subfloor, and feeds mold growth on the floor joists. Homeowners often don't discover it until a musty odor migrates into the living space or a home inspector flags it during a sale. Remediating crawl space mold in these homes typically includes encapsulation of the ground plane to interrupt the moisture source — treating only the visible mold without addressing the vapor pathway means the problem returns.
