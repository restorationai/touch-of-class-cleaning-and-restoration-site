Girard sits in Trumbull County's industrial belt, where a significant share of the housing stock dates to the steel-boom decades of the early and mid-twentieth century. Those older wood-frame and brick-veneer homes burn differently than newer construction — balloon framing allows fire and smoke to travel vertically through wall cavities with almost no resistance, and the dense plaster-and-lath interiors hold smoke odor long after the visible char is gone. When a fire moves through one of these properties, the damage is rarely limited to the room where it started, and the restoration work has to account for that hidden spread from the first hour on site.

## Why Girard's Older Housing Stock Complicates Fire Damage

The majority of residential fire losses in Girard involve homes built before modern platform framing became standard. In balloon-framed construction, wall cavities run continuously from the foundation sill to the roof line, which means smoke, heat, and combustion gases can migrate two or three floors above the fire's origin. Soot deposits inside those cavities are invisible from the surface but continue off-gassing long after cleanup appears complete. Brick-veneer exteriors common throughout Trumbull County add another layer of complexity: the wythe of brick traps heat and can transfer it into adjacent structural members hours after suppression.

The local climate compounds the challenge. Girard's winters are cold and humid, and a home that has absorbed firefighting water in January or February is at serious risk of secondary mold colonization within 24 to 48 hours of the initial loss — especially in basements and crawl spaces where ventilation is limited. Addressing fire and water damage as a combined event, not sequentially, is essential in this climate.

## Our Fire and Smoke Restoration Process in Girard

Every fire loss is different, but the sequence is consistent. The first step on any Girard job is a full structural assessment — identifying load-bearing members that may have been compromised by heat, mapping smoke migration through wall and ceiling cavities, and documenting moisture intrusion from suppression water. Thermal imaging helps locate wet materials hidden behind plaster surfaces that read dry to the touch.

Once the structure is confirmed safe, controlled demolition removes charred and unsalvageable materials. HEPA-filtered air scrubbers run continuously to pull particulates from the air while crews clean soot from framing, masonry, and mechanical systems. Thermal fogging and hydroxyl generation address smoke odor at the molecular level — masking agents are not used, because in a tightly built older home, odor that isn't neutralized at the source will return when temperatures rise. Structural drying equipment stays on-site until moisture readings confirm the building envelope is within acceptable parameters before any rebuild begins.

As an IICRC Certified Firm with technicians holding FSRT (Fire and Smoke Restoration) and ASD (Applied Structural Drying) credentials, the team follows documented industry standards throughout — which also matters for insurance documentation.

## Working with Insurance on Girard Fire Claims

Fire damage claims are among the most documentation-intensive losses a carrier processes. The DISS team photographs and itemizes affected materials room by room, produces moisture and air-quality readings that support the scope of work, and communicates directly with adjusters throughout the process. Homeowners in Girard are not expected to negotiate the technical scope themselves — that's a conversation between the restoration team and the carrier. Supplement requests, when warranted by conditions found during demolition, are submitted with supporting documentation rather than verbal estimates.

## Local Note

Girard's older homes frequently have original knob-and-tube or early-generation aluminum wiring that was never updated. After a fire, even in a room that appears minimally damaged, that wiring needs to be evaluated by a licensed electrician before restoration crews work in those areas — and before power is restored to any circuit that ran near the fire's path. This is not a restoration company's call to make, but it is something the DISS team flags immediately and coordinates around, because skipping that step creates liability for everyone involved and can void portions of an insurance claim.
