## Why Sharon's Older Housing Stock Complicates Fire Losses

Balloon-frame construction, common in Sharon homes built before the 1950s, lacks the fire blocking that platform-frame buildings rely on. In a balloon-frame house, wall cavities run continuously from the foundation sill to the roof rafters. That means smoke, heat, and combustion gases travel vertically through the structure long after the visible fire is out, depositing soot and odor compounds in spaces that look untouched from the outside. A room that appears clean above the fire floor may have smoke residue inside the wall cavity above it.

Mercer County's cold winters add another layer of complexity. When firefighters suppress a blaze in January or February, the water used during suppression can freeze inside wall assemblies before crews can begin extraction. Ice in a cavity delays moisture readings and extends the drying timeline before any structural or cosmetic repairs can begin. That sequence, fire suppression, water extraction, structural drying, then smoke remediation, has to happen in the right order or secondary damage compounds the original loss.

## Our Fire Damage Restoration Process in Sharon

The first priority on any fire loss is safety and documentation. Before any cleaning begins, the crew walks the structure to identify compromised framing, check for live utilities, and photograph every affected surface for the insurance claim file. Sharon Gas Company and West Penn Power are the utility providers most commonly involved in post-fire shutoffs in this area, and coordinating with them early prevents delays once interior work starts.

Once the structure is cleared, the process moves through distinct phases:

- **Soot and smoke removal**: Dry-chemical sponges remove loose soot before any wet cleaning is applied. Applying water or cleaning agents to a dry-soot surface first drives the residue deeper into porous materials.
- **Odor neutralization**: Thermal fogging and hydroxyl generation reach into the same cavities smoke traveled through. On older plaster walls, odor compounds absorb into the substrate itself, so surface cleaning alone rarely resolves the smell.
- **Structural drying**: Suppression water is extracted and monitored with moisture meters and thermal imaging. IICRC S500 and S700 drying protocols govern target moisture levels before any rebuild begins.
- **Controlled demolition and rebuild coordination**: Charred framing, saturated insulation, and smoke-damaged drywall or plaster are removed to verified clean lines. The rebuild phase follows once the structure is dry and documented.
