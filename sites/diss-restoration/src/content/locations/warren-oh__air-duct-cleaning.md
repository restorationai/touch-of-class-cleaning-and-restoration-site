## Why Warren Properties See Duct Issues

Trumbull County's climate sits in a zone that gets meaningful lake-effect moisture off Lake Erie, and Warren's older residential neighborhoods reflect decades of that exposure. A large share of the housing stock here was built between the 1920s and the 1960s — an era when duct systems were often oversized for the furnace, poorly sealed at joints, and routed through unconditioned crawl spaces or uninsulated attic chases. Those conditions invite condensation, and condensation invites biological growth.

Fiberglass duct liner, common in systems installed through the 1980s, absorbs moisture and holds it. Once that liner is colonized by mold or bacteria, no amount of filter changes at the air handler will clean what's already coating the duct walls. The problem circulates every time the system runs.

Dryer vents are a separate but related concern. In Warren's attached and semi-detached homes — the kind common in the older residential corridors near downtown — dryer vents sometimes run long horizontal distances before exiting the structure. Lint accumulates at every elbow, and a partially blocked dryer vent is one of the leading causes of residential fires in the region.

## Our Air Duct Cleaning Process in Warren

The process starts before any equipment goes into the duct. A technician does a visual inspection of the accessible supply and return runs, checks the condition of the air handler and coil, and identifies any sections with damaged liner, disconnected joints, or visible biological growth. That inspection shapes everything that follows — a house with compromised liner needs a different approach than one with intact sheet metal ductwork.

Cleaning uses negative-air pressure: a high-powered vacuum unit is connected to the main trunk, creating suction throughout the system while technicians work each branch with rotary brush agitation. Debris is captured at the vacuum, not redistributed into the living space. Supply registers and return grilles are removed, cleaned separately, and reinstalled.

When sanitization is warranted — after a moisture event, visible mold growth in the duct, or a confirmed pest intrusion — an EPA-registered antimicrobial is applied by fogging after the mechanical cleaning is complete. The system is not returned to service until the product has fully dried.

Dryer vent cleaning follows the same inspection-first approach. Technicians run a brush through the full length of the vent, clear any lint accumulation at the elbow transitions, and confirm the exterior termination cap is functioning and unobstructed.

## Local Note

Warren's older two-story homes — particularly those built in the post-war decades with gravity-fed or early forced-air systems — often have duct runs that were retrofitted rather than designed from scratch. It's common to find sheet metal transitions into flexible duct that was added during a later renovation, and those flex sections frequently sag or kink over time. A kinked flex run doesn't just restrict airflow; it creates a low point where condensation pools and biological growth accelerates. When cleaning ductwork in homes of this era, technicians note any flex sections that need replacement before they become a recurring problem — that's a detail that matters specifically in Warren's older housing stock and doesn't come up the same way in newer construction.
