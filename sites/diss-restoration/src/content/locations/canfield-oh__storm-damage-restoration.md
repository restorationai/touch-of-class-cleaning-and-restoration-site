## Why Canfield Properties See Significant Storm Damage

Canfield's housing stock skews toward mid-century ranch and two-story colonial construction, with a meaningful share of homes built in the 1950s through 1970s. Those decades predate modern roof decking standards and the widespread use of hurricane straps or ring-shank nails. A 60-mph straight-line wind event — common in Mahoning County during summer convective storms — can lift tab shingles or peel back older three-tab sections, leaving roof decking exposed within minutes. Once the decking is wet, water migrates along rafter tails and into wall cavities before a single interior ceiling stain appears.

The township's mature tree canopy is an asset in calm weather and a liability in storms. Large oaks and silver maples that shade Canfield's residential streets have root systems that can be compromised by wet soil after heavy rain, making wind-throw more likely than in areas with younger or smaller trees. A tree strike on a roof is not just a structural problem — it opens the envelope to rain intrusion, and every hour the interior stays exposed accelerates secondary damage to insulation, framing, and finished surfaces.

## Our Storm Damage Restoration Process in Canfield

The first priority after a storm strike is stopping ongoing intrusion. Our crews carry roof tarps, plywood, and fasteners to every storm call so emergency board-up and weatherproofing happen on the same visit as the initial assessment — not a day later when a second crew can be scheduled.

Once the building envelope is secured, the process shifts to documentation and moisture mapping. We use thermal imaging and calibrated moisture meters to trace water migration through wall assemblies and subflooring, because storm-driven rain rarely stops where it enters. Water that enters through a soffit can travel horizontally along a top plate and appear in a first-floor wall cavity two rooms away. That full picture matters for accurate scope development and for the insurance adjuster reviewing your claim.

Structural drying follows IICRC S500 and S520 protocols — equipment is placed based on psychrometric readings, not by room count. Daily monitoring logs document drying progress, which carriers require before authorizing reconstruction. Our IICRC-certified team holds credentials in Water Damage Restoration, Structural Drying, and Applied Microbial Remediation, so if mold colonization has already begun in a cavity that stayed wet before the storm was reported, we can address it under the same mobilization.

## Working with Insurance After a Canfield Storm

Most storm damage claims in Ohio are filed under the dwelling coverage section of a homeowner's policy, with wind and hail typically covered as named perils. The documentation we produce — moisture maps, thermal images, itemized scope — is formatted to align with what adjusters and desk reviewers expect to see. We communicate directly with your carrier throughout the process and can meet an adjuster on-site if your policy requires an in-person inspection before work begins.

One practical note for Canfield homeowners: Ohio does not cap the number of roof claims a carrier can use to non-renew a policy the way some states do, so it is worth reviewing your policy terms before filing if the damage is borderline. We can help you assess scope honestly so you make an informed decision.
