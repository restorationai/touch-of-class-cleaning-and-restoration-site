## Why Boardman Properties See Water Damage Issues

Boardman's housing stock spans several decades of construction, from mid-century ranch homes to 1980s and 1990s split-levels and newer builds along the township's busier corridors. Older homes frequently have galvanized or early copper supply lines that corrode from the inside out, often without visible warning. Basements in this part of Mahoning County tend to run deep, which is a selling point until a sump pump fails during a heavy spring rain and several inches of standing water collect on a finished floor. The clay-heavy soils throughout the Mahoning Valley shed water rather than absorbing it, meaning surface runoff from storms reaches foundations quickly and can overwhelm drainage systems that were sized for lighter loads.

Flat or gently sloped lots, common in many of Boardman's residential neighborhoods, give water fewer natural paths away from the structure. That combination of soil type, lot grade, and aging mechanical systems means water intrusion here tends to be a recurring pattern rather than a one-time event.

## Our Emergency Water Cleanup Process in Boardman

The first priority on any water call is stopping ongoing intrusion if the source is still active, then getting standing water out before it migrates further. Extraction equipment pulls water from carpet, pad, hardwood subfloor, and concrete, but the visible puddle is rarely the whole story. Moisture meters and thermal imaging cameras reveal what is saturated behind drywall, inside wall cavities, and under flooring where water travels along framing before it ever shows on a surface.

Once extraction is complete, the drying phase begins. Structural drying in Boardman homes follows the IICRC S500 standard, which means placing air movers and dehumidifiers in calculated positions based on the affected square footage, material types, and ambient conditions, then monitoring moisture readings daily until materials reach documented dry standards. In finished basements with multiple material layers, that process typically takes several days of active monitoring, not a single visit.

Documentation runs parallel to every step: photos, moisture logs, and equipment placement records that your insurance adjuster will need to process the claim.

## Insurance Coordination for Boardman Water Claims

Most standard homeowner policies cover sudden and accidental water discharge, which includes burst pipes, appliance failures, and certain storm-related intrusion. What they typically exclude is gradual leakage that went unaddressed, so the documentation DISS produces from the first hour on-site matters. The moisture logs, photographs, and written scope of loss give your adjuster a clear picture of when the damage occurred and what it affected.

If your policy includes contents coverage, the inventory process starts at the same time as extraction so nothing is overlooked before materials are moved for drying access.

## Local Note

Boardman's split-level homes from the 1970s and 1980s present a specific drying challenge that does not come up as often in single-story construction. The half-level between the garage and the main living area often contains the mechanical room, laundry, and sometimes a finished family room, all on a concrete slab with wood framing above. When water enters that level, it can wick up into the framing that supports the floor above before anyone notices moisture on the upper level. Technicians working in these homes check the subfloor system from below as a standard step, not an afterthought, because the framing can read saturated even when the concrete slab itself has already dried.
