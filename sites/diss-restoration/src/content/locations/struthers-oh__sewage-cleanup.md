## Why Struthers Properties See Sewage Backup Issues

Much of Struthers's residential housing stock dates to the mid-20th century, built during the steel-industry boom that shaped the entire Mahoning Valley. Homes from that era were typically connected to municipal sewer lines that are now decades past their design life. Cast iron and clay tile lateral lines — the short runs connecting a home to the street main — are prone to root intrusion and joint separation, especially after the freeze-thaw cycles that hit this part of northeast Ohio hard every winter. When those laterals fail, the backup doesn't announce itself gradually; it arrives as raw sewage at the floor drain, laundry tub, or lowest toilet in the house.

The clay-heavy glacial soils throughout the Struthers area also slow surface drainage after heavy rain, which raises hydrostatic pressure against basement walls and can force ground-saturated water — sometimes mixed with sewer overflow — through foundation cracks. Homes with older sump systems that lack a backflow preventer on the drain line are especially vulnerable during combined storm and sanitary sewer events, which are not uncommon in this part of Ohio's infrastructure landscape.

## Our Sewage Cleanup and Sanitization Process in Struthers

Sewage is classified as Category 3 water — the highest contamination level — which means the work follows a specific protocol from the moment the crew arrives.

**Containment and assessment first.** Before any material is moved, the affected area is assessed for the full extent of contamination. In Struthers's older basement layouts, sewage often travels farther than it appears: it runs under utility shelving, behind water heater pads, and into wall cavities at the base of block foundations. Thermal imaging and moisture meters map the spread before removal begins.

**Extraction and removal.** Standing sewage is extracted with truck-mounted units. Porous materials — drywall, carpet, insulation, wood paneling — that have contacted Category 3 water are removed rather than dried in place. This is non-negotiable from both an IICRC standard and a public health standpoint.

**Sanitization.** After structural materials are exposed, all affected surfaces are treated with EPA-registered disinfectants appropriate for sewage contamination. The process addresses bacteria, viruses, and the odor compounds that persist long after visible waste is gone. In homes with concrete block walls, which are common in this area's basement construction, the disinfection step includes the mortar joints, where contaminated water tends to pool.

**Drying and documentation.** Remaining structural materials are dried to IICRC S500 standard using commercial desiccant and refrigerant dehumidifiers and air movers. Every reading is logged for your insurance carrier.
