## Why Campbell Properties Face Distinct Water Damage Risks

The housing stock throughout Campbell skews older, with a large share of homes built before World War II. That era of construction means galvanized steel supply lines that have spent decades corroding from the inside out, cast-iron drain stacks that crack rather than flex, and basement walls poured without modern waterproofing membranes. A pinhole leak in galvanized pipe can run undetected for weeks behind plaster-and-lath walls, which absorb moisture differently than modern drywall. By the time a stain appears on the surface, the wood lath and framing behind it may already be wet enough to support mold growth within 24 to 48 hours.

Campbell's position along the Mahoning River also means that heavy rain events can overwhelm the combined sewer infrastructure that serves older neighborhoods, sending sewage-laden water back through floor drains. That is a category-3 contaminated water situation, not a simple wet-floor problem, and it requires a different extraction and disinfection protocol than a clean supply-line break.

## Our Emergency Water Cleanup Process in Campbell

When a call comes in, the first priority is stopping any active source. If that requires coordinating a shutoff with Ohio American Water, we walk you through the steps while the crew is already in transit. On arrival, technicians use thermal imaging cameras and penetrating moisture meters to map the full extent of saturation, including inside wall cavities and under flooring where water migrates invisibly.

Extraction comes next. Truck-mounted and portable extractors pull standing water from hard floors, carpet, and subfloor assemblies. In Campbell's older homes, this step often reveals water that has tracked under hardwood floors and into the crawl space or basement below. Once bulk water is removed, the crew sets a calculated array of commercial air movers and refrigerant or desiccant dehumidifiers calibrated to the square footage and material types present. Drying logs are recorded daily and shared with your insurance adjuster as documentation.

If contaminated water is involved, affected porous materials are removed, bagged, and disposed of per standard protocols before any drying equipment is placed. Surfaces are treated with an EPA-registered disinfectant before the space is considered safe to re-occupy.

## Local Note

In Campbell's older neighborhoods, many homes were built with full-depth plaster walls over wood lath rather than the gypsum drywall used in post-1970 construction. Plaster holds moisture stubbornly. A moisture meter reading at the surface can show acceptable numbers while the lath and framing behind the plaster remain saturated. Technicians working in these homes need to take readings at multiple depths and extend drying timelines accordingly, sometimes by several days compared to a comparable-sized loss in newer construction. Skipping that step and closing out a job too early is one of the more common reasons secondary mold problems develop weeks after a water loss appears to be resolved.
