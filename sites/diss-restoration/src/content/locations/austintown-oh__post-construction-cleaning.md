## Our Post-Construction Cleaning Process in Austintown

A post-construction clean isn't a single pass — it's typically three distinct phases, and skipping any one of them shows up the moment natural light hits the room.

**Rough clean** removes the bulk debris: scrap lumber, packaging, drywall offcuts, and the heavy accumulation of dust from surfaces and subfloors. This phase also includes clearing construction adhesive, paint overspray, and caulk smears before they cure further and become harder to lift without damaging the finish beneath.

**Detail clean** is where most of the time goes. Every window sill, light fixture, cabinet interior, door frame, and vent register gets wiped individually. HVAC returns are vacuumed with HEPA-filtered equipment so construction particulate isn't redistributed the first time the system runs. Flooring — whether new LVP, hardwood, or tile — is cleaned with products matched to the material so the protective finish isn't compromised before the owners even move in.

**Final inspection clean** happens closest to occupancy or handoff. Touch-up on glass, polishing of fixtures, and a walk-through against the contractor's punch list. If the general contractor or property owner needs documentation for a certificate of occupancy or a client walkthrough, we can provide a written scope of what was cleaned and when.

## Equipment and Methods for Post-Construction Cleaning

Construction dust isn't ordinary household dust. It contains silica, fiberglass fibers, and chemical compounds from adhesives and coatings that require commercial-grade HEPA vacuuming rather than standard shop vacs that recirculate fine particles back into the air. The team uses industrial HEPA vacuums, microfiber systems rated for fine particulate, and where air quality is a concern, air scrubbers that filter the room environment while surface cleaning is underway. For specialty cleaning needs — pressure washing of exterior concrete, degreasing of commercial kitchen buildouts, or deep cleaning of newly finished bathrooms — equipment and chemistry are matched to the surface and the contamination type.

## Local Note

Austintown's older ranch homes — particularly those built in the 1950s through 1970s — often have forced-air duct systems that run through unconditioned crawl spaces or low attic spaces. During a renovation, those ducts act like collection points for construction dust even when registers are taped off, because the tape seal rarely holds through weeks of work. On these properties, we treat duct cleaning as part of the post-construction scope rather than an optional add-on, because running the HVAC before the ducts are cleared just redistributes everything the surface clean removed.
