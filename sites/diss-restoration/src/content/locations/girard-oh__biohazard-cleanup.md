## Why Girard's Housing Stock Shapes Biohazard Cleanup

A significant portion of Girard's residential properties date to the early and mid-twentieth century — an era of plaster-and-lath construction, unfinished basement floors, and porous concrete block foundations. These materials behave very differently from modern drywall and sealed concrete when they come into contact with biological material. Plaster and raw concrete are highly absorbent; contamination can wick into subsurface layers that look clean at the surface. Thorough remediation here requires more than surface treatment — it means assessing what is hidden beneath, not just what is visible.

That older construction also tends to mean narrower interior doorways, steep staircases, and limited ventilation — all practical factors that affect how a crew stages equipment and moves materials safely out of a home without cross-contaminating unaffected areas. These are not obstacles, just realities of the local building stock that an experienced team accounts for before work begins.

## Our Biohazard Cleanup Process in Girard

Every situation is different, and the process is always adapted to what is actually present — not a generic checklist.
