**Musty couch cushions after a damp Shenango Valley summer?** Upholstery in Greenville homes picks up humidity fast, and once a sofa or armchair holds moisture, odor and mildew follow within days. A professional cleaning resets the fabric before that happens, and for leather pieces it keeps the hide from drying out and cracking under Mercer County's swing from humid summers to bone-dry winter heat.

## Why Greenville Upholstery Takes On Moisture and Odor

Greenville sits along the Shenango River, and that river valley setting means summer humidity lingers in homes longer than it does on higher, drier ground elsewhere in the county. A lot of the housing stock here is older, built well before central air was standard, and many living rooms still rely on window units or open windows for cooling. That combination, older construction plus seasonal humidity, means fabric sofas and chairs absorb ambient moisture even when there's no obvious spill or leak. Add pets, wood stoves that leave a faint smoke film on nearby furniture, or a finished basement den where humidity runs higher than the rest of the house, and upholstery starts holding odor well before anyone notices visible staining.

## Our Upholstery Cleaning Process

We start by identifying the fabric, natural fiber, synthetic blend, or leather, because each responds differently to moisture and cleaning agents. Cotton and linen blends common in older Greenville homes can water-spot if the wrong method is used, so we test a hidden area first. From there we vacuum to lift dry soil, pre-treat any stains or odor sources, and use either low-moisture steam extraction for fabric or a pH-balanced conditioning clean for leather. Fabric pieces get a controlled amount of hot water extraction, enough to lift embedded soil and allergens without oversaturating the cushion fill, since foam and batting hold water longer than the fabric shell itself and that's what actually drives musty odor if drying isn't managed correctly.
