## Why Boardman Properties See Asbestos Concerns

The housing stock along the corridors that grew out from Youngstown during the mid-twentieth century was built when asbestos was considered a feature, not a liability. In Boardman, that means a large share of homes constructed before 1980 may contain asbestos in places that aren't immediately visible — behind drywall, beneath vinyl flooring layers, around HVAC duct wrap, and in the joint compound used on original plaster seams. Popcorn ceilings are among the most commonly flagged materials, particularly in homes where the original texture was never scraped or painted over.

Ohio's freeze-thaw cycle adds another layer of urgency. Repeated seasonal movement stresses older pipe insulation and floor tile adhesives, causing microscopic cracking that can begin releasing fibers before anyone realizes there's a problem. A plumbing repair, a basement finishing project, or even aggressive HVAC servicing can disturb materials that have been stable for decades. Because Boardman sits in a climate zone where hard winters are routine, the mechanical stress on these materials is ongoing rather than occasional.

## Our Asbestos Abatement Process in Boardman

Every project starts with a thorough inspection and bulk sampling of suspect materials before any work begins. Samples are sent to an accredited laboratory, and no abatement activity starts until results are confirmed — that sequence matters both for your safety and for Ohio EPA compliance, which governs how asbestos-containing material must be handled, contained, and disposed of in Mahoning County.

Once a positive identification is made, the affected area is isolated using negative air pressure containment. HEPA-filtered air scrubbers run continuously during removal to prevent fiber migration into adjacent living spaces. Wet methods are used throughout to keep disturbed material from becoming airborne. All waste is sealed in labeled, double-bagged containers and transported to an approved disposal facility — Ohio has specific requirements for manifesting and transporting asbestos waste that differ from general construction debris rules.

Air clearance testing follows removal. Clearance must confirm fiber counts are within acceptable limits before containment is broken and the space is returned to use. The team is EPA Lead-Safe Certified and OSHA trained, which matters in older Boardman homes where asbestos and lead-based paint frequently coexist in the same structure.

## Boardman Insurance Coordination

Asbestos abatement is sometimes covered under homeowner's policies when the disturbance is tied to a covered loss — a pipe burst that soaks a floor with asbestos tile adhesive, for example, or fire damage that exposes insulated materials. Coverage varies significantly by carrier and policy language, and Ohio adjusters handle these claims differently depending on whether the asbestos was pre-existing or incidentally disturbed. The team documents affected materials thoroughly — photographs, sample chain-of-custody records, and written scope — so the claim file is complete regardless of which carrier is involved.

## Local Note

Boardman's mid-century ranch homes frequently have original floor tile running under multiple layers of later flooring — a second layer of vinyl, then carpet, then sometimes a floating laminate on top. Homeowners and contractors often don't realize the original 9×9 or 12×12 asbestos-containing tile is still present until demolition begins. In these situations, the overlying layers need to be carefully lifted and assessed before any grinding, cutting, or adhesive removal takes place. Rushing that step because the tile "looks old but intact" is how fiber release happens in an otherwise controlled renovation. If you're planning a flooring replacement in a Boardman home built before 1980, a pre-renovation inspection is worth scheduling before the first board comes up.
