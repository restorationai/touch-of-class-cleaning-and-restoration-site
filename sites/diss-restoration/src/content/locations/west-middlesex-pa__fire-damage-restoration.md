## Why West Middlesex Properties Are Vulnerable to Severe Fire Damage

Mercer County's housing stock skews older, and West Middlesex is no exception. Many homes in the borough and surrounding township were built between the 1920s and 1960s, when balloon-frame construction was standard. In a balloon-frame house, wall cavities run continuously from the basement sill plate to the roof rafters with no fire blocking between floors. A fire that starts in a first-floor wall can travel vertically through the entire structure in minutes, and smoke follows the same path long after the flames are out. That smoke lodges in attic insulation, inside wall cavities, and behind original plaster, where standard cleaning cannot reach it without targeted removal and treatment.

The valley's cold winters add another layer of complication. When fire suppression soaks a structure in January or February, standing water in unheated spaces freezes quickly, expanding inside subfloor assemblies and masonry foundations. Structural drying has to begin before temperatures drop again, which compresses the window for mitigation work.

## Our Fire Damage Restoration Process in West Middlesex

The first step on any fire job is a thorough assessment of what the fire actually touched versus what smoke and suppression water reached. Those are often very different boundaries, and confusing them leads to incomplete restoration. After the structure is secured and utilities are confirmed safe, the process moves through several distinct phases:

**Debris and char removal** clears materials that cannot be restored and exposes the true extent of penetration into framing and subfloor.

**Dry ice blasting and HEPA vacuuming** remove soot from framing, joists, and masonry surfaces without abrading the substrate, which matters in older homes where original wood framing is irreplaceable.

**Odor neutralization** uses thermal fogging and hydroxyl generation to reach the same cavities smoke traveled through. Surface deodorizers applied to finished walls do not eliminate smoke odor in a balloon-frame home; the source is behind the wall, not on it.

**Structural drying** runs concurrently using industrial dehumidifiers and air movers calibrated to the specific material assemblies present, following IICRC S700 guidelines for fire and smoke restoration.

Because many West Middlesex homes have original plaster over wood lath, the drying phase requires moisture mapping at the lath layer, not just at the plaster face. Plaster can read dry at the surface while the lath behind it stays saturated for days.
