## Why Mercer Properties See Mold Issues

Mercer County sits in a climate band that delivers real humidity swings: summer dew points climb high enough to condense moisture on basement walls and crawl space joists, while winter brings repeated freeze-thaw cycles that work water into masonry and mortar joints. Many homes in the Mercer area were built before modern vapor barriers and building envelope standards became common practice. Fieldstone foundations, uninsulated rim joists, and plaster-and-lath interior walls are not unusual here, and each of those assemblies holds moisture differently than modern construction.

Crawl spaces are a particular concern in this region. Bare-earth crawl spaces under older homes allow ground moisture to evaporate upward into the floor framing year-round. When that vapor has nowhere to escape, wood subfloor members and floor joists can sustain enough relative humidity to support mold growth even without a visible leak. Homeowners sometimes notice a musty odor in the living area above before they ever see discoloration on a surface.

## Our Mold Remediation Process in Mercer

Every job starts with a thorough inspection rather than a visual scan. Moisture meters and thermal imaging help locate saturation behind plaster, inside wall cavities, and under flooring where surface appearance tells you nothing. Once the moisture map is complete, the scope of remediation becomes clear.

Containment comes next. Affected areas are sealed with polyethylene sheeting and placed under negative air pressure using HEPA-filtered air scrubbers. This keeps spores from migrating to unaffected rooms during the removal process. Contaminated porous materials, including drywall, insulation, and in some cases plaster, are removed and bagged for disposal following established industry protocols.

Surface treatment of structural components uses EPA-registered antimicrobial agents applied after mechanical cleaning. The goal is to address the colonized material, not simply mask it. After remediation, the area is dried to target moisture content before any reconstruction begins. In homes with plaster-and-lath construction, that drying phase requires patience: plaster releases absorbed moisture more slowly than drywall, and rushing it risks setting up conditions for a repeat problem.

## Local Note

Older homes in the Mercer area built with plaster-and-lath walls present a specific challenge during mold remediation that is easy to underestimate. Plaster can read at acceptable surface moisture levels on a pin-type meter while the wood lath behind it remains saturated and actively supporting mold growth. Technicians working in this housing stock need to probe deeper and allow longer drying times before closing up wall cavities. Skipping that step is one of the more common reasons mold returns after a remediation that appeared complete.
