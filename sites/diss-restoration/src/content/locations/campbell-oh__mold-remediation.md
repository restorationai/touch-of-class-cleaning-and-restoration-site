## Why Campbell Properties See Mold Issues

Campbell's residential neighborhoods are dominated by homes built during the steel-boom decades of the early to mid-twentieth century. That era's construction relied on plaster-and-lath walls, wood-framed basements, and minimal vapor barriers, none of which were designed with today's understanding of moisture management in mind. Plaster absorbs water slowly and releases it even more slowly, which means a surface that reads dry to the touch can still be feeding mold colonies behind the wall for weeks.

The valley topography compounds the problem. Low-lying streets in Campbell can hold surface water after heavy rains, and the region's clay-dominant soil drains poorly, keeping foundation walls damp long after the storm passes. Basements and crawl spaces in this part of Mahoning County are among the most common starting points for mold growth, particularly when sump pumps fail or floor drains back up during high-water events on the Mahoning River corridor.

## Our Mold Remediation Process in Campbell

Every remediation starts with a thorough inspection, not a visual scan. Moisture meters and thermal imaging reveal what the eye misses, including wet framing inside finished walls and saturated subfloor material under vinyl or hardwood. Once the scope is clear, the crew establishes negative-air containment using polyethylene barriers and HEPA-filtered air scrubbers to prevent spores from migrating to unaffected rooms while work is underway.

Affected porous materials, drywall, insulation, and in older Campbell homes, plaster sections that have lost their bond, are removed and bagged for disposal. Structural framing and masonry surfaces are treated with EPA-registered antimicrobial agents. The area is then dried to IICRC S520 standard before any rebuild begins. DISS Restoration is EPA Lead-Safe Certified, which matters in Campbell's pre-1978 housing stock where disturbing walls during mold work can expose lead paint.
