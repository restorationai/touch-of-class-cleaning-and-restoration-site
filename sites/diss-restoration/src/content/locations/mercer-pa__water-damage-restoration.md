## Why Mercer Properties See Water Damage Issues

Mercer Borough and the surrounding townships carry a large share of housing built before 1960. In homes that old, the plumbing is often a patchwork of galvanized steel, copper, and cast iron, each with its own failure mode. Galvanized supply lines corrode from the inside out, narrowing over decades until a cold snap or a pressure spike splits them. Cast-iron drain stacks crack quietly, sometimes weeping behind plaster walls for months before a homeowner notices a stain on the ceiling below.

The soil across much of Mercer County is clay-heavy, which means it drains slowly. After a significant rain event, that clay holds saturation close to foundation walls, and any crack or gap in a block or poured-concrete foundation becomes a path for groundwater intrusion. Finished basements, which are common in the area's split-levels and ranches, can absorb a surprising amount of water before the damage is visible at floor level. By the time you see it, the subfloor and lower wall cavities are already wet.

## Our Water Damage Restoration Process in Mercer

Every job starts with a moisture survey before any equipment is placed. Thermal imaging and calibrated moisture meters map where water has traveled, including inside wall cavities, under hardwood flooring, and behind tile backer board. That map drives every decision that follows.

Water extraction comes next, using truck-mounted and portable extraction units sized to the loss. Standing water in a basement comes out first; then the crew addresses absorbed water in carpet, padding, and subfloor assemblies. In Mercer's older homes with tongue-and-groove hardwood floors, the extraction approach matters: aggressive surface extraction can pull enough moisture to warp boards that might otherwise be saved with controlled drying.

Structural drying uses a calculated combination of commercial desiccant or refrigerant dehumidifiers, high-velocity air movers, and where needed, injectidry panels that direct airflow into wall cavities without requiring full demolition. Moisture readings are logged at each visit so you and your insurance adjuster can see the drying curve in writing, not just take our word for it.

Demolition, when it is necessary, is scoped to what the moisture data supports. Removing only what is wet keeps your rebuild cost lower and shortens the overall timeline.

## Local Note

In Mercer's older two-story homes, plaster-on-lath wall construction is still common, and it behaves very differently from modern drywall when it gets wet. Plaster is dense and slow to release moisture, so a wall that reads nearly dry at the surface with a pin meter can still be holding significant moisture in the lath and framing behind it. Crews who are not calibrated for this can pull equipment too early and leave conditions that support mold growth inside the wall assembly. The IICRC S500 standard guides drying goals regardless of wall type, but reaching those goals in plaster construction takes patience and more frequent monitoring than a standard drywall job.

If you are dealing with water damage in Mercer and the home has plaster walls, mention it when you call. It changes the drying plan from the start.
