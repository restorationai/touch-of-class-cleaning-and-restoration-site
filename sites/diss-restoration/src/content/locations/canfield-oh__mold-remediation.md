## Why Canfield Properties See Mold Issues

Mahoning County's climate delivers cold, damp winters followed by humid summers — a cycle that stresses building envelopes in ways that show up as moisture intrusion long after the season that caused it. Canfield's housing stock includes a substantial number of homes built in the mid-twentieth century, when vapor barriers were minimal or absent and crawl spaces were left unencapsulated. That combination means ground moisture wicks upward through concrete block foundations and into floor systems, creating the sustained humidity that mold colonies need to establish and spread.

Clay soils throughout this part of Mahoning County also drain slowly after heavy rain. When gutters overflow or grading has settled toward a foundation over the decades, water pools against the structure rather than sheeting away. A finished basement that feels dry in July can carry elevated moisture readings in the framing by September — and by the following spring, visible mold growth on wood joists or OSB sheathing is not unusual.

Older homes with plaster walls add another layer of complexity. Plaster absorbs and holds moisture differently than modern drywall; the surface can feel and even read dry while the wood lath behind it stays damp enough to support mold growth for weeks after a leak is resolved.

## Our Mold Remediation Process in Canfield

Every project starts with a thorough inspection — moisture mapping with thermal imaging and calibrated meters to locate the actual wet zone, not just the visible staining. Mold is almost always a symptom of an ongoing moisture condition, and addressing the growth without resolving the source is what causes recurrence.

Once the scope is established, the crew builds negative-pressure containment around the affected area using 6-mil poly sheeting and HEPA-filtered air scrubbers. This keeps disturbed spores from migrating into unaffected living spaces during removal — a step that matters especially in the ranch-style and split-level layouts common across Canfield, where HVAC returns can pull air from a basement or utility room directly into the main living area.

Affected porous materials — drywall, insulation, wood framing where colonization has penetrated the surface — are removed, bagged, and disposed of properly. Structural wood that can be retained is treated and wire-brushed clean. The area is then dried to documented moisture targets before any reconstruction begins. Because DISS Restoration holds EPA Lead-Safe certification, homes built before 1978 get the additional precautions required when remediation disturbs painted surfaces — a relevant consideration in Canfield's older neighborhoods.
