## Why Girard Homes Have Unique Renovation Needs

Girard sits in Trumbull County, where the housing stock skews heavily toward mid-century and earlier construction. Homes built before 1978 are common throughout the city, and that matters the moment you start opening walls. Lead-based paint is a real consideration in pre-renovation work here — not a formality. As an EPA Lead-Safe Certified firm, DISS Restoration follows proper containment and disposal protocols before any remodel work begins, which protects your family and keeps the project compliant.

The Mahoning Valley climate adds its own pressure. Freeze-thaw cycles through Girard winters are hard on older foundations, window frames, and exterior masonry. Inside, homes that have experienced even minor water intrusion over the years often show it in subfloor rot, warped framing, or plaster that's lost its key. A renovation that skips past those underlying conditions just buries the problem. The right approach is to assess what's there, address what's compromised, and then build the finish work on top of something solid.

Older plumbing configurations — galvanized supply lines, cast iron drain stacks — are common in Girard's pre-1960s homes and frequently surface during kitchen and bathroom remodels. Knowing that going in means fewer surprises mid-project and more accurate scoping from the start.

## Local Note

Girard's older neighborhoods include a significant number of homes with original plaster-and-lath interior walls rather than drywall. During renovation work — especially kitchen and bathroom remodels that require opening walls for plumbing or electrical — plaster behaves differently than modern drywall. It's heavier, it generates more debris, and it requires more careful demolition to avoid damaging adjacent surfaces. On post-water-damage rebuilds, plaster also holds residual moisture longer than drywall does, which means the drying verification step before closing walls is especially important. Crews who haven't worked in older Mahoning Valley housing stock sometimes underestimate this and close walls prematurely. It's a detail worth asking any contractor about before work begins.
