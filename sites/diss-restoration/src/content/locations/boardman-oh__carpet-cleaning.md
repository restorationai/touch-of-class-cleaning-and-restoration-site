## Why Boardman Carpets Take a Harder Hit

Mahoning County sits in the Great Lakes snow belt, and Boardman is no exception. Salt and calcium chloride tracked in from Southern Boulevard parking lots and Market Street strip centers work their way into carpet backing and padding, where they attract moisture and accelerate fiber breakdown. Homes built in Boardman's established residential corridors — many dating from the postwar boom of the late 1940s through the 1960s — often have original subfloors that hold humidity longer than modern OSB. That means a spill or a pet accident doesn't just affect the carpet; it can saturate the pad and reach the subfloor before it's visible at the surface. Identifying how deep contamination has traveled is the first thing a professional cleaning needs to address, not an afterthought.

Commercial properties along Boardman's busy retail corridors face a different version of the same problem: high foot traffic compresses fibers and drives fine particulate deep into the pile, where it acts like sandpaper with every step. ## Our Carpet Cleaning Process in Boardman

Every job starts with a walkthrough to identify fiber type, soiling pattern, and any areas of concern — pet odor, set-in stains, or moisture-related discoloration that suggests pad saturation. From there, the process follows a consistent sequence:

- **Pre-treatment**: A pH-appropriate conditioner is applied to loosen soil and break down organic matter before any water touches the carpet.
- **Agitation**: A grooming tool works the pre-treatment into the pile, particularly important in high-traffic lanes where compaction is heaviest.
- **Hot-water extraction**: Truck-mounted equipment heats water to the temperature range proven most effective for the fiber type and flushes soil out rather than redistributing it.
- **Spot treatment**: Stains that don't respond to the main extraction pass — rust, tannin, or protein-based stains — receive targeted chemistry matched to the stain type.
- **Speed drying**: High-velocity air movers reduce drying time, which matters in Boardman's humid summers when ambient moisture can extend natural drying to 24 hours or more.

For jobs where pet odor or biological contamination is involved, carpet sanitization is applied after extraction — not before — so the agent reaches clean fibers rather than soiled ones.
