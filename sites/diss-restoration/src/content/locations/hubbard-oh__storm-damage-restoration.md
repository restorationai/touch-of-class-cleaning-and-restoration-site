## Why Hubbard Properties See Storm Damage the Way They Do

Hubbard's housing stock tells a story in its bones. A significant portion of the city's residential neighborhoods were built between the 1920s and 1960s, when balloon-frame construction was standard and attic ventilation details were minimal by today's code. When wind-driven rain finds a gap in an aging soffit or a lifted shingle, it doesn't just wet the insulation — it travels down interior wall cavities and pools at the sole plate before anyone sees a stain on the ceiling below.

The region's clay-heavy soil compounds the problem. Trumbull County's glacially deposited clay drains slowly, so the ground saturates quickly during prolonged storm events. Once the soil is at capacity, water that would otherwise percolate away instead migrates toward foundation walls. Older block and poured-concrete foundations common in Hubbard's mid-century homes are particularly susceptible to hydrostatic pressure after a heavy storm — a dynamic that can push moisture into basements hours after the rain has stopped.

The tree canopy throughout Hubbard is mature and dense, which adds another layer of risk. Silver maples and cottonwoods — both common in this part of Ohio — have shallow root systems and brittle branch structures that fail under ice load or sustained wind. A downed limb that punches through a roof deck opens the structure to whatever weather follows.

## Our Storm Damage Restoration Process in Hubbard

The first priority after any storm event is stopping the loss from growing. That means emergency tarping of breached roofs, boarding compromised windows and doors, and extracting any standing water before it migrates further into the structure. IICRC-certified technicians use moisture mapping — thermal imaging paired with calibrated meters — to trace water migration through wall cavities and subfloor assemblies, not just the surfaces you can see.

Once the structure is stabilized and documented, the drying phase begins. Industrial desiccant and refrigerant dehumidifiers, along with high-velocity air movers, are positioned based on the moisture map rather than a generic room-by-room approach. In Hubbard's older homes, where plaster-over-lath walls are still common, drying timelines run longer than they would in modern drywall construction — plaster holds moisture deep in the substrate and releases it slowly, so equipment placement and monitoring intervals are adjusted accordingly.

After structural drying is verified with daily readings, the reconstruction phase addresses what the storm actually damaged: roof decking, sheathing, framing, insulation, drywall, and exterior cladding. The team documents every step with photographs and written logs, which feeds directly into the insurance claim file.

## Insurance Coordination After a Storm in Hubbard

Most homeowner policies in Ohio cover sudden and accidental storm damage, but the documentation burden falls on the policyholder to demonstrate the cause and extent of loss. DISS Restoration produces a detailed scope of work — line-item estimates, moisture readings with timestamps, and photo documentation of every affected area — formatted to work with major carriers' claims platforms.
