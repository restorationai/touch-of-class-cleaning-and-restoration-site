## Why New Castle's Older Housing Stock Complicates Fire Damage

A significant share of New Castle's residential neighborhoods were built before modern building codes introduced fire-blocking requirements inside wall cavities. In balloon-frame construction, a single stud bay runs continuously from the foundation sill to the roof rafters. Fire and smoke exploit that open channel, meaning a kitchen fire can deposit soot inside second-floor walls or beneath roof sheathing without any visible burn damage on the surface. Homeowners and even some contractors underestimate the spread because the exterior looks intact.

Plaster walls compound the challenge in a different way. Plaster is dense and alkaline, and it absorbs smoke odor compounds differently than modern drywall. Ozone and hydroxyl treatments that work quickly on painted drywall may need longer dwell times or additional thermal fogging cycles to neutralize odor locked into a thick plaster coat. Recognizing the construction type before choosing a deodorization method is not optional here; it is what separates a job that passes a final sniff test from one that smells like smoke again the following summer when humidity rises.

## Our Fire Damage Restoration Process in New Castle

The first priority after the fire department clears a structure is stabilization. That means boarding windows and doors, tarping any compromised roof sections, and securing the property against weather. Lawrence County winters bring wet, heavy snow and freeze-thaw cycles that can push water into a fire-damaged structure within hours of the initial loss, turning a fire claim into a combined fire-and-water claim.

Once the structure is stable, the process moves through these stages:

- **Scope and documentation.** Every affected surface is photographed and measured before a single item is moved. This documentation supports your insurance claim and establishes a baseline for what requires cleaning versus replacement.
- **Debris removal and content pack-out.** Salvageable contents are inventoried and transported to a controlled environment for cleaning. Items that cannot be restored are documented for the adjuster.
- **Structural cleaning.** Soot is acidic and continues to etch and corrode surfaces the longer it sits. Exposed framing, subfloor, and masonry are cleaned using dry chemical sponges, HEPA vacuuming, and appropriate wet cleaning agents matched to the surface type.
- **Deodorization.** Thermal fogging and hydroxyl generation address residual odor in cavities and porous materials. In homes with original plaster, this phase often runs longer than industry averages.
- **Reconstruction coordination.** Once cleaning and drying are complete, the structure is ready for repairs. DISS Restoration coordinates the rebuild phase so you are working with one point of contact rather than managing separate contractors.
