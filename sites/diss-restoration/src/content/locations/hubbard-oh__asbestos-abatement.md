## Why Hubbard Properties See Asbestos Concerns

Hubbard sits in a part of northeastern Ohio where the postwar building boom left a dense footprint of homes constructed between the late 1940s and the mid-1970s — the exact window when asbestos use in residential construction peaked. Vinyl floor tiles in basements and kitchens, duct wrap on forced-air systems, textured ceiling coatings, and joint compound in drywall installations from that era routinely tested positive for asbestos-containing materials (ACMs). The region's freeze-thaw cycles are hard on older construction: foundation movement cracks tile, pipe insulation shifts and flakes, and ceiling materials that were once stable become friable over time. Friable ACMs — materials that can be crumbled by hand — release fibers into the air far more readily than intact ones, which is why a routine basement renovation or HVAC replacement in an older Hubbard home can quickly become an abatement situation.

Ohio also requires notification to the Ohio EPA for certain asbestos projects before work begins, and Trumbull County properties are subject to those state-level oversight requirements. That regulatory step matters for homeowners planning additions or gut renovations — skipping it can result in project shutdowns and fines.

## Our Asbestos Abatement Process in Hubbard

Abatement isn't simply pulling out old materials. It follows a defined sequence designed to prevent fiber release at every stage.

**Inspection and bulk sampling** come first. A visual inspection identifies suspect materials — floor tiles, pipe wrap, ceiling texture, roofing underlayment, insulation board — and bulk samples are collected and sent to an accredited laboratory. Results determine whether materials are ACM and whether they are friable or non-friable, which shapes the abatement approach.

**Containment setup** isolates the work area using heavy-gauge polyethylene sheeting, negative air pressure units with HEPA filtration, and regulated entry/exit decontamination chambers. This step is non-negotiable regardless of project size.

**Wet removal** keeps fibers from becoming airborne during extraction. ACMs are wetted with amended water before and during removal, then double-bagged in labeled, sealed disposal containers.

**Air clearance testing** — conducted by an independent party — confirms fiber counts are below acceptable thresholds before containment is removed and the space is returned to use. This step protects both the occupants and the project record.

All waste is transported and disposed of at a licensed facility in compliance with Ohio EPA requirements.

## Equipment & Methods We Use for Asbestos

The equipment on an abatement job is specific to the hazard. HEPA-filtered negative air machines maintain negative pressure inside containment zones so that any airborne fibers are drawn away from living spaces rather than toward them. Full-face respirators with P100 cartridges and disposable Tyvek suits are standard for anyone entering the work area. Wet-wipe decontamination of all surfaces before containment teardown, followed by HEPA vacuuming, addresses residual fiber that wet removal alone may not capture. For popcorn ceiling asbestos removal — one of the more common requests in older Hubbard homes — ceiling area is wetted in sections and removed carefully to avoid the overhead scatter that dry scraping causes.
