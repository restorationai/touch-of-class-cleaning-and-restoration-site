## Why Boardman Properties Are Particularly Exposed to Storm and Fire Openings

Much of Boardman's residential housing stock dates to the postwar building boom of the late 1940s through the 1960s, when the township grew rapidly as families moved south from Youngstown's urban core. That era of construction means a lot of homes here have original wood-frame windows, aging soffit and fascia, and roofs that have been patched rather than fully replaced. When a storm or fire event compromises one of those original openings, the damage doesn't stay contained — wind-driven rain can push through a broken window frame and saturate plaster walls within hours, and a fire-damaged roof deck left open overnight will absorb moisture that complicates every repair that follows.

Mahoning County's clay-heavy soil also matters here. It drains slowly, which means standing water around a foundation after a storm event is common. If a board-up or tarp isn't installed correctly — if it allows water to channel toward the foundation rather than away from it — you can end up with a secondary water intrusion problem on top of the original loss.

## Our Emergency Board-Up and Tarping Process in Boardman

When a call comes in, the first priority is a fast visual assessment of every compromised opening — not just the obvious ones. A fire that vents through a window on one side of a house often creates pressure that cracks or loosens glazing on the opposite side. A roof breach from a fallen limb may have also displaced flashing around a chimney or vent stack. We document all of it before any material goes up.

For window and door openings, we use structural-grade plywood cut and fastened to the framing, not just draped over the opening. For roof damage, heavy-duty polyethylene tarps are secured with battens and weighted edges rather than stapled at the perimeter — a method that holds through follow-on wind events instead of peeling back by morning. All work is photographed systematically, which matters when an insurance adjuster needs to verify the scope of emergency mitigation before authorizing the repair estimate.

Because DISS Restoration holds IICRC FSRT certification in fire and smoke restoration, the crew doing your board-up understands how fire damage behaves structurally — they're not just covering holes, they're looking at what the opening tells them about the burn pattern and what that means for the interior.

## Local Note

One thing that catches property owners off guard in Boardman's older neighborhoods: the original double-hung windows on postwar ranch homes are often set in frames that have shifted over decades of frost heave. A standard plywood cut won't sit flush against a frame that's racked even a quarter inch — it leaves a gap at the corner that lets wind and rain in despite the board being in place. The crew accounts for this by scribing the cut to the actual opening rather than relying on nominal dimensions, and by using foam backer rod to seal the perimeter before the panel is fastened. It's a small detail that makes a significant difference when the next storm rolls through before the glass company can get there.
