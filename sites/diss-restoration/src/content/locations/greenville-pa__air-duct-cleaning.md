## Why Greenville Properties See Duct Issues

Greenville sits in Mercer County, where the building stock skews toward older single-family homes with decades-old HVAC infrastructure, many converted from oil or coal heat to forced-air systems at some point over the years. That conversion history matters: ductwork retrofitted into an older frame often has more joints, more elbows, and more places for dust, pet dander, and insulation fibers to collect than duct runs designed into new construction from the start. Add Pennsylvania's humid summers and you get a second problem, condensation inside supply ducts that run through unconditioned basements or crawlspaces, which gives mold and mildew a foothold long before anyone notices a smell. Winters bring the opposite issue: furnaces running hard for months, pulling accumulated dust through the system every time the thermostat kicks on.

## Our Air Duct Cleaning Process in Greenville

We start with an inspection of the full system, supply runs, return air, and the furnace or air handler itself, using a camera where access is limited, which is common in homes with finished basements or additions built over the original ductwork. From there we use negative air machines paired with rotary brush agitation to dislodge debris along the duct walls, followed by HEPA-filtered vacuum extraction so dust doesn't just get stirred up and resettled in the living space. Dryer vent cleaning gets its own pass, since older Greenville homes frequently have longer vent runs from the laundry room to the exterior wall, and lint buildup in those stretches is a real fire risk that standard duct cleaning doesn't address. For homes with visible mold growth inside the ductwork, we apply an EPA-registered sanitizing treatment after extraction rather than masking the problem with fragrance.

## Local note

A lot of Greenville's older homes were built with return air routed through stud bays or floor joist cavities rather than dedicated sheet metal duct, a shortcut common in houses from that era. Those cavities can't be cleaned the same way a standard round or rectangular duct can, so we flag them during the inspection and talk through realistic options with the homeowner rather than running a brush through a joist bay and calling it done. It's a detail that trips up cleaning crews who aren't used to the area's housing patterns.
