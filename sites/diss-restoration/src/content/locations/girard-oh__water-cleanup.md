## Why Girard Properties See Water Damage Issues

Girard's housing stock leans heavily on construction from the mid-20th century and earlier, when basements were built with poured concrete block walls rather than modern waterproofed foundations. Those block walls are porous by nature. When snowmelt or heavy spring rain saturates the clay-heavy Mahoning Valley soil, hydrostatic pressure pushes moisture through the mortar joints and into finished or semi-finished basement spaces. That seepage can go unnoticed for days behind paneling or carpet padding, giving mold a head start before anyone sees standing water.

The city's older sewer infrastructure is another factor. Combined sewer systems common in Trumbull County municipalities can surcharge during heavy storms, sending wastewater back up through floor drains. That category of water loss carries contamination concerns that plain pipe-burst water does not, and it changes how extraction and disinfection are handled.

## Our Emergency Water Cleanup Process in Girard

The first thing the crew does on arrival is document everything: moisture readings at the wall cavities, floor assembly, and subfloor using calibrated meters, plus photographs that support your insurance claim. Standing water comes out with truck-mounted extraction before any drying equipment goes in.

From there, the process follows the IICRC S500 standard for water damage restoration. Affected materials are assessed for salvageability. In Girard's older homes, that often means evaluating original hardwood floors, plaster-and-lath walls, and basement block construction, each of which behaves differently under drying conditions than modern materials. Structural drying equipment, including industrial air movers and desiccant or refrigerant dehumidifiers, is positioned based on psychrometric readings, not guesswork. Moisture logs are recorded daily until the structure reaches documented dry standard.

If the source was a sewage backup or contaminated floodwater, affected porous materials are removed rather than dried in place, and EPA-registered disinfectants are applied to all contacted surfaces before drying equipment is set.

Most homeowner policies cover sudden and accidental water losses, but the documentation has to support the claim. ## Local Note

Girard's older block-foundation basements present a drying challenge that does not show up in newer construction: the block itself holds moisture inside the hollow cores long after the surface reads dry on a pin meter. A crew that pulls equipment too early based on surface readings will leave a basement that smells musty within a week. The correct approach is to use a non-invasive sensor that reads through the block face and to keep dehumidification running until core moisture levels, not just surface levels, reach the target range. That extra step is the difference between a job that stays dry and one that generates a mold call six weeks later.
