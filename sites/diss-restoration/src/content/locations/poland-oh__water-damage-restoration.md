## Why Poland Properties See Water Damage Issues

Poland Village's roots go back to the early 1800s, and a lot of that original housing stock, along with the farmhouses and brick homes built around it over the following century, still stands today. Fieldstone and rubble foundations were common in that era, and they don't behave like poured concrete. Water finds the mortar joints first, wicks upward slowly, and can sit in a damp crawlspace for weeks before anyone notices a musty smell upstairs. Add Mahoning County's clay-heavy soil, which drains slowly after heavy rain or spring thaw, and basements near Yellow Creek's floodplain see recurring seepage even without a dramatic storm event. Newer homes further from the historic core deal with more conventional issues: failed washing machine hoses, water heater ruptures, and ice damming along rooflines during the freeze-thaw cycles that hit this part of Ohio every winter.

## Our Water Damage Restoration Process in Poland

The first visit starts with locating the source and shutting it down, whether that's a supply line, a roof penetration, or groundwater intrusion. From there we extract standing water with truck-mounted or portable extraction units depending on access, which matters on some of Poland's older lots where driveways and foundation walls weren't built with modern equipment in mind. Moisture mapping comes next: we check subfloor, framing, and in older homes, the stone or mortar itself, since fieldstone can read dry on an infrared scan while still holding moisture deep in the joints. Structural drying follows the IICRC S500 standard, with air movers and dehumidification sized to the actual structure rather than a one-size approach, and we monitor daily until materials hit a stable dry standard before closing out the job.

## Local Note

Fieldstone and rubble foundations common in Poland's older homes store moisture differently than poured concrete. A moisture meter reading on the surface can look acceptable while the mortar joints a few inches deeper are still saturated, which is why we extend drying time and recheck with penetrating moisture probes on these foundation types rather than relying on a single surface pass.
