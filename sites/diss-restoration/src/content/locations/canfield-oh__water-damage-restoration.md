## Why Canfield Properties See Water Damage Issues

Mahoning County's clay-heavy glacial till is the underlying factor most homeowners never think about until they have a wet basement. Clay doesn't drain — it holds moisture against foundation walls, and when hydrostatic pressure builds after a sustained rain or a rapid thaw, block foundations common in Canfield's mid-century residential neighborhoods can bow inward or allow seepage through mortar joints. The township's mix of older ranch-style homes and split-levels means many crawl spaces and lower levels sit at or near the water table during wet seasons.

Canfield's winters compound the problem. Freeze-thaw cycles — sometimes multiple times in a single week during January and February — stress supply lines in uninsulated exterior walls and garage utility runs. Copper and galvanized pipe that has been in service for decades is especially vulnerable. A pinhole leak behind a finished wall can go undetected for weeks, and by the time a homeowner notices a soft spot in the drywall or a musty smell, moisture has already colonized the wall cavity.

## Our Water Damage Restoration Process in Canfield

Every job starts with a full moisture mapping inspection — thermal imaging and calibrated moisture meters on walls, subfloor, and ceiling assemblies — so we know exactly where water traveled, not just where it pooled. We don't guess at drying boundaries; we document them.

From there, the process moves through five concrete stages:

1. **Water extraction** — truck-mounted and portable extractors pull standing water from hard floors, carpet, and subfloor assemblies. In Canfield's split-level homes, where lower-level family rooms often have carpet over concrete slab, we use weighted extraction heads to pull moisture that a standard wand leaves behind.
2. **Controlled demolition** — saturated drywall, insulation, and flooring that cannot be dried in place are removed to the documented wet line. We photograph every cut before and after for your insurance carrier.
3. **Structural drying** — commercial desiccant and refrigerant dehumidifiers, high-velocity air movers, and in-wall drying panels run in a closed system calibrated to the specific volume and material composition of each affected space. Drying targets follow the IICRC S500 standard.
4. **Daily monitoring** — a technician checks moisture readings each day and adjusts equipment placement until every reading reaches the dry standard. We don't pull equipment early.
5. **Clearance documentation** — final moisture readings are logged and provided to you and your adjuster before any reconstruction begins.

## Local Note

Canfield's housing stock includes a notable share of homes built in the 1950s through 1970s with plaster-over-lath wall construction rather than modern drywall. Plaster reads dry at the surface much faster than it actually is — a moisture meter on the face coat can show acceptable numbers while the lath and framing behind it remain saturated. We use deep-wall probes and thermal imaging on any plaster-wall home to confirm the actual drying front, not just the surface reading.
