## Why Farrell's Older Housing Stock Complicates Fire and Smoke Restoration

Balloon-frame construction, common in homes built before the 1950s, lacks the fire blocking that platform-frame homes built later would have. In a fire, that means smoke and heat travel vertically through wall cavities with little resistance, depositing oily, protein-based residue in spaces that aren't visible from the room where the fire started. A kitchen fire that looks contained can leave detectable odor and residue two floors up.

Plaster walls compound the challenge. Plaster is porous in a way that modern drywall is not. Smoke particles and odor-causing compounds absorb into the lime and gypsum matrix, and surface cleaning alone doesn't reach them. Restoration in these homes requires controlled demolition in affected cavities, HEPA-filtered negative air pressure, and thermal fogging or hydroxyl treatment to address odor at the molecular level rather than masking it.

Farrell's winters also matter. Cold temperatures cause soot to behave differently: oily residues become more viscous and harder to lift from surfaces, and the temperature differential between a fire-damaged interior and the outside air can drive condensation into already-compromised structural cavities. Work that begins in January requires different sequencing than the same scope in July.

## Our Fire Damage Restoration Process in Farrell

Every job begins with a structured assessment, not a visual walk-through. Technicians use thermal imaging and air sampling to map where smoke has traveled beyond the visible burn zone. In Farrell's older homes, that map often extends well past what the homeowner expects.

From there, the process moves through these phases:

**Stabilization and board-up.** Openings created by the fire or by firefighting operations are secured against weather and unauthorized entry. In winter conditions, this step protects both the structure and the contents that remain.

**Contents separation and pack-out.** Salvageable contents are inventoried, packed, and transported to a controlled environment for cleaning. Items with heavy smoke absorption are documented for the insurance claim.

**Structural cleaning and demolition.** Char, soot, and smoke-damaged materials are removed to clean substrate. In balloon-frame cavities, this often means opening wall sections to clean and treat framing that would otherwise continue off-gassing odor for months.

**Odor neutralization.** Thermal fogging, hydroxyl generation, or ozone treatment is selected based on the materials present and the scope of penetration. The method matters: ozone is effective on hard surfaces but can degrade certain materials, so selection is deliberate.

**Documentation throughout.** Photographs, moisture readings, air quality data, and scope notes are compiled in a format that supports the insurance claim from the first day on site.
