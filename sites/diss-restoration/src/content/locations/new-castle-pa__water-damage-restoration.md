## Why New Castle Properties See Water Damage Issues

Lawrence County's winters are hard on plumbing. Temperatures routinely dip below freezing for extended stretches, and the older homes throughout New Castle were often built with supply lines running through exterior walls or uninsulated crawl spaces. When those pipes freeze and burst, the water doesn't announce itself quietly. A single three-quarter-inch copper line can release hundreds of gallons before a shutoff is reached.

The soil is the other factor. Much of the Shenango Valley sits on dense clay that sheds surface water rather than absorbing it. During heavy spring rains or rapid snowmelt, that runoff finds the path of least resistance, which is often a basement wall joint or a window well. Homes built before the 1960s, which represent a large share of New Castle's residential stock, frequently have block or stone foundations without modern waterproofing membranes. Water intrusion in those structures isn't unusual; it's almost expected after a significant storm.

Sump pump failures compound the problem. When power flickers during a storm and the pump stops, a basement that was dry an hour ago can have several inches of standing water by the time the homeowner notices.

## Our Water Damage Restoration Process in New Castle

The first priority on any water loss is stopping the spread. Our crew arrives with truck-mounted extraction equipment capable of pulling standing water from basements, finished lower levels, and main-floor spaces quickly. Once bulk water is removed, the real diagnostic work begins.

Older New Castle homes often have plaster walls over wood lath, and plaster behaves differently than modern drywall. The surface can feel dry to the touch while the lath behind it holds significant moisture. We use thermal imaging cameras and calibrated moisture meters to map the actual wet boundary, not the visible one. Drying plans are built around that data, not assumptions.

Structural drying follows the IICRC S500 standard. We place commercial-grade desiccant dehumidifiers and high-velocity air movers in configurations calculated for the specific room geometry and material types. Readings are logged at each visit so drying progress is documented, which matters for insurance claims and for confirming that materials have reached acceptable moisture levels before any rebuild begins.

If building materials are saturated beyond recovery, controlled demolition removes only what cannot be saved. We work to preserve original trim, hardwood floors, and plaster where drying curves support it, because replacement materials in older homes rarely match what was there.

## Working with Insurance on New Castle Water Losses

Most homeowner policies cover sudden and accidental water discharge, which includes burst pipes, appliance failures, and certain storm-related intrusions. What they typically do not cover is long-term seepage or gradual leaks, which is why documenting the cause and timeline of a loss matters from the first hour.

DISS Restoration photographs affected areas systematically, notes moisture readings at each measurement point, and produces documentation formatted for adjuster review. We bill carriers directly and work with your adjuster through the claim process. If there is a coverage question about the cause of loss, our documentation gives the adjuster what they need to make a determination.
