## Why Warren Properties Face Elevated Asbestos Risk

Trumbull County's building boom ran roughly from the 1920s through the 1970s, tracking closely with the rise and peak of the regional steel industry. Homes built during that window — particularly the compact two-story and Cape Cod styles common throughout Warren's older residential corridors — frequently contain asbestos in places that aren't immediately visible: inside wall cavities wrapped around heating pipes, beneath multiple layers of vinyl floor tile, in the joint compound between drywall panels, and in the spray-applied texture on ceilings.

Popcorn ceiling asbestos removal is one of the most common requests in this area precisely because that finish was popular through the late 1970s and was routinely applied with asbestos-containing material before the EPA began restricting its use. Disturbing that surface — even with a scraper and a drop cloth — releases fibers that are invisible to the naked eye and can remain airborne for hours. The same risk applies to vermiculite attic insulation, which was widely used in homes of this era and has a documented association with asbestos contamination at the source.

## Our Asbestos Abatement Process in Warren

Every project begins with a thorough asbestos inspection and bulk sampling before any abatement work is planned. Samples are sent to an accredited laboratory, and results drive the scope — there's no one-size protocol because the materials involved, their condition, and their location all affect the removal method.

Once testing confirms the presence of regulated asbestos-containing material, the work area is isolated using negative air pressure containment with HEPA-filtered air scrubbers. Friable materials — those that can be crumbled by hand pressure — require full wet removal and double-bagging in labeled, sealed containers. Non-friable materials in good condition may qualify for encapsulation depending on the project scope and applicable Ohio EPA requirements. All waste is transported to a licensed disposal facility; Ohio law governs both the handling and the disposal chain, and documentation is provided at project close.

After abatement, air clearance testing confirms fiber levels have returned to acceptable thresholds before containment is removed. That final clearance is not optional — it's the step that protects both the occupants and any contractors coming in behind us.

Older homes in Warren that were built on slab or have partially finished basements sometimes have floor tile running continuously under partition walls added in later decades.
