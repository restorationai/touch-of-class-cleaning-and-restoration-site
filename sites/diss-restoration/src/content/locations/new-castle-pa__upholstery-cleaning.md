## Why New Castle Homes See Upholstery Wear and Odor Buildup

A lot of New Castle's housing stock dates back to the city's steel and tin-plate era, and that means smaller rooms, less airflow, and furniture that's often been passed down rather than replaced. Older homes near the river also run more humid in summer, and without strong central air circulation, that moisture works into cushions and fabric backing over time. Pet hair, cooking odors, and wood smoke from older heating systems settle into fibers the same way, and once a fabric sofa or loveseat has absorbed that combination, surface vacuuming or a home remedy usually just masks it for a week or two.

## Our Upholstery Cleaning Process in New Castle

We start by identifying the fabric or leather type, since a cotton-blend sofa, a microfiber sectional, and a leather recliner all need different chemistry and pressure. We run a colorfastness test on an inconspicuous spot before anything else touches the fabric. From there we use hot water extraction for most upholstered fabrics, pulling embedded soil and moisture out rather than pushing more water in, which matters in a humid climate where over-wetting can slow drying and invite odor to come back. Leather pieces get cleaned and conditioned rather than steamed, since heat and excess moisture can crack or dull leather finishes over time. We finish with targeted drying, sometimes with air movers, because in a valley climate like New Castle's, ambient humidity alone won't dry a cushion core fast enough to prevent that trapped, damp smell from returning.

We still see a fair number of horsehair-stuffed and cotton-batting sofas in New Castle, especially pieces that came down through a steelworker family a generation or two back.
