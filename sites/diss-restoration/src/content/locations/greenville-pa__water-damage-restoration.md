## Our Water Damage Restoration Process in Greenville

The first priority on any water loss is stopping ongoing intrusion and identifying hidden moisture. Technicians use thermal imaging cameras and calibrated moisture meters to map saturation behind walls and under flooring before a single piece of material is removed. In Greenville's older plaster-and-lath construction, this step is especially important because moisture readings at the plaster face often understate how wet the structural cavity behind it actually is.

Once the scope is mapped, the process moves through extraction, controlled demolition of unsalvageable material, and then structural drying using industrial air movers and refrigerant or desiccant dehumidifiers sized to the affected area. Drying is documented daily with moisture readings logged against the IICRC S500 standard so there is a clear, insurer-ready record of progress from wet to dry. Nothing is closed up until readings confirm the structure has returned to baseline moisture levels for the region.
