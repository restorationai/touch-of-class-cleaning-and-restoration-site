Girard sits in Trumbull County's industrial belt, where decades of steel-era construction left behind a housing stock that skews older and denser than most of northeast Ohio. When a renovation or new build wraps up here — whether it's a gut rehab on a 1940s double or a commercial tenant improvement off State Route 422 — the post-construction cleanup is rarely straightforward. Drywall dust settles into original hardwood gaps, silica from masonry work coats vintage radiators, and construction adhesive residue clings to surfaces that weren't designed for modern materials. DISS Restoration handles that final clean with the same systematic approach we bring to every restoration job: documented, thorough, and calibrated to what the building actually is.

## Why Girard Properties Require a Different Approach to Post-Construction Cleaning

Girard's older residential and commercial buildings present conditions that generic cleaning crews aren't equipped for. Homes built before 1978 frequently contain lead-based paint on trim and window casings — surfaces that get disturbed during renovation and leave fine particulate behind. Because DISS Restoration is EPA Lead-Safe Certified, we follow proper containment and disposal protocols when lead-containing dust is a factor, which matters in a city where a significant portion of the housing stock predates modern paint regulations.

Beyond lead, older Girard construction often means plaster walls, cast-iron plumbing, and original millwork — materials that require softer cleaning methods than the aggressive scrubbing appropriate for new builds. Abrasive techniques that work fine on fresh drywall can etch plaster or strip patina from original woodwork. We adjust our methods room by room based on what we're actually looking at, not a one-size checklist.

There's also the matter of Trumbull County's climate. Northeast Ohio winters are wet and cold, and construction projects that run through late fall or winter leave job sites with elevated moisture levels from concrete curing, grout work, and exterior air infiltration. Before a final clean can be considered complete, residual moisture in subfloors and wall cavities needs to be assessed — otherwise you're handing a property back to the owner with hidden conditions that could become a mold problem within weeks.

## Our Post-Construction and Specialty Cleaning Process in Girard

Post-construction cleaning has a sequence, and skipping steps creates callbacks. Here's how we work through a Girard job:

**Rough clean first.** Before any detail work, bulk debris — drywall scraps, fasteners, packaging, excess caulk — gets removed. This phase protects finished surfaces from being scratched by debris dragged across them during later cleaning.

**HEPA vacuuming of all surfaces.** Construction dust is fine enough to stay airborne for hours and re-settle after a standard vacuum pass. We use HEPA-filter equipment on walls, ceilings, light fixtures, ductwork registers, and any exposed HVAC components before wiping begins. In Girard's older homes with gravity-fed duct systems or original floor registers, this step is especially important — those systems circulate whatever is sitting in them.

**Surface-by-surface detail clean.** Windows, sills, cabinetry interiors, countertops, tile grout lines, and hardware all get individual attention. Specialty surfaces — natural stone, original hardwood, period tile — get appropriate products, not whatever is on the truck by default.

**Moisture check.** Particularly in winter and early spring builds, we assess subfloor and wall cavity moisture with calibrated meters before signing off. If readings are elevated, we document them and discuss next steps with the property owner or general contractor.

**Final walkthrough and documentation.** We photograph completed areas and note any pre-existing conditions we observed during cleaning — damage that predates our work, surfaces the GC should address, or areas that may need a second pass after punch-list trades return.
