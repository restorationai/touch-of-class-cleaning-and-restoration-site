## Why Boardman Properties See Sewage Backup Issues

Much of Boardman's residential stock was built between the 1940s and 1970s, a period when cast-iron and clay-tile lateral lines were standard. Those materials have a finite service life. Clay tile in particular is vulnerable to root intrusion — tree roots follow moisture gradients and can crack or completely block a lateral over the course of a decade without any visible warning at the surface. When the line finally fails, the backup often happens during or after a heavy rain event, because storm infiltration raises the load on the combined or sanitary system faster than the line can pass it.

Mahoning County's soil profile also plays a role. Heavy clay soils drain slowly, which means groundwater stays elevated longer after significant rainfall. That elevated water table can create hydrostatic pressure against basement walls and floor drains, pushing water — and whatever is in the sewer system — back toward the lowest fixture in the house. Finished basements in Boardman's established neighborhoods are especially vulnerable because the drain invert is often only a foot or two above the main sewer elevation.

## Our Sewage Cleanup and Sanitization Process in Boardman

Sewage is classified as Category 3 water — the most contaminated class — and the process reflects that. The moment a crew arrives, the priority is containment: isolating the affected zone to prevent cross-contamination to living areas, then removing all standing sewage using truck-mounted extraction equipment. Porous materials that have absorbed Category 3 water — drywall, carpet, insulation, wood subfloor — are documented, photographed, and removed. There is no drying porous material that has been saturated with raw sewage; the contamination is not reversible with airflow.

Once the space is cleared to hard surfaces, the sanitization phase begins. Technicians apply EPA-registered antimicrobial agents to all affected surfaces, including concrete floors, block walls, and any structural framing that remains. Dwell time matters here — the product has to sit long enough to achieve the kill claims on the label. After sanitization, the space is dried to IICRC S500 standards using commercial dehumidifiers and air movers. A final post-remediation check confirms moisture readings are within acceptable range before any reconstruction begins.

For homes with septic systems rather than municipal sewer connections, the process adds a step: confirming the tank and field are not the source of the backup before closing the job. A septic overflow that isn't addressed at the source will recur.

Homeowner policies vary significantly on sewer backup coverage. Many standard policies exclude it by default, and coverage often requires a specific sewer backup rider that not every Boardman homeowner knows they have — or don't have. DISS Restoration documents the loss thoroughly from the moment the crew arrives: photographs of affected materials, moisture readings, scope of removal, and a written summary of conditions found.
