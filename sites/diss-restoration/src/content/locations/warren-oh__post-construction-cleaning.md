## Why Warren Properties Present Specific Post-Construction Cleaning Challenges

Warren's housing stock skews older. Much of the residential construction in the city dates to the mid-20th century or earlier, and renovation projects in these homes regularly uncover layered materials — original plaster over lath, multiple generations of flooring, galvanized pipe runs, and occasionally lead-based paint on trim or window sills. That last point matters: any renovation disturbing pre-1978 painted surfaces creates a cleanup situation that goes beyond aesthetics. DISS Restoration holds EPA Lead-Safe Certification, which means post-construction cleaning on older Warren properties is handled with the containment and disposal protocols that work is required to follow.

Beyond the building stock, Trumbull County's climate adds its own complications. Winter construction projects — common here because contractors schedule interior work through the cold months — leave sites with elevated humidity from concrete curing, fresh drywall mud drying, and inadequate temporary heat. That moisture, if not addressed before final cleaning, can leave a film on surfaces that looks like dust but behaves differently under a mop. Identifying and resolving that moisture condition before the final clean is part of what separates a proper post-construction clean from a cosmetic pass.

## Our Post-Construction and Specialty Cleaning Process in Warren

Post-construction cleaning happens in phases, and skipping a phase creates rework. The sequence matters.

The first pass — rough clean — removes bulk debris: scrap material, packaging, tape, overspray, and construction adhesive. This is physical work, not cleaning in the traditional sense, and it has to happen before any surface cleaning begins.

The second phase is detail cleaning. Windowsills, door frames, cabinet interiors, light fixtures, outlet covers, and baseboards all collect fine particulate that won't move with a standard cloth. We use HEPA-filtered vacuums to pull dust from surfaces before wiping, so we're not just redistributing it. HVAC registers and returns get vacuumed and wiped — construction dust in ductwork will circulate through the building for months if it's not cleared at this stage.

The third phase is the final clean: floors, glass, fixtures, and any specialty surfaces (polished concrete, tile grout, stainless appliances) that require specific products and methods. For commercial buildouts in Warren, this phase often includes coordination with the general contractor for a walkthrough before turnover.

Specialty cleaning — pressure washing of exterior concrete, post-fire odor treatment, deep cleaning of HVAC systems after renovation, or cleaning following a hoarding situation — follows its own protocol depending on the scope.
