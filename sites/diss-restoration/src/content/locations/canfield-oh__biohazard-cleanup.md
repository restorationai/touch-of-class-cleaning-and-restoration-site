## Why Canfield Properties Present Unique Considerations for Biohazard Cleanup

Canfield's housing stock leans heavily toward mid-century construction — ranch homes and split-levels built through the 1950s, 60s, and 70s that feature hardwood subfloors, plaster-and-lath wall assemblies, and older HVAC configurations. These materials behave differently than modern construction when biological contamination occurs. Hardwood subfloor planks can wick and hold fluids well below the surface, and plaster walls absorb moisture and organic material in ways that standard visual inspection won't reveal. Thorough remediation here means going beyond the visible surface — it means testing, treating, and sometimes removing materials that look intact but are not.

Mahoning County's humid continental climate also plays a role. Warm, humid summers accelerate the growth of secondary biological hazards, including mold and bacteria, in the hours and days following an incident. In a sealed room during July or August, conditions can shift quickly. Prompt response and proper containment are not optional — they are what separates a contained remediation from a compounding problem.
