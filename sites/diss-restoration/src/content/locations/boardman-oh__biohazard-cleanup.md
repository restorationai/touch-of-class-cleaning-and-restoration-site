## Why Boardman Properties Present Unique Biohazard Considerations

Much of Boardman's residential housing stock dates from the 1950s through the 1970s — an era of construction that relied heavily on plaster-and-lath walls, older HVAC ductwork, and porous concrete block foundations. These materials matter in biohazard remediation because porous surfaces absorb biological fluids and odor compounds far more readily than modern drywall or sealed concrete. What appears contained at the surface can have migrated into wall cavities or subfloor assemblies, requiring a more methodical assessment before any cleaning begins.

Boardman's climate adds another layer. Mahoning Valley winters push moisture into structures through freeze-thaw cycling, and humid summers keep indoor relative humidity elevated — conditions that accelerate microbial activity in any affected area. A thorough remediation accounts for ambient moisture, not just visible contamination.

## Our Biohazard Cleanup Process in Boardman

Every situation is different, but the process follows a consistent, professionally structured sequence.

**Assessment and containment** come first. The affected area is evaluated to establish the scope of contamination, and physical barriers are set to prevent cross-contamination to unaffected spaces — important in the attached-garage ranch homes and multi-unit buildings common throughout Boardman.

**Removal of affected materials** follows. Porous materials that cannot be adequately decontaminated — flooring, insulation, sections of drywall or plaster — are carefully removed, bagged, and transported for proper disposal in compliance with Ohio EPA regulations governing biohazardous waste. Nothing is left to chance on the disposal side.

**Cleaning, disinfection, and deodorization** address all surfaces that remain. Hospital-grade EPA-registered disinfectants are applied, and odor neutralization goes beyond masking — enzymatic and thermal fogging treatments break down odor-causing compounds at the molecular level, which matters especially in older Boardman homes where plaster and wood framing can hold odors long after visible contamination is gone.

**Clearance verification** closes the job. The space is documented and confirmed safe before the crew leaves.

The team is IICRC certified, EPA Lead-Safe certified, and OSHA trained — relevant in Boardman's older housing stock where lead-based paint is a realistic co-concern during any remediation that involves disturbing wall or floor surfaces.
