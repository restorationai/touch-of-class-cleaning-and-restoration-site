## Why Hubbard Properties See Water Damage Issues

Trumbull County's clay-heavy soil drains poorly, which means groundwater pressure against basement walls builds quickly during spring thaw and heavy rain events. Hubbard's housing stock leans heavily on mid-century construction, and homes built in the 1950s through 1970s frequently have original cast-iron drain lines that corrode from the inside out, failing without obvious warning. Sump basins in those older homes were often undersized for the loads modern storm events create. When the sump can't keep pace, water moves across the floor slab and wicks into block foundation walls before anyone notices the pump is struggling. That combination of soil pressure, aging infrastructure, and below-grade living space makes basement flooding one of the most common calls in this part of Ohio.

## Our Emergency Water Cleanup Process in Hubbard

When a crew arrives, the first step is locating the source and stopping active water entry if it's still flowing. From there, the work moves in a specific order because skipping steps creates bigger problems later.

- **Standing water extraction**: Truck-mounted or portable extraction units pull bulk water from floors, carpet, and low-lying cavities. Getting water out fast limits how far it migrates into subfloor material.
- **Moisture mapping**: Thermal imaging and calibrated moisture meters map the full extent of saturation. Water behind plaster or inside block walls can read dry at the surface while the substrate stays wet, so readings guide placement of every piece of drying equipment.
- **Structural drying**: Industrial air movers and refrigerant or desiccant dehumidifiers run in a calculated configuration based on the IICRC S500 standard. Drying logs are recorded daily so progress is documented for your insurance carrier.
- **Material assessment**: Saturated drywall, insulation, and flooring that cannot be dried in place are removed to prevent mold colonization. Mold can begin to establish within 24 to 48 hours of a water event, which is why the timeline matters.
- **Clearance readings**: Drying is not complete until moisture levels return to baseline for the material type. The crew does not pull equipment early.

## Local Note

Hubbard's older homes frequently have plaster-and-lath wall construction rather than modern drywall, and that distinction changes the drying timeline in a meaningful way. Plaster is dense and holds moisture longer than gypsum board, and the wood lath behind it can stay saturated even after the plaster face feels dry to the touch. In those homes, drying equipment typically needs to run longer, and readings need to be taken deeper into the wall assembly before the structure is cleared. Knowing this going in prevents a situation where equipment is removed too early and moisture left in the lath creates a secondary mold problem weeks later.
