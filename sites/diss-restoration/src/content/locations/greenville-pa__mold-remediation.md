## Why Greenville Properties See Mold Issues

Mercer County's climate delivers a long shoulder season of freeze-thaw cycles that stress masonry and mortar joints. Water that seeps through a cracked foundation wall in late February can stay trapped behind plaster long after the surface looks dry. Greenville's housing stock leans heavily toward pre-1960 construction, and those homes were built before modern building codes required continuous vapor barriers or pressure-treated sill plates. That combination, damp soil, aging masonry, and interior materials that absorb and hold moisture, creates conditions where mold can establish itself quietly in crawl spaces, rim joists, and wall cavities without triggering an obvious visual cue until the colony is well-established.

Basements finished with wood framing directly against concrete block walls are especially common in this part of western Pennsylvania, and that assembly is one of the most reliable environments for hidden mold growth. A musty smell that gets stronger in humid weather is often the first real signal that something is active behind the wall.

## Our Mold Remediation Process in Greenville

Every job starts with a thorough inspection using moisture meters and thermal imaging to map where water has traveled, not just where mold is visible. In older Greenville homes, that moisture map often tells a different story than the surface does: plaster can read dry at the touch while the wood lath behind it remains saturated and actively feeding a colony.

Once the scope is confirmed, the affected area is isolated with physical containment barriers and negative air pressure to prevent spores from migrating to clean parts of the home during removal. Contaminated porous materials, drywall, insulation, wood framing where necessary, are removed and bagged per EPA guidelines. Remaining structural surfaces are treated and dried to IICRC S520 standard before any rebuilding begins. Throughout the process, air scrubbers with HEPA filtration run continuously to capture airborne spores. The job is not considered complete until post-remediation clearance testing confirms spore counts are within acceptable limits.

## Local Note

Greenville's older homes frequently have plaster-and-lath wall systems that behave very differently from modern drywall when mold is present. Because plaster is denser and more alkaline, surface mold sometimes does not penetrate as deeply into the plaster itself, but the wood lath behind it is a different story. Lath is untreated dimensional lumber that absorbs moisture readily, and once mold colonizes it, the lath often cannot be saved. Homeowners who see a small patch of mold on a plaster wall sometimes assume the problem is superficial.
