## Why Wheatland Properties See Upholstery Issues

A lot of Wheatland's housing stock dates back to the borough's steel-industry years, and that era favored heavy wool blends, mohair, and early synthetic velvets that behave very differently under water than the polyester microfiber common in newer furniture. Add in the humidity that settles along the Shenango River valley, and upholstered furniture in basements, sunporches, and enclosed rear additions tends to hold moisture and odor longer than pieces in drier, more ventilated rooms. Forced-air heating in older homes also dries leather unevenly over a winter, which is why cracking and discoloration often show up worse on the side of a chair nearest a vent or radiator. None of this means the furniture is ruined, it just means the cleaning approach has to account for what the piece is actually made of and where it's been sitting.

## Our Upholstery Cleaning Process in Wheatland

We start by identifying the fiber content and checking for colorfastness before any solution touches the piece, which matters more with the older wool and mohair blends still common in Wheatland's mid-century homes. ## Equipment & Methods We Use for Upholstery

Fabric upholstery cleaning runs through pH-balanced, fabric-specific solutions and hot water extraction equipment that pulls soil and moisture out rather than pushing it deeper into the cushion fill. Leather gets a separate cleaning and conditioning process built to match grain type, since bonded and full-grain leather respond very differently to the same product. We bring moisture meters to every job so drying decisions are based on what the cushion actually reads, not a guess.
