## Why Hermitage Furniture Sees More Wear Than You'd Expect

Hermitage sits in a part of northwest Pennsylvania that gets real humidity swings, muggy in July and August, then dry heat from furnaces running most of the winter. That cycle is hard on upholstery fibers and leather finishes alike: fabric absorbs moisture and holds onto odor-causing bacteria during the summer, then the same pieces dry out and crack under forced-air heat come January. Add in the area's hard water, which leaves mineral deposits behind on any upholstery that's been spot-cleaned with tap water, and you get the dull, slightly gray look that shows up on lighter fabrics after a few seasons even in well-kept homes.

## Our Upholstery Cleaning Process in Hermitage

We start every job with a fabric and fiber test, because a microfiber sectional and a wool-blend armchair don't respond the same way to heat or moisture, and getting that wrong is how shrinkage and water rings happen. From there we vacuum with an upholstery attachment to lift loose soil, pre-treat stains and traffic patterns, then use hot water extraction for most fabric pieces or a pH-balanced cleaner and conditioner for leather. Drying time in Hermitage runs a little longer during peak summer humidity, so we set fans and, when needed, a dehumidifier to keep dry times reasonable and prevent the mildew smell that can set in if a piece stays damp too long.
