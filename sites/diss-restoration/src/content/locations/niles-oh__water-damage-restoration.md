## Why Niles Properties See Water Damage Issues

The Mahoning Valley's geology works against homeowners here. The clay-rich soil surrounding Niles compacts and expands seasonally, shifting foundations and cracking the parging that keeps basement walls relatively dry. During spring snowmelt and heavy summer thunderstorms — both common in Trumbull County — that same clay becomes nearly impermeable, directing surface water straight toward foundation walls rather than letting it percolate away.

The housing stock compounds the risk. A large share of Niles homes were built between the 1920s and 1950s, when basements were finished with cinder block rather than poured concrete, and interior drain tile systems were either minimal or nonexistent. Older cast-iron and galvanized supply lines in these homes have reached or exceeded their service life, making supply-side failures — burst pipes, failed shutoff valves, corroded fittings — a recurring source of interior water damage, especially during the hard freezes that hit the Mahoning Valley each January and February.

## Our Water Damage Restoration Process in Niles

Every job starts with a thorough moisture inspection using thermal imaging and calibrated moisture meters. In Niles's older homes, surface readings can be deceptive — a plaster wall may feel firm and read nearly dry at the face while the wood lath behind it holds significant moisture. We map the full moisture boundary before any equipment is placed, so drying targets are based on actual conditions rather than visible water lines.

Extraction comes next. Standing water is removed with truck-mounted extraction units, and residual moisture in subfloor assemblies — common in homes with original hardwood over board sheathing — is addressed with targeted drying equipment before flooring decisions are made. We then establish a drying system using commercial-grade air movers and desiccant or refrigerant dehumidifiers, calibrated to the specific materials and ambient conditions. Readings are logged at each visit so the drying curve is documented for your insurance file. Structural drying follows the IICRC S500 standard, and our technicians hold the IICRC ASD (Applied Structural Drying) credential.

## Local Note

Niles homes built in the 1930s and 1940s frequently used plaster over wood lath rather than drywall, and that construction detail changes the drying timeline in a meaningful way. Plaster itself is relatively moisture-resistant, but the wood lath behind it absorbs water and releases it slowly — sometimes taking two to three times as long to reach drying goals as modern drywall under the same conditions. When we encounter this wall assembly, we adjust the drying plan accordingly and communicate the extended timeline to both the homeowner and the adjuster upfront, rather than discovering it mid-job when the readings aren't moving as expected.
