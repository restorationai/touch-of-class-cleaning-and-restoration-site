## Why Sharon Properties See Water Damage Issues

Sharon's housing stock tells the story of a steel and manufacturing town that grew quickly in the early twentieth century and slowed after mid-century. A large share of the residential properties in the city were built before 1960, which means galvanized steel supply lines, cast iron drain stacks, and original plaster-and-lath wall assemblies. Galvanized pipe corrodes from the inside out, narrowing over decades until a section finally gives way, often inside a wall cavity where the leak runs for hours before anyone notices the ceiling stain.

The Shenango River valley geography compounds the problem. Sharon's lower-lying streets drain slowly after heavy rain, and basement floor drains can reverse when the municipal system is overwhelmed. Mercer County's freeze-thaw cycle, with temperatures swinging across the freezing mark repeatedly through winter and early spring, puts stress on any pipe that runs through an unheated garage, crawl space, or exterior wall. That combination of aging infrastructure and climate pressure means water intrusion here tends to be more severe than the original source would suggest.

## Our Water Damage Restoration Process in Sharon

The first thing a crew does on arrival is locate the source and stop the water if it hasn't been stopped already. From there, the work follows a documented sequence calibrated to what the structure actually contains, not a generic checklist.

Moisture mapping comes before any equipment is placed. Thermal imaging and calibrated moisture meters identify where water has traveled behind wall surfaces and under flooring, because in a plaster-wall home the surface can read nearly dry while the wood lath behind it holds significant moisture. Skipping this step leads to incomplete drying and mold growth inside wall cavities weeks later.

Once the affected area is mapped, water extraction removes standing and absorbed water using truck-mounted and portable extraction units. Structural drying follows with industrial air movers and refrigerant or desiccant dehumidifiers sized to the cubic footage of the space. Readings are logged daily against the IICRC S500 drying standard so there is a documented record for your insurance carrier and for your own peace of mind. Affected materials that cannot be dried in place, such as saturated insulation or buckled subfloor sections, are removed and documented before drying begins.

## Sharon Insurance Coordination

Most standard homeowner policies cover sudden and accidental water discharge, which includes burst pipes, appliance failures, and certain storm-related intrusion. What they typically do not cover is gradual leakage, meaning a slow drip behind a wall that went unnoticed for months. The distinction matters when a claim is filed, and the documentation we produce during the drying process, moisture logs, photographs, and a written scope of damage, gives your adjuster what they need to process the claim accurately.

We communicate directly with insurance carriers and can work with the adjuster's timeline for inspection before materials are removed when the structure is stable enough to wait. If the situation is urgent and materials must come out immediately to prevent further damage, we document everything before demolition.

## Local Note

Sharon's older brick and block construction, common in commercial properties and mixed-use buildings along State Street and the downtown corridor, behaves differently from wood-frame residential during a water event. Masonry absorbs and holds moisture at a much slower release rate than wood or drywall, which means drying timelines in those buildings run longer and require monitoring over more days. Crews working in that building stock need to account for moisture migrating through mortar joints into adjacent spaces, a detail that matters both for drying completeness and for accurate scope documentation on the insurance claim.
