## Why Canfield Properties Face Particular Post-Construction Cleanup Challenges

Mahoning County's climate runs the full range — humid summers that keep airborne construction dust suspended longer than drier climates, and winters that push contractors to seal buildings fast, trapping particulate inside before ventilation is fully operational. In Canfield specifically, a meaningful portion of the housing stock dates to mid-century construction, meaning renovations often uncover original plaster, older subflooring, and cast-iron or galvanized plumbing — materials that generate finer, harder-to-capture debris than modern drywall and PVC. Newer builds in Canfield's growing residential corridors present a different challenge. ## Our Post-Construction and Specialty Cleaning Process in Canfield

The sequence matters as much as the effort. Starting with floors before cleaning ceilings just redistributes debris — so the process runs top-down, every time.

**Phase 1 — Rough debris removal:** Construction material scraps, packaging, and bulk waste are cleared before any surface cleaning begins. This is especially important on projects where multiple trades have been through the space.

**Phase 2 — HEPA vacuuming of all surfaces:** Walls, window sills, door frames, cabinet interiors, light fixtures, and HVAC registers are vacuumed with HEPA-filtered equipment before any wet cleaning. This captures the fine silica and drywall particulate that a standard vacuum recirculates into the air.

**Phase 3 — Detail surface cleaning:** Windows (interior and exterior where accessible), hard flooring, countertops, and fixtures are cleaned with appropriate agents for each material — no single-solution approach that risks damaging new finishes.

**Phase 4 — Final inspection pass:** A walk-through under direct lighting catches adhesive residue on glass, grout haze on tile, and paint overspray on hardware — the details that show up the moment a client steps inside.

For specialty cleaning requests — post-fire odor neutralization on a renovated space, deep cleaning after a hoarding situation, or sanitizing a commercial kitchen after a remodel — the process is scoped individually based on what the space has been through.

## Local Note: Canfield's Mid-Century Homes and Renovation Debris

Homes in Canfield's established residential areas that were built between roughly 1950 and 1975 often have original plaster-and-lath wall systems beneath later drywall additions. When a renovation opens those walls, the debris profile changes: plaster dust is denser and more alkaline than standard drywall dust, it settles differently, and it can be abrasive to finished flooring if dragged across the surface during cleanup. Crews working in these homes adjust their approach — more frequent HEPA filter changes, careful sequencing around original hardwood floors that may have been exposed during the renovation, and attention to plaster residue in corners and along baseboards where it accumulates and is easy to miss.
