## Why Youngstown Properties See More Sewage Backup Problems

Much of Youngstown was built during the steel boom of the early 1900s, and the combined sewer systems that served those neighborhoods were designed for a city with a much larger population drawing on them daily. As the city's population declined over decades, those same lines — some original clay tile — now carry less daily flow, which means grease, sediment, and root intrusion accumulate faster. Heavy rain events compound the problem: Youngstown's combined sewer system handles both stormwater and sanitary waste on the same lines, so a significant rainfall can push raw sewage backward through floor drains and basement toilets across entire blocks at once.

Homes in the Wick Park and Idora Park neighborhoods are particularly representative of this pattern. The large older homes in these areas often have original cast-iron drain stacks and clay lateral lines running to the street. Those laterals are prime candidates for root infiltration from the mature tree canopy that defines both neighborhoods. When a lateral collapses or blocks, the backup doesn't stop at the cleanout — it fills the lowest fixture in the house first, which is almost always an unfinished basement.

## Our Sewage Cleanup and Sanitization Process in Youngstown

Sewage cleanup is not a single step — it's a sequenced process, and cutting corners at any stage leaves biological hazards behind.

**Containment and safety assessment** come first. Raw sewage is classified as Category 3 water — the most contaminated classification under the IICRC S500 standard — and any porous material it has contacted for more than 24 hours is typically non-restorable. Our IICRC-certified technicians assess what can be dried and what must be removed before any equipment goes in.

**Extraction and removal** follows. Standing sewage is extracted using truck-mounted and portable units. Saturated materials — drywall, insulation, carpet, subfloor sections — are removed and bagged for disposal. In Youngstown's older homes, this often means dealing with subfloor assemblies that include original tongue-and-groove boards over floor joists rather than modern OSB, which holds moisture differently and requires careful evaluation before a drying decision is made.

**Sanitization and antimicrobial treatment** address what extraction cannot. EPA-registered disinfectants are applied to all affected structural surfaces, and the space is treated to eliminate the bacteria and viruses associated with raw sewage exposure. This is not optional — it's the step that determines whether the space is actually safe to reoccupy.

**Structural drying** closes the job. Our IICRC ASD-certified technicians place desiccant or refrigerant dehumidifiers and air movers calibrated to the specific conditions of the space, and moisture readings are logged until materials reach accepted drying goals.
