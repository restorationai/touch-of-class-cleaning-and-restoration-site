## Why Hubbard's Building Stock Shapes Every Renovation

Hubbard sits in Trumbull County, where a significant portion of the residential inventory was built between the 1940s and the 1970s. That era of construction carries specific implications for any remodel. Knob-and-tube wiring and early aluminum branch circuits appear in homes that look perfectly modern from the street. Cast iron drain lines and galvanized supply pipes are common behind walls that have already been drywalled over once or twice. Lead paint is a documented concern in pre-1978 construction throughout this region — and because DISS Restoration holds EPA Lead-Safe Certification, we handle disturbing those surfaces the right way rather than treating it as an afterthought.

Ohio's climate adds another layer. Trumbull County winters are hard on foundations, sills, and exterior envelope assemblies. Freeze-thaw cycling works mortar joints loose, drives moisture into masonry, and can heave concrete flatwork in ways that affect interior finishes. A kitchen remodel that seems purely cosmetic sometimes reveals a rim joist that has been holding moisture for years. A bathroom remodel can expose subfloor damage that started at a window sill long before the tile showed any sign of failure. Scoping a renovation in Hubbard means accounting for what the climate has been doing quietly.

## Local Note

One thing that comes up repeatedly in Hubbard remodels involving older homes: the original exterior walls were often built with minimal or no cavity insulation, and the interior plaster was applied directly over wood lath. When a bathroom or kitchen remodel opens those walls, the temptation is to insulate quickly and close back up. But plaster-over-lath walls hold residual moisture differently than modern drywall assemblies — especially on north-facing exterior walls that don't dry out as efficiently through Trumbull County winters. Taking a moisture reading before insulating and closing is a small step that prevents a much larger problem two or three years later.
