**Thinking about removing that popcorn ceiling in your Campbell home?** Stop before you start scraping. Campbell's housing stock dates largely to the early and mid-1900s, built up around the old Campbell Works steel operation on the Mahoning River, and homes from that era routinely carry asbestos in ceiling texture, floor tile, pipe wrap, and old furnace insulation. Testing first costs far less than a remediation project scoped around a guess.

## Why Campbell Homes See Asbestos Issues

Campbell grew as a mill worker's town, and the housing that went up to support that workforce, modest bungalows, duplexes, and a handful of larger frame homes, was built during the decades when asbestos was standard in construction materials. Nine-by-nine vinyl floor tile and its black mastic adhesive, pipe insulation wrapped around steam and hot water lines in basements, and the troweled-on or sprayed ceiling texture that came later in renovations from the 1960s and 70s all show up consistently in this housing stock. Attic insulation in some of these homes may also be vermiculite, a material that in some sourcing carries asbestos contamination. None of this is visible to the eye; it has to be sampled and tested in a lab before anyone touches it.

## Our Asbestos Abatement Process in Campbell

We start with inspection and sampling, not demolition. A technician collects small material samples from suspect areas, ceiling texture, tile and mastic, pipe lagging, and sends them to an accredited lab for analysis. If results come back positive, we isolate the work area with poly sheeting and negative air pressure, set up a decontamination chamber, and remove material using wet methods to keep fibers from becoming airborne. Debris goes into sealed, labeled containers for transport to a licensed disposal facility. For older Campbell homes with layered renovations, we frequently find more than one asbestos-containing material in the same project, which is part of why full testing matters more than spot-checking the obvious ceiling or tile.

## Local Note

A lot of Campbell's older homes were renovated during the 1960s and 70s boom in popcorn ceilings, which means the texture coat is often a different age, and a different material composition, than the plaster or lath underneath it. We've found that testing both the surface texture and the substrate separately gives a cleaner picture than a single sample, because contractors in that era sometimes skim-coated directly over older asbestos-containing plaster rather than removing it first. Skipping that second sample is how a homeowner ends up with a project that reopens mid-renovation.

## Campbell Insurance & HOA Coordination

Asbestos abatement is typically a renovation or pre-sale cost rather than an insurance claim, since most homeowner policies don't cover material removal absent a covered peril like fire or water damage that exposed the material. When abatement is tied to a fire or water loss, we document the material testing and removal as part of that claim and coordinate with the adjuster directly. Campbell doesn't have the kind of dense HOA development seen in newer subdivisions elsewhere in the county, so most of our coordination here is with homeowners directly or with real estate agents managing a pre-sale inspection timeline.
