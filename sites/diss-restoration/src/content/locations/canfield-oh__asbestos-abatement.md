## Why Canfield Properties See Asbestos Risks

Mahoning County's residential construction history closely mirrors the broader Rust Belt pattern: rapid suburban expansion after World War II, followed by a long plateau. Homes built during that window routinely incorporated asbestos in floor tile adhesive (mastic), vermiculite attic insulation, pipe wrap on older forced-air and steam systems, roofing felt, and the textured spray-applied ceiling finishes that became popular in the 1960s and 1970s. What makes Canfield's housing stock particularly worth noting is the prevalence of split-level and ranch-style homes from that era — construction styles that often feature original mechanical rooms, unfinished basement utility areas, and ductwork that was insulated at the factory with materials we now know to be hazardous.

Beyond the homes themselves, older commercial and light-industrial buildings in the area carry similar risk profiles. Any renovation, addition, or demolition project that disturbs these materials without prior testing can release airborne asbestos fibers — which is why Ohio requires licensed abatement contractors and proper disposal at approved facilities before renovation work proceeds on suspect materials.

## Our Asbestos Abatement Process in Canfield

Every project begins with a thorough inspection and, where suspect materials are identified, bulk sampling sent to an accredited laboratory. Results typically return within a few business days, and we walk you through what the findings mean in plain language before any abatement work is scheduled.

Once abatement is authorized, the process follows a strict sequence:

- **Containment setup**: Affected work areas are isolated with polyethylene sheeting and negative-air pressure units to prevent fiber migration into living spaces.
- **Wet removal**: Asbestos-containing materials are wetted before removal to suppress airborne fiber release — a standard required under EPA NESHAP regulations.
- **Sealed disposal**: Removed materials are double-bagged in labeled, approved containers and transported to a licensed disposal facility. Ohio EPA rules govern both the transport and the disposal site.
- **Air clearance**: After removal, air sampling confirms fiber counts are within acceptable limits before containment is taken down and the space is returned to you.

Our team holds EPA Lead-Safe certification and is OSHA trained, and we follow IICRC standards across all remediation work. Documentation of the full process — sampling results, waste manifests, air clearance — is provided to you and can be submitted to your insurance carrier or a prospective buyer's inspector.

## Canfield Insurance Coordination

Asbestos abatement is sometimes covered under homeowner's policies when the exposure is tied to a covered loss — a burst pipe that requires tearing into an older wall, for example, or fire damage that disturbs insulation containing asbestos. Whether coverage applies depends on the specific policy language and the circumstances of the loss. We document the scope of work thoroughly, photograph affected materials before and after removal, and can communicate directly with your adjuster to support the claim. We do not bill insurance directly for asbestos abatement in every case, but we provide the documentation carriers need to evaluate coverage.

## Local Note

One pattern worth knowing if you own a Canfield-area home built in the 1950s or 1960s: the original floor tile in basements and utility rooms from that period was almost universally 9-inch vinyl asbestos tile (VAT), and it was frequently installed over a layer of black mastic adhesive that also contained asbestos. The tile itself may look intact and unremarkable — sometimes it has been painted over or covered with a second layer of flooring — but disturbing it during a remodel without testing first is a common and avoidable mistake. If you are planning any basement renovation in an older Mahoning County home, have the floor materials tested before the first tile comes up.
