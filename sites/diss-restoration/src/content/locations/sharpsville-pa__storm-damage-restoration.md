## Why Sharpsville Properties See Storm Damage Issues

Sharpsville sits close enough to Lake Erie to catch the tail end of lake-effect squalls in winter, and close enough to the Ohio Valley to take the brunt of summer thunderstorm lines that track up from Youngstown and Warren before they reach here. That combination means the town gets hit twice a year in different ways: ice-loaded branches snapping power lines in January, then straight-line wind and heavy rain cells dropping trees and stripping shingles in July and August. The Shenango River, which runs right through town, also means low-lying lots near its banks see faster water rise during sustained rain than properties on higher ground. Add in a housing stock that's heavy on early-to-mid 1900s steel-era frame construction, roofs and fascia boards that have been through sixty or more winters, and you get buildings that are more vulnerable to wind uplift and water intrusion than newer builds would be.

## Our Storm Damage Restoration Process in Sharpsville

The first visit is about stopping further loss, not full repair. We tarp open roof sections, board broken windows, and extract standing water before it migrates into subfloor or wall cavities, especially in homes with the older plaster-and-lath construction common in this part of Mercer County, which holds moisture longer than drywall and needs a slower, monitored drying cycle. Once the structure is stable, we document every affected surface with photos and moisture readings for your insurance file, then move into structural drying using the IICRC S500 standard as the benchmark. Downed trees get coordinated with a tree service before we touch roofing repairs underneath, since cutting and rigging has to happen safely first. Throughout, we're tracking humidity and material moisture content daily so drying equipment doesn't run longer than it needs to.

## Local note

Older Sharpsville homes near the river and in the original steel-era blocks closer to downtown often still have the original clay tile or cast iron roof drains tied into aging gutter systems. After a wind event that knocks debris into those drains, water backs up onto the roof deck instead of running off, which can cause interior staining days after the storm has already passed and the yard looks clear. We check those drain lines as part of the initial inspection even when the visible damage looks like it's limited to shingles.
