## Why Boardman Properties See Contents Damage

Mahoning County's climate is hard on structures and everything inside them. The Youngstown–Warren metro area averages meaningful snowfall and repeated freeze-thaw cycles from November through March, and the older housing stock throughout Boardman — much of it built in the postwar boom of the 1950s and 1960s — carries plumbing that was never designed to flex through decades of that stress. Cast-iron drain lines and galvanized supply pipes common in that era corrode from the inside out, often failing without warning. When they go, water doesn't just wet the floor — it wicks into subfloor assemblies, travels along wall cavities, and soaks into upholstered furniture, cabinetry, and stored belongings before anyone realizes the extent of the problem.

Fire and smoke losses add a separate layer of complexity. Smoke residue from a kitchen or electrical fire doesn't stay in the room where the flame was. It migrates through ductwork and settles into fabric, wood grain, and porous surfaces throughout the house. Contents that look undamaged can carry odor-causing particles that resurface months later if they aren't properly cleaned.

## Our Contents Pack-Out and Restoration Process

The first step is a thorough inventory. Every item that leaves your property is photographed, catalogued, and assigned a condition note before it's packed. That documentation matters for your insurance claim and gives you a clear record of what was taken and in what state.

From there, items go to a controlled environment where our IICRC-certified team assesses what each piece needs. Upholstered furniture may require ultrasonic cleaning or ozone treatment to neutralize smoke odor. Hard surfaces — wood furniture, cabinetry, decorative items — are cleaned with methods matched to the material so finishes aren't damaged in the process. Electronics and documents get routed to specialists when the situation calls for it. Clothing and textiles go through deodorization before laundering.

Items that can be restored are stored in a climate-controlled facility until your property is ready to receive them. That matters in Boardman's climate: fluctuating humidity during storage is one of the more common ways secondary damage happens to wood furniture and paper documents after a loss. Stable temperature and humidity during the interim period protects the work already done.

Most homeowners' policies in Ohio cover sudden and accidental water damage and fire losses, and contents restoration is typically a covered line item under personal property coverage.
