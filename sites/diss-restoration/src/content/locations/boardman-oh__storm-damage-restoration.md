## Why Boardman Properties See Storm Damage the Way They Do

Boardman's residential fabric is largely a product of the 1950s through 1980s, a construction era that predates modern impact-resistant roofing standards and engineered wood sheathing. Older asphalt shingles lose granule coverage over time, meaning a hailstorm that would leave a newer roof with minor bruising can strip an older roof to bare felt in the same pass. Soffit and fascia on homes of that vintage are often aluminum or wood — materials that dent, crack, or rot when storm debris hits them at speed.

The area's clay-heavy soil compounds the problem. After a heavy rain event, water that can't infiltrate fast enough pools against foundation walls and works through any crack or gap that storm winds opened up. Crawl spaces and finished basements — common in Boardman's older neighborhoods — become secondary damage zones within hours of a roof breach if water isn't intercepted at the source.

Tall, established trees are part of what makes Boardman's residential streets appealing, but they're also the source of some of the most serious storm claims in the area. A single large limb through a roof creates an immediate opening for rain, and a full tree strike can compromise structural framing that requires both restoration and reconstruction work before the home is safe to occupy.

## Our Storm Damage Restoration Process in Boardman

The first priority after a storm strike is stopping the bleeding. That means emergency tarping and board-up to close any opening in the building envelope — roof, wall, or window — before the next rain event or overnight temperature drop makes things worse. DISS Restoration responds 24/7, so that initial protective work happens regardless of when the storm moves through.

Once the structure is secured, the assessment phase begins. Our IICRC-certified team uses moisture meters and thermal imaging to map where water has already traveled — through insulation, behind drywall, under flooring — before it shows up as a visible stain or a musty smell. In Boardman's older homes, water behind plaster or beneath hardwood subfloor can read dry at the surface while the underlying structure stays saturated. Missing that hidden moisture is how secondary mold problems start.

From there, the process moves through controlled drying using commercial-grade air movers and dehumidifiers, debris removal, structural repairs, and final restoration of interior finishes. Every phase is documented with photographs and moisture readings — the kind of record that makes insurance claims move faster and closes disputes before they start.
