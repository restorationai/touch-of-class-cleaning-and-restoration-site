## Why Sharon Properties See Mold Issues

The Shenango River corridor creates a humidity pattern that is distinct from flatter parts of western Pennsylvania. During shoulder seasons, warm air moving through the valley meets cooler surfaces inside homes and condenses. In houses built before the 1960s, which make up a significant portion of Sharon's residential stock, that condensation has decades of accumulated pathways: original plaster and lath walls, stone or block foundations without drainage membranes, and cast-iron plumbing that sweats in summer. Crawl spaces under these homes often sit on bare soil with no vapor barrier, and standing groundwater after heavy rain can persist long enough for mold colonies to take hold within 24 to 48 hours of saturation.

Roofing and siding on older Sharon homes also tends to be layered over original materials rather than stripped, which means water that finds a gap can travel behind multiple material layers before it becomes visible inside. By the time a homeowner notices a musty smell or a dark stain near a baseboard, the actual colonization is often well behind the finished surface.

## Our Mold Remediation Process in Sharon

Every job starts with a thorough visual inspection and moisture mapping using thermal imaging and calibrated meters. Because Sharon's older homes frequently have plaster over wood lath, surface readings can look acceptable while the lath itself remains saturated. We probe deeper than the finished surface before drawing any conclusions about scope.

Once the affected area is identified, we establish negative-air containment using 6-mil poly barriers and HEPA-filtered air scrubbers. This keeps spores from migrating to unaffected rooms during the remediation work. Affected porous materials, including drywall, insulation, and in some cases original lath, are removed and bagged under containment. Structural framing and masonry surfaces are cleaned with EPA-registered antimicrobial solutions and allowed to dry to documented moisture levels before any reconstruction begins.

We do not close out a job until moisture readings confirm the structure is dry and, where an industrial hygienist has been engaged for post-remediation verification, clearance air sampling meets acceptable thresholds. That final step matters especially in Sharon, where the same valley humidity that caused the original problem can slow drying if containment is lifted too early.

## Sharon Insurance Coordination

Mold claims in Pennsylvania can be complicated by policy language that distinguishes between mold resulting from a sudden covered loss, such as a burst pipe, and mold attributed to long-term moisture intrusion, which many carriers exclude or limit. We document the loss thoroughly: moisture readings, photo evidence, material samples where appropriate, and a written scope of work. That documentation gives your adjuster what they need to evaluate the claim accurately. We work with most major carriers and can communicate directly with your adjuster to reduce the back-and-forth you would otherwise manage on your own.

## Local Note

Sharon's pre-war housing stock frequently has original horsehair plaster over wood lath, and that combination behaves differently from modern drywall when mold is present. The plaster face can read at acceptable moisture levels on a pin meter while the lath behind it is still wet enough to support active growth. On these properties, we use both resistance meters and thermo-hygrometers at multiple depths before finalizing a remediation scope. Skipping that step in a Sharon home of this era is how a remediation job gets called back six months later.
