## Why Hubbard Properties See Contents Damage Differently

The Mahoning Valley's freeze-thaw cycle is punishing. Hubbard's winters regularly push pipes past their tolerance, and when a supply line lets go inside a plaster-walled home, the water doesn't just pool on the floor — it wicks into wood lath, saturates subfloors, and migrates toward furniture and stored items faster than most homeowners expect. Smoke from a kitchen or furnace fire behaves similarly: the tight floor plans common in Hubbard's post-war bungalows and two-story colonials let odor and residue travel through every room within hours.

Contents in these homes often carry real value — not just monetary, but sentimental. Upholstered furniture, area rugs, framed photographs, and electronics all respond differently to water and smoke, and the window for effective restoration narrows with every hour they sit in a compromised environment.

## Our Contents Restoration and Storage Process in Hubbard

When we arrive at a Hubbard property, the first step is a room-by-room contents inventory. Every item is photographed, tagged, and logged before anything moves. That documentation matters for your insurance claim and gives you a clear record of what left your home and in what condition.

From there, pack-out is handled with moving blankets, sealed bins, and climate-controlled transport. Items go to our secure storage facility where they are sorted by material type — upholstery, hard goods, electronics, documents — because each category requires a different cleaning method. Textiles go through thermal fogging and ozone treatment for smoke odor; hard surfaces are cleaned with appropriate agents calibrated to the finish; electronics are assessed by a technician before any power is applied.

Once the structure has been cleared and is ready for move-back, contents are returned in the same documented sequence they left. Nothing is returned until it passes a final inspection.
