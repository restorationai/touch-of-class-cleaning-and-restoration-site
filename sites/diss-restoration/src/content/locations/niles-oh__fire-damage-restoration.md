## Why Niles Properties See Distinct Fire Damage Patterns

The age of Niles's housing stock is the single biggest factor shaping fire damage here. Balloon-frame homes — where wall cavities run uninterrupted from the basement sill to the roof — allow fire and superheated smoke to travel vertically through the structure far faster than in platform-frame construction. That means a kitchen fire that looks contained can leave smoke deposits and heat damage in second-floor ceilings and attic spaces that weren't anywhere near the ignition point. Older plaster-on-lath walls also absorb smoke odor compounds deeply into the substrate; surface cleaning alone won't remove them.

Niles winters are cold and damp, and fire suppression adds hundreds of gallons of water to an already stressed structure. In February, that water can begin freezing inside wall cavities within hours if heat is lost during the event. Addressing the water intrusion from firefighting efforts is not a secondary concern — it runs parallel to the smoke and char work from the very first day.

## Our Fire Damage Restoration Process in Niles

Every fire loss is different, but the sequence of work follows a proven framework calibrated to what we actually find in Niles homes.

**Emergency stabilization** comes first: boarding windows and doors, tarping roof penetrations, and shutting off utilities if the fire department hasn't already done so. In older Niles properties, this step sometimes reveals additional hazards — asbestos-containing floor tile or pipe insulation disturbed by the fire — that require documentation before any further work proceeds. As an EPA Lead-Safe Certified firm, we handle pre-1978 painted surfaces according to federal guidelines throughout the restoration.

**Smoke and soot mapping** follows. Using IICRC FSRT protocols, we trace smoke migration through the structure — including into HVAC ducts, attic insulation, and those balloon-frame wall cavities — so that no affected area is missed and left to off-gas odor later.

**Structural drying** runs concurrently. Firefighting water saturates subfloors, plaster, and framing. Our IICRC ASD-certified structural drying process uses calibrated desiccant and refrigerant dehumidifiers alongside air movers to bring moisture levels back to regional norms before any rebuild begins.

**Cleaning, deodorization, and content restoration** address char residue, smoke films on surfaces, and odor at the molecular level using thermal fogging and hydroxyl generation where appropriate.

**Reconstruction coordination** closes the job — working with your insurance adjuster to scope and execute repairs that bring the structure back to pre-loss condition.
