## Why Campbell Properties See Sewage Backup Issues

Campbell's housing stock is largely pre-1960, built during the steel-industry boom that made the Mahoning Valley one of the most densely developed corridors in northeastern Ohio. Those homes were plumbed with clay-tile laterals that connect to municipal mains running under streets that have settled and shifted over sixty-plus years. Clay tile is porous, and tree roots from mature street trees find every joint. When roots partially block a lateral, the first sign is often a slow drain, until a heavy rain event overwhelms the system and sewage pushes back through floor drains, toilets, and utility sinks simultaneously.

The Mahoning River floodplain also plays a role. During periods of sustained rain, groundwater infiltration raises the water table and can surcharge the city's combined sewer infrastructure, sending wastewater backward into connected structures. Homes in lower-lying sections of Campbell are especially vulnerable when the river corridor is saturated. That combination of aging laterals and periodic surcharging means sewage events here tend to be more severe than a simple clog, they often involve significant standing water carrying solid waste, bacteria, and pathogens.

## Our Sewage Cleanup and Sanitization Process in Campbell

Sewage cleanup follows a strict sequence because cutting corners at any stage leaves invisible contamination behind. When a crew arrives, the first priority is stopping ongoing exposure: ventilating the space, identifying the source, and confirming the lateral or main has been cleared by a plumber before restoration work begins. Restoration cannot safely proceed while sewage is still entering the structure.

Once the source is controlled, extraction removes standing water and solid waste using truck-mounted units capable of handling the heavy, particulate-laden water that raw sewage produces. Every porous material that absorbed Category 3 water, carpet, pad, drywall below the flood line, insulation, wood subfloor, is assessed for removal. In Campbell's older homes, that often means dealing with original hardwood subfloors over board sheathing rather than modern plywood, which changes how moisture migrates and how thoroughly the cavity beneath needs to be dried.

After removal, all affected surfaces are treated with EPA-registered disinfectants rated for Category 3 contamination. Structural drying follows using commercial dehumidifiers and air movers calibrated to the specific volume and material composition of the space. Moisture readings are logged at each visit so the drying curve is documented, that documentation matters when your insurance adjuster reviews the claim.
