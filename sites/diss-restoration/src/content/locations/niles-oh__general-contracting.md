## Why Niles Homes Require a Different Renovation Approach

The Mahoning Valley's freeze-thaw cycle is hard on older structures. Niles winters push moisture into brick mortar, expand it, and pull it back out — a cycle that, over decades, can shift lintels, crack parging, and create hidden voids behind interior finishes. When a homeowner opens a wall for a bathroom remodel or a kitchen expansion, it's not unusual to find deteriorated framing, outdated knob-and-tube wiring that complicates permit approvals, or plaster that has separated from its lath and needs full replacement rather than a simple skim coat.

Niles also sits in a region where basement moisture is a recurring issue. The relatively flat terrain and clay-heavy soils in parts of Trumbull County don't drain quickly after heavy rain or snowmelt, and basements in older Niles homes frequently show evidence of long-term seepage even when no single flood event is on record. A general contracting scope that doesn't account for that underlying condition is one that risks redoing work within a few years.

## Local Note: What Older Niles Plaster Walls Mean for Your Remodel

Plaster-and-lath construction — common in Niles homes built before the 1950s — behaves differently than modern drywall in ways that matter for both renovation budgets and post-damage drying. Plaster is denser and holds moisture longer, which means that after a water loss, walls that read dry at the surface can still be saturated at the lath layer for days. On the renovation side, plaster removal generates significantly more debris than drywall demo, and matching new work to original plaster profiles requires a different finishing approach than simply hanging new sheetrock. Knowing this going in — rather than discovering it mid-demo — keeps the project on schedule and prevents the kind of scope creep that erodes budgets.
