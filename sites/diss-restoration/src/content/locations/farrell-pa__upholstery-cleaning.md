## Our Upholstery Cleaning Process in Farrell

We start with a fiber and dye test on an inconspicuous area, because Farrell's older homes hold a mix of furniture generations: newer synthetic blends next to inherited pieces with natural fibers that react very differently to moisture and heat. From there we vacuum with an upholstery attachment to lift embedded grit before any liquid touches the fabric, since grit is what actually cuts fibers during the cleaning process rather than the cleaning itself. Fabric pieces get a low-moisture steam clean calibrated to dry faster in humid conditions, which matters more here than in a drier climate because slow-drying fabric is exactly what lets odor and mildew set back in. Leather gets cleaned and conditioned separately, with attention to seams and piping where oils and dust collect over time.

## Equipment & Methods We Use for Upholstery

Fabric upholstery gets a hot water extraction method similar to what's used on carpet, but at lower pressure and with a specialized upholstery tool that controls how much moisture goes into the cushion fill. Leather is treated with pH-balanced cleaners rather than steam, since heat and excess moisture are what crack and dry out leather over time rather than restore it. For pieces with down or foam fill, common in older Farrell homes where furniture gets handed down through a family rather than replaced, we adjust drying time and sometimes recommend a fan-assisted dry rather than rushing the process, since trapped moisture inside the cushion is what leads to odor coming back a week later.
