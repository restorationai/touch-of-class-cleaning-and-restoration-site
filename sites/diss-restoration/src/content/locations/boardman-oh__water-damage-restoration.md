## Why Boardman Properties See Water Damage Issues

Boardman's housing stock tells the story. A significant portion of the township's residential neighborhoods were built between the late 1940s and the 1970s, when homes were constructed with plaster-and-lath walls, cast-iron supply lines, and basement drainage systems that were sized for a different era's storm volumes. Those older supply lines corrode from the inside out — you won't see rust on the exterior until the pipe is already compromised. When they fail, water travels fast through plaster, which absorbs moisture deeply before the surface shows a stain.

Mahoning County's clay-heavy soil compounds the problem. Clay doesn't drain the way sandy or loamy soil does — during a heavy rain event, it holds water against foundation walls and forces it through cracks in block or poured concrete. Sump pumps in Boardman homes work hard, and when a pump fails during a storm or a power outage, a finished basement can take on several inches of water in under an hour. Spring snowmelt on top of saturated ground is a particular pressure point for homes throughout the area.

## Our Water Damage Restoration Process in Boardman

The first step on any water loss is containment and assessment. When a crew arrives, they use moisture meters and thermal imaging cameras to map exactly where water has traveled — not just where it's visible. Water behind plaster can read dry at the surface while the lath and framing behind it stay saturated. That hidden moisture is what leads to mold colonization if it isn't addressed within the first 24 to 48 hours.

Once the extent of the loss is mapped, standing water is extracted using truck-mounted and portable extraction units. After bulk water is removed, industrial air movers and dehumidifiers are positioned according to the IICRC S500 standard for structural drying — the number and placement of units is calculated based on the square footage, material types, and moisture readings, not guesswork. Crews return daily to log readings and adjust equipment until every affected material reaches its target moisture content. Nothing is closed up until the numbers confirm it's dry.

## Local Note

Older homes in Boardman built with plaster walls require a different drying approach than the paper-faced drywall common in newer construction. Plaster releases moisture slowly — it can feel firm and cool to the touch while still holding significant water content in the base coat and the wood lath behind it. Rushing the drying timeline and pulling equipment too early is one of the most common reasons mold shows up weeks after a loss that seemed resolved. Crews working in Boardman's older neighborhoods account for this by extending monitoring periods and using lower-velocity air movement to draw moisture out gradually rather than driving it deeper into the assembly.
