## Why Niles Properties Present Specific Post-Construction Cleaning Challenges

Niles sits in a part of northeast Ohio where the building stock spans nearly a century of construction eras. Homes and commercial buildings put up in the mid-1900s — the kind of two-story brick construction common throughout the city's older residential corridors — were built with plaster walls, cast-iron plumbing, and original hardwood flooring. When those properties get renovated today, the debris profile is more complex than a new build. Plaster dust is finer and heavier than drywall compound dust; it infiltrates HVAC ductwork faster and is harder to capture with standard shop vacuums. If a contractor cut into original cast-iron pipe, there may be rust particulate mixed into the debris field as well.

Trumbull County's climate adds another layer. Niles experiences genuine freeze-thaw cycles through winter and into early spring, which means renovation projects that run through colder months often leave moisture trapped under temporary weather barriers, inside newly framed walls, or beneath freshly poured concrete that cured slower than expected. By the time the final clean is scheduled, that trapped moisture can already be promoting microbial growth behind baseboards or under subfloor material — a problem that a surface cleaning crew with no moisture detection capability will simply clean around and leave in place.

## Our Post-Construction Cleaning Process in Niles

The first thing we do on any post-construction or specialty cleaning job in Niles is a walkthrough with a moisture meter and a thermal imaging camera. Construction debris is visible; moisture intrusion often isn't. If we find elevated readings, we flag them before we touch a surface — because cleaning over a wet substrate and sealing it with paint or flooring adhesive is how a small remediation problem becomes a large mold remediation project six months later.

Once the substrate is confirmed dry, the cleaning sequence moves from top to bottom and from inside out:

- **HEPA vacuuming** of all surfaces, including ceiling fixtures, window tracks, door frames, and exposed ductwork before any filter changes
- **Detail wipe-down** of all hard surfaces using appropriate chemistry for the material — no single cleaner works on both fresh grout haze and construction adhesive overspray
- **Floor care** calibrated to the surface type: hardwood, LVP, tile, and concrete each require different tools and pH levels to avoid dulling or etching
- **Final air scrubbing** with HEPA-filtered equipment to pull residual airborne particulate before the space is handed back

For specialty cleaning requests — post-fire odor neutralization in a renovated space, deep cleaning after a hoarding abatement, or cleaning following a biohazard event — the process is scoped individually based on what materials are present and what the end use of the space will be.
