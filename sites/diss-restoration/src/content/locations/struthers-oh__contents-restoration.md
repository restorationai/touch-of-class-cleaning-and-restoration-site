Struthers sits in a river valley where the Mahoning cuts through decades of industrial history, and the homes that line its streets reflect that past — tight lots, older construction, and basements that have seen their share of water. When a pipe fails or a kitchen fire leaves smoke baked into upholstered furniture, the belongings inside those homes need more than a wipe-down. Contents restoration and storage is the part of disaster recovery most homeowners don't think about until they're standing in a smoke-damaged living room wondering whether their grandmother's armchair can be saved.

## Why Struthers Homes Present Unique Contents Challenges

The housing stock throughout Struthers skews older — many properties were built between the 1920s and 1960s, during the steel-era boom that shaped the Mahoning Valley. That era of construction means plaster walls, original hardwood floors, and furniture that was built to last but absorbs smoke and moisture deeply. Wool upholstery, solid-wood cabinetry, and oil-based painted surfaces all respond differently to fire residue and water than modern synthetic materials do.

The valley's climate adds another layer. Struthers winters bring freeze-thaw cycles that stress older plumbing, and the spring thaw can push groundwater into basements faster than sump pumps can keep up. When a loss happens — whether it's a burst supply line in January or a sump failure in April — the contents inside those homes are often sitting in water or smoke exposure for longer than ideal before a crew arrives. Every additional hour matters: smoke odor bonds more permanently to porous materials, and wet fabric begins to develop microbial growth quickly in the humid Ohio Valley air.

## Our Contents Pack-Out and Storage Process in Struthers

The first step when we arrive at a Struthers property is a room-by-room inventory. Every item is photographed, logged, and assessed before anything is moved. This documentation matters for insurance claims and gives you a clear record of what left your home and in what condition.

Items that can be restored — furniture, clothing, artwork, electronics, documents — are carefully packed out and transported to our climate-controlled facility. There, each category gets the treatment it needs:

- **Soft goods and textiles** go through ozone and hydroxyl treatment to neutralize smoke odor at the molecular level, followed by professional laundering or dry-cleaning where appropriate.
- **Hard surfaces and furniture** are cleaned using ultrasonic equipment or hand-cleaning methods depending on the material — lacquered wood, upholstery, and painted surfaces all require different chemistry.
- **Electronics and documents** are assessed for salvageability and routed to the appropriate specialist process.
- **Items that cannot be restored** are documented thoroughly for your insurance adjuster before any disposal decision is made.

While restoration is underway, your belongings are stored in a secure, climate-controlled environment — not a warehouse where temperature swings could cause secondary damage to wood, leather, or canvas.

## Working with Insurance on Contents Claims in Struthers

Contents losses are often the most contested part of a homeowner's insurance claim. Adjusters want documentation; you want your belongings back. We photograph every item before it leaves your home, maintain a detailed inventory throughout the restoration process, and provide itemized reports that align with what carriers need to process a contents claim. We work with most major insurance carriers and can communicate directly with your adjuster to keep the claim moving.

If your policy includes additional living expense coverage, your adjuster may also need to account for the storage period — we can provide documentation of storage duration and conditions to support that portion of the claim.

## Local Note

Older Struthers homes built close to the Mahoning River corridor often have basement walls constructed with the local clay brick common to the valley — a material that wicks moisture laterally in ways that modern concrete block doesn't. When a basement floods and contents are stored on shelving against those walls, the brick can hold residual moisture long after the floor appears dry, leading to secondary mold growth on items that seemed unaffected at first. During pack-out in these properties, we pay particular attention to items stored low and against exterior walls, even when they look dry on the surface.
