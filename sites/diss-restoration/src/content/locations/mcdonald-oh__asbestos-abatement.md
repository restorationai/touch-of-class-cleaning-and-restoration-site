**Dealing with asbestos abatement in McDonald?** A lot of the village's housing stock went up during the mid-20th century boom years tied to the old steel mills along the Mahoning Valley, and that generation of construction leaned hard on asbestos for insulation, floor tile, and ceiling texture. If you're renovating a McDonald bungalow or ranch and you've hit a popcorn ceiling, old vinyl tile, or crumbling pipe wrap in the basement, that's worth stopping work on until it's tested.

## Why McDonald Properties See Asbestos Issues

Trumbull County's housing stock skews older than the state average, and McDonald in particular grew up around the steel industry in the early-to-mid 1900s, which means a high share of homes were built or renovated before the 1980s asbestos phase-out. Popcorn ceilings, floor tile mastic, vermiculite attic insulation, and pipe lagging around old boiler systems show up constantly in these houses. Basement mechanical rooms are a frequent trouble spot, since many McDonald homes still run original cast-iron plumbing or early-generation furnace ductwork wrapped in asbestos-laced insulation that's now brittle enough to shed fibers when disturbed.

## Our Asbestos Abatement Process in McDonald

We start with a visual inspection and sample collection from suspect materials, whether that's ceiling texture, tile, siding, or insulation, and send those samples to a third-party lab rather than guessing based on age or appearance alone. Once results confirm asbestos content, we seal the work area with poly sheeting and negative air pressure before anything gets disturbed, because containment is what actually protects the rest of the house, not the removal itself. Materials come out wet-wrapped to control fiber release, bagged in labeled asbestos waste containers, and hauled for disposal at a facility licensed to accept it. For older McDonald homes with plaster-and-lath construction, we also check for asbestos in the plaster skim coat itself, which is a detail that gets missed when a crew only tests the obvious insulation or tile.

## Local note

One thing we watch for in McDonald specifically: a lot of the smaller post-war homes near the old mill-era streets have additions or garage conversions done without permits decades ago, and those add-on spaces sometimes used different materials than the original structure, including floor tile or ceiling panels installed well after the main house was built. That means a clean asbestos test on the original portion of the house doesn't automatically clear an addition, so we sample both separately rather than assuming consistency across the whole structure.
