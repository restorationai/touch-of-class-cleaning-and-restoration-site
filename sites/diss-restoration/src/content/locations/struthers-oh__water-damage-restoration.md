## Why Struthers Properties Are Vulnerable to Water Damage

Much of Struthers was built during the mid-twentieth-century steel boom, when the mills along the Mahoning were running full shifts. That era of construction means a lot of homes here have block or poured-concrete basements, cast-iron or galvanized plumbing that has been in service for sixty or more years, and original hardwood subfloors over unventilated crawl spaces. Each of those features changes how water behaves after a loss.

Galvanized pipes corrode from the inside out, narrowing gradually until a fitting fails — often at a joint behind a wall or under a cabinet where the leak runs for days before anyone smells the dampness. Block basement walls are porous by design; they don't fail dramatically, they seep. Over time, efflorescence (the white mineral crust you see on older block) signals that water has been moving through the wall repeatedly. And hardwood subfloors over unventilated crawl spaces can cup and buckle within 48 to 72 hours of saturation — a window that closes fast in the humid Ohio summers the Mahoning Valley sees every year.

## Our Water Damage Restoration Process in Struthers

When a call comes in, the first priority is stopping the source — whether that means coordinating with the homeowner to shut the main, or working around an active roof intrusion. From there, the process follows the IICRC S500 standard for water damage restoration:

**Inspection and moisture mapping.** Thermal imaging and calibrated moisture meters locate water that has traveled beyond the visible wet area. In Struthers homes with plaster-over-lath walls — common in pre-1960 construction — the lath can stay saturated long after the plaster surface reads dry to the touch.

**Water extraction.** Truck-mounted and portable extraction units pull standing water from flooring, carpeting, and subfloor assemblies. The faster this step happens, the more material can be saved rather than replaced.

**Structural drying.** Industrial desiccant and refrigerant dehumidifiers, paired with high-velocity air movers, create a controlled drying environment. Drying logs are recorded daily so the insurance carrier has documentation of every reading.

**Monitoring and clearance.** Drying is not complete when the floor feels dry — it's complete when moisture readings return to the regional equilibrium moisture content for this climate zone. We don't pull equipment early.

Most homeowners' policies in Ohio cover sudden and accidental water losses — a burst pipe, an appliance failure, an ice dam that forces water under the roof deck. What they typically exclude is long-term seepage, which is exactly the kind of loss that block basement walls in older Struthers homes can produce over time. Knowing that distinction matters before you file.

In Struthers homes with original hardwood floors over unventilated crawl spaces — a construction pattern common in the neighborhoods that grew up near the former mill corridor — water extraction from the surface is only half the job.
