## Why Campbell Properties See Severe Storm Damage

Campbell's housing stock skews older, with a significant share of homes built during the mid-20th century steel-boom years. Those homes were constructed well, but decades of Mahoning Valley winters and wet springs have worked on their roofing systems, fascia boards, and window frames. When a fast-moving storm hits, the weak points are already there: dried-out caulk around dormers, aging flashing at chimney bases, and wood siding that has absorbed years of freeze-thaw cycles.

The Mahoning Valley's position between Lake Erie to the north and the Appalachian foothills to the east creates a corridor for lake-effect precipitation in late fall and early winter, and for severe thunderstorm lines in spring and summer. That means Campbell properties face storm risk across a longer season than many Ohio communities farther south. Hail damage to asphalt shingles is common enough that granule loss, which looks minor from the ground, often signals that the roof's waterproofing layer is already compromised before the next storm arrives.

## Our Storm Damage Restoration Process in Campbell

The first step on any storm call is a full exterior and interior assessment before any materials are removed or covered. We document everything with photographs and written notes structured for insurance review, because a carrier's adjuster will want to see the pre-mitigation condition.

Once the scope is clear, emergency tarping and board-up protect the structure from additional water intrusion while permanent repairs are planned. If rain has already entered through a failed roof or broken window, we deploy industrial air movers and dehumidifiers calibrated to the volume of the affected space. Moisture mapping with thermal imaging and pin-type meters tells us where water has traveled inside wall cavities and under flooring, not just where it is visible at the surface. Structural drying follows the IICRC S500 standard, with daily readings logged until materials reach acceptable moisture content.

Tree impact calls require an additional step: assessing whether the structural framing beneath the point of impact has been racked or compromised. A limb through a roof deck is visible; the load transfer into rafters and ceiling joists below it is not always obvious without probing.

## Local Note

Campbell's older homes often have full-dimension lumber framing, which is denser and heavier than modern dimensional lumber, but it also holds moisture longer once it gets wet. When storm water enters through a roof breach and soaks into full-dimension rafters or ceiling joists, those members can read at acceptable surface moisture levels while still carrying significant moisture deeper in the wood. We account for this by extending drying cycles and using penetrating moisture readings rather than relying on surface readings alone. It is a detail that matters in a community where so much of the housing stock was built before modern dimensional lumber became standard.
