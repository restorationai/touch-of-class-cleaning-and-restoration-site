## Why Hubbard Furniture Needs a Different Approach

Hubbard's housing stock leans older, a lot of the homes near the village center and along State Route 7 were built when the steel mills were still running full shifts, and the furniture inside them has often been reupholstered at least once. That means layers: original horsehair or cotton batting under newer foam, sometimes multiple fabric coverings stacked from past recovering jobs. Standard couch cleaning that just skims the top layer won't touch what's trapped underneath. We also see a fair amount of leather upholstery in the area, family pieces that have dried out and cracked from decades near radiators or wood stoves, which calls for conditioning treatment rather than the wet extraction we'd use on a fabric sofa.

## Our Upholstery Cleaning Process in Hubbard

We start every job with a fabric and fiber test, pulling a small sample from a hidden seam to confirm whether a piece can handle water-based cleaning or needs a dry solvent approach. From there we do a dry pre-vacuum to lift loose soil and pet hair, which matters more than people expect since a lot of Hubbard households have dogs and cats that spend time on the furniture. Furniture steam cleaning follows for most fabric pieces, using low-moisture hot water extraction so the cushions aren't left saturated in a humid Ohio summer. Leather gets a separate cleaning and conditioning pass, since the mill-era homes in Hubbard often have leather furniture that's spent years near forced-air vents drying it out from the inside.

## Local note

Hubbard's water carries more mineral content than city supplies in bigger nearby markets, and we see it show up as faint rings on light-colored fabric upholstery, especially on pieces cleaned previously with a machine that didn't fully extract the rinse water. We run a final dry pass and check moisture levels before we consider a piece finished, because leaving residual moisture in mineral-heavy water is what causes those rings to reappear days later.
