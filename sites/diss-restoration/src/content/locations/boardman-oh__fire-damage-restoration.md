## Why Boardman Properties Face Distinct Fire Damage Challenges

Much of Boardman's residential stock was built during the postwar decades — the 1950s through the 1970s — when construction standards, insulation materials, and electrical systems looked very different from what code requires today. Homes from that era often have knob-and-tube wiring that was upgraded in pieces rather than all at once, older gas appliances with aging connections, and attic insulation that can act as a sponge for smoke particulates. When a kitchen fire or furnace malfunction ignites in one of these homes, smoke doesn't just travel through open rooms — it moves through wall cavities, crawl spaces, and ductwork in ways that a surface cleaning will never fully address.

The Mahoning Valley's cold winters add another layer. Firefighting water freezes fast in sub-freezing temperatures, and the water used to suppress a fire can cause secondary damage — saturated subflooring, ice in wall cavities, compromised insulation — that compounds the fire loss if restoration crews don't address it immediately alongside the smoke and soot work. Boardman's mix of ranch-style homes and two-story colonials on relatively compact lots also means that smoke and odor can migrate to neighboring structures, a factor worth noting for anyone in a duplex or a home with an attached garage.

## Our Fire and Smoke Restoration Process in Boardman

The first thing a crew does on arrival is a structured scope assessment — not a sales walk, but a systematic check of every space where smoke or combustion byproducts may have traveled. That includes the HVAC system, attic, basement, and any shared wall cavities. Soot chemistry matters here: protein-based soot from a kitchen fire behaves differently than the thick, oily residue left by a structural fire involving synthetic materials, and the cleaning agents and methods have to match.

Once the scope is documented and the structure is confirmed safe to enter, the process moves through content pack-out (salvageable belongings removed and catalogued for off-site cleaning), structural surface cleaning using dry sponging and wet chemical methods calibrated to the surface type, deodorization using thermal fogging and hydroxyl generation to neutralize odor at the molecular level rather than masking it, and — where fire suppression water is present — structural drying per the IICRC S500 standard. Boardman homes with older plaster walls require particular attention during drying: plaster holds moisture longer than drywall and can read as dry at the surface while the lath behind it stays saturated, which means moisture mapping with thermal imaging and pin meters is essential before any reconstruction begins.
