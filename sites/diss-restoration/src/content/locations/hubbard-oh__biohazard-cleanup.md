## Why Hubbard Properties Present Specific Biohazard Challenges

Hubbard's residential neighborhoods lean heavily toward mid-century construction — ranch homes, two-story colonials, and older rental conversions built between the 1940s and 1970s. That era of building used materials that complicate biohazard remediation in ways a newer home wouldn't. Subflooring is often tongue-and-groove wood rather than OSB, and it absorbs biological material deeply and unevenly. Plaster walls over wood lath — common in Hubbard's older sections — can wick fluids behind the surface, where standard surface cleaning won't reach. Identifying the true extent of contamination in these structures requires more than a visual inspection; it requires moisture meters, ATP testing, and the kind of methodical room-by-room assessment that doesn't cut corners because the clock is running.

Sharps and needle cleanup adds another layer of complexity in any community. Proper containment, labeling, and disposal of infectious sharps must follow Ohio EPA and OSHA guidelines — not just for the safety of occupants, but for the crew and for anyone who enters the property afterward. Our team is OSHA trained and EPA Lead-Safe certified, which matters in older Hubbard homes where lead paint is a realistic co-contaminant alongside any biohazard situation.
