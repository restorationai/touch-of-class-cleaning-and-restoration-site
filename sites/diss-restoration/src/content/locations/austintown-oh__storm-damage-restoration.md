## Why Austintown Properties See Significant Storm Damage

Mahoning County's position in northeastern Ohio puts it squarely in a corridor that sees both lake-effect weather systems in winter and severe convective storms in the warmer months. Austintown's housing stock reflects decades of post-war residential development — a large share of homes here were built between the late 1940s and the 1970s, meaning many have original roof decking, older asphalt shingles with limited wind ratings, and wood-framed soffits that can fail quickly under hail impact or sustained gusts.

That construction era also means a lot of homes have crawl spaces or slab-on-grade foundations that are vulnerable to the rapid surface runoff that follows a hard storm on Mahoning County's clay-heavy soils. Clay doesn't absorb water quickly — it sheds it toward foundations, window wells, and basement walls. A two-inch rain event that would drain away in sandier terrain can push water into a finished basement here within the hour.

Tornado watches and warnings are not uncommon for this part of Ohio in spring and early summer. Even storms that don't produce confirmed tornadoes can generate microburst winds capable of dropping large trees onto structures — and tree damage to a roof creates an immediate interior exposure that worsens with every subsequent rain.

## Our Storm Damage Restoration Process in Austintown

The first priority after any storm event is stopping further damage. That means tarping compromised rooflines, boarding broken windows and doors, and extracting any standing water before it migrates into wall cavities or subfloor assemblies. Our IICRC-certified team uses thermal imaging and moisture meters to map exactly where water has traveled — not just where it's visible.

From there, the process moves into structural drying calibrated to actual conditions in the structure. Older homes common in Austintown often have plaster walls over wood lath rather than modern drywall. Plaster is dense and slow to release moisture — it can read near-normal at the surface while the lath and framing behind it remain saturated. We adjust drying equipment placement and run times accordingly rather than relying on surface readings alone.

Debris removal, temporary weatherproofing, and documentation for your insurance claim happen in parallel. Every affected material is photographed, measured, and logged — the kind of thorough documentation that supports a complete claim rather than a partial settlement.
