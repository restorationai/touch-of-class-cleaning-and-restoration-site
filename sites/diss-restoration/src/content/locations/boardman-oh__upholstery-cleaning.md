## Why Boardman Furniture Needs Deep Cleaning More Often

Boardman grew fast in the 1950s and 60s, and a lot of that housing stock is still standing: brick ranches, split-levels, and colonials with original hardwood or plaster construction. Homes from that era often ran (or still run) forced-air furnaces and wood stoves that circulate fine soot particles through the house. That soot settles into upholstery fibers long before anyone notices smoke smell on a jacket. Add in the Mahoning Valley's humid summers, which slow natural fiber drying and give mildew and dust mites more time to establish themselves in cushions, and fabric furniture in this area works harder than furniture in a drier region. ## Our Upholstery Cleaning Process in Boardman

We start with a fiber and dye test on an inconspicuous panel, because a fabric blend that looks like cotton canvas can react very differently to hot water extraction than it does to a low-moisture method. For most fabric sofas and sectionals, we use hot water extraction with a pH-balanced pre-conditioner to break down oils and ground-in soil before rinsing and extracting. Leather gets a different approach entirely: a pH-neutral cleaner, gentle agitation, and a conditioning step to keep the hide from drying and cracking, which matters more here than in drier climates because Ohio's humidity swings stress leather's finish over a full year. Given how slow air-dries in a humid Boardman summer, we run air movers on fabric pieces after extraction so cushions and frames dry fully instead of trapping moisture that invites mildew.
