## Why Hermitage Properties Face Particular Fire Damage Challenges

Mercer County's housing stock includes a substantial share of homes built between the 1940s and 1970s. Older construction in this region commonly features plaster-and-lath walls, balloon-frame framing, and original knob-and-tube or early-generation wiring that has sometimes been patched rather than fully replaced over the decades. Balloon framing, in particular, creates open vertical channels inside walls that allow smoke and combustion gases to travel from the basement to the attic in a single, unobstructed path. A kitchen fire that looks contained to one room can leave odor-bearing soot deposits two floors away.

Hermitage also experiences significant temperature swings between seasons. In winter, the freeze-thaw cycle stresses older plumbing, and homes that have already sustained fire damage are especially vulnerable: suppression water sitting in a damaged structure can freeze inside wall cavities before drying is complete, compounding the structural damage. That overlap between fire cleanup and moisture control is not hypothetical here; it is a routine part of working in this climate.

## Our Fire Damage Restoration Process in Hermitage

Every fire loss is different, but the sequence of work follows a consistent logic. The first priority after the fire marshal clears the structure is a thorough assessment: what burned, what was smoke-affected, and where suppression water traveled. Thermal imaging and moisture meters map the full extent of damage before any material is removed, which matters for both accurate scoping and insurance documentation.

Board-up and tarping come next if the structure has compromised windows, doors, or roof sections. Hermitage winters move fast, and an open structure loses heat rapidly, which slows drying and risks secondary damage. Once the structure is secured, the team begins controlled demolition of unsalvageable materials, followed by HEPA vacuuming and dry-chemical sponging of smoke residue from surfaces. Odor control using thermal fogging or hydroxyl generation runs concurrently with structural drying, because soot particles that are not physically removed will continue off-gassing long after visible residue is cleaned.

Throughout the process, documentation is continuous: photographs, moisture readings, and material inventories that support your insurance claim from the first day on-site.

## Local Note

Homes in older sections of Hermitage built with balloon framing often have no fire blocking between floor levels inside the wall cavities. After a fire, this means smoke odor can reappear weeks later in rooms that initially seemed unaffected, because the soot-laden air that traveled through those open channels left deposits on framing members that were never directly cleaned. Experienced crews account for this by opening wall sections strategically to treat the framing itself, not just the finished surfaces. Skipping that step is one of the most common reasons a fire-damaged home continues to smell months after a less thorough restoration.
