## Restoration emergencies common in Wheatland

Wheatland's position on the Shenango River means groundwater and surface water both play a role in local losses. Heavy spring rain and snowmelt push river levels up and saturate low-lying yards and basements before a homeowner ever sees standing water indoors. The borough's older housing stock adds a second layer of risk: aging supply lines, cast iron drains, and roofs original to an earlier era are more prone to failure than newer construction, and a slow leak behind plaster can go unnoticed far longer than it would in open-stud drywall framing. Winters bring freeze-thaw cycles that stress pipes and foundations, and the region's humid summers give mold a head start whenever moisture sits unaddressed for more than a day or two.
