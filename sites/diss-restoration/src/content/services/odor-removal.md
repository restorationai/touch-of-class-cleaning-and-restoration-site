## What Odor Removal and Deodorization actually involves

Smoke residue, sewage backups, and dead rodents in a wall cavity all leave behind odor molecules that bond to porous surfaces: drywall, insulation, subflooring, HVAC ductwork. Masking sprays and scented candles cover the smell for a few hours and then it comes back, usually worse, because the source material is still off-gassing. Professional odor elimination works differently. It identifies where the odor-causing residue is actually sitting, physically removes what can be removed, and then uses equipment matched to the chemistry of the problem, ozone for non-occupied spaces, hydroxyl generators for occupied homes, thermal fogging to drive deodorizer into the same microscopic pathways the smoke or contamination traveled through in the first place.

Timelines vary by source. A single room with light smoke odor from a kitchen fire might clear in one to two treatment cycles over a day or two. A whole-house tobacco odor job, or an odor tied to prior water damage and hidden mold growth, can take several days of treatment paired with source removal (cutting out affected drywall, replacing contaminated insulation, sealing subfloor). Odor work is rarely a single visit. It's diagnostic first, then layered treatment, then a verification step to confirm the smell is actually gone and not just temporarily suppressed.

## Our process

1. **Source identification.** We walk the property to pinpoint where odor-causing residue has settled, not just where the smell is strongest, since odor can travel through ductwork and wall cavities far from its origin.
2. **Material assessment.** Porous materials (carpet pad, insulation, untreated wood, drywall) that have absorbed smoke, sewage, or biological odor usually can't be deodorized in place and need to come out. We document what's salvageable and what isn't before touching anything.
3. **Source removal and cleaning.** Charred or contaminated materials are removed. Remaining surfaces are cleaned with products matched to the residue type, protein-based soot cleans differently than synthetic soot, and sewage-contaminated surfaces get an antimicrobial pass.
4. **Active deodorization treatment.** Depending on the space and whether it's occupied, we deploy ozone generators (vacant spaces only, due to respiratory irritation), hydroxyl generators (safe to run with people or pets present), or thermal fogging, which vaporizes a deodorizing agent so it penetrates the same cracks and fibers the original odor did.
5. **Verification.** We re-check the space, including a final walkthrough with the homeowner, before calling the job complete. If odor persists, that's a sign a source was missed, not that the equipment needs to run longer.

## What separates a good odor removal response from a bad one

The most common mistake is treating odor as a smell problem instead of a material problem. Running an ozone machine in a room with saturated carpet pad or soot-covered insulation still in place will temporarily mask the odor and then it returns within days, because the source was never removed. A good technician identifies source materials first and treats those; a rushed job sprays deodorizer over the top and leaves.

Ozone also gets misused. It's effective but it oxidizes organic material and can degrade rubber, certain plastics, and some finishes if run too long or in an occupied space. It should never be used around people, pets, or plants, and the space needs to be ventilated afterward before reoccupying. Hydroxyl generators are slower but don't carry that risk, which matters for a job where residents need to stay in the home.

Insurance adjusters reviewing an odor claim tied to fire or water damage typically want documentation showing what was removed versus treated, and why. A scope that lists "deodorized living room" without specifying what materials were cut out or replaced is a common reason claims get questioned or underpaid.

## What does Odor Removal and Deodorization cost?

Costs depend heavily on the odor source, how far it's traveled into the structure, and whether contaminated materials need to be removed before treatment can work. Every loss is different, which is why DISS Restoration provides a written scope before any work begins rather than a flat estimate over the phone. Homeowners insurance typically covers odor removal when it's tied to a covered peril, such as a fire or a sudden pipe failure, but it generally does not cover odor from ongoing issues like a pet or long-term smoking, which carriers treat as a maintenance matter.

## Seasonal & regional considerations

Youngstown's humid summers slow odor dissipation and can reactivate smoke odor in homes that were treated incompletely after a fire, since heat and moisture re-release trapped particles from porous surfaces. In winter, closed-up homes with less air exchange tend to trap odors from furnace puff-backs or sewage backups longer than they would in a well-ventilated season, which is part of why source removal matters more than extended run-time on a machine.
