## How to Compare Water Damage Restoration Companies

The right company is the one that can dry your structure correctly, document the loss for your insurer, and handle any secondary damage that turns up. Three things separate competent restoration from a crew that just runs fans and leaves.

**IICRC certifications are the baseline.** The IICRC S500 is the industry standard for water damage restoration. Look for technicians with WRT (Water Damage Restoration Technician) and ASD (Applied Structural Drying) credentials at minimum. These are not honorary titles. They require passing exams on moisture science, equipment use, and drying validation. A company that cannot name its certifications when you ask is a red flag.

**24/7 availability matters more than you think.** Water damage gets worse by the hour. Wet drywall that sits for 48-72 hours before drying begins can develop mold growth, per [EPA guidance on mold and moisture](https://www.epa.gov/mold). A company that cannot respond until Monday morning is not an emergency restoration company, regardless of what their website says.

**Written scope before work begins.** Any reputable restoration company will provide a written scope of work before starting. This protects you and your insurance claim. If a company wants to start immediately with no documentation, walk away. Your adjuster will ask for a scope, a moisture log, and photo documentation. Make sure your restoration company produces all three.
