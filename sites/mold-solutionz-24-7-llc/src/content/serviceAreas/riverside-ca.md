## Restoration emergencies common in Riverside

Riverside sits in a semi-arid inland basin that swings between dry Santa Ana wind events and sudden, heavy winter rain — a combination that creates predictable but destructive patterns. During dry stretches, wood framing and drywall shrink, opening small gaps around plumbing penetrations and window flashing. When the rains arrive, those same gaps funnel water into wall cavities. Because Riverside's housing stock skews older — a substantial share of single-family homes in neighborhoods like Wood Streets and Magnolia Center were built before modern moisture barriers were standard — water finds paths that newer construction rarely offers.

The region's hard water also accelerates corrosion inside supply lines. Water heater failures and pinhole leaks in copper pipes are among the most common calls we receive from Riverside homeowners. Add the Santa Ana winds, which push embers and deposit heavy smoke residue into attic spaces during wildfire seasons, and you have a city where restoration needs are genuinely year-round.

Riverside's relative distance from the coast means humidity stays low most of the year — but that's deceptive. When moisture does enter a structure, it often goes undetected longer because residents don't notice the musty smell until mold has already colonized behind drywall. The IICRC S520 standard we follow for mold remediation accounts for exactly this kind of hidden growth.
