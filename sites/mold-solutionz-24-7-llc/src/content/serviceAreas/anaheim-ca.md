## Services we provide in Anaheim

### Water damage mitigation

Water damage in Anaheim often starts quietly — a slow slab leak under a kitchen in the Packing District loft district, a failed washing-machine hose in a Colony Park condo, a clogged roof drain over a flat commercial roof near Angel Stadium of Anaheim. By the time visible staining appears, moisture has usually been wicking through framing for days. We use thermal imaging and pin-type moisture meters to map the full extent before a single panel comes off the wall, then deploy industrial air movers and LGR dehumidifiers sized to the actual cubic footage — not the minimum that looks good on a work order.

### Mold remediation

Mold can begin colonizing wet drywall in as little as 24 to 48 hours under Anaheim's warm interior conditions. Our remediation process follows IICRC S520 protocols: containment with negative air pressure, HEPA filtration, controlled demolition of unsalvageable materials, and post-clearance testing to confirm the work is done before we rebuild. We coordinate directly with industrial hygienists when third-party testing is required — common for HOA-governed properties and commercial landlords who need documentation for their records.

### Fire and smoke damage restoration

House fires in Anaheim's denser neighborhoods — particularly the blocks of older single-family homes between Ball Road and Lincoln Avenue — often leave smoke residue in wall cavities and HVAC ducts long after the visible char is removed. Smoke odor from protein-based fires (kitchen fires are the most common type we see) bonds to surfaces at a molecular level and resurfaces with heat. We use thermal fogging, hydroxyl generators, and targeted sealers to address odor at the source, not just the surface.

## Building stock, site conditions, and permits in Anaheim

Most of Anaheim's housing went up between the late 1940s and the 1970s, and almost all of it sits on slab-on-grade over Santa Ana River alluvium — sand, silt, and gravel rather than deep clay. There are no basements to speak of. That changes the work: water under a slab has nowhere to drain and travels sideways under flooring and along bottom plates, so we look for it with moisture mapping rather than assuming the wet spot is the whole story.
