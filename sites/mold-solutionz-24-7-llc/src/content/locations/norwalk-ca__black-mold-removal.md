## Why Norwalk Properties Are Prone to Black Mold

A large share of Norwalk's residential housing stock was built between the late 1940s and the mid-1970s, when vapor barriers were minimal or absent and interior wall cavities were often insulated with materials that hold moisture rather than release it. Slab foundations common throughout the city create a direct pathway for ground moisture to wick upward into framing and drywall, especially after a slow plumbing leak that goes undetected for weeks. The Santa Ana winds that sweep through the region in fall and winter drop indoor humidity sharply — which can temporarily mask a mold problem by slowing visible growth — only for the moisture to return when conditions shift. That cycle of dry and damp is one reason homeowners sometimes discover a mold problem that is far more advanced than the visible surface suggests.

Bathrooms in older Norwalk homes frequently have tile set over a mud-bed mortar layer rather than modern cement board. When grout cracks or caulk fails, water migrates behind the tile and into that mortar layer, feeding Stachybotrys colonies that remain invisible until the tile is removed. By the time a homeowner notices discoloration on a wall or ceiling, the contamination has often spread into the adjacent stud bay.

## Our Black Mold Removal Process in Norwalk

Black mold remediation is not a spray-and-wipe job. Stachybotrys produces mycotoxins that bind to surface materials, so physical removal of contaminated substrate — not just treatment — is the industry standard under the IICRC S520 protocol.

The process begins with a thorough inspection using moisture meters and thermal imaging to map the full extent of moisture intrusion, including areas behind walls and under flooring that show no visible growth. Affected areas are then isolated with polyethylene containment barriers and placed under negative air pressure using HEPA-filtered air scrubbers, which prevents spores from migrating to unaffected rooms during demolition.

Contaminated drywall, insulation, and wood framing that has absorbed mycotoxins is carefully removed and bagged for disposal in accordance with California Department of Public Health guidelines. Remaining structural surfaces are HEPA-vacuumed, treated, and dried to a confirmed moisture content before any reconstruction begins. A post-remediation air quality verification test is conducted before containment is removed — this step matters especially in Norwalk's older housing stock, where cavities can harbor spore reservoirs that are easy to miss without clearance testing.
