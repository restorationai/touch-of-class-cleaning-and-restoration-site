## Why Long Beach Properties See Mold Issues

The combination of coastal humidity and aging building stock creates conditions that are genuinely different from inland Southern California cities. Unlike Riverside or Fontana, Long Beach rarely gets the dry Santa Ana winds that would naturally reduce interior moisture levels for weeks at a time. Instead, the city sits in a humidity band that rarely drops below 60 percent overnight — right in the range where *Cladosporium*, *Penicillium*, and *Stachybotrys* (commonly called black mold) can sustain growth on cellulose materials like wood framing, drywall paper, and ceiling tiles.

Homes in the Zaferia and Rose Park neighborhoods frequently feature original hardwood subfloors over shallow crawl spaces with minimal vapor barriers — a configuration that traps ground moisture against wood for years before visible mold appears on the surface. Multifamily buildings along the corridors closer to downtown, many of them constructed in the 1940s and 1950s, often have shared plumbing chases where a single leak can wick moisture into multiple units before any resident notices.

## Our Mold Remediation Process in Long Beach

Every job starts with a thorough visual inspection and moisture mapping using thermal imaging cameras and pin-type moisture meters. We identify not just where mold is visible but where elevated moisture readings suggest hidden growth behind walls or under flooring — the places a surface wipe-down will never reach.

Containment comes next. We seal off the affected area with 6-mil poly sheeting and establish negative air pressure using HEPA-filtered air scrubbers, which prevents spores from migrating to clean areas of the home during the removal process. This step matters especially in Long Beach's older attached housing, where open floor plans and shared HVAC systems can distribute spores quickly.

Physical removal follows containment. Porous materials that cannot be adequately cleaned — sections of drywall, insulation batts, compromised wood framing — are bagged and disposed of per California Department of Public Health guidelines. Structural surfaces that remain are treated with an EPA-registered antimicrobial agent. Drying equipment, including refrigerant dehumidifiers and high-velocity air movers, then runs until moisture readings return to baseline. Our team is IICRC WRT certified (certificate #70145645), and we dry to the IICRC S520 standard before any reconstruction work begins.

## Local Note

In Long Beach's pre-war bungalow neighborhoods — particularly in the blocks around Wrigley and parts of the 90813 ZIP code — original plaster walls are still common. Plaster behaves differently from modern drywall during mold remediation: it is denser and less porous, so surface mold is sometimes easier to clean without full demolition. However, the wood lath behind plaster absorbs moisture slowly and releases it even more slowly, which means drying times run longer than they would in a comparable drywall assembly. Crews that aren't familiar with this construction type sometimes declare a job dry prematurely. We extend monitoring periods on plaster-wall jobs and verify readings at the lath layer before closing out containment.
