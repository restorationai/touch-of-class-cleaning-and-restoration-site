## Why Fullerton Homes See Elevated Mold Risk

Fullerton sits in a basin where cool marine air from the coast collides with warmer inland temperatures, producing condensation cycles that residents in drier inland cities like Riverside rarely deal with. That daily humidity swing is hard on older building envelopes. Homes built before 1960 — and there are thousands of them in the neighborhoods surrounding downtown Fullerton and along Malvern Avenue — were constructed without modern vapor barriers, and their original wood-frame walls absorb and release moisture repeatedly over a season. Plumbing in those homes is often galvanized steel or early copper, both prone to pinhole leaks behind lathe-and-plaster walls where a leak can go undetected for months.

Fullerton also sits atop expansive clay soils common throughout northern Orange County. When those soils shift during wet winters, foundation cracks appear, and groundwater intrusion follows. A slab crack that looks cosmetic in October can be channeling water under flooring by February, feeding hidden mold growth long before a musty smell becomes obvious.

## Our Mold Inspection and Testing Process

Every inspection begins with a visual survey of the entire structure — attic, crawl space if accessible, all bathrooms, kitchen, laundry area, and any room with an exterior wall. Moisture mapping with a calibrated pin and pinless meter follows, building a picture of where water has traveled beyond the visible damage zone. In Fullerton's older housing stock, that map often extends further than the homeowner expects.

Air sampling is collected at a minimum of two indoor locations and one outdoor baseline. The outdoor baseline matters here: Fullerton's proximity to Coyote Hills and the Santa Ana River bottom means ambient outdoor spore counts for Cladosporium and Alternaria can run higher than in coastal cities, and a lab result needs to be read against that local baseline to be meaningful. Tape-lift or bulk samples are taken from any visually suspect material. All samples go to an accredited third-party laboratory — results typically return within 24 to 48 hours.

The written report identifies affected materials, quantifies spore types and concentrations, and includes a scope-of-work recommendation if remediation is warranted. That documentation is formatted to meet insurance carrier requirements and, when applicable, the disclosure standards that California real estate transactions require.
