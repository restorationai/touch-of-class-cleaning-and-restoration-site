## Why Cerritos Properties See Attic Mold Issues

Most of Cerritos's single-family housing stock was built between 1965 and 1985, when builders used nominal-thickness OSB or plywood sheathing and installed soffit-and-ridge ventilation systems that were adequate for that era but degrade over decades. Blocked or collapsed soffit vents — common in homes that have had multiple re-roofing projects — trap humid air against the sheathing. Add a slow drip from an aging composition shingle field or a flashing gap around a vent pipe, and the wood surface stays above 19% moisture content long enough for *Cladosporium*, *Penicillium*, or *Stachybotrys* to colonize.

Cerritos also experiences occasional Santa Ana wind events that drive warm, dry air through attic spaces and then abruptly reverse when marine influence returns. That rapid humidity swing stresses wood grain and opens micro-cracks that hold moisture longer than smooth surfaces. Homes near the Cerritos Towne Center corridor on South Street tend to have had more commercial-adjacent landscaping irrigation running close to foundations, which can elevate whole-house relative humidity and compound attic condensation problems.

## Our Attic Mold Removal Process in Cerritos

Every job starts with a full attic inspection — not just a flashlight sweep from the hatch. A technician walks the deck (or crawls it, depending on truss design) and logs moisture readings with a pin and pinless meter across every accessible rafter bay. We photograph affected sheathing panels before touching anything, which matters when you're filing an insurance claim.

Containment comes next. Because attic access is often through a hallway or bedroom ceiling hatch, we seal the opening with poly sheeting and run negative air pressure with a HEPA-filtered air scrubber before any mechanical removal begins. This keeps spores from migrating into living spaces during the work.

For surface mold on sheathing that hasn't structurally compromised the wood, we use a combination of HEPA vacuuming, wire brushing, and an EPA-registered antimicrobial treatment applied to bare wood. Panels with deep penetration or structural softening are removed and replaced. After treatment, we re-check moisture readings — if the number isn't below 16%, we don't close the job. The attic also gets a ventilation assessment; fixing the mold without correcting the airflow problem just means the mold comes back.

Our team holds IICRC WRT certification (#70145645), and all work follows the IICRC S520 mold remediation standard — the same framework insurers and independent hygienists use when they audit completed jobs.
