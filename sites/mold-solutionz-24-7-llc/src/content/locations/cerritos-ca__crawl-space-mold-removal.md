## Why Cerritos Properties Develop Crawl Space Mold

Most of Cerritos's residential housing stock was built between the late 1960s and the early 1980s, when crawl space ventilation standards were less rigorous than they are today. Those homes — many of them in the neighborhoods surrounding the Cerritos Towne Center and along the tracts near 183rd Street — were constructed with minimal cross-ventilation and vapor barriers that have long since degraded, torn, or been punctured by pest activity.

The local soil compounds the problem. Cerritos lies on Lakewood Formation clay, which drains poorly after rain and holds ground moisture at a level that keeps crawl space relative humidity elevated well into the dry season. When the marine layer rolls in off the coast during June and July — what locals call "June Gloom" — that ambient humidity can push crawl space moisture readings above 70 percent even without any plumbing issue. At that level, mold doesn't need a burst pipe. It needs only time.

Older cast-iron drain lines common in homes built before 1975 are also a factor. Slow seeps from corroded joints go unnoticed for months because they happen below the floor, out of sight. By the time a homeowner smells something musty coming through the subfloor, mold has often spread across multiple joist bays.

## Our Crawl Space Mold Removal Process in Cerritos

Every crawl space job starts with a thorough inspection — not a visual scan from the access hatch, but a physical entry with moisture meters, a thermal camera, and an air sampling pump. Mold growth in a Cerritos crawl space is rarely limited to one spot; the low clearance and poor airflow mean spores travel. We map the full affected area before any remediation begins.

Once we understand the scope, the process follows the IICRC S520 standard for mold remediation:

1. **Containment** — Polyethylene sheeting isolates the crawl space from the living area above. Negative air pressure keeps disturbed spores from migrating into the home.
2. **Removal of compromised materials** — Degraded vapor barriers, insulation batts that have absorbed moisture, and any wood members with active surface mold are removed and bagged for proper disposal.
3. **Mechanical and chemical treatment** — Affected wood surfaces are HEPA-vacuumed, wire-brushed where needed, and treated with an EPA-registered antimicrobial agent. We do not encapsulate over active mold.
4. **Drying** — Commercial desiccant dehumidifiers and high-velocity air movers run until wood moisture content reaches equilibrium with the surrounding environment — typically below 16 percent for Southern California conditions.
5. **Vapor barrier replacement** — A new 20-mil reinforced vapor barrier is installed, sealed at seams and taped to the foundation walls, to interrupt the ground moisture cycle that fed the problem in the first place.

We hold IICRC WRT certification (#70145645), which means our technicians are trained in the psychrometrics of drying — not just in wiping surfaces.
