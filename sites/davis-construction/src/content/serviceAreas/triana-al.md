## Restoration emergencies common in Triana

Triana sits along the Tennessee River in Madison County, and that proximity shapes what goes wrong in local homes. Heavy spring storms and the occasional tropical remnant push through the Tennessee Valley and can overwhelm older drainage systems faster than homeowners expect, especially on lots closer to the river where ground saturates quickly. Humid Alabama summers mean any water intrusion, a slow roof leak, a failed supply line, a damp crawlspace, has a short window before it turns into a mold problem. Straight-line wind events tied to spring severe weather are also a regular cause of roof damage and downed limbs on older tree canopy common throughout the area.

## Building stock, site conditions, and permits in Triana

Much of Triana's housing stock is modest, single-story construction built from the 1960s through the 1990s, with a mix of slab-on-grade and crawlspace foundations. Crawlspace homes in this area are the ones we watch most closely after a leak, since moisture can sit against floor joists and sill plates for weeks before it's visible inside the house. Plumbing in homes from this era is often a mix of copper and galvanized pipe, and galvanized lines nearing the end of their service life are a common source of slow leaks that go unnoticed until a ceiling stain or a spike in the water bill shows up.

The river-adjacent soil in parts of Triana holds water longer after heavy rain, which puts extra stress on foundations and crawlspace vents. Structural repair or rebuild work generally requires a permit through Madison County, and any job touching load-bearing framing or significant electrical and plumbing work should be pulled and inspected properly rather than patched over.
