## Why Roofing in the Redstone Arsenal Area Wears Faster Than You Might Expect

The Tennessee Valley's weather pattern is the primary culprit. Summer heat in this part of Alabama regularly pushes surface temperatures on dark asphalt shingles well above ambient air temperature, accelerating granule loss and causing the underlying mat to become brittle over time. Then, between October and March, the region sees enough freeze-thaw cycling to work existing cracks wider with each cold snap. Flat or low-slope sections on older structures are especially vulnerable because standing water has nowhere to go before it freezes.

The soil around this area also matters. The clay-heavy ground common throughout Madison County shifts seasonally, and even minor foundation movement can telegraph up through exterior walls and rafter plates, creating stress points at the ridge and along the eaves. A roof that looks intact from the street may already be losing its seal at the fasteners.

Finally, the mix of residential and light commercial structures near the Arsenal includes a meaningful number of buildings from the mid-twentieth century, many with original decking that was never designed for today's heavier architectural shingles. When we pull old material during a replacement, we inspect decking condition before anything new goes down.

## Our Roofing Installation and Replacement Process

Every project starts with a thorough inspection, not a quick drive-by estimate. We walk the roof, probe the decking, check flashing at every penetration, and look at the attic side for signs of moisture intrusion or inadequate ventilation. In this climate, attic ventilation is not a footnote; an under-ventilated attic traps heat that degrades shingles from below while also driving up cooling costs.

Once we have a clear picture of what the structure needs, we walk the property owner through material options suited to North Alabama conditions. Heavier architectural shingles with strong wind ratings are the standard choice here given the region's storm exposure. For structures with low-slope sections, we discuss modified bitumen or TPO membrane systems that handle standing water better than traditional shingles ever will.

During installation, we follow manufacturer specifications for underlayment, starter strips, and nailing patterns. Shortcuts in those details are what cause early failures in high-wind events. After the field work is done, we inspect flashing at chimneys, vents, and walls, clear gutters of debris from the tear-off, and do a final ground-level and rooftop walkthrough before we consider the job closed.

## Local Note

One pattern we see consistently in structures near the Arsenal and throughout the older sections of Madison County is original wood decking that was installed in narrow boards rather than plywood sheets. That board decking can develop gaps over decades as the wood dries and shrinks, and those gaps allow wind-driven rain to enter even under intact shingles. When we encounter board decking during a tear-off, we assess whether a layer of plywood overlay is the right call before new shingles go down. It adds material cost, but it also gives the new roof a solid, continuous nailing surface and closes the gaps that have been letting moisture in. This is not something every contractor flags, but in buildings of that era it makes a real difference in how long the new roof performs.
