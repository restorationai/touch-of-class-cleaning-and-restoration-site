## Why Athens Homes Have Unique Remodeling Considerations

Limestone County's climate sits in a humid subtropical zone — hot, wet summers and mild but damp winters — and that moisture history shows up inside walls when you start opening them. Brick ranch homes built between 1950 and 1975 often have no vapor barrier between the exterior masonry and interior framing. When we demo a kitchen or bathroom in one of these homes, we routinely find insulation that has been cycling through moisture for fifty years. Addressing that before new cabinets or tile go in isn't optional; it's what separates a remodel that holds up from one that starts showing problems in three years.

The proximity to Swan Creek also matters for homes in the lower-lying parts of the city. Crawl spaces in those areas can carry persistent humidity that migrates upward into subfloors and lower cabinets. We assess crawl space conditions as part of any kitchen or bathroom scope in affected ZIP codes like 35611 before finalizing material selections — because luxury vinyl plank that performs beautifully in a dry environment can buckle in a crawl space that hasn't been encapsulated.

## Local Note: What Athens Ranch Homes Teach You Fast

One thing that surprises homeowners in established Athens neighborhoods is how often the original 1960s tile work in bathrooms is set directly on a mortar bed over the subfloor — no cement board, no modern waterproofing membrane. That mortar bed is actually quite durable, but when it does fail, water has often been sitting between the mortar and the subfloor for years before the tile shows any movement. We've learned to probe subfloor condition in these bathrooms before quoting a tile-only refresh, because what looks like a $4,000 regrout job can reveal a subfloor replacement underneath. Knowing that going in protects both the budget and the timeline.
