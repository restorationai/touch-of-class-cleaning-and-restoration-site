## Why Ardmore Roofs Wear the Way They Do

Northern Madison County's climate is the defining factor in how long a roof lasts here. Ardmore sits close enough to the Tennessee Valley that it catches the same high-humidity summers that accelerate algae growth on asphalt shingles, and the area gets enough freeze-thaw cycling in winter to work ice under improperly sealed flashing and ridge caps. Homes in this area tend to be a mix of older ranch-style construction and newer builds on larger lots, and the older stock often has original roof decking that has absorbed decades of moisture. When we pull back deteriorated shingles on a house that was built in the 1970s or 1980s, soft decking is a common finding, not a surprise.

Beyond age, the pitch and orientation of a roof matters here. South- and west-facing slopes in Ardmore take the full force of afternoon sun from May through September, which shortens shingle life noticeably compared to north-facing slopes on the same house. Ventilation is another recurring issue: attics that were adequate for the original insulation levels often become heat traps after energy upgrades, and trapped heat degrades shingles from the underside while the sun works on them from above.

## Our Roofing Installation and Replacement Process in Ardmore

Every project starts with a roof inspection that goes beyond a visual scan from the ground. We walk the surface, check the decking for soft spots, inspect the flashing at every penetration and valley, and assess the ventilation system. That inspection shapes the scope of work before any materials are ordered.

For a full replacement, the process runs in a consistent sequence:

1. Remove existing shingles and underlayment down to the deck.
2. Inspect and replace any damaged or soft decking sections.
3. Install a code-compliant underlayment appropriate for Alabama's climate zone.
4. Apply drip edge, valley flashing, and step flashing before any field shingles go down.
5. Install the shingle field, working from eave to ridge with proper exposure and fastener patterns.
6. Cap the ridge and seal all penetrations including vents, pipes, and chimneys.
7. Conduct a final walkthrough and magnetic nail sweep of the yard and driveway.

Material selection matters in this climate. Heavier architectural shingles with a Class 4 impact rating hold up better against the hail events that move through the Tennessee Valley in spring. We discuss options with each homeowner before committing to a product, because the right shingle for a shaded lot in a wooded area is not necessarily the right one for an exposed ridge-line property.
