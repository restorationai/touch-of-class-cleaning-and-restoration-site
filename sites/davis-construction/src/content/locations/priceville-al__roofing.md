## Why Priceville Properties See Roofing Problems

Morgan County's climate is the primary driver of premature roof failure in this area. Priceville sits in a zone that receives significant hail events during spring storm season, and those impacts compound over time, cracking granules, compressing mat fibers, and creating invisible pathways for water infiltration that don't show up as interior staining until months later. Summer heat cycling, where surface temperatures swing dramatically from midday to midnight, causes shingles to expand and contract repeatedly, loosening fasteners and lifting edges over a span of years.

The housing stock in and around Priceville skews toward construction from the 1990s through the 2000s boom period, meaning a significant share of roofs in the area are now approaching or past the 20-to-25-year threshold where asphalt shingles begin to lose their protective granule coating at an accelerating rate. Roofs installed during that era often used standard three-tab shingles rather than the heavier architectural products that have become the regional norm. If your home was built during that window, a full replacement is likely more cost-effective than continued patching.

## Our Roofing Installation and Replacement Process in Priceville

Every project starts with a thorough inspection of the existing deck, not just the visible shingle surface. In Alabama's humid climate, the OSB or plywood decking beneath a failing roof can absorb moisture for years before the damage is obvious from the attic side. We check for soft spots, delamination, and any areas where previous repairs have trapped moisture against the deck.

Once the scope is clear, we handle material selection with the local environment in mind. For most Priceville homes, we recommend architectural shingles rated for high-wind uplift, given the severe weather patterns common to the Tennessee Valley region. Proper underlayment and ice-and-water shield at eaves and valleys are non-negotiable in this climate, even though hard freezes are infrequent, because the combination of wind-driven rain and temperature swings creates the same infiltration risk as ice damming does further north.

Installation follows a sequenced process: full tear-off of existing material, deck inspection and repair, underlayment, flashing at all penetrations and transitions, and then shingle installation starting at the eaves. We do not layer new shingles over old ones. The additional weight and the inability to inspect the deck are both problems that compound over time, and in Morgan County's storm environment, a compromised deck is a liability.

## Local Note

One pattern worth knowing about in the Priceville and broader Morgan County area: homes built on slab foundations with low-pitch rooflines, common in subdivisions developed during the 1990s, tend to have shorter roof-to-wall transitions that make proper flashing installation tricky. Contractors who are not familiar with this construction style sometimes underflash the drip edge at the eaves, which allows wind-driven rain to wick back under the starter course. It is a subtle detail that does not cause problems in a mild rain but fails visibly during the kind of sustained sideways rainfall that a Gulf-fed storm system delivers to North Alabama. We account for this during installation on every low-pitch roof in the area.
