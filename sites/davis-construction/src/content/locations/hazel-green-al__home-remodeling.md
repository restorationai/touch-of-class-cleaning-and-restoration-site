## Why Hazel Green Homes Present Unique Remodeling Conditions

The soil profile across much of northern Madison County runs toward expansive clay, the kind that swells after a wet spring and contracts during a dry August. For older ranch-style homes common to the Hazel Green area, that seasonal movement shows up as sticking doors, hairline cracks along drywall seams, and subfloor gaps that widen and close with the calendar. Before any interior remodeling begins, a thorough assessment of the foundation and floor system is essential, not optional. Skipping that step means finishing a kitchen or bathroom over a floor that will continue to move.

Hazel Green also sits close enough to the Tennessee border that homes built in the 1970s and 1980s may reflect older Alabama building codes that have since been updated. Electrical panels from that era frequently need evaluation before a whole home remodel can proceed, particularly if the scope includes adding circuits for a modern kitchen, a home office, or a primary suite addition. Knowing what you are opening up before the first wall comes down saves time and avoids mid-project surprises.

## Local Note

One thing that comes up repeatedly on Hazel Green remodels is the age of the vapor barrier in crawl space homes. The humidity in this part of Madison County is persistent, and older poly sheeting, especially anything installed before the mid-1990s, tends to degrade and gap over time. When we open up a floor for a remodel and find a compromised vapor barrier underneath, we address it as part of the project rather than covering it back up. A finished interior sitting above an uncontrolled moisture source will show problems within a few years, usually through buckled flooring or musty odors that are hard to trace. Catching it during a remodel is far less disruptive than chasing it afterward.
