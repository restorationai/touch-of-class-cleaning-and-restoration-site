## Why Triana Homes Present Distinct Remodeling Challenges

The Tennessee River floodplain that borders Triana creates a high-moisture environment that affects homes from the foundation up. Crawl space encapsulation, vapor barriers, and proper subfloor ventilation are not optional upgrades here, they are the baseline that makes any interior remodel last. Homes built in the mid-twentieth century, which make up a significant portion of Triana's residential stock, often used dimensional lumber and construction methods that have held up well but now require careful evaluation before walls are opened or load-bearing elements are touched.

The clay-heavy soils common throughout this part of Madison County expand and contract with rainfall cycles, which means pier-and-beam foundations and slab edges can shift incrementally over decades. Before we finalize a remodeling plan for any Triana home, we assess how that movement has expressed itself: doors that no longer hang square, tile grout that has cracked along consistent lines, or gaps at window frames that weren't there ten years ago. Addressing those underlying conditions during a remodel, rather than covering them with new finishes, is what separates a renovation that lasts from one that shows problems within a few years.

Homes in the Triana area that were built close to the river often have pier foundations that were set when the water table was a primary engineering concern.
