## Why Hartselle Properties See Roofing Problems

Morgan County's climate is hard on roofing in a particular way: the combination of high summer UV exposure and periodic freeze-thaw cycles in winter accelerates the granule loss on asphalt shingles faster than it would in a more temperate zone. Homes built in the 1960s and 1970s, which make up a significant portion of Hartselle's residential stock, often have original roof decking that was never designed to be re-covered more than once. When that decking is soft or the sheathing has absorbed years of attic humidity, a surface-level shingle replacement won't solve the underlying problem. A proper assessment looks at the decking condition, the ventilation setup, and the flashing around any penetrations before a single shingle goes down.

Hartselle also sits close enough to the Tennessee River valley that wind-driven rain events can push water up under low-slope roof sections in ways that flat-rate installation crews sometimes underestimate. Proper drip edge installation and step flashing at wall intersections matter more here than in drier climates.

## Our Roofing Installation and Replacement Process in Hartselle

Every project starts with a roof inspection that goes beyond a visual scan from the ground. The crew checks decking integrity, measures the slope, identifies any existing ventilation gaps, and photographs the current flashing condition. That documentation shapes the material recommendation and the scope of work before any contract is signed.

For full replacements, the process follows a consistent sequence: tear-off of existing layers down to the deck, deck repair where needed, installation of a self-adhering underlayment at the eaves and valleys, synthetic felt over the field, and then the finish material. In Hartselle's climate, a high-wind-rated shingle with a Class 4 impact resistance rating is worth the conversation, particularly for homes that sit on open lots with less tree cover to buffer storm debris.

Flashing is replaced, not reused. Reusing old metal flashing around chimneys and pipe boots is one of the most common reasons a new roof develops leaks within a few years of installation.

Hartselle homes built in the 1950s through the early 1970s frequently have low-pitch roof sections over rear additions or covered porches that were added without permits and without the underlayment standards that would be required today. If your home has one of these low-slope additions, it's worth having it evaluated separately from the main roof, because the material and flashing requirements are different and the failure modes are different too.
