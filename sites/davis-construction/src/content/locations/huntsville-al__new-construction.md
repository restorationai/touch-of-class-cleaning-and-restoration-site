## Why Building New in Huntsville Requires Local Know-How

Huntsville sits in a transitional climate zone that most national home-building guides underestimate. Winters are mild enough that builders sometimes skip frost-depth precautions — a mistake, because the Tennessee Valley does see hard freezes, and footings poured too shallow will shift. Spring is the bigger story: April and May bring the region's most serious tornado and hail risk, which means construction scheduling, temporary weatherproofing, and roof material selection all need to account for severe weather windows.

The soil across Huntsville varies more than you'd expect across a single ZIP code. In the 35802 corridor toward South Huntsville, you'll encounter more clay-heavy subsoil that expands and contracts with moisture, putting real stress on slab foundations if drainage isn't engineered carefully from day one. Closer to Monte Sano's slopes, rock ledge sits surprisingly close to the surface, which affects excavation costs and foundation type selection. These aren't surprises we discover mid-project — they're factors we price and plan around upfront.

## Local Note: Huntsville's Tornado Season and Construction Timing

Anyone who has built in the Tennessee Valley long enough learns to watch the April–May severe weather window carefully. Framing and roofing stages are the most vulnerable — an open structure with no sheathing or roofing can sustain significant damage from a hail event or straight-line winds. We build weather contingency days into every Huntsville project schedule during spring months, and we use temporary weatherproofing on framed structures when a significant storm system is in the forecast. It adds a small cost but prevents the kind of delays and material losses that can push a completion date back by weeks. Clients building near the Monte Sano State Park ridge line should also know that wind exposure is meaningfully higher at elevation — something worth discussing with your framing crew and your insurance carrier before the build starts.
