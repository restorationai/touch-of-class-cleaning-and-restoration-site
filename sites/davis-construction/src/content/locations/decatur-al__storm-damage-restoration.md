**Storm damage in Decatur can go from a few lifted shingles to water pouring into your attic in the time it takes a front to cross Wheeler Lake.** Call now for emergency service if wind, hail, or a downed limb has opened up your roof or siding. Decatur sits in the stretch of north Alabama storm spotters call Dixie Alley, where spring squall lines and summer microbursts roll off the Tennessee River corridor fast and often with little warning, and the roofs and siding of Morgan County take the brunt of it.

## Why Decatur Properties See Storm Damage So Often

The weather pattern here is the main driver. Warm, humid air sitting over the Tennessee Valley collides with fronts pushing down from the Appalachians, and the result is straight-line wind events and hail cells that build quickly and hit hard. A lot of Decatur's housing stock, brick ranch homes from the postwar boom years alongside newer vinyl-sided subdivisions on the city's edges, was built with shallow-pitch roof lines that are efficient in heat but give wind-driven rain more surface area to work under the shingle line. Add in mature hardwoods throughout older sections of town, and limb strikes on roofs and gutters are a routine call for us after any significant line of storms moves through Morgan County.

## Our Storm Damage Restoration Process

We start with a roof and exterior assessment, documenting wind-lifted or missing shingles, cracked siding panels, soffit and fascia damage, and any point where water has already tracked into the attic or wall cavities. If the roof deck is exposed, we tarp and board immediately to stop secondary water damage before it compounds, because a few hours of unprotected exposure during a Tennessee Valley thunderstorm can turn a repair into a full rebuild. From there we scope the structural repair, match siding and roofing materials to what's already on the house where possible, and sequence the rebuild so the exterior envelope is closed before we address any interior drywall, insulation, or flooring affected by water intrusion.

## Decatur Insurance Coordination

Most storm damage claims in this area follow a familiar pattern: a named event or a documented severe weather system triggers a wave of claims across Morgan County at once, which means adjusters are often stretched thin and homeowners are competing for inspection slots. We photograph and document damage at the time of our assessment, not just after repairs begin, so there's a clear record if an adjuster can't get out for several days. We also write repair estimates in the same line-item format most carriers use, which tends to move approvals faster than a generic contractor bid.

## Local note

Homes closer to the river bottoms and lower-lying stretches of Decatur tend to see roof and siding damage paired with yard drainage problems after the same storm, because the ground is often already saturated from Tennessee River humidity before a severe system even arrives. When we scope a storm repair in those areas, we check gutter discharge and grading at the same time as the roof, since a repair that ignores where the water goes after it leaves the roofline tends to come back as a second claim within a season.
