Arab, Alabama sits in the southern reaches of Marshall County where summer heat builds fast, afternoon thunderstorms roll through with little warning, and winters occasionally deliver ice loads that stress roofing systems well past their design limits. That combination of thermal cycling, wind-driven rain, and periodic freeze-thaw activity means roofs in Arab age differently than they do in flatter, drier parts of the state. Davis Construction Contractors has been working across North Alabama since 2008, and we understand what that specific climate pattern does to shingles, underlayment, and flashing over time.

## Why Arab Properties See Accelerated Roofing Wear

Marshall County's terrain plays a bigger role in roof longevity than most homeowners realize. Arab's elevation and the ridge-and-valley topography surrounding the city funnel wind in ways that create uplift pressure on the leading edges of roofs, particularly on homes with steeper pitches or wide overhangs. During storm season, that uplift is where shingle tabs first start to lift and lose adhesion.

The heat is the other factor. Attic temperatures in Arab homes can climb well above ambient air temperature on a July afternoon, and that sustained heat accelerates the oxidation of asphalt shingles from the underside. Granule loss follows, and once granules shed, UV exposure compounds the damage quickly. Homes that sit on south- or southwest-facing slopes, which are common throughout the Arab area, tend to see this pattern earlier than north-facing properties.

Older housing stock in and around Arab also presents a layer consideration. Homes built in the 1970s and 1980s frequently have a single layer of original shingles under a re-roof layer added in the 1990s. When we inspect those roofs before installation, we assess whether the deck beneath is still structurally sound, because adding a third layer is not permitted under current Alabama residential code and the underlying decking sometimes shows moisture intrusion that was never addressed.

## Our Roofing Installation and Replacement Process in Arab

Every project begins with a thorough inspection of the existing roof system, not just the shingles. We check the decking for soft spots, assess the condition of ridge vents and soffit ventilation, and document flashing at every penetration point, chimneys, pipe boots, skylights, and valleys. In Arab's climate, improper flashing is consistently where water finds its first path inside.

Once the scope is established, we remove existing material down to the deck, replace any damaged sheathing, and install a self-adhering ice-and-water shield at the eaves and in the valleys before laying synthetic underlayment across the field of the roof. That layering sequence matters in a region that sees both summer downpours and occasional winter ice events. We then install the chosen shingle system, cut and seal all penetrations, and finish with proper ridge cap installation to allow the attic to breathe.

We walk every completed roof before we leave the site and photograph the finished work for your records. Cleanup includes a magnetic sweep of the yard for fasteners.

## Local Note

One pattern we see specifically in Arab and the surrounding Marshall County hill communities: homes built on sloped lots often have a shorter roof section on the uphill side and a much longer run on the downhill face. That asymmetry means the downhill section carries significantly more water volume during heavy rain than a standard calculation would suggest. If your gutters on the downhill side are undersized or your downspout placement was never adjusted for that run length, water backs up at the eave and works under the starter course over time. When we replace a roof on that type of lot, we flag the gutter sizing as part of the project conversation, because a new roof over an overwhelmed drainage system will show premature edge wear within a few years.
