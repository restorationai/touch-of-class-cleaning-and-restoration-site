## Why New Market Properties See Roofing Problems

The Tennessee Valley climate is harder on roofing systems than many homeowners realize. Summer surface temperatures on a dark asphalt shingle can exceed 150 degrees Fahrenheit during the long Alabama stretch from May through September, accelerating the oxidation that makes shingles brittle and prone to cracking. Then, when a fast-moving thunderstorm drops golf-ball hail or sustained 60-mph gusts, those already-stressed shingles fail at the tab rather than flexing.

New Market's housing stock adds another layer of complexity. Many homes in the area were built during the suburban expansion of the 1970s through the 1990s, when 20-year three-tab shingles were the standard. Those roofs are well past their design life, and the original decking underneath may have absorbed enough seasonal moisture over the decades that it needs replacement before new shingles go down. Skipping a thorough deck inspection at the start of a re-roof is one of the most common reasons a new roof underperforms.

## Our Roofing Installation and Replacement Process in New Market

Every project starts with a full roof inspection: we walk the surface, probe the decking for soft spots, check ridge vents and valley flashing, and photograph everything before a single shingle comes off. That documentation matters for insurance claims and gives you a clear picture of what you are actually paying for.

Once the scope is agreed on, tear-off and disposal happen in a single mobilization when the project size allows. New decking goes down where needed, followed by a peel-and-stick ice-and-water barrier along eaves and in valleys. In Alabama, that barrier is not just a northern-climate precaution: wind-driven rain during summer storms can push water back up a low-slope section faster than gravity moves it down. Synthetic underlayment covers the field, then shingles are installed to manufacturer specifications with the correct nail pattern and exposure. Flashings at chimneys, skylights, and wall intersections are the last line of defense against leaks, so we replace metal flashings rather than re-use old ones whenever the project is a full replacement.

Final walkthrough includes a magnet sweep of the yard for roofing nails, gutter inspection, and a review of the completed work with you before we leave the site.

## Local Note

One thing worth knowing if your home sits on a lot with mature hardwoods: New Market's tree canopy is dense in many residential areas, and shaded roof sections stay damp longer after rain than open sections do. That persistent moisture accelerates algae and moss growth on the north-facing slopes, which is often mistaken for normal aging. When we inspect a roof in a heavily wooded setting, we pay close attention to those shaded areas because the granule loss there tends to run ahead of the sun-exposed sections by several years. Zinc or copper-treated shingles are worth discussing if your site has significant canopy cover.
