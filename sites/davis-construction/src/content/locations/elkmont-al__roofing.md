That combination puts roofs here under a kind of stress that a contractor who only works in flatter, drier parts of the state might underestimate. ## Why Elkmont Roofs Face Particular Wear

Limestone County sits in a transitional climate zone where summer heat and humidity push asphalt shingles through repeated expansion and contraction cycles. By late August, surface granule loss on a roof that's ten or more years old is often visible from the ground. Then, between November and February, overnight temperatures can drop below freezing while afternoons stay mild enough to melt any ice that formed, creating the slow freeze-thaw action that lifts flashing, widens seams, and eventually lets water migrate under the underlayment.

Older homes in the Elkmont area, particularly those built in the mid-twentieth century, frequently have roof decks made of board sheathing rather than plywood or OSB. That original lumber can be sound for decades, but once moisture gets under failing shingles, the boards absorb and release water unevenly, which accelerates sheathing rot in ways that aren't always obvious until a replacement crew pulls the old material. Identifying that before the new roof goes on is part of what separates a thorough installation from one that just looks right on day one.

High-wind events are also a real factor in this part of Alabama. The Tennessee Valley's geography can funnel and intensify storm winds, and even a storm that doesn't make the news can lift tab shingles, crack ridge caps, or push water horizontally under low-slope sections. When that kind of damage goes unaddressed, the next heavy rain finds every gap.

## Our Roofing Installation and Replacement Process in Elkmont

Every project starts with a thorough inspection of the existing roof system, not just the shingles but the decking, flashing, ridge, soffits, and any penetrations like vents or chimneys. We document what we find before a single piece of material comes off the house.

Once we've reviewed the scope with the homeowner, we schedule the tear-off and installation in a sequence that keeps the structure protected. In Elkmont, where afternoon thunderstorms can appear with little warning from May through September, we pay close attention to the daily forecast and stage work accordingly. We don't leave a stripped deck exposed overnight if rain is in the forecast.

New underlayment goes down before any shingles, and flashing is set at every wall intersection, valley, and penetration. Ridge ventilation is evaluated on every job, because attic heat buildup in an Alabama summer shortens shingle life measurably. If the existing ventilation is inadequate, we address it as part of the installation rather than leaving it as a future problem.

Final cleanup includes a magnetic sweep of the yard and driveway for fasteners, which matters especially on properties where children or pets use the outdoor space.

## Local Note

One thing worth knowing if you're replacing a roof on an older Elkmont home: board-sheathed decks common in houses built before the 1970s often have gaps between the boards that, when covered with modern synthetic underlayment, can telegraph as slight surface irregularities in the finished shingles. It's not a defect in the new material; it's the old deck showing through. In some cases, installing a layer of thin OSB over the original boards before laying underlayment produces a flatter, cleaner finished surface and adds a meaningful layer of weather resistance. It's an option worth discussing during the inspection phase rather than after the shingles are already down.
