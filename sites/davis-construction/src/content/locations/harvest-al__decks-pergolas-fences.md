## Why Harvest's Climate and Soil Make Outdoor Structures a Specialized Build

North Alabama's humidity doesn't just affect the finished product. It affects the build itself. Lumber delivered to a job site in Harvest in July arrives at a different moisture content than the same board would in, say, October, and a contractor who doesn't account for that will see gaps open up in decking boards as the wood dries and contracts through the fall. The clay soils common throughout this part of Madison County also expand and contract significantly with seasonal moisture changes, which means post footings need to be set deeper and with more attention to drainage than a standard regional spec might call for.

Fencing presents its own challenge here. Privacy fences in Harvest neighborhoods are popular, and they're also the structure most exposed to wind load during the severe thunderstorms that move through the Tennessee Valley from spring through early fall. A fence that's set too shallow or framed without adequate bracing at the posts can lean or fail after a single strong storm. We design for that load from the start, not as an afterthought.
