## Ground-Up Construction and Harvest's Climate Demands

North Alabama's climate puts specific demands on new residential construction. Hot, humid summers mean that moisture management in the building envelope is not optional, it is a design requirement. Improperly detailed wall assemblies in this climate can trap humidity inside the structure, leading to long-term performance problems. We specify materials and details suited to mixed-humid conditions, including vapor management strategies appropriate for the Madison County climate zone.

Winter in Harvest brings enough freeze-thaw cycling to matter for concrete work. We monitor weather windows for foundation pours and flatwork, because concrete placed in marginal conditions does not cure the same way it does under ideal temperatures. That kind of scheduling discipline is easy to skip when a project is behind, and it is exactly where corners get cut on jobs we sometimes get called in to evaluate.

## Local Note

One thing that catches some builders off guard in the Harvest area is the variability in lot topography even within the same subdivision. It is not uncommon to find that adjacent lots in a newer development have meaningfully different drainage characteristics because grading during the subdivision phase was done to meet minimum requirements rather than to optimize individual lot performance. When we evaluate a Harvest site, we look at how water moves across the whole block, not just the parcel lines, because a home's long-term dryness depends on what happens on the neighbors' lots too.
