## Why Huntsville Properties See Mold Problems

Huntsville's building history creates two very different mold risk profiles. In the Twickenham Historic District, homes built between the 1920s and 1950s rely on solid-masonry construction — brick and mortar walls that absorb ground moisture and wick it inward over decades. Crawl spaces under these older homes are often unvented or poorly vented, and the combination of clay-heavy Madison County soil and seasonal rainfall means standing moisture under the floor structure is common after wet winters. Mold in these properties frequently colonizes subfloor joists and the underside of hardwood flooring before anyone notices a smell.

On the other end of the spectrum, newer Hampton Cove subdivisions were built fast during Huntsville's defense and tech boom, and some of that construction left gaps around HVAC penetrations and window rough openings that allow condensation to pool inside wall cavities. Hampton Cove's HOA architectural review requirements also mean exterior remediation work — replacing sheathing, repairing stucco, repainting — has to go through an approval process before work begins. We've handled enough jobs in that area to know which HOA management companies to contact first, which prevents delays.

Huntsville's April–May tornado and severe hail season adds another layer: storm-damaged roofing that goes unrepaired for even a few weeks during humid summer months is a reliable mold incubator. We see a predictable surge in remediation calls every June and July from storm damage that sat through the spring.

## Our Mold Remediation Process in Huntsville

Every job starts with a thorough moisture mapping inspection — thermal imaging and pin-type moisture meters to find wet material behind surfaces that look dry. We don't guess at the boundary of contamination; we measure it. Once the scope is defined, containment goes up: heavy-gauge poly barriers, negative air pressure with HEPA-filtered air scrubbers, and sealed HVAC registers to prevent spore migration to unaffected rooms.

Physical removal of contaminated material follows — drywall, insulation, subflooring, or framing, depending on what the moisture readings show. We treat remaining structural surfaces with EPA-registered antimicrobial agents, then run commercial dehumidification and air movement equipment until the wood moisture content reads within normal range for Huntsville's climate (typically targeting below 16% in summer conditions). Before we close any wall cavity, we document final moisture readings. Reconstruction — drywall, insulation, paint — is handled in-house, so you're not coordinating a second contractor.

## Local Note

In Twickenham and the older Five Points neighborhoods, the solid-brick exterior walls hold moisture differently than modern framed construction. When mold remediation requires opening an interior wall cavity in these homes, the masonry behind it is often still damp even weeks after the original water event — the brick releases moisture slowly, and drying times run significantly longer than in a standard stick-frame house. We account for this by extending dehumidification cycles and taking additional moisture readings before closing walls, which prevents callbacks and keeps the remediation from failing clearance.
