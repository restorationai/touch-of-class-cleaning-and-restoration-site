## Why Harvest Homes Often Need More Than a Cosmetic Update

North Alabama's climate puts real stress on residential interiors. Hot, humid summers drive moisture into wall cavities, and the temperature swings between January and July are wide enough to work caulk joints loose and cause wood trim to cycle through expansion and contraction year after year. In Harvest specifically, the rapid subdivision buildout of the 2000s meant that many homes used materials that met code at the time but are now showing their age in ways that go deeper than paint. Popcorn ceilings, hollow-core interior doors, single-pane window surrounds, and vinyl flooring glued directly over concrete slabs are common findings once a remodel gets underway.

Beyond materials, the slab-on-grade construction that dominates Madison County's newer subdivisions creates specific considerations for any project that touches plumbing or flooring. Running new drain lines or repositioning a bathroom requires concrete cutting, not just subfloor work, which affects both the project timeline and the dust management plan inside the home.

Most of Harvest falls under Madison County jurisdiction for building permits, which means projects that alter the footprint, move walls, or change the roofline require county review before work begins. Many Harvest subdivisions also have active homeowners associations with architectural review requirements for exterior changes, additions, and even some visible interior alterations like window replacements.
