## Why Decatur Homes Often Need More Than a Cosmetic Update

Decatur's housing stock tells a layered story. Many homes built between the 1940s and 1970s used construction methods and materials that were standard at the time but create real challenges during remodeling: knob-and-tube or early aluminum wiring, galvanized steel supply lines, single-pane windows set in frames that have shifted with decades of seasonal movement, and subfloors laid over pier-and-beam foundations that may have settled unevenly. The Tennessee River's proximity also means that crawl spaces in lower-lying parts of the city deal with persistent humidity, which works its way into floor systems and wall cavities long before a homeowner notices soft spots underfoot.

A remodel here isn't always just about choosing new cabinets or opening up a floor plan. It often means uncovering what the original builders left behind and deciding what needs to come out before the new work goes in. That's a different conversation than you'd have in a newer subdivision, and it's one we're used to having honestly.

## Local Note: What Pier-and-Beam Foundations Mean for Your Remodel

A detail worth knowing if you're planning interior remodeling in an older Decatur home: pier-and-beam foundations are common across the city, and they behave differently than a poured slab when you're moving walls or adding weight. Before any load-bearing change, we assess the existing beam and post layout in the crawl space. In homes near the river or in areas with higher soil moisture, we also look for signs of post deterioration or settling that would need to be addressed before the interior work begins. Skipping that step is how a kitchen remodel turns into a much larger project six months later.
