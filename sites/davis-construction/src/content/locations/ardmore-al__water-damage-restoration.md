**Water damage emergency in Ardmore?** Call now for emergency service and get a crew assessing the damage before standing water has a chance to wick into drywall, subfloor, and framing. Ardmore sits on the Alabama-Tennessee state line in Limestone County, where older farmhouses, brick ranch homes, and newer construction all share the same enemy: red clay soil that drains slowly and holds moisture against foundations long after a storm has passed.

## Why Ardmore Properties See Water Damage Issues

Much of Ardmore's housing stock was built decades before modern grading standards, and many lots were never regraded when additions or outbuildings went up later. That means water from a heavy rain or a failed gutter often has nowhere to go but back toward the foundation. Combine that with the area's mix of well water and municipal lines feeding older galvanized plumbing, and pipe failures behind walls or under slab are common. When a pipe lets go in a house built in the 1960s or 70s, the water usually finds plaster or early drywall, and it absorbs and spreads differently than the moisture-resistant board used in newer builds.

## Our Water Damage Restoration Process in Ardmore

The first step is always extraction, pulling standing water off floors and out of carpet pad before it migrates further. From there we moisture-map the affected rooms, checking walls, baseboards, and subfloor with meters rather than guessing based on what looks dry on the surface. Drywall and ceiling water damage repair almost always means cutting back to sound material, because saturated gypsum board loses its structural integrity even after it dries. Drying itself follows the IICRC S500 standard for structural drying, which sets target moisture levels for wood framing and subfloor before we sign off on any rebuild work. Only after those numbers are hit do we move into drywall water damage repair, ceiling water damage repair, and the broader post-leak renovation, matching texture, paint, and trim so the repaired section doesn't stand out from the rest of the room.

## Local note

A lot of the older homes we see in and around Ardmore were built with plaster-and-lath ceilings or early drywall installed directly over furring strips with little to no vapor barrier. When a roof leak or upstairs pipe failure soaks that ceiling, it tends to sag and hold water in pockets rather than showing an even stain, which means the visible wet spot is often smaller than the actual area of saturation above it. We check well beyond the stain line before calling a ceiling section sound.
