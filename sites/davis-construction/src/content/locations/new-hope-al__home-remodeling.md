## Why New Hope Homes Often Need More Than a Cosmetic Update

A significant portion of the residential housing in and around New Hope was built during the post-war expansion through the 1970s and into the 1980s, when construction standards, insulation requirements, and electrical codes looked very different than they do today. Homes from that era frequently have undersized electrical panels that can't support modern appliances, galvanized plumbing that has narrowed with mineral buildup over decades, and wall cavities with little or no insulation by today's standards.

Madison County's clay-heavy soil also plays a role. Seasonal moisture swings cause that soil to expand and contract, which over time can shift foundations subtly, rack door frames out of square, and crack tile or drywall at corners. A remodel that ignores what's happening behind the walls is a remodel that will need redoing sooner than it should. We assess the bones of a home before finalizing any scope of work, so the finished product isn't just beautiful but structurally sound.

## Local Note

Homes in the New Hope area that were built before the mid-1980s frequently have original aluminum wiring in branch circuits, a detail that becomes relevant the moment a kitchen or bathroom remodel opens up walls. Aluminum wiring isn't automatically a code violation, but it does require specific connectors and outlets rated for aluminum, and many electricians unfamiliar with the local housing stock overlook it. We flag this during our initial walkthrough and coordinate with a licensed electrician to address it properly before drywall goes back up, which saves homeowners from a failed inspection or a callback after the project is complete.
