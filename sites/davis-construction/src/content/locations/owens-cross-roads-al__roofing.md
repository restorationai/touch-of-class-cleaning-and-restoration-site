## Why Owens Cross Roads Properties See Roofing Problems

The Tennessee Valley's climate creates a specific set of roofing challenges. Summer surface temperatures on dark asphalt shingles in this region can exceed 150 degrees, which accelerates granule loss and causes shingles to cup or curl at the edges over time. Then, when a fast-moving storm drops two inches of rain in forty minutes, those compromised shingles are the first thing standing between your attic and a water intrusion problem.

Beyond heat and rain, the freeze-thaw cycles that arrive in January and February matter more than most homeowners expect. Ice damming is less common here than in northern states, but a cold snap after a warm stretch can push water under lifted shingle edges and into the decking below. Homes in the Owens Cross Roads area that were built in the 1980s and 1990s often have decking that's reached the end of its serviceable life alongside the shingles, which means a full replacement is the right call rather than a layer-over.
