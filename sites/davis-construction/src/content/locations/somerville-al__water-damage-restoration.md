## Why Somerville Properties See Water Damage Issues

Much of Somerville's housing stock was built before modern vapor barrier standards became common, which means crawlspaces under many homes sit closer to grade than you'd see in newer subdivisions. Combine that with the clay-heavy soil typical of this part of Morgan County, and you get slow-draining ground that holds water against foundations long after a storm passes. That moisture works its way up through subfloors and into baseboards and drywall before anyone notices a problem. Add in the region's humid summers, and a leak that would dry out in a drier climate instead sits there feeding mold growth within 24 to 48 hours. Older galvanized plumbing, still present in a fair number of Somerville homes, is also prone to pinhole leaks that go undetected inside wall cavities for weeks before a stain finally shows up on a ceiling.

## Our Water Damage Restoration Process in Somerville

We start with extraction, pulling standing water out before it has a chance to wick further into flooring and framing. From there we use moisture meters to map how far the water has traveled, because in a lot of Somerville's older homes the visible wet drywall is only part of the story, the insulation and framing behind it can stay saturated much longer. Once the structure is drying, we move into the repair phase: cutting out and replacing damaged drywall, addressing any ceiling sections that have sagged or stained, and matching texture so the patch doesn't stand out. For jobs where the water damage goes deeper, we handle the full rebuild, from framing repairs to flooring replacement, so you're not coordinating separate contractors for drying and reconstruction.

## Local note

A lot of Somerville's rural and semi-rural properties sit on septic systems rather than municipal sewer, which matters more than people expect when a water damage event involves a backed-up drain line near the house. We check septic proximity and grading before we set up drying equipment outdoors, because running fans and dehumidifiers too close to a saturated drain field can slow things down rather than help.
