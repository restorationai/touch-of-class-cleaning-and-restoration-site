## Why New Market Homes Have Their Own Remodeling Story

Much of the residential housing stock in the New Market area developed during periods of rapid suburban growth along the U.S. 72 corridor, which means you'll find a wide mix of construction vintages on the same street. Ranch-style homes from the 1970s and 1980s sit alongside newer builds, and each era brings its own quirks. Older homes in this part of Madison County frequently have original single-pane windows that drive up energy costs, undersized electrical panels that can't support a modern kitchen or home office, and crawl space insulation that has compressed or failed over years of seasonal humidity swings.

The clay soil common throughout this region expands and contracts with rainfall cycles, and that movement telegraphs into slab edges, door frames, and tile work over time. A remodel that ignores what the soil is doing will need to be redone sooner than it should. ## Local Note

One thing worth knowing about remodeling homes in the New Market area: the combination of clay soil and pier-and-beam or crawl space foundations means door and window rough openings are often slightly out of square by the time a home is a few decades old. This isn't a defect, it's normal movement for this soil type and climate, but it affects how finish carpentry gets installed. Trim that's cut square to a blueprint looks wrong against a frame that has racked a quarter inch over thirty years. Experienced crews account for this during installation rather than fighting the opening. It's a small thing that separates a remodel that looks right from one that looks like it was built in a different house.
