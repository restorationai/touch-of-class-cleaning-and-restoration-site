Gurley sits in the foothills of northeastern Madison County, where the terrain shifts quickly from flat valley floor to rolling clay-heavy ridgelines, and that geology shapes every foundation decision a new home builder has to make before a single framing nail goes in. Davis Construction Contractors has been building ground-up residential projects across this corner of Alabama since 2008, and the work here in Gurley demands a different kind of site preparation than you'd find on the sandier soils closer to Huntsville. If you're ready to build a house on a lot in this area, the details below explain exactly how we approach it.

## Why Building in Gurley Calls for Site-Specific Planning

The clay soils common throughout this part of Madison County expand when wet and contract sharply during dry stretches. That cycle puts real stress on slab foundations if the subgrade isn't properly stabilized and compacted before the pour. New home construction in Gurley also has to account for the area's position in a region that sees occasional severe weather, including high winds and heavy rainfall events that can saturate a freshly graded lot in hours. Proper drainage planning, including swales and strategic lot grading, isn't optional here, it's the difference between a foundation that performs for decades and one that starts showing settlement cracks within a few years.

Gurley's rural character also means many parcels are on well and septic rather than municipal utilities. Coordinating with Madison County Environmental Health for septic permitting adds a step that buyers coming from more urbanized areas sometimes don't anticipate. We walk every client through that process from the start so there are no surprises during the permit phase.

## Our New Home Construction Process in Gurley

Every project starts with a site visit, not a phone estimate. We walk the lot, assess slope and drainage patterns, confirm setback requirements with Madison County, and identify any utility coordination needed before design is finalized. From there, the process moves in a clear sequence:

**Site prep and foundation:** Clearing, grading, and compaction testing come before any concrete is poured. On clay-heavy lots, we may recommend a thickened-edge slab or pier-and-beam system depending on what the soil assessment shows.

**Framing and rough-ins:** Structural framing goes up once the foundation has cured and passed inspection. Electrical, plumbing, and HVAC rough-ins follow, all coordinated to minimize re-work before drywall.

**Inspections and close-in:** Madison County inspections are scheduled at each required phase. We don't skip ahead, every inspection sign-off is documented before the next phase begins.

**Finish work and final walkthrough:** Interior finishes, exterior cladding, landscaping grading, and a final punch-list walkthrough with the homeowner before keys are handed over.

Timelines vary by square footage and finish level, but a typical single-family build in this area runs several months from permit issuance to certificate of occupancy. We give clients a project schedule at contract signing and update it whenever something changes.

## Navigating Permits and Approvals in Madison County

Residential construction in Gurley falls under Madison County's building department jurisdiction. Permit applications require engineered drawings for most new builds, and the county's review timeline can affect your overall project schedule. Septic system design and approval through Madison County Environmental Health runs on a parallel track and needs to be initiated early, especially on larger lots where perc testing is required before the system can be sized and permitted.

If your lot is in a subdivision with deed restrictions or a homeowners association, those covenants may set standards for exterior materials, roof pitch, garage orientation, or minimum square footage. We review any known deed restrictions before finalizing plans so that design choices don't create approval delays later.

## Local Note

One thing that catches some buyers off guard when building in the Gurley area: the elevation change across a single lot can be more dramatic than it looks on a plat map. What appears to be a gently sloping parcel on paper sometimes has a significant grade break mid-lot that affects where the house can sit, how the driveway runs, and where the septic field can be located without conflicting with setbacks. We flag this during the initial site visit because catching it early keeps the design process on track and avoids costly redesigns after engineering is already underway.
