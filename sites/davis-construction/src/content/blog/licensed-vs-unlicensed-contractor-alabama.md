**TL;DR:** In Alabama, general contractors must be licensed by the state for any project valued at $50,000 or more. Residential builders have a separate license through the Home Builders Licensure Board. Hiring an unlicensed contractor on a qualifying project can void your homeowner's insurance coverage, expose you to mechanic's liens from unpaid subcontractors, and leave you with no legal recourse if the work fails. Verification takes about two minutes on the state's public license lookup tool.

You've got three bids on your kitchen remodel or new addition, and one of them is noticeably lower. Before you sign anything, the first question isn't about the price, it's about whether the contractor is legally allowed to do the work. In Alabama, that answer lives in two state databases, and checking them takes less time than reading this paragraph. Here's what you need to know.

## Do contractors in Alabama need a license?

Yes, Alabama has two separate licensing systems depending on the type and size of the project. The [Alabama Licensing Board for General Contractors](https://genbd.alabama.gov/) covers commercial and residential projects with a contract value of $50,000 or more. The [Home Builders Licensure Board (HBLB)](https://www.hblb.alabama.gov/) covers residential new construction and remodeling regardless of dollar amount, any contractor building or substantially renovating a home in Alabama needs an HBLB license.

Those two boards cover most of what homeowners hire for. A contractor doing a $30,000 bathroom remodel doesn't need a General Contractors Board license, but they do need an HBLB license. A contractor building a $400,000 custom home needs both. Specialty trades, electrical, plumbing, HVAC, have their own separate licensing boards, and a licensed general contractor is responsible for verifying that their subs hold the right credentials too.

The short version: if someone is building, remodeling, or making structural changes to your home in Alabama, they need a current state license. "I've been doing this for 20 years" is not a substitute.

## What's the difference between licensed and unlicensed work?

A licensed contractor has passed a trade exam, demonstrated financial responsibility, and carries the insurance the state requires. An unlicensed contractor has done none of those things, and that gap matters in several concrete ways.

**Insurance and liability.** If an unlicensed worker is injured on your property, your homeowner's insurance may deny the claim. Many policies explicitly exclude coverage for work performed by unlicensed contractors. That means you could be personally liable for a worker's medical bills.

**Mechanic's liens.** Alabama's lien law allows subcontractors and material suppliers to place a lien on your property if they aren't paid, even if you paid the general contractor in full. Licensed contractors are required to manage these relationships properly and provide lien waivers. With an unlicensed contractor, you have far less legal protection if they disappear after taking your deposit.

**Permits and inspections.** Most structural work, roofing replacements, additions, and [new construction](/services/new-construction/) require a building permit. Many Alabama jurisdictions will not issue a permit to an unlicensed contractor. If work is done without a permit, the city or county can require you to tear it out, at your expense, or refuse to issue a certificate of occupancy. That's a serious problem if you try to sell the home.

**Warranty and recourse.** If licensed work fails, you have a documented path to file a complaint with the licensing board, which can pull a contractor's license, require remediation, or impose fines. With an unlicensed contractor, your only option is civil court, and if they've moved on, good luck collecting.

## How do you verify a contractor's license in Alabama?

Verifying a license takes about two minutes and requires only the contractor's name or company name.

1. **For general contractors (projects $50,000+):** Go to [genbd.alabama.gov](https://genbd.alabama.gov/) and use the license lookup tool. You'll see the license number, current status (active, expired, suspended), and the classifications the contractor holds.
2. **For residential builders and remodelers:** Go to [hblb.alabama.gov](https://www.hblb.alabama.gov/) and search by name or license number. This covers anyone doing new home construction or residential remodeling.
3. **Ask for the license number directly.** Any legitimate contractor will give it to you without hesitation. If they hedge, that's your answer.
4. **Confirm insurance separately.** Ask for a certificate of general liability insurance and workers' compensation. Call the insurer to verify the policy is active, certificates can be outdated.
5. **Check the expiration date.** A license that expired six months ago is not a current license. Renewal is the contractor's responsibility; it's your job to check.

## Is it illegal to hire an unlicensed contractor in Alabama?

The contractor is the one breaking the law, not the homeowner, but that doesn't mean you're protected from the consequences. Under Alabama Code, performing contracting work without the required license is a misdemeanor for the contractor. However, any contract signed with an unlicensed contractor may be unenforceable in court, which means you could lose the ability to sue for damages even if the work is defective.

Some homeowners assume that hiring an unlicensed contractor is only a problem if something goes wrong. That's backward. The risk exposure exists from the moment you sign the contract, not just if a roof leaks or a wall cracks later.

The [National Association of Home Builders](https://www.nahb.org/) recommends verifying licensure and insurance before any work begins, not after problems surface. That advice applies whether you're in Alabama or anywhere else.

## What should you do before signing a construction contract?

Before any money changes hands, run through this short list.

- **Look up the license** on the HBLB or General Contractors Board site. Confirm it's active and covers the type of work being proposed.
- **Get proof of insurance**, general liability and workers' comp. Confirm the policy is current with a quick call to the insurer.
- **Ask who pulls the permit.** The answer should be the contractor, not you. If they ask you to pull the permit yourself, that's a red flag, it shifts legal responsibility to you.
- **Get a written contract** with a detailed scope of work, payment schedule, and timeline. Vague contracts protect no one.
- **Understand the lien process.** Ask your contractor to provide lien waivers from subcontractors and suppliers as work progresses. This is standard practice for licensed professionals.

For [home remodeling projects](/services/home-remodeling/) specifically, the permit and inspection process varies by city and county in Alabama. Madison, Huntsville, and Athens each have their own building departments, and the permit requirements for structural work, electrical, and plumbing are enforced at the local level, not just the state level.

If you're pricing out a significant [roofing project](/services/roofing/) or a full addition, the contractor's license status should be confirmed before you compare bids in any meaningful way. A lower bid from an unlicensed contractor isn't a deal, it's a transfer of risk from them to you.
