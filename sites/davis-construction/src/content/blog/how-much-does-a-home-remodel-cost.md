**TL;DR:** Home remodel costs in North Alabama range from about $8,000 for a basic bathroom update to $150,000+ for a whole-home renovation. Kitchen remodels typically land between $18,000 and $55,000. Bathroom remodels run $8,000–$28,000. Whole-home renovations average $80–$150 per square foot depending on scope and finish level. Room additions start around $120 per square foot for conditioned space. Material costs, permit fees, and the age of your home's systems all move the number significantly.

If you're sitting on a stack of contractor bids that are $20,000 apart and you're not sure which one is realistic, you're not alone. Remodeling costs in Alabama are genuinely hard to benchmark because the range is so wide, a 200-square-foot bathroom can cost $9,000 or $32,000 depending on tile selection, plumbing relocation, and whether the subfloor needs replacement. This guide breaks down what projects actually cost in North Alabama in 2026, what drives the spread, and how to read a bid before you sign anything.

## What does a home remodel cost by project type?

Remodel costs vary most by project type, not by square footage alone. Here's a realistic range for the most common projects in North Alabama:

| Project Type | Budget Tier | Mid-Range | High-End |
|---|---|---|---|
| Kitchen remodel (full) | $18,000–$28,000 | $30,000–$45,000 | $50,000–$80,000+ |
| Primary bathroom remodel | $8,000–$14,000 | $16,000–$24,000 | $26,000–$40,000 |
| Guest or hall bath | $5,500–$9,000 | $10,000–$16,000 | $18,000–$28,000 |
| Whole-home renovation | $75–$95/sq ft | $100–$130/sq ft | $140–$175/sq ft |
| Room addition (conditioned) | $120–$145/sq ft | $150–$175/sq ft | $180–$220/sq ft |
| Basement finish | $35–$55/sq ft | $60–$80/sq ft | $85–$120/sq ft |
| Open-concept conversion | $8,000–$15,000 | $16,000–$28,000 | $30,000–$50,000 |

Budget tier means builder-grade materials, no layout changes, and minimal surprises. Mid-range means upgraded fixtures, some layout adjustments, and standard permit work. High-end means custom cabinetry, tile work, or structural changes. Most homeowners land in the mid-range.

North Alabama labor rates run slightly below the national average tracked by [NAHB](https://www.nahb.org/), but material costs have largely converged with national pricing since 2022. Expect to pay within 5–10% of national benchmarks for materials; labor is where you see the regional discount.

## What drives the cost range on a kitchen remodel?

Kitchen remodel cost is driven by four things: cabinet selection, appliance package, whether plumbing or electrical moves, and what's hiding behind the walls. Cabinets alone can swing a kitchen budget by $10,000–$25,000.

Here's how the budget breaks down on a typical 180-square-foot kitchen mid-range remodel in North Alabama:

- **Cabinets and hardware**: $8,000–$18,000 (stock vs. semi-custom vs. custom)
- **Countertops**: $2,500–$7,000 (laminate vs. quartz vs. granite)
- **Appliances**: $3,500–$12,000 (builder-grade package vs. stainless mid-tier vs. professional series)
- **Tile and flooring**: $2,000–$6,000
- **Plumbing (sink, dishwasher, ice maker)**: $800–$3,500
- **Electrical (lighting, outlets, range hood)**: $1,200–$4,000
- **Permits and inspections**: $400–$900 (Madison and Huntsville both require permits for electrical and plumbing work)
- **Labor**: $6,000–$14,000 depending on scope

The number that surprises most homeowners is what happens when you open the walls. Homes built in the 1970s and 1980s, common in Old Town Madison and established Huntsville neighborhoods, often have aluminum wiring, galvanized supply lines, or undersized electrical panels that need upgrading before a remodel can proceed. Budget a 10–15% contingency for a home built before 1990.

For homes in newer subdivisions built after 2000, the systems are usually current, but HOA architectural review can add 2–4 weeks to the timeline if exterior-visible elements are changing.

## How much does a bathroom remodel cost per square foot?

Bathroom remodels cost more per square foot than almost any other room because you're concentrating plumbing, tile, and waterproofing into a small space. A 60-square-foot primary bath at $20,000 works out to $333 per square foot, that's normal.

The cost breakdown on a mid-range primary bathroom (60–80 sq ft):

- **Tile (floor + shower walls)**: $3,500–$8,000 (porcelain vs. natural stone; labor-intensive large-format tile costs more)
- **Vanity and fixtures**: $1,800–$5,500
- **Shower or tub surround**: $2,500–$9,000 (prefab surround vs. custom tile shower vs. freestanding tub)
- **Toilet**: $350–$900
- **Plumbing rough-in and finish**: $1,500–$4,500
- **Electrical (GFCI, exhaust fan, lighting)**: $800–$2,000
- **Waterproofing membrane (required under tile)**: $600–$1,200
- **Labor**: $4,000–$8,000

One line item homeowners frequently underestimate: waterproofing. North Alabama's humidity means a shower that wasn't properly waterproofed will show moisture intrusion within 3–5 years. Schluter Kerdi or a comparable membrane system adds $600–$1,200 to the job but prevents the kind of water damage behind tile that can cost $8,000–$15,000 to remediate later. The [EPA's guidance on moisture control](https://www.epa.gov/mold/mold-course-chapter-2) is clear that moisture management during construction is the most cost-effective mold prevention strategy.

If you're dealing with an existing bathroom that already has moisture damage behind the walls, that needs to be addressed before tile goes back up. ## What does a whole-home renovation cost in North Alabama?

A whole-home renovation in North Alabama typically runs $80–$150 per square foot for the finished living area, depending on how much of the structure and systems you're touching. A 1,800-square-foot ranch home renovated at $110/sq ft comes to roughly $198,000, that's a realistic mid-range number for a home that needs new kitchen, two bathrooms, flooring throughout, updated electrical, and fresh paint.

The per-square-foot model breaks down when you start adding structural work. Moving a load-bearing wall costs $3,500–$12,000 depending on span and beam size, that cost doesn't scale with square footage, it's a fixed scope item. Same with HVAC replacement ($8,000–$16,000 for a full system), electrical panel upgrade ($2,500–$5,500), or foundation repair.

For whole-home projects, scope the work in phases if budget is a constraint:

1. **Phase 1, Systems**: electrical, plumbing, HVAC, insulation. These are invisible after completion but protect everything else.
2. **Phase 2, Structural**: any wall removals, additions, or foundation work.
3. **Phase 3, Finishes**: kitchen, bathrooms, flooring, paint, trim.

Phasing lets you spread cost and ensures finish work doesn't get damaged by subsequent rough-in work. It also means permits close cleanly before the next phase opens.

According to Remodeling Magazine's [Cost vs. Value Report](https://www.remodeling.hw.net/cost-vs-value/), mid-range kitchen and bath remodels in the South Atlantic region recoup 60–75% of cost at resale. That's a reasonable expectation, remodeling is primarily about livability, not pure investment return.

## What factors move the number up or down?

Five things consistently push remodel costs above the initial estimate:

**1. Age of the home.** Pre-1990 homes in North Alabama frequently have deferred maintenance items that surface during demo: galvanized pipes with mineral buildup, original aluminum wiring, inadequate attic insulation (R-11 where current code requires R-38 in Alabama), or asbestos-containing floor tile or pipe insulation. Any of these requires remediation before finish work can proceed.

**2. Permit scope.** Madison and Huntsville both require permits for structural, electrical, plumbing, and mechanical work. Permit fees are modest ($300–$900 for most residential projects), but inspections add time. A project that skips permits creates title problems at resale and may require tear-out if discovered. Budget the permits in; they're not optional.

**3. Material lead times.** Custom cabinetry currently runs 8–14 weeks from order to delivery. Semi-custom is 4–6 weeks. If your project timeline is tight, stock cabinetry (2–3 week lead) may be the practical choice even if it's not your first preference.

**4. Scope creep.** The single most reliable way to blow a remodel budget is to add items mid-project. Every change order on an active job costs more than the same decision made before demo starts. Lock your selections before work begins.

**5. Subfloor and structural condition.** Bathrooms and kitchens in homes that had any prior water damage often have compromised subfloor. Replacing a 60-square-foot subfloor section adds $800–$2,500 to the job. It's not avoidable, tile laid over a soft subfloor will crack within a year.

## How do you get an accurate remodel estimate?

Get accurate remodel estimates by requesting itemized bids, not lump-sum numbers. A bid that says "Kitchen remodel: $32,000" tells you nothing about where the money goes or what happens if demo reveals a problem. An itemized bid breaks out labor, materials, permits, and a contingency line.

When comparing bids:

- Confirm the scope is identical across all bids. If one contractor is excluding electrical and another is including it, the numbers aren't comparable.
- Ask each contractor what their allowance is for cabinets, countertops, and tile. A $28,000 kitchen bid with a $4,000 cabinet allowance will become a $38,000 job the moment you pick real cabinetry.
- Verify that permit costs are included, not listed as "owner's responsibility."
- Ask about their subcontractor relationships for plumbing and electrical. A GC who uses the same licensed subs consistently will have more predictable pricing than one who bids it out each time.
