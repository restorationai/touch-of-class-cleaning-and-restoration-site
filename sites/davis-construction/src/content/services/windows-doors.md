Drafty frames, fogged glass between panes, a door that no longer latches cleanly against its weatherstripping, these are the signs that windows and doors have aged past the point of caulk and adjustment. In Madison's climate, where summer humidity pushes hard against interior air conditioning and winter cold snaps stress older frames, a failing window or door isn't just a comfort problem. It's a utility bill that climbs every month and a security gap that doesn't announce itself until something goes wrong.

## What window and door installation actually involves

Replacing a window or exterior door is a precision trade job, not a swap-and-go task. The rough opening, the framed cavity in your wall, has to be square, plumb, and correctly sized before any new unit goes in. If the opening is out of square even slightly, the new window or door will bind, gap at the corners, or fail to seal properly, defeating the entire purpose of the replacement.

For window replacement, the work includes removing the existing sash and frame (or just the sash on insert jobs), inspecting the rough opening for rot or water infiltration, shimming and leveling the new unit, sealing the perimeter with low-expansion foam, and finishing with interior and exterior trim. Energy efficient windows, double- or triple-pane units with low-E coatings and inert gas fills, require careful handling so the gas seal isn't compromised during installation.

Exterior door installation adds threshold work and weatherstripping to that list, plus hardware alignment so the deadbolt and latch engage cleanly under repeated use. Patio door installation involves larger, heavier units and often requires two-person handling to keep the frame from racking during placement. On older Madison homes with wood framing from the mid-20th century, the rough openings frequently need some degree of remediation before a modern pre-hung unit will sit correctly.

## Our process

1. **Measure and assess the opening.** Before any product is ordered, we measure the rough opening, check for square and plumb, and inspect the surrounding framing and sill for rot, moisture damage, or insect activity. A window ordered to the wrong dimension or installed into a compromised opening will fail prematurely.

2. **Select and source the right unit.** Window and door specifications matter: frame material (vinyl, fiberglass, wood-clad), glass package, U-factor and solar heat gain coefficient for energy performance, and hardware finish. We help you match the unit to your home's construction and your energy goals before anything is ordered.

3. **Prepare the rough opening.** The existing unit comes out carefully to preserve surrounding drywall and exterior cladding where possible. The opening is cleaned, any damaged framing is addressed, and the sill is flashed to direct any incidental water away from the structure.

4. **Set, level, and seal the new unit.** The replacement window or door is shimmed into plumb and level, fastened per the manufacturer's specification, and sealed at the perimeter with flashing tape and low-expansion foam. Exterior trim and caulk complete the weather barrier.

5. **Inspect operation and finish work.** Every installed unit is tested for smooth operation, proper latch engagement, and a consistent weatherstrip seal around the full perimeter. Interior trim is reinstalled or replaced, and the work area is cleaned before we leave.

## What separates a good installation from a bad one

The most common failure point in window and door installation isn't the product, it's the flashing. Water that gets behind the exterior trim and sits against the rough opening framing will rot the sill and jack studs silently, sometimes for years, before it becomes visible as soft flooring or bubbling paint. Proper head flashing above the unit and sill pan flashing below it are non-negotiable steps that some installers skip to save time.

A second common problem is using the wrong foam. High-expansion foam in a door frame can bow the jamb enough that the door binds or the deadbolt misaligns. Low-expansion foam, applied in stages, is the correct choice for door frames and most window perimeters.

On energy efficient windows, the glass package has to match the orientation and climate. A high solar heat gain coefficient makes sense on a north-facing window in a cold climate; it works against you on a south- or west-facing window in a Madison summer. Specifying the wrong glass package is a mistake that costs money on every utility bill for the life of the window.

For insurance-related replacements, storm damage, hail impact, or wind-driven water intrusion, accurate documentation of the damaged unit, the opening condition, and the scope of any associated framing repair is what supports a complete claim. Adjusters look for photo documentation of the pre-existing opening condition alongside the new installation.

## What does window and door installation cost?

Typical costs vary significantly based on unit type, frame material, glass package, and the condition of the existing opening. Most homeowners pay more for patio doors and specialty shapes than for standard double-hung replacements, and any framing repair discovered during removal adds to the scope. Homeowners insurance typically covers window and door replacement when the damage results from a covered peril, wind, hail, or impact, but does not cover replacement for age, wear, or energy efficiency upgrades alone.

## Seasonal and regional considerations

Madison sits in a climate zone where both summer heat gain and winter heat loss matter. Homes built before the 1990s in the area frequently have single-pane aluminum-frame windows that transfer heat almost as efficiently as an open gap in the wall. Replacing these with low-E double-pane units makes a measurable difference in both directions.

Timing also matters. Caulk and sealant cure best above 40°F, so installations during a January cold snap require more care and sometimes supplemental heat at the opening. Summer installations in direct sun can cause sealants to skin over before they fully bond if application isn't timed to shaded conditions or cooler parts of the day.
