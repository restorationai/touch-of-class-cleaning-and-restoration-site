A finished build can still fail a final walkthrough over things that have nothing to do with the construction itself: drywall dust settled into the HVAC returns, a film of joint compound ground into the grout lines, paint overspray fogging the shower glass, adhesive residue left on cabinet hardware from protective tape. Standard janitorial cleaning pushes this grit around instead of removing it, which is why post-construction cleaning is its own trade. Done right, it's what gets a house or a commercial space ready for occupancy, not just ready to look at.

## What post-construction & specialty cleaning actually involves

This isn't a mop-and-wipe service. Construction debris behaves differently than household dirt: drywall dust is fine enough to travel through HVAC systems and resettle for days after a surface cleaning, paint and adhesive overspray bonds to glass and tile in ways that regular glass cleaner won't touch, and protective films left on flooring or fixtures can leave residue if pulled too late or too fast. The work typically spans a full property, top to bottom: light fixtures, ceiling fans, window tracks, cabinet interiors and exteriors, baseboards, floor finishes, and any glass or mirrored surfaces that caught overspray during paint or texture work. Equipment matters here. HEPA-filtered vacuums pull fine particulate instead of redistributing it into the air, and solvent choice has to match the surface, what clears latex overspray off a window won't be the right product for a quartz countertop. Timelines run from a single day for a touch-up clean on a small remodel to several days for a new-construction single-family home or a commercial buildout, depending on square footage and how much trade debris is left behind.

## Our process

1. **Rough clean and debris haul-off.** Packaging, offcuts, labels, and leftover materials get cleared out first so detail work isn't happening around trash.
2. **Dust abatement, top-down.** HEPA-filtered vacuuming starts at ceiling height, hits HVAC vents and returns, light fixtures, and crown molding, then works down to avoid resettling dust on surfaces already cleaned.
3. **Detail cleaning.** Window tracks, cabinetry (inside and out), fixture hardware, and glass get cleared of paint overspray, adhesive, and joint compound residue using surface-appropriate solvents.
4. **Floor finishing.** Protective film or paper comes off, and floors get cleaned and finished according to material, LVP, hardwood, tile, or carpet all need different approaches at this stage.
5. **Final walkthrough against the punch list.** Surfaces get checked room by room so the space is ready to show or occupy, not just ready to leave.

## What separates a good post-construction cleaning response from a bad one

The most common miss is treating this like a standard deep clean. Crews without construction-cleaning experience often skip the inside of HVAC returns and vents, which means dust that settled during framing and drywall phases gets pulled back into circulation the first time the system runs, undoing the clean within days. Another frequent problem is using the wrong tools on new finishes: abrasive pads or the wrong solvent can dull a new quartz counter, scratch LVP flooring, or haze glass that was supposed to be protected. Pulling floor protection film too early, before trades are fully done, or too late, after adhesive has cured, causes residue or damage either way. A careful operator also checks light fixtures and ceiling fans specifically, since dust up high is easy to miss and obvious once the lights are on. For commercial buildouts with an occupancy deadline, sequencing matters too: cleaning has to happen after the last trade is out, not scheduled around them.

## What does post-construction & specialty cleaning cost?

Costs vary with square footage, how much trade debris is left on-site, and the finish level of the space, a builder-grade spec home cleans up faster than one with extensive glass, tile, and custom millwork. Every property is different, so Davis Construction Contractors provides a written scope before work begins rather than a flat rate off the phone. Homeowners insurance typically does not cover post-construction cleaning on new builds or voluntary renovations, since it's considered part of the construction cost rather than damage restoration; it may apply if the cleaning follows a covered loss like fire or water damage repair.

## Seasonal & regional considerations

Madison's building season runs heaviest in spring and summer, which is also when pine and oak pollen coats job sites in a yellow film that settles into open windows and HVAC intakes during the final construction phases. Summer humidity also makes dust and adhesive residue stickier and harder to lift cleanly, so timing the final clean as close to occupancy as possible matters more here than in drier climates. Builders working through a North Alabama summer should expect the cleaning window to need a bit more buffer for this reason.
