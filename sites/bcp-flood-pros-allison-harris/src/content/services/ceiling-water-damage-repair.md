## What ceiling water damage repair actually involves

The repair itself is really two jobs stacked on top of each other. First, the water intrusion has to be traced and stopped, whether that's a failed supply line, an ice-dam backup, a roof flashing failure, or an upstairs plumbing fixture. Second, the ceiling assembly has to be dried, and in most cases, rebuilt. That can mean a single drywall patch the size of a dinner plate, or it can mean cutting out an entire section of ceiling, pulling wet insulation, and replacing drywall, tape, texture, and paint to match. Equipment on a typical job includes a moisture meter to map how far the water traveled past the visible stain, an infrared camera to find hidden wet pockets in the joist bays, and air movers and dehumidifiers positioned to dry the cavity from both sides before anything gets closed back up.

## Our process

1. **Source identification.** Before any drywall comes down, we trace the leak back to its origin, whether that's a plumbing line, a roof penetration, or condensation from an HVAC unit in the attic.
2. **Moisture mapping.** We use a moisture meter and thermal imaging to find the actual boundary of the wet material, which is almost always larger than the visible stain on the ceiling surface.
3. **Controlled demolition.** Compromised drywall, insulation, and any affected framing get removed in a way that limits dust and debris in the living space below, not just torn open.
4. **Structural drying.** Air movers and dehumidifiers are set to dry the remaining framing and insulation per the IICRC S500 drying standard before any rebuild begins, so you're not closing moisture into a new ceiling.
5. **Rebuild and finish.** New drywall, tape, texture, and paint are matched to the existing ceiling so the repair blends in rather than standing out as a patch.

## What separates a good ceiling water damage response from a bad one

The most common mistake is patching the visible stain without checking how far the water actually traveled. Water runs along the top of drywall and down the low point of a joist bay before it ever shows through paint, so a repair that only addresses the spot you can see often leaves wet insulation or framing behind the new patch. Insurance adjusters look specifically for moisture readings documented before and after drying, not just photos of the stain, so a response that skips the measurement step can leave you under-documented if the claim is questioned later. Another frequent miss is ignoring the attic side of the problem entirely, which matters a lot in American Fork where ice-dam backups during winter push water above the ceiling line rather than straight down through it.
