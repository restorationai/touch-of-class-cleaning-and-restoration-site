## What burst pipe cleanup and repair actually involves

Burst pipe cleanup and repair covers two separate problems that have to be solved together: the broken supply or drain line itself, and the water it already released into the structure. A pipe break can come from a hard freeze that splits copper or PEX, a corroded galvanized line finally giving out, a slab leak under the foundation, or a fitting failure behind a washing machine or water heater. Once the water is shut off, the job shifts to extraction with truck-mounted or portable pumps, moisture mapping of walls and subfloor with a meter and thermal imaging, and setting air movers and dehumidifiers to dry the structure to a documented, pre-loss moisture level. Drywall, baseboard, and flooring often need to come out if water sat for more than a few hours, because saturated materials stay wet long after the surface looks dry. Depending on the pipe location, the repair side can mean cutting into drywall or a slab, replacing a section of line, and coordinating with a licensed plumber on the actual plumbing fix while we handle the water damage and reconstruction.

## Our process

1. **Locate and confirm the shutoff.** Before anything else, we verify the water is off at the main or the fixture, because re-flooding during cleanup is a common and preventable mistake.
2. **Extract standing water.** Pumps and extraction wands pull out bulk water from floors, carpet, and low points before it migrates further into subfloor and framing.
3. **Map moisture and open the wall or floor cavity where needed.** Pinless and pin-type meters, along with thermal imaging, show where water traveled behind finished surfaces, not just where it's visible.
4. **Dry in place with monitored equipment.** Air movers and dehumidifiers run on a schedule, with daily moisture readings logged so drying stops when materials hit a verified dry standard, not on a guess.
5. **Repair or rebuild affected materials.** Drywall, trim, flooring, and cabinetry that didn't survive the break get replaced, and the pipe repair itself is coordinated or completed as part of the scope.

## What separates a good burst pipe cleanup and repair from a bad one

The most common mistake is drying the surface and calling it done while the wall cavity, subfloor, or insulation stays wet. Carpet pad and drywall can feel dry to the touch within a day while the material behind them holds moisture for a week or more, and that's exactly the condition mold needs to start colonizing, often within 24 to 48 hours in a warm, humid cavity. Adjusters look for documented moisture readings over time, not a single reading, because that log is what proves the structure was actually dried rather than just aired out. Another frequent miss is treating a pipe break as a single-source event when the water actually traveled along a joist bay or down a wall into a lower floor, which means rooms that look untouched still need to be checked. A thorough response opens enough material to confirm the full extent of the water path before closing anything back up, which costs less in the long run than a second opening after mold shows up.

## Seasonal & regional considerations

American Fork and the surrounding Utah County area see hard freezes most winters, and unheated spaces like garages, crawlspaces, and exterior-wall plumbing runs are the most common failure points when temperatures drop overnight. Older homes in the area with galvanized supply lines are also more prone to gradual corrosion failures, which can show up as a slow weep before turning into a full break. Spring thaw adds its own risk, as pipes stressed by a freeze-thaw cycle sometimes don't fail until weeks later.
