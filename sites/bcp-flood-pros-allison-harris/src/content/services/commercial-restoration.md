A broken fire sprinkler head over a retail floor, a kitchen fire at a restaurant two hours before dinner service, a slow roof leak that's been staining ceiling tiles in a third-floor office suite for weeks: these losses don't just damage a building, they stop revenue. Commercial restoration is a different discipline from residential work because the clock is measured in lost business days, lease obligations, and tenant notices, not just drywall and carpet.

## What commercial restoration actually involves

Commercial restoration covers water intrusion, fire and smoke damage, and the reconstruction that follows, but scoped around a building that has tenants, inventory, equipment, or customers depending on it. An office suite with a burst supply line behind a breakroom wall is handled differently than a house: crews often work after hours or in phases so part of the space stays operational, contents like server racks, retail inventory, or medical records get separated and documented before any demolition starts, and drying equipment gets placed to avoid blocking exits, point-of-sale stations, or ADA-required clearances. Fire and smoke jobs add soot characterization (protein versus synthetic residues clean up differently) and usually require coordination with the fire marshal before reoccupancy is cleared.

## Our process

1. **Business impact triage.** Before anything gets touched, we identify what has to keep functioning: data closets, walk-in coolers, point-of-sale systems, egress paths, so the mitigation plan protects operations, not just materials.
2. **Extraction or soot removal, phased around occupancy.** Water gets pulled and drying equipment staged in sections, or soot and smoke residue get removed room by room, so unaffected parts of the building can often stay open.
3. **Documentation built for commercial claims.** Commercial policies typically separate building damage from business personal property and business interruption, so we photograph and log contents inventory, equipment, and structural damage as distinct line items for the adjuster.
4. **Reconstruction scoped to code.** Rebuilding a commercial space usually means permits, inspections, and sometimes ADA or fire-code triggers that a residential job never hits. We write the scope of work with that in mind before framing or finishing begins.
5. **Pre-reopening walkthrough.** A final punch list confirms the space is ready for occupancy, not just dry or rebuilt.

## What separates a good commercial response from a bad one

The most common mistake is treating a commercial loss like an oversized residential one. A crew that doesn't separate business personal property from building structure in their documentation creates headaches later when the adjuster splits the claim into two coverages. Another frequent miss: not checking whether HVAC ductwork carried smoke or moisture into spaces that look untouched, if the air handler pulled contaminated air through a shared system, other tenant suites can be affected even with no visible damage. Adjusters on commercial claims also look closely at whether drying followed a documented standard like IICRC S500, whether moisture readings were logged over time instead of just once, and whether the contractor coordinated with the property manager or landlord on responsibility for shared systems like plumbing stacks or rooftop units. Skipping any of that tends to slow the claim down, not speed it up.

## What does commercial restoration cost?

Commercial restoration costs vary more than residential work because building size, occupancy type, and how much the business needs to stay operational during the job all factor in. ## Seasonal & regional considerations

American Fork and the surrounding Utah County area see hard winter freeze-thaw cycles that stress commercial plumbing, particularly in older strip malls and office buildings with exposed pipe runs in unheated mechanical rooms. Spring runoff and heavier snowmelt years raise the risk of basement and crawlspace water intrusion in buildings along lower-lying grades. Summer brings occasional intense monsoonal storms that test flat commercial roofing systems, which are common on retail and office construction throughout the valley, flat roofs pond water differently than pitched residential roofs and can hide a leak's true entry point for weeks.
