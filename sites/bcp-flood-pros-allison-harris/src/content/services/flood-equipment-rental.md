## What flood & drying equipment rental actually involves

Consumer-grade box fans and hardware-store dehumidifiers move air and pull moisture, but not at the volume or precision a water loss demands. A single room affected by a burst supply line can hold hundreds of pounds of moisture trapped in framing, insulation, and flooring assemblies. Professional drying equipment is rated in CFM (cubic feet per minute) for air movers and pints-per-day for dehumidifiers, and those ratings matter when you're racing a 48-hour mold clock.

The equipment in a typical rental package includes:

- **Submersible or trash pumps** for standing water removal before drying begins
- **Low-grain refrigerant (LGR) dehumidifiers** that pull moisture efficiently even when ambient humidity is already dropping
- **Axial and centrifugal air movers** positioned to create a directed airflow across wet surfaces, pushing evaporated moisture toward the dehumidifier intake
- **Moisture meters and hygrometers** so you can track drying progress rather than guessing

A properly configured drying system creates a closed drying loop: air movers accelerate evaporation from wet materials, the dehumidifier captures that moisture-laden air and exhausts dry air back into the space, and readings from a moisture meter confirm when structural materials have returned to normal levels, typically between 8% and 13% moisture content for wood framing in Utah's climate.

## Our process

1. **Assess the loss and select the right equipment load.** Not every flood is the same. A dishwasher overflow onto a vinyl kitchen floor calls for different equipment than a sump failure that soaked a finished basement. Before you take anything off the truck, the affected area, water category, and material types determine how many air movers and what dehumidifier capacity the job actually needs.

2. **Extract standing water first.** Air movers accomplish nothing while water is still pooled. A submersible pump or wet-vac extraction removes bulk water so drying equipment can work on what's left, moisture absorbed into the structure itself.

3. **Position equipment using a drying pattern.** Air movers are placed in a daisy-chain pattern around the perimeter of the wet area, angled low across the floor and against walls. Dehumidifiers are centrally located to capture the evaporated moisture. Improper placement, air movers pointed at each other, dehumidifiers exhausting into a corner, dramatically extends drying time.

4. **Monitor daily with a moisture meter.** Drying is not a set-it-and-forget-it process. Readings taken at the same reference points each day tell you whether materials are tracking toward dry or whether you have a hidden moisture pocket, inside a wall cavity, under a floating floor, or beneath a concrete slab, that the surface reading missed.

5. **Confirm dry standard before equipment return.** Equipment comes off rent when affected materials reach their dry standard, not when the air feels dry or the floor looks fine. Returning equipment a day early because the surface reads acceptable is one of the most common causes of secondary mold damage.

## What separates a good drying response from a bad one

The most common mistake in DIY and under-equipped drying jobs is under-powering the setup. Industry guidelines suggest roughly one air mover per 50–100 square feet of wet surface area, plus dehumidifier capacity scaled to the room volume and material types. Running two air movers in a 600-square-foot basement does not create adequate airflow, it creates the appearance of drying while moisture migrates deeper into the structure.

A second frequent failure is skipping daily moisture readings. Without data, there's no way to know whether drying is progressing, stalled, or whether a wall cavity is holding water that the surface doesn't reveal. Insurance adjusters reviewing a claim will look for a moisture log, dated readings at consistent reference points, as documentation that drying was performed to a measurable standard, not just until the equipment got returned.

Finally, Category 2 and Category 3 water losses (gray water from appliances, sewage backups, or floodwater from outside) require containment and antimicrobial treatment before and during drying. Running air movers in a contaminated space without containment can aerosolize contaminants and spread them to unaffected areas of the home.

## Seasonal & regional considerations

Utah's climate creates two distinct peaks for water damage. Spring snowmelt, particularly in years with heavy mountain snowpack above American Fork Canyon, raises the water table and puts pressure on foundation walls and sump systems throughout Utah County. Homes in lower-elevation neighborhoods near the Jordan River corridor or with older clay-tile drain systems are especially vulnerable during rapid melt events.

Winter brings a different risk: frozen supply lines in exterior walls and unheated crawl spaces. When temperatures drop sharply overnight, pipes in under-insulated rim joist areas can freeze and burst, releasing water into wall cavities that may not be discovered until a room starts to smell musty days later. Cold ambient temperatures also slow evaporation rates, meaning drying equipment may need to run longer than it would during warmer months, a factor worth accounting for when estimating rental duration.
