## What water heater flood cleanup actually involves

A water heater burst or slow tank leak behaves differently than a supply line break elsewhere in the house. The unit usually sits against drywall in a confined closet, so water wicks straight up the base of the wall and under adjoining flooring, carpet pad, or baseboard before it's visible. If the leak has been slow, there may be scale or sediment residue mixed into the water, and the enclosed, poorly ventilated space around most water heaters is exactly the kind of environment where mold can start colonizing studs and subfloor within 24 to 48 hours. Cleanup involves extracting standing water, pulling baseboard and sometimes lower drywall to check the wall cavity, drying the closet and any adjacent rooms the water reached, and documenting everything for your insurance file. We don't repair or replace the water heater itself, that's a plumber's scope, but we coordinate timing so drying equipment isn't fighting an active leak.

## Our process

1. **Confirm the source is off.** We verify the water supply valve and power or gas to the unit are shut down before any extraction starts, and note the tank's apparent age and condition for the claim file.
2. **Extract standing water.** We pull water from the closet, utility room, or basement floor, moving the water heater itself if needed to reach water trapped behind or underneath it.
3. **Map moisture beyond the obvious.** Using moisture meters, we check the base of adjoining walls, under vinyl or tile flooring near the closet, and into the subfloor, since water from a water heater rarely stays contained to the footprint of the unit.
4. **Dry the structure, not just the puddle.** Air movers and dehumidifiers go in based on what the moisture readings show, with daily monitoring until materials read dry per the IICRC S500 standard.
5. **Document for the plumber and the carrier.** We photograph tank condition, water path, and affected materials so both your plumber's repair estimate and your insurance claim have a clear record.

## What separates a good water heater flood response from a bad one

The most common miss is treating a water heater leak like a simple mop-up job. Because the unit sits in a closet, it's easy to dry the visible floor and call it done while the wall cavity behind the tank, or the subfloor under an adjacent hallway, stays saturated. That's how mold shows up in a water heater closet two weeks after the "cleanup" was finished. A thorough response pulls baseboard or opens a small drywall section to confirm the cavity is actually dry, not just surface-dry to the touch. Insurance adjusters also look closely at tank age and corrosion pattern on water heater claims, since a leak from a 12-year-old tank past its expected service life can affect how a claim is handled versus a sudden fitting failure. Getting clear photos of the tank and the water heater leak damage before anything is moved protects your claim either way.

## What does water heater flood cleanup cost?

Costs depend on how long the leak ran before it was found, what flooring and wall materials got wet, and whether the water reached beyond the closet itself. Every loss is different, which is why FIX Restoration provides a written scope before work begins rather than a flat quote over the phone. Most homeowners insurance policies cover sudden water heater failures as part of a standard water damage claim, though gradual leaks from long-term neglect can be treated differently, so it's worth checking your policy language.
