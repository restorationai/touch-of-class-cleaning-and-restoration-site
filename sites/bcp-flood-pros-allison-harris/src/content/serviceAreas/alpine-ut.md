## Restoration emergencies common in Alpine

Alpine sits at the base of the Wasatch Range, and that geography drives most of the restoration calls the city sees. Spring snowmelt from the foothills can overwhelm drainage swales and push water against foundations faster than it drains. Hard freeze-thaw cycles through late winter and early spring split supply lines and split the caulk seals around penetrations in ways that go unnoticed until a ceiling shows a stain. Summer convective storms drop heavy rain in short windows, and because much of Alpine's soil is rocky and compacted near the bench, runoff sheds quickly rather than absorbing, sending water toward window wells and garage slabs. Wildfire smoke from the Wasatch and Uinta ranges has also become a recurring seasonal concern, leaving odor and particulate residue in HVAC systems and on porous surfaces.

## Services we provide in Alpine

### Water damage restoration

Water damage in Alpine frequently starts in finished basements and lower-level living spaces, where snowmelt and storm runoff find the path of least resistance. Moisture behind drywall can read dry at the surface while the framing and insulation behind it stay saturated for days. We use thermal imaging and calibrated moisture meters to map the actual wet zone, then dry to the IICRC S500 standard before any rebuild work begins.

### Fire and smoke damage restoration

House fires in Alpine often involve the wood-framed construction common to the area's newer subdivisions, where engineered lumber and synthetic materials produce persistent smoke residue that penetrates HVAC ductwork and cabinet interiors. Smoke odor that seems to clear in the first days typically resurfaces when temperatures rise. Thorough structural cleaning, content cleaning, and duct decontamination are all part of a complete fire scope here.

### Mold remediation

Mold colonization in Alpine homes most often follows a slow leak, a failed window seal, or a crawlspace with inadequate vapor control. We assess affected areas, contain the work zone, and remove compromised materials following established remediation protocols.

## Building stock, site conditions, and permits in Alpine

Most of Alpine's residential housing was built from the 1990s onward, with significant development continuing through the 2000s and 2010s.
