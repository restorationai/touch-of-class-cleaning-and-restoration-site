## Restoration emergencies common in Draper

Draper sits at the base of the Wasatch Front, and that geography drives most of the restoration calls the area generates. Spring snowmelt and rapid runoff from the foothills push water toward foundations faster than storm drains can handle it during heavy thaw events. Winter freeze-thaw cycles crack supply lines inside walls and attic spaces where insulation has compressed over the years. Summer thunderstorms, common along the Wasatch from July through September, can drop an inch of rain in under an hour, overwhelming window wells and below-grade entries in homes that were graded flat during the construction boom of the 1990s and 2000s.

Wildfire smoke is a seasonal reality in this part of Utah County. When fires burn in the Traverse Mountains or the canyons to the east, fine particulate and odor compounds infiltrate HVAC systems and settle into soft materials, requiring more than an air-out to resolve. Draper's elevation and dry summer air also mean that a slow leak behind a finished basement wall can stay hidden for weeks before visible mold growth signals the problem.

## Services we provide in Draper

### Water damage restoration

Water damage in Draper frequently involves finished basements. Drying a finished basement correctly means reading moisture in the framing and concrete, not just the surface materials, and pulling vapor out of the slab before reinstalling flooring.

### Mold remediation

Mold growth after a water event follows a predictable timeline: in Draper's climate, conditions for colonization can develop within 24 to 48 hours in a space with limited airflow, such as a crawlspace, a closed utility room, or a basement bathroom. Remediation scopes here often include testing, physical removal of affected materials, and treatment of adjacent framing, followed by clearance verification before reconstruction begins.
