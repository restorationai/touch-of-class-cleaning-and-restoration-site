## Restoration emergencies common in Sandy

Sandy sits at the base of the Wasatch Range, which shapes the weather patterns that drive most restoration calls in the area. Spring snowmelt from the mountains can overwhelm drainage on properties that back up to the foothills, and the soil in many parts of Sandy contains expansive clay that shifts as moisture levels change, stressing foundations and slab edges. Winter freeze-thaw cycles are hard on older supply lines, and a pipe that survives a cold snap in January may crack and fail in February when temperatures swing. Summer monsoon moisture, while less dramatic here than in southern Utah, still delivers short, intense rain events that can back up window wells and push water into below-grade spaces. Fire risk from Wasatch-adjacent wildfire smoke and ember travel is a real seasonal concern for properties on the eastern bench, and smoke residue from a nearby structure fire can penetrate HVAC systems and soft goods even when flames never reach a home.

## Services we provide in Sandy

### Water damage restoration

Water damage in Sandy frequently involves finished basements. The housing stock here leans heavily toward homes built in the 1970s through the 1990s, many of which have fully finished lower levels with drywall, carpet, and mechanical rooms that share space with water heaters and aging supply lines. When a water heater fails or a supply line lets go, water wicks into drywall and insulation quickly. FIX Restoration uses moisture mapping and thermal imaging to find saturation behind surfaces before it becomes a mold problem.

### Mold remediation

Mold can begin colonizing wet building materials within 24 to 48 hours of a moisture event, which means a slow leak behind a vanity or under a slab can produce a significant colony before a homeowner notices a smell. Remediation in Sandy often involves older drywall assemblies and fiberglass batt insulation that hold moisture longer than they appear to from the surface. Work is scoped to contain the affected area, remove compromised materials, and treat the substrate before reconstruction begins.

### Fire and smoke damage restoration

Smoke residue is acidic and continues to damage surfaces, metals, and fabrics long after the fire is out. Restoration after a fire involves cleaning and sealing affected surfaces, addressing odor at the source rather than masking it, and coordinating with your insurance adjuster on scope and documentation. Homes with forced-air HVAC systems require duct inspection and cleaning as part of any fire scope.

### Reconstruction

After mitigation is complete, FIX Restoration handles the rebuild so property owners are not managing two separate contractors. Reconstruction scope in Sandy can range from replacing a single bathroom's drywall and tile to full room rebuilds following major water or fire losses.

## Building stock, site conditions, and permits in Sandy

Most of Sandy's residential housing was built between the late 1960s and the mid-1990s, with a significant portion of that stock using wood-frame construction over full basements or partial crawlspaces. Copper supply lines are common in homes from this era, but galvanized drain lines appear in older sections and are prone to interior corrosion and reduced flow capacity. Homes built before 1980 may contain asbestos-containing materials in floor tile, joint compound, or pipe insulation, which affects how a restoration scope is written and what abatement steps precede reconstruction. Structural repair and reconstruction work in Sandy falls under the jurisdiction of Sandy City's building department, and permits are typically required for work that involves structural framing, electrical, or plumbing.
