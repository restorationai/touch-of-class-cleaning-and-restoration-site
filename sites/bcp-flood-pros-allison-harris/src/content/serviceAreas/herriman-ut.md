## Restoration emergencies common in Herriman

Harriman's explosive growth over the past two decades means a large share of its housing stock sits on sloped terrain at the base of the Oquirrh Mountains, where drainage decisions made during subdivision grading have long-term consequences. Snowmelt from the range runs downhill fast in late winter and early spring, and when it finds a low foundation wall, a window well without a drain, or a yard that grades toward the house rather than away, it ends up in a basement. Summer also brings its own risk: the Wasatch Front's late-afternoon monsoon pattern can dump an inch of rain in under an hour, overwhelming storm drains and sending sheet flow across driveways and into garages. Inside the home, the combination of hard Utah water and the PEX and CPVC plumbing common in post-2000 construction creates conditions where fittings and manifold connections are the most likely failure points, especially after a hard freeze.

Wildfire smoke is a seasonal concern as well. When fires burn in the Oquirrhs or in the canyons to the east, smoke and ash infiltrate homes through HVAC systems and gaps in the building envelope, leaving odor and particulate residue that requires more than standard cleaning to address.

### Water damage restoration

### Fire and smoke damage restoration
