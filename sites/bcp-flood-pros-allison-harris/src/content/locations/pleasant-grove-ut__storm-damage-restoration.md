## Why Pleasant Grove Properties See Repeated Storm Damage

Pleasant Grove's position along the Wasatch bench creates a specific weather pattern that residents know well: late-spring thunderstorms that build over Utah Lake and then accelerate as they climb toward the foothills, and fall windstorms that drop out of the canyon mouths with enough force to strip shingles and scatter fence panels across entire blocks. The city's housing stock adds another layer of complexity. Neighborhoods developed in the 1970s and 1980s, which make up a large share of Pleasant Grove's single-family homes, were built with wood-framed construction and composition shingles that have now aged past their rated service life. A storm that a newer roof sheds cleanly can open a 1978 home to significant water intrusion within minutes.

The soil along the bench grades from sandy loam near the valley floor to clay-heavy substrate closer to the mountain base. After a heavy storm, that clay soil saturates quickly and sheds water toward foundations rather than absorbing it, a pattern that shows up as basement seepage and crawl space flooding even when the roof itself holds.

## Our Storm Damage Restoration Process in Pleasant Grove

The first priority after any storm event is stopping the water that is still moving. That means emergency tarping of breached rooflines, boarding damaged windows and doors, and extracting standing water from basements or crawl spaces before it migrates into wall cavities. Once the structure is stabilized, the assessment work begins in earnest.

Storm damage in Pleasant Grove homes often hides. Hail impact on a shingle may look like surface bruising but has actually fractured the mat underneath, leaving the roof vulnerable to the next rain. Wind-driven water behind siding can saturate wall sheathing while the interior drywall reads dry to the touch. We use moisture mapping and thermal imaging to find what the eye misses, then document everything, photos, moisture readings, affected material quantities, in a format that supports an insurance claim directly.

Debris removal, structural drying, and repairs to roofing, siding, and interior finishes are sequenced to match the drying timeline, not the other way around. Closing up a wall before the framing has reached acceptable moisture content is how mold problems start.
