## Why Cottonwood Heights Properties Present Unique Biohazard Considerations

Much of the residential housing stock in Cottonwood Heights was built during the 1960s through the 1980s, a period when split-level and tri-level floor plans were popular throughout the eastern Salt Lake Valley. These layouts often feature finished lower levels with concrete slab subfloors, carpet glued directly to concrete, and HVAC returns positioned low in the structure. In a biohazard event, porous materials at or below grade can absorb contaminants quickly, and low-positioned HVAC intakes can pull odor and particulate through the system before anyone realizes the scope of the problem. Identifying what has been affected, and what has not, is the first step before any cleaning begins.

Salt Lake County requires that biohazardous waste generated during remediation be transported and disposed of through licensed medical waste carriers. That is not a detail homeowners should have to navigate on their own during an already difficult time. FIX Restoration manages waste packaging, manifesting, and disposal in compliance with county and state requirements so the property is cleared through proper channels.

## Our Biohazard Cleanup Process in Cottonwood Heights

Every job begins with a thorough assessment of affected and potentially affected surfaces. Technicians identify porous materials, including carpet, padding, drywall, and wood subfloor, that may require removal rather than surface cleaning. The work area is isolated with physical containment barriers and negative air pressure where appropriate to prevent cross-contamination to unaffected rooms.

Cleaning and disinfection follow EPA-registered protocols for the specific type of biological material involved. After visible material is removed and surfaces are treated, technicians conduct ATP testing or similar verification to confirm that contamination levels meet accepted clearance thresholds before containment comes down. Affected materials that cannot be cleaned to standard are bagged, labeled, and removed for licensed disposal. The goal at every stage is to leave the property in a condition that is both safe and documentable.
