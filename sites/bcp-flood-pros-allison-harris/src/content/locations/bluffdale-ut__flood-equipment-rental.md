## Why Bluffdale Properties See Elevated Water Intrusion Risk

Bluffdale's position along the western bench of the Wasatch Range means the soil transitions from sandy loam near the foothills to heavier clay-dominant profiles closer to the valley floor. Clay soil sheds water rather than absorbing it, so heavy rain or rapid snowmelt in late March and April moves laterally toward foundations instead of draining away. Homes built on slab foundations, which are common in Bluffdale's newer subdivisions developed through the 2000s and 2010s, can still experience water intrusion through garage thresholds, utility penetrations, and improperly graded landscaping. Crawl space construction, found in some of the older ranch-style homes in the area, creates a different problem: standing water under the floor that raises humidity throughout the entire living space and begins promoting mold growth within 48 to 72 hours if airflow is not established.

The Jordan River corridor to the west also influences groundwater levels. During high-flow periods, the water table in lower-elevation parts of Bluffdale rises, and basement sump pits can cycle continuously. When a sump pump fails during a high-table event, water can enter faster than a single consumer-grade wet vac can remove it.

## Equipment We Provide for Flood & Drying in Bluffdale

The right equipment for a Bluffdale drying job depends on the structure type and the volume of water involved. FIX Restoration's rental inventory includes:
