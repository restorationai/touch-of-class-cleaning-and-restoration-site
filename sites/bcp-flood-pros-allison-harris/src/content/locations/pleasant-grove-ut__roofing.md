Pleasant Grove sits at the foot of Mount Timpanogos, where elevation, UV intensity, and dramatic seasonal temperature swings put roofing systems through a stress cycle that flat-country contractors rarely account for. Winters bring heavy snowpack and repeated freeze-thaw cycles that work ice and water under shingles at the ridge and eave lines. Summers push surface temperatures on dark asphalt shingles well above ambient air temperature, accelerating granule loss and brittleness. When it is time to replace or install a roof on a Pleasant Grove home, the material choices and installation details that protect against those specific conditions are not optional extras, they are the baseline.

## Why Pleasant Grove Properties See Accelerated Roof Wear

The Wasatch Front's climate is not gentle on roofing materials. Pleasant Grove's position along the base of the mountains means it catches orographic precipitation, storms that drop more moisture here than they do a few miles west on the valley floor. That extra moisture, combined with freeze-thaw cycling from November through March, is hard on flashing joints, ridge caps, and any area where two roof planes meet. Ice damming is a recurring issue on homes with lower-slope sections or inadequate attic ventilation, and once an ice dam forms, water backs up under the shingle layer and finds every imperfect nail hole or lap joint.

The housing stock in Pleasant Grove also matters. Many neighborhoods developed rapidly during the 1980s and 1990s, meaning a large share of the city's homes are now carrying roofs that are approaching or past their manufacturer's rated lifespan. Original 20-year three-tab shingles installed during those build-outs are overdue for replacement, and the underlying decking on those homes sometimes shows moisture damage that only becomes visible once the old material is stripped.

## Our Roofing Installation and Replacement Process in Pleasant Grove

Every project starts with a full deck inspection once the old roofing material is removed. In this climate, we treat deck assessment as a non-negotiable step rather than a visual check from the ground. Soft spots, delaminated OSB, and compromised rafter tails are common findings on homes that have been through several cycles of ice damming, and replacing shingles over a compromised deck shortens the life of the new roof significantly.

From there, the process follows a deliberate sequence: ice-and-water shield along all eaves and valleys (a critical layer given Pleasant Grove's ice dam exposure), synthetic underlayment across the field, and then the primary roofing material installed per manufacturer specifications. Flashing at chimneys, skylights, and wall transitions is replaced rather than reused, reusing old step flashing under new shingles is a shortcut that shows up as a leak within a few years in this climate. Ridge ventilation is checked and upgraded where attic airflow is insufficient, because proper ventilation is the single biggest factor in preventing premature shingle failure from heat buildup.
