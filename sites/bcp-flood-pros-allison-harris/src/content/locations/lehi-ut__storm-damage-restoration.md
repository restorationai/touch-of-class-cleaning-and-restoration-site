## Why Lehi Properties See Significant Storm Damage

Lehi's rapid growth over the past two decades means the city's housing stock spans a wide range of construction eras and styles. Newer master-planned communities in the northern and western parts of the city feature concrete tile roofs that resist impact well but can crack under large hail, sending fragments into gutters and leaving hairline fractures that don't leak immediately, only after the next storm. Older homes closer to Main Street and the historic core tend to have wood-framed roofs with three-tab shingles that show granule loss and bruising after a single severe hail event.

Lehi's position between Utah Lake to the west and the Wasatch foothills to the east creates a funnel effect during summer convective storms. Winds that might measure 30 mph in Salt Lake City can gust significantly harder as they compress through the valley, enough to lift unsecured patio covers, shear mature cottonwood limbs, and drive rain horizontally into soffit vents. Once water enters through a soffit, it typically travels along roof sheathing before dripping onto attic insulation, damage that reads dry at the ceiling surface for days while moisture saturates the structure above.

Winter brings a different threat: heavy, wet snow loads that accumulate on low-pitch roofs common in many of Lehi's newer subdivisions. Ice dams form when interior heat escapes through under-insulated attic spaces, melts the base of the snowpack, and refreezes at the cold eave, backing water under shingles and into wall cavities.

## Our Storm Damage Restoration Process in Lehi

Every storm job starts with a methodical inspection rather than a quick visual sweep. Hail damage to roofing, siding, and HVAC equipment is documented with photographs and moisture readings before any material is removed, that documentation is what your insurance adjuster needs to process the claim accurately.

For wind and tree damage, the first priority is stopping additional water intrusion. Roof tarping and board-up happen the same visit as the inspection. Once the structure is protected, we assess interior moisture using thermal imaging and calibrated meters to find water that has migrated behind walls or under flooring, places a visual inspection misses entirely.

Drying is structured around the affected materials. Wet insulation in an attic cavity dries on a different timeline than saturated OSB sheathing or water-logged drywall. We set drying targets based on the material type and monitor daily until readings confirm the structure has returned to an acceptable moisture range. Only then does reconstruction begin, replacing damaged sheathing, roofing, siding, or interior finishes.

## Lehi Insurance Coordination

Storm claims in Utah County follow standard homeowner policy structures, but the documentation requirements matter. Insurers want to see the date of loss tied to a verifiable weather event, itemized damage by trade (roofing, siding, structural, interior), and moisture readings that support the scope of drying work billed. We compile that documentation as part of our standard process, not as an add-on, so your adjuster has what they need without multiple site visits.

If your property is in an HOA community, some associations require pre-approval before exterior repairs begin, even for storm damage. It's worth a quick call to your HOA management company before roofing or siding replacement starts, as some Lehi HOAs specify approved materials and color matches for repairs.

## Local Note

Lehi's newer subdivisions built on the west side of the city sit on soil that was historically wetland and agricultural land adjacent to Utah Lake. That substrate drains slowly. After a heavy storm, window wells and below-grade entries in those neighborhoods can accumulate standing water faster than the soil can absorb it, and sump pumps in those homes work hard during a single event. If your sump pump ran continuously during the last storm and you're now noticing a musty smell in the basement, the pump may have kept up with the water, but not before some got under the slab or behind the foundation insulation. That's worth having checked before mold has time to establish.
