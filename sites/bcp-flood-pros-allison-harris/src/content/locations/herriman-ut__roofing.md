## Why Herriman Properties See Accelerated Roofing Wear

House for house, Herriman's roofing challenges come down to three converging factors: elevation, rapid growth, and soil movement.

At roughly 4,900 feet, Herriman receives meaningfully more snowfall than lower valley communities. That snowpack loads rooflines for weeks at a time, and when daytime temperatures climb above freezing while the roof deck stays cold, ice dams form at the eaves. Water backs up under shingles, works into the underlayment, and eventually reaches the sheathing and framing. By the time a homeowner notices a ceiling stain, the damage is often several layers deep.

High-growth communities also carry a specific risk: homes built quickly during a development boom can have roofing systems installed under schedule pressure, with underlayment laps, flashing details, or ventilation that does not meet the full intent of the building code. Herriman's expansion over the past two decades means a significant share of the housing stock is now approaching the 15-to-20-year mark, which is exactly when asphalt shingles begin losing granule coverage and losing their ability to shed water cleanly.

Finally, the expansive clay soils common across this part of Salt Lake County shift seasonally as they absorb and release moisture. That movement telegraphs into roof structures over time, opening gaps at ridge lines and around penetrations that were sealed correctly at installation.

## Our Roofing Installation and Replacement Process in Herriman

Every roofing project starts with a thorough inspection of the existing system, not just the surface shingles. We check the decking for soft spots, assess the condition of the underlayment, and look at every penetration point: vents, pipes, skylights, and chimneys. In Herriman's climate, flashing is where most failures originate, so we pay particular attention to step flashing along dormers and counter-flashing at chimney bases.

Once the scope is defined, tear-off and disposal are handled in a single mobilization. We install a high-quality synthetic underlayment rated for this climate, apply ice-and-water shield along the eave edges and in valleys where ice damming risk is highest, and then set the new shingles with proper nailing patterns for wind uplift resistance. The Oquirrh foothills can channel strong gusts, and fastener placement matters.

Ridge ventilation is sized to match the intake ventilation so the attic breathes correctly year-round. Poor attic ventilation is one of the fastest ways to shorten a new roof's lifespan in cold climates, and it is a step that is easy to skip or undersize.
