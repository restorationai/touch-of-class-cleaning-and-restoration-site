Herriman sits at the base of the Oquirrh Mountains, and that geography shapes how storms behave here. Cold fronts sweeping down from the peaks accelerate as they hit the valley floor, turning moderate rain events into wind-driven deluges that peel roofing, snap mature trees, and push water through exterior walls faster than most homeowners expect. When that happens, FIX Restoration is the team Herriman residents call to stop the damage from compounding and get the property back to a livable state.

## Why Herriman Properties Are Particularly Vulnerable to Storm Damage

Harriman's growth has been rapid. Large portions of the city were built in the 2000s and 2010s, which means the housing stock is mostly wood-frame construction with engineered lumber trusses, OSB sheathing, and composite roofing. These materials perform well under normal conditions, but they are sensitive to prolonged moisture exposure. When a storm drives rain under lifted shingles or through a compromised soffit, OSB can begin to swell and delaminate within hours, and the damage is not always visible from the attic hatch.

The clay-heavy soils common to this part of Salt Lake County also play a role. During heavy rain, water that cannot percolate fast enough pools against foundations and window wells, creating hydrostatic pressure that finds its way into basements and crawl spaces. Newer homes with unfinished basements are especially susceptible because the concrete is often the only barrier between standing exterior water and finished living space.

High winds are a year-round concern here, not just a spring phenomenon. The canyon effect created by the Oquirrh range can produce sustained gusts that uproot recently planted trees, which are common in newer subdivisions where the landscaping has not yet established deep root systems. A fallen tree on a roof is a structural emergency, not just a cosmetic one.

## Our Storm Damage Restoration Process in Herriman

The first priority after any storm event is stabilizing the structure. That means tarping breached rooflines, boarding compromised windows, and identifying any load-bearing elements that have shifted or been damaged. We document every affected area with photos and moisture readings before anything is moved or removed, because that documentation is what your insurance adjuster needs to process the claim accurately.

Once the structure is secured, we move to water extraction and drying. Storm water is not clean water. It carries debris, sediment, and contaminants from roofing materials, gutters, and soil, so it is handled accordingly. Industrial air movers and desiccant dehumidifiers run until every affected cavity reaches the target moisture content, which we verify with penetrating and non-penetrating meters rather than visual inspection alone.

Tree and debris removal is coordinated alongside the structural work, not sequenced after it. Leaving a fallen limb on a damaged roof while waiting for a separate crew adds hours of additional water intrusion. We stage the work so debris comes off and tarping goes on in the same site visit when possible.

Reconstruction follows once the structure is dry and the scope is fully documented. Roofing, siding, window replacement, drywall, insulation, and finish work are all within our scope, which means you are not managing a chain of separate contractors during an already stressful period.

## Local Note

One pattern we have observed in Herriman's newer subdivisions is that window wells are often installed without adequate gravel drainage beds beneath them, a shortcut that is not always caught during final inspection. During a heavy storm, these wells fill quickly and the water has nowhere to go except through the basement window frame. If your home has below-grade windows and you have experienced any water in the basement after a storm, the window well drainage is worth examining before the next weather event, not after it.
