## Why Bluffdale Properties Are Particularly Vulnerable to Open-Structure Damage

Bluffdale's rapid residential growth over the past two decades means the city's housing stock spans a wide range of construction vintages and styles. Newer subdivisions built on the western benches often feature open-span roof trusses and wide eaves designed for Utah's dry climate, but those same roof profiles can catch wind loads that older, steeper-pitched homes shed more easily. When a truss section lifts or a ridge cap blows free, the exposed sheathing can absorb significant moisture from even a brief rain event before drying conditions return.

The city also sits in a corridor where cold air drains south off the Oquirrh Mountains and collides with warmer valley air, producing localized gusts that can exceed what regional forecasts suggest. A storm that registers as moderate in Salt Lake County can arrive in Bluffdale with enough force to compromise a fire-damaged roof or push a boarded window back out of its frame. That local wind pattern is something to account for when selecting fastener spacing and plywood thickness during a board-up.

## Our Board-Up and Tarping Process, Calibrated to Bluffdale Conditions

Every job starts with a quick structural read before any material goes up. On a fire-damaged property, that means identifying load-bearing walls and checking whether the ceiling plane is still intact before a crew member steps onto a roof. On a storm-damaged home, it means locating the full perimeter of the breach, including any lifted flashing or cracked fascia that might not be obvious from the ground.

For roof tarping, we use reinforced polyethylene sheeting secured with batten boards screwed through the tarp and into solid sheathing, not just draped and weighted. That method holds through the kind of sustained afternoon gusts Bluffdale sees in March and April. For window and door board-ups, we cut plywood to fit the opening, seal the perimeter with foam backer rod where the frame is intact, and fasten into the structural framing rather than just the trim. On fire-damaged openings where the frame itself is compromised, we build a secondary anchor point before the panel goes in.

We photograph every step, label each panel, and leave a written summary of what was covered and why. That documentation travels directly to your insurance adjuster.

## Bluffdale Insurance Coordination

Temporary protective measures like board-up and tarping are almost always covered under the same claim as the underlying loss, whether that is fire, wind, or storm damage. Carriers typically require documentation that the work was necessary to prevent further damage, which is exactly what our photo log and written scope provide. We bill most major carriers directly and can communicate with your adjuster about the scope of temporary protection so that nothing is overlooked when the permanent repair estimate is assembled.

If your neighborhood falls under an HOA with exterior appearance standards, temporary boards and tarps are generally exempt from those rules during an active insurance claim, but we can provide the HOA with documentation of the work if they request it.

## Local Note

Bluffdale's newer subdivisions frequently use OSB roof sheathing rather than plywood, and OSB responds differently to moisture exposure once the protective coating on its edges is compromised. A tarp that is not properly battened and sealed at the edges can allow wind-driven rain to wick into exposed OSB within hours, causing the panel to swell and delaminate in ways that complicate the permanent roofing repair. We account for that material difference when selecting tarp overlap dimensions and batten placement, adding extra coverage at rake edges and ridge lines where OSB exposure is most likely.
