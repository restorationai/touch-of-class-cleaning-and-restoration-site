Sandy sits at the base of the Wasatch Front where snowmelt, irrigation season, and the occasional cloudburst can push water into a home faster than a sump pump can keep up. When a supply line fails behind a finished basement wall or a washing machine hose lets go on a Sunday morning, the window to prevent secondary damage is measured in hours, not days. FIX Restoration has been responding to water emergencies across the Salt Lake Valley since 2012, and the conditions specific to Sandy shape how we approach every job here.

## Why Sandy Properties Face Particular Water Damage Risks

Sandy's housing stock spans several decades of construction, and that range matters when water gets into a structure. Homes built in the 1970s and 1980s along the eastern benches often have slab-on-grade foundations with copper supply lines that have had decades to develop pinhole leaks. Newer developments closer to the I-15 corridor tend to feature finished basements with engineered wood subfloors, which can buckle and delaminate within 24 to 48 hours of standing water exposure.

The valley's clay-heavy soils compound the problem. When the ground is already saturated from spring runoff or a heavy irrigation cycle, water has nowhere to drain, and hydrostatic pressure against foundation walls increases. Window wells fill, crawl spaces flood, and even a modest rainstorm can overwhelm a drainage system that worked fine all winter. Sandy's position between the Wasatch foothills and the valley floor means elevation changes are real: a property on the upper bench drains differently than one closer to the Jordan River corridor, and we account for that when we assess source and migration patterns.

## Our Emergency Water Cleanup Process in Sandy

The first priority on any water call is stopping the source. If a homeowner hasn't already shut off the main, we locate it immediately. Sandy is served by the Salt Lake County Water Conservancy District and several municipal water systems depending on the specific address, so we're familiar with the shutoff configurations common to this area.

Once the source is controlled, we extract standing water using truck-mounted and portable extraction units. Water under flooring is a particular concern here: tile set over concrete is common in Sandy's ranch-style homes, and moisture migrates laterally under the slab before it shows up at the surface. Thermal imaging cameras help us map the actual wet zone, which is almost always larger than what's visible to the eye.

After extraction, we place drying equipment calibrated to the materials present: low-grain refrigerant dehumidifiers pull moisture from the air while air movers accelerate evaporation from structural cavities. We monitor moisture readings at each visit and adjust equipment placement until readings return to baseline for the region's ambient conditions.

## Local Note

Sandy has a significant number of homes built with stucco exteriors over wood-frame construction, particularly in subdivisions developed through the 1990s. Stucco is deceptively good at trapping moisture: the surface can feel dry to the touch while the sheathing behind it stays wet for weeks. If a roof leak or window flashing failure drives water into a stucco-clad exterior wall, standard moisture meters often underread the actual saturation. We use both pin and pinless meters together on stucco assemblies here, and we probe at multiple depths before declaring a wall cavity dry.
