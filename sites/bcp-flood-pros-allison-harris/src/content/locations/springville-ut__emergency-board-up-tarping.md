## Why Springville Properties Are Vulnerable After Storm and Fire Events

The housing stock in Springville spans a wide range of eras. Older homes in the central city, many built in the mid-twentieth century, use dimensional lumber framing and original single-pane windows that are more fragile under pressure and harder to seal with standard plywood cuts. Newer subdivisions climbing toward the foothills tend to have steeper roof pitches and larger roof planes, which means a single wind event can displace a larger section of underlayment in a short time.

Springville's position along the Wasatch Front also means it sits in a corridor for canyon-driven wind events funneling out of Spanish Fork Canyon to the south. The proximity to Utah Lake to the northwest also means humidity can move in quickly after a storm, accelerating the window in which an open roof or broken window allows moisture to reach insulation and sheathing.

## Our Board-Up and Tarping Process in Springville

Every job starts with a walk of the exterior to identify all breach points, not just the obvious ones. A fire that vents through a soffit can leave charred gaps that aren't visible from the street. A wind event that lifts flashing can allow water to travel laterally under sheathing before it shows up as a ceiling stain.

For roof tarping, we use reinforced polyethylene tarps secured with weighted battens and screwed perimeter boards rather than just staples or sandbags, a method that holds through the secondary wind events that commonly follow the initial storm in this part of Utah County. For window and door board-up, we cut plywood to fit the rough opening and fasten it in a way that preserves the surrounding trim so the eventual repair is cleaner and less costly.

Where a fire has compromised structural members or left a roof section unstable, we stage work from the ground or use extension equipment rather than putting crew on a weakened deck. The goal is a secure, weathertight envelope, not a cosmetic patch.
