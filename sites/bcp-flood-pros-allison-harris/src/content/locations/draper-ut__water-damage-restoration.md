## Our Water Damage Restoration Process in Draper

The first step on any water loss is locating the source and stopping it. That sounds obvious, but in multi-story homes common throughout Draper, water travels. A leak at a second-floor washing machine connection can saturate the subfloor, wick into the wall cavity below, and pool inside a first-floor ceiling before anyone sees a stain. Thermal imaging and moisture meters map the actual extent of the damage rather than just the visible wet area.

Once the source is controlled, water extraction begins. For standing water on hard surfaces, truck-mounted extraction units pull large volumes quickly. For water that has absorbed into carpet, pad, and subfloor, weighted extraction tools press down to pull moisture from the material rather than just the surface. After bulk extraction, the drying phase uses industrial air movers and refrigerant or desiccant dehumidifiers calibrated to the room's volume and the material types present. Moisture readings are logged at each visit so the drying curve is documented rather than estimated.

Walls that read dry at the surface can still hold significant moisture in the cavity. In Draper's newer construction, OSB sheathing and engineered lumber are common, and both hold moisture differently than dimensional lumber. Drying protocols are adjusted based on what's actually in the wall, not a generic assumption.
