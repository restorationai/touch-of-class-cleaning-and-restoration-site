## Why Saratoga Springs Properties See Fire Damage Differently

Saratoga Springs has grown faster than almost any city in Utah over the past two decades, and that growth shows in the housing stock. The vast majority of homes here were built after 2000, which means engineered lumber, OSB sheathing, and open floor plans, materials and layouts that burn faster and collapse sooner than older stick-frame construction. When fire moves through an open-concept kitchen and great room, smoke infiltrates HVAC systems, wall cavities, and attic insulation in minutes rather than hours.

The high desert climate adds another layer. With relative humidity regularly dropping into the teens during summer and winter months, smoke odor compounds bond more aggressively to porous surfaces like drywall, carpet padding, and cabinetry. That same dry air also means fire crews use significant water to suppress structure fires, and the resulting water damage, soaked insulation, saturated subfloor, standing water in basements, becomes a second restoration problem on top of the fire damage itself.

## Our Fire Damage Restoration Process in Saratoga Springs

The first step after a fire is a thorough structural and smoke assessment, not a quick walk-through, but a systematic inspection of load-bearing elements, roof decking, and every HVAC penetration where smoke traveled. In Saratoga Springs homes, that often means checking the truss systems common in newer builds, which can be compromised by heat even when they look intact from below.

Once the structure is cleared for re-entry, the process moves through these stages:

- **Board-up and tarping** to secure the property against weather and unauthorized entry while work is scoped
- **Debris removal and selective demolition** of charred materials, including any OSB or engineered lumber that has lost structural integrity
- **Smoke and soot cleaning** using dry chemical sponges, HEPA vacuuming, and thermal fogging to reach odor compounds in wall cavities and ductwork
- **Deodorization**, in Utah's dry climate, hydroxyl or ozone treatment is often necessary to neutralize smoke odor that has bonded deeply to low-moisture surfaces
- **Structural drying** for any water intrusion from suppression, followed by reconstruction to code

Every phase is documented with photographs and moisture readings for insurance purposes.
