Sandy sits at the base of the Wasatch Front where dry canyon winds off the mountains can drop humidity fast, but winter inversions trap moisture close to the ground for days at a time. When a burst pipe, kitchen fire, or basement flood damages the belongings inside a Sandy home, that swing between arid and stagnant air complicates how quickly furniture, documents, textiles, and electronics can be safely stabilized. FIX Restoration handles contents restoration and storage as a dedicated process, not an afterthought to structural drying, so your belongings get the same methodical attention as the walls around them.

## Why Sandy Homes Face Distinct Contents Damage Challenges

Sandy's housing stock spans several decades and construction styles. Neighborhoods closer to the older city core often feature homes built in the 1960s and 1970s with finished basements that doubled as family rooms and storage areas. Those below-grade spaces are exactly where contents losses concentrate: water migrates down, smoke and soot settle low, and the items stored there tend to be irreplaceable, things like family photos, seasonal clothing, holiday keepsakes, and older wood furniture that absorbs odor differently than modern composite materials.

The Wasatch Front's high-altitude UV and low average humidity mean that smoke residue on fabrics and hard surfaces behaves differently here than in coastal climates. Soot particles dry and bond to surfaces faster when indoor humidity drops, which is common during Sandy's long dry stretches. Acting quickly, before that bonding becomes permanent, is the difference between a textile that can be restored and one that cannot.

## Our Contents Restoration Process in Sandy

Every contents job begins with a room-by-room inventory before anything is moved. Items are photographed, catalogued, and assessed for restoration potential on-site. This documentation matters practically: it becomes the foundation of your insurance claim and protects you from disputes about what was present and what condition it was in before the loss.

After inventory, salvageable contents are packed out and transported to a climate-controlled storage facility. Sandy's temperature swings, from summer highs that push well past 95°F to winter nights well below freezing, make on-site drying risky for sensitive items. Controlled-environment storage removes that variable entirely. Inside the facility, items go through the appropriate restoration track: ultrasonic cleaning for hard goods and collectibles, ozone or hydroxyl treatment for odor in textiles and upholstered furniture, freeze-drying for water-damaged documents and photographs, and dry-cleaning or laundering for clothing.

Once your home is structurally cleared and dried, contents are returned, placed back in the rooms they came from, and cross-checked against the original inventory. Nothing is marked complete until you have confirmed the return.

## Sandy Insurance Coordination for Contents Claims

Contents losses are frequently underdocumented in insurance claims, not because adjusters are difficult, but because homeowners rarely have a pre-loss inventory. Our room-by-room photo catalogue fills that gap. We document item descriptions, visible damage, and restoration category for each piece, then organize that record in a format most major carriers accept directly.

Sandy properties in HOA communities sometimes carry master policies that interact with individual homeowner policies in ways that affect which carrier handles contents versus structure. We have worked through those coordination questions before and can help you identify which policy applies to which portion of the loss before work begins.

## Local Note

Homes in Sandy's older established areas often have wood-paneled basement rooms and built-in shelving that was finished in place decades ago. When water or smoke affects those spaces, the contents stored on and around that built-in millwork absorb odor from the wood itself, not just from the loss event. Treating the contents without addressing the millwork odor source produces a temporary result. We flag this during the initial assessment so the structural and contents scopes are coordinated from the start rather than discovered separately mid-project.
