## Why Draper Properties Face Particular Board-Up and Tarping Risks

Draper's elevation and position along the Wasatch Front create weather conditions that catch even well-maintained homes off guard. Chinook-style wind events can strip shingles and push debris through windows with little warning. In winter, heavy snow loads on steeper-pitched roofs common to newer Draper subdivisions can cause localized collapses or ice-dam failures that leave roof decking exposed overnight. In summer, the same geography funnels afternoon thunderstorms that bring hail capable of punching through older skylight glazing or damaging soffit panels.

Fire damage is the other common trigger. A kitchen fire or garage fire that is contained quickly still typically leaves one or more windows vented by firefighters for smoke exhaust. Those openings need plywood or polycarbonate sheeting before the structure can be left unattended, both for security and to satisfy most homeowner insurance policies that require reasonable mitigation steps.

## Our Board-Up and Tarping Process in Draper

Every job starts with a perimeter assessment before a single sheet of plywood goes up. We document every compromised opening with photographs, note the structural condition of the framing around it, and flag any areas where the substrate is too damaged to hold fasteners safely. That documentation goes directly into the claim file.

For roof tarping, we use reinforced polyethylene tarps secured with baton strips and screwed into sound decking rather than draped loosely over the ridge. A loose tarp in a Wasatch Front windstorm is not a tarp for long. For window and door openings, we cut plywood to fit the rough opening, fasten it to the surrounding framing, and seal the perimeter with foam backer rod where the gap is wide enough to allow water intrusion. On fire-damaged structures, we also check that the board-up does not trap residual smoke heat against smoldering material before we close the opening.

When HOA architectural guidelines apply, we note the board-up as temporary emergency work in our paperwork. Most Draper HOA communities recognize the distinction between emergency mitigation and a permanent alteration, but having the documentation in hand prevents misunderstandings with property management.

## Draper Insurance and HOA Coordination

Most standard homeowner policies in Utah cover emergency board-up and tarping as a mitigation expense, meaning it is typically billed against the same claim as the underlying damage rather than as a separate out-of-pocket cost. We photograph every opening before and after, measure the materials used, and provide itemized documentation formatted for carrier review. That paperwork is what adjusters need to approve mitigation costs without a site visit, which speeds up the overall claim.

For Draper properties governed by an HOA, we include a written description of the work performed and its temporary nature. If the HOA requires notification before exterior work, we can assist with that communication, though the homeowner remains the primary contact with their association.

## Local Note

Draper's newer master-planned communities tend to feature tile and synthetic shake roofing materials that behave differently under a tarp than standard asphalt shingles. Tile in particular requires baton placement that avoids direct pressure on individual tiles, which can crack under load and create new leak points. We adjust fastening patterns on those roofs to distribute weight across the battens rather than concentrating it on tile faces. It is a small detail that matters when the goal is to prevent additional damage, not create it.
