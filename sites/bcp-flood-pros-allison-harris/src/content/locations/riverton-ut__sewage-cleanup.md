## Why Riverton Properties See Sewage Backup Issues

Riverton's growth corridor along Redwood Road and Bangerter Highway brought thousands of homes online in a compressed window, many of them connected to sewer laterals that were installed quickly and are now aging into their second and third decade. Clay and cast-iron laterals common in older sections of the city are prone to root intrusion from the mature trees that line established streets. When roots crack a lateral or a grease blockage builds in a shared main, the pressure has nowhere to go but backward into the lowest drain in the house, which is almost always a basement floor drain, laundry tub, or toilet.

The valley's clay-heavy soil compounds the problem. During spring runoff from the Oquirrh Mountains to the west, groundwater tables rise and can infiltrate sewer lines through cracked joints, increasing flow volume at the municipal level and raising the likelihood of backups throughout the system. Homes on the lower elevations of Riverton, closer to the Jordan River corridor, are statistically more exposed to this pattern than properties on the higher benches.

## Our Sewage Cleanup and Sanitization Process in Riverton

Category 3 water, the classification that covers raw sewage, requires a different approach than a clean-water pipe burst. The contamination includes bacteria, viruses, and in some cases parasites, so the first priority is containment, not just extraction.

Here is how the process unfolds on a typical Riverton job:

- **Containment and assessment**: We identify the source and stop active flow where possible, then assess which materials are salvageable and which must be removed. Porous materials like drywall, insulation, and carpet that have direct sewage contact are almost always removed rather than cleaned.
- **Extraction and removal**: Standing sewage is extracted with truck-mounted or portable units. Contaminated materials are bagged, documented, and disposed of according to Utah solid waste handling requirements.
- **Disinfection and sanitization**: Hard surfaces, framing, and concrete are treated with EPA-registered disinfectants rated for Category 3 contamination. We work methodically from the highest contaminated point downward.
- **Structural drying**: After sanitization, we set drying equipment, including air movers and dehumidifiers, and monitor moisture readings daily until the structure reaches acceptable drying goals.
- **Post-remediation verification**: We document final moisture readings and surface conditions before closing out the project, giving you a clear record for your insurance file.

## Local Note

Riverton has a significant number of homes built in the 1990s and early 2000s with finished basements that were permitted as living space, complete with drywall to the floor, carpet, and in many cases a bathroom rough-in. When sewage backs up into one of these spaces, the contamination wicks into the bottom course of drywall within minutes and into the insulation behind it shortly after. Homeowners sometimes try to dry the surface without removing the drywall, which leaves saturated insulation and contaminated framing hidden behind a wall that reads dry on the outside. On Riverton jobs in this housing stock, we always probe behind finished walls before signing off on drying, because the visible surface is rarely the full picture.
