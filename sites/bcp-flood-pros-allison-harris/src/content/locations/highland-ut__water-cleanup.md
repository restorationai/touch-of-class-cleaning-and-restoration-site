## Why Highland Properties See Water Damage Issues

Highland's housing stock skews toward homes built in the 1990s through the 2010s, a construction era that favored engineered wood subfloors, OSB sheathing, and foam-backed carpet over the more forgiving materials of earlier decades. OSB is particularly unforgiving when wet: it can begin to swell and delaminate within hours of saturation, and once the face layer lifts, the subfloor often needs replacement rather than just drying. Finished basements, a near-universal feature in this part of Utah County, add another layer of complexity, because water trapped behind drywall or under luxury vinyl plank can read dry at the surface while the framing and insulation behind it stay wet for days.

The clay-heavy soils across much of northern Utah County also drain slowly. During heavy spring runoff or a sustained rain event, that poor drainage can overwhelm window wells and foundation drains, sending water into basements faster than a sump pump can cycle. If the pump loses power during a storm, or simply wasn't sized for a worst-case event, standing water can accumulate quickly in finished lower levels.

## Our Water Cleanup Process in Highland

The first priority on any water cleanup job is stopping the source, whether that means helping a homeowner locate the main shutoff, coordinating with a plumber, or identifying where surface water is entering the structure. Once the flow is controlled, the extraction phase begins. Truck-mounted extraction equipment pulls standing water from carpet, hard flooring, and low-lying areas far more efficiently than shop vacs or consumer-grade wet-dry units, and that speed matters: the longer water sits, the deeper it wicks into subfloor layers and wall cavities.

After extraction, the drying phase is where most of the technical work happens. Industrial air movers and desiccant or refrigerant dehumidifiers are positioned based on moisture readings taken with thermal imaging cameras and pin-type meters, not guesswork. In Highland's climate, where outdoor humidity can be low but indoor conditions vary widely depending on HVAC settings and basement depth, getting the equipment placement right from the start shortens the drying timeline and reduces the chance of mold colonization, which can begin within 24 to 48 hours on wet organic materials.

## Highland Insurance Coordination

Most standard homeowners policies cover sudden and accidental water discharge, a burst pipe, an appliance failure, an overflow, but not gradual leaks or groundwater intrusion, which is typically a separate flood policy. The distinction matters in Highland, where spring snowmelt events can blur the line between a drainage backup (often covered) and true surface flooding (usually not).
