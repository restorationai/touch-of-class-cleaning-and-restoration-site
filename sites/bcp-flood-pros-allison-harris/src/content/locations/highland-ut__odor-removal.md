That combination creates the conditions where odors don't just linger, they embed. Smoke from a single fireplace event, pet dander, or a slow moisture intrusion behind drywall can saturate insulation and framing when a house is buttoned up for months at a time. ## Why Highland Properties Hold Odors Differently

Highland's elevation and semi-arid climate mean homes rely heavily on mechanical ventilation rather than passive airflow. When a source odor, whether from smoke, a pet, a sewage backup, or a decomposition event, gets into the HVAC system, it circulates through the house repeatedly before anyone realizes the ductwork itself has become part of the problem. Tight building envelopes, which are common in Highland's newer subdivisions built to modern energy codes, trap volatile organic compounds (VOCs) that would otherwise dissipate in a leakier structure.

The area also sees a meaningful number of homes with finished basements, which are popular in Utah County for the added living space they provide. Basements in Highland can experience condensation during the shoulder seasons, spring and fall, when soil temperatures and interior temperatures diverge. That moisture, even at low levels, feeds the microbial activity that produces musty odors, and those odors are notoriously difficult to mask or neutralize without addressing the source and then treating the space with the right deodorization technology.

## Our Odor Removal and Deodorization Process in Highland

Effective odor elimination is not a single step. The process starts with identifying the source and the pathways the odor has traveled, which in Highland homes often means inspecting HVAC returns, checking finished basement walls for hidden moisture, and testing air quality in rooms that share a ventilation zone with the affected area.

Once the source is contained or removed, the treatment method is matched to the odor type and the materials involved:

- **Thermal fogging** penetrates porous surfaces, wood framing, drywall, and upholstery, the same way smoke does, neutralizing odor molecules rather than coating over them. It is particularly effective after fire and smoke events.
- **Hydroxyl deodorization** uses UV-generated hydroxyl radicals to break down odor-causing compounds in the air and on surfaces. Unlike ozone, hydroxyl generators can operate in occupied or semi-occupied spaces, which matters when a homeowner needs to stay nearby during treatment.
- **Ozone treatment** is reserved for unoccupied spaces and is one of the most thorough methods for severe odors. We calculate dwell time based on room volume and odor severity, then fully ventilate before re-entry.
- **HEPA air scrubbing** runs continuously during and after treatment to capture particulates that carry odor, including smoke soot and mold spores.

In Highland's climate, we often run dehumidification alongside deodorization in basement and crawl space areas to prevent residual moisture from reactivating microbial odors after treatment is complete.

## Equipment and Methods We Use for Odor Removal

Not every odor job calls for the same equipment, and part of what separates professional deodorization from consumer-grade products is the ability to match the tool to the problem. Thermal fogging equipment generates a dry fog that follows the same physical pathways smoke traveled, reaching into wall cavities and subfloor spaces that a surface spray cannot touch. Hydroxyl generators are particularly well-suited to Highland homes where occupants or pets cannot be fully relocated during treatment. Ozone machines deliver the highest concentration treatment but require strict unoccupied protocols that we walk every homeowner through before setup.

For smoke odor specifically, we combine thermal fogging with HEPA air scrubbing and, where soot has settled on surfaces, a pre-cleaning step using dry chemical sponges before any wet cleaning begins. Wet-cleaning soot before dry removal drives it deeper into porous materials and makes the odor harder to eliminate.

## Local Note

Highland's newer residential construction, much of it built in the 2000s and 2010s, frequently uses engineered lumber and OSB (oriented strand board) sheathing rather than solid-wood framing. OSB absorbs smoke and moisture odors more readily than dimensional lumber because of its resin-bonded composite structure, and it off-gasses those odors over a longer period after the source event. When we treat a Highland home that has experienced a smoke or water event, we specifically test OSB sheathing areas, including roof decking accessible from the attic, because surface readings can appear normal while the panel core is still holding odor compounds. Homeowners who notice a smell returning weeks after a fire or water event often have an OSB issue that was not addressed in the initial cleanup.
