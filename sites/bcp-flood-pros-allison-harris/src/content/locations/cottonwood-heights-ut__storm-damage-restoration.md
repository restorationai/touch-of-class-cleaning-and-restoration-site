Cottonwood Heights sits at the base of the Wasatch Front where the mountains compress and redirect storm systems in ways that can surprise even longtime Utah residents. Afternoon thunderstorms that look mild from the Salt Lake Valley floor can arrive here with hail, wind gusts, and enough debris to strip shingles, punch through soffits, and leave standing water in crawl spaces before the thunder stops echoing off the ridgeline. When that happens, FIX Restoration is ready to assess the damage, stop it from spreading, and get your property back to where it was.

## Why Cottonwood Heights Properties See Concentrated Storm Damage

The geography here is the starting point. Storms tracking down Big Cottonwood and Little Cottonwood canyons funnel wind and precipitation onto the benches and foothills neighborhoods that define much of Cottonwood Heights. What registers as a moderate storm in Murray or Holladay can arrive here with meaningfully more force, and the mature tree canopy throughout the city means falling limbs are a consistent hazard during high-wind events.

The housing stock adds another layer. A significant portion of Cottonwood Heights was developed in the 1960s and 1970s, and homes from that era typically have wood-framed roofs with original sheathing, single-pane windows in aluminum frames, and older flashing details around chimneys and skylights that were not engineered for the freeze-thaw cycling the Wasatch foothills deliver every spring. A hailstorm that leaves a newer home with cosmetic bruising can leave a 1968 ranch with compromised underlayment and water intrusion that does not show up as a stain on the ceiling until days later.

Soil conditions matter too. The bench soils common at this elevation drain reasonably well in normal conditions, but heavy storm runoff can overwhelm window wells, saturate foundation perimeter drains, and push water into basements faster than a sump pump can keep up, especially in homes where the original drainage infrastructure has never been updated.

## Our Storm Damage Restoration Process in Cottonwood Heights

The first step on any storm loss is a thorough exterior and interior inspection before any materials are moved or covered. Tarping a damaged roof is urgent, but tarping it before documenting the extent of the damage can create disputes with your insurance carrier. Our crew photographs and measures every affected surface, notes pre-existing conditions separately, and produces a scope of work that your adjuster can follow line by line.

Once the structure is stabilized, interior work begins in order of urgency: water intrusion points are dried with commercial dehumidifiers and air movers, debris is removed and categorized for your claim, and structural components are assessed for load-bearing integrity before any cosmetic repairs start. In homes with older plaster ceilings, which appear throughout Cottonwood Heights neighborhoods developed before 1975, we take extra time with moisture mapping because plaster holds water differently than modern drywall. The surface can read dry on a pin meter while the lath behind it stays saturated, which means drying timelines are longer and require more monitoring passes.

Tree damage cleanup follows a specific sequence as well. A tree through a roof requires a structural engineer's sign-off before the tree is fully removed in many cases, because the trunk may be carrying load temporarily. We coordinate that step rather than leaving it to the homeowner to chase down.

## Cottonwood Heights Insurance Coordination

Most standard homeowner policies in Utah cover sudden and accidental storm damage, but the documentation requirements vary by carrier and the language around "maintenance versus storm damage" can complicate claims on older roofs. We photograph conditions before, during, and after mitigation, and we produce a written scope that separates storm-caused damage from pre-existing wear. That distinction matters when your adjuster reviews the claim, and having it documented from day one avoids the back-and-forth that delays settlements.
