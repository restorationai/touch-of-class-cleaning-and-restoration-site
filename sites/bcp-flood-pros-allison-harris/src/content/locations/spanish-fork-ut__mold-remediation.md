## Why Spanish Fork Properties See Mold Issues

The Spanish Fork area experiences wide temperature swings between seasons, and the valley's position between the lake plain and the canyon mouth means humidity levels can shift significantly after rain or snowmelt events. Clay-dominant soils in much of Utah County drain poorly, so water that pools against a foundation tends to stay there longer than homeowners expect. Crawl spaces, common in homes built across Spanish Fork during the post-war and 1970s–1990s building booms, are especially vulnerable. Vapor barriers degrade, vents get blocked, and the combination of ground moisture and limited airflow creates exactly the environment mold needs. Attics are another recurring problem: inadequate soffit ventilation traps humid air in winter, and condensation on the underside of roof decking can go unnoticed until the discoloration is visible from inside the living space.

## Our Mold Remediation Process in Spanish Fork

Every job starts with a thorough visual inspection and moisture mapping using thermal imaging and calibrated meters, because surface mold is often the smallest part of the problem. What reads dry to the touch can be saturated two inches in, particularly in older plaster-and-lath walls or the OSB sheathing common in 1990s-era construction throughout the area.

Once we understand where moisture is hiding, we establish containment using negative air pressure and poly barriers to prevent spores from spreading to unaffected rooms during removal. Affected materials, drywall, insulation, subfloor sections, are removed, bagged, and disposed of according to Utah solid waste guidelines. We then treat structural surfaces with EPA-registered antimicrobial agents and run HEPA-filtered air scrubbers until spore counts in the work zone return to acceptable levels. The final step is a clearance inspection to confirm the space is ready for reconstruction before any new materials go in.
