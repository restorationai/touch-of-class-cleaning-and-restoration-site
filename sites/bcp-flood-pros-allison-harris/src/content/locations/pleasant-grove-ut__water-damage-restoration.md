## Why Pleasant Grove Properties See Water Damage Issues

The housing stock in Pleasant Grove spans several distinct eras. Neighborhoods developed in the 1970s and 1980s tend to feature slab-on-grade construction with copper supply lines that have had decades to fatigue at fittings and elbows. Newer subdivisions built in the 2000s and 2010s use PEX tubing, which is more flexible but still vulnerable to freeze-thaw stress in poorly insulated garage walls and exterior chases. Older homes near the historic downtown core sometimes retain original cast-iron drain lines that can crack under root intrusion or ground settlement.

Beyond plumbing, Pleasant Grove's position against the Wasatch bench means that heavy spring runoff and the occasional summer cloudburst can overwhelm window wells and foundation drains. Finished basements, extremely common in Utah County homes, are the first place that water collects, and carpet over concrete holds moisture long after the surface feels dry to the touch.

## Our Water Damage Restoration Process in Pleasant Grove

The first step on any water loss is containment and assessment. The crew identifies the water source, shuts it off if it hasn't been already, and uses moisture meters and thermal imaging cameras to map how far water has migrated through walls, subfloors, and ceiling assemblies. What reads dry at the surface is often still saturated an inch or two in, especially in the thicker drywall and insulated interior walls common to Utah County construction built for cold winters.

Once the scope is mapped, standing water is removed with truck-mounted extraction equipment. After bulk water removal, the drying phase begins: industrial air movers are positioned to create directed airflow across wet surfaces, and refrigerant or desiccant dehumidifiers pull moisture vapor out of the air and out of the building materials themselves. Drying is monitored daily with logged moisture readings so the process can be adjusted and documented, that documentation matters when an insurance adjuster reviews the claim.

Materials that cannot be dried in place, saturated insulation, buckled hardwood flooring, wicking drywall, are removed during the mitigation phase. Structural components like framing and subfloor sheathing are dried to manufacturer and IICRC S500 standard targets before any reconstruction begins.

One pattern worth knowing about Pleasant Grove specifically: homes built on the bench areas east of State Route 89 often have longer lateral drain runs to the city sewer connection because of the grade change.
