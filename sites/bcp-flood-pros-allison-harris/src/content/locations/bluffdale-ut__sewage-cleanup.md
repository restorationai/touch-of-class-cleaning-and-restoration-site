## Why Bluffdale Properties Experience Sewage Backups

The Wasatch Front's freeze-thaw cycle is hard on underground infrastructure. During late winter and early spring, ground movement along Bluffdale's valley floor can shift sewer laterals just enough to crack a joint or allow root intrusion from the ornamental trees common in newer subdivisions. When a lateral fails, the backup typically surfaces at the lowest drain in the home, which in most Bluffdale construction means a basement utility sink, a floor drain, or a basement bathroom. The problem is not always dramatic at first. Slow drains and a sulfur odor near floor-level fixtures are early signals that a lateral is partially blocked before a full overflow occurs.

Septic systems are also present in portions of Bluffdale that were built before the municipal sewer network expanded southward. A septic overflow carries the same Category 3 contamination risk as a municipal sewer backup and requires the same level of response: full removal of affected material, disinfection of structural surfaces, and air quality management to control airborne pathogens.

## Our Sewage Cleanup and Sanitization Process in Bluffdale

Raw sewage is classified as Category 3 water, meaning it carries bacteria, viruses, and other pathogens that make standard water extraction protocols insufficient on their own. The process FIX Restoration follows in Bluffdale addresses both the visible contamination and the materials that absorbed it.

**Containment and assessment** come first. The affected area is isolated to prevent cross-contamination to unaffected rooms, and the extent of sewage migration is mapped, including any wicking into drywall, insulation, or subfloor material.

**Extraction and removal** follow. Standing sewage is extracted, and porous materials that absorbed contaminated water, including drywall, baseboards, carpet, and pad, are removed. In Bluffdale's newer construction, engineered wood subfloors are common and are particularly susceptible to delamination when saturated with sewage; affected sections are documented and removed rather than dried in place.

**Disinfection and sanitization** of structural surfaces use EPA-registered antimicrobial agents applied to concrete, framing, and any hard surfaces that can be retained. This step is not optional and cannot be abbreviated, because residual pathogens on structural surfaces can cause ongoing health concerns.

**Drying and dehumidification** bring the structural cavity to a safe moisture level before any reconstruction begins.
