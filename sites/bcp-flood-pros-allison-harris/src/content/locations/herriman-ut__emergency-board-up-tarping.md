## Why Herriman Properties Are Particularly Vulnerable to Open-Structure Damage

Harriman has grown rapidly over the past two decades, and that growth means the housing stock spans a wide range of construction vintages and styles. Newer subdivisions built on the city's western benches often feature open-span roof trusses and large window configurations that look striking but leave significant surface area exposed when glass breaks or roofing fails. At the same time, the city's elevation and position along the Oquirrh foothills mean wind speeds during spring and fall fronts regularly exceed what's typical for the broader Salt Lake Valley floor. A compromised roof deck or boarded opening that would hold fine in calmer conditions can fail quickly here when a pressure system rolls through.

The soil composition in much of Herriman also trends toward expansive clay in lower-lying areas, which means foundation movement is an ongoing reality for some homeowners. Cracked or shifted exterior walls create gaps that aren't always obvious until a fire or impact event reveals them. When we board up a fire-damaged structure in Herriman, we probe for those secondary gaps before we leave, because a single missed opening can let wind-driven rain reach framing that was otherwise unaffected.

## Our Board-Up and Tarping Process in Herriman

Every board-up and tarping job starts with a perimeter walk to identify every breach, not just the obvious ones. Fire damage in particular creates openings that aren't visible from the street: melted soffit vents, warped door frames that no longer seat, and window glazing that's intact but cracked through. We photograph and document each opening before we cover it, which matters later when your insurance adjuster reviews the scope.

For window and door board-up, we use plywood cut to fit and secured with structural screws rather than staples or adhesive, which hold poorly against Herriman's wind loads. For roof tarping, we use reinforced poly tarps weighted and fastened at the ridge and eaves, not just draped and tucked. A tarp that lifts in a 40-mph gust does nothing for the structure underneath and can create a hazard for neighboring properties. Where roofing damage is extensive, we may use multiple overlapping tarp sections to ensure full coverage across the affected span.
