## Why Cottonwood Heights Properties See Water Damage Differently

The geology under Cottonwood Heights works against homeowners during a water event. The Wasatch Front's clay-rich soils absorb moisture slowly and hold it longer than sandy or loam-based ground, which means water that enters a crawl space or lower level has nowhere to wick. Homes built on the benches above the valley floor often have walk-out basements or daylight foundations, and those below-grade spaces are the first to flood when a supply line bursts or a window well fills.

The area also experiences a wide swing in seasonal conditions. Hard freezes in January and February expand water in supply lines that run through exterior walls or uninsulated garage spaces, and rapid thaws in March can overwhelm drainage systems that were not designed for that volume. Homes built in the 1970s and 1980s, which make up a significant share of the housing stock in this part of Salt Lake County, frequently have galvanized or early-copper supply lines that are now at or past their service life. A small pinhole leak in one of those lines can saturate wall cavities for days before the surface shows any sign.

## Our Emergency Water Cleanup Process in Cottonwood Heights

The first priority when we arrive is stopping active water intrusion. If the source is a plumbing failure, we confirm the shutoff is complete before any equipment goes in. From there, the process moves in a specific order because skipping steps creates hidden moisture problems that surface weeks later.

**Water extraction** comes first. We use truck-mounted and portable extraction units to pull standing water from hard surfaces, carpet, and subfloor assemblies. In finished basements common to Cottonwood Heights ranch-style homes, that often means pulling carpet and pad to reach the concrete slab beneath, which holds water in its pores even after the surface looks dry.

**Moisture mapping** follows extraction. We use thermal imaging cameras and calibrated moisture meters to trace where water has traveled inside wall assemblies, under flooring, and into ceiling cavities if an upper-floor supply line failed. This step matters especially in homes with spray-foam or blown-in insulation, where water can migrate along framing without appearing on the surface.

**Structural drying** uses commercial air movers and dehumidifiers sized to the cubic footage of the affected space. We set drying goals based on the material type and ambient conditions, and we return to check readings until the structure reaches its dry standard. In Cottonwood Heights winters, the low outdoor humidity actually helps interior drying when ventilation is possible, but sealed, energy-efficient homes built after 2000 require more mechanical dehumidification because they do not breathe naturally.

**Documentation** runs throughout the process. Moisture readings, photos, and equipment logs are compiled into a report that supports an insurance claim or provides a clear record for a property sale.
