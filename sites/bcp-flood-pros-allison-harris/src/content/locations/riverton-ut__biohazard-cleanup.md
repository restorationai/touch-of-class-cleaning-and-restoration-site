## Why Riverton Properties Present Unique Biohazard Considerations

Riverton's growth pattern matters here. A significant portion of the city's residential stock was built during the 1990s and early 2000s construction boom, which means many homes feature engineered wood subfloors, OSB sheathing, and composite materials that absorb biological contamination differently than solid wood or concrete. These materials are porous at a microscopic level, and surface cleaning alone does not address what has wicked into the substrate. Proper remediation requires removal of affected materials down to a cleanable surface, not just treatment of what is visible.

Riverton also has a notable share of finished basements, which are common throughout Salt Lake County. Finished lower levels with carpet, drywall, and drop ceilings create layered environments where contamination can migrate behind walls or under flooring without being immediately apparent. ## Our Biohazard Cleanup Process in Riverton

Every job begins with a careful assessment of what materials are affected and what can be safely restored versus what must be removed. Throughout the process, the crew works in personal protective equipment and uses contained work practices to prevent cross-contamination to unaffected areas of the home or building.
