## Why Riverton Properties See Mold Problems

The Jordan River corridor and the irrigation-heavy landscaping common throughout Riverton's newer subdivisions keep soil moisture elevated through much of the spring and summer. Homes built on slab foundations in the 1990s and early 2000s, which make up a large share of Riverton's housing stock, can develop slab edge moisture intrusion that wicks into bottom plates and lower wall cavities without any visible leak ever occurring. The finished basements that are nearly universal in this area add another layer of complexity: vapor barriers installed during original construction age and fail, and by the time a musty smell reaches the living space, mold colonies behind the drywall may already be well established.

Winter in Riverton also contributes. Cold exterior walls and warm interior air create condensation zones inside wall assemblies, particularly on north-facing exposures. That repeated freeze-thaw cycle through Utah winters keeps framing members cycling between damp and dry, which is exactly the condition mold needs to persist even when there is no active leak.

## Our Mold Remediation Process in Riverton

Effective mold abatement starts with understanding what you cannot see. Before any containment goes up, the scope of the problem needs to be mapped using moisture meters, thermal imaging, and air sampling. Surface mold that is visible on drywall or framing is almost always the indicator, not the full extent.

Once the affected area is defined, containment barriers with negative air pressure isolate the work zone so spores are not redistributed through HVAC systems into clean areas of the home. Affected materials that cannot be cleaned, typically drywall, insulation, and compromised OSB sheathing, are removed and bagged for disposal. Remaining structural surfaces are treated and dried to the moisture content levels required to stop regrowth. The final step before reconstruction can begin is clearance verification, confirming that airborne spore counts in the remediated area are back within normal range.

In Riverton's finished basements, this process often requires removing and replacing sections of drop ceiling and wall framing that were installed directly against foundation walls without an adequate air gap, a common construction shortcut in subdivisions built through the late 1990s.
