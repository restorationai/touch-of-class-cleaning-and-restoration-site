## Why Lehi Properties Are Vulnerable to Water Damage

Lehi sits at the edge of Utah Lake's basin, and the water table in lower-elevation areas of the city can be surprisingly shallow, a factor that becomes critical when a foundation drain backs up or a sump pump fails during spring snowmelt. The Wasatch Front's freeze-thaw cycle is another persistent driver of water losses here: temperatures that drop hard overnight in January and February can freeze supply lines in garages, exterior walls, and crawl spaces, then thaw rapidly during an afternoon warm spell. That sudden pressure release is one of the most common sources of emergency calls in this area.

Lehi's newer master-planned communities, many built quickly to keep pace with one of the fastest-growing cities in the country, sometimes feature tract construction where plumbing runs share wall cavities across multiple units. A leak in one unit's supply line can migrate laterally before it ever shows up on the floor below, making the source harder to trace and the affected area larger than it first appears.

## Our Water Damage Restoration Process in Lehi

When we arrive at a Lehi property, the first priority is stopping the source, whether that's a failed washing machine supply hose, a ruptured irrigation line, or a water heater that let go in a utility closet. From there, the process moves through distinct phases:

**Assessment and moisture mapping.** We use thermal imaging and calibrated moisture meters to trace water migration behind drywall, under flooring, and into structural cavities. In Lehi's newer construction, engineered lumber and OSB subfloor absorb water differently than solid wood, they can delaminate if drying is delayed, so mapping happens before any equipment is placed.

**Water extraction.** Truck-mounted and portable extraction units pull standing water from hard surfaces, carpet, and pad. In finished basements, common in Lehi's larger single-family homes, we extract from multiple access points to avoid leaving pockets of moisture under slab transitions.

**Structural drying.** Industrial air movers and refrigerant dehumidifiers run in a calculated configuration based on the room's cubic footage and the materials affected. Drying typically takes several days; we monitor readings at each visit and adjust equipment placement as conditions change.

**Documentation.** Every moisture reading, equipment log, and photo is recorded in a format your insurance adjuster can use directly.

## Lehi Insurance Coordination

Most homeowner policies in Utah cover sudden and accidental water discharge, a burst pipe, an appliance failure, an ice dam that forces water under roofing. What they typically exclude is slow seepage or gradual leaks, which is why the cause of loss matters and why we document it carefully from the moment we arrive. We photograph conditions before any work begins, generate moisture logs that align with carrier documentation requirements, and communicate directly with adjusters when questions come up about scope. If your HOA has a master policy that overlaps with your individual unit coverage, a common situation in Lehi's townhome and condo communities, we can help you understand which policy applies to which materials before work begins.

## Local Note

Lehi's older neighborhoods near Main Street include homes built in the mid-20th century with plaster-over-lath wall systems rather than modern drywall. When water gets behind plaster, the surface can feel and read nearly dry while the lath and framing behind it remain saturated, a condition that standard pin meters can miss if they're not inserted deeply enough. We adjust our monitoring protocol on these older homes, using both penetrating and non-penetrating sensors to get an accurate read on actual drying progress rather than surface conditions alone. It's a small procedural difference that prevents a lot of callbacks.
