## Why Eagle Mountain Properties See Mold Issues

Eagle Mountain sits at a higher elevation than much of the valley floor, but that does not insulate it from moisture problems. The city's clay-heavy soils drain slowly, which means water from snowmelt or a hard spring rain can pool against foundation walls and wick inward over time. Many of the newer subdivisions were built quickly to meet demand, and vapor barriers, window flashing, and roof-to-wall transitions are the details most likely to be rushed. A pinhole gap in window flashing on a west-facing wall can let moisture accumulate inside the wall cavity for months before it becomes visible on the interior surface. By then, mold has often colonized the paper facing of the drywall and the wood framing behind it.

The city's growth also means a wide range of construction vintages sitting side by side. Homes built in the early 2000s used different moisture management standards than those built in the last few years, and older crawlspace designs in some of the earlier phases of development can trap ground moisture under the living space. Crawlspaces in Eagle Mountain that lack adequate cross-ventilation or a sealed vapor barrier are among the most common sources of mold calls we receive from this part of the county.

## Our Mold Remediation Process in Eagle Mountain

Mold remediation is not the same as mold removal. Wiping visible growth off a surface without addressing the moisture source and the spore load in the air accomplishes very little. The process starts with a thorough assessment: moisture readings taken at the surface and behind it, visual inspection of adjacent cavities, and documentation of the affected area before any material is disturbed.

Containment comes next. Plastic sheeting and negative air pressure isolate the work zone so that spore-laden dust does not migrate into unaffected rooms during demolition. Affected drywall, insulation, or flooring is removed and bagged before it leaves the containment area. HEPA-filtered air scrubbers run continuously throughout the process. Once the structure is clean and dry, an antimicrobial treatment is applied to framing and other porous surfaces that can be retained. The area is then dried to acceptable moisture levels before any reconstruction begins. In Eagle Mountain's climate, where indoor humidity can spike in winter when homes are sealed tight, we verify those moisture readings carefully before signing off.
