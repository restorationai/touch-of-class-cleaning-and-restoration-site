## Why Riverton Properties See Water Damage Issues

Riverton's growth over the past two decades brought a mix of housing stock: established neighborhoods with homes built in the 1980s and 1990s alongside newer subdivisions that pushed toward the Herriman border in the 2000s and 2010s. The older homes frequently have original polybutylene or early CPVC supply lines that have reached or passed their expected service life. Polybutylene in particular is notorious for failing at fittings without warning, often inside walls or under slabs where the leak goes unnoticed until a ceiling starts to sag or a floor begins to buckle.

Riverton also sits at an elevation where winter temperature swings are sharp. A stretch of below-freezing nights followed by a warm January afternoon creates the freeze-thaw cycle that cracks exterior hose bibs and uninsulated garage supply lines. Crawl spaces in the area's ranch-style homes are especially vulnerable because they often lack adequate vapor barriers, leaving wood framing exposed to both ground moisture and the condensation that follows a pipe failure.

Spring runoff from the Wasatch adds another variable. When snowmelt volume exceeds what the Jordan River corridor can absorb, shallow water tables rise, and window wells and below-grade entries in Riverton's split-level and daylight-basement homes become collection points for intrusion water.

## Our Water Damage Restoration Process in Riverton

When we arrive, the first priority is stopping ongoing water movement. That means locating the source, confirming it is controlled, and then mapping the actual spread of moisture with thermal imaging cameras and calibrated moisture meters. Water visible on a surface is rarely the full picture. In Riverton's stucco-clad homes, moisture can travel laterally inside the wall cavity for several feet before showing any outward sign.

Once we have a clear moisture map, we extract standing water with truck-mounted and portable extraction units, then set a calculated drying system: commercial-grade refrigerant dehumidifiers, high-velocity air movers, and desiccant units for tighter or colder spaces. Drying is not guesswork. We log moisture readings at every affected material, every day, and adjust equipment placement based on the data. Structural drying in a Riverton basement with a concrete slab typically takes longer than drying above-grade wood framing because concrete releases moisture slowly and unevenly, particularly when the slab is in contact with damp soil.

Contents that can be moved are inventoried and relocated to a dry area or packed out for off-site cleaning. Affected drywall and insulation that cannot be dried in place are removed to the flood cut line, which also gives us a clear view of the framing and subfloor so nothing stays hidden behind closed walls.

Most standard homeowner policies in Utah cover sudden and accidental discharge, which covers the majority of pipe failures and appliance supply line breaks common in Riverton homes. ## Local Note

Riverton's newer subdivisions built in the mid-2000s through the 2010s frequently used engineered hardwood and glue-down LVP flooring over concrete slabs. When water gets under those floors, the adhesive traps moisture against the slab and prevents natural evaporation. Standard air movers placed on top of the floor accomplish very little in that situation. The correct approach is to pull the flooring, dry the slab directly, and test the concrete's moisture vapor emission rate before any new flooring goes down. Skipping that step is one of the most common reasons secondary mold issues appear weeks after a water loss appears to be resolved.
