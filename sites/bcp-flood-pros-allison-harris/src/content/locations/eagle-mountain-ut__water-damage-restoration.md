## Why Eagle Mountain Homes See Water Damage Differently

Most of Eagle Mountain's housing stock was built between roughly 2000 and the present, which means two-by-six framing, OSB sheathing, and engineered lumber floor systems rather than the solid-wood framing you find in older Wasatch Front neighborhoods. OSB is efficient and strong when it stays dry, but it swells, delaminates, and loses structural integrity faster than dimensional lumber once water gets behind it. A slow leak behind a dishwasher or a pinhole in a PEX supply line can saturate an OSB subfloor for weeks before the laminate flooring above shows any visible sign.

The other factor is elevation and pressure. Eagle Mountain's water is delivered through a series of pressure zones that serve its hillside terrain. Higher-pressure zones can stress fittings and appliance connections over time, and when a fitting does let go, the volume of water released in a short window is significant. Finished basements, which are common here given the larger lot footprints, are the first casualty.

## Our Water Damage Restoration Process in Eagle Mountain

When FIX Restoration arrives at a water loss in Eagle Mountain, the first step is containment, not extraction. Stopping the source matters before anything else, and we work alongside the homeowner or a plumber to confirm the supply is shut before equipment goes in.

From there, the process follows a structured sequence:

- **Moisture mapping**: Thermal imaging and calibrated moisture meters read behind walls, under flooring, and inside cavities where water travels but does not announce itself.
- **Water extraction**: Truck-mounted and portable extraction units pull standing water from carpet, pad, and hard flooring. On slab-on-grade construction common in Eagle Mountain, we pay particular attention to the perimeter where water pools against the foundation stem wall.
- **Structural drying**: Commercial-grade desiccant and refrigerant dehumidifiers, paired with high-velocity air movers, are positioned to dry the structure from the inside out. Drying targets follow industry standards for moisture content in wood and building materials.
- **Daily monitoring**: A technician checks readings each day. Drying is not declared complete based on time alone; it is declared complete when the numbers say so.
- **Documentation**: Every moisture reading, every equipment placement, and every photo is logged. That documentation matters when you file with your carrier.
