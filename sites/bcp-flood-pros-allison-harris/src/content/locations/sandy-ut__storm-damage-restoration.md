That combination of high-desert heat, sudden moisture, and canyon-driven wind gusts creates a storm damage pattern that is genuinely different from what contractors see in the Salt Lake valley floor. ## Why Sandy Properties See Distinctive Storm Damage

The terrain matters more than most homeowners realize. Cold air drains out of Little Cottonwood and Big Cottonwood canyons during late-summer thunderstorms, accelerating surface winds just as hail is falling. Roofs on the east-facing slopes of Sandy's hillside neighborhoods absorb the brunt of that combination, while homes closer to the valley floor deal with sheet-flow water that has nowhere to go once storm drains back up.

Sandy's housing stock adds another layer of complexity. Neighborhoods developed in the 1970s and 1980s often have original wood-framed construction with T1-11 siding or cedar shake roofing that has decades of weathering behind it. A storm that would cause cosmetic damage to newer fiber-cement siding can split or delaminate older wood panels entirely, opening wall cavities to water intrusion within hours. Attic insulation in those same homes is frequently fiberglass batt that compresses and loses R-value when it gets wet, which means the damage is not always visible from the street or even from inside the attic hatch.

## Our Storm Damage Restoration Process in Sandy

The first step after any storm is a thorough exterior and interior assessment. That means walking the roof, probing siding seams, checking the attic plane for daylight, and using moisture meters at the wall base and around window frames where wind-driven rain tends to find its way in. What looks like surface damage on a shingle or a bent gutter is often the least of the problem.

Once the scope is clear, the work moves in a logical sequence: emergency tarping or board-up to stop active water entry, controlled demo of saturated materials, structural drying with commercial-grade dehumidifiers and air movers, and then reconstruction. For tree-impact damage, structural framing often needs evaluation before any cosmetic repair begins, because a limb that punches through a roof deck can shift rafter connections even when the visible hole looks contained.

Throughout the process, FIX Restoration documents every affected area with photographs and moisture readings. That documentation is the foundation of a clean insurance claim, and it protects homeowners if a carrier questions the scope weeks later.
