## Why Cedar Hills Properties Face Distinct Fire Damage Challenges

Cedar Hills is a relatively young city, with most of its residential development concentrated in subdivisions built from the 1990s onward. That housing stock means homes are largely wood-frame construction with engineered lumber, OSB sheathing, and composite materials that burn differently than older solid-wood framing. When those materials ignite, they produce dense, oily soot that clings to walls, insulation, and ductwork in ways that simple cleaning cannot address.

The city's position along the Wasatch foothills also means homes are frequently exposed to dry, gusty canyon winds, particularly in late summer and fall. Those conditions accelerate fire spread and can push smoke deeper into a structure before suppression crews arrive. Attic spaces and crawlspaces, common in the area's ranch and two-story floor plans, often trap smoke and heat in ways that are not immediately visible from the main living areas.

Utah County's elevation and low humidity also affect how soot and smoke odor behave after a fire. Dry air at altitude can cause smoke particulates to migrate further through porous surfaces, making odor neutralization more involved than it would be in a more humid climate.

## Our Fire Damage Restoration Process in Cedar Hills

Every fire loss is different, but the sequence of work follows a clear path designed to stop ongoing damage first, then restore.

The first step on-site is a full structural assessment: identifying what is safe to enter, what has compromised load-bearing capacity, and where smoke and soot have traveled beyond the burn area. Thermal imaging helps locate heat migration into walls and ceilings that look intact on the surface.

From there, the process moves into controlled demolition of materials that cannot be cleaned, followed by HEPA-filtered dry soot removal on surfaces that can be restored. Smoke odor is addressed through a combination of hydroxyl generation and thermal fogging, which reaches into wall cavities and ductwork rather than just masking surface smells. Content pack-out is coordinated for salvageable belongings, with itemized documentation that supports the insurance claim.

Structural drying runs concurrently when firefighting water is present, which is common. Water damage layered under fire damage is one of the more complex restoration scenarios, and addressing both at the same time prevents secondary mold growth from taking hold in the weeks after the fire.

## Local Note

Cedar Hills homes built in the 2000s and 2010s frequently used spray foam insulation in attic spaces, which was common in Utah County new construction during that period. Spray foam holds smoke odor tenaciously and is difficult to clean in place. In a fire loss where the attic is involved, that insulation often needs to be removed rather than treated, which affects both the scope and the timeline of restoration. It is worth flagging this with your adjuster early, because insurers sometimes initially scope attic work as a cleaning line item rather than a removal and replacement line, and the difference in cost is significant.

If your Cedar Hills home has spray foam in the attic and has experienced any fire or smoke intrusion into that space, ask specifically about attic insulation assessment when you call. It changes the plan.
