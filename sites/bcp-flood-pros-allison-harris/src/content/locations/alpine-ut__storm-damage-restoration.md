## Why Alpine Properties Face Particular Storm Risks

Alpine's position along the Wasatch Front means it catches both canyon-driven windstorms and the heavy late-spring snowfall that can persist at this elevation long after the valley floor has dried out. That combination is hard on roofing systems. Homes built in Alpine's earlier subdivisions often have wood-framed attic structures with older felt underlayment beneath the shingles. When that underlayment is compromised by wind uplift or ice damming, water can travel several feet laterally before it finds a ceiling seam, making the entry point difficult to trace from inside.

The soil in this part of Utah County also tends to be dense and slow-draining in areas where clay content is high. During a heavy storm event, that means surface water pools against foundations rather than percolating away, and window wells and basement egress points become the path of least resistance. Crawlspace flooding after a severe storm is a pattern FIX Restoration sees repeatedly in this elevation band of the Wasatch foothills.

## Our Storm Damage Restoration Process in Alpine

The first step on any storm job is a systematic exterior and interior assessment before any materials are moved or covered. Wind damage in particular can create hidden pathways: a lifted soffit panel, a cracked ridge cap, or a displaced flashing section may not look dramatic, but each one is an open door for the next rainfall. We document every affected area with photos and written notes before work begins, which matters both for your insurance claim and for making sure nothing is missed.

Once the scope is clear, the sequence typically runs from exterior stabilization inward. Tarping and board-up protect the structure while interior drying begins. For water intrusion from storm-driven rain or roof failure, we use commercial desiccant and refrigerant dehumidifiers alongside air movers calibrated to the room volume and material type. Saturated insulation in attic spaces is removed rather than dried in place, because fiberglass and cellulose batts that have been soaked rarely return to their original thermal performance even after drying. Structural materials are moisture-mapped with penetrating meters before any reconstruction begins.
