## Why Eagle Mountain Properties Face Distinct Board-Up and Tarping Challenges

Eagle Mountain's rapid growth over the past two decades means the city's housing stock is almost entirely builder-grade construction from the early 2000s onward. That era of framing used engineered lumber and OSB sheathing rather than solid dimensional lumber, which matters during emergency board-up work because OSB exposed to precipitation degrades quickly. A roof section left open after storm damage or a fire can see sheathing delaminate within a single wet cycle, turning a contained structural problem into a much larger one.

The Cedar Valley also sits in a high-elevation wind corridor. Gusts that move through the valley can lift improperly secured tarps off a damaged roof in hours, not days. Standard poly tarps stapled at the edges are not adequate here. Ballasted and mechanically fastened systems, with overlapping layers weighted at ridgelines and valleys, hold through the overnight temperature swings that are common from October through April.

Fire damage board-up in Eagle Mountain also involves the city's newer HOA communities, many of which have architectural review requirements. While emergency protective work is generally exempt from standard approval timelines, some HOAs request documentation that the board material and fastening method will not cause additional damage to exterior finishes. Keeping a paper trail from the start avoids friction when permanent repairs begin.

## Our Emergency Board-Up and Tarping Process in Eagle Mountain

When FIX Restoration arrives on site, the first step is a visual perimeter assessment before any material goes up. For fire damage, that means checking whether the structural load path is intact enough to safely access the roof or upper floors. For broken window board-up, it means confirming the frame is stable and that glazing fragments are cleared before plywood is seated.

For roof tarping, the crew measures the affected span and selects a tarp that extends well past the damaged area on all sides, with enough material to wrap over the ridge rather than terminate at it. Ridgeline termination is a common failure point in high-wind areas. Boards are cut on site to fit the specific opening, not pre-cut to a standard size, because Eagle Mountain's production homes vary enough in window and door dimensions that standard sizing leaves gaps.

Documentation runs throughout the process: photos of the damage before any material is placed, photos of each fastening point, and a written scope that the homeowner receives before the crew leaves. That record supports insurance claims and gives the repair contractor a clear picture of what is underneath the protective layer.

## Eagle Mountain Insurance Coordination

Most homeowner policies in Utah cover emergency protective measures as part of the covered loss, meaning board-up and tarping costs are typically foldable into the same claim as the underlying damage. FIX Restoration documents the work in a format that most major carriers accept: line-itemized scope, timestamped photos, and material quantities. Providing that package to your adjuster early prevents disputes over whether the protective work was necessary or proportionate.

If your carrier requires a preferred vendor, ask them whether independent contractors with documented scopes are accepted. Many Utah policies allow the homeowner to choose their contractor as long as the work is properly documented.
