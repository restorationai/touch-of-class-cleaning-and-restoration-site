## Why Pleasant Grove Properties See Mold Issues

The city's position along the Wasatch bench means soil drainage varies sharply from one block to the next. Homes built closer to the foothills tend to sit on rockier ground with faster runoff, while properties on the valley floor, particularly in older subdivisions developed through the 1970s and 1980s, can have shallow water tables and crawl spaces that collect seasonal moisture. That crawl space moisture is one of the most common mold entry points we see in this area: vapor barriers degrade, vents get blocked by landscaping, and fiberglass batt insulation between floor joists absorbs humidity and holds it against the subfloor for months.

Pleasant Grove also experiences significant freeze-thaw cycling through late winter and early spring. Water that infiltrates a foundation crack during a warm spell can freeze, expand, and widen that crack before the next thaw, and each cycle pushes a little more moisture toward interior wall cavities. By the time a homeowner notices discoloration on a baseboard or a persistent musty odor, the mold colony behind the wall may already be well established.

## Our Mold Remediation Process in Pleasant Grove

Every job starts with a thorough moisture assessment, not just a visual inspection, but thermal imaging and moisture meter readings at the wall cavity level. Mold abatement that skips this step frequently misses the actual source, and the problem returns within a season.

Once we've mapped the affected area, we establish containment using negative air pressure and physical barriers to prevent spores from migrating to unaffected rooms during removal. Porous materials, drywall, insulation, wood framing that has lost structural integrity, are removed and bagged on-site. Non-porous surfaces are cleaned with EPA-registered antimicrobials and HEPA-vacuumed. Drying equipment runs until moisture readings return to baseline for the material type, not just until the surface feels dry to the touch.

For homes with crawl spaces, which are common throughout Pleasant Grove's older residential stock, we include encapsulation assessment as part of the scope. A mold cleanup that stops at the subfloor without addressing the crawl space vapor barrier is an incomplete job in this climate.

## Local Note

Pleasant Grove has a meaningful share of homes built in the 1960s through 1980s that used paper-faced drywall with lower moisture resistance than modern products. In those homes, mold remediation often reveals that the paper facing has fully delaminated from the gypsum core, which means the board needs to come out entirely rather than being surface-treated. Homeowners sometimes expect a mold cleanup to be a cleaning job; in this housing stock, it's frequently a controlled demolition and rebuild. Knowing that before the estimate prevents surprises mid-project.
