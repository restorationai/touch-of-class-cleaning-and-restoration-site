## Why Cottonwood Heights Properties See Water Damage Issues

The geography here works against homeowners in a few specific ways. Properties on the eastern benches sit on clay-heavy soils that drain poorly once saturated, so a heavy spring storm or a slow irrigation leak can push groundwater laterally into crawl spaces and slab edges before anyone notices. The Wasatch Front's dramatic temperature swings, from subfreezing nights to warm afternoons in late winter, create ideal conditions for supply line failures: copper and older galvanized pipes expand and contract repeatedly until a joint or fitting gives way.

Cottonwood Heights also has a substantial inventory of homes built in the 1960s through 1980s, when builders favored finished basements with drop ceilings and wall cavities that are difficult to inspect without removing material. Water that enters through a window well or a crack in the foundation block can travel behind paneling and sit against the framing for days before it becomes visible. By that point, secondary damage to insulation and structural wood is already in progress.

## Our Water Damage Restoration Process in Cottonwood Heights

Every job starts with a thorough moisture assessment using thermal imaging and calibrated meters, not just a visual walk-through. In homes with the kind of layered construction common here, that step is what separates a complete dry-out from one that leaves hidden moisture behind.
