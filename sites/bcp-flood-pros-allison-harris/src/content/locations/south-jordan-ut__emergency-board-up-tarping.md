## Our Emergency Board-Up and Tarping Process in South Jordan

When a call comes in, the first step is a quick phone assessment: what type of opening, how large, what caused it, and whether the structure is safe to enter. That information lets the crew load the right materials before leaving the shop rather than making a second trip.

On site, the process follows a consistent sequence:

1. **Hazard assessment** before any ladders go up. Fire-damaged framing, compromised soffits, and wet roof decks all change how a crew stages.
2. **Measurement and material cut** for plywood panels or heavy-gauge poly sheeting, sized to overlap intact structure rather than just cover the opening.
3. **Fastening to sound substrate.** On South Jordan's common truss-framed roofs, that means locating rafter tails or ridge board rather than relying on OSB alone, which will not hold screws under wind load.
4. **Sealing edges** with construction-grade tape or batten strips to prevent wind uplift and water infiltration at the margins.
5. **Photo documentation** of all openings before and after, sized and labeled, which becomes part of the insurance file.

For roof tarping specifically, the tarp is anchored with 2x4 battens screwed through the tarp into solid framing, not simply weighted at the corners. A tarp that lifts in a Jordan Narrows wind event has done nothing.

## South Jordan Insurance and HOA Coordination

Most homeowners insurance policies treat emergency board-up and tarping as a covered mitigation expense, separate from the underlying claim for fire or storm damage. FIX Restoration documents the work in a format that most major carriers accept: itemized scope, photographs, and material quantities. ## Local Note

South Jordan's newer subdivisions frequently use tile roofing over lightweight concrete or clay, which is common in communities developed after 2000 in this part of the valley. Tile roofs require a different tarping approach than asphalt shingle: the tarp cannot simply be laid flat and battened because the tile profile creates gaps that channel water underneath. The crew needs to work around tile courses rather than across them, and in some cases the damaged tile field needs to be temporarily cleared to create a flat substrate before the tarp goes down. Homeowners who have had a contractor simply throw a tarp over broken tile and call it done often find water intrusion continues until the tarp is re-set correctly.
