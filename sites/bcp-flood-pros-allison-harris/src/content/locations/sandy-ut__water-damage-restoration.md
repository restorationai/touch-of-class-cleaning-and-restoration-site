When a supply line fails at 2 a.m. or a backed-up drain sends water across a finished basement floor, the clock starts immediately. ## Why Sandy Properties See Water Damage Issues

Sandy's elevation and position along the Wasatch foothills mean the city gets meaningful snowpack every winter. When temperatures swing in late February and March, that snow melts fast. Soil that has been frozen solid for weeks can't absorb runoff quickly, and that water finds the path of least resistance: window wells, crawlspace vents, and foundation cracks. The Jordan River corridor and the lower-elevation neighborhoods closer to the valley floor also sit on soils with higher clay content, which holds moisture against foundations rather than draining it away.

The city's housing stock adds another layer of complexity. Sandy saw significant residential development in the 1970s and 1980s, and homes from that era commonly have galvanized steel supply lines that corrode from the inside out. A pipe that looks intact at the wall can be paper-thin at a fitting. Polybutylene plumbing, installed in many Utah homes through the early 1990s before it was phased out, is another recurring source of sudden failures. When those pipes let go inside a finished basement, water spreads under LVP and laminate flooring before it's visible at the surface.

## Our Water Damage Restoration Process in Sandy

The first priority on any water loss is stopping the source and containing the spread. Once that's done, the work moves through a structured sequence: moisture mapping with thermal imaging and calibrated meters to find water that has migrated behind walls or under subfloor; extraction of standing water using truck-mounted and portable units; and then controlled drying using commercial dehumidifiers and air movers positioned to pull moisture out of structural materials rather than just the air.

In Sandy's colder months, drying timelines can run longer than the IICRC S500 standard baseline because cold ambient temperatures slow evaporation. We account for that by adjusting equipment placement and monitoring readings daily rather than assuming a fixed schedule. Affected materials are documented with moisture readings at each visit so there's a clear record for your insurance carrier and for the contractor handling any reconstruction.

## Local Note

One thing that comes up specifically in Sandy's hillside neighborhoods is the combination of stucco exteriors and finished walkout basements. Stucco can look perfectly intact while holding moisture in the scratch coat behind it after a roof leak or window seal failure. That trapped moisture wicks down the wall cavity and shows up as a water stain on the basement ceiling weeks after the original event. By then, the source is dry and the stain looks like old damage, but the framing inside the wall is still elevated. Thermal imaging is the only reliable way to confirm whether the moisture is active or historic before deciding on a repair scope.

## Sandy Insurance Coordination

Most standard homeowners policies in Utah cover sudden and accidental water losses, which includes burst pipes, appliance failures, and certain types of roof-related intrusion. Gradual leaks and maintenance-related deterioration are typically excluded, and carriers will look closely at the timeline and cause of loss when evaluating a claim. FIX Restoration documents the loss thoroughly from the first visit: photos of affected materials, moisture readings with timestamps, and a written scope of work that aligns with the carrier's requirements.
