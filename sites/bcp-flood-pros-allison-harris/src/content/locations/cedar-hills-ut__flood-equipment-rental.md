## The Drying Process: What to Expect When You Rent

Effective drying follows a sequence, and skipping steps is how people end up with mold behind baseboards six weeks after a loss. When you pick up or receive delivered equipment from FIX Restoration, the process should look like this:

1. **Remove standing water first.** Air movers and dehumidifiers cannot overcome active pooling. A submersible pump clears the bulk water so drying equipment can do its job.
2. **Establish airflow across wet surfaces.** Air movers are positioned to create a vortex effect, pulling humid air off wet materials and pushing it toward the dehumidifier's intake.
3. **Capture the moisture.** The dehumidifier condenses water vapor out of the air and either drains it continuously or collects it in a tank that needs emptying.
4. **Monitor daily.** Moisture readings taken at the same locations each day tell you whether the drying curve is trending correctly or whether a hidden pocket of saturation is being missed.

In Cedar Hills homes with finished basements, pay particular attention to the junction between the concrete slab and the bottom plate of framed walls. That seam is where water wicks upward into framing and insulation long after the floor surface feels dry.

## Local Note

Cedar Hills homes built in the 2000s frequently used a popular floating laminate floor system over concrete slabs in finished basements. When water gets under that flooring, the laminate acts as a vapor barrier in reverse, trapping moisture against the concrete and the bottom of the underlayment pad. Standard air movers positioned at floor level will not dry this assembly effectively. The floor panels need to be lifted in sections and air movers directed horizontally across the exposed slab surface. It is a step that adds time to the job but prevents the musty odor and buckled replacement costs that show up months later if the slab is sealed back over while still wet.
