Sandy sits at the foot of the Wasatch Range, where high-desert wind events, heavy spring snowpack, and the occasional hailstorm can compromise a roof or shatter a window with little warning. When that happens, the gap between the damage and a proper repair is exactly when a property is most vulnerable, to weather, to theft, and to secondary losses that insurers scrutinize closely. Getting structural openings sealed fast is not a courtesy; it is the first line of defense against a manageable claim becoming a much larger one.

## Why Sandy Properties Face Distinct Board-Up and Tarping Needs

Sandy's elevation and position along the Wasatch Front create weather patterns that differ meaningfully from the Salt Lake valley floor. Cold air drainage off the mountains accelerates overnight temperature drops, which means a broken window or a fire-damaged roof opening that feels manageable at dusk can allow significant moisture intrusion, or freezing pipe exposure, by early morning. The region also sits in a corridor that funnels canyon winds southward, and those gusts can lift an improperly anchored tarp in hours.

The city's housing stock adds another layer of complexity. Neighborhoods developed during the rapid suburban growth of the 1970s and 1980s feature wood-frame construction with roof pitches and attic ventilation designs that were standard for the era but can make tarping more involved than it looks from the ground. Older shake or composition roofs, common on homes built before the mid-1990s, are more brittle when cold, which means crews need to move carefully to avoid creating new damage while securing the tarp.

## Our Emergency Board-Up and Tarping Process in Sandy

The process begins with a fast assessment of every compromised opening, not just the obvious ones. Fire damage, for example, often weakens structural members near windows and eaves that are not immediately visible. A crew that boards only the front-facing breach and misses a softened soffit panel is leaving the property exposed.

For window and door board-up, we use exterior-grade plywood cut and fastened to preserve the structural integrity of the frame rather than just covering the opening. For roof tarping, we secure tarps with weighted boards and mechanical fasteners rather than relying on adhesive or bungee systems that fail in Wasatch Front wind events. Every tarp is lapped and sealed at the ridge or high point so water sheds away from the opening rather than pooling over it.

We photograph every step. That documentation goes directly into the claim file and gives adjusters a clear record of the property's condition at the time of service, which matters when there is any question about pre-existing damage versus storm or fire loss.
