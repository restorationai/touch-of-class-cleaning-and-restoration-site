Cottonwood Heights sits at the base of the Wasatch Range, where elevation changes, heavy spring snowpack, and freeze-thaw cycles through winter put roofs through conditions that flatland contractors rarely encounter. A roof that performs adequately in the Salt Lake valley floor can fail years early here, where ice dams form along eaves and UV exposure at altitude degrades shingles faster than manufacturer warranties typically account for. If your home is showing curling shingles, granule loss in the gutters, or daylight through the attic, a full roofing installation or replacement is likely overdue.

## Why Cottonwood Heights Roofs Wear Faster Than You Might Expect

The city's position along the Wasatch Front means it receives some of the heaviest snowfall in the greater Salt Lake area, with significant accumulation in a single storm season. That weight load, combined with the dramatic temperature swings between sunny winter afternoons and sub-freezing nights, creates repeated expansion and contraction cycles that work fasteners loose and crack sealants around flashings. Homes built in the 1970s and 1980s, which make up a substantial portion of the residential stock in Cottonwood Heights, were typically built with 3-tab shingles and minimal attic ventilation by today's standards. Inadequate ventilation traps heat in summer and moisture in winter, both of which shorten shingle life significantly. By the time a homeowner notices interior staining, the decking underneath may already have soft spots.

The city's proximity to Big Cottonwood Canyon also means wind events are not rare. Katabatic winds funneling down canyon corridors can lift improperly nailed tab edges and compromise ridge cap sections that were already weathered.

## Our Roofing Installation and Replacement Process in Cottonwood Heights

Every project begins with a roof inspection that goes beyond counting missing shingles. We assess decking condition, evaluate flashing at every penetration point (chimneys, skylights, plumbing vents, HVAC curbs), and check soffit and ridge ventilation to ensure the new system will perform correctly from day one.

Once the scope is confirmed, the process follows a clear sequence:

- **Tear-off and decking evaluation**: Old materials are removed and decking is inspected for rot, delamination, or fastener pull-through. Any compromised sections are replaced before new materials go down.
- **Ice and water shield installation**: Given Cottonwood Heights winters, ice-and-water barrier is applied at eaves and in valleys, not just at code minimums.
- **Underlayment and shingle installation**: We use synthetic underlayment for improved moisture resistance, then install shingles with fastener patterns appropriate to the local wind exposure zone.
- **Flashing and ridge cap**: Step flashing, counter flashing, and ridge cap are installed and sealed to handle the thermal movement common at this elevation.
- **Final inspection and cleanup**: All debris is cleared, gutters are checked, and the completed roof is photographed for your records and any warranty documentation.

Homes built along the steeper grades in Cottonwood Heights often have complex roof geometries, including multiple valleys, dormers, and varying pitches on a single structure.
