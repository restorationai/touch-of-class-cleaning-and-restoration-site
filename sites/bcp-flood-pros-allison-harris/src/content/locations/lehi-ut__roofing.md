## Why Lehi Properties Face Roofing Pressure

Elevation and climate combine here in ways that shorten roof lifespans compared to lower-altitude communities along the Wasatch Front. Lehi sits at roughly 4,500 feet, where ultraviolet exposure is meaningfully more intense than at sea level. Asphalt shingles that might last 25 years in a milder climate can show significant granule loss and brittleness within 15 to 18 years here. Add the pressure from ice damming, which forms when heat escapes through under-insulated attic decks and refreezes at the eave line, and you have a cycle that stresses flashing, gutters, and the sheathing underneath.

Lehi's newer master-planned communities also tend to have steeper decorative roof pitches, which look sharp but concentrate runoff at valleys and require precise flashing installation to prevent water intrusion at those seams. On the other side of the city, older homes built before the tech-corridor boom often have simpler roof geometries but aging underlayment that no longer provides a reliable secondary water barrier.

## Our Roofing Installation and Replacement Process in Lehi

Every project starts with a full roof inspection: we walk the deck, probe for soft spots in the sheathing, and check the condition of all penetrations, including plumbing vents, HVAC curbs, and skylights. If the decking is compromised, we document it before any new material goes down, because covering deteriorated sheathing is one of the most common shortcuts that leads to repeat failures.

For full replacements, we strip to bare deck, install a self-adhering ice-and-water shield at all eaves and valleys (a step that matters especially in Lehi winters), then layer synthetic underlayment across the field before any shingles or roofing panels are set. Starter courses, ridge ventilation, and step flashing at all wall intersections are installed to manufacturer specifications. We pull the necessary permits through Lehi City before work begins, and inspections are coordinated so the project closes with a clean record.

New installations on homes still under construction follow the same material standards, with scheduling coordinated around the framing and HVAC rough-in timeline so roofing is dried-in before interior work proceeds.

## Local Note

Lehi's growth has been so fast that some newer subdivisions were built on fill soil that continues to settle unevenly in the years after construction.
