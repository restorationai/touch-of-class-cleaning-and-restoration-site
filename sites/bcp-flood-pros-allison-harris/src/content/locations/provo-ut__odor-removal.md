## Why Provo Properties Hold Odors Longer

Provo's housing stock spans a wide range of construction eras, from mid-century brick ramblers near the university corridor to newer stucco builds in the foothills. The older brick and plaster construction common in many established Provo neighborhoods is particularly absorptive. Porous masonry and original hardwood subfloors act like sponges for smoke and biological odors, pulling volatile compounds into the material matrix rather than letting them sit on the surface. A surface spray or a plug-in air freshener does nothing for odor that has migrated into a brick wall cavity or beneath a 1960s-era oak floor.

The valley's inversion events compound this. During late fall and winter, cold air pools along the valley floor and traps smoke from fireplaces, wildfire events, and even neighboring properties. Homes with older, less-airtight windows and doors absorb ambient smoke during these episodes in ways that newer, tightly sealed construction does not. If your home sat through a prolonged inversion while a neighbor's chimney was drafting poorly, the odor you are smelling in your living room may have nothing to do with anything that happened inside your house.

## Our Odor Removal and Deodorization Process in Provo

Effective odor elimination starts with identifying where the odor compound has actually settled, not just where it smells strongest. We use thermal imaging and moisture meters to locate secondary sources behind walls and under flooring before any treatment begins. From there, the method depends on the odor type and the materials involved.

For smoke odor removal following a fire or an inversion event, thermal fogging is often the most effective first step. A fogging agent is heated into a dry vapor that follows the same pathways smoke traveled, reaching into wall cavities, HVAC ducts, and subfloor voids. For biological odors from sewage, pets, or decomposition, hydroxyl deodorization is frequently the better choice because hydroxyl generators work continuously in occupied or semi-occupied spaces without requiring evacuation. Ozone treatment is highly effective for severe smoke or mold odors in unoccupied structures, but ozone requires the space to be vacated and properly ventilated before re-entry, which affects scheduling in ways we discuss with every Provo homeowner upfront.

After treatment, we verify results with air quality readings rather than relying on a technician's nose. Odor perception is subjective; instrument readings are not.
