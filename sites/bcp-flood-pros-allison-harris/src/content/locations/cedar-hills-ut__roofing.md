## Why Cedar Hills Roofs Wear Out Faster Than You Might Expect

The Wasatch Front's elevation swing is the biggest factor most homeowners underestimate. Cedar Hills regularly sees temperatures drop well below freezing overnight in late fall and early spring, then climb above freezing by midday. That daily freeze-thaw cycle forces water into microscopic cracks in shingles, flashing, and ridge caps, then expands it. Over a few seasons, what starts as a hairline gap becomes a pathway for water to reach the decking.

Winter snow loads compound the problem. The Wasatch Range funnels significant snowfall into the foothills communities along the north end of Utah County, and Cedar Hills is directly in that path. Heavy, wet snow sitting on a roof that was installed without proper ventilation or adequate underlayment will shorten the lifespan of even a quality shingle by years. Ice dams form at eave lines when attic heat escapes unevenly, and the water backed up behind those dams finds every seam it can.

The housing stock in Cedar Hills also matters. Much of the city developed during the 1990s and early 2000s, which means a significant number of roofs in the area are now approaching or past the 20-to-25-year mark where asphalt shingles typically begin to fail. Granule loss, curling edges, and cracked tabs are common findings on homes of this vintage throughout the area.

## Our Roofing Installation and Replacement Process in Cedar Hills

Every project starts with a thorough inspection of the existing system, including the decking beneath the shingles. In Utah County's climate, it is common to find moisture damage in the decking that is invisible from the surface, particularly around valleys and near dormers where snow tends to accumulate. Any compromised decking is replaced before new materials go down, because a new roof over a soft deck is a short-term fix that will fail early.

Once the deck is confirmed sound, the installation sequence is calibrated for the Wasatch Front's conditions:

- **Ice and water shield** is run along all eaves and valleys, extending well past the interior wall line to protect against ice dam backup.
- **Synthetic underlayment** covers the full field of the deck, providing a secondary moisture barrier that holds up better than traditional felt in high-wind and high-UV environments.
- **Shingle selection** is matched to the home's pitch, exposure, and the homeowner's long-term goals. For Cedar Hills properties, impact-resistant shingles rated Class 3 or Class 4 are worth a serious look given the hail exposure the area sees in late spring and early summer.
- **Ridge ventilation and soffit intake** are evaluated and corrected if needed, because proper airflow is what prevents the attic heat buildup that causes ice dams in the first place.

All work is coordinated with Utah County permitting requirements. Permit pulls and inspections are part of the process, not an afterthought.
