## Why Orem Properties Are Vulnerable to Moisture Damage

Orem sits at the base of the Wasatch Front, and that geography shapes how water behaves here. The soil across much of the city transitions between clay-heavy layers near the foothills and sandier fill in the lower elevations closer to Utah Lake. Clay soil doesn't drain, it holds water against foundation walls and pushes hydrostatic pressure against basement slabs for days after a storm or a snowmelt event. Homes built during Orem's rapid growth periods in the 1960s through the 1980s often have uncoated block foundations and minimal interior drainage, which means a wet spring can translate directly into standing water on a basement floor.

The region also experiences temperature swings that accelerate secondary damage. A basement that floods in March may still be cold enough to slow evaporation naturally, which sounds helpful but actually extends the window for mold colonization, mold can begin establishing in as little as 24 to 48 hours in damp, poorly ventilated spaces, even at lower temperatures. Running the right combination of air movers and a refrigerant or desiccant dehumidifier keeps the drying process moving regardless of outdoor conditions.

## Local Note: Orem's Older Housing Stock and Subfloor Drying

Homes built in Orem during the 1960s and 1970s frequently have tongue-and-groove subfloor panels or board sheathing rather than modern OSB or plywood. That older wood is often denser and more dimensionally stable, which sounds like an advantage, but it also means it releases absorbed moisture more slowly than modern engineered panels. If you've had water under your carpet or hardwood for more than a few hours, plan your equipment rental for a longer run than you might expect. Surface readings on a moisture meter can show improvement while the subfloor below is still well above safe levels. Positioning air movers at low angles along the floor and running the dehumidifier continuously, not cycling it off overnight, is the difference between a five-day dry and a ten-day dry.
