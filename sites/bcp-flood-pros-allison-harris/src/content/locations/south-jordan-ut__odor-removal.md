South Jordan sits in the Salt Lake Valley at an elevation where summer heat regularly pushes past 100°F and winter temperature swings are sharp. That combination matters for odor problems: heat drives volatile organic compounds deeper into porous materials, while the valley's dry air creates a false sense that a smell has dissipated when the source is still very much present. Whether smoke from a kitchen fire has settled into the drywall of a newer Daybreak-area townhome or pet odor has worked its way into the carpet pad of an older South Jordan rambler, surface sprays and ventilation alone will not reach what the odor molecules have already bonded to. FIX Restoration uses equipment-based deodorization methods calibrated to the specific material and contamination type, not a one-size approach.

## Why South Jordan Properties Hold Odors Longer Than You'd Expect

The valley's low humidity is a double-edged factor. Dry air slows the natural off-gassing that would otherwise carry odor compounds out of a structure, meaning smoke residue, sewage gases, and protein-based odors from fire or decomposition can stay chemically active inside porous materials for months without treatment. South Jordan's newer residential construction, which makes up a large share of the housing stock west of Bangerter Highway, relies heavily on engineered wood framing, spray foam insulation, and tightly sealed building envelopes. Tight envelopes are great for energy efficiency but they trap odor-laden air and give it nowhere to go. A structure that holds heat well in January also holds smoke smell well in March.

Older homes closer to the city's original core tend to have more permeable construction: wood-framed walls with less airtight sheathing, original hardwood subfloors, and crawl spaces that communicate more freely with the living area. Those properties present a different challenge: odors can migrate into spaces that are harder to access and treat.

## Our Odor Removal and Deodorization Process in South Jordan

Effective deodorization starts with identifying what generated the odor and what materials it has penetrated, because the treatment method follows the contamination, not the other way around.

**Inspection and source confirmation.** Before any equipment is deployed, the affected area is assessed for residue type, material porosity, and HVAC pathways. Smoke from a structural fire behaves differently than cigarette smoke that has built up over years, and both differ from a sewage backup or a decomposition event.

**Thermal fogging.** For smoke and fire odors that have penetrated wall cavities, cabinet interiors, and soft goods, thermal fogging delivers a deodorizing agent in a fine particulate that follows the same pathways the smoke originally traveled. It reaches inside wall seams and ductwork in ways that spray-on products cannot.

**Hydroxyl generation.** Hydroxyl generators produce hydroxyl radicals that break down odor-causing molecules at the chemical level without requiring occupants to vacate for extended periods. This method is well suited to South Jordan homes where re-occupancy timelines matter, particularly in owner-occupied properties where families need to return quickly.

**Ozone treatment.** High-concentration ozone is reserved for severe contamination in unoccupied spaces. It is highly effective on protein odors and deep smoke saturation but requires the space to be completely vacated and properly ventilated before re-entry.

**HVAC and duct deodorization.** Because South Jordan homes run forced-air systems year-round in both heating and cooling seasons, contaminated ductwork will redistribute odors throughout a structure every time the system cycles. Duct treatment is a standard part of the process, not an add-on.
