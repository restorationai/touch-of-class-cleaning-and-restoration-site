## Why Cottonwood Heights Homes Face Particular Contents Challenges

The housing stock in Cottonwood Heights spans several decades, with a significant portion of homes built during the post-war suburban expansion of the 1960s and 1970s. Those homes often feature original hardwood floors, built-in cabinetry, and older upholstered furniture that absorbs smoke odor and water differently than modern materials. Smoke residue from a kitchen fire or a wildfire smoke event settles into porous wood grain and fabric fibers; at Cottonwood Heights elevations, lower humidity during late summer and fall means that soot particles dry and bond to surfaces faster than they would at valley floor elevations.

Water losses here also carry a specific risk. When a pipe bursts during a January cold snap or a roof fails under heavy snowpack, the water that soaks into contents is often very cold. Cold water slows the visible signs of damage, which can give homeowners a false sense that items are recoverable when mold colonization is already beginning in upholstered cushions and cardboard storage boxes. Acting within the first 24 to 48 hours matters enormously for contents that have been soaked at near-freezing temperatures.

## Our Contents Restoration Process, Calibrated to This Area

Contents restoration is not simply cleaning and returning items. The process begins with a detailed inventory at your home, photographing and cataloging every affected item before anything is moved. This documentation is essential for insurance claims and gives you a clear record of what left your property.

Items that cannot be safely restored on-site, including upholstered furniture, rugs, artwork, electronics, and soft goods, are packed and transported to a climate-controlled storage facility. In Cottonwood Heights, where outdoor temperatures can swing dramatically between a warm afternoon and a freezing night, off-site climate control is not optional for sensitive materials. It prevents secondary damage that would otherwise occur if items sat in an unheated garage or a moving truck.

Cleaning methods are matched to the material: ultrasonic cleaning for hard goods and non-porous items, dry-cleaning and ozone treatment for fabrics and soft furnishings, and specialized document drying for paper records. Smoke odor is addressed at the molecular level, not masked with fragrance. Once items are confirmed clean and dry, they are stored securely until your home is ready to receive them.

Homes in Cottonwood Heights that were built in the 1960s and 1970s frequently have finished basement spaces with drop ceilings and carpet over concrete slab.
