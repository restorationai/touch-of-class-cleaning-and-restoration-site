Orem sits at the foot of the Wasatch Range, and that geography shapes how storms arrive and what they leave behind. Spring systems that funnel down Utah Valley can drop hail the size of marbles on rooftops along State Street one afternoon and dump two inches of rain on the benchlands east of I-15 the next morning, sometimes within the same storm cell. When that happens, the damage is rarely just cosmetic. Torn shingles, wind-driven debris, and overwhelmed gutters can push water into wall cavities and attic assemblies before a homeowner realizes the storm has passed. FIX Restoration, based in American Fork, responds to storm losses across Orem and the broader Utah County area.

## Why Orem Properties Are Vulnerable to Storm Damage

Utah Valley's position between the Wasatch Front and the Oquirrh Mountains creates a compression effect that can accelerate wind speeds as storms move through. Orem's elevation and exposure to the lake-effect moisture patterns that sweep down from the Salt Lake Valley mean the city sees a wider range of storm types than many Utah communities, heavy wet snow in late spring, intense summer thunderstorms, and occasional microbursts that snap mature trees and drive branches through roofing.

The housing stock adds another layer of complexity. Neighborhoods developed in the 1960s and 1970s, which make up a significant portion of Orem's residential areas, were built with roof pitches and sheathing standards that predate modern wind-load requirements. Older wood-framed roofs with board sheathing rather than plywood can delaminate unevenly when exposed to prolonged moisture, and the damage may not be visible from the street. Stucco exteriors, common on mid-century Orem homes, can absorb and hold water behind the finish layer when flashing is compromised, a problem that often doesn't surface until weeks after a storm.

## Our Storm Damage Restoration Process in Orem

The first priority after any storm loss is stopping water from traveling further into the structure. That means emergency tarping of breached rooflines, boarding compromised windows, and, when standing water has entered living spaces, beginning extraction before drying equipment is even staged. In Orem's climate, where daytime temperatures can swing significantly between seasons, the drying strategy has to account for ambient humidity and temperature inside the structure, not just what the weather station is reporting outside.

Once the structure is stabilized, the work moves to documentation. Every affected surface is photographed, moisture readings are logged with calibrated meters, and the scope of structural damage is mapped before any material is removed. This documentation matters both for the repair process and for the insurance claim, carriers want to see a clear before-and-after record, and a well-documented loss file reduces the back-and-forth that can delay your settlement.

Debris removal, downed branches, displaced roofing material, shattered glass, is handled as part of the restoration scope, not as a separate line item you have to coordinate with a landscaper or hauler. Tree limb removal from the structure itself falls within our work; full stump and root removal for severely damaged trees may involve a separate arborist depending on the situation.

## Orem Insurance Coordination for Storm Claims

Most homeowner policies in Utah cover sudden storm damage to the structure, but the documentation requirements vary by carrier and policy type. Hail damage to roofing is one of the more contested categories, some adjusters want to see a minimum number of impacts per square foot before approving a full replacement, and the threshold differs between carriers. Having a restoration contractor on-site who can walk the adjuster through the damage findings, rather than leaving that conversation to a roofer who may not document interior moisture intrusion, tends to produce more complete claim outcomes.

We bill most major carriers directly and can provide the moisture logs, photo documentation, and scope of work in the format adjusters expect. If your HOA has exterior repair guidelines that affect material selection or color matching, we factor those into the scope before work begins so approvals don't stall the job mid-stream.
