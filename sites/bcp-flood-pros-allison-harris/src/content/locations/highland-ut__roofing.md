Highland sits at the base of the Wasatch Front where elevation, UV intensity, and dramatic seasonal temperature swings put roofing materials through a punishing cycle year after year. Shingles that might last two decades in a milder climate can show granule loss, cracking, and lifted edges well ahead of schedule here, especially on south- and west-facing slopes that absorb the full force of Utah County summers. If your home needs a new roof or a full replacement of a failing one, the conditions specific to this city shape every material choice and installation decision from day one.

## Why Highland Properties See Roofing Problems Earlier Than Expected

Highland's elevation keeps it noticeably cooler than the valley floor in winter, which means freeze-thaw cycling hits roofing systems hard. Water that works its way under a lifted tab or through a compromised flashing joint freezes overnight, expands, and widens the gap. By the time spring arrives, what started as a minor vulnerability can be a pathway for water into the roof deck itself.

The city's housing stock also plays a role. A significant portion of Highland's single-family homes were built during the rapid residential expansion of the 1980s and 1990s, when three-tab asphalt shingles were the default choice. Those roofs are now at or past their expected service life. Dimensional shingles, metal roofing, and Class 4 impact-resistant products have all improved substantially since that era, and a replacement project is an opportunity to upgrade both performance and curb appeal.

HOA covenants are common throughout Highland's planned communities, and they typically govern approved colors, materials, and sometimes even the profile of the roofline. Before any replacement project begins, it pays to pull the current CC&Rs and confirm what the association requires for prior approval, especially if you are considering a material change from the original installation.

## Our Roofing Installation and Replacement Process in Highland

Every project starts with a thorough inspection of the existing roof system, not just the visible shingles but the decking, underlayment, flashing at penetrations and valleys, ridge ventilation, and soffit intake. In Highland's climate, inadequate attic ventilation is one of the most common hidden contributors to premature shingle failure. Heat builds up in an underventilated attic, bakes the shingles from below, and accelerates granule loss even when the exterior installation looks fine.

Once the scope is clear, material selection is matched to the specific exposure of your home. South-facing slopes in Highland receive intense UV radiation through the long summer days at this elevation, so we factor that into product recommendations. After materials are confirmed and any HOA approvals are in hand, the old roofing is removed, the decking is inspected and repaired where needed, and new underlayment goes down before a single shingle is set. Flashing is replaced rather than reused, because reusing old flashing around chimneys and skylights is one of the most common sources of callbacks on otherwise solid installations.

## Highland Insurance and HOA Coordination

Hail events in Utah County can be significant enough to trigger insurance claims, and Highland is not exempt from the storm cells that track along the Wasatch Front in late spring and early summer. When a storm prompts a claim, documentation matters: photos of impact marks on shingles, gutters, and soft metals like vents and flashing tell the story an adjuster needs to see. FIX Restoration works through the documentation process with homeowners and communicates directly with carriers to support a smooth claim.

For HOA-governed properties, we help gather the product specifications and color samples that most associations require before approving a replacement. Getting that approval in writing before the project starts protects you from disputes after the work is complete.

## Local Note

One thing that comes up repeatedly on Highland roofing projects is the effect of the city's position relative to Point of the Mountain. Wind patterns in this corridor can be stronger and more directional than homeowners expect, and starter strips and hip-and-ridge cap installation need to account for that. A roof installed with standard nailing patterns and without attention to wind-uplift details at the eaves can develop lifted shingles within a season or two, even when the rest of the installation is solid. It is a detail that matters more here than it does in more sheltered parts of Utah County.
