## Why Cottonwood Heights Properties Face Distinct Water Intrusion Risks

The city's elevation and canyon-fed drainage patterns create conditions that differ meaningfully from the Salt Lake Valley floor. That combination means water that enters a crawl space or basement slab doesn't drain away on its own. Homes built in Cottonwood Heights during the 1960s and 1970s, which represent a substantial portion of the city's housing stock, frequently have slab-on-grade or shallow crawl space construction. ## Equipment We Provide for Flood and Drying Work

The right equipment combination depends on the scope of the loss and the materials involved. For standing water removal, submersible flood pumps clear high volumes quickly before extraction equipment takes over. Truck-mounted or portable extractors pull residual moisture from carpet, padding, and hardwood. Once visible water is gone, the drying phase begins in earnest.

Low-grain refrigerant dehumidifiers pull moisture from the air and from building materials simultaneously. Paired with high-velocity air movers positioned at precise angles to wall cavities and floor assemblies, they create the airflow patterns that accelerate evaporation without simply pushing humid air from one room to another. For jobs involving hardwood floors or engineered wood, desiccant dehumidifiers maintain lower grain readings than standard refrigerant units, which matters when you're trying to dry materials without causing cupping or buckling.
