## Why Cedar Hills Homes Hold Odors Longer

The housing stock in Cedar Hills skews toward newer construction, but newer does not mean odor-resistant. Homes built with engineered lumber, spray foam insulation, and tight building envelopes trap odors more efficiently than older, draftier builds. When smoke from a kitchen fire or wildfire smoke from a Red Flag Warning event infiltrates a well-sealed home, the HVAC system circulates it through every room before anyone realizes what is happening. The same tight envelope that keeps heating bills manageable in a Utah winter keeps odor molecules cycling through ductwork long after the visible source is gone.

Pet odors present a different challenge. Urine compounds break down into ammonia and mercaptans that bond to carpet padding, subfloor OSB, and drywall paper. In Cedar Hills, where finished basements are common and often serve as primary living space, a single untreated source can make an entire lower level uninhabitable.

## Our Deodorization Process, Calibrated to Cedar Hills Conditions

Odor removal is not a single treatment. It is a sequence, and skipping steps is why DIY approaches fail.

**Source removal first.** No deodorization technology works if the physical source remains. Charred material, saturated padding, or contaminated insulation comes out before any equipment is deployed.

**Thermal fogging** penetrates porous materials the same way smoke did, reaching inside wall cavities, behind baseboards, and into subfloor gaps. The fog particles bond with odor molecules and neutralize them at the molecular level rather than masking them.

**Hydroxyl generation** is safe for occupied spaces and works continuously over days, breaking down volatile organic compounds without requiring the home to be vacated. For Cedar Hills homeowners who cannot leave during treatment, this is often the right tool.

**Ozone treatment** is reserved for unoccupied spaces where a high-concentration, time-limited application is appropriate. Ozone is one of the most effective tools for smoke odor in enclosed areas, but it requires proper ventilation protocols before re-entry.

After treatment, we verify results with air quality monitoring rather than relying on a technician's nose, which adapts to ambient odors quickly and is an unreliable instrument.
