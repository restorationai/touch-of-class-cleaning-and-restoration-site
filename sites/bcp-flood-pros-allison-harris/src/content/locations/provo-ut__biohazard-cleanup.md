## Why Provo's Housing Mix Shapes Biohazard Response

Provo's central neighborhoods include a significant number of homes and multi-unit buildings constructed before modern subfloor and wall-cavity standards. Older construction often means porous concrete basement floors, unsealed block foundations, and original hardwood or softwood subfloors that absorb biological material below the surface layer, meaning what looks contained at eye level may have already wicked into the structure underneath. Rental properties near campus frequently have layered flooring from multiple renovation cycles, which complicates both assessment and disposal. Knowing what's likely underneath before the first cut matters, and it's something that varies block by block in a city with Provo's construction history.

Utah County's dry climate is sometimes misread as a protective factor. In reality, low ambient humidity slows the visible signs of contamination spread but does not stop pathogen viability on porous surfaces. Biological material on concrete or wood in a sealed interior space, a basement unit, a closet, a utility room, can remain a health concern long after it appears dry to the eye.

## Our Biohazard Cleanup Process in Provo

Every job begins with a contained assessment, the goal is to understand the full scope before any materials are disturbed. From there, the work follows a structured sequence: personal protective equipment and site containment first, then removal of affected materials in sealed, labeled biohazard containers, followed by treatment of all contacted surfaces with EPA-registered disinfectants appropriate to the specific biological risk category. Structural materials that cannot be adequately decontaminated are removed and disposed of through licensed medical and biohazardous waste channels, not general construction debris.

Documentation runs parallel to the physical work. We photograph affected areas before, during, and after remediation, and we prepare a written scope of work that your insurance adjuster or property manager can use directly. Discretion is standard: unmarked vehicles, no unnecessary personnel on site, and communication only with the people you designate.

## Local Note

Provo has a substantial inventory of basement apartments, legal and informal, that were converted from storage or utility space over the decades. These units often have unfinished or lightly finished concrete walls and floors with no vapor barrier between the slab and the living space. In a biohazard situation, that construction detail matters: concrete without a sealer is effectively porous, and biological material can penetrate the surface rather than sitting on top of it. Standard surface wipe-down is not sufficient in those cases. We test, and if the slab has absorbed contamination, we treat it accordingly rather than assuming a visual clean means a safe one.
