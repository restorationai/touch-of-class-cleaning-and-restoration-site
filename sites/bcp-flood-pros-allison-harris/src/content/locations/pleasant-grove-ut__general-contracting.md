## Why Pleasant Grove Homes Present Unique Renovation Challenges

Much of Pleasant Grove's residential stock was built during two distinct eras: the post-war decades when ranch-style homes went up quickly on large lots, and the suburban expansion of the 1990s and early 2000s when tract developments filled in the benchland between State Street and the foothills. Each era brings its own renovation complexity.

Older homes in the city often have original single-pane windows, minimal wall insulation, and plumbing that predates modern PEX systems. Opening a wall for a kitchen remodel in one of these houses can surface knob-and-tube wiring, asbestos-containing drywall compound, or galvanized supply lines, none of which are surprises to an experienced general contractor, but all of which require a revised scope and honest communication before the project moves forward.

Newer homes from the 1990s buildout tend to have engineered lumber and OSB sheathing that responds poorly to long-term moisture intrusion, a relevant concern in a city where irrigation season runs well into fall and sprinkler overspray against foundation walls is common. A bathroom remodel that involves moving a shower or tub surround in one of these homes often uncovers sheathing damage that needs to be addressed before tile work begins.

## The Renovation and Remodel Process FIX Restoration Uses in Pleasant Grove

Every project starts with a walkthrough that goes beyond surface measurements. The crew looks at what's behind the walls, insulation type, framing condition, plumbing and electrical routing, before committing to a scope. In Pleasant Grove, that pre-construction assessment often includes checking for soil movement at the foundation perimeter, particularly on properties that back up to the bench or sit on fill from earlier grading.

From there, the process follows a sequenced build-out:

- **Permitting**: Utah County issues building permits for Pleasant Grove residential work through the city's building department. Permit timelines vary by project type and season; FIX Restoration handles the application and coordinates inspections so homeowners aren't tracking multiple agencies.
- **Demolition and rough work**: Structural changes, framing additions, and mechanical rough-ins happen before any finish work is touched. This sequencing prevents rework and keeps inspections on schedule.
- **Finish trades**: Tile, cabinetry, countertops, flooring, and paint are coordinated in the order that protects each finished surface from the trade that follows.
- **Final walkthrough and punch list**: Every project closes with a documented walkthrough. Items that don't meet the agreed standard get corrected before the job is signed off.

For post-damage rebuilds, homes that need reconstruction after water, fire, or mold remediation, FIX Restoration can carry the project from the remediation phase directly into the rebuild, which eliminates the gap where a homeowner has to find a separate contractor while living out of their home.
