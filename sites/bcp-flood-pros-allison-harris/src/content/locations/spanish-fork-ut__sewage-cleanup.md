## Why Spanish Fork Properties See Sewage Backup Issues

The geology beneath Spanish Fork plays a bigger role in sewage problems than most homeowners realize. The valley floor is underlain by fine-grained lacustrine soils, remnants of ancient Lake Bonneville, that drain slowly and shift subtly with seasonal moisture changes. That ground movement stresses buried sewer laterals over time, creating low spots, root intrusion points, and joint separations that invite backups. Older neighborhoods closer to the city center often have clay or Orangeburg sewer pipe still in the ground, materials that were standard decades ago but are well past their service life.

Beyond the pipes themselves, Spanish Fork's growth has added significant impervious surface to a relatively flat basin. During heavy spring snowmelt or a summer cloudburst, the municipal system can see surges that push back through the lowest fixtures in a home, floor drains in basements, ground-floor toilets, and laundry standpipes. Homes on septic systems on the city's eastern and southern edges face a different risk: saturated soils during wet cycles reduce the drain field's ability to absorb effluent, and the system backs up into the house instead.

## Our Sewage Cleanup and Sanitization Process in Spanish Fork

Raw sewage is classified as Category 3 water, the most contaminated classification in the industry, which means the cleanup process is more involved than a standard water loss. The work follows a defined sequence:

**Containment and safety setup.** The affected area is isolated to prevent cross-contamination. Anyone entering wears appropriate PPE, and HVAC registers are sealed so airborne particulates don't migrate through the duct system.

**Extraction.** Standing sewage and contaminated water are removed with truck-mounted or portable extraction equipment. Porous materials, carpet, pad, saturated drywall, and compromised insulation, that cannot be disinfected to a safe standard are removed and bagged for disposal.

**Disinfection.** EPA-registered antimicrobial agents are applied to all affected hard surfaces, framing, and concrete. This step isn't a single spray-and-walk-away; contact time matters, and surfaces are treated in passes to ensure adequate dwell time.

**Drying and monitoring.** After disinfection, the structural drying phase begins. Moisture readings are logged at each visit so the drying curve is documented, important both for confirming the work is complete and for any insurance claim file.

**Clearance.** Before work is closed out, a final inspection confirms moisture readings have returned to normal baselines for Spanish Fork's climate conditions and that no residual odor, a reliable indicator of remaining biological contamination, is present.
