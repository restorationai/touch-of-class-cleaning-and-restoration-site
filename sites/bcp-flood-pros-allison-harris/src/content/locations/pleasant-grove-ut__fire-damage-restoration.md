## Why Pleasant Grove Homes Are Particularly Vulnerable to Fire Spread

The Wasatch Front's low humidity is a year-round factor in Utah County, and Pleasant Grove is no exception. Dry interior air, common from late summer through winter, means structural wood, trim, and stored materials reach low moisture content levels that accelerate ignition and spread. Homes built along the older grid streets closer to State Street often feature original wood-lathe construction beneath updated drywall, a combination that can smolder inside wall cavities long after the visible flame is out. That hidden combustion is one of the most dangerous post-fire conditions because it produces concentrated smoke and carbon deposits in spaces that aren't immediately visible.

Newer construction in Pleasant Grove's eastern bench neighborhoods tends to use engineered lumber and composite materials that burn at different rates and leave different residue profiles, synthetic smoke odors penetrate porous surfaces like carpet and HVAC ductwork more aggressively than natural-wood smoke. Both housing types present real challenges; the approach has to match the building.

## Our Fire Damage Restoration Process in Pleasant Grove

The first step on any fire loss is a thorough structural assessment, not a visual walk-through, but a methodical inspection of wall cavities, attic spaces, and the HVAC system, because smoke travels wherever air moves. In Pleasant Grove's older ranch-style homes, that often means checking crawl spaces where smoke can pool and deposit soot on subfloor framing.

Once the scope is clear, the work moves in a deliberate sequence:

- **Board-up and tarping** to secure the structure against weather and unauthorized entry
- **Debris removal** of materials too compromised to restore, documented photographically for your insurance claim
- **Dry ice blasting or HEPA sanding** of char and soot from framing members where structural wood can be cleaned rather than replaced
- **Thermal fogging and hydroxyl treatment** to neutralize smoke odor at the molecular level, not just mask it
- **Duct cleaning** to prevent re-contaminating restored spaces through the HVAC system
- **Reconstruction** of affected walls, ceilings, and finishes to pre-loss condition

Every phase is documented with photos and moisture readings that support the insurance claim process.
