## Why Lindon Homes Are Particularly Vulnerable After a Fire

Most of Lindon's residential development happened in waves, with a significant portion of the housing stock built during the suburban expansion of the 1980s and 1990s. Those homes commonly feature open floor plans with vaulted ceilings, which act like chimneys during a fire, pulling smoke into attic cavities and HVAC systems far faster than a traditional ranch layout would. Once smoke enters a forced-air system, it distributes odor and fine particulates to every room in the house, even rooms the fire never touched.

Lindon's position along the Wasatch Front also means temperature swings between seasons are significant. In winter, homes are sealed tight against the cold, which concentrates smoke odor indoors and slows the natural off-gassing that helps with deodorization. Restoration work done in January requires more aggressive thermal fogging and hydroxyl treatment than the same scope of work performed in a ventilated summer environment.

## Our Fire Damage Restoration Process in Lindon

Every fire loss is different, but the sequence of work follows a disciplined order that protects both the structure and the people inside it.

**Stabilization and documentation** come first. Before any cleaning begins, the affected areas are photographed and measured in detail. This documentation is the backbone of your insurance claim and establishes a clear before-and-after record for your adjuster.

**Soot and smoke removal** is the most labor-intensive phase. Dry chemical sponges lift loose soot from ceilings and walls before any wet cleaning, because introducing moisture to dry soot smears it deeper into the surface. Protein-based residues from kitchen fires require enzymatic cleaners and are treated differently than the oily residues left by synthetic materials.

**Odor neutralization** in Lindon homes often requires attention to the HVAC system specifically. Duct cleaning and filter replacement are standard steps, but in homes where smoke has penetrated the air handler itself, the coil and blower assembly may need separate treatment.

**Structural assessment and reconstruction** close out the process. Load-bearing members are inspected for heat damage, drywall is replaced where it has been compromised, and finishes are restored to pre-loss condition.
