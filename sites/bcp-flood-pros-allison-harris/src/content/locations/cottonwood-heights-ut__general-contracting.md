## Why Cottonwood Heights Homes Present Unique Renovation Challenges

Much of Cottonwood Heights was developed in the 1960s and 1970s, a period when building codes, insulation standards, and moisture management details looked very different than they do today. Homes from that era frequently have original single-pane windows, minimal wall insulation, and plumbing that runs through exterior walls without adequate protection from the cold. When renovation work opens up those walls, it is common to find conditions that need to be addressed before new finishes go in: outdated wiring, galvanized supply lines that have narrowed with corrosion, or subfloor damage that was never visible from the surface.

The city sits at elevations ranging from roughly 4,500 to over 5,000 feet depending on the neighborhood. That altitude means UV exposure is more intense than lower-elevation communities in the valley, which accelerates the degradation of exterior finishes, caulking, and deck coatings. Contractors who work primarily at valley floor elevations sometimes underestimate how quickly exterior materials weather here. Specifying the right products for that exposure level is a detail that matters on every exterior remodel or addition project in Cottonwood Heights.

Salt Lake County also has clay-heavy soil in many areas, and Cottonwood Heights is no exception. Clay soil expands when wet and contracts when dry, which creates ongoing stress on concrete flatwork, foundation walls, and any below-grade waterproofing. Renovation projects that involve basement finishing or exterior drainage work need to account for that soil behavior rather than treating it as a generic flatland project.
