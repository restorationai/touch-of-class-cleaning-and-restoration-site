Cottonwood Heights sits at the base of the Wasatch Front, where late-season snowstorms can drop heavy, wet snow on roofs that were built for lighter alpine loads, and where summer hailstorms roll in fast off the Oquirrh range with little warning. When a storm tears off shingles, a fire blows out windows, or a vehicle strikes a garage door, the structure is exposed to whatever comes next. That second wave of damage, water soaking into framing, smoke residue spreading through an open building, is almost always preventable if the opening is secured within the first few hours.

## Why Cottonwood Heights Properties Face Elevated Board-Up and Tarping Risk

The city's elevation and its position against the mountains create weather patterns that are genuinely different from the Salt Lake Valley floor. Freeze-thaw cycles hit harder here. A roof that loses flashing or shingles in October faces repeated freeze events before a permanent repair is practical, making a properly weighted tarp not a temporary patch but a critical part of protecting the structure through winter. Many homes in the area were built in the 1960s and 1970s, when roof decking was thinner and attic ventilation standards were different. Those older decks are more vulnerable to rapid moisture intrusion once the weather barrier is broken.

Fire damage is the other common trigger for emergency board-up in Cottonwood Heights. A kitchen fire that vents through a window or burns through a soffit leaves the interior open to ambient air, wildlife, and anyone who walks the property. Boarding windows and doors to code-acceptable standards after a fire is also a practical insurance requirement: most carriers will reduce or deny a supplemental claim for interior damage that occurred after the initial loss if the property was left unsecured.

## Our Emergency Board-Up and Tarping Process in Cottonwood Heights

When we arrive, the first step is a perimeter assessment before any material goes up. We identify every compromised opening, including ones that are not immediately obvious, such as attic vents displaced by wind or crawl space access panels left open by emergency responders. For roof tarping, we use reinforced polyethylene tarps weighted and secured with lumber battens rather than stapled to the decking, which matters on older Cottonwood Heights roofs where the decking itself may be borderline. Staples pull through degraded wood; battens distribute load.

For fire damage board-up, we use plywood cut to fit each opening flush, fastened in a way that is secure against forced entry but removable by the homeowner or adjuster without damaging the frame. Every opening is photographed before and after, and we provide a written scope of what was secured and with what materials. That documentation goes directly to your insurance file.

## Local Note

Homes built on the benches above the valley floor in Cottonwood Heights often have steeply pitched roofs designed to shed heavy snow loads. That pitch is an asset in winter but a genuine safety factor during tarping work. Steep-slope tarping requires different anchor points and longer batten runs than a standard 4:12 pitch, and it is not work that should be attempted without fall protection equipment. It is one reason we assess the roof slope on every call before committing to a tarping method, and why the material and labor cost on a steep Cottonwood Heights roof is sometimes higher than a homeowner expects from a flat-rate quote they found online.
