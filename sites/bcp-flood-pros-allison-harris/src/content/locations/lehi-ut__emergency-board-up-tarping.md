## Our Emergency Board-Up and Tarping Process in Lehi

The first thing a crew does on arrival is a rapid perimeter walk to identify every breach, not just the obvious fire hole or broken window, but secondary openings like attic vents, soffit gaps, and crawlspace access points that wind can exploit. Tarping a roof without sealing the soffits, for example, can still allow driven rain to enter horizontally.

For roof tarping, crews use heavy-duty polyethylene sheeting weighted and anchored with wood battens screwed into the decking, not sandbags alone, which shift in wind. The tarp is run past the ridge when possible so that water sheds to both sides rather than pooling at a seam. For wall and window board-up, half-inch or thicker OSB is cut to fit and fastened with structural screws rather than nails, which back out under repeated wind pressure. All materials are marked with the date, crew initials, and a contact number so that insurance adjusters, building inspectors, and subsequent contractors can immediately identify what was installed and when.

Photographic documentation happens throughout, before any material goes up, during installation, and after completion. That photo set becomes part of the claim file and helps adjusters confirm the scope of protective work without a second site visit.
