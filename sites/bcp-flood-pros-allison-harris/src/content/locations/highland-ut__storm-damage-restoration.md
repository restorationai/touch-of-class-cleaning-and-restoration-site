Highland sits at the base of the Wasatch Front where afternoon thunderstorms can roll in fast off the mountains, dropping hail, driving rain, and wind gusts that strip shingles and push water into places it was never meant to go. The elevation and exposure that make Highland one of Utah County's more scenic communities also make it one of the more vulnerable to sudden, severe weather events, and the damage those storms leave behind rarely announces itself fully until days later, when a ceiling starts to sag or a wall cavity turns into a mold incubator.

## Why Highland Properties See Repeated Storm Damage

Highland's position along the western slope of the Wasatch Range creates a microclimate that concentrates storm activity. Summer convective storms build quickly over the peaks and descend with little warning, bringing hail large enough to punch through asphalt shingles and rain intense enough to overwhelm gutters and window flashing in minutes. Winter brings a different threat: heavy, wet snow loads that stress roof structures, followed by freeze-thaw cycles that work ice into any gap the storms opened.

The housing stock in Highland skews toward larger, custom-built homes on generous lots, many with complex rooflines, multiple valleys, and premium exterior materials that, when breached, allow water to travel far from the original entry point before it becomes visible. A damaged ridge cap on a high-pitched roof can funnel water into an attic for several storms before a homeowner notices staining on an interior ceiling. By that point, the structural sheathing may already be compromised and secondary mold growth is a real possibility.

## Our Storm Damage Restoration Process in Highland

The first thing we do on any storm-damaged property is a systematic exterior and interior inspection, not a quick walk-around, but a methodical check of every roof penetration, every piece of flashing, every window and door frame. Hail damage in particular is easy to underestimate from the ground; impact marks on soft metal (gutters, vents, AC condenser fins) are often the clearest early indicator of what the shingles absorbed.

Once we've mapped the damage, the process moves in a defined sequence:

1. **Emergency tarping and board-up**, stopping active water intrusion before anything else.
2. **Moisture mapping**, using thermal imaging and calibrated moisture meters to trace water migration through walls, subfloors, and ceiling assemblies.
3. **Controlled demolition**, removing only what cannot be dried in place, documented for your insurance claim.
4. **Structural drying**, commercial desiccant and refrigerant dehumidifiers, air movers positioned to dry cavities, not just surfaces.
5. **Repair and reconstruction**, from shingle replacement to full interior rebuilds, coordinated so you're dealing with one contractor, not three.

## Highland Insurance and HOA Coordination

Most storm damage claims in Highland go through homeowners' insurance, and the documentation phase is where claims are won or lost. We photograph and catalog every affected material before anything is removed, generate moisture logs that adjusters can follow, and communicate directly with your carrier's field adjuster when they come out to inspect. If your neighborhood has an HOA with exterior repair approval requirements, common in several of Highland's planned communities, we can provide the scope of work in whatever format the association needs before repairs begin, so you're not caught between your insurance timeline and an HOA review cycle.
