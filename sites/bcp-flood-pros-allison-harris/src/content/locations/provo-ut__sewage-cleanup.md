## Why Provo Properties Experience Sewage Backups

Provo's sewer system includes segments that date back decades, and those older clay or cast-iron laterals are prone to root intrusion from the mature trees lining residential streets throughout the city. When roots crack a lateral, the line can partially or fully block, and the next heavy use sends sewage backward into the lowest drain in the building, typically a basement floor drain or utility sink.

The city also sits on a valley floor with a relatively high water table in certain areas, particularly during spring snowmelt from the Wasatch Range. ## Our Sewage Cleanup and Sanitization Process in Provo

Sewage is classified as Category 3 water, the most contaminated type, which means the cleanup protocol is more involved than a standard water loss. The process begins with containment: affected areas are isolated to prevent cross-contamination to unaffected rooms, and the crew puts on appropriate personal protective equipment before any extraction begins.

All standing sewage and contaminated water is extracted using truck-mounted or portable units. Porous materials that have absorbed raw sewage, drywall, insulation, carpet, and subflooring, are removed and bagged for proper disposal rather than dried in place, because drying contaminated porous material does not eliminate the pathogens it has absorbed. Hard surfaces are then cleaned, treated with an EPA-registered disinfectant, and allowed to dwell for the contact time required to be effective.

After sanitization, the space is dried using commercial dehumidifiers and air movers. In Provo's semi-arid climate, ambient humidity is generally low, which helps drying times, but that same dry air can mask moisture that has wicked into concrete block foundations or adobe-style construction found in some of the city's mid-century homes. Moisture mapping with thermal imaging and pin meters catches what the eye misses before any rebuild begins.
