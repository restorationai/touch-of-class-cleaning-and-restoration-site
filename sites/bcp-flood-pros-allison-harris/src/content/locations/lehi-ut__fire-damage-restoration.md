## Why Lehi Properties Face Distinct Fire Damage Challenges

Lehi sits at the edge of Utah Lake's valley floor, where dry summers and low relative humidity are the norm for much of the year. That climate is a double-edged factor after a fire. On one hand, the dry air can help surface materials dry out faster; on the other, it allows smoke particulates and odor molecules to penetrate deep into porous materials, drywall, insulation, subflooring, even concrete block, before anyone realizes how far the contamination has traveled. Homes with forced-air HVAC systems, common in Lehi's newer subdivisions, are especially vulnerable: the ductwork can circulate smoke residue through every room in the house within minutes of ignition, even rooms that never saw a flame.

The city's building boom has also produced a large share of homes with engineered lumber framing and composite materials that behave very differently under heat than dimensional lumber does. Engineered I-joists and LVL beams can lose structural integrity faster than solid wood, which means a post-fire structural assessment isn't optional, it's the first thing that has to happen before any restoration work begins.

## Our Fire Damage Restoration Process in Lehi

Every fire loss starts the same way: a thorough walk-through to separate what can be restored from what has to be replaced, and a moisture reading to catch any water damage left behind by suppression efforts. Firefighting water in a sealed structure, especially in Lehi's colder months when windows stay shut, can begin supporting mold growth within 24 to 48 hours, so that secondary damage gets addressed alongside the fire cleanup.

From there, the process moves through debris removal, HEPA vacuuming of soot from surfaces and cavities, chemical sponge dry-cleaning of smoke-affected walls and ceilings, and odor neutralization using thermal fogging or hydroxyl generators depending on the materials involved. Ductwork gets inspected and cleaned if smoke traveled through the HVAC system. Structural repairs, framing, sheathing, drywall, insulation, follow once the affected area is confirmed clean and dry. The goal is handing back a home that doesn't carry any trace of what happened.

Fire claims in Utah County typically move through standard homeowners' carriers, but the documentation requirements can vary by policy.
