## Why Pleasant Grove Properties See Sewage Backup Issues

The geology beneath Pleasant Grove is a significant factor that most homeowners don't think about until something goes wrong. The valley floor here is underlain by expansive clay soils that shift seasonally, swelling with spring snowmelt from the Wasatch peaks and contracting through the dry summer months. That repeated movement puts lateral stress on older clay-tile and cast-iron sewer laterals, causing hairline cracks and root intrusion points that eventually allow backups into the home.

Pleasant Grove's older neighborhoods, many built in the 1950s through 1970s, frequently still have original vitrified clay laterals connecting to the municipal main. Those pipes are past their typical service life, and a single heavy rainfall event or a surge from snowmelt can overwhelm a partially obstructed line and push sewage back through floor drains, toilets, and tub drains simultaneously. Newer subdivisions on the city's eastern and northern edges tend to have PVC laterals, but they're not immune: improper backfill during construction can allow settling that creates low spots where solids accumulate.

## Our Sewage Cleanup and Sanitization Process in Pleasant Grove

Sewage cleanup is not the same as water damage restoration. The contamination category changes everything about how the work is done.

When we arrive at a Pleasant Grove property, the first priority is containment, establishing physical barriers so that foot traffic and HVAC airflow don't spread pathogens to unaffected areas of the home. All standing sewage is extracted using truck-mounted and portable units rated for Category 3 material. Affected porous materials, carpet, pad, drywall, insulation, and in many cases subfloor sheathing, are removed and properly bagged for disposal, because no amount of drying makes contaminated porous material safe to leave in place.

Once the structure is stripped to cleanable hard surfaces, we apply EPA-registered disinfectants at appropriate dwell times, not just a surface wipe. Structural drying follows using commercial dehumidifiers and air movers sized to the space. Moisture readings are logged at each visit so you have a documented drying record, something your insurance adjuster will want to see.

## Local Note

One thing worth knowing if you own an older home in Pleasant Grove: many mid-century properties here were built with concrete block or brick basement walls rather than poured concrete. Those block cores are porous, and when a sewage backup floods a block-walled basement, contaminated water wicks into the mortar joints and the hollow cores of the blocks themselves, areas that standard surface disinfection doesn't reach. Proper remediation in these basements requires extended dwell times on disinfectants and, in some cases, HEPA vacuuming of joint areas before any encapsulation or rebuild. It's a step that's easy to skip and expensive to discover later.

If your Pleasant Grove home has a block basement and you've had any sewage intrusion, make sure whoever handles the cleanup is accounting for that wall assembly, not just the floor.
