## Why Draper Properties See Mold Issues

The geography here matters. Cold air draining down Traverse Mountain and Little Cottonwood Canyon during shoulder seasons creates condensation on interior surfaces, especially in homes with inadequate vapor barriers or insufficient attic ventilation. Newer subdivisions built on the benches above the valley sometimes have crawl spaces that were graded during construction and never properly sealed, leaving a reservoir of ground moisture just below the living space. Finished basements, which are nearly universal in Draper-area homes, create the enclosed, low-light, moderate-humidity environment that mold spores prefer. A slow supply-line drip behind a bathroom vanity or a roof flashing failure after a spring storm can feed a colony for weeks before the smell becomes obvious.

## Our Mold Remediation Process in Draper

Effective mold remediation is not scrubbing visible growth and calling it done. The process starts with a thorough inspection using moisture meters and thermal imaging to find the wet material that is feeding the colony, not just the surface where spores are visible. Once the moisture source is confirmed and stopped, affected areas are contained with negative-air pressure and poly barriers to prevent cross-contamination to clean rooms. Porous materials that have absorbed growth into their structure, including drywall, insulation, and in some cases OSB sheathing, are removed rather than treated in place. Structural surfaces are HEPA-vacuumed, treated, and dried to documented moisture targets before any reconstruction begins. Air scrubbers run throughout the process to capture airborne spores. The job is not complete until clearance readings confirm the space is back to normal background levels.
