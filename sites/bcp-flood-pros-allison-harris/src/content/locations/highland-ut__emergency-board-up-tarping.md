## Why Highland Properties Face Particular Board-Up and Tarping Needs

Highland's elevation and position along the Wasatch bench mean it sees more freeze-thaw cycling than communities lower in Utah Valley. That cycling is hard on roofing materials, flashing lifts, shingles crack, and ridge caps loosen in ways that may go unnoticed until a storm pushes water through. When a fire, wind event, or falling branch tears away already-stressed roofing, the exposure is immediate and the weather window to get a tarp in place is narrow.

The housing stock in Highland also tends toward larger footprints, two-story colonials and craftsman-influenced homes on generous lots built from the late 1980s through the 2000s. Vaulted ceilings and complex roof lines with multiple valleys and dormers are common. Those geometries make tarping more involved than a simple gable roof: a crew needs to account for ridge lines, penetrations, and drainage paths so the tarp sheds water rather than pooling it. Getting that wrong can add a secondary water-damage claim to an already complicated fire or storm loss.

## Our Board-Up and Tarping Process in Highland

When FIX Restoration arrives at a Highland property, the first step is a rapid exterior assessment, identifying every breach point, not just the obvious one. A fire that blew out a front window often leaves heat-cracked glass in adjacent frames that will fail under the next temperature swing. A crew that boards only the visible opening leaves the property still vulnerable.

For window and door openings, we use structural-grade plywood secured with exterior screws into framing, not just into trim or drywall, the kind of attachment that holds through a Utah Valley wind gust rather than pulling free by morning. For roof openings, tarps are weighted, screwed, and lapped so they drain away from the breach. On larger or more complex roof sections, we may use weighted lumber along the tarp edges to prevent uplift. Every installation is photographed before and after, which matters when your insurance carrier is reviewing the mitigation scope.

If Highland's American Fork Canyon corridor is pushing high winds at the time of service, we factor that into the installation method, additional anchor points, heavier-gauge tarps, and a post-installation check before the crew leaves the site.

## Local Note

Highland's newer subdivisions, particularly those developed in the late 1990s and 2000s, frequently used OSB roof sheathing rather than plywood. OSB is structurally sound when dry but swells and delaminates faster than plywood when exposed to prolonged moisture, which means the clock on a roof breach is shorter than it might be on an older home with solid-board or plywood sheathing. A tarp that goes on within hours of a storm or fire event can be the difference between replacing a few damaged sections of sheathing and replacing an entire roof deck.
