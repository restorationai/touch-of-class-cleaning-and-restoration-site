## Why Springville Properties See Roofing Problems

Springville's position along the Wasatch Front creates conditions that accelerate roof wear faster than many homeowners expect. Winter snowloads are real here, and when temperatures swing above freezing during the day and drop back overnight, ice dams form along eaves and push water under shingles before it has a chance to drain. Older homes throughout the city, many built in the mid-twentieth century with steeper gable pitches that were standard at the time, often have original or once-replaced decking that has absorbed decades of that freeze-thaw stress. The decking itself can be soft or delaminated well before the surface shingles show obvious curling.

Summer brings a different threat. Hailstorms that roll off the mountains can deposit golf-ball-sized ice in a narrow corridor, leaving one block heavily damaged and the next block untouched. That patchwork damage pattern makes accurate assessment important, because a partial repair on compromised decking rarely solves the underlying problem.

## Our Roofing Installation and Replacement Process in Springville

Every project starts with a full deck inspection, not just a surface shingle count. We pull back damaged sections to check for soft spots, rot, or delamination in the OSB or plywood beneath. In Springville's climate, skipping that step means installing new materials over a substrate that will fail within a few seasons.

Once the deck condition is confirmed, we address ventilation before a single new shingle goes down. Attic ventilation is frequently undersized in homes built before modern energy codes, and poor airflow is one of the primary reasons new roofs age prematurely in cold climates. We size ridge and soffit ventilation to match the attic square footage so heat and moisture can escape year-round.

Material selection matters here. Architectural shingles rated for high-wind and impact resistance perform better in Springville's hail corridor than standard three-tab products. We walk homeowners through the tradeoffs between shingle grades, metal roofing options, and warranty coverage so the choice fits both the home's exposure and the budget.

Flashing at chimneys, valleys, and dormers gets particular attention. These are the points where water finds its way in first, and step flashing that was installed without proper sealant integration is one of the most common sources of interior water damage we see on Springville roofs.
