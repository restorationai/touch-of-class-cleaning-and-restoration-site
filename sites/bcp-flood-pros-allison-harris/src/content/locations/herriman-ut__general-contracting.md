## Why Herriman Homes Have Specific Renovation Needs

Herriman sits at the foot of the Oquirrh Mountains, and the elevation shift across the city is not trivial. Homes on the western benches can experience soil movement and settling that their neighbors on flatter ground do not. That settling shows up as sticking doors, cracked tile grout lines, and drywall seams that open up over time. Before any cosmetic renovation begins, a thorough assessment of the framing and subfloor conditions is worth the time, because finishing over an unaddressed structural shift just means redoing the work sooner.

The climate adds its own pressure. Herriman winters bring freeze-thaw cycles that stress exterior materials, and the low humidity of Utah summers dries out wood faster than homeowners expect. Hardwood floors installed without adequate acclimation time will gap and cup. Grout in bathrooms and kitchens needs a flexible, polymer-modified product rather than a rigid sanded mix if it is going to hold through seasonal movement. These are not generic renovation cautions. They are the specific material decisions that separate a remodel that lasts from one that starts showing problems in year two.

## Coordinating with Herriman HOAs and Local Requirements

A large share of Herriman's residential neighborhoods fall under homeowner association covenants, and those covenants frequently govern exterior materials, paint colors, fence heights, and even window styles. Before any exterior renovation moves forward, we review the applicable CC&Rs with the homeowner and, where needed, submit the required architectural review applications. Skipping that step can mean tearing out work that a neighbor or board member flags after the fact.

For interior remodels, HOA rules are less commonly a factor, but multi-unit properties and townhome communities sometimes restrict work hours, require dust barriers in common areas, or mandate that contractors use designated parking and entry points. We ask about those requirements at the start of every project so the crew is not caught off guard on day one.

## Local Note

Herriman's rapid buildout means a significant portion of the housing stock was framed during a period when OSB sheathing and engineered lumber were the standard, rather than dimensional solid wood. That matters for renovation work because engineered lumber does not tolerate repeated moisture exposure the way old-growth dimensional framing does. If a bathroom remodel or post-flood rebuild involves opening walls in a home built in the 2005-to-2015 range, the crew needs to inspect the LVL beams and I-joists carefully before closing everything back up. Swollen or delaminated engineered members need to be addressed at that stage, not discovered during a future project.
