Sandy sits at the base of the Wasatch Front where elevation changes, high-UV summers, and freeze-thaw cycles through winter put roofing materials through a stress test most other Utah communities don't see at the same intensity. If you're dealing with a roof that's lifting at the edges, losing granules after a hailstorm, or simply aging out after years of temperature swings between 100-degree July afternoons and January nights well below freezing, FIX Restoration has been handling roofing installation and replacement across the Salt Lake Valley since 2012.

## Why Sandy Roofs Wear Differently Than the Valley Floor

The geography here matters. Sandy sits higher than much of the valley, which means wind-driven snow loads and ice damming are real seasonal concerns rather than occasional ones. When temperatures drop overnight and melt during the day, water works its way under shingles, refreezes at the eave line, and forces the edge flashing away from the fascia. Over several winters, that cycle can compromise a roof that looks fine from the street.

Sandy also sees its share of summer hail. The storms that build over the Oquirrh Mountains to the west can drop hail fast and without much warning. Asphalt shingles take the brunt of it, and granule loss from impact is cumulative. A roof that survives one storm with minor bruising may be significantly weakened before the next one arrives. That's worth knowing when you're deciding between a targeted repair and a full replacement.

The housing stock in Sandy spans several decades. Neighborhoods developed through the 1970s and 1980s often have original or once-replaced roofs sitting on decking that has absorbed moisture over many seasons. When we remove old shingles during a replacement, we routinely inspect the sheathing for soft spots and delamination before any new material goes down. Skipping that step is how a new roof develops problems within a few years.

## Our Roofing Replacement Process in Sandy

Every project starts with a thorough inspection, not just a visual pass from the ground. We check flashing around chimneys, skylights, and pipe boots, look at ridge and soffit ventilation, and assess the decking condition before we give you a scope of work.

Once the project is underway, the sequence matters:

- **Tear-off and disposal**: Old shingles, underlayment, and damaged decking come off completely. We don't layer new material over old.
- **Deck repair**: Any sheathing that shows rot, delamination, or moisture damage gets replaced before anything else goes on.
- **Underlayment and ice-and-water barrier**: Given Sandy's freeze-thaw exposure, we install ice-and-water shield at the eaves and in valleys, not just standard felt across the field.
- **Shingle installation**: We work with asphalt architectural shingles rated for the wind and impact conditions common to this part of the Wasatch Front.
- **Flashing and finishing**: Ridge cap, step flashing, and drip edge are installed to manufacturer spec and local building requirements.

Permitting through Salt Lake County applies to most full replacements. We handle the paperwork so the project stays above board and your home's records reflect the work correctly.
