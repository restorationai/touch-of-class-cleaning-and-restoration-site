When a sewage backup forces its way into a finished basement or utility room, the clock starts immediately, raw sewage carries bacteria, viruses, and pathogens that begin contaminating porous surfaces within hours. ## Why Highland Properties Experience Sewage Backups

Highland's growth over the past two decades has layered newer construction on top of older infrastructure in some areas, and that mismatch matters when something goes wrong underground. The expansive clay soils common throughout Utah County shift with freeze-thaw cycles, a pattern that repeats every winter along the Wasatch Front, and that movement puts lateral sewer lines under stress year after year. Root intrusion from mature trees, a common feature in Highland's more established residential areas, compounds the problem by gradually narrowing the pipe until a heavy-use event triggers a full backup.

Highland also has a significant number of homes on private septic systems rather than municipal sewer. Septic tanks that haven't been pumped on schedule, or drain fields that have become saturated after a wet spring, can overflow into crawl spaces and lower levels with very little warning. The result looks similar to a sewer line backup but requires a different containment approach, one that accounts for the ongoing source until the system is serviced.

## Our Sewage Cleanup and Sanitization Process in Highland

Sewage is classified as Category 3 water, the most contaminated type, and the cleanup process reflects that. When we arrive at a Highland property, the first priority is containment: sealing off affected areas to prevent cross-contamination from foot traffic or HVAC circulation. We then extract all standing sewage and remove materials that cannot be effectively sanitized, including saturated drywall, insulation, and flooring that has absorbed contaminated water.

After extraction, the affected surfaces are treated with EPA-registered antimicrobial agents applied to concrete, framing, and any hard surfaces that remain. Drying equipment, air movers and dehumidifiers calibrated to the space, runs until moisture readings confirm the structure has returned to normal levels. Throughout the process, we document conditions with photographs and moisture logs, which becomes important when an insurance claim is involved.

Because Highland winters can drop temperatures significantly, we pay attention to crawl space and basement conditions that slow drying. Cold ambient air holds less moisture, which means dehumidifiers work harder and run longer in the colder months, a factor we account for when setting drying timelines with homeowners.

## Highland Insurance and HOA Coordination

Many Highland homeowners carry sewer backup endorsements on their homeowner's policies, a separate rider from standard water damage coverage that specifically covers sewage events. If you're not sure whether your policy includes it, we can help you read the declarations page when we arrive.
