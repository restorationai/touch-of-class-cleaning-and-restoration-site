## Why Sandy Properties See Sewage Backup Problems

The geology beneath Sandy plays a direct role in how sewer systems behave. The valley's clay-dominant soils shift seasonally, especially during spring snowmelt when the water table rises and saturated ground puts lateral pressure on older clay or cast-iron sewer pipes. That pressure causes joints to separate or pipes to belly, creating low spots where solids collect and blockages form. When the main line backs up, it has nowhere to go except back through the lowest fixture in the home, which is almost always a basement floor drain, toilet, or utility sink.

Homes built in the 1970s and 1980s, which make up a significant portion of Sandy's residential stock, often have original sewer laterals that were never upsized or relined. Those pipes are now decades past their typical service life. In the spring, when snowmelt from the Wasatch peaks runs downhill and groundwater infiltrates cracked lines, backup calls spike across the city. Septic systems in the less-dense edges of Sandy face a parallel problem: saturated soil reduces the drain field's ability to absorb effluent, and overflow can surface in yards or find its way into crawl spaces.

## Our Sewage Cleanup and Sanitization Process in Sandy

Sewage is classified as Category 3 water, the most contaminated classification in the restoration industry, because it contains bacteria, viruses, and pathogens that survive on surfaces long after the visible water is gone. The process has to address both the physical waste and the biological hazard.

The first step is containment: isolating the affected area to prevent cross-contamination into clean parts of the home. From there, the standing sewage is extracted using truck-mounted or portable units capable of handling solid-laden water. Porous materials that absorbed sewage, including drywall, insulation, carpet, and certain types of subflooring, are removed and disposed of properly because they cannot be reliably sanitized once saturated.

After removal, every affected surface is treated with an EPA-registered disinfectant formulated for Category 3 losses. This is not a spray-and-wipe step; contact time matters, and surfaces need to stay wet with the disinfectant long enough for it to be effective. Structural drying follows using commercial dehumidifiers and air movers, and moisture readings are documented throughout the process to confirm the space is dry before any reconstruction begins.

Most homeowner policies in Utah cover sudden and accidental sewage backups, but coverage for slow or gradual damage is often excluded, which is why documenting the loss thoroughly from the first hour matters. ## Local Note

One thing that comes up specifically in Sandy's older subdivisions: homes built on slab foundations in this part of the valley sometimes have under-slab drain lines that are difficult to inspect visually. When a sewer backup occurs in a slab-on-grade home, the contamination can wick into the concrete itself and into any expansion joints or cracks in the slab. Surface extraction removes the standing sewage, but the slab needs to be tested for residual moisture and treated accordingly before flooring goes back down. Skipping that step is one of the more common reasons a sewage remediation job develops an odor problem weeks later.
