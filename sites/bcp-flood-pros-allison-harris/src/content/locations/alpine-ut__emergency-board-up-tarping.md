Alpine sits in a narrow corridor between the Wasatch Front and the foothills above American Fork Canyon, where winter storms can drop heavy, wet snow on rooftops with very little warning. When that weight shifts a ridge board, cracks a skylight, or a late-spring hailstorm punches through a window, the gap between the damage and the next round of precipitation is often measured in hours. Emergency board-up and tarping is the work that fills that gap, keeping water, wind, and opportunistic entry out of a home until permanent repairs can begin.

## Why Alpine Properties Face Distinct Board-Up and Tarping Risks

Alpine's elevation and position along the Wasatch Front create weather patterns that differ noticeably from the valley floor communities to the north. Storms that arrive from the southwest can accelerate as they funnel through the canyon corridor, and the temperature swings between afternoon and overnight are sharp enough to cause ice damming along eaves well into March. Ice dams force meltwater under shingles and into wall cavities, and when a dam finally releases, it can tear gutters away and expose the roof deck in one event.

The housing stock here also matters. Much of Alpine developed in the 1980s and 1990s, meaning a significant portion of homes use OSB sheathing beneath the roofing material. OSB is effective when it stays dry, but once the surface layer is breached and moisture gets in, delamination begins quickly. A tarp installed within the first few hours after a breach can be the difference between replacing a handful of shingles and replacing a full roof deck section.

Fire damage is another driver. The wildland-urban interface along Alpine's eastern edge creates real fire exposure risk during dry summers, and a structure fire that burns through a soffit or roof section leaves an opening that needs to be secured before the investigation or the insurance inspection can proceed.

## Our Emergency Board-Up and Tarping Process in Alpine

The first step on any board-up or tarping call is a quick exterior assessment to identify every breach, not just the obvious one. In Alpine's multi-story homes, that often means checking dormers, second-floor windows, and roof valleys that may have sustained secondary damage from the same event.

For roof tarping, we use heavy-duty polyethylene tarps anchored with 2x4 wood battens screwed into the decking rather than weighted at the edges. Alpine's wind patterns make edge-weighted tarps unreliable, particularly on steeper-pitched roofs common in the area's craftsman and traditional colonial-style homes. The batten method keeps the tarp secure even when gusts come across the ridge.

Board-up work on windows and doors uses plywood cut to fit the opening, secured with structural screws rather than nails, so the panels can be removed cleanly when the replacement window or door arrives. We mark every panel with the date and the FIX Restoration contact number so your insurance adjuster and contractor both know who installed it and when.

## Alpine Insurance and HOA Coordination

Most homeowner's insurance policies treat emergency board-up and tarping as a covered mitigation expense, separate from the underlying repair claim. We document the damage with photographs before any material goes up, which gives your adjuster a clear record of the condition at the time of service. That documentation also supports your claim if there is any question about the scope of damage.

Alpine has active homeowner associations in several of its planned subdivisions, and some HOA governing documents include provisions about the appearance of temporary repairs. We keep board-up installations as clean and uniform as possible, and if your HOA requires notification before work begins on the exterior of a home, we can help you identify that requirement quickly so it does not slow down the emergency response.
