## Why Lindon Properties Experience Sewer Backups

Lindon's residential growth accelerated through the 1990s and 2000s, meaning a large share of the housing stock is now old enough that original cast-iron or early PVC lateral lines are showing age. Root intrusion from the mature trees planted along those subdivision streets is one of the most common culprits. When roots find a joint, they widen it over years until a heavy rain event or a simple flush pushes the system past its limit.

Lindon also sits in Utah County, where irrigation-heavy summers followed by hard winters create significant ground movement. That movement can cause belly sections in sewer laterals, spots where the pipe sags and sewage pools rather than flows. A belly doesn't fail all at once. It accumulates grease and solids over months until one morning the backup arrives without warning. Homes on larger lots with longer lateral runs to the municipal main tend to see this pattern most often.

Septic systems are less common inside Lindon's city limits than in surrounding rural areas, but properties near the city's edges sometimes still rely on them. A septic overflow carries the same contamination risk as a municipal sewer backup and requires the same category-3 water response protocol.

## Our Sewage Cleanup and Sanitization Process in Lindon

Raw sewage is classified as category-3 water, the highest contamination tier in the industry. The process is not simply mopping up and airing out. Every step is sequenced to prevent cross-contamination and to document the scope for your insurance carrier.

The work begins with containment. Affected areas are isolated so that foot traffic and HVAC airflow don't carry pathogens into clean spaces. Standing sewage is extracted using truck-mounted and portable extraction units. Porous materials that have absorbed contaminated water, including drywall, insulation, and certain flooring substrates, are typically removed rather than dried in place. Drying a contaminated material does not sanitize it.

Once the structure is cleared of saturated materials, the remaining surfaces are treated with EPA-registered disinfectants formulated for category-3 losses. Drying equipment runs until moisture readings in the framing and subfloor return to normal baselines. A final inspection confirms the space is safe before reconstruction begins.
