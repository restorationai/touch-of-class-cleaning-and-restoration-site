## Why Provo Properties Face Distinct Fire Damage Challenges

Provo's housing stock spans a wide range of construction eras. The blocks closest to downtown and Brigham Young University include homes built in the mid-twentieth century, many with original wood-framed walls, older electrical panels, and in some cases knob-and-tube or early Romex wiring that can complicate both the fire's origin and the restoration scope. When a fire starts in one of these older homes, the structural framing often absorbs smoke and heat differently than engineered lumber does, and charred old-growth wood can mask deeper damage that a surface inspection misses.

Utah County's low relative humidity, especially in late summer and early fall when the region sees its driest stretches, accelerates how quickly soot bonds to surfaces. Oily smoke residue from synthetic materials (upholstery, insulation, cabinetry) becomes harder to neutralize the longer it sits. In Provo's climate, that bonding process can happen within 24 to 72 hours of the fire, which is why the timeline from your first call to our crew arriving on-site matters.

## Our Fire Damage Restoration Process in Provo

Every fire loss is different, but the sequence of work follows a disciplined order that protects both the structure and the people inside it.

**Emergency stabilization** comes first. If the fire compromised the roof, exterior walls, or windows, we board and tarp before anything else, Provo's afternoon winds can push rain or debris into an open structure quickly, and a secondary water intrusion on top of fire damage compounds the loss significantly.

**Smoke and soot removal** is where the real technical work begins. Dry soot from fast-burning fires requires dry chemical sponges; wet, oily soot from smoldering fires calls for alkaline cleaners and careful agitation. We assess the burn type before choosing a method, because using the wrong approach can grind soot deeper into a surface rather than lifting it.

**Odor neutralization** follows structural cleaning. In Provo's dry air, smoke odors can linger in HVAC ductwork, subflooring, and wall cavities long after visible residue is gone. Thermal fogging and hydroxyl generation are two methods we use depending on occupancy needs and the materials involved.

**Structural drying and reconstruction** closes the process, addressing any water damage from firefighting, repairing or replacing compromised framing, and restoring finishes to pre-loss condition.

## Local Note

Provo's older residential blocks, particularly those built in the 1940s through 1960s, frequently have plaster-and-lath interior walls rather than modern drywall. After a fire, plaster can look intact while hiding smoke infiltration in the lath cavity behind it. Because plaster is denser than drywall, soot particles don't always penetrate the face, but they migrate through cracks and electrical cutouts into the wall system. We probe these cavities before deciding whether a wall can be cleaned in place or needs to come down, a step that matters a great deal for both air quality and long-term odor control in a Provo home of that era.
