## Why Herriman's Housing Stock Shapes Fire Damage Recovery

Most of Herriman's residential construction happened after 2000, which means the predominant building materials are engineered wood products, spray foam insulation, and composite trim. These materials behave very differently in a fire than the dimensional lumber and plaster found in older Salt Lake City neighborhoods. Engineered lumber can lose structural integrity faster under heat, and composite materials tend to off-gas a dense, oily smoke that leaves a stubborn residue on walls, ceilings, and contents. That residue is not just cosmetic. Left untreated, it continues to corrode metal fixtures and embed itself deeper into porous surfaces over days and weeks.

High-desert elevation also plays a role. Herriman's dry air means smoke particles stay suspended longer before settling, which can push contamination further through an open floor plan before the fire is even out. Ductwork in two-story homes here frequently connects every room on both levels, so smoke odor restoration almost always involves a thorough duct cleaning alongside surface treatment.

## Our Fire Damage Restoration Process in Herriman

The first step after a fire is a room-by-room scope assessment. Every affected surface is categorized: what can be cleaned, what needs replacement, and what requires structural evaluation before any other work begins. Structural fire damage is documented with photographs and written notes formatted to meet insurance carrier requirements.

From there, the process moves through debris removal, dry soot cleaning, and wet chemical cleaning depending on the surface type. Porous materials like drywall and insulation that have absorbed smoke deeply are typically removed rather than cleaned. Odor control follows, using thermal fogging or hydroxyl generation to neutralize smoke compounds that surface cleaning alone cannot reach. The final phase is reconstruction, restoring the home to pre-loss condition so the repair work is not visible from the finished rooms.

HOA architectural standards matter here. Many of Herriman's planned communities have specific requirements for exterior finishes, roofing materials, and paint colors. When fire damage reaches the exterior, replacement materials need to match the approved palette, which FIX Restoration coordinates with the homeowner and HOA before ordering.

## Local Note

Homes built in Herriman's newer subdivisions frequently have whole-home air filtration systems and smart thermostats that continue running after a fire event, which can spread smoke particles through the duct system even after the fire is out.
