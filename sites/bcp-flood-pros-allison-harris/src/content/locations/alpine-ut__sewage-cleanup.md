## Why Alpine Properties See Sewage Backup Issues

The geology beneath Alpine is a significant factor. The valley soils here contain substantial clay content, which drains slowly and expands when saturated. During spring snowmelt off the Wasatch Front, that slow drainage puts hydrostatic pressure on underground lines and can force groundwater into lateral sewer connections. Homes built on the steeper bench lots common throughout Alpine also deal with settling over time, which can cause older PVC lateral lines to shift, sag, or separate at joints.

Alpine's growth through the late 1990s and early 2000s brought large numbers of homes with finished walkout basements, many of which sit below the municipal sewer main elevation. That below-grade position means a blockage or main-line surcharge has nowhere to go except back through the lowest fixture in the house, typically a basement floor drain or laundry sink. Homes on private septic systems, which are still common on the larger lots toward the eastern edges of the city, face a different but equally serious risk: a saturated drain field during a wet spring can cause the tank to back up into the structure before the homeowner realizes there is a problem.

## Our Sewage Cleanup and Sanitization Process in Alpine

Sewage is classified as Category 3 water under industry standards, meaning it carries bacteria, viruses, and other pathogens that require a structured response rather than a simple dry-out. The process begins with containment. Affected areas are isolated to prevent cross-contamination to clean portions of the home, which matters especially in Alpine's open-plan finished basements where a single overflow can reach carpet, drywall, and HVAC return air intakes within a short distance of each other.

Extraction of standing sewage comes next, followed by the removal of porous materials that cannot be safely restored. Drywall, carpet, and pad that have absorbed Category 3 contamination are bagged and disposed of according to Utah County waste handling requirements. Hard surfaces are then treated with EPA-registered disinfectants applied at appropriate dwell times. Deodorization follows sanitization, because even after pathogens are addressed, volatile organic compounds from sewage can linger in porous substrates like concrete block and wood framing. Moisture readings are taken throughout to confirm the structure is dry before any reconstruction begins.
