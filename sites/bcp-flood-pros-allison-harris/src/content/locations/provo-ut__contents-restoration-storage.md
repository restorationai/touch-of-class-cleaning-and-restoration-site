Provo's dry, high-desert climate creates a deceptive problem for property owners dealing with water or fire damage: the arid air pulls surface moisture away quickly, making belongings feel dry long before the damage inside them has stabilized. Furniture joints swell and crack, photographs curl, electronics corrode from residual humidity trapped in their housings, and smoke odor from Utah County's older wood-frame construction settles deep into upholstery fibers. Contents restoration and storage gives your possessions a controlled environment to recover in, separate from the chaos of the structure itself, while your home or building is being dried, repaired, or rebuilt.

## Why Provo Properties Face Distinct Contents Challenges

Provo sits at roughly 4,500 feet in elevation, and that altitude shapes how damage behaves. Lower atmospheric pressure means water evaporates faster at the surface, which can mask how deeply moisture has penetrated wood furniture, cabinetry, or textiles. A bookcase that feels dry to the touch after a dishwasher overflow may still have a moisture content in its core that will cause warping weeks later if it isn't properly conditioned.

The city's housing stock adds another layer of complexity. Provo has a significant number of homes built in the mid-20th century, many with original hardwood floors, plaster-and-lath interior walls, and older furniture construction using hide glue and solid wood rather than modern composites. These materials respond to heat, smoke, and water differently than contemporary furnishings, they're often more salvageable, but they require slower, more deliberate treatment. Rushing the drying or cleaning process on a 1950s-era dresser or an upholstered chair with horsehair padding can cause irreversible damage that faster, more aggressive methods would have prevented.

Winter temperature swings in the Wasatch Front also matter. When a pipe freezes and bursts during a January cold snap, the contents loss can happen fast, and moving saturated items into an unheated garage or storage unit to "deal with later" is one of the most common mistakes that turns a restorable item into a write-off.

## Our Contents Restoration Process in Provo

When we arrive at a Provo property, contents work begins with a room-by-room inventory before anything is moved. Every item is photographed, catalogued, and assigned a condition rating. This documentation matters both for your insurance claim and for ensuring nothing gets misplaced during pack-out.

From there, affected contents are carefully packed and transported to a climate-controlled facility. Provo's seasonal temperature extremes, summer highs that push past 100°F and winter lows that drop well below freezing, make climate control non-negotiable during the restoration period. Items stored in conditions that swing between those extremes will continue to deteriorate even after they've been cleaned.

At the facility, each category of item follows its own process:

- **Soft goods** (upholstery, rugs, clothing, bedding) are assessed for smoke odor, soot, and moisture. Ozone treatment, thermal fogging, or ultrasonic cleaning may be used depending on the material and the type of loss.
- **Hard goods** (furniture, cabinetry, electronics, décor) are cleaned, dried to target moisture levels, and treated for odor before storage.
- **Documents and photographs** receive specialized attention, freeze-drying is sometimes the right call for water-saturated paper items, and the sooner that decision is made, the better the outcome.

Once your property is ready, contents are returned, inventoried again at delivery, and placed back in their original rooms where possible.
