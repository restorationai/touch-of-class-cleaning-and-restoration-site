Riverton sits at the base of the Wasatch Front where the valley floor meets the foothills, and that geography shapes how storms behave here. Cold air funneling down from the mountains can turn a routine spring thunderstorm into a hail event that strips shingles, splinters fence boards, and drives water into attic cavities before the rain even registers on a weather app. When that happens, FIX Restoration is the team Riverton homeowners can call to assess the damage honestly and start work without delay.

## Why Riverton Properties See Significant Storm Damage

The Salt Lake Valley's semi-arid climate creates a pattern that surprises a lot of homeowners: long dry stretches followed by intense, compressed storm events. When a summer microburst or a late-season front rolls through, the ground is often too hard and dry to absorb runoff quickly, which means water sheets across driveways and window wells and finds every gap in a home's envelope. Hail is a particular concern along the Wasatch bench communities, where storms that form over the lake track east and intensify before they drop. Golf-ball-sized hail is not a once-a-decade event in this corridor.

Riverton's housing stock adds another layer of complexity. Many neighborhoods developed rapidly through the 1990s and early 2000s, meaning a large share of homes use builder-grade roofing materials that were installed when they were new and have never been replaced. After 20-plus years, those materials are at or past their rated lifespan, and a single hail event can cause damage that would have bounced off a newer roof. Attic ventilation in that era of construction was also inconsistent, so when a storm drives moisture past a compromised roof deck, it can sit in the attic space and begin promoting mold growth within 48 to 72 hours.

## Our Storm Damage Restoration Process in Riverton

Every job starts with a thorough exterior and interior inspection. We document the roof, siding, fascia, gutters, and any penetrations where water may have entered. Inside, we use moisture meters and thermal imaging to find water that has already migrated behind walls or into ceiling assemblies, because what looks dry at the surface often is not.

Once the scope is clear, we move to emergency stabilization: tarping compromised roof sections, boarding broken windows, and extracting any standing water before it spreads further. Structural drying follows, using commercial-grade dehumidifiers and air movers calibrated to the specific volume and material composition of the affected space. Utah's low ambient humidity is an asset during this phase, but it does not replace the need for metered drying confirmation. We track moisture readings daily and do not close out the drying phase until materials reach their target levels.

If tree impact is involved, debris removal and a structural assessment come before any interior work. A tree through a roof changes the load path of the structure, and that has to be evaluated before crews work underneath it.

Most storm damage claims in Riverton run through homeowners insurance, and the documentation phase of our inspection is built with that process in mind. ## Local Note

One thing worth knowing about Riverton specifically: the clay-heavy soils common across much of Salt Lake County can shift and settle after a heavy storm event, especially when dry soil absorbs a large volume of water rapidly. That movement can open hairline cracks in foundation walls or window well surrounds that were not visible before the storm. On jobs where we see interior water intrusion at the basement level, we always check whether the entry point is a roof or gutter issue or whether it is a foundation gap that opened up during the event. Treating the wrong source means the next storm produces the same result.
