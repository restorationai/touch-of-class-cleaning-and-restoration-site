## Why Springville Properties See More Water Intrusion Than You'd Expect

The soil along the Springville bench and lower foothills is a mix of clay-heavy glacial till and alluvial deposits that drains poorly after sustained rain or snowmelt. Water that can't percolate downward finds the path of least resistance, which is often a foundation wall, a window well, or a crawl space vent. Homes built in the 1950s through 1970s, which make up a significant share of Springville's residential neighborhoods, typically have block or poured-concrete basements without modern waterproofing membranes. That construction style holds up well structurally, but it wicks moisture. A slow seep that goes unnoticed for a week can saturate subfloor sheathing and wall framing before the surface feels damp to the touch.

Springville also sits in a climate zone where temperatures swing hard between seasons. Pipes in uninsulated garage walls and exterior-facing cabinets freeze more often than homeowners expect, and a burst pipe in February can dump dozens of gallons into a finished basement before the water reaches the floor drain. Getting a high-capacity pump and a stack of air movers on-site within hours, not days, is what keeps that event from becoming a mold problem by the following weekend.

## Equipment We Rent for Flood and Drying Jobs

Not every water event needs the same tool. A sump failure that left two inches of standing water in a basement calls for a submersible flood pump first, then high-velocity air movers to begin surface drying, then a commercial-grade desiccant or refrigerant dehumidifier to pull residual moisture out of the air and building materials. A carpet-only event from a washing machine overflow is a different profile, you may only need a carpet drying fan and a single dehumidifier.

## Local Note: Older Springville Homes Dry Differently

Here's something worth knowing if your home was built before 1975: the interior wall assemblies in many Springville-area houses from that era used rock lath and plaster rather than modern drywall. Plaster is denser and holds moisture longer than gypsum board, it may read dry at the painted surface while the lath and framing behind it are still well above acceptable moisture content. If you're using rental equipment after a water event in an older home, plan for a longer drying window than online guides suggest, and use a moisture meter at multiple depths before you consider the job done. Pulling equipment too early is the most common reason a contained water event turns into a mold call six weeks later.
