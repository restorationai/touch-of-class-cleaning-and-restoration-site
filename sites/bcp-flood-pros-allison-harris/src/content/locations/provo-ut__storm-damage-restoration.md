## Why Provo Properties Take Storm Hits Differently

The Wasatch Range doesn't just make Provo scenic, it shapes the weather in ways that catch property owners off guard. Orographic lift forces moisture-laden air upward, wringing out intense, localized downpours that can drop an inch of rain in under an hour. That kind of rainfall overwhelms the older clay-tile sewer laterals common in Provo's established residential neighborhoods, pushing water back through floor drains and window wells before homeowners realize what's happening.

Provo's housing stock adds another layer of complexity. Much of the city's residential construction dates from the post-WWII boom through the 1970s, meaning a significant portion of homes have wood-framed roofs with original or once-replaced sheathing, single-pane attic vents, and brick veneer that can admit water at the mortar joints when wind-driven rain hits at an angle. Hail that would dent an asphalt shingle on a newer home can fracture aging mortar or crack older vent covers entirely, damage that isn't obvious from the street but creates a direct path for water into wall cavities.

High-altitude UV exposure also degrades roofing membranes and flashing faster than manufacturers' ratings assume, so a storm that would be a minor event in a lower-elevation city can expose pre-existing vulnerabilities that have been quietly developing for years.

## Our Storm Damage Restoration Process in Provo

The first priority after any storm loss is stopping new water from entering. We begin with emergency tarping and board-up if the structure is breached, roof, windows, or siding, before any interior assessment begins. Letting rain continue into an open cavity while waiting on an adjuster is one of the fastest ways to turn a manageable claim into a mold remediation project.

Once the envelope is secured, we conduct a systematic moisture survey. Storm water doesn't travel in straight lines; it follows framing, insulation, and gravity to places that look dry on the surface. We use thermal imaging and calibrated moisture meters to map where water has migrated, not just where it visibly pooled. In Provo's older brick-and-frame construction, that often means checking interior wall cavities along the windward elevation even when the damage entry point appears to be the roof.

From there, the process moves through structural drying, debris removal, and documentation, photographs, moisture readings, and material inventories, formatted for insurance submission. We coordinate directly with carriers and can work alongside your adjuster's inspection to make sure nothing is missed before the scope is written.
