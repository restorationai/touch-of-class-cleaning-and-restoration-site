## Why Sandy Properties Hold Odors Longer Than You'd Expect

The housing stock across Sandy spans several decades of construction, from ranch-style homes built in the 1960s and 1970s to newer developments closer to the Dimple Dell area. Older builds frequently used textured drywall, wood-paneled interiors, and carpet over concrete slabs, all materials that absorb odor molecules deeply. When smoke from a kitchen fire or wildfire smoke infiltration gets into that textured finish, surface cleaning removes the visible soot but leaves volatile organic compounds embedded in the gypsum and paint layers.

The valley's inversion seasons compound the problem. During winter inversions, Sandy's air quality drops sharply, and homes are sealed tight for days or weeks at a time. That extended closure allows odors from any source, cooking smoke, pet dander, mold off-gassing, to recirculate and saturate soft goods and wall cavities without any fresh-air dilution. By the time a homeowner notices the smell is not clearing on its own, the odor source has already bonded to porous surfaces throughout the space.

## Our Odor Removal and Deodorization Process in Sandy

Effective deodorization starts with identifying where the odor compounds are actually living, not just where they are strongest. FIX Restoration technicians begin with a structured assessment: locating the primary source, mapping secondary absorption zones (HVAC returns, subfloor cavities, interior wall faces), and determining which treatment method fits the material and the severity.

For most residential losses in Sandy, the process involves a combination of methods:

- **Thermal fogging** penetrates porous surfaces by converting a deodorizing solution into a fine fog that follows the same pathways smoke traveled, neutralizing odor molecules on contact.
- **Hydroxyl deodorization** uses UV-generated hydroxyl radicals to break down odor compounds in the air and on surfaces without requiring occupants to vacate for extended periods, useful in occupied homes or multi-unit buildings.
- **Ozone treatment** is reserved for unoccupied spaces where a higher-intensity approach is warranted; ozone at working concentrations requires full evacuation and careful post-treatment ventilation before re-entry.
- **HEPA air scrubbing** runs continuously during and after treatment to capture particulates that carry odor, particularly relevant after smoke events that leave fine ash in the air column.

The method selected depends on the odor type, the materials involved, and the occupancy situation, not a one-size approach applied to every job.

## Equipment and Methods for Odor Removal

Not every odor problem calls for the same tool. A home that has had a single smoke event differs from a property with years of embedded pet odor or one that has had sewage backflow. FIX Restoration carries thermal fogging units, commercial hydroxyl generators, ozone machines, and HEPA air scrubbers, and technicians are trained to match the equipment to the loss type. Ozone and hydroxyl are not interchangeable: ozone works faster in unoccupied spaces but requires strict re-entry protocols, while hydroxyl can run safely in occupied environments and is often the right choice for Sandy families who cannot easily relocate during treatment.

## Local Note

Sandy homes built during the 1970s and early 1980s frequently have forced-air systems with original ductwork that was never designed for easy cleaning access. When smoke or mold odors enter the HVAC system in these homes, the smell recirculates every time the furnace or AC runs, even after the source room has been treated.
