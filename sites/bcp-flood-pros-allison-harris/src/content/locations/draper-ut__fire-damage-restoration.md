That same arid climate that keeps summer comfortable also means a house fire burns hotter and spreads faster than in more temperate regions, and the smoke residue it leaves behind penetrates porous materials with unusual depth. ## Why Draper Properties Face Distinct Fire Damage Challenges

Draper's housing stock spans a wide range of eras and construction types. Subdivisions developed through the 1990s and early 2000s feature open floor plans with engineered lumber and lightweight truss systems, which ignite and fail faster under fire conditions than older dimensional framing. Newer master-planned communities closer to the Point of the Mountain corridor often include finished basements and multi-story layouts where smoke stratifies across several levels, complicating the scope of restoration work considerably.

The region's low relative humidity, especially in late summer and through winter, accelerates the bonding of dry smoke particles to painted surfaces, textiles, and HVAC components. What wipes off easily in a humid climate can etch into finishes here within 24 to 48 hours. Getting a crew on-site quickly is not a cliché in Draper; it is a measurable factor in how much material can be saved versus replaced.

## Our Fire Damage Restoration Process in Draper

Every fire loss is different, but the sequence of work follows a logic that applies directly to Draper properties. The first priority is a thorough structural assessment, including the roof deck and attic space where embers and superheated gases travel before occupants notice any ceiling damage. In homes with tile or concrete roofing common throughout Draper's newer developments, the underlayment beneath the tile is often compromised even when the tile itself looks intact.

From there, the process moves through controlled demolition of unsalvageable materials, HEPA vacuuming of all smoke-affected surfaces, and chemical treatment of odor-bearing substrates. Ozone and hydroxyl generation address airborne odor compounds in the structure before reconstruction begins. Ductwork receives its own cleaning protocol because smoke-contaminated HVAC systems will reintroduce odor into a rebuilt space every time the system runs.

Throughout this process, FIX Restoration documents every affected material and every step taken, producing a detailed scope that your insurance adjuster can review line by line.
