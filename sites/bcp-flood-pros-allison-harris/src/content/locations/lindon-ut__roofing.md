## Why Lindon Properties Experience Roofing Wear Faster Than You'd Expect

Lindon's position along the Wasatch Front means roofs here absorb intense summer UV radiation from high-altitude sun exposure, then endure freeze-thaw cycling through late fall and early spring. That combination is particularly hard on asphalt shingles: UV breaks down the asphalt binder, and repeated freezing of any moisture that infiltrates cracked or lifted shingles accelerates granule loss and edge curling. Flat or low-slope sections on homes built during the rapid residential expansion of the late 1980s and 1990s are especially prone to membrane fatigue.

Lindon also sits at an elevation where snowpack can linger longer than in lower-lying parts of Utah County. Ice damming at eaves is a real and recurring issue, particularly on north-facing roof planes that don't get enough direct sun to shed snow cleanly. When ice dams form, meltwater backs up under shingles and into the decking, and by the time the interior shows a stain on the ceiling, the sheathing may already be compromised.

## Our Roofing Installation and Replacement Process in Lindon

Every project starts with a thorough inspection of the existing system, not just the shingles but the decking, underlayment, flashing at penetrations, and ventilation. In Lindon's climate, inadequate attic ventilation is one of the most overlooked contributors to premature roof failure. Heat buildup in summer degrades shingles from underneath; moisture trapped in winter accelerates mold in the decking. We assess both before a single old shingle comes off.

During tear-off, we check every sheet of decking for soft spots, delamination, or staining that signals prior water intrusion. Any compromised decking is replaced before the new system goes down. Underlayment selection matters here: in a climate with meaningful snow loads and freeze-thaw exposure, a synthetic underlayment outperforms traditional felt for long-term moisture resistance.

Flashing details at chimneys, skylights, and roof-to-wall transitions are where most Utah County roofs eventually fail. We use step flashing and counter-flashing at every masonry intersection rather than relying on caulk as a primary seal. Ridge ventilation is sized to match the intake at the soffits so the attic breathes correctly year-round.
