## What to Look for When Choosing a Water Damage Restoration Company

The right restoration company does three things well: they stop the damage fast, they dry the structure correctly, and they document everything your insurance company needs. Here's how to evaluate any company on this list.

**Drying protocol.** Ask whether they use LGR (low-grain refrigerant) dehumidifiers and how they track moisture readings across the drying period. The [IICRC S500 Standard for Professional Water Damage Restoration](https://www.iicrc.org) defines the drying targets a competent crew should be hitting. Vague answers about "industrial fans" are a yellow flag.

**Water category.** Category 1 water (clean supply line) is very different from Category 2 (gray water, like a washing machine overflow) or Category 3 (black water, sewage or floodwater). The cleanup protocol, PPE requirements, and material salvageability all differ. A company that treats all three the same isn't following best practice.

**Insurance documentation.** Ask specifically: do you produce a moisture log, a scope of work, and photo documentation in a format compatible with Xactimate? If the answer is vague, that's a problem. Your adjuster will ask for all of it.

**Full-service vs. mitigation only.** Some companies do extraction and drying, then stop. Others carry through mold remediation, contents restoration, and reconstruction. If you want one point of contact from day one through move-back-in, confirm the company handles the full scope before you sign anything.

## What Happens During Water Damage Restoration in American Fork?

Water damage restoration follows a defined sequence. Understanding the phases helps you ask better questions and know whether a crew is cutting corners.

1. **Emergency extraction.** Standing water is removed with truck-mounted or portable extractors. The faster this happens, the less the water migrates into wall cavities, subflooring, and framing.
2. **Moisture mapping.** Technicians use thermal cameras and calibrated moisture meters to find water that has traveled beyond the visible wet area. In Utah County homes with finished basements, water from an upstairs pipe can travel surprisingly far before it's visible.
3. **Structural drying.** Air movers and LGR dehumidifiers run in a calculated pattern based on the affected square footage and materials. Drying typically takes 3 to 5 days for a standard loss, though Category 2 or 3 losses and larger affected areas take longer.
4. **Antimicrobial treatment.** Affected materials are treated to inhibit mold growth. In Utah's climate, mold can begin colonizing wet materials within 24 to 48 hours, so this step is not optional on anything beyond a very minor clean-water loss.
5. **Material removal.** Drywall, insulation, and flooring that can't be dried in place are removed. This is called a "demo" or "tear-out" phase and is necessary for thorough drying of the structural framing behind.
6. **Documentation and drying logs.** Daily moisture readings are recorded and compiled into a drying log. This is the document your insurance adjuster uses to verify the scope of work.
7. **Reconstruction.** Once the structure passes final moisture readings, rebuild begins: new drywall, insulation, flooring, paint. A company like FIX Restoration that handles general contracting completes this phase in-house.

## What to Do Right Now If You Have Water Damage in American Fork

If water is actively spreading, do these four things before anything else:

1. **Shut off the water source** if it's a supply line failure and you can safely reach the shutoff.
2. **Cut power to affected areas** if there's any risk of water near electrical panels or outlets.
3. **Move valuables and documents** off wet floors and out of the affected space.
4. **Call a restoration company.** The clock on secondary damage (mold, structural deterioration, contents loss) starts the moment water contacts materials.
