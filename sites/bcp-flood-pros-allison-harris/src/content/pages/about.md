FIX Restoration has been doing this work since 2012, which means over a decade of showing up when pipes burst in the middle of January, when a kitchen fire leaves a family standing in the driveway, when a slow leak behind a wall finally makes itself known. The company is based in American Fork, Utah, and the work has always been local, specific, and hands-on.

## What we do

Most of what restoration looks like day-to-day isn't dramatic. It's arriving at a house where the washing machine supply line failed overnight, pulling back flooring to find out how far the water traveled, setting equipment, and then coming back every day until the readings confirm the structure is genuinely dry, not just dry at the surface. It's walking a homeowner through what their insurance adjuster will need to see, and making sure the documentation is thorough enough that the claim doesn't stall. It's answering questions honestly, even when the honest answer is that drying will take longer than anyone wants it to.

The calls come from homeowners, landlords, property managers, and small businesses. The losses are different every time, water, fire, smoke, mold, but the situation is almost always the same: something went wrong, the property is at risk of getting worse, and the person calling needs to know what happens next.

## Our certifications and licensure

Certifications in this industry exist because the consequences of doing the work wrong are real. Structural materials that look dry but aren't will grow mold. Smoke residue that isn't fully neutralized keeps off-gassing. Lead-containing paint disturbed in an older home without the right protocols becomes an exposure hazard.

The certifications FIX Restoration holds reflect the standards that govern how this work is supposed to be done, not as a marketing line, but as the actual technical baseline for drying, decontamination, and safe handling of hazardous materials. When you see an industry certification on a restoration contractor's page, what it means in practice is that the crew has been trained to the standard, not just to the job.

## Where we work

American Fork sits in Utah County, roughly midway along the Wasatch Front between Salt Lake City and Provo. The surrounding communities, Lehi, Pleasant Grove, Lindon, Orem, and others across the valley, are where most of the work happens. The housing stock here spans a wide range: newer subdivisions built on the valley floor, older neighborhoods closer to the foothills, and everything in between. Homes built before the 1980s often have plumbing and construction details that require different handling than newer builds. Utah's climate, dry most of the year, with real freeze-thaw cycles in winter and occasional heavy snowmelt, creates predictable patterns for certain types of losses, particularly pipe failures in late winter and moisture intrusion during spring runoff.
