## Why Kaneohe Properties See Elevated Mold Risk

The numbers tell the story: parts of the Koʻolau Range above Kaneohe receive upward of 150 inches of rain per year, and that moisture pressure is constant, not seasonal. Slab-on-grade homes fare differently than raised-foundation structures, but neither is immune. The result is a floor that feels fine underfoot but registers elevated moisture readings when measured with a calibrated meter.

## Our Mold Inspection and Testing Process in Kaneohe

A mold inspection here isn't the same checklist you'd run in a dry-climate market. The assessment begins with a thorough visual survey of the areas most likely to harbor hidden growth: attic sheathing under roof penetrations, the underside of subfloors in raised sections, bathroom exhaust pathways, and any wall cavity adjacent to an exterior that faces the prevailing trade winds. We document what we find with photos and moisture readings before a single sample is collected.

For mold spore testing and indoor air quality testing, we collect both air samples and, where visible growth is present, surface swab or tape-lift samples. Air samples are drawn at multiple locations — typically a baseline outdoor sample plus interior samples in the rooms of concern — so the lab comparison reflects actual spore load relative to the outdoor environment. Samples go to an accredited third-party laboratory for analysis. You receive a written report identifying the species present, the concentration levels, and the areas of the property where readings were elevated. That documentation is yours to keep, share with your physician, or submit to your insurer as part of a property damage claim.
