## Why Hickam Housing Carpets Face Unusual Wear

The Hawaiian climate is beautiful, but it is genuinely hard on interior textiles. Relative humidity on the Ewa side of Oahu regularly sits above 70 percent, which means carpet fibers stay slightly damp even without a spill. That moisture creates ideal conditions for odor-causing bacteria and dust mite colonies to establish themselves deep in the pile, well below where a residential vacuum reaches. Add the fine particulate from Pearl Harbor-area flight operations and the reddish laterite soil common across this part of Oahu, and you have a recipe for carpets that look and smell older than they are.

Military housing units also cycle through many families over their lifespan. Each transition brings different foot traffic patterns, pet histories, and cleaning routines. By the time a carpet reaches its third or fourth occupancy, surface-level cleaning is rarely enough to address what has accumulated at the backing level.

## Our Carpet Cleaning Process, Calibrated for Coastal Conditions

Because humidity slows drying, our process is specifically sequenced to account for Hawaii's climate rather than treating it like a continental job.

We begin with a dry pre-treatment pass to loosen the laterite soil and salt crystals that abrasive vacuuming alone cannot fully lift. A pH-balanced hot-water extraction follows, using truck-mounted equipment powerful enough to flush contaminants from the backing, not just the surface fibers. Extraction pressure and water temperature are dialed in to the fiber type, which matters in housing units that may have a mix of nylon, olefin, and older polyester carpeting installed at different points over the years.

After extraction, we apply a targeted drying protocol. In a high-humidity coastal environment, leaving carpet damp for hours invites the mold and mildew growth you were trying to eliminate in the first place. High-velocity air movers and dehumidification equipment run until moisture readings confirm the carpet and pad are genuinely dry, not just dry at the surface.

Stain removal is addressed as a separate step rather than bundled into the extraction pass. Pet urine, red-clay tracking, and food stains each respond to different chemistry, and applying a single product to all of them produces mediocre results on each. We identify the stain type first, then treat it with the appropriate agent before the final rinse cycle.
