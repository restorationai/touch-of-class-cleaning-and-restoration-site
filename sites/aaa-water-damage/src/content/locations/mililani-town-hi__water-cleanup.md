## Why Mililani Town Properties See Water Damage Differently

Mililani Town was developed in phases beginning in the early 1970s as one of Hawaii's first master-planned communities. That construction era means a large share of the housing stock uses post-tension concrete slab foundations with copper supply lines that are now well past their expected service life. Pinhole leaks in aging copper are among the most common sources of slow, hidden water intrusion here. Because the slabs are finished with tile or vinyl laid directly on concrete, water has nowhere obvious to go, so it migrates laterally under the flooring and into the base of interior partition walls before anyone notices a wet spot.

The climate adds another layer. Mililani Town receives significantly more rainfall than coastal Honolulu, and the surrounding red laterite soil drains poorly during sustained downpours. Homes on lots that slope toward the street can see surface water push against garage door seals or enter through low foundation vents during heavy kona storm events. That combination of aging plumbing and a wetter microclimate means water damage here often involves both a plumbing source and a secondary exterior moisture pathway at the same time.

## Our Emergency Water Cleanup Process in Mililani Town

The first priority on any call is stopping water from spreading. Once the moisture boundary is established, truck-mounted extraction equipment pulls standing water and surface saturation from flooring.
