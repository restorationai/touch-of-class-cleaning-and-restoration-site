## Why Kalaeloa Properties See Mold Issues

The former Naval Air Station Barbers Point footprint that defines much of Kalaeloa today left behind a specific type of building stock: concrete masonry units (CMU) built to military spec, often with minimal vapor barriers and mechanical ventilation systems that were designed for a different era of occupancy. CMU walls wick moisture from the ground and from condensation cycles in ways that wood-framed construction does not. When humidity sits between 70 and 90 percent for extended stretches, which is common on the leeward side during Kona wind events, that moisture has nowhere to go inside a poorly ventilated block structure.

Flat roofs, common throughout the area, are also a recurring culprit. A small membrane breach or a clogged scupper can allow water to pool and slowly migrate into roof decking and ceiling assemblies. By the time water stains appear on interior ceilings, mold colonization in the cavity above may already be several weeks old. Mold can begin to grow on wet organic material in as little as 24 to 48 hours under the right temperature and humidity conditions, and Kalaeloa's climate rarely dips below those thresholds.

## Our Mold Remediation Process in Kalaeloa

Every remediation starts with a thorough inspection and moisture mapping. We use thermal imaging and calibrated moisture meters to locate wet building materials that look dry at the surface, because surface readings alone miss the problem in CMU and stucco assemblies common here.

Once the scope is established, the affected area is isolated with negative air pressure containment before any disturbed material is removed. This keeps spores from migrating to unaffected rooms during the remediation process. Contaminated porous materials are removed, bagged, and disposed of per Hawaii Department of Health guidelines. Hard surfaces are HEPA-vacuumed and treated with an EPA-registered antimicrobial agent. Air scrubbers with HEPA filtration run continuously throughout the project.

After physical remediation is complete, clearance testing by an independent third party is the appropriate final step to confirm the space is ready for reoccupancy. We can coordinate that referral for you, because a clearance test performed by the same company doing the remediation is not an independent result.

## Local Note: Flat Roofs and the Kona Wind Season

One pattern that comes up repeatedly in leeward Oahu properties is mold that traces back not to a plumbing failure but to a Kona wind event. Kona winds bring moisture-laden air from the south and southwest, reversing the usual trade wind direction. Flat-roofed concrete buildings that drain adequately under trade wind conditions can back up when wind-driven rain hits from an unusual angle, pushing water into roof penetrations and parapet walls. If your property experienced a Kona wind event in the past year and you have not had a moisture inspection since, that is worth addressing before the next rainy stretch arrives.
