## Our Commercial Restoration Process in Diamond Head

Every commercial loss is different, but the sequence is consistent: stop the source, assess the scope, document thoroughly, extract and dry, then restore. In Diamond Head's older multi-unit buildings, the documentation step carries extra weight. Co-op boards and HOA managers need a clear record of affected units, moisture readings by floor and wall cavity, and photographic evidence of pre-existing versus new damage — all of it organized in a format that holds up when the association's insurer reviews the claim.

Once documentation is complete, we deploy commercial-grade extraction and structural drying equipment sized to the actual affected area. In high-rise buildings, that often means moving equipment through service elevators and coordinating staging with building management to avoid disrupting residents. Drying timelines are validated with calibrated moisture meters, not guesswork — walls and subfloors are cleared to acceptable levels before any reconstruction begins.

For fire and smoke losses — which can affect commercial kitchens, mechanical rooms, or shared amenity spaces in these older buildings — the process adds odor neutralization and surface cleaning before any rebuild.
