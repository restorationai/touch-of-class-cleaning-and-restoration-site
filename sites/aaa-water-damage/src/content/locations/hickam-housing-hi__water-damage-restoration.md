## Why Hickam Housing Properties Face Distinct Water Damage Risks

The housing stock here was built in phases across several decades, and older units often feature concrete block or hollow-tile construction rather than the wood-frame walls common in continental U.S. homes. That matters during water damage restoration because concrete and tile assemblies absorb moisture differently: the surface can feel dry to the touch while the interior of a block wall stays saturated for days. Standard drying timelines used elsewhere routinely underestimate what it takes to dry these structures properly.

The tropical climate compounds the challenge. Oahu's average relative humidity rarely drops below 60 percent, and inside a home after a plumbing failure it can climb well above 80 percent. At those humidity levels, secondary damage, including microbial growth on organic materials, can begin within 24 to 48 hours of the initial event. Getting extraction and dehumidification equipment on-site quickly is not a convenience here; it is the difference between a contained restoration and a much larger remediation project.

Flat rooflines common in mid-century military housing also create ponding conditions during heavy rainfall. When roof drains back up during a downpour, water can enter through parapet flashings and travel horizontally inside a ceiling cavity before it ever becomes visible below. By the time a stain appears on a ceiling tile, the affected area is often larger than it looks.

## Our Water Damage Restoration Process in Hickam Housing

Every job begins with a thorough moisture assessment using thermal imaging and calibrated moisture meters. In concrete block construction, this step is especially important because visual inspection alone misses saturation inside wall cavities. We map the full extent of the affected area before any materials are moved or removed.

Water extraction comes next. Standing water is removed with truck-mounted and portable extraction units. For flooring assemblies common in this region, including tile over concrete slab, we check the slab-to-tile bond and the condition of any underlayment before deciding whether flooring can be dried in place or needs to be lifted.

Once bulk water is out, we set a drying system calibrated to the ambient conditions. In Hickam Housing's humidity, that typically means a higher ratio of dehumidification capacity relative to airmovers than you would use in a drier climate. We monitor moisture readings at each visit and adjust equipment placement as the structure responds. Documentation is generated throughout, which supports the insurance claim process and satisfies any base housing inspection requirements at close-out.
