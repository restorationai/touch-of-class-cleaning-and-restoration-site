## Our Emergency Water Cleanup Process in Kaimuki

The first priority on arrival is stopping water movement. That means locating the source, whether a burst galvanized fitting under the house or storm intrusion at a low foundation vent, and confirming it is controlled before equipment goes in.

Once the source is addressed, the focus shifts to extraction. Standing water in single-wall homes travels fast along wall cavities because there is no insulation or vapor barrier slowing it down. Truck-mounted extraction pulls surface water quickly, but the real work is identifying how far moisture has migrated using thermal imaging and calibrated moisture meters. In older plaster-over-lath walls, the surface can read close to dry while the lath behind it holds significant saturation.

Drying in Kaimuki's climate requires attention to ambient humidity. Honolulu's trade winds keep outdoor humidity elevated year-round, which means simply opening windows does not accelerate drying the way it might in a drier climate. Industrial dehumidifiers and directed airflow equipment create a controlled drying environment inside the structure regardless of what the outdoor air is doing. Moisture readings are logged at each visit so the drying curve is documented, not estimated.

All affected materials are assessed against drying standards before equipment is removed. Wood framing that does not reach target moisture content stays in the drying protocol until it does.

## Local Note

Single-wall construction is the detail that surprises people unfamiliar with Kaimuki's housing. In a standard mainland home, a wet wall cavity is bounded by insulation and a vapor barrier that slow lateral spread. In a single-wall home, the interior board is the exterior board, and water that gets behind it has a direct path along every stud bay from floor to ceiling. Crews working in these homes probe higher on the wall than they would in a newer structure, because moisture readings at knee height can look fine while the top of the same wall bay is still holding water.
