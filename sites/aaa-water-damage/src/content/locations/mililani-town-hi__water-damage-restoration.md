## Why Mililani Town Properties See Water Damage Issues

Mililani Town was developed largely in the 1970s and 1980s as a master-planned community, which means a significant portion of the housing stock is now 40 to 50 years old. Galvanized steel supply lines common in that era are well past their expected service life, and pinhole leaks inside wall cavities can saturate insulation and framing for days before a homeowner notices a soft spot in the drywall or a musty odor in a hallway.

The central plateau elevation also creates a specific drainage challenge. During periods of heavy rain, Oahu's red laterite soil becomes saturated quickly and sheds water rather than absorbing it. Homes with slab-on-grade foundations, which are common throughout Mililani Town, can experience water intrusion at the slab perimeter when surface runoff overwhelms the grade around the foundation. That water wicks into flooring adhesives and subfloor materials before it ever becomes visible on the surface.

Air conditioning systems are another frequent culprit. In Hawaii's humid climate, condensate drain lines clog with algae growth, and a blocked line can overflow an air handler pan inside a ceiling or closet, dripping onto drywall and insulation below for hours before anyone notices.

## Our Water Damage Restoration Process in Mililani Town

The first step on any water loss is a thorough moisture mapping of the affected area. We use thermal imaging cameras and calibrated moisture meters to locate water that has migrated behind walls or under flooring, because what is visible on the surface is rarely the full picture. In Mililani Town's ranch-style homes, water from a kitchen or laundry leak frequently travels along the slab and shows up in an adjacent room before it is detected.

Once the scope is documented, water extraction begins using truck-mounted and portable extraction units suited to the space. Hard-surface floors, carpet, and pad each require different extraction methods, and we adjust the approach based on what is present. After extraction, we establish a drying system using commercial-grade air movers and dehumidifiers sized to the cubic footage of the affected area. Drying targets are set per industry standards and monitored with daily moisture readings until all materials reach acceptable levels.

Throughout the process, we document conditions with photographs and written logs. That documentation matters when a claim is filed, and it also matters if a question arises later about whether materials were dried adequately before reconstruction began.
