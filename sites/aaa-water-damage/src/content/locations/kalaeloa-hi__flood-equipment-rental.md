## Why Kalaeloa Properties Face Unusual Drying Challenges

Kalaeloa was built largely on reclaimed military land, and much of the residential and light-commercial stock here reflects that origin: concrete slab foundations, older CMU (concrete masonry unit) block construction, and in some cases original mid-century framing that was never designed with modern vapor barriers in mind. Slab-on-grade buildings are particularly unforgiving after a flood because water migrates laterally through the concrete and can saturate the base of interior walls for several feet before it becomes visible at the surface. A single dehumidifier from a hardware store will not move enough moisture to address that kind of saturation. Industrial-grade desiccant or refrigerant dehumidifiers pulling 100-plus pints per day, paired with directional air movers positioned at the wall-floor interface, are what actually change the drying curve in these structures.

The leeward side of Oahu also sees less natural ventilation than Windward or North Shore communities. Without the trade wind assist, outdoor air introduced through open windows can actually raise indoor relative humidity rather than lower it during certain times of day. Proper equipment rental here means using sealed-system drying rather than ventilation-based drying, which changes both the equipment mix and the placement strategy.
