## Our Upholstery Cleaning Process, Calibrated for Aina Haina

Every piece of furniture is different, and so is every home. Before any cleaning begins, a technician inspects the upholstery fabric type — whether that's a performance microfiber on a family room sectional, a vintage barkcloth on a mid-century chair, or a full-grain leather sofa that's been conditioned (or not) through years of salt-air exposure. That inspection determines the cleaning method, the solution pH, and the drying approach.

For fabric upholstery, the process typically involves:

- **Pre-treatment** of high-traffic areas and stains with a pH-appropriate spotter
- **Hot-water extraction** (commonly called steam cleaning) that flushes soil and residue out of the fiber rather than pushing it deeper
- **Controlled drying** — critical in Aina Haina's humidity, where air-drying alone can leave foam cores damp long after the surface feels dry

For leather, the approach shifts entirely: extraction equipment stays off the leather face, and the focus moves to a careful clean-and-condition sequence that removes salt residue and body oils without stripping the protective finish that keeps leather from cracking in Hawaii's UV-intense environment.

Portable hot-water extraction units — rather than truck-mounted systems — are the practical choice for upholstery work inside Aina Haina homes.
