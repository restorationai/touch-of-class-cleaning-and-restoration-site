## Our Flood Damage Restoration Process in Kaimuki

Every flood job starts with moisture mapping, not assumptions. We use thermal imaging and calibrated moisture meters to trace exactly where water traveled — inside single-wall construction, that path is rarely obvious from the surface. Water wicks laterally through the thin board sheathing common in pre-1950 Kaimuki homes faster than it does through modern framing, so the wet zone is almost always wider than the visible damage suggests.

Once we know the scope, we extract standing water, then position drying equipment — industrial air movers and dehumidifiers — based on the actual moisture readings, not a generic room layout. Drying targets follow the IICRC S500 standard for structural drying, and we monitor and log readings daily so you have documentation for your insurance claim. Photograph the damage yourself before we arrive if you can do so safely; that record supports your claim from the start.

For homes with ohana units, we plan the mitigation schedule to minimize disruption to occupied spaces. That often means phasing equipment placement so one unit can remain livable while the other dries — something that takes coordination but matters enormously in multigenerational households.

## Local Note: What Single-Wall Construction Means for Drying Time

Kaimuki's plantation-era single-wall homes present a drying challenge that doesn't show up in textbook restoration scenarios. Standard double-wall framing creates an air gap that helps drying equipment pull moisture out efficiently. Single-wall board sheathing, by contrast, is dense and continuous — moisture trapped inside it releases slowly, and the boards can cup or split if drying is rushed with too much airflow too fast. We calibrate equipment settings specifically for this construction type, running lower-velocity air movement over longer drying cycles rather than the aggressive short-cycle approach that works on modern drywall. Homeowners sometimes expect a two-day turnaround; in a genuine single-wall flood scenario, four to five days of active drying is more realistic, and skipping that time is how secondary mold problems start.
