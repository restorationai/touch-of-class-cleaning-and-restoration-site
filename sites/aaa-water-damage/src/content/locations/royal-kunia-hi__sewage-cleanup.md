## Why Royal Kunia Properties See Sewage Backup Issues

The Ewa Plain's compacted red clay soil drains poorly, and that characteristic shapes how sewer and septic systems behave in this part of Oahu. During heavy Kona storm events, groundwater pressure builds against lateral sewer lines, pushing back through the lowest fixtures in a home. Royal Kunia's residential development, which expanded rapidly through the 1990s and early 2000s, means many homes share infrastructure that has now aged into its second or third decade without significant rehabilitation.

Septic systems in the area face particular stress. The shallow water table limits the effective absorption field depth, and a saturated field after prolonged rainfall can force effluent back toward the home rather than away from it. Homes on the outer edges of the community that rely on individual systems rather than municipal sewer connections are especially vulnerable after multi-day rain events. The result is a septic overflow cleanup situation that involves not just the interior of the home but potentially the yard and any crawl-space access points beneath the structure.

## Our Sewage Cleanup and Sanitization Process in Royal Kunia

Sewage cleanup is a category-three water loss, meaning every material it contacts is presumed contaminated until proven otherwise. The process here is not simply mopping and airing out: it requires containment, controlled removal, and verified sanitization.

The crew begins by establishing a containment perimeter to prevent cross-contamination into unaffected areas of the home. Standing sewage is extracted using truck-mounted or portable units capable of handling solid-laden waste, not standard water extractors. Affected flooring, baseboards, and lower wall cavities are assessed for saturation and removed if they cannot be adequately sanitized in place. Tile and concrete can often be cleaned and treated with EPA-registered disinfectants; carpet, drywall, and insulation that have absorbed raw sewage are typically removed and bagged for disposal in accordance with Hawaii Department of Health guidelines.

After physical removal, surfaces are treated with hospital-grade antimicrobial agents and allowed to dwell before a secondary wipe-down. Drying equipment, including air movers and dehumidifiers, runs until moisture readings confirm the structure is within acceptable ranges. In Royal Kunia's climate, ambient humidity rarely drops below 60 percent, which means mechanical drying does more of the work than open-air ventilation alone.
