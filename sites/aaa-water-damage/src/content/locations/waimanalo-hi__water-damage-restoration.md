## Why Waimanalo Properties See Water Damage Issues

The windward side of the Ko'olau Range squeezes rainfall out of passing clouds at a rate that can overwhelm even well-maintained drainage systems. Waimanalo's low-lying coastal plain means runoff has nowhere fast to go, and during sustained rain events, water can sheet across yards and find its way through foundation vents, garage slabs, and around door thresholds before a homeowner realizes there is a problem inside.

Homes built in the 1950s and 1960s, which make up a meaningful share of Waimanalo's residential stock, often have jalousie windows, louvered eaves, and open-air construction details that were designed for ventilation, not water resistance. Those same features can let wind-driven rain penetrate wall cavities. Older plumbing in these homes, frequently copper or galvanized steel that has never been replaced, is also more prone to pinhole leaks and joint failures, especially where pipes run through concrete slabs that shift with seasonal soil movement.

## Our Water Damage Restoration Process in Waimanalo

When we arrive, the first priority is stopping ongoing water intrusion and identifying hidden moisture. In Waimanalo's humid environment, a surface reading on a moisture meter can look acceptable while the substrate underneath, whether it is the concrete slab, the wall framing, or the subfloor, is still holding significant water. We use thermal imaging and penetrating moisture meters to map the full extent of saturation before any drying equipment is placed.

Water extraction comes next. Standing water is removed with truck-mounted and portable extraction units, including equipment suited for the tight interior layouts common in older single-wall construction homes. Once extraction is complete, we set drying systems calibrated for Waimanalo's ambient humidity, which is consistently higher than the 50 percent relative humidity that standard drying protocols assume. Dehumidifiers run harder and longer here, and we monitor conditions daily to adjust equipment placement as the structure releases moisture.

Structural drying on the windward side of Oahu routinely runs longer than the same job would take in a low-humidity climate. We document every moisture reading throughout the process so that when the job closes, there is a clear record showing materials reached dry standard before equipment was removed.

## Local Note

Single-wall construction, the building method where a single layer of boards or panels serves as both the interior and exterior wall surface, is common in older Waimanalo homes and it behaves in a way that surprises homeowners who have dealt with water damage in mainland houses. Because there is no insulated cavity between interior and exterior, water that penetrates the wall surface saturates the structural material directly. Drying has to happen from both sides simultaneously, which changes how we position air movers and sometimes requires removing exterior cladding to get airflow to the framing. If a contractor treats single-wall construction the same as standard double-wall framing, they will pull equipment too early and leave moisture behind.
