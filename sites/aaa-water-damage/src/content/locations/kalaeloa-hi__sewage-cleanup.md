## Why Kalaeloa Properties See Sewage Backup Issues

Kalaeloa was developed largely on dredged and filled land originally prepared for Barbers Point Naval Air Station. That fill history matters for drainage: the substrate beneath many properties here does not drain the way native soil does, and during heavy Kona storm events, lateral pressure on aging sewer lines increases significantly. Properties that were converted from military housing or industrial use often have plumbing infrastructure that predates modern PVC standards, meaning cast-iron or clay-tile sewer laterals that are prone to root intrusion and joint separation.

The area also sits in a zone where municipal sewer service and private septic systems coexist. Homes and commercial parcels on the older sections of the former base footprint may still rely on septic tanks that were sized for a different era's occupancy loads. When those systems back up, the overflow typically surfaces through floor drains, low-point fixtures, or through the ground itself, which means contamination can spread horizontally before it is even noticed.

## Our Sewage Cleanup and Sanitization Process in Kalaeloa

Raw sewage is classified as Category 3 water, the most contaminated classification in the industry, and the response reflects that. The first step on any job is containment: isolating the affected area to prevent cross-contamination into clean spaces, and establishing negative air pressure where the layout allows. Affected porous materials, including drywall, insulation, and flooring underlayment, are removed rather than dried in place, because pathogens embed in those materials in ways that surface-level sanitization cannot reach.

Once the contaminated material is out, the structural surfaces that remain are treated with EPA-registered disinfectants appropriate for Category 3 losses. In Kalaeloa's climate, where humidity rarely drops below 60 percent even in dry months, the sanitization phase has to account for the fact that residual moisture accelerates microbial regrowth. Industrial dehumidification runs alongside the disinfection process, not after it. Final clearance is based on moisture readings and visual inspection, not a fixed number of days.

## Local Note

One thing that catches property owners off guard in Kalaeloa is the presence of coral rock aggregate in older slab construction and fill beneath some structures. Unlike poured concrete on the mainland, coral aggregate is highly porous and can wick sewage contamination laterally well beyond the visible spill footprint. This means the affected zone on a slab-on-grade property here is often larger than it appears at the surface, and moisture mapping with thermal imaging is a necessary step before any sanitization work begins, not an optional add-on. Skipping that step risks leaving contamination in place under a clean-looking floor.
