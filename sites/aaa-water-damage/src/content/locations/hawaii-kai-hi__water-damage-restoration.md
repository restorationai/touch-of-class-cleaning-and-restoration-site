## Why Hawaii Kai Properties See Water Damage Differently

Homes built during the Kaiser development boom of the 1960s and '70s carry plumbing that was never designed to last this long in a marine environment. Galvanized steel supply lines corrode from the inside out; copper fittings develop pinhole leaks behind tile walls for months before anyone notices the stain. Slab-on-grade construction — common throughout West Marina and Mariners Cove — means there's no crawl space to catch a slow drip. Water migrates laterally under the slab and surfaces feet away from the actual break, making the source harder to locate without moisture mapping equipment.

The HOA dimension adds another layer. Many of Hawaii Kai's marina-front townhome complexes share wall assemblies, and a leak in one unit routinely wets the framing in the units on either side. HOA bylaws often dictate which party is responsible for what portion of the repair, and documentation — moisture readings, scope of work, photo logs — becomes essential for sorting out those claims.

Kona Low storm events bring a different problem entirely. Low-lying lots near the Hawaii Kai Marina can take on storm runoff faster than the city's drainage infrastructure handles it, pushing water under doors and into garages. That's not a plumbing failure; it's a flood-category event, and the extraction and drying approach is calibrated accordingly.

## Our Water Damage Restoration Process in Hawaii Kai

The first step on any job is finding all the water — not just the visible puddle. We use thermal imaging cameras and calibrated moisture meters to map how far saturation has traveled through walls, subflooring, and slab edges. In slab-on-grade homes, this step often reveals that water has traveled several feet from the source, which changes the drying plan significantly.

Once the scope is clear, extraction comes first: truck-mounted and portable extraction units pull standing water from carpet, hardwood, and tile. From there, we place industrial air movers and dehumidifiers in a calculated pattern based on the room geometry and measured humidity levels — not just one unit per room. Drying logs are taken every 24 hours so progress is documented and the equipment configuration can be adjusted if a wall cavity or subfloor is drying slower than expected.

For affected drywall and insulation that can't be dried in place, controlled demolition removes only what needs to go. In townhome units where a neighboring HOA unit shares the wall, we coordinate with the property manager before opening any shared assembly.

Contractors who work primarily on the mainland sometimes underestimate how much Hawaii's ambient humidity slows structural drying. Even on a clear trade-wind day, outdoor relative humidity in Hawaii Kai hovers between 60 and 75 percent — well above the threshold where building materials release moisture efficiently into the air.
