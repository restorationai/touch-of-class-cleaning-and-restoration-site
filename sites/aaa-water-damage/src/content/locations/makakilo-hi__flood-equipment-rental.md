## Why Makakilo Properties See Unique Water Damage Challenges

Makakilo's position on the Waianae foothills creates a microclimate that catches many homeowners off guard. The community sits above the coastal plain, so it receives measurably more rainfall than the beaches below, and that moisture does not always drain the way residents expect. Homes here are built on sloped lots with compacted red laterite soil that sheds water quickly across the surface but can channel runoff directly against foundations and garage slabs. During periods of heavy rain, that runoff finds its way under doors, through weep holes, and into the lower levels of split-level homes that are common throughout the neighborhood.

The housing stock in Makakilo skews toward construction from the 1970s through the early 2000s, when concrete block and slab-on-grade construction dominated western Oahu development. Concrete block walls wick moisture inward slowly, and a slab that stays wet underneath can take significantly longer to dry than a wood-subfloor system where airflow can be introduced from below. Knowing that going in changes how you stage drying equipment: more low-profile air movers positioned to sweep across the slab surface, and a dehumidifier sized for the actual square footage rather than a residential unit pulled from a hardware store shelf.
