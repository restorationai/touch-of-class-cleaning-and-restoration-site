## Our Flood Damage Restoration Process in Diamond Head

We respond around the clock, so the call you make at 2 a.m. gets the same answer as the one at noon. Once on site, the process follows a deliberate sequence calibrated to what we actually find in Diamond Head properties:

**Assessment and source control.** Before any drying equipment goes in, we identify where the water originated. In a high-rise building, that often means working with the resident manager or co-op board to isolate the affected riser or valve and confirm the source is secured. Skipping this step wastes everyone's time.

**Extraction.** Truck-mounted and portable extractors pull standing water from flooring, carpet, and subfloor assemblies. In older concrete-framed buildings, water migrates into expansion joints and hollow-core floor systems — areas that look dry on the surface but register saturated on a moisture meter.

**Structural drying.** Industrial air movers and dehumidifiers run in a calculated configuration based on the room volume, material types, and ambient humidity. Hawaii's baseline humidity means equipment runs longer than it would in a dry climate — we monitor readings daily and adjust placement until materials reach acceptable moisture levels.

**Documentation throughout.** Every moisture reading, every photo, every equipment log is recorded. For co-op boards and HOA resident managers coordinating with an association insurer, thorough documentation is not optional — it's what makes the claim defensible. We provide a complete written record of conditions found, work performed, and final clearance readings.

## Local Note

One thing that catches people off guard in Diamond Head high-rises: the buildings constructed closest to Kaimana Beach and the Diamond Head State Monument corridor often used terrazzo and quarry tile throughout common areas and individual units in the original construction. Terrazzo is dense and non-absorbent on its surface, but the sand-set mortar bed beneath it holds water tenaciously. A flood event that looks contained to the surface layer can leave the mortar bed saturated for weeks if drying equipment isn't positioned to address the underside — something that requires drilling small access points and directing airflow beneath the tile rather than just running movers across the top. Missing that step leads to mold colonization in the mortar layer months after the visible damage is gone.
