The Ko'olau Range funnels rainfall directly into the valleys and wetlands around Heeia, and the area's historically high water table means groundwater can push upward through concrete slabs and crawl spaces even after surface flooding recedes. Homes built closer to He'eia Stream or the fishpond corridor often sit on soils with limited drainage capacity, so water that enters a structure doesn't simply evaporate on its own once the rain stops.

## Local Note

One thing that catches people off guard in Heeia specifically: the trade winds that make windward O'ahu feel comfortable outdoors can actually slow indoor drying when windows and doors are left open during equipment rental. Outdoor relative humidity on the windward side of the Ko'olau range regularly runs higher than on the leeward side, meaning ambient air coming through open windows can introduce more moisture than the dehumidifier is removing. The correct approach indoors is to seal the drying zone, let the LGR dehumidifier work against the contained air mass, and vent only when outdoor dew point drops. It's counterintuitive if you're used to "open everything up to dry it out," but it's the reason rental equipment in Heeia needs to be sized and positioned differently than it would be in, say, a dry Phoenix garage.
