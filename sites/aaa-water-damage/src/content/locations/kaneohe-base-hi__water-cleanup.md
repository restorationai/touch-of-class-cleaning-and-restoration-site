## Why Kaneohe Base Properties Face Elevated Water Damage Risk

The windward coast receives some of the highest annual rainfall in the Hawaiian Islands, and the clay-heavy soils common to this side of Oahu drain slowly. When rain falls faster than the ground can absorb it, water finds the path of least resistance, which is often through a foundation crack, a garage threshold, or a ground-floor door frame. Military housing and older residential construction in the surrounding Kaneohe corridor frequently features concrete slab foundations with minimal vapor barriers, a design that made sense for the climate decades ago but leaves modern occupants vulnerable when groundwater pressure builds beneath the slab.

Base housing units built in earlier construction phases also tend to have interior wall assemblies that trap moisture behind surface materials. A slow leak behind a cabinet or beneath a bathroom floor can stay hidden for weeks in a climate where ambient humidity is already high, making it difficult to notice the difference between normal condensation and active intrusion until discoloration or odor appears.
