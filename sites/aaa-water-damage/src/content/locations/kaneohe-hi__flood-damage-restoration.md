## Why Kaneohe Properties See Flood Damage Differently

The numbers tell the story: parts of the Koʻolau Range above Kaneohe receive upward of 200 inches of rain annually, and that moisture doesn't stay on the ridge. It sheets down into subdivisions built in the 1960s and 1970s — homes with original cast-iron or galvanized plumbing, crawl spaces that were never designed for today's storm intensities, and lots that sit just a few feet above seasonal stream channels. Kahaluu and Ahuimanu are particularly vulnerable: properties along those stream corridors can go from dry to knee-deep in under an hour during a flash-flood advisory. Hillside runoff also carries a heavy sediment load, which means flood water here isn't just water — it's a slurry that stains concrete, clogs floor drains, and leaves a fine clay film on every surface it touches.

The chronically high humidity compounds everything. Mold colonization that might take 72 hours in a drier climate can begin in 24 to 48 hours in Kaneohe's windward air, especially inside wall cavities and under flooring where ventilation is poor. That compressed timeline changes how urgently extraction and structural drying need to begin.

## Our Flood Damage Restoration Process in Kaneohe

When we arrive, the first priority is safety — confirming utilities are isolated before anyone steps into standing water. From there, the process moves in a deliberate sequence:

**Extraction** — Truck-mounted and portable extractors pull bulk water from flooring, subfloor assemblies, and crawl spaces. In homes along the Kaneohe Bay Drive corridor, we frequently encounter concrete slab construction over compacted fill, which holds water differently than a wood-framed subfloor and requires longer extraction passes.

**Moisture mapping** — Thermal imaging cameras and calibrated moisture meters document exactly how far saturation has migrated into walls, framing, and cabinetry. This documentation matters for your insurance claim records, so photograph the visible damage yourself before we begin as well.

**Structural drying** — Industrial desiccant and refrigerant dehumidifiers, paired with high-velocity air movers, drive moisture out of structural materials. Drying logs are recorded daily. In Kaneohe's ambient humidity, dehumidifier capacity has to be sized up — a unit that would dry a comparable room in a drier climate may need to be supplemented here.

**Antimicrobial treatment** — Given the accelerated mold risk on the windward side, affected surfaces receive EPA-registered antimicrobial application after drying targets are confirmed.

**Debris removal and scope documentation** — Materials that cannot be dried to standard (saturated insulation, buckled hardwood, compromised drywall) are removed and documented for your records.
