## Our Flood Damage Restoration Process in Aina Haina

Flood restoration in single-wall redwood homes requires a different approach than work in modern drywall construction. Drywall is cut out and replaced quickly; redwood framing and board siding must be carefully assessed — some can be dried in place, some must be removed to allow airflow into cavities that would otherwise stay wet for weeks.

Here's how the work typically unfolds:

- **Rapid water extraction** — Standing water is removed using truck-mounted and portable extraction equipment. In lower-floor spaces and garages, where Aina Haina's valley runoff tends to pool, we prioritize getting water out before it migrates under flooring and into subfloor assemblies.
- **Moisture mapping** — Thermal imaging and calibrated moisture meters identify exactly where water has traveled inside walls and under floors. In older homes, water often travels farther than it looks.
- **Controlled drying** — Industrial air movers and dehumidifiers are positioned to dry structural cavities, not just surface materials. In Hawaii's baseline humidity, ambient air alone will not dry a flooded wall cavity — dedicated equipment is essential.
- **Contamination assessment** — Valley flood water picks up runoff from driveways, streets, and hillside soil. We assess contamination level before determining whether materials can be dried in place or must be removed and replaced.
- **Documentation** — Photographs and moisture readings are logged throughout, giving you a clear record for your insurance claim.
