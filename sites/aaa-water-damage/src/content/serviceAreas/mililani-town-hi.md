## Restoration emergencies common in Mililani Town

Mililani Town sits in the center of Oahu, roughly 20 miles north of Honolulu, at an elevation that catches trade-wind moisture from both the Ko'olau and Waianae ranges. That geography means the community sees more rainfall than coastal Honolulu, and the afternoon humidity rarely drops low enough for wet building materials to dry on their own. When a supply line fails, a roof flashing leaks, or a washing machine overflows, moisture moves fast through the wall cavities and flooring assemblies common in this planned community.

Mililani Town was developed primarily from the late 1960s through the 1990s, and the housing stock reflects that era: single-family homes and townhomes built on slab-on-grade foundations, with stucco exteriors, tile roofs, and interior walls that are typically drywall over wood framing. Slab construction eliminates crawlspace flooding, but it concentrates plumbing under concrete, which means a slab leak can saturate flooring and lower wall sections for days before it is noticed. The community's HOA structure also means that restoration work on shared walls or common-area systems often requires coordination with association management before or during the job.
