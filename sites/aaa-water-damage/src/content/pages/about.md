Water damage doesn't wait for a convenient moment. A pipe bursts at 2 a.m., a washing machine hose fails while you're at work, a heavy Kona storm pushes water under a door you didn't know was vulnerable. AAA Water Damage Restoration & Carpet Care was built around that reality — a Honolulu-based company focused on the kind of work that has to happen fast, be done right, and leave a home genuinely dry rather than just surface-dry.

## What we do

The day-to-day of restoration work is less glamorous than it sounds. It's arriving at a house where a family has already moved furniture onto their lanai and is watching water wick up the baseboards. It's setting up extraction equipment, mapping the moisture with meters rather than guessing, and explaining to a homeowner why the drying process takes days — not hours — before walls can be closed back up. It's answering the phone at 3 a.m. because our hours are around the clock, every day of the week. And it's the follow-through: returning to check readings, adjusting airflow, and not signing off until the numbers confirm the structure is dry.

Carpet care is woven into the same discipline. A flood-soaked carpet tells a story in layers — the fiber, the backing, the pad, the subfloor beneath. Knowing which layers can be salvaged and which need to go is the difference between a job that holds up and one that breeds odor and mold six weeks later.

## Our certifications and licensure

This section will be updated as credentialing information is confirmed. What we can say plainly: restoration work in Hawaii carries real stakes. Pre-1978 homes — common across older Honolulu neighborhoods like Kalihi, Palama, and parts of Manoa — can contain lead-based paint, which federal guidelines require contractors to handle under specific protocols. Mold work involves containment and cross-contamination risks that go beyond a shop vac and a box fan.
