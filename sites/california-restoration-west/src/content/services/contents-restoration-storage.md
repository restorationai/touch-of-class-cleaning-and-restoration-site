When smoke, soot, water, or mold hits a home, the structure gets most of the attention, but the furniture, clothing, documents, electronics, and irreplaceable keepsakes inside it are often what homeowners lose sleep over. Contents restoration is the discipline of recovering those items rather than replacing them, and it requires a completely different skill set than drying walls or removing mold. The window to save smoke-saturated upholstery or water-logged artwork is narrow, and what happens in the first 24 to 72 hours determines what can be brought back and what cannot.

## What Contents Restoration & Storage actually involves

Contents restoration covers everything that is not permanently attached to the structure: furniture, clothing, electronics, appliances, artwork, documents, photographs, collectibles, and personal items of every kind. After a loss event, these items are inventoried, categorized by material and damage type, and then routed to the appropriate cleaning or restoration method.

Soot from a kitchen fire behaves differently on silk upholstery than on a painted wood cabinet. Water-damaged paper documents require freeze-drying or air-drying under controlled humidity before any other treatment. Electronics that have been exposed to smoke or moisture need professional evaluation before power is ever applied again. Each material category has its own protocol, and skipping steps or applying the wrong chemistry causes permanent damage.

Items that cannot be safely restored on-site are packed out and transported to a climate-controlled facility where they are cleaned, dried, deodorized, and stored until the structure is ready to receive them. That pack-out process is documented with a room-by-room inventory, photographs, and condition notes, a record that matters both for the restoration process and for your insurance claim.

## Our process

1. **Pre-cleaning inventory and condition assessment.** Every item in the affected area is photographed and logged before anything is moved or touched. Condition is noted at intake: pre-existing damage is separated from loss-related damage. This protects you and creates the documentation your adjuster needs.

2. **Pack-out and secure transport.** Items are packed by material type and fragility, wrapped and boxed to prevent secondary damage in transit, and transported to a climate-controlled storage and restoration facility. Specialty items, artwork, antiques, electronics, are handled with separate protocols.

3. **Material-specific cleaning and restoration.** Upholstered furniture may go through ultrasonic cleaning or dry-cleaning processes. Hard surfaces are cleaned with pH-appropriate chemistry matched to the soil type (soot, mold, sewage residue, or mineral deposits from water). Documents and photographs are stabilized. Electronics are evaluated and cleaned by technicians who understand the difference between surface contamination and internal damage.

4. **Deodorization.** Smoke and sewage odors bond to porous materials at a molecular level. Effective deodorization for contents uses ozone treatment, hydroxyl generation, or thermal fogging depending on the material and odor source, not just masking sprays. Items are treated, aired, and tested before being cleared.

5. **Storage, final inspection, and return.** Restored items are stored in a secure, climate-controlled facility until the structure is ready. At return, a final condition check is completed against the intake inventory so nothing is delivered back in worse shape than it left.

## What separates a good contents response from a bad one

The most common failure in contents restoration is treating all materials the same. Applying alkaline soot cleaners to fabrics that need dry-cleaning, or running water-damaged documents through a standard drying process instead of freeze-drying, causes the kind of irreversible damage that no amount of additional work can fix.

A second failure point is documentation. Insurance adjusters reviewing a contents claim need a line-item inventory with pre-loss condition noted, photographs at intake, and a clear record of what was restored versus what was deemed a total loss. Without that documentation, legitimate claims get disputed or underpaid.

A third issue is odor. Many operators clean visible soot or soil and consider the job done. Odor molecules that have penetrated porous materials, foam cushions, wood grain, fabric fibers, will off-gas for months if not treated at the source. The IICRC S500 and related standards address this, and a certified firm follows those protocols rather than cutting the deodorization step.

## Seasonal and regional considerations

Ventura County's dry summers and Santa Ana wind events create conditions where wildfires move fast and smoke infiltrates homes well beyond the fire perimeter. Fine ash and soot particles from wildfire smoke are smaller than those produced by a typical structure fire, which means they penetrate deeper into upholstery, HVAC systems, and stored clothing. Contents in homes that were never directly threatened can still carry significant smoke contamination after a regional fire event. Coastal humidity in Ventura and Oxnard also means that water-damaged contents left in place, even after the structure is dried, can develop secondary mold growth on fabric and wood surfaces faster than in drier inland climates.
