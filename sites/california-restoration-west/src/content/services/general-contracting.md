Not every renovation starts with a demo day you planned. A good number of ours start with a water line that let go behind a vanity, a kitchen fire that stopped at the cabinets but left the drywall behind them unusable, or a roof leak that turned a ceiling into something that has to come down before it falls down. California Restoration West handles both tracks: renovations and remodels homeowners choose to do, and the rebuild work that follows a covered loss once the drying, demolition, or mitigation phase is finished.

## What renovations, remodels and general contracting actually involves

General contracting on a residential project covers the full span between a torn-out room and a finished one: framing, drywall, electrical and plumbing rough-in, insulation, flooring, cabinetry, paint, and trim. A kitchen remodel usually means new layout or cabinet runs, counter and backsplash work, and updated electrical to meet current code for GFCI and appliance circuits. A bathroom remodel carries its own complications, mainly waterproofing: the membrane behind tile, the slope of the shower pan, and how the subfloor was treated matter more than the finish tile itself. Post-damage rebuild work is different again. It starts where mitigation ends, meaning the wet materials are already out or the soot-damaged drywall already stripped, and the job is to put the structure back to pre-loss condition, documented against the insurance scope line by line.

## Our process

1. **Scope of work.** We walk the space, measure what's there, and write a scope that spells out materials, labor, and sequence, not a vague estimate. For insurance-related rebuilds, this scope is built to reconcile against the carrier's line-item estimate.
2. **Permits and code review.** Anything touching structure, electrical, plumbing, or egress gets checked against current code before a wall goes back up. We handle the permit submission and inspection scheduling with the local building department.
3. **Framing and rough-in.** Structural repairs, wall framing, and the rough electrical, plumbing, and HVAC work happen before anything gets closed in, so trades can inspect their own work and the inspector can sign off on it.
4. **Finishing.** Drywall, flooring, cabinetry, tile, paint, and fixtures go in roughly in that order, since each trade depends on the one before it being done and dry.
5. **Final walkthrough.** We go through the finished space against the original scope, note anything that needs touch-up, and close out permits and, where applicable, the insurance file.

## What separates a good renovation response from a bad one

The mistakes that cost homeowners money later are rarely visible on move-in day. A rebuild that skips moisture testing before closing in a wall can trap residual dampness behind new drywall, and the mold problem shows up again in eight months, this time hidden. A bathroom remodel that reuses an old shower pan liner instead of replacing it is a leak waiting on a timeline nobody can predict. A kitchen remodel that doesn't update an undersized electrical panel or circuit to match new appliance loads passes a casual look but fails a real inspection, or worse, trips breakers for years.

On insurance-related rebuilds, adjusters are checking for a few specific things: that the rebuild scope matches the approved estimate in material grade and quantity, that permits were pulled where required, and that the work is documented with photos at each stage, not just before-and-after shots. A contractor who treats the insurance scope as a ceiling to pad, or a floor to shortcut under, creates problems for the homeowner down the line. We write to the scope as it stands and flag discrepancies before work starts, not after.

## What does renovation and general contracting work cost?

Costs vary by scope, material selection, and whether the work is a standalone remodel or a post-damage rebuild tied to an insurance claim, and the ranges below reflect typical industry figures rather than a quote. Every loss and every renovation is different, which is why California Restoration West provides a written scope of work with line-item pricing before anything starts. ## Seasonal & regional considerations

Ventura County's older housing stock, especially homes built before the 1980s, often has galvanized plumbing, undersized electrical panels, or single-pane windows that come up during any remodel that opens a wall. Coastal humidity and marine air can also accelerate corrosion on exterior-facing fixtures and framing near the coast, which is worth checking during any kitchen or bathroom remodel that touches an exterior wall. For post-damage rebuilds following winter storm season, permitting and inspection schedules with local building departments can run longer when storm-related rebuild volume is high across the county.
