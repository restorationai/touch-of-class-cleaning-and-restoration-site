## Why Simi Valley Properties See Mold Issues

Simi Valley sits in an inland valley that funnels cool, moisture-laden air from the coast on certain weather patterns, then bakes dry for weeks at a time. That cycling — wet then dry, wet then dry — stresses building materials and opens micro-cracks in stucco and grout that invite water intrusion. Homes built during the tract-development boom of the 1960s and 1970s, which make up a large share of the housing stock across the valley, were often constructed with minimal attic ventilation and single-pane windows that condensed heavily in winter. Condensation that drips repeatedly into a wall cavity or sits under a windowsill can produce visible mold colonization in as little as 24 to 48 hours under the right temperature conditions.

Flat and low-slope roofs common on mid-century ranch homes are another persistent entry point. A small roof membrane failure can route water to the top plate of an exterior wall, where it wicks downward invisibly for months before showing up as a stain or a soft spot in drywall. By that point, the mold colony is well established behind the surface.

## Our Mold Remediation Process in Simi Valley

Every job starts with a thorough assessment — moisture meters, thermal imaging where warranted, and a visual inspection of the spaces most likely to harbor hidden growth: attic sheathing, subfloor cavities, HVAC air handlers, and the interior face of exterior walls. We document findings with photographs before any work begins, which matters when an insurance claim is involved.

Containment comes next. Negative air pressure barriers isolate the affected area so that disturbing mold colonies does not push spores into clean living spaces. HEPA-filtered air scrubbers run continuously during remediation. Affected porous materials — drywall, insulation, wood framing that cannot be effectively cleaned — are removed, bagged, and disposed of per California Department of Public Health guidelines. Remaining structural surfaces are treated and dried to the moisture levels specified in the IICRC S520 standard before any reconstruction begins.

Clearance testing, performed by an independent industrial hygienist if the scope warrants it, confirms that airborne spore counts have returned to normal background levels. We do not perform our own clearance testing — keeping that step independent protects the homeowner.

## Local Note

Simi Valley's stucco-clad homes from the 1970s and 1980s present a specific challenge during mold remediation: the original brown-coat and finish-coat stucco system can trap moisture against wood sheathing for far longer than modern exterior assemblies. Moisture readings at the stucco face often look acceptable while the OSB or plank sheathing behind it remains well above safe thresholds. We probe and meter at multiple depths on these exteriors before concluding that a wall cavity is dry — a step that is easy to skip and costly to overlook.
