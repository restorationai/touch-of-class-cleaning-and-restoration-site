## Why Simi Valley Properties Take Storm Damage Differently

The city's location at the eastern edge of Ventura County means it sits at the convergence of several weather patterns that rarely affect coastal communities the same way. Santa Ana wind events — which accelerate through mountain gaps — can arrive with little warning and peel roofing material, topple mature trees, and drive rain horizontally under eaves and around window frames that were never designed for that angle of impact.

Simi Valley's housing stock adds another layer of complexity. A significant portion of the city's single-family neighborhoods were built during the rapid suburban expansion of the 1960s through the 1980s. Homes from that era commonly feature wood-framed roofs with composition shingles, stucco exteriors over wood sheathing, and original aluminum-frame windows — all of which have specific failure points under sustained wind or hail. Stucco that looks intact after a storm can be hiding water intrusion at cracks too fine to see from the ground, and that moisture migrates into the sheathing and wall cavity before it ever shows up as a stain on interior drywall.

The region's clay-heavy soils compound the drainage problem. During a heavy rain event, saturation happens quickly, runoff increases, and low-lying areas near drainage channels can experience localized flooding even when the storm itself isn't classified as severe.

## Our Storm Damage Restoration Process in Simi Valley

When we arrive, the first priority is a systematic exterior and interior assessment — not a visual scan, but a methodical inspection that includes the roof deck, attic space, wall cavities at penetration points, and any below-grade areas. We use moisture meters and thermal imaging to find water that has already traveled beyond the visible damage zone, because storm water rarely stays where it enters.

Once the scope is documented, we move through a defined sequence:

- **Emergency stabilization**: tarping compromised roof sections, boarding broken windows or doors, and addressing any active water entry points before the next weather event.
- **Water extraction and structural drying**: standing water is extracted immediately; affected building materials are dried using industrial air movers and dehumidifiers calibrated to the ambient conditions. Drying is managed to the IICRC S500 standard, with daily moisture readings logged for your insurance file.
- **Debris and tree damage removal**: downed limbs and wind-driven debris are cleared from the structure and staging areas before interior work proceeds.
- **Damage documentation**: every affected surface is photographed, measured, and recorded in a format that supports a complete insurance claim.
- **Repair and reconstruction**: once materials are confirmed dry and stable, we move into repairs — from replacing damaged sheathing and roofing to repairing stucco, drywall, and interior finishes.

All field staff are OSHA trained, and the company holds IICRC certifications in Water Restoration, Structural Drying, and Applied Microbial Remediation — the last being relevant any time storm water sits long enough for biological growth to begin, which in warm Ventura County conditions can happen faster than most homeowners realize.

## Local Note

One pattern worth knowing if you own property in the hillside or foothill-adjacent areas of Simi Valley: post-storm debris flows can carry sediment and organic material into subfloor crawlspaces and garage slab perimeters through foundation vents and low-clearance gaps. The damage is easy to miss in the immediate aftermath when attention is on the roof and interior, but sediment-laden moisture in a crawlspace creates conditions for both structural wood deterioration and microbial growth. If your property sits on a slope or backs up to a drainage swale, ask us to include the crawlspace in the initial inspection — it takes fifteen minutes and can prevent a much larger problem from developing quietly over the following weeks.
