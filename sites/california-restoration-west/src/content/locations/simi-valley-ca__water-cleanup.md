## Why Simi Valley Properties See Water Damage Differently

The geology and climate here create a specific combination of risk. Much of Simi Valley sits on expansive clay soils that swell when wet and shrink when dry — a cycle that stresses foundation walls, slab joints, and underground plumbing year after year. Homes built during the valley's major growth periods in the 1970s and 1980s often have galvanized steel supply lines that are now well past their service life. When those pipes finally let go, they can discharge significant water into wall cavities before the pressure drop is even noticeable at the fixtures.

Santa Ana wind events compound the problem in a different direction: they drive embers and debris that can damage roof coverings, and the low humidity they carry can mask early moisture readings — a wall cavity that reads borderline dry on a surface meter may still hold saturation deeper in the framing when the air outside is at 5% relative humidity. Our technicians account for ambient conditions when interpreting moisture readings, not just the meter number.

## Our Water Cleanup Process in Simi Valley

When we arrive, the first priority is stopping the source if it's still active — shutting the supply valve, documenting the point of failure, and photographing the affected area before anything is moved. From there, the process follows a structured sequence:

**Extraction** comes first. Standing water is removed with truck-mounted or portable extractors before any drying equipment is placed. Water under flooring — common in slab-on-grade homes throughout Simi Valley — requires weighted extraction tools and sometimes perforation of floor coverings to reach the moisture sitting against the concrete.

**Moisture mapping** establishes the full boundary of the loss. We use thermal imaging alongside calibrated moisture meters to trace where water has wicked into drywall, baseboards, and subfloor materials. In homes with stucco exteriors, water that enters at a window or roof-wall junction can travel several feet inside the wall cavity before it becomes visible.

**Structural drying** is set up to IICRC S500 standards — the right number of air movers and dehumidifiers for the affected square footage and material types, repositioned and monitored on each visit until readings confirm the structure has returned to normal moisture levels. We document every reading so your insurance adjuster has a complete drying log.

**Affected material decisions** — what can dry in place versus what needs to come out — are made based on material type, saturation level, and how long the water has been present. Drywall that has been wet for more than 24 to 48 hours typically cannot be restored; framing that reads within acceptable limits can usually remain.

## Local Note: Stucco Walls and Hidden Migration Paths

Simi Valley's housing stock is overwhelmingly stucco-clad wood-frame construction — a combination that looks solid but creates a specific challenge after a water intrusion. Traditional three-coat stucco is relatively vapor-impermeable, which means water that gets behind it through a failed control joint, a cracked window flashing, or a roof leak can be trapped against the sheathing and framing with nowhere to go. By the time a wet spot appears on an interior wall, the framing and sheathing behind it may have been wet for days. On these homes, we always extend our moisture mapping beyond the visible damage boundary — the actual wet zone is almost always larger than the stain suggests.
