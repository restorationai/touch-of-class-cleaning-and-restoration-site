## Why Somis Properties Are Prone to Crawl Space Moisture Problems

The Santa Clara River Valley's agricultural heritage means much of the land around Somis was irrigated for decades before residential development arrived. That history leaves behind soil with high organic content and irregular drainage patterns. When a home sits on that ground, groundwater and irrigation runoff can migrate laterally under the foundation, raising relative humidity in the crawl space well above the threshold where mold and wood rot become active concerns.

Vented crawl spaces, which are common in Ventura County homes built before the 1990s, were designed around the assumption that outside air would dry the space out. In coastal inland areas like Somis, that logic breaks down. The outside air itself often carries 70 to 85 percent relative humidity during morning marine layer hours, so venting the crawl space can actually introduce more moisture than it removes. Encapsulation seals the space from that cycle entirely.

Wood-framed floor systems in older ranch-style homes in this area are also particularly vulnerable. Joists that stay damp for extended periods soften gradually, and the damage is rarely obvious until a floor develops noticeable flex or a musty odor starts migrating into the living space.

## Our Crawl Space Encapsulation Process in Somis

Every encapsulation project starts with a full crawl space inspection: measuring humidity levels with a calibrated hygrometer, checking wood moisture content at multiple joist locations, and looking for existing mold growth, pest intrusion, or failed vapor barrier material. In Somis, we pay particular attention to the perimeter walls and any areas near irrigation lines or low spots in the yard where water tends to pool after rain.

Once we have a clear picture of the space, the process moves through these stages:

- **Debris removal and prep**: Old, deteriorated vapor barriers, insulation batts, and any organic debris are removed and bagged.
- **Mold treatment if needed**: Any active mold on joists or blocking is addressed before sealing the space. Our IICRC-certified team follows IICRC AMRT protocols for any remediation work.
- **Heavy-duty liner installation**: We install a reinforced polyethylene barrier across the floor and up the foundation walls, sealed at all seams and penetrations. The liner thickness and overlap dimensions matter here; undersized material fails at seams within a few seasons.
- **Vent sealing**: Existing foundation vents are closed and insulated, converting the space from vented to conditioned.
- **Dehumidification**: A properly sized crawl space dehumidifier is installed and set to maintain relative humidity below 55 percent year-round.

The result is a space that stays stable regardless of what the morning marine layer is doing outside.

## Local Note

One thing that comes up specifically in the Somis area: many properties here are on larger parcels with mature landscaping and drip irrigation systems that run close to the foundation. Drip lines that have shifted or developed slow leaks over the years often go unnoticed because the surface stays dry, but the water is moving toward the lowest point, which is frequently the crawl space perimeter. Before sealing a crawl space in this area, it is worth walking the foundation line and checking whether any irrigation emitters are pointed toward the house or sitting directly against the stem wall. Redirecting those lines before encapsulation prevents the new liner from trapping water against the foundation rather than keeping it out.
