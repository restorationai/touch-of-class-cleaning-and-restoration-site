Before you dial your insurance company after a storm, stop. The first call matters — and what you say, document, and hand over in those early hours can shape how smoothly (or slowly) your claim moves. This checklist walks you through exactly what to gather, photograph, and preserve before you report the loss. It applies whether you're dealing with a roof torn open by a Ventura County wind event, water intrusion from a backed-up storm drain, or a fallen tree that punched through your fence or siding.

## Step 1: Make the Property Safe First

Before anything else, your safety and the safety of anyone in the building takes priority over documentation.

- **Shut off electricity** to any area where water has entered. If you're not sure whether wiring is affected, treat it as if it is. Wet drywall near an outlet panel is a reason to call your utility company, not to flip breakers yourself.
- **Stop active water intrusion if you safely can.** A tarp over a damaged roof section, a rolled towel against a door threshold, or moving furniture off a wet floor are all reasonable. Do not climb onto a wet or wind-damaged roof.
- **Photograph hazards before you touch them.** If a tree limb has cracked a wall or a skylight is open to the sky, document it in its original state. Insurers sometimes dispute whether damage was pre-existing; your first photos are your strongest evidence.
- **Do not throw anything away yet.** Damaged shingles, broken glass, waterlogged insulation — all of it may be inspected by an adjuster.

Once the immediate hazard is controlled, start your documentation.

## Step 2: Build Your Documentation Before You Call

This is the part most homeowners skip, and it costs them. Adjusters work from evidence. The more organized yours is, the less room there is for a disputed estimate.

**Photograph and video everything — in this order:**

1. The exterior of the home from all four sides, including the roof if you can safely see it from the ground or a window.
2. Every point of entry where water, wind, or debris came in: broken windows, lifted flashing, cracked siding, displaced vent covers.
3. Interior rooms in sequence — shoot wide to show the full room, then close-up on stained ceilings, buckled flooring, wet walls, and damaged belongings.
4. Standing water depth (a ruler or tape measure in the frame helps).
5. Any visible mold, discoloration, or efflorescence — though in Southern California, mold can begin colonizing porous materials within 24 to 48 hours of a moisture event, so don't wait days to document.

**Write a written inventory of damaged items.** For each item, note: what it is, approximate age, approximate replacement cost, and whether you have a receipt or photo from before the storm. Appliances, furniture, electronics, and stored items in a garage or attic all count.

**Pull your policy before you call.** Know your deductible, your dwelling coverage limit, and whether you have a separate wind or named-storm deductible — these are different from your standard deductible in some California policies. Also check whether your policy covers "additional living expenses" if you need to stay elsewhere during repairs.

## Step 3: What NOT to Do Before the Adjuster Visits

A few common mistakes that can complicate your claim:

- **Don't make permanent repairs before the adjuster sees the damage.** Temporary fixes — tarping a roof, boarding a window — are expected and appropriate. Replacing the roof or repainting a water-stained ceiling before the adjuster visits removes the evidence they need to value your claim.
- **Don't sign a contract with a contractor that includes an assignment of benefits clause** until you understand what you're signing. In California, you have the right to choose your own licensed contractor. Some storm-chasing contractors ask homeowners to sign over their insurance rights at the door — this can limit your control over the repair process.
- **Don't overstate or guess at damage.** If you're not sure whether the crack in the stucco is from the storm or was there before, say so. Adjusters are trained to spot inconsistencies, and an inflated claim can trigger a more adversarial review.
- **Don't discard packaging or manuals for damaged appliances.** Model and serial numbers help establish replacement cost.

## Step 4: What to Say When You Call

When you do call your insurer to open a claim, keep the first conversation factual and brief:

1. **Date and time the damage occurred** (or the approximate window, if the storm hit overnight).
2. **Type of damage** — wind, water intrusion, falling object, or a combination.
3. **Whether the property is currently safe and habitable.**
4. **Whether you've taken any emergency protective measures** (tarping, boarding, water extraction).

Ask the representative: What is my claim number? What is my adjuster's direct contact? What is the expected timeline for an adjuster visit? What documentation do you need from me, and in what format?

Write everything down, including the name of the person you spoke with and the time of the call.

## Step 5: The Days After — What the Recovery Process Looks Like

Once your claim is open, here's the general sequence:

- **Adjuster inspection**: Typically scheduled within a few days to a couple of weeks, depending on how widespread the storm event was. After a major Ventura County wind or rain event, adjusters can be backlogged.
- **Estimate and coverage determination**: The adjuster produces an estimate. You have the right to get your own independent estimate from a licensed contractor. If there's a significant gap, you can negotiate or invoke your policy's appraisal clause.
- **Mitigation vs. reconstruction**: Insurers generally expect you to mitigate ongoing damage — meaning if water is still getting in, you need to stop it, and that cost is typically covered. Full reconstruction (new roofing, drywall, flooring) comes after the claim is settled or as part of a phased approval.
- **Supplements**: If the contractor finds additional damage once walls are opened or materials are removed, a supplement to the original estimate is submitted. This is normal and expected on storm losses.

If your damage involves water intrusion — and in Ventura's older housing stock, that often means water behind stucco, under tile, or into a raised foundation — professional moisture mapping matters. Surface readings can look dry while framing and subfloor remain saturated. A restoration contractor with moisture meters and thermal imaging can give you (and your adjuster) an accurate picture of what actually got wet.
