## What to Ask Any Water Damage Company Before You Hire Them

The right questions separate qualified restoration contractors from general handymen who own a shop vac. Before you commit, ask these four things.

**Are you an IICRC Certified Firm, and do your technicians hold WRT and ASD credentials?** The [IICRC](https://www.iicrc.org) sets the industry standard for water damage restoration. WRT covers extraction and initial drying; ASD covers the psychrometric science of structural drying. A company without these credentials is guessing at drying targets, which can leave hidden moisture that causes mold and voids your insurance claim.

**Do you document drying progress with daily moisture readings?** Your insurance adjuster needs a drying log, not a verbal assurance. Every day of the drying phase should produce a written moisture reading tied to a floor plan. If a company cannot describe this process, that is a red flag.

**Will you work directly with my insurance company?** Most reputable restoration companies handle direct insurance billing and will prepare the scope of work in a format your adjuster accepts. If a company asks for full payment upfront before contacting your insurer, proceed carefully.

**Do you handle mold remediation in-house if it develops?** In Ventura's coastal climate, secondary mold growth behind drywall is a real risk after any water loss. A company with in-house AMRT certification can catch and address it without handing your job to a subcontractor mid-claim.

## What Happens During Water Damage Restoration in Ventura?

Understanding the process helps you evaluate whether a company is doing the job correctly. A properly executed water damage restoration job follows these phases.

**Emergency extraction (0-4 hours):** Truck-mounted or portable extractors remove standing water. The faster this happens, the less water migrates into wall cavities, subfloor, and insulation. In Ventura homes with stucco exteriors and wood-frame construction, water travels fast through wall assemblies.

**Moisture mapping (day 1):** Technicians use thermal imaging cameras and pin/pinless moisture meters to map the full extent of water intrusion. This is not optional. Visible water is rarely the full picture.

**Structural drying setup (day 1-2):** LGR (Low Grain Refrigerant) dehumidifiers and high-velocity air movers are positioned according to a drying plan. The IICRC S500 standard defines target moisture levels for each material type (drywall, hardwood, concrete). Equipment runs continuously, typically for three to five days depending on the loss.

**Daily monitoring:** Moisture readings are taken and logged each day. Equipment is repositioned as drying progresses. This documentation becomes part of your insurance claim file.

**Mold assessment:** If moisture readings remain elevated after 48-72 hours, or if visible growth appears, a qualified technician assesses for secondary mold. In Ventura's coastal humidity, this step matters more than it does in drier climates.

**Clearance and documentation:** When all materials reach dry standard, the job is documented and the insurance scope is finalized. A written scope before work begins protects you from surprise costs.
