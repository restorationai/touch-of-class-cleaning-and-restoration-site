If water is actively entering your home right now, start here: shut off the water supply, cut power to any flooded rooms at the breaker box, and get people and pets out of standing water. Those three steps — in that order — protect lives and limit how far the damage spreads. Everything else on this page is about what comes next, in the hours before a restoration crew arrives or before you decide whether you need one.

## Stop the Source Before Anything Else

You cannot dry a room that is still getting wet. The first task is finding and stopping whatever is feeding the water.

**If it's a plumbing failure** — a burst pipe, a failed supply line under the sink, a broken washing machine hose — turn off the main shutoff valve. In most Southern California homes it's near the front of the house, either in a ground-level utility box at the curb or on the exterior wall near the garage. If you can't find it, the meter box at the street has a secondary shutoff.

**If it's an appliance** — dishwasher, refrigerator ice maker, water heater — pull the plug or flip the breaker for that circuit first, then look for the braided supply line behind or beneath the unit and close its individual shutoff valve.

**If it's a roof leak or window intrusion** during a storm — you can't stop rain, but you can limit the entry point. Tarps, towels, and buckets buy time. Move anything that can absorb water (rugs, furniture, cardboard boxes) out of the wet zone.

Once the source is controlled, photograph everything before you touch it. Your insurance adjuster will need documentation of the initial conditions, and photos taken before cleanup begins are far more useful than photos taken after.

## Turn Off Power to Wet Areas

Water and electricity share walls, floors, and ceilings in ways that aren't obvious from the surface. Before you wade into a flooded room, go to your breaker panel and cut power to every circuit that serves that space — outlets, overhead lights, HVAC, anything. If the panel itself is in the flooded area or you're not certain which breakers control what, don't guess. Leave the room and call your utility company or an electrician before re-entering.

This step is non-negotiable even for what looks like a small amount of water. A quarter-inch of standing water over a baseboard outlet is a shock hazard. Ventura's older neighborhoods — particularly the bungalows and ranch homes built in the 1950s and 1960s — often have wiring that wasn't designed with ground-fault protection, which makes the risk higher.

## What To Do in the First Two Hours

Once the source is stopped and the power is off, you have a short window to limit secondary damage. Here's the sequence:

1. **Remove standing water** using a wet/dry shop vac if you have one. Mops and towels work but are slow — prioritize getting water off hard floors and away from wall bases where it will wick upward into drywall.
2. **Move wet items out of the room.** Rugs, upholstered furniture, and anything sitting on a wet floor will keep feeding moisture into the subfloor. Get them outside or into a dry space. Furniture legs left sitting in water can stain and warp flooring within hours.
3. **Open windows and interior doors** if outdoor humidity is lower than indoor humidity — which on most dry Ventura days it will be. Moving air slows mold's early colonization window.
4. **Do not run central HVAC** to dry the space. Forced-air systems can pull mold spores and contaminated air through ductwork and deposit them in rooms that weren't affected. Use fans pointed out of windows instead.
5. **Lift carpet from the tack strip** along one wall if the carpet is soaked. Padding beneath carpet holds water like a sponge and will not dry on its own — it almost always needs to come out.
6. **Document as you go.** Short video walkthroughs with narration ("this wall, this corner, this baseboard") are more useful than still photos alone.

Mold can begin colonizing organic material — drywall paper, wood framing, carpet backing — within 24 to 48 hours in warm, humid conditions. Ventura's mild climate means your home rarely gets the cold dry air that would slow that clock. Acting in the first two hours matters.

## What Not To Do

Some common instincts make the situation worse:

- **Don't use a regular household vacuum.** The motor is not sealed for water and you risk electrocution or destroying the machine.
- **Don't run a dehumidifier in a room with standing water.** It won't keep up, and you're better off removing the bulk water first.
- **Don't assume the surface is the whole story.** If the leak is behind drywall, you'll often see a bubble or soft spot before you see a stain. By the time a stain appears, the insulation and framing behind it may already be saturated. Pressing on the wall or using a moisture meter (many hardware stores rent them) tells you more than looking.
- **Don't discard damaged materials before your insurance company has seen them.** Adjusters and restoration professionals need to assess the original damage. Stack damaged items in the garage or on a tarp outside rather than sending them to the curb.
- **Don't ignore a musty smell.** A faint earthy or sour odor after a water event is an early mold signal, not just a wet smell. It means microbial growth is already underway somewhere, even if you can't see it.

## When To Call a Water Damage Restoration Professional

Some water events are genuinely manageable with a shop vac and a few fans. A small, clean-water drip caught within an hour, on a tile floor, with no wall penetration — that's a homeowner job.

Call a restoration professional when:

- The water source was a toilet, sewage line, or any drain line (this is Category 3 water — contaminated, requiring protective equipment and proper disposal)
- The affected area is larger than roughly 10 square feet, or you can't tell where the water stopped
- Water has been sitting for more than a few hours, or you don't know how long the leak was running
- You can feel soft spots in the floor, or walls feel spongy
- You smell mold or see visible growth
- The water reached any electrical panel, subpanel, or in-wall wiring
- You're in a home with older construction that may contain lead paint or asbestos in the materials being disturbed

A professional assessment includes thermal imaging and moisture meters that read through walls and floors without cutting them open — tools that tell you whether the structure is actually dry or just dry on the surface. Drywall can read dry at the surface while the lath or framing behind it stays saturated for weeks.

## The Longer Recovery: What Happens After Day One

Professional water damage restoration follows a structured drying protocol. Industrial air movers and desiccant or refrigerant dehumidifiers run continuously — usually for three to five days — while moisture readings are logged at each affected surface. Drywall, insulation, and flooring that can't be dried in place are removed and replaced. The goal is returning all structural materials to their pre-loss moisture content before any reconstruction begins.

If you're filing an insurance claim, the restoration company will typically document the loss, photograph affected materials, and communicate directly with your adjuster throughout the process. Keep your own copies of everything.

The visible repairs — new drywall, paint, flooring — come last, after the structure is confirmed dry. Finishing over wet materials is one of the most common causes of mold problems that surface months later.
