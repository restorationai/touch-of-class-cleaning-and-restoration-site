## How Much Does Water Damage Restoration Cost in Macomb, MI?

Most water damage restoration jobs in Macomb fall between $1,800 and $12,000, with the national average sitting around $3,500 for a mid-size residential loss. That range is wide because the variables are wide. A Category 1 (clean water) pipe break caught within four hours in a single carpeted bedroom is a very different job from a Category 3 (sewage-contaminated) basement flood in a finished space with drywall, carpet, and stored belongings.

Here is how cost breaks down by loss scenario:

| Loss Scenario | Typical Cost Range |
|---|---|
| Single room, clean water (Cat 1) | $1,500 - $4,000 |
| Single room, gray water (Cat 2) | $2,500 - $6,000 |
| Finished basement, clean water | $4,000 - $9,000 |
| Finished basement, gray or black water | $7,000 - $18,000 |
| Whole first floor, clean water | $6,000 - $14,000 |
| Whole first floor, gray or black water | $10,000 - $25,000+ |
| Crawl space flooding | $1,500 - $5,000 |
| Appliance leak (dishwasher, washing machine) | $1,000 - $3,500 |

These are industry-typical ranges for Southeast Michigan. Labor rates, material costs, and disposal fees in Macomb County are broadly in line with the metro Detroit market.

## What Does Water Damage Restoration Cost Per Square Foot?

Per-square-foot pricing gives you a quick sanity check against a contractor's estimate. Mitigation alone (extraction, drying, antimicrobial treatment) typically runs $3.75 to $7.00 per square foot for clean water losses. Add reconstruction and that number climbs to $8 to $20 per square foot depending on the finish level.

| Water Category | Mitigation Only (per sq ft) | Mitigation + Basic Reconstruction (per sq ft) |
|---|---|---|
| Category 1 (clean) | $3.75 - $5.50 | $8 - $14 |
| Category 2 (gray) | $5.00 - $7.50 | $10 - $18 |
| Category 3 (black/sewage) | $7.00 - $12.00 | $14 - $25+ |

A 400-square-foot finished basement flooded by a backed-up drain (Category 3) could realistically hit $4,800 to $10,000 in mitigation alone before a single sheet of drywall goes back up. That math surprises a lot of homeowners who expected a simpler number.

For the technical definitions behind these categories, the [IICRC S500 Standard for Professional Water Damage Restoration](https://www.iicrc.org/page/IICRCS500) is the industry reference that adjusters and contractors both use.

## What Line Items Will My Insurance Adjuster Expect to See?

A properly documented water damage claim breaks the work into distinct phases. Each phase is a separate line item on the scope of work. Knowing these terms helps you read an estimate and understand what you're being charged for.

**1. Emergency water extraction**
Truck-mounted or portable extractors pull standing water from floors, carpet, and subfloor. Typical cost: $500 to $2,000 depending on volume and access. This is the first thing that happens and the most time-sensitive, every hour of delay allows water to migrate further and saturate more material.

**2. Structural drying**
Commercial-grade LGR (low-grain refrigerant) dehumidifiers and high-velocity air movers run continuously, typically for three to five days, until moisture readings return to pre-loss baselines. Equipment rental and monitoring typically adds $800 to $2,500 to a job. Drying logs (daily moisture meter readings) are required documentation for most insurance claims.

**3. Controlled demolition (tear-out)**
Wet drywall, insulation, baseboards, and flooring that cannot be dried in place must come out. Leaving wet material behind is how mold starts, [the EPA notes that mold can begin growing within 24 to 48 hours](https://www.epa.gov/mold) of a water event. Demolition costs vary widely: $500 for a single wet wall cavity, $3,000 to $8,000 for a finished basement gut.

**4. Antimicrobial treatment**
After demolition and drying, affected framing, concrete, and subfloor surfaces are treated with EPA-registered antimicrobial agents. This step is required by most insurers and by the IICRC S500 protocol for Category 2 and Category 3 losses. Typical cost: $200 to $800.

**5. Content handling**
Moving, inventorying, and storing furniture, electronics, and personal belongings during the drying phase. Some items go to a pack-out facility for drying and cleaning. Content handling can add $500 to $3,000 on a larger loss.

**6. Reconstruction**
Once the structure is dry and cleared, reconstruction begins: new drywall, insulation, flooring, paint, trim, cabinetry. This is the most variable phase. A single bedroom might need $1,500 in drywall and paint. A finished basement could need $15,000 in flooring, drywall, electrical, and finish work.

## What Pushes a Water Damage Quote Up or Down?

Several factors move the final number significantly. Understanding them helps you evaluate a scope of work before you sign it.

**Water category.** This is the single biggest cost driver. Category 1 (clean supply-line water) is the least expensive to remediate. Category 2 (gray water from appliances, washing machines, or toilet overflow without solids) requires more protective measures and more aggressive antimicrobial treatment. Category 3 (black water, sewage backup, floodwater, or any water that has sat long enough to become contaminated) is the most expensive because all porous materials in contact must be removed, not just dried.

**Response time.** Water migrates fast. A pipe that runs for six hours saturates subfloor, wicks up drywall, and reaches framing. The same pipe caught in one hour might affect only the surface layer of flooring. Every hour of delay expands the affected area and the cost. This is why extraction speed matters more than almost anything else.

**Materials affected.** Concrete and tile are relatively easy to dry. Hardwood flooring is expensive to dry (specialty drying mats, longer timelines) and often needs replacement anyway. Spray foam insulation traps moisture and must be cut out. Finished basements with drywall, carpet, and drop ceilings cost far more to restore than unfinished spaces with exposed block walls.

**Class of loss.** The IICRC S500 defines four classes of water intrusion based on how much material is wet and how difficult it is to dry. Class 1 (minimal absorption) dries quickly. Class 4 (specialty drying situations, hardwood, plaster, concrete) requires extended drying times and specialty equipment, which adds cost.

**Mold presence.** If the water event went undetected for more than 48 to 72 hours, mold remediation may be required in addition to water mitigation. Mold remediation is a separate scope of work and a separate cost. For context on the timeline, see [How Quickly Does Mold Grow After Water Damage?](/blog/mold-after-water-damage/)

**Reconstruction finish level.** Replacing drywall with basic paint is one cost. Replacing tile, cabinetry, hardwood, or custom trim is another. Reconstruction costs track the finish level of the original space.

**Permit requirements.** Some reconstruction work in Macomb Township and surrounding Macomb County municipalities requires permits, especially for electrical or structural work. Permit fees and inspection timelines add cost and schedule.

## What Does the Restoration Process Actually Look Like?

Knowing the phases helps you understand why the bill is what it is.

1. **Initial assessment.** A technician inspects the loss, maps the moisture with a calibrated meter, and classifies the water category and loss class. This determines the scope.
2. **Extraction.** Standing water is removed with truck-mounted or portable extractors. Wet carpet and pad are typically pulled at this stage.
3. **Demolition.** Unsalvageable materials (wet drywall below the flood line, saturated insulation) are removed and bagged for disposal.
4. **Drying setup.** LGR dehumidifiers and air movers are placed according to a drying plan. Equipment is monitored daily and adjusted as readings change.
5. **Antimicrobial application.** Treated surfaces are documented with photos and product application records.
6. **Final moisture verification.** When readings reach pre-loss baselines across all affected materials, drying is complete and equipment is removed.
7. **Reconstruction.** Framing, drywall, insulation, flooring, and finishes are restored to pre-loss condition.
