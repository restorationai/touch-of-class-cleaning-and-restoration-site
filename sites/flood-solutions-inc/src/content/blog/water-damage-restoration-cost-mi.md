**Quick answer:** In Michigan, most water damage restoration jobs run **$1,200 to $8,000**, with small single-room clean-water jobs at the low end and multi-room or sewage-contaminated losses at the high end. Whole-house structural drying with reconstruction can exceed $20,000. The final number depends on how much water intruded, how long it sat, and what it touched.

If you're standing in a wet basement in Macomb or Clinton Township right now trying to figure out whether to call a plumber, your insurance agent, or a restoration company first, the cost question is usually the second one you ask, right after "how bad is this." Southeast Michigan's freeze-thaw winters and heavy clay soil make basement seepage and sump pump failures common here, and the price tag depends heavily on which type of water event you're dealing with.

## What Does Water Damage Restoration Cost in Michigan?

Most Michigan homeowners pay between $1,200 and $8,000 for a typical residential water damage job, with sewage backups and whole-house losses running higher. Here's how that breaks down by scenario:

| Scenario | Typical Cost Range |
|---|---|
| Water extraction only, small area, Cat 1 clean water | $200 - $600 |
| Single room, clean water, drywall + flooring affected | $1,200 - $3,500 |
| Multi-room loss, Cat 2 gray water (washing machine, dishwasher overflow) | $3,500 - $7,500 |
| Basement flooding from sump pump failure or seepage | $3,000 - $8,500 |
| Category 3 black water / sewage backup | $5,000 - $12,000+ |
| Whole-house structural drying with reconstruction | $10,000 - $30,000+ |

## What Factors Change the Price?

The biggest cost driver is the category and class of water involved, followed closely by how fast the water gets addressed. Clean water from a supply line costs far less to remediate than gray or black water, because contaminated water requires more demolition, more disinfection, and stricter disposal of porous materials.

A few specifics that move the number:

- **Category of water.** Category 1 (clean, from a supply line) is the cheapest to treat. Category 2 (gray water, from appliances) requires antimicrobial treatment. Category 3, also called black water, includes sewage backups and requires removal of most porous materials the water touched, per the [IICRC S500](https://www.iicrc.org) standard.
- **Square footage and how far the water traveled.** Water that wicked under baseboards into an adjacent room adds drying zones and equipment.
- **Response time.** Water sitting more than 24-48 hours starts to warp wood flooring and raises the risk of secondary mold growth, which adds remediation cost on top of drying.
- **Materials affected.** Carpet and pad are cheaper to replace than hardwood flooring or built-in cabinetry, which may need controlled drying instead of removal.
- **Equipment needed.** A single room might need two air movers and a dehumidifier for three days. A basement flood can require a dozen air movers, a commercial dehumidifier, and a week of monitoring.
- **Code upgrades.** If drywall or insulation is removed down to the studs, some jurisdictions require bringing that section up to current code, which adds modest cost.

## Will Homeowners Insurance Cover the Cost?

Most Michigan homeowners policies cover sudden and accidental water damage, like a burst supply line or a failed water heater, but they typically exclude gradual leaks and exclude flooding from rising groundwater or overflowing rivers. That last exclusion matters in Macomb County, where basement seepage tied to heavy spring rain or the Clinton River watershed is common and usually falls under a separate flood policy, not your standard homeowners policy.

A few things that affect your claim:

- **Deductibles** on Michigan homeowners policies commonly range from $500 to $2,000, which comes off the top of any covered claim.
- **Sump pump failure** is sometimes covered only if you purchased a specific sump pump or water backup endorsement, so check your policy language before assuming it's included.
- **Sewage backups** from a municipal line or your own plumbing usually need a separate sewer backup rider.
- **Documentation matters.** Photos of standing water, moisture readings, and an itemized list of affected contents all strengthen a claim. Flood Solutions inc documents moisture levels and drying progress throughout the job, which gives your adjuster the paper trail they need.

## What Happens During a Water Damage Restoration Job?

A typical restoration job runs through four phases: assessment, extraction, drying, and repair. Here's what each one actually involves.

1. **Assessment.** A technician inspects the affected area, identifies the water category and class of loss, and takes baseline moisture readings on walls, subfloor, and framing.
2. **Extraction.** Standing water gets pulled out with truck-mounted or portable extraction equipment. This is the fastest way to stop water from wicking further into materials.
3. **Drying and dehumidification.** Air movers and industrial dehumidifiers get positioned to bring moisture content in the structure back to a normal range, typically monitored daily with a moisture meter until readings stabilize. This phase usually runs three to five days for a single room, longer for larger losses.
4. **Cleaning, disinfection, and repair.** Category 2 and 3 water require antimicrobial treatment of affected surfaces. Once everything reads dry, drywall, flooring, and trim get repaired or replaced.

For sewage backups specifically, disinfection is a bigger part of the job than drying, since contaminated water needs to be treated and, in many cases, porous materials it touched need to be removed rather than dried in place. ## What Should You Do Next?

If water is actively coming in, shut off the source if you can safely reach it, then call for an assessment rather than waiting to see if it dries on its own. Standing water and wet drywall left untreated for more than a couple of days raise both the mold risk and the eventual repair bill.
