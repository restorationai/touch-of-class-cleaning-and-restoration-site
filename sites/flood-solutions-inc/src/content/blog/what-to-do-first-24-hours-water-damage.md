Stop what you're doing and move through these steps in order: shut off the water source, cut power to flooded rooms, get people and pets out of standing water, then document everything with your phone before touching a single wet item. The first 24 hours after water damage are the window that separates a manageable repair from a gut-renovation, mold can begin colonizing wet drywall and insulation within 24 to 48 hours, and saturated hardwood starts cupping in even less time. Every hour you wait, the damage spreads.

## Step One: Stop the Water and Make the Space Safe

Before anything else, find the source.

- **Burst pipe or appliance leak?** Turn off the main shutoff valve. In most Macomb-area homes, it's near the water meter, often in the basement, crawl space, or utility room. If you can't locate it, your municipality's emergency line can walk you through it.
- **Roof or window intrusion?** You can't stop rain, but you can move valuables out of the path and place buckets or plastic sheeting to slow secondary spread.
- **Sewage backup?** Don't touch the water without rubber gloves and boots. Blackwater carries pathogens and requires a different cleanup protocol than a clean-water pipe burst.

Once the water source is controlled, go to your electrical panel and shut off the breaker for any room with standing water. Water and live outlets are a lethal combination, and this step is non-negotiable before you wade in to start moving furniture.

## Step Two: Document Before You Touch Anything

Your insurance claim lives or dies on documentation. Spend 10 minutes, even if the situation feels urgent, photographing and videoing everything:

1. Walk the perimeter of the affected area and record continuously before picking anything up.
2. Get close-up shots of the water source (the cracked pipe, the failed supply line under the sink, the seam where the roof leaked).
3. Photograph water lines on walls, saturated flooring, and any furniture or belongings sitting in water.
4. Note the time and date in your video narration, this establishes the timeline for your adjuster.

Do not throw anything away yet, even ruined items. Adjusters need to see the loss in place or have a documented inventory before disposal. Moving things is fine; discarding them before an adjuster or restoration crew has assessed them can complicate your claim.

## Step Three: Start Water Removal, Carefully

If you have a wet/dry shop vac, use it. If you have a mop and buckets, use those. Removing standing water immediately reduces how deeply moisture penetrates flooring and wall assemblies. Here's what to prioritize:

1. **Extract standing water first.** Even an inch of water on a hardwood floor can push into the tongue-and-groove seams within hours.
2. **Pull up saturated area rugs.** Rugs trap moisture against the subfloor and accelerate mold growth underneath. Get them outside or into a garage.
3. **Move wet furniture off flooring.** Place aluminum foil or plastic bags under furniture legs if you can't move pieces entirely, this prevents rust and dye stains from transferring to the floor.
4. **Open windows if outdoor humidity is lower than indoor humidity.** In Michigan summers, this is often not the case, outdoor air can be just as humid. Use your judgment; if it's a dry, cool day, ventilation helps.
5. **Run dehumidifiers and fans if you have them.** Point box fans across wet surfaces, not directly at walls, you want air movement across the floor plane.

What you're doing here is slowing the spread. You are almost certainly not drying the structure, the moisture that has already wicked into subfloor, wall cavities, and insulation requires professional-grade equipment to extract.

## What NOT to Do in the First 24 Hours

Some common instincts make water damage significantly worse:

- **Don't use a household vacuum on standing water.** Standard vacuums are not rated for liquid and can electrocute you or destroy the motor.
- **Don't run your HVAC system.** Forced air through a wet duct system can spread mold spores throughout the house before any remediation happens.
- **Don't assume the floor is dry because the surface feels dry.** Water behind plaster or drywall can read completely dry at the surface while the lath, framing, or subfloor beneath stays saturated for days. This is one of the most common reasons mold appears weeks after a loss that seemed "handled."
- **Don't peel back wet drywall yourself** unless you have confirmed there's no asbestos or lead paint risk. Macomb County has a significant stock of homes built before 1980, and disturbing older drywall compounds or joint tape without testing can create a separate hazardous-materials problem.
- **Don't sign anything from a restoration company that shows up uninvited** before you've contacted your insurance carrier. Assignment-of-benefits solicitation happens after major weather events; know what you're signing.

## When the Damage Is Beyond a DIY Response

There's a clear line between mopping up a slow toilet supply-line leak and managing structural water intrusion. Call a professional water damage restoration company when:

- The water source was a sewage line, a sump pump failure, or outdoor flooding (any of these may involve contaminated water).
- Water has been standing for more than a few hours, or you discovered the leak after the fact, a stain on the ceiling, a smell, a soft spot in the floor.
- The affected area is larger than roughly one room, or water has reached wall cavities, insulation, or a crawl space.
- You can smell a musty or earthy odor, that smell is microbial activity that's already started.
- Your flooring is hardwood, engineered wood, or laminate over a wood subfloor, these materials require controlled drying conditions that fans and dehumidifiers alone can't achieve.

Professional restoration crews use thermal imaging cameras to find moisture behind walls without opening them, industrial desiccant or refrigerant dehumidifiers that pull dramatically more moisture per hour than consumer units, and moisture meters to verify when structural materials have actually reached safe drying thresholds, not just when they feel dry.

## The Longer Recovery: What Comes After the First Day

Once the immediate crisis is stabilized, the work shifts from emergency response to structured drying and assessment. A professional crew will typically monitor moisture readings every 24 hours, adjusting equipment placement until the structure reaches equilibrium. This process usually takes several days, not hours, and skipping or shortening it is the most common reason homeowners end up with mold problems a month after a water loss.

After drying is verified, the reconstruction phase begins: replacing drywall, flooring, insulation, and any cabinetry or trim that couldn't be salvaged. Your insurance adjuster will typically want a scope of work from the restoration company before that phase starts, so keeping communication open between the contractor and your carrier matters throughout.

Keep a written log of every call, every visit, and every piece of equipment placed in your home. Dates, times, and names. This protects you if there's ever a dispute about what was done and when.
