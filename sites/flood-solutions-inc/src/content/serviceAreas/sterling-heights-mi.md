## Restoration emergencies common in Sterling Heights

Sterling Heights sits in Macomb County at the intersection of two patterns that drive restoration work: Michigan's freeze-thaw cycle and a housing stock built largely during the postwar suburban boom of the 1950s through the 1980s. When temperatures swing between single digits and the mid-40s in a single week, which happens regularly between December and March, water finds every gap in a foundation wall, every crack in a chimney crown, every ice dam that has been building since the last snowfall. The city's flat topography and heavy clay soils mean that spring snowmelt and heavy rain have nowhere to drain quickly, and sump pumps work overtime. When a pump fails or a drain line backs up, finished basements fill fast. Summer brings its own pressure: humidity in the high 80s can push attic moisture levels high enough to start mold colonization within 24 to 48 hours of a roof leak going unnoticed.

## Services we provide in Sterling Heights

### Water damage restoration

Water damage in Sterling Heights most often starts below grade. A backed-up floor drain, a failed sump, or a slow seep through a poured-concrete or block foundation wall can saturate carpet padding, drywall, and wood framing before the smell gives it away. Flood Solutions uses moisture mapping and thermal imaging to locate water that has migrated behind walls or under flooring, because in a 1960s ranch with a finished basement, surface readings rarely tell the whole story.

### Fire and smoke damage restoration

House fires in older Sterling Heights neighborhoods leave smoke residue that works into plaster, ductwork, and attic insulation. Odor that seems to clear in the first week often returns when temperatures rise and the structure off-gasses again. Restoration here means cleaning and sealing structural surfaces, not just replacing what burned.

### Mold remediation

The combination of clay soil, basement construction, and humid summers makes mold a recurring issue in Sterling Heights homes. Mold can establish behind finished basement walls or in crawlspace framing well before it becomes visible. Remediation scope is driven by what the investigation finds, not a flat-rate assumption.

### Storm and wind damage

Macomb County storms regularly produce wind events that lift shingles, crack ridge caps, and push water into attic cavities. When a roof is compromised mid-storm, emergency tarping and interior drying have to happen in sequence to prevent a roofing claim from becoming a mold claim.

### Reconstruction handoff

When water, fire, or mold remediation requires removing drywall, flooring, or structural framing, Flood Solutions documents the cleared scope and coordinates with your insurance carrier's adjuster so the documented scope matches the repair plan your reconstruction contractor works from.

## Building stock, site conditions, and permits in Sterling Heights

Most of Sterling Heights was developed between the late 1950s and the mid-1980s, which means the dominant housing stock is ranch and split-level construction on full basements, poured concrete or concrete block foundations, with some older block walls that are prone to lateral cracking under clay soil pressure. Homes from this era were plumbed with copper supply lines and cast-iron drain stacks; galvanized supply lines appear in the oldest structures and corrode from the inside out. Any scope involving pre-1980 materials should account for the possibility of asbestos-containing floor tile, pipe insulation, or drywall joint compound, and lead paint in pre-1978 homes. The heavy clay soils throughout Macomb County expand when wet and contract in drought, putting consistent lateral pressure on basement walls. Structural or drywall permits for restoration work are issued through the City of Sterling Heights Building Department, and work that touches structural framing, electrical, or mechanical systems typically requires a permit and inspection before close-in.
