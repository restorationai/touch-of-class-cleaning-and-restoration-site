## Restoration emergencies common in Clinton Township

Macomb County sits on a clay-heavy glacial plain, and Clinton Township's drainage reflects that. When spring thaw arrives or a summer thunderstorm drops two inches in an hour, that clay doesn't absorb, it sheds. Sump pumps run hard, window wells fill, and basement floor drains back up before a homeowner realizes what's happening. The township's housing stock is largely post-war through 1980s construction, meaning many basements are finished living space: drywall, carpet, drop ceilings, and mechanicals that absorb water quickly and hide moisture longer than an open concrete room would. Winters bring a different risk: freeze-thaw cycles stress supply lines in exterior walls and uninsulated crawlspaces, and a burst pipe on a cold January night can saturate framing before the water is even noticed. Fire calls tend to cluster around older electrical panels and aging gas appliances, and the dense suburban layout means smoke and soot travel through shared attic spaces faster than owners expect.

## Services we provide in Clinton Township

### Water damage restoration

Water damage in Clinton Township most often starts below grade, a failed sump, a backed-up lateral, or a slow foundation seep that's been wicking into block walls for months. Flood Solutions uses thermal imaging and moisture meters to find what the surface doesn't show, then sets up commercial drying equipment calibrated to the actual conditions in the space rather than a one-size schedule. Finished basements require careful material assessment: some drywall and insulation can be dried in place; other assemblies have to come out to prevent mold colonization in the wall cavity.

### Mold remediation

Mold can begin colonizing porous materials within 24 to 48 hours of a moisture event, a timeline that matters in Clinton Township's humid summers. Remediation here often follows a water loss that wasn't fully dried, a slow roof leak behind drywall, or a crawlspace with chronic humidity. The scope includes containment, HEPA filtration, removal of affected materials, and treatment of the substrate before reconstruction begins.

### Fire and smoke damage restoration

Smoke residue is acidic and keeps damaging surfaces long after the flames are out. In Clinton Township's ranch-style and split-level homes, smoke travels through ductwork and into rooms that never saw fire. Restoration involves odor neutralization, soot cleaning on hard and soft surfaces, duct cleaning, and documenting any structural elements the fire compromised so your rebuild contractor can restore them.

### Reconstruction handoff

When a loss requires more than drying and cleaning, when framing, subfloor, drywall, or roofing has to be replaced, that reconstruction is handled by your builder. Flood Solutions documents the full scope of what was removed and stays the point of contact for the adjuster, so the handoff to your rebuild contractor is clean.

## Building stock, site conditions, and permits in Clinton Township

The majority of Clinton Township's residential housing was built between the late 1940s and the mid-1980s, with a second wave of construction through the 1990s. Older homes in this range commonly have copper supply lines and cast-iron drain stacks, both durable, but cast iron corrodes from the inside over decades and can fail without visible warning. Homes built before 1978 should be assumed to have lead-based paint in some assemblies; pre-1980 construction may also contain asbestos-containing materials in floor tile, pipe insulation, or textured ceilings, which affects how a restoration scope is written and what abatement steps precede dryout or demo.

Foundations in Clinton Township are predominantly poured concrete or concrete block basements. The underlying clay soil holds water against the foundation wall rather than draining away from it, which is why hydrostatic pressure and efflorescence are common findings in older basements. Structural repairs and any work that alters the building envelope typically require a permit through Clinton Township's Building Department. Restoration contractors working in the township should be prepared to pull permits for framing, electrical, and mechanical work that's part of the reconstruction scope.
