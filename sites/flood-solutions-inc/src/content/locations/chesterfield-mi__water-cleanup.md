Chesterfield Township sits at the edge of Macomb County where the clay-heavy soils and proximity to Lake St. Clair's drainage basin make standing water a recurring reality, not just a freak event. When a sump pump fails during a spring thaw or a basement drain backs up after a heavy rain, water spreads fast across finished floors and into wall cavities before most homeowners realize the full extent of the damage. Flood Solutions Inc. has been responding to exactly these situations across the Macomb County area since 1996, and the team is familiar with what Chesterfield's soil conditions and housing stock actually do to a structure when water gets in.

## Why Chesterfield Properties See Water Damage Issues

The glacial clay soils that underlie much of Chesterfield Township are notoriously slow to drain. During snowmelt in March and April, or after the heavy convective storms that roll in off Lake St. Clair in summer, that clay holds saturation close to the surface, and hydrostatic pressure builds against basement walls and floor slabs. Homes built in the 1970s and 1980s, which make up a significant portion of Chesterfield's residential neighborhoods, typically have block foundation walls that are more susceptible to lateral water intrusion than poured concrete. Cove joint seepage, where the floor meets the wall, is one of the most common entry points we see in homes of that era.

The township's extensive network of retention ponds and drainage swales, common in the subdivisions that developed rapidly through the 1990s, can also overwhelm during sustained rainfall. When those systems back up, sump basins receive more volume than a single pump can handle, and finished basements take the hit.

## Our Water Cleanup Process in Chesterfield

Water cleanup is not a single step, it is a sequence, and the sequence matters. The process begins with a thorough assessment using moisture meters and thermal imaging to map where water has traveled, including behind drywall, under flooring, and into insulation cavities. What reads dry at the surface is often still saturated an inch deeper, particularly in homes with vinyl plank or tile over a concrete slab.

Once the scope is mapped, standing water is extracted using truck-mounted and portable extraction equipment. After bulk water removal, the focus shifts to structural drying, placing commercial-grade air movers and refrigerant dehumidifiers in a calculated pattern based on the room's cubic footage and the materials involved. Drying logs are taken at regular intervals so there is a documented record of progress, which matters both for your peace of mind and for any insurance claim. Affected materials that cannot be dried in place, saturated insulation, compromised drywall, are removed and disposed of properly before the drying phase concludes.

## Chesterfield Insurance Coordination

Most standard homeowner policies in Michigan cover sudden and accidental water damage, a burst pipe, an appliance failure, an overflow, but they typically exclude gradual seepage or flood events tied to rising surface water. That distinction matters in Chesterfield, where the cause of loss is not always obvious at first glance. A sump pump failure during a storm, for example, may or may not be covered depending on whether the homeowner carries sump pump rider coverage.

Flood Solutions Inc. documents the loss thoroughly from the first visit: photographs of affected areas, moisture readings, and a written scope of work. That documentation goes directly to your adjuster and helps move the claim forward without you having to reconstruct the timeline from memory.

## Local Note

Homes in Chesterfield's subdivisions built during the late 1980s and 1990s frequently have finished basements with drop ceilings, and that ceiling system hides a significant amount of mechanical and structural space above it. Water from an upper-floor leak or a supply line failure can travel horizontally across the top of a drop ceiling grid and pool in ceiling tiles for hours before a single drop reaches the floor below. By the time a homeowner notices the staining, the subfloor above and the wall framing around it may already be well into the moisture absorption window that accelerates mold colonization. Pulling a few ceiling tiles early, before extraction equipment even arrives, is one of the first things a technician will do to understand the true spread of a loss in these homes.
