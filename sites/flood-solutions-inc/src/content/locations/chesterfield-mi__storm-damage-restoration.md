## Why Chesterfield Properties See Significant Storm Damage

Macomb County sits in a corridor that funnels lake-effect weather systems from Lake Huron and Lake St. Clair, meaning Chesterfield regularly deals with high-wind events, hail, and sudden heavy rain that other inland communities miss. The township's residential development accelerated through the 1980s and 1990s, which means a large share of the housing stock features wood-framed construction with dimensional lumber roof systems. Those roofs handle normal Michigan winters well, but they are vulnerable to the kind of straight-line winds that accompany fast-moving summer squalls. When shingles lift or a soffit panel tears away, water enters the attic decking within minutes, not hours.

The soil composition compounds the problem. Chesterfield's clay-dominant ground holds moisture rather than draining it, so saturated yards push water toward foundations long after the storm has passed. Sump pumps that ran continuously during the event often fail from overheating just as the secondary surge arrives. That combination of wind-driven roof intrusion above and hydrostatic pressure below is a pattern the Flood Solutions crew encounters repeatedly across the township.

## Our Storm Damage Restoration Process in Chesterfield

The first priority on any storm loss is stopping ongoing water intrusion before any drying or reconstruction begins. That means a thorough inspection of the roof, fascia, soffits, windows, and any penetrations where wind-driven rain may have entered. Tarping and board-up work happens the same visit when structural openings are found.

Once the building envelope is secured, the focus shifts to moisture mapping. Thermal imaging and calibrated moisture meters reveal where water traveled inside wall cavities and subfloor assemblies, not just where it pooled visibly. Chesterfield homes built in the late 1980s and 1990s frequently used OSB sheathing, which swells and delaminates faster than older plywood when it stays wet, so identifying saturation early matters.

Controlled drying follows, using industrial air movers and refrigerant dehumidifiers sized to the affected square footage. Readings are logged daily so the drying curve is documented for both the homeowner and the insurance carrier. Once materials reach target moisture content, reconstruction can begin, whether that means replacing a section of roof decking, rehanging drywall, or repairing a damaged soffit line.
