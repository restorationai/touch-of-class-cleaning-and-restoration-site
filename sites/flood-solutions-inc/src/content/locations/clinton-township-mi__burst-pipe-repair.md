## Why Clinton Township Homes Are Particularly Vulnerable to Pipe Failures

Much of Clinton Township's residential stock was built during the postwar suburban expansion of the 1950s through the 1970s. Those homes frequently have galvanized steel or early copper plumbing that has been in service for fifty or sixty years. Galvanized pipe corrodes from the inside out, narrowing the interior diameter over time, which makes it more brittle under pressure spikes, and more likely to crack when temperatures drop hard overnight. The township's flat terrain also means that when a water line breaks, there's no natural grade to carry water away from the structure. It pools, it wicks into slab edges, and it finds its way under flooring before a homeowner realizes anything is wrong.

The soil composition across much of Macomb County runs toward clay-heavy glacial till. Clay holds moisture and expands when it freezes, which puts lateral pressure on buried service lines as well as on foundation walls. A water line break that starts outside the home can migrate inward along that pressure path, and the first sign inside might be a damp carpet corner or a musty smell near a baseboard, not a dramatic flood.

## Our Burst Pipe Cleanup Process in Clinton Township

The first priority when we arrive is stopping the source. If the homeowner hasn't already located the main shutoff, we do that before anything else. From there, the process follows a clear sequence calibrated to what we actually find on site.

**Extraction and containment.** Standing water comes out with truck-mounted or portable extraction equipment. We work from the perimeter inward to avoid spreading contamination, and we check adjacent rooms and cavities, because water travels horizontally under flooring long before it surfaces.

**Moisture mapping.** Thermal imaging cameras and calibrated moisture meters let us read what's happening behind drywall, under hardwood, and inside wall cavities without tearing into surfaces we don't need to open. In Clinton Township's older homes, this step matters especially: plaster-and-lath construction, common in homes built before the mid-1960s, holds moisture differently than modern drywall. The surface can feel dry while the lath behind it stays saturated for days.

**Structural drying.** Commercial-grade desiccant or refrigerant dehumidifiers and high-velocity air movers run in a configuration matched to the room volume and material types. We monitor readings daily and adjust equipment placement as materials dry at different rates.

**Documentation for your claim.** Every affected area is photographed, measured, and logged. We produce the documentation your insurance adjuster needs to process a pipe break water damage claim, and we communicate directly with carriers to keep the process moving.

## Local Note: What the Township's Slab-on-Grade Homes Add to the Equation

A meaningful share of Clinton Township's ranch homes from the 1960s and early 1970s were built on slab-on-grade foundations rather than basements. When a supply line or in-slab drain line fails in one of these homes, water has nowhere to go except outward through the slab surface or along the vapor barrier beneath the flooring. Detection is harder because there's no basement ceiling to show staining, and the first visible sign is often a buckled vinyl floor or a soft spot in laminate. If you're in a slab-on-grade home and you notice unexplained increases in your water bill alongside any floor irregularity, that combination warrants a call before you have a visible flood on your hands.
