## Why Clinton Township Homes Hold Smoke Differently

A large portion of Clinton Township's residential housing stock was built between the 1950s and the 1980s, a period when builders used wood-paneled interiors, plaster-and-lath walls, and natural-fiber insulation products that are particularly absorbent. Smoke particles are oily and acidic, and they bond aggressively to these older materials in ways that water-based cleaning alone cannot reverse. Homes in this era also tend to have original ductwork with gaps and joints that were never sealed to modern standards, which means smoke from a kitchen fire can deposit residue in every room within minutes.

Macomb County's climate adds another layer. The region's cold winters mean homes are kept tightly sealed for months at a time, which traps smoke odor compounds inside the building envelope. A fire that happens in January or February will have its odors locked in by storm windows and weatherstripping, concentrating the problem rather than allowing any natural ventilation to help. That seasonal factor affects how aggressively deodorization needs to be approached.

## Our Smoke Damage Restoration Process in Clinton Township

Smoke restoration is not a single step. The process moves in a deliberate sequence because skipping ahead creates problems that surface weeks later.

First, the affected structure is assessed room by room. Smoke behaves differently depending on what burned (synthetic materials leave a wet, sticky residue; natural wood fires produce a dry, powdery char soot), and identifying the residue type determines which cleaning agents and methods will work without damaging the underlying surface.

Next, porous materials that cannot be effectively cleaned are documented and removed. This includes smoke-saturated insulation, charred framing, and drywall that has absorbed odor compounds past the point of surface treatment. Structural materials that can be saved go through a dry-soot vacuuming process before any wet cleaning begins, because applying liquid to dry smoke residue without vacuuming first spreads the contamination rather than removing it.

Odor control follows mechanical cleaning. Thermal fogging and hydroxyl generation are used to reach the spaces that cleaning cloths cannot, including wall cavities, subfloor voids, and ductwork interiors. The goal is neutralizing the odor compounds at the molecular level, not masking them with fragrance.

Finally, the HVAC system is inspected and cleaned. In Clinton Township's older homes, this step is frequently where a restoration is made or undone. A duct system that ran during or after the fire has distributed smoke throughout the house, and leaving it unaddressed means the odor returns every time the furnace kicks on.
