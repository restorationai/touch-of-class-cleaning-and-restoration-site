## Why Warren Properties See Hidden Leak Problems

Much of Warren's residential housing stock was built between the late 1940s and the early 1970s, a period when copper supply lines and cast-iron drain stacks were standard. Copper holds up well, but older fittings and soldered joints corrode over time, especially where the soil stays damp. Warren's clay-dominant soil drains slowly, which means ground moisture stays in contact with foundation walls and slab penetrations longer than it would in sandier terrain. That sustained contact accelerates corrosion at any point where a pipe transitions from underground to inside the structure.

Slab construction is common in this part of Macomb County, and slab leaks are among the most expensive problems to ignore. Water migrating under a concrete slab can travel several feet laterally before surfacing, which means the wet spot on your floor may be nowhere near the actual break. Homes built on slabs in Warren also tend to have minimal crawl space access, so visual inspection alone rarely locates the source.

## Our Leak Detection Process in Warren

Finding a hidden leak without unnecessary demolition requires layered diagnostic tools. The process typically starts with a pressure test on the supply lines to confirm a leak exists and to isolate it to a cold or hot water circuit. From there, thermal imaging cameras scan walls, ceilings, and floor surfaces for temperature differentials that indicate moisture migration. Acoustic listening equipment picks up the sound signature of pressurized water escaping a pipe, even through several inches of concrete or plaster.

In Warren's older brick ranches, interior plaster walls complicate thermal reads because plaster holds temperature differently than modern drywall. The crew adjusts baseline readings accordingly and cross-references acoustic data before marking a location. That extra step matters: a false positive on a slab leak means unnecessary concrete cutting, and nobody wants that.

Once the leak is pinpointed, the findings are documented with photographs and a written scope so that your plumber, your contractor, and your insurance adjuster are all working from the same information.

Warren's clay soil creates a specific pattern worth knowing: after a wet spring or a heavy rain event, hydrostatic pressure against basement walls can force water through hairline cracks in block or poured concrete foundations.
