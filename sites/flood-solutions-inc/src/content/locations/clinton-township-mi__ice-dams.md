Ice dams in Clinton Township rarely announce themselves until a ceiling stain shows up over a bedroom closet or water starts dripping behind a soffit during a January thaw. With Macomb County sitting close enough to Lake St. Clair to catch lake-effect snow bands on top of standard Michigan winter systems, roofs here go through more freeze-thaw cycling than homeowners expect, and that cycling is exactly what builds the ice ridge that backs meltwater under shingles.

## Why Clinton Township Roofs Are Prone to Ice Dams

A lot of the township's housing stock, especially the ranches and split-levels built through the 1960s and 70s, was framed with minimal attic insulation by today's standards and shallow soffit-to-ridge ventilation paths. When warm attic air escapes upward and melts snow on the upper roof deck, that water runs down to the cold eave overhang and refreezes, building a dam that forces the next melt cycle sideways under the shingles instead of into the gutter. Clinton River drainage patterns and the generally flat grading across the township also mean gutters and downspouts already carry heavy spring melt loads, so an ice-blocked gutter system backs up fast once temperatures swing above freezing during the day and drop back down at night, which is the normal pattern for a Macomb County January and February.

## Our Ice Dam Process in Clinton Township

We start by identifying where the melt is actually entering, which is often several feet from where the stain shows up on a ceiling, since water travels along rafters and sheathing before it drips. Steam and low-impact ice removal clears the dam itself without granular rock salt or chipping methods that damage shingles, and we check attic insulation depth and bath fan or kitchen exhaust venting while we're up there, since those are the two most common sources of the warm air that starts the melt cycle in the first place. If water has already tracked into drywall, insulation, or framing, we handle extraction and structural drying the same way we would any water intrusion, using moisture meters to map how far it traveled rather than assuming the damage stops at the visible stain. Roof and attic work gets photographed at each stage for the insurance file, since ice dam claims are judged heavily on documentation of the water path.

## Reaching Clinton Township from Macomb

Our crews are based in Macomb, a short run down Gratiot or Hall Road into the township, so we're not routing equipment across the county for most calls here. That matters during a hard freeze-thaw stretch when several households along the same street can develop dams within a day or two of each other, since the same storm system and the same roof age tend to produce the same failure points across a neighborhood.

## Insurance Coordination for Ice Dam Claims

Most carriers treat ice dam water intrusion as a sudden and accidental loss rather than gradual damage, but only if the interior water damage is documented quickly and tied clearly to the ice buildup rather than a long-standing roof leak. We photograph the ice formation itself where it's still visible, document moisture readings inside the affected rooms, and provide a written scope that separates the roof and gutter repair from the interior drying and reconstruction, since those often fall under different line items or even different adjusters on the same claim.

## Local note

Homes with attached garages built under living space, common in a lot of Clinton Township's mid-century construction, tend to develop a second, smaller ice dam right above the garage ceiling line, because that section of roof sits over unconditioned space and cools faster than the rest of the attic. Homeowners often fix the main roof dam and miss this one entirely, so we check that transition point specifically rather than assuming the whole roofline is behaved the same way.
