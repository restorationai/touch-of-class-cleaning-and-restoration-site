## Our Leak Detection Process in Rochester Hills

Finding a hidden water leak without tearing out walls or jackhammering a slab requires layering multiple detection methods, because no single tool catches every leak type.

The process typically begins with a pressure isolation test: shutting off fixtures and appliances and watching whether the supply-side pressure holds. A steady pressure drop confirms active loss somewhere in the system. From there, acoustic listening equipment goes to work. Leak noise travels through pipe walls and into surrounding material, and trained ears with amplified sensors can triangulate a source to within a few inches even through a concrete slab or behind finished drywall.

When acoustic signals are ambiguous, thermal imaging adds a second data layer. A pressurized water leak changes the temperature of surrounding material, and an infrared camera renders that temperature difference as a visible pattern. In Rochester Hills homes with poured concrete basements, thermal imaging is particularly useful because concrete holds temperature gradients longer than wood framing, making the anomaly easier to read.

For suspected slab leaks, tracer gas detection is the most precise option. An inert, non-toxic gas is introduced into the line, and a surface sensor detects where it escapes through the slab. This method pinpoints the breach without any exploratory demolition, which matters when the leak is under finished flooring or a tiled bathroom.
