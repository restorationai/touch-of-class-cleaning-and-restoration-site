## Why Rochester Hills Properties See Environmental Consultants Issues

Oakland County's clay-heavy soil doesn't drain quickly, which means basements across Rochester Hills hold groundwater pressure longer after a heavy spring thaw or summer storm than homes built on sandier ground. That slow drainage is exactly the condition that lets moisture sit against foundation walls and framing long enough for mold colonies to establish, sometimes inside wall cavities where nobody notices until a musty smell shows up weeks later. Homes built before the 1990s are also more likely to carry asbestos-containing materials in floor tile, pipe insulation, or old duct wrap, which changes how a consultant has to approach any demolition or remediation scope. Michigan's freeze-thaw cycle adds another layer: repeated swelling and cracking in foundation walls opens new pathways for water intrusion year after year, so a property that tested clean two winters ago isn't guaranteed to be clean now.

Basements in the older sections of Rochester Hills, particularly homes built in the 1960s and 70s before modern vapor barrier requirements, often show moisture readings that look fine at the surface of a concrete floor while the soil directly beneath stays saturated for days after a storm.
