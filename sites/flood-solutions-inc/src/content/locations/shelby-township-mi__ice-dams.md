Shelby Township's mix of 1970s and '80s ranch and split-level homes, many with low-pitch roof lines and shallow overhangs, make an ideal setup for ice dams once Macomb County settles into its typical late-December freeze-thaw pattern. Warm attic air meets a roof deck cooled by lake-effect snow off nearby Lake St. Clair, and the melt-refreeze cycle backs water up under shingles faster than most homeowners expect. By the time a ceiling stain shows up inside, water has usually already worked past the roofline and into insulation, drywall, or framing.

## Why Shelby Township Homes See Ice Dam Problems

A lot of the township's housing stock predates today's attic ventilation standards, which means bath fans, can lights, and undersized soffit vents leak just enough heat into the attic to melt snow unevenly. That meltwater runs down to the colder eave overhang, where it refreezes into a dam and pushes standing water back under the shingles. Township lots with mature tree cover tend to hold snow load longer into the season, which extends the freeze-thaw window and gives water more chances to find a gap in the roof deck. Homes near open farmland-turned-subdivision areas also catch more wind-driven snow buildup along north-facing roof slopes, which is usually where we find the worst damage.

## Our Ice Dam Process in This Climate

We start outside, assessing the dam itself, the gutter system, and where water is actively intruding before touching anything indoors. Steam and low-pressure methods are used to clear ice without cracking shingles that are already brittle from the cold, which matters in a Michigan winter when re-freezing overnight can undo careless work fast. Once the roof is clear, we move inside to check insulation, drywall, and framing along the affected eave line, because ice dam water tends to travel sideways under the roof deck before it ever shows up as a ceiling stain. Drying equipment is placed based on moisture readings, not guesswork, and we document every reading so the scope of loss is clear if a claim is filed. For older Shelby Township homes with plaster-over-lath ceilings, drying takes longer than drywall construction because the plaster holds moisture and releases it slowly, so we adjust drying timelines and equipment placement accordingly rather than pulling equipment on a fixed schedule.

## Insurance Coordination for Ice Dam Damage

Most homeowner policies in Michigan cover sudden water damage from ice dams but draw a line at the ongoing cost of fixing inadequate attic insulation or ventilation, since that's treated as a maintenance issue rather than storm damage. We separate those two categories clearly in our documentation: what the ice dam caused versus what existed beforehand, with photos and moisture logs that hold up under adjuster review. For township homes under an HOA, such as some of the newer cluster developments off the main corridors, we've found it helps to loop in the HOA's maintenance contact early if exterior roof or gutter repairs are part of the scope, since some associations have their own approved vendor list for roofline work.

## Local note

One pattern we watch for in Shelby Township specifically: homes built with cathedral ceilings over great rooms, common in several of the township's 1990s-era subdivisions, often have almost no attic space above the peak, which means there's nowhere for heat to dissipate before it hits the roof deck. Ice dams on these roofs tend to form lower and wider than on a standard attic roof, and the water intrusion point is often several feet from where the ceiling stain actually appears, so we always trace the water path before opening drywall rather than cutting at the visible stain.

Ice dams rarely wait for convenient timing, and in a Macomb County winter the freeze-thaw cycle that caused the problem can also make it worse within a day if it's left alone.
