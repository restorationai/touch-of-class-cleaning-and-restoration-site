## Why Sterling Heights Properties See Mold Problems

Macomb County's clay-dense soil drains slowly. After a heavy rain or a spring thaw, water that can't percolate fast enough finds the path of least resistance: window wells, basement wall joints, and floor cracks. Sterling Heights has a significant share of ranch-style and split-level homes built between the 1960s and 1980s, many with block-wall basements that are inherently more porous than poured concrete. Block walls wick moisture inward, and once that moisture reaches the paper facing of drywall or the back side of wood paneling, mold can begin colonizing within 24 to 48 hours.

The city also sits close enough to Lake St. Clair that summer humidity regularly pushes indoor relative humidity above the 60% threshold where mold growth accelerates. Air conditioning helps, but older homes with original duct systems often have condensation issues inside the ductwork itself, a spot that standard cleaning misses and mold spores love.

## Our Mold Remediation Process in Sterling Heights

Every remediation starts with a thorough inspection using moisture meters and thermal imaging. Surface mold is rarely the whole story; the more important question is what's wet behind the surface. Once we've mapped the moisture, we establish containment using negative air pressure and poly sheeting to prevent spores from spreading to unaffected rooms during the removal process.

Affected materials, drywall, insulation, wood framing, are removed to a clean margin, bagged, and disposed of per Michigan Department of Environment, Great Lakes, and Energy (EGLE) guidelines. Remaining structural surfaces are HEPA-vacuumed and treated with an EPA-registered antimicrobial. Air scrubbers with HEPA filtration run continuously throughout the job to capture airborne spores. Before we close walls or sign off, we verify that moisture readings have returned to normal dry-standard levels, because sealing damp framing is how mold comes back.

In Sterling Heights, a detail that catches some homeowners off guard: the block-wall basements common in neighborhoods developed during the 1970s often have a thin layer of interior waterproofing paint applied over the years.
