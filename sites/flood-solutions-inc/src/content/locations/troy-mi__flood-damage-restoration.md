## Why Troy Properties See Flood Damage Differently

Troy's development history matters here. Large portions of the city were built out during the 1960s and 1970s on relatively flat terrain with clay-heavy soil that drains slowly. When a significant rain event hits, that clay holds water near the surface rather than letting it percolate, which increases hydrostatic pressure against basement walls and floor slabs. Homes in Troy's older established neighborhoods often have block foundation walls rather than poured concrete, and block is more permeable, water migrates through the mortar joints in ways that are easy to miss until the interior finish materials are already soaked.

Troy also sits in a region where sump pump failure during a power outage is one of the most common causes of residential flood damage. Detroit Edison serves most of the city, and during severe storms the combination of high winds and saturated ground can knock out power for hours, defeating even a well-maintained sump system. A battery backup helps, but it is not a guarantee, and many Troy homeowners discover the limits of their system only after the water is already on the floor.

## Our Flood Damage Restoration Process in Troy

Flood cleanup in Troy is not a single step, it is a sequenced process where skipping ahead creates problems that surface weeks later.

**Water extraction** comes first. Truck-mounted and portable extraction units pull standing water from finished basements, utility rooms, and any carpeted areas. Speed here reduces the total drying time and limits secondary damage to drywall, insulation, and wood framing.

**Moisture mapping** follows immediately. Thermal imaging and calibrated moisture meters identify water that has wicked into wall cavities, under flooring, and behind cabinetry. In Troy's block-foundation homes, this step is especially important because water can travel laterally through the block and appear in rooms that were never directly exposed to the source.

**Controlled demolition** removes materials that cannot be dried in place: saturated drywall, soaked carpet and pad, and compromised insulation. Leaving wet materials behind is the single most common reason a flood restoration job ends with a mold problem.

**Structural drying** uses industrial air movers and dehumidifiers positioned to create airflow through the affected assembly. Readings are logged daily until the structure reaches its pre-loss moisture baseline.

**Final documentation** captures moisture readings, equipment logs, and photo records, all of which matter when you are working through a homeowner's insurance claim.

Most standard homeowner policies in Michigan cover sudden and accidental water damage, but flood damage from surface water intrusion, the kind that comes in through a window well or a low-lying entry point during a heavy rain, is typically handled under a separate NFIP or private flood policy.
