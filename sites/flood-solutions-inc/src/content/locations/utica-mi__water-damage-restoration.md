## Why Utica Properties See Water Damage Issues

Macomb County's glacially deposited clay soils are notoriously poor at absorbing surface water. After a significant rain event, that moisture doesn't percolate, it pools, it follows grade, and it finds the path of least resistance into crawlspaces and basement walls. Utica's mix of mid-century ranch homes and 1980s-era subdivisions compounds the problem: older homes in this area frequently have clay tile drain lines that have cracked or collapsed over decades, and sump pumps in those homes are often undersized for the volume of water a Macomb County storm can push.

Winter brings a different threat. Freeze-thaw cycles along the M-59 corridor are aggressive, and water lines running through uninsulated exterior walls or garage spaces are among the most common sources of burst-pipe calls in late January and February. When a pipe fails inside a finished basement, a common layout in Utica's split-level homes, water migrates under LVP flooring and into wall cavities before anyone sees a drop on the surface.

## Our Water Damage Restoration Process in Utica

Every water loss is different, but the sequence that protects a Utica home follows a clear logic: stop the source, extract standing water, measure moisture in materials, dry to standard, and document everything for the insurance carrier.

Extraction comes first. Truck-mounted and portable extraction units pull standing water from carpet, subfloor, and concrete before it has time to wick further into framing. After extraction, the real work begins: thermal imaging and calibrated moisture meters map where water has traveled inside wall assemblies and under flooring, places that look and feel dry at the surface but are still saturated an inch in. Drying equipment is then placed based on that map, not guesswork. Air movers and dehumidifiers run in a calculated configuration until daily moisture readings confirm materials have returned to acceptable levels. Every reading is logged, which matters when your adjuster asks for documentation.

## Local Note

One thing worth knowing about Utica's older ranch-style homes: many were built with concrete block basement walls rather than poured concrete. Block walls are more porous than poured walls, and once water infiltrates the cores of the blocks, standard surface drying is not enough, moisture trapped inside the block cavities will continue to feed humidity into the basement long after the floor looks dry. Drying protocols for block-wall basements in this area require extended dehumidification run times and more frequent moisture checks than a comparable poured-wall home. If your basement has that characteristic painted-block look, make sure whoever you call understands the difference.
