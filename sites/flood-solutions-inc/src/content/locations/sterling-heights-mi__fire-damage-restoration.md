## Why Sterling Heights Homes Face Particular Challenges After a Fire

The dominant housing stock in Sterling Heights is single-family construction from the 1960s through the 1980s, with a significant number of ranch-style and split-level homes. These floor plans tend to have open attic spaces and minimal fire blocking between wall cavities, which means smoke and heat move laterally through the structure faster than in newer builds with modern fire-stop requirements. Blown-in fiberglass or older batt insulation in those attic spaces absorbs smoke odor deeply and often needs full replacement rather than surface cleaning.

Michigan's freeze-thaw cycle adds another layer of complexity. A fire in January or February in Sterling Heights often means firefighting water freezes in wall cavities before it can be extracted. That trapped moisture creates a secondary mold risk that begins within 24 to 48 hours once the structure warms back up. Addressing fire damage here means treating the smoke, the soot, and the moisture simultaneously, not sequentially.

## Our Fire Damage Restoration Process in Sterling Heights

The first step on any fire loss is a thorough assessment: which materials are salvageable, which have structural compromise, and where smoke has migrated beyond the obvious burn zone. Soot behaves differently depending on what burned. A kitchen grease fire produces a wet, sticky residue that bonds to cabinet surfaces and ceiling paint. An electrical fire in a wall cavity leaves a dry, powdery char that spreads further but lifts more cleanly with the right chemical sponges and HEPA vacuuming.

Once the scope is mapped, the process moves through debris removal, structural drying (critical in Michigan winters when moisture is trapped), odor neutralization using thermal fogging and hydroxyl generators, and surface restoration. Ductwork cleaning is almost always part of the scope in Sterling Heights homes because forced-air systems pull smoke particulate into every room of the house during a fire event. Skipping that step means the smell returns the first time the furnace kicks on.

Documentation runs alongside every phase. Photographs, moisture readings, and material inventories are compiled in a format that most major insurance carriers accept for direct billing, which reduces the paperwork burden on the homeowner during an already stressful period.

## Local Note

Sterling Heights has a large number of attached-garage ranch homes where the garage shares a wall with the living space. Fires that start in the garage, whether from a vehicle, stored fuel, or an electrical issue, often push smoke into the main living area through the shared wall before the fire itself crosses over. That wall assembly frequently contains older drywall without the fire-rated thickness now required by code, and smoke infiltration into the living side can be significant even when the visible burn damage is confined to the garage. Restoration crews working in Sterling Heights encounter this pattern often enough that it shapes how the initial assessment is structured: the living-side inspection happens at the same time as the garage, not after.
