## Why Sterling Heights Properties See Water Damage So Often

The city's residential construction is heavily concentrated in homes built between the 1960s and the 1990s, a generation of building that relied on clay-heavy soil backfill around foundations. That clay holds moisture rather than draining it, so when spring snowmelt or a heavy summer rain event saturates the ground, hydrostatic pressure builds against block and poured-concrete foundations alike. Sump pits in these homes work overtime, and a power outage or pump failure at the wrong moment turns a manageable situation into several inches of water across a finished floor.

The city's flat topography compounds the problem. Unlike areas with natural grade relief, Sterling Heights lots have limited surface runoff, which means water lingers near foundations longer than it would on sloped terrain. Storm drains in established residential sections can back up during high-volume rain events, occasionally pushing water toward homes rather than away from them.

## Our Water Removal Process in Sterling Heights

When the call comes in, the first priority is containment, not cleanup. The crew arrives with truck-mounted extraction equipment capable of pulling hundreds of gallons from a flooded basement before the water has time to wick further into wall cavities or subflooring.

After bulk extraction, the focus shifts to what you cannot see. Moisture meters and thermal imaging cameras map the actual wet zone, which in Sterling Heights homes often extends behind drywall or beneath vinyl plank flooring laid directly over concrete. That hidden moisture is where mold colonization typically begins, and it starts within 24 to 48 hours of a water event. Drying equipment, including commercial-grade dehumidifiers and air movers, is placed based on the readings, not by guesswork.

Documentation runs in parallel with every step. Photos, moisture readings, and a written scope go into a file that travels with the job from the first extraction through the final clearance check. That record matters when an insurance adjuster needs to understand why certain materials required removal.

Most homeowner policies in Michigan cover sudden and accidental water discharge, which includes burst pipes and appliance failures, but the language around sump pump backup and ground-water intrusion varies significantly by carrier and endorsement.
