## Why Troy Properties See Water Damage Issues

Troy's housing stock spans several decades of construction, from mid-century ranch homes with original copper supply lines to newer builds in subdivisions developed through the 1990s and 2000s. Older copper plumbing, while durable, develops pinhole leaks as it ages, often inside wall cavities where the moisture saturates insulation and framing long before a stain appears on the ceiling. Slab-on-grade construction, common in many of Troy's ranch-style neighborhoods, can hide water intrusion beneath finished flooring for weeks; by the time a homeowner notices buckling laminate or a musty odor, the subfloor may already be compromised.

Oakland County's clay-heavy soil compounds the problem. Clay doesn't drain, it sheds. During heavy spring rains or rapid snowmelt, water that can't percolate through the ground migrates toward foundation walls and window wells. Basement flooding in Troy is frequently a drainage issue rather than a plumbing failure, which means the source of water matters as much as the volume when determining the right mitigation approach.

The region's temperature swings, from sub-zero cold snaps to humid summers, also accelerate mold colonization after a water event. Under warm, humid conditions, mold can begin establishing itself on wet building materials within 24 to 48 hours. That timeline is not a scare tactic; it's the standard referenced in the IICRC S520 and is why water extraction and structural drying need to begin as quickly as possible after a loss.

## Our Water Damage Restoration Process in Troy

Every job starts with moisture mapping, not just a visual inspection, but thermal imaging and calibrated moisture meters used on walls, floors, and ceilings to find where water has traveled beyond what's visible. In Troy homes with finished basements, water from a supply-line failure on the first floor can track down interior wall cavities and pool behind drywall at the base of the foundation without ever appearing on the surface.

Once the full extent of the moisture is documented, extraction comes first. Standing water is removed with truck-mounted or portable extraction equipment, and wet materials, carpet padding, saturated drywall, compromised insulation, are assessed for salvageability. Materials that hold moisture and cannot be dried in place are removed to allow airflow to the structural components behind them.

Structural drying follows using industrial-grade desiccant or refrigerant dehumidifiers and high-velocity air movers positioned according to the drying plan developed from the moisture map. Readings are logged daily so drying progress is documented, not estimated. This documentation matters when the job goes to an insurance adjuster, and it matters to the homeowner who wants to know when it's actually safe to rebuild.

## Troy Insurance & HOA Coordination

Most homeowner policies in Michigan cover sudden and accidental water damage, a burst pipe, an appliance failure, an ice dam that forces water through the roof line, but coverage for long-term seepage or gradual leaks is typically excluded. The distinction matters because the cause of loss determines what gets paid. Flood Solutions Inc documents the loss with photographs, moisture readings, and written scope notes that give your adjuster a clear picture of what happened and what it will take to restore the property. That documentation is prepared from the first visit, not assembled after the fact.

If your property falls under an HOA with shared walls or common-area drainage systems, a pattern found in several of Troy's townhome and condominium developments, the coordination between your individual claim and the association's policy can get complicated. We've navigated that kind of multi-party claim process before and can work alongside your adjuster and the HOA's carrier simultaneously.

## Local Note

Troy's ranch homes built in the 1950s and 1960s frequently have original plaster-and-lath interior walls rather than drywall. Plaster reads differently on a moisture meter than gypsum board, it can appear dry at the surface while the wood lath behind it remains saturated. Standard drying timelines don't apply the same way, and crews who treat plaster walls like drywall risk signing off on a structure that still holds significant moisture. When we work in older Troy homes, drying protocols are adjusted and extended monitoring periods are built into the plan to account for how plaster releases moisture.
