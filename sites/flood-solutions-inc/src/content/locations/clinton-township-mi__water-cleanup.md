## Why Clinton Township Properties See Water Damage Issues

Macomb County's soil composition is a significant part of the story. The dense clay layer that underlies much of the township doesn't drain the way sandy or loam soils do, during a heavy spring storm or a rapid snowmelt, water has nowhere to go quickly. It pools against foundations, works through mortar joints in block walls, and finds its way into crawl spaces and basement floors that may have been dry for years. Sump pumps that handle normal conditions can be overwhelmed in a matter of hours.

The housing stock adds another layer of complexity. Clinton Township grew rapidly through the postwar decades, and many of the ranch-style and split-level homes built in the 1950s through the 1970s were constructed with block or poured concrete basements that were never designed with modern waterproofing membranes. Older homes in the township may also have cast-iron drain lines that have corroded or shifted over decades, making sewer backups a recurring problem rather than a one-time event. When sewage mixes with groundwater intrusion, the cleanup requires a different protocol than a clean water loss, the category of the water matters for both safety and for how your insurance claim is documented.

## Our Water Cleanup Process in Clinton Township

The first step on any water damage job is assessment, not extraction. Before equipment goes in, the source of the water needs to be confirmed and stopped. A wet basement that keeps receiving groundwater through a failing sump system will not dry regardless of how many air movers are running. Once the source is addressed, we use moisture meters and thermal imaging to map where water has migrated beyond the visible wet area. Water behind drywall, under vinyl flooring, or inside wall cavities reads dry at the surface while the structure underneath stays saturated.

Extraction comes next, standing water is removed with truck-mounted or portable extraction units depending on access. After bulk water is out, the drying phase begins with calibrated air movers and dehumidifiers sized to the affected area. In Clinton Township's humid summer months, ambient moisture in the air actively works against the drying process, which is why equipment selection and placement matter more than simply running units on high. Readings are logged daily so the drying progress is documented, that documentation matters when your insurance adjuster reviews the claim.

## Clinton Township Insurance Coordination

Water damage claims in Michigan can be complicated by the distinction between sudden-and-accidental losses (typically covered) and long-term seepage or groundwater intrusion (often excluded or covered only under a separate flood endorsement). That distinction matters from the first call. We document the loss thoroughly, photographs, moisture readings, affected material inventories, in a format that aligns with how adjusters review residential water claims. We work directly with most major carriers and can communicate with your adjuster on scope so you're not translating between two different conversations.

## Local Note

One thing worth knowing about Clinton Township's older ranch-style homes: many were built on slab-on-grade foundations with copper supply lines run through or under the concrete. When those lines fail, and after 50-plus years, they do, water can travel under the slab before it ever surfaces, and the first visible sign is often a warm spot on the floor or a spike in the water bill rather than obvious pooling. By the time water appears, it may have been migrating under the slab for days. If you're seeing unexplained moisture in a home built before 1980, it's worth having the slab checked before assuming the source is surface water.
