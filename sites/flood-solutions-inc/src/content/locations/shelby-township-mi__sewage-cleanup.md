Shelby Township sits on a broad clay-heavy plain in Macomb County where spring snowmelt and heavy summer storms have nowhere fast to go. When that drainage pressure backs up into a home through a floor drain, toilet, or laundry standpipe, the result is raw sewage contamination that soaks into subfloor assemblies, drywall, and stored belongings within minutes. Flood & Fire Solutions has been responding to exactly this kind of loss across Macomb County since 1996, and the conditions specific to Shelby Township shape how that work gets done.

## Why Shelby Township Properties See Sewage Backup Problems

Macomb County's underlying soils run dense and clay-rich, which means they shed water rather than absorb it. During a hard rain or a rapid spring thaw, the ground stays saturated near the surface and storm runoff finds the path of least resistance: the municipal sewer system. When the system surpasses capacity, sewage can reverse direction and push back through the lowest fixtures in a home, typically a basement floor drain or a first-floor toilet.

Shelby Township's residential growth accelerated through the 1970s, 1980s, and 1990s, meaning a large share of the housing stock is now 30 to 50 years old. Lateral sewer lines from that era were often installed with minimal slope and in some cases used clay tile or early PVC that has since shifted, cracked, or accumulated root intrusion from the mature trees that line many of the township's subdivisions. A partially blocked lateral doesn't fail dramatically at first. It slows drainage quietly until a heavy-use event, a full household morning routine or a heavy rain event, pushes it past its limit.

Homes on septic systems in the more rural pockets of the township face a different but related problem. Drain fields sized for smaller households can become overwhelmed after a wet season, and when the field saturates, effluent has nowhere to go but back toward the home.

## Our Sewage Cleanup and Sanitization Process in Shelby Township

Raw sewage is classified as Category 3 water, the most contaminated class of water intrusion, and the cleanup protocol reflects that. The process doesn't begin with extraction. It begins with containment: isolating the affected area to prevent cross-contamination into clean living spaces, and protecting HVAC returns that could distribute airborne pathogens throughout the home.

Once the space is contained, standing sewage and saturated materials are removed. Porous materials that have absorbed Category 3 water, including drywall, insulation, carpet, and in some cases subfloor sheathing, typically cannot be restored and are removed. This is not a shortcut; it is the only way to eliminate the microbial load those materials carry.

After physical removal, affected surfaces are treated with EPA-registered disinfectants appropriate for sewage contamination. Structural cavities are inspected with moisture meters and thermal imaging to confirm that contamination hasn't wicked further than the visible damage suggests. In Shelby Township homes with finished basements, that step matters especially because the gap between the concrete slab and the finished floor can trap moisture and organic material well beyond the obvious damage boundary.

Drying equipment runs until moisture readings return to pre-loss baselines, not on a fixed schedule.

## Shelby Township Insurance Coordination for Sewage Losses

Sewer backup coverage is not automatically included in a standard homeowner's policy. Many Shelby Township homeowners discover this at exactly the wrong moment. If you added a sewer backup rider, that endorsement typically covers extraction, sanitization, and structural drying, but the documentation the carrier requires is specific: moisture logs, photographic evidence of the source, and itemized records of materials removed.

Flood & Fire Solutions documents losses in the format most major carriers expect, which reduces back-and-forth during the claim process. If your policy does not include sewer backup coverage, we can walk through what the scope of work involves so you understand the costs before work begins.

## Local Note

Shelby Township subdivisions built during the 1980s and early 1990s frequently used finished basement designs with a narrow crawl space or utility chase running along the perimeter wall. When a sewage backup reaches that chase, contaminated water can travel laterally along the wall base and appear in a room well removed from the actual source drain. Homeowners sometimes assume the spread is limited to the room where the backup was first visible. Probing the perimeter of the finished space with a moisture meter before assuming the scope is contained is a step that changes the remediation plan more often than not in this township's housing stock.
