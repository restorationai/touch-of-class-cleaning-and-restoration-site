## Why Clinton Township Commercial Properties Face Distinct Restoration Challenges

The township sits on relatively flat glacial lake plain, and that topography matters when stormwater has nowhere to drain quickly. During heavy spring and summer rain events, parking lot drainage backs up against foundation walls, and the slab-on-grade construction common in Clinton Township's commercial strip centers gives water a direct path into finished tenant spaces. Older brick-and-block buildings along the township's commercial corridors often have unreinforced parapet walls and aging roof membranes that accelerate interior damage when a fire or storm event occurs.

Commercial HVAC systems in the township's office parks and medical suites add another layer of complexity. A water intrusion event that contacts ductwork can distribute moisture and, if left unaddressed for more than 24 to 48 hours, create conditions favorable to microbial growth across a much larger footprint than the original source. Fire events create their own spread pattern: smoke and soot travel through HVAC returns and settle in areas far from the fire's origin, which means a kitchen fire in a multi-tenant strip can leave smoke odor in adjacent suites that share the same air handling system.

## Our Commercial Restoration Process in Clinton Township

Every commercial job starts with a scope assessment that separates what is visible from what the building is hiding. Moisture mapping with thermal imaging and calibrated meters gives us a picture of the actual damage boundary before any demolition decisions are made. That matters especially in Clinton Township's older commercial stock, where plaster, masonry, and original hardwood floors can read dry at the surface while holding significant moisture behind the finish layer.

From there, the process follows a sequenced approach:

- **Containment and safety**: Isolate affected areas to protect inventory, equipment, and any occupied tenant spaces.
- **Extraction and structural drying**: Industrial-grade extractors and desiccant or refrigerant dehumidifiers sized to the actual cubic footage of the loss, not a generic estimate.
- **Smoke and odor mitigation** (fire losses): Thermal fogging, hydroxyl generation, and surface cleaning calibrated to the material types present, whether that is painted CMU block, suspended ceiling tile, or laminate millwork.
- **Documentation**: Photo and moisture-log documentation formatted for commercial property insurance carriers and, where applicable, for landlord-tenant lease requirements.
- **Reconstruction coordination**: Flood Solutions connects directly with your general contractor or can coordinate trades in-house to return the space to pre-loss condition.

## Insurance and Lease Coordination for Clinton Township Businesses

Commercial losses involve more parties than residential claims. You may be dealing with a commercial property insurer, a business interruption policy, a landlord's carrier, and a property management company simultaneously. Flood Solutions documents losses in a format that supports the claims process: itemized moisture readings, time-stamped photographs, material identification, and written scope narratives. ## Local Note

Clinton Township's commercial buildings from the 1970s and 1980s, particularly the single-story office and light-industrial properties scattered through the township's interior streets, were frequently built with steel stud framing and fiberglass batt insulation in the exterior walls. After a water intrusion event, that insulation holds moisture against the metal framing in a way that does not show on a surface moisture reading. We probe those wall assemblies rather than relying on surface meters alone, because leaving saturated insulation in place behind a dry-reading exterior finish is how a remediated loss becomes a mold claim six weeks later.
