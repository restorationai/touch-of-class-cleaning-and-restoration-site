## Why Shelby Township's Building Stock Creates Large Loss Risk

Shelby Township sits in a zone where two distinct construction eras coexist. Older ranch-style homes built through the 1970s and 1980s, common throughout the township's interior neighborhoods, often have original cast-iron drain stacks, crawl spaces with marginal vapor barriers, and attic insulation that retains moisture long after a roof breach. Newer commercial developments built since the early 2000s along the M-59 and 23 Mile Road corridors feature large-span steel construction, complex mechanical systems, and finished interiors that can sustain enormous damage from a single sprinkler activation or pipe failure.

Macomb County's clay-heavy soil compounds the problem. Clay does not drain quickly, so ground saturation after a major storm event pushes water toward foundations and slab edges rather than away from them. A single severe weather event can trigger simultaneous losses across multiple properties in the same development, exactly the scenario that demands a coordinated, large loss response rather than a series of independent service calls.

## Our Large Loss Process in Shelby Township

Catastrophic restoration at this scale is a project management discipline as much as a technical one. The first step on any large loss is a rapid damage assessment that segments the structure into containment zones, identifies which areas are safe for immediate entry, and establishes a priority sequence for extraction and stabilization. We document everything photographically and with moisture mapping before a single piece of material is removed, that documentation becomes the backbone of the insurance claim.

From there, we coordinate equipment deployment scaled to the loss: industrial-grade drying systems, negative air machines, and, where structural compromise is present, temporary weatherproofing and shoring. For commercial catastrophic restoration in Shelby Township, we also coordinate directly with large loss adjusters from the outset. Carriers handling multi-million dollar restoration claims operate differently than residential adjusters, they expect detailed scope documents, unit-cost breakdowns, and regular progress reporting. We build that reporting cadence into the job from day one.

Reconstruction sequencing matters here too. A phased approach that restores critical business functions first, electrical, HVAC, occupied tenant spaces, minimizes business interruption losses, which are often as significant as the physical damage costs on a commercial property.

## Local Note

Shelby Township's newer subdivision developments, particularly those built in the 2000s along the northern sections of the township, frequently feature finished basements with engineered wood flooring over concrete slabs. When a catastrophic water event reaches these spaces, the flooring can appear intact at the surface while the OSB substrate and the concrete beneath it hold significant moisture. Standard surface readings miss this entirely. We use penetrating moisture meters and thermal imaging on every large loss assessment in these homes, it is the only reliable way to establish a true drying baseline before reconstruction begins.
