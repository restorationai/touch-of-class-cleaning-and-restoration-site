## Why Troy Properties See Smoke Damage Differently

Troy's residential construction boom ran roughly from the mid-1960s through the 1980s, producing neighborhoods full of brick ranch homes, two-story colonials, and split-levels with open floor plans. That open layout, combined with the era's ductwork design, means smoke from a contained kitchen or garage fire rarely stays contained. Older fiberglass duct insulation absorbs odor compounds, and the textured ceilings common in homes of that period trap soot in ways that smooth modern drywall does not.

The city also sits in Oakland County, where winters push residents to seal homes tightly for energy efficiency. That reduced air exchange means smoke odor lingers longer without active intervention. Protein smoke from a cooking fire, which leaves almost no visible residue, is especially deceptive in these conditions: the smell returns every time the heat kicks on, even after a surface cleaning that looks thorough.

## The Smoke Damage Restoration Process in Troy

Effective smoke residue cleanup starts with a full assessment before any cleaning begins. That means testing surfaces for the type of residue present, wet smoke from slow-burning fires behaves very differently than dry smoke from fast, high-heat fires, and each requires a different chemical approach. HVAC systems get inspected and, when necessary, isolated so cleaning efforts are not immediately undone by recirculating contaminated air.

Porous materials like drywall, wood trim, and upholstered furniture require different treatment than hard surfaces. Thermal fogging or hydroxyl generation can reach into wall cavities and ductwork where manual cleaning cannot. Structural materials that cannot be deodorized to an acceptable level are documented for removal and replacement. Every step is photographed and recorded, which matters when you are working with an insurance adjuster.
