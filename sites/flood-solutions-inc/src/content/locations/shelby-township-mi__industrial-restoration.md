## Why Shelby Township Industrial Facilities Face Distinct Restoration Challenges

Macomb County's climate puts industrial buildings through a punishing annual cycle. Winters bring repeated freeze-thaw events that stress roof membranes, expansion joints, and overhead plumbing. When a joint fails or a drain freezes solid, water can travel across a concrete slab for hundreds of feet before anyone notices, saturating insulation, wicking into dock seals, and pooling beneath racking systems. Summer brings heavy convective storms that overwhelm flat-roof drainage systems common on warehouses and manufacturing plants built through the 1980s and 1990s.

Shelby Township also sits on clay-heavy glacial soils that drain slowly. Exterior grade around older industrial pads tends to settle over time, directing surface water toward foundation walls and loading dock aprons rather than away from them. Facilities that have not had their perimeter drainage assessed in the last decade are often surprised by how much subsurface moisture is already present before a major event occurs.

## Our Industrial Restoration Process in Shelby Township

Industrial restoration is not scaled-up residential work. The process starts with a rapid facility assessment that maps affected zones by function: production areas, storage, mechanical rooms, offices, and shipping docks each carry different contamination risks and drying requirements. Moisture mapping with thermal imaging and penetrating meters identifies water migration behind insulated metal panels and beneath epoxy-coated slabs, where surface readings can look dry while the substrate remains saturated.

Extraction and drying equipment for a large-format industrial loss is matched to the cubic footage, not just the square footage. High-capacity desiccant dehumidifiers handle the low-temperature drying demands that standard refrigerant units cannot meet in unheated warehouses during Michigan winters. Structural drying documentation is maintained throughout, which matters when a facility manager needs to demonstrate to a carrier or a tenant that the building envelope has been returned to pre-loss moisture conditions.

For industrial fire damage, the scope extends beyond visible char. Smoke and combustion byproducts travel through HVAC ductwork, settle on inventory and equipment surfaces, and leave corrosive residues on metal components. Odor control in a large open-bay environment requires thermal fogging and hydroxyl treatment at a scale that takes planning, not just equipment.

## Local Note

Shelby Township's industrial buildings from the late 1980s and early 1990s frequently used fiberglass batt insulation inside metal stud cavity walls along office and breakroom additions.
