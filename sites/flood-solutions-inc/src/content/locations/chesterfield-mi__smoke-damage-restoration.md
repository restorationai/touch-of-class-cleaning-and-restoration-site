## Why Chesterfield Properties See Persistent Smoke Damage

Chesterfield's climate plays a direct role in how smoke residue behaves after a fire. The township sits in Macomb County, where cold winters and tight home construction mean houses are sealed against drafts for a good portion of the year. That tight envelope, which is an energy efficiency asset in January, becomes a liability after a fire. Smoke particles circulate through the HVAC system and settle into every room the forced-air system serves, not just the room where the fire originated. Homes built before modern vapor barrier standards are also more likely to have fibrous insulation in the walls that traps odor-causing particles and resists surface cleaning entirely.

The result is a pattern that shows up repeatedly in this part of Macomb County: a kitchen fire that looks contained to one room leaves a persistent smoke odor in bedrooms, closets, and finished basements weeks later. Visible soot on walls is only part of the story. The invisible, gaseous phase of smoke, the volatile organic compounds that cause that sharp, throat-catching smell, absorbs into drywall, subflooring, and soft furnishings and off-gasses slowly over time if not treated at the source.

## Our Smoke Damage Restoration Process in Chesterfield

Effective smoke damage restoration is a sequenced process, not a single cleaning pass. The work begins with a thorough inspection using thermal imaging and air quality monitoring to map where smoke has traveled beyond the fire origin area. In Chesterfield homes with finished basements and attached garages, both common in the township's subdivisions, those secondary spaces are a frequent source of overlooked contamination.

Once the scope is documented, the process moves through several distinct phases:

- **Dry soot removal** using HEPA vacuuming and dry chemical sponges before any wet cleaning is introduced. Wet cleaning over dry soot embeds particles further into porous surfaces.
- **Surface cleaning and degreasing** of walls, ceilings, cabinetry, and hard surfaces using professional-grade alkaline cleaners calibrated to the soot type (protein-based from kitchen fires behaves differently than petroleum-based from synthetic materials).
- **Duct cleaning and HVAC decontamination**, which is non-negotiable in forced-air homes where smoke has circulated through the system.
- **Odor neutralization** using thermal fogging or hydroxyl generation to reach the same porous surfaces that absorbed smoke in the first place. Air fresheners and ozone treatments alone do not resolve embedded odor.
- **Structural assessment** for materials that cannot be cleaned and need controlled removal before reconstruction begins.

Documentation runs throughout every phase, with photographs and written scope notes that support the insurance claim process.
