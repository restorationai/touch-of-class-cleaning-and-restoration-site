## Why Shelby Township Commercial Properties Face Distinct Restoration Challenges

Macomb County sits on a clay-heavy glacial till that drains slowly. When spring snowmelt arrives or a summer storm drops two inches of rain in an hour, that clay holds water against foundation walls and under slab floors rather than letting it percolate away. For commercial buildings, especially the slab-on-grade retail and light-industrial construction common throughout Shelby Township, that means water intrusion can appear days after a storm event, long after an owner assumes the danger has passed.

The township also saw a significant wave of commercial construction through the 1980s and 1990s, which means many strip centers and office parks are now old enough that original roof membranes, HVAC condensate lines, and sprinkler risers are reaching the end of their service life. A failed condensate drain on a rooftop unit can saturate a drop-ceiling grid and the insulation above it before anyone notices the staining. Fire events in older commercial kitchens often involve grease-laden smoke that penetrates HVAC ductwork and leaves an odor signature that persists long after visible soot is cleaned.

## Our Commercial Restoration Process in Shelby Township

Every commercial job starts with a documented scope, not a verbal estimate. We walk the property with the owner or property manager, photograph affected materials, and produce a line-item damage report formatted to meet most commercial carrier requirements. That documentation matters because commercial claims are scrutinized more closely than residential ones, and a well-organized file shortens the adjuster review cycle.

For water losses, we use thermal imaging and calibrated moisture meters to map saturation behind walls and under flooring before any demolition begins. In slab-on-grade buildings common along the Van Dyke corridor, moisture trapped beneath vinyl composition tile or epoxy coatings can read dry at the surface while the concrete substrate stays elevated for weeks. We monitor daily and adjust drying equipment placement based on readings rather than on a fixed schedule.

For fire and smoke losses, the restoration sequence matters as much as the cleaning chemistry. Dry soot is removed before any wet cleaning begins; reversing that order can permanently set staining into porous masonry and ceiling tile. Ductwork is inspected and, when contaminated, cleaned or isolated before the HVAC system is returned to service.

Commercial losses almost always involve more parties than a residential claim: a building owner, a tenant, a property management company, a commercial carrier, and sometimes a franchisor or lender with their own documentation requirements. ## Local Note

Shelby Township's newer mixed-use and office developments, particularly those built after the mid-2000s near the M-59 and Van Dyke intersection, frequently incorporate shared mechanical rooms and common-area HVAC systems that serve multiple tenants.
