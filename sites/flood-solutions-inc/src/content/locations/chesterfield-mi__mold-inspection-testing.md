Chesterfield Township sits on the western edge of Lake St. Clair's coastal plain, where clay-heavy soils slow drainage and seasonal humidity swings push moisture into crawl spaces and wall cavities year after year. That combination makes mold inspection and testing here a different exercise than in drier inland communities. A surface that looks clean after a wet spring can still harbor active spore colonies behind drywall or under subfloor sheathing, and without air sampling and moisture mapping, there is no reliable way to know what you are actually dealing with.

## Why Chesterfield Properties See Mold Issues

Macomb County's shoreline communities, including Chesterfield, experience some of the most persistent relative humidity readings in southeast Michigan. Lake-effect moisture from St. Clair keeps summer dew points elevated well into September, and that ambient humidity feeds mold growth long after a visible water event has dried. Homes built on the township's low-lying parcels often have slab-on-grade or shallow crawl space foundations that wick ground moisture upward, particularly during spring thaw when frozen clay releases weeks of accumulated water all at once.

The housing stock adds another layer of complexity. Subdivisions developed through the 1970s and 1980s used kraft-faced fiberglass batts and paper-backed drywall that mold colonizes readily once moisture content climbs above roughly 60 percent relative humidity. Newer construction is tighter, which reduces air exchange and can trap humidity inside if mechanical ventilation is undersized. Either scenario creates conditions where mold can establish quietly for months before a homeowner notices discoloration or odor.

## Our Mold Inspection and Testing Process in Chesterfield

A thorough inspection begins before any samples are collected. A technician walks the property with a calibrated thermal imaging camera and non-invasive moisture meters, mapping areas where readings deviate from the surrounding baseline. Thermal anomalies behind walls or under flooring often indicate trapped moisture that surface observation would miss entirely.

Once the physical inspection is complete, air sampling captures what is actually circulating through the living space. Spore trap cassettes are collected from each affected zone and from at least one outdoor control location so the lab can establish a meaningful comparison. Surface swab or tape-lift samples are added when visible growth needs species identification. All samples go to an accredited third-party laboratory, and the written report details spore counts by genus, moisture readings by location, and a clear summary of what the findings mean for the property.

The inspection report is structured to be useful whether the next step is remediation, a real estate transaction, or a conversation with an insurance adjuster. Findings are documented with photographs, floor plan annotations, and moisture data tables so there is no ambiguity about what was found and where.
