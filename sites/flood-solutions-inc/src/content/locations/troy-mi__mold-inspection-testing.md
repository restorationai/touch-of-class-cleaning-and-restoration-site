That trapped humidity, especially through the long Michigan shoulder seasons when windows stay closed and HVAC systems cycle inconsistently, gives mold spores exactly the foothold they need. ## Why Troy Properties See Mold Issues

Oakland County's clay-dense subsoil drains slowly. After heavy spring rains or a winter thaw, water doesn't percolate away quickly, and hydrostatic pressure builds against basement walls and slab edges. Homes built in the 1960s through the 1980s, which make up a large share of Troy's residential stock, frequently have block foundation walls that are porous by nature. Block foundations wick moisture inward gradually, and because the migration is slow, surface readings can appear normal while the interior of a wall cavity stays damp enough to sustain mold growth for weeks.

Troy's winters compound the problem. When outdoor temperatures drop sharply and interior heating runs continuously, cold exterior walls create condensation zones on the inside surface, particularly around window frames, rim joists, and any area where insulation has settled or was never fully installed. These are the spots mold inspectors look at first, because they're where the moisture budget consistently runs over.

## Our Mold Inspection and Testing Process in Troy

A mold assessment is not a single swab and a report. The process starts with a visual walkthrough using moisture meters and thermal imaging to map areas where humidity is elevated or where building materials are reading wet beneath the surface. This step matters in Troy specifically because finished basements and drywall-over-block construction can hide active moisture intrusion that isn't obvious to the eye.

Once problem zones are identified, air samples and, where warranted, surface samples are collected. Air sampling captures the spore count and species profile of what occupants are actually breathing, which is the core of indoor air quality testing. Surface samples help confirm whether a stain is mold or something else entirely. All samples are sent to an accredited third-party laboratory. The written report you receive identifies the species present, compares indoor spore counts to outdoor baseline levels, and notes which areas warrant remediation and which are within normal range.

The inspection and the remediation are kept as separate scopes intentionally. An independent assessment protects you: the findings drive the remediation plan rather than the other way around.

## Equipment and Methods We Use for Mold Assessment

Thermal imaging cameras detect temperature differentials that point to moisture behind finished surfaces without requiring destructive opening of walls. Calibrated pin and pinless moisture meters give readings at different depths so inspectors can distinguish surface condensation from deeper saturation. Air sampling pumps draw a measured volume of air through a spore trap cassette at each collection point, ensuring the lab analysis reflects actual conditions rather than a momentary spike.

For Troy homes with finished lower levels, inspectors also check mechanical spaces carefully. HVAC air handlers, humidifiers, and condensate drain lines are common mold sources that get overlooked when attention focuses only on the visible living space.

## Local Note

Troy's newer subdivisions, particularly those developed in the 1990s and early 2000s along the northern corridors of the city, were built with OSB sheathing and paper-faced drywall that are significantly more mold-susceptible than the older plaster and masonry construction found closer to the city's center. When mold spore testing turns up elevated Stachybotrys or Chaetomium counts in these homes, the source is often a slow roof leak or a failed window flashing that has been feeding moisture into the wall cavity for a full season before any visible sign appears inside. Inspectors working in this part of Troy routinely probe the attic and exterior wall assemblies even when the complaint originates in a finished room below.
