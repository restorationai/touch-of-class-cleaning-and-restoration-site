## Why Rochester Hills Properties See Flood Damage

Oakland County's clay-dominant soils drain poorly, and Rochester Hills is no exception. During sustained rainfall or rapid snowmelt, water tables rise quickly, and hydrostatic pressure builds against basement walls and floor slabs. Many homes in Rochester Hills were built in the 1970s through the 1990s, a period when basement waterproofing standards were less rigorous than current code requires. Block-wall foundations common in that era are especially vulnerable: the mortar joints absorb water slowly and can allow seepage long after the storm event ends.

## Our Flood Cleanup Process in Rochester Hills

The first priority on any flood call is stopping ongoing water intrusion if it's still active, then measuring what's actually wet. Standing water is obvious; moisture that has wicked two feet up a drywall panel is not. Technicians use thermal imaging cameras and calibrated moisture meters to map the full extent of saturation before any material is removed. This documentation also becomes the foundation of the insurance claim file.

Once the scope is mapped, water extraction begins with truck-mounted and portable units capable of pulling water from carpet, pad, hardwood subfloor, and concrete. Structural drying follows using commercial-grade desiccant and refrigerant dehumidifiers placed according to the room geometry and the materials involved. Air movement is calculated, not guessed: the goal is to drive moisture out of wall cavities and subfloor assemblies before secondary damage like mold colonization can begin, which typically starts within 24 to 48 hours of initial saturation in a warm environment.

Content that can be restored is inventoried and moved to a dry staging area. Materials that have absorbed enough water to compromise structural integrity or that cannot be dried in place are removed and documented for the adjuster.

## Rochester Hills Insurance Coordination

Flood damage claims in Michigan involve a distinction that catches many homeowners off guard: standard homeowners policies typically exclude rising water from outside the structure, while sudden and accidental discharge from an interior source is usually covered. Knowing which cause applies changes how the claim is filed and what documentation the adjuster needs. The Flood Solutions Inc. team photographs affected materials systematically, records moisture readings at multiple depths, and produces a scope of loss that aligns with the format carriers expect. Homes built in Rochester Hills during the 1980s and early 1990s frequently used a basement wall system that combined poured concrete footings with concrete block above the footing line.
