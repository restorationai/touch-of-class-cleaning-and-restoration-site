## Why Troy Commercial Properties Face Elevated Catastrophic Loss Exposure

Oakland County's clay-heavy soils drain slowly, and Troy sits in a zone where that drainage limitation intersects with a high concentration of large-footprint commercial buildings, many of them constructed between the late 1970s and early 2000s. Buildings from that era frequently have flat or low-slope roofing systems that are now approaching or past their design life. A single membrane failure during a heavy rain event can introduce thousands of gallons of water into ceiling assemblies, server rooms, and finished office space before anyone on site identifies the source.

Troy also carries meaningful freeze-thaw exposure. The temperature swings common to southeastern Michigan, where a January thaw can be followed by a hard freeze within 48 hours, put mechanical systems and rooftop equipment under repeated stress. Sprinkler lines in unconditioned spaces, pipe chases on exterior walls, and rooftop HVAC supply lines are recurring failure points in the commercial stock along corridors like Livernois and Rochester Road.

## What Large Loss Response Actually Looks Like in Troy

Catastrophic restoration at commercial scale is a logistics and documentation problem as much as it is a technical one. When Flood Solutions Inc mobilizes for a large loss in Troy, the first priority is simultaneous: stop ongoing damage, protect undamaged contents and systems, and begin the documentation chain that carriers and large loss adjusters will rely on throughout the claim.

That means deploying multiple crews, industrial-scale drying and extraction equipment, and a dedicated project manager who functions as the single point of contact for the property owner, the building's facilities team, the carrier's large loss unit, and any specialty subcontractors. For a multi-story commercial building, structural drying plans are built floor by floor, with psychrometric readings logged at regular intervals to demonstrate drying progress in a format that satisfies both the IICRC S500 standard and carrier documentation requirements.

Business interruption is always on the table in these losses. Phased restoration, temporary enclosures, and coordinated scheduling around tenant operations are part of the plan from day one, not an afterthought.

## Working with Large Loss Adjusters and Carriers in Troy

Large loss claims in Oakland County typically involve dedicated large loss adjusters assigned by the carrier rather than the standard residential or small commercial desk. These adjusters expect detailed scope documentation, Xactimate line-item estimates, and a restoration partner who can speak their language. Flood Solutions Inc prepares scope packages that include moisture mapping, photo documentation keyed to the floor plan, equipment logs, and daily drying reports. This documentation discipline shortens the supplement cycle and reduces the back-and-forth that extends claim timelines.

For properties with multiple tenants, the coordination layer expands further. Each tenant's space may involve a separate contents inventory, separate business interruption considerations, and in some cases separate carriers. The project manager role on a large loss engagement is built around managing exactly that complexity.

## Local Note

Troy's commercial buildings from the 1980s and 1990s frequently used a steel stud and drywall interior construction system with suspended ceiling grids. When water infiltrates from above, it wicks laterally along the ceiling grid runners and can travel 30 to 40 feet from the point of entry before it becomes visible as a ceiling stain or drip. On a large loss engagement in a building like this, the actual affected area is almost always significantly larger than the visible damage suggests. Thermal imaging and moisture mapping across the full ceiling plane, not just the obvious wet zone, is standard practice on every Troy commercial large loss we assess.
