Clinton Township sits in one of Michigan's most active storm corridors, where Lake St. Clair weather systems push hard rain inland and Macomb County's flat clay-heavy soils give standing water nowhere to go fast. When a large-scale loss hits here, whether it's a commercial roof collapse after a February ice load, a multi-unit apartment complex with a ruptured main, or a manufacturing facility swamped by a backed-up municipal drain, the damage math compounds quickly. Flood Solutions inc has been working large and catastrophic losses across this region since 1996, and the scale of a Clinton Township event demands a response built around coordination, not just equipment.

## Why Clinton Township Properties Face Elevated Catastrophic Loss Risk

Macomb County's clay soil profile is a defining factor in how water behaves here. Unlike sandy substrates that drain relatively quickly, dense clay holds moisture at grade and forces it toward foundations, crawlspaces, and slab edges. Commercial properties along the township's busier corridors often sit on large impervious footprints that accelerate runoff toward neighboring structures during heavy events. Older multi-family housing stock, some of it built in the postwar boom decades when Clinton Township's population expanded rapidly, frequently relies on aging cast-iron drain lines and undersized sump systems that were never engineered for the rainfall intensities now common in southeast Michigan.

When a catastrophic event triggers simultaneous damage across multiple buildings or multiple floors of a single large structure, the loss quickly moves beyond what a single crew and a single truck can address. That threshold is where large loss response protocol begins.

## Our Large Loss Process in Clinton Township

Catastrophic restoration at scale requires a different operational structure than a standard residential water call. The first step is a rapid site assessment to establish scope: how many affected zones, what materials are involved, what the structural risk profile looks like, and whether any environmental hazards (asbestos-containing materials in older construction, for example) require abatement sequencing before restoration can proceed.

From there, Flood Solutions inc deploys a dedicated large loss project manager who serves as the single point of contact for the property owner, the carrier, and any third-party adjusters assigned to the file. Large loss adjusters work differently than residential claims handlers; they expect detailed documentation packages, moisture mapping, and scope narratives that align with Xactimate line items. We build that documentation from day one rather than reconstructing it after the fact.

Drying and extraction at commercial scale means deploying industrial desiccant dehumidification where refrigerant units hit their limits, high-CFM axial fans for structural cavities, and thermal imaging to confirm drying progress in wall assemblies and below concrete-encased plumbing chases. Progress is logged and reported on a schedule the adjuster and property manager can track.

## Insurance and Adjuster Coordination for Large Losses

Multi-million dollar restoration files attract a different level of carrier scrutiny than a standard homeowner claim. Large loss adjusters and independent appraisers are often brought in specifically to audit scope and pricing. Flood Solutions inc's project management approach is built around that reality: we document with the adjuster's review process in mind from the first day on site, not as an afterthought when a supplement is disputed.

For commercial property managers and building owners in Clinton Township, direct carrier billing and structured progress reporting reduce the administrative burden during what is already a disruptive period. ## Local Note

Clinton Township's postwar residential and light commercial construction era produced a significant amount of block and brick veneer construction with minimal drainage plane detailing by modern standards. When a catastrophic water event saturates these wall assemblies, surface readings from a pin meter can show acceptable numbers while the block cores and mortar joints remain wet for weeks. Large loss drying protocols here need to account for that hidden reservoir effect, using extended monitoring periods and cavity drying equipment rather than calling a job complete based on surface data alone. It is a detail that matters most on the larger commercial and multi-family structures where a premature close-out can lead to mold colonization inside wall cavities months after the restoration invoice is paid.
