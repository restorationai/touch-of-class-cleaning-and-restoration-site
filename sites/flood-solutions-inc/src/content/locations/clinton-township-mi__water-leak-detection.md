## Why Clinton Township Properties See Hidden Leak Problems

The township's housing stock tells the story clearly. A significant portion of the residential neighborhoods were built between the late 1950s and early 1980s, when copper supply lines and cast-iron drain stacks were standard. Copper develops pinhole leaks as it ages, especially where water chemistry is slightly acidic, a known characteristic of treated municipal water in this part of Macomb County. Those pinholes can weep inside a wall cavity or beneath a concrete slab for months before a stain appears on drywall or a water bill spikes.

Clay soil compounds the problem. Unlike sandy loam, clay holds moisture against a foundation rather than shedding it. After a wet spring thaw, the kind that comes through reliably in March and April, hydrostatic pressure can push water through hairline cracks in a slab, and that moisture then migrates laterally under flooring. A homeowner notices a soft spot in laminate or a musty smell in a finished basement long after the actual entry point has dried on the surface.

## Our Leak Detection Process in Clinton Township

The first step on every job is a pressure test of the supply system. By isolating zones and watching for pressure drop, the crew can confirm whether the leak is in a pressurized supply line or a drain and waste line, two very different problems requiring different tools. For supply leaks, electronic acoustic listening equipment picks up the vibration signature of water escaping under pressure, even through a concrete slab or behind plaster walls.

For slab leak detection specifically, thermal imaging cameras add a second layer of confirmation. A slab leak warms or cools the concrete surface in a pattern that's visible on a thermal scan, giving the technician a precise target before any concrete is touched. In Clinton Township's older ranch-style homes, where the slab may be 50 or 60 years old and the plumbing was not mapped in any surviving document, this non-invasive step saves significant time and avoids unnecessary demolition.

For water in walls, moisture meters and borescope cameras let the crew trace the migration path without opening every stud bay. The goal is always the smallest access point needed to make the repair, important in finished basements and tiled bathrooms where reconstruction costs add up fast.

## Working with Insurance on a Hidden Leak Claim

Hidden water leak claims are among the more contested in residential property insurance. Carriers often distinguish between sudden and accidental loss, which is typically covered, and long-term seepage, which may not be. The documentation Flood Solutions Inc produces during leak detection, moisture readings, thermal images, pressure test logs, and photographs of the point of failure, is exactly what an adjuster needs to classify the loss correctly. Bringing that evidence to the claim early reduces the back-and-forth that delays repair authorization.
