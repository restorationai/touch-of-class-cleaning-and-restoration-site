## Why Troy Industrial Properties Face Distinct Restoration Challenges

Oakland County's industrial base includes a dense mix of automotive suppliers, precision manufacturers, and large-format warehouses, many of them built in the 1970s and 1980s when construction standards for vapor barriers and roof drainage differed from today's codes. Flat or low-slope roofs on buildings from that era are prone to membrane fatigue, and a single compromised seam during a Michigan ice storm can introduce hundreds of gallons of water into a facility before anyone notices. That water doesn't behave the way it does in a residence: it follows concrete floor drains, wicks into pallet racking bases, saturates insulated wall panels, and can reach electrical panels and control systems before surface pooling becomes visible.

Fire events in industrial settings carry their own complexity. Smoke residue from burning synthetic materials, rubber components, or coated metals is chemically different from wood-smoke and requires different cleaning agents and dwell times. Soot can coat ductwork, settle into CNC equipment, and contaminate HVAC systems that circulate air across an entire production floor. Getting a facility back to operational condition means more than wiping surfaces; it means tracing contamination through the building's mechanical systems.

## Our Industrial Restoration Process in Troy

The first priority on any large-facility loss is a structured assessment that separates safe zones from affected zones. For water events, that means thermal imaging and moisture mapping across the full floor plate, not just the visible wet area. Concrete slabs in Michigan industrial buildings often sit on compacted fill over clay-heavy soil, and that substrate holds moisture. A slab that reads dry on the surface can be releasing vapor upward for weeks if the underlying fill is saturated, which matters for flooring reinstallation timelines and for any equipment that's sensitive to humidity.

For fire and smoke losses, the process begins with air quality documentation before any cleaning starts, so there's a baseline for what the facility contained and what needs to be cleared. Structural materials are assessed for heat damage, and the scope of content cleaning is determined by the type of residue and the sensitivity of the equipment involved. Industrial-grade HEPA filtration and negative air pressure containment keep unaffected areas of the building clean while remediation work proceeds in the loss zone.

Throughout the project, Flood Solutions inc coordinates directly with the facility's operations team to sequence work around production needs where possible. Phased restoration, where one section of a building is cleared and returned to use while another is still being dried or cleaned, is often the right approach for large Troy facilities that cannot afford a full shutdown.

## Local Note

Troy industrial buildings constructed before the mid-1980s frequently used fiberglass-faced wall panels in warehouse sections, and those panels behave unpredictably after a water event. The facing can appear undamaged while the fiberglass core has absorbed significant moisture, which doesn't show on a standard pin-type moisture meter. Flood Solutions inc uses non-invasive scanning alongside pin readings on panel-walled structures specifically because of this, since missing saturated panels is one of the more common causes of secondary mold growth in older Oakland County industrial facilities.
