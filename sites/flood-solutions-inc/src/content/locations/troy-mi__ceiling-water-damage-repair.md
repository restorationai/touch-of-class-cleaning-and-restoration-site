## Why Troy Ceilings Are Particularly Vulnerable

Troy's housing stock spans several decades and construction styles. Subdivisions built in the 1970s and 1980s, which make up a significant portion of the city's residential neighborhoods, were commonly built with drywall ceilings over wood-framed trusses. That combination absorbs water quickly but releases it slowly. By the time a stain is visible on the surface, the drywall above the paint layer is often already saturated, and the insulation batts in the attic space above may be holding several times their dry weight in water.

Upstairs bathroom supply lines and wax ring failures are another common culprit in two-story Troy homes. A slow leak from a toilet or a supply valve can travel along a subfloor for weeks before it finds a seam and drips through to the ceiling below. The damage visible on the ceiling is almost always smaller than the wet zone above it.

Oakland County's clay-heavy soil also affects drainage around foundations and window wells, and in heavy rain events, water that cannot percolate fast enough sometimes finds its way into attic spaces through soffit vents or deteriorated fascia, creating ceiling damage that looks like a roof leak but originates at the roofline perimeter.

## What the Repair Process Looks Like

The first priority when water is coming through a ceiling is stopping the source and protecting what's below. Before any repair work begins, the wet material has to be assessed. Moisture meters and thermal imaging cameras read what the eye cannot: a ceiling that looks dry around the edges of a stain often has a much larger wet footprint.

Once the extent of the damage is mapped, saturated drywall is removed. There is no shortcut here. Drywall that has been wet long enough to sag or bubble has lost structural integrity and cannot be dried in place reliably. The cavity above is inspected for wet insulation, which is also removed if saturated, because insulation holds moisture against framing and dramatically slows drying time.

Drying equipment, including air movers and dehumidifiers, is positioned to bring the framing and subfloor above down to acceptable moisture content before any new material goes up. Rushing this step is the most common reason ceiling repairs fail: new drywall installed over framing that still reads elevated moisture will show staining and paint failure within weeks.

Once the structure is confirmed dry, new drywall is hung, taped, mudded, and textured to match the existing ceiling. Matching texture is a craft step that matters in Troy homes where popcorn or orange-peel finishes were standard in the era of construction. The final painted surface should be indistinguishable from the surrounding ceiling.
