A slow drip behind a finished basement wall or beneath a concrete slab can go undetected for weeks, quietly saturating framing and insulation while your water bill climbs. ## Why Utica Properties See Hidden Leak Problems

Much of Utica's residential development happened in the 1960s through the 1980s, which means a significant share of homes are running on copper supply lines that are now 40 to 60 years old. Copper that age is prone to pinhole corrosion, particularly where the pipe contacts the alkaline clay soil common throughout Macomb County. That corrosion rarely announces itself. Instead, it seeps. The moisture wicks into the slab or migrates along the exterior of the pipe, and by the time a homeowner notices a damp patch on the floor or a musty smell in a finished room, the source can be several feet away from the visible symptom.

Utica also sees a meaningful number of slab-on-grade homes built without basements, which concentrates plumbing runs beneath the concrete pad. A slab leak in one of these homes is nearly impossible to locate by visual inspection alone. The surface reads dry while water moves laterally under the pour, following the path of least resistance through the subbase.

## Our Leak Detection Process in Utica

We do not open walls or break concrete until we know exactly where the leak is. The process starts with a pressure test on the supply lines to confirm whether the loss is on the hot side, cold side, or in the drain system. From there, we use acoustic listening equipment to isolate the sound signature of escaping water through the slab or behind finished surfaces. For leaks that are not producing enough flow to register acoustically, we supplement with thermal imaging, which maps temperature differentials in walls and floors caused by evaporating moisture.

In Utica's older ranch-style homes, where plumbing often runs through interior partition walls rather than exterior walls, thermal imaging is particularly useful because the insulation values are lower and the temperature contrast is more readable. Once we have a confirmed location, we mark it precisely so any excavation or access work is targeted, not exploratory.

One pattern we see consistently in Utica's slab-on-grade homes is that the gravel subbase beneath the concrete pad, which was standard construction practice in Macomb County through the 1970s, channels water horizontally much farther than homeowners expect. A pinhole leak under the utility room can push moisture 10 to 15 feet toward the living area before it finds a crack in the slab to rise through.
