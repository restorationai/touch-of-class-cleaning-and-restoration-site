## Why Chesterfield Homes See Water Heater Damage Differently

Chesterfield's housing stock leans heavily on slab-edge and shallow-crawl construction from the late 1980s through the early 2000s, with mechanical rooms often tucked into finished lower levels. When a tank-style water heater fails, whether from a corroded anode rod, a cracked tank, or a failed pressure relief valve, water follows the path of least resistance into wall cavities and under flooring systems that were never designed to drain. The clay-heavy soils common across Macomb County don't help: exterior drainage moves slowly, and any groundwater pressure against foundation walls can compound what started as an interior appliance leak.

Older tank-style heaters installed during the township's build-out years are also reaching the end of their service life. A 15- or 20-year-old unit that lets go during a cold stretch in January, when temperatures in Chesterfield regularly drop into the single digits, creates a secondary risk: the water spreads fast across cold concrete, and materials like OSB subfloor and fiberglass batt insulation begin absorbing moisture within the first hour.

## Our Water Heater Flood Cleanup Process in Chesterfield

The first priority on any water heater loss is stopping ongoing exposure. If the unit is still leaking, the cold-water supply valve to the heater gets shut before any equipment moves into position. From there, the process follows a structured sequence calibrated to the way Chesterfield homes are built.

Standing water is extracted using truck-mounted and portable extraction units, reaching into corners and under cabinetry where water pools against the foundation wall. Once surface water is removed, moisture mapping with thermal imaging and calibrated meters identifies where water has migrated behind drywall, under vinyl plank or tile, and into wall cavities adjacent to the mechanical room. This step matters more in finished lower levels, where water can travel 10 to 15 feet from the heater before it becomes visible.

Drying equipment, including refrigerant dehumidifiers and directional air movers, is positioned based on the moisture map rather than placed by guesswork. Progress readings are logged on a daily basis so the drying timeline is documentable, which matters when a claim is filed with a homeowner's insurance carrier.

## Working with Homeowner's Insurance on Water Heater Losses

A sudden water heater failure is typically a covered peril under standard homeowner's insurance policies, but the documentation the carrier requires can be detailed. Flood Solutions Inc photographs affected materials before any demolition, logs moisture readings at each inspection point, and provides a written scope of work that itemizes extraction, drying, and any material removal. That documentation package is what adjusters use to approve the claim, and having it organized from the start of the job reduces back-and-forth that can delay the drying process.

If the carrier sends an independent adjuster to the property, the crew can walk through the moisture map and equipment logs on-site.
