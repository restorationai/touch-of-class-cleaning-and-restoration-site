## Why Chesterfield Properties See Mold Problems

Macomb County's soil profile is dominated by dense glacial clay that drains slowly. After a heavy rain or a rapid spring thaw, that water has nowhere to go quickly, and it finds its way into foundation cracks, window wells, and sump pits. Homes built in Chesterfield during the 1970s and 1980s, which make up a large share of the township's residential inventory, were often constructed with block foundations that are more porous than poured concrete. Block mortar joints deteriorate over decades, and once moisture is inside the wall assembly, the paper facing on fiberglass batt insulation becomes a ready food source for mold.

The township's proximity to Lake St. Clair also means summer humidity stays elevated longer than it does further inland. Basements that feel dry in April can register elevated relative humidity by July without any active leak present, and that ambient moisture is enough to sustain mold growth on wood framing and drywall if the space is not mechanically dehumidified.

## Our Mold Remediation Process in Chesterfield

Remediation begins with a thorough inspection, not a guess. Moisture meters and thermal imaging identify where water has migrated beyond the visible stain, because mold follows moisture, not light. Once the affected zones are mapped, the work area is isolated with poly sheeting and negative air pressure to prevent spores from migrating into living spaces during removal.

Contaminated drywall, insulation, and wood materials that cannot be cleaned to a safe standard are removed and bagged for disposal. Structural surfaces that remain are treated with an EPA-registered antimicrobial solution. HEPA air scrubbers run continuously during and after removal to capture airborne spores. The area is not cleared until moisture readings return to normal and a final visual inspection confirms no visible mold remains. For Chesterfield homes where the HVAC system may have circulated spores, duct inspection is part of the standard scope.

## Chesterfield Insurance Coordination

Whether mold is covered depends heavily on the cause. A sudden, accidental water loss, such as a burst pipe, is typically covered under standard homeowner policies. Mold that developed from long-term seepage or deferred maintenance is more often excluded.
