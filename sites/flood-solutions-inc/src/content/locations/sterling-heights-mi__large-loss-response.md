## Why Sterling Heights Commercial Properties Face Outsized Catastrophic Loss Risk

The city's building stock tells the story. Much of Sterling Heights developed rapidly through the 1960s and 1970s to support the auto supply chain, and those decades produced large-footprint buildings with flat or low-slope roofing systems that were never designed to shed the kind of ice loads Michigan winters now regularly deliver. When a warm spell follows a deep freeze, meltwater backs up behind ice dams and finds its way through seams, penetrations, and aging membrane edges. A single breach in a 40,000-square-foot warehouse roof can release thousands of gallons before anyone notices ponding on the floor below.

The soil profile across this part of Macomb County is predominantly clay-heavy, which means poor drainage and a high water table during spring thaw. Parking lots and loading docks that appear dry at the surface can conceal saturated subgrade conditions that complicate structural drying and foundation stabilization after a flood event. For large loss adjusters reviewing a scope, that subsurface reality often adds line items that were not in the initial estimate.

## Our Large Loss Response Process in Sterling Heights

Catastrophic restoration at this scale is a project management discipline as much as a trades discipline. The first priority on any large loss is containment and documentation, stopping active damage while simultaneously building the evidentiary record that the insurance claim depends on. That means moisture mapping across every affected square foot, thermal imaging to identify hidden saturation behind walls and above ceilings, and photo and video documentation logged by zone before a single piece of debris is moved.

From there, the work moves in parallel rather than in sequence. Structural drying equipment is staged while demolition of unsalvageable material proceeds in adjacent zones. Temporary power, temporary climate control, and security boarding are coordinated so the building stays protected and the drying environment stays stable. Throughout, a dedicated project manager maintains a single point of contact for the property owner, the carrier, and any third-party consultants the adjuster brings in.

For Sterling Heights properties with active manufacturing or warehouse operations, we work with facility managers to sequence the work around operational needs wherever code and safety allow. Keeping even a portion of a facility running during restoration reduces business interruption losses, which matters to both the owner and the carrier.

## Local Note

Sterling Heights has a significant concentration of auto-tier and light manufacturing facilities built with tilt-up concrete panel construction, particularly in the industrial zones east of Van Dyke. These buildings present a specific challenge in catastrophic water loss events: the panel joints and embedded conduit runs create pathways for water to migrate laterally far beyond the visible wet zone. Standard moisture mapping protocols can underestimate the true affected area in tilt-up construction if the crew is not specifically looking for panel joint infiltration. On large loss jobs in these buildings, extended mapping along every panel seam is standard practice before the drying scope is finalized.
