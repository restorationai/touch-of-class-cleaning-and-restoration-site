## Why Sterling Heights Industrial Facilities Face Distinct Restoration Challenges

The city's manufacturing corridor developed rapidly through the 1970s and 1980s, which means a significant share of the industrial building stock is now 40 to 50 years old. Roof systems on structures from that era often use steel decking over bar joists with minimal insulation value, and when a roof-level water intrusion or fire suppression discharge soaks that assembly, moisture migrates laterally across the deck before it ever drips to the floor. By the time a facility manager notices standing water on the production floor, the steel above may have been wet for hours. Older vapor barriers under concrete slabs in this building vintage are also frequently degraded, which complicates drying timelines when water works its way into the slab.

Macomb County's clay-heavy soil adds another layer of complexity. During spring thaw or heavy rain events, hydrostatic pressure against below-grade walls and dock-level foundations can force water into areas that appear structurally sound. Facilities near the low-lying sections of Sterling Heights, where natural drainage is slower, tend to see this pattern more often than properties on higher ground closer to the county line.

## Our Industrial Restoration Process in Sterling Heights

The first step on any commercial or industrial loss is a scope assessment that accounts for what is running, what cannot be moved, and what the facility's insurance carrier will need to see documented. We photograph and measure affected areas before a single piece of equipment is staged, because industrial claims adjusters require that level of documentation to authorize large-scope work.

For water damage, industrial extraction is a different scale from residential work. High-capacity truck-mounted extraction units pull standing water from concrete floors while desiccant dehumidifiers handle the ambient moisture load that standard refrigerant units cannot manage in large open bays. Desiccant systems are particularly effective in Michigan winters, when ambient temperatures inside an unheated warehouse can drop below the dew point range where refrigerant dehumidifiers lose efficiency.

For fire and smoke damage, the chemistry of industrial smoke is more complex than residential. Burning plastics, lubricants, coatings, and synthetic materials leave residues that are corrosive to metal surfaces and electrical components. We assess affected machinery and structural steel for residue type before selecting cleaning agents, because the wrong chemistry on aluminum tooling or precision surfaces causes secondary damage that compounds the original loss.

Throughout the job, we coordinate directly with your operations team on sequencing, so restoration work can proceed in sections while other parts of the facility stay productive where safe to do so.

We document losses in a format that supports that review process, including moisture mapping reports, thermal imaging records, and itemized material assessments. ## Local Note

One pattern specific to Sterling Heights industrial properties: facilities built along the Mound Road corridor in the late 1970s frequently have original overhead crane rail systems embedded in the structural steel. When a fire or significant water event affects the roof structure, the crane rail assembly can trap moisture against the steel flange for weeks after surface materials appear dry. We flag these assemblies during scope and include them in moisture monitoring plans, because a rail system that corrodes internally after a loss can create a liability that shows up long after the restoration invoice is closed.
