When a basement full of storage totes, furniture, and photo albums takes on water near Anchor Bay, the instinct is to start pulling things out onto the driveway. In Chesterfield Township, where many homes sit close to Lake St. Clair's water table, that instinct often does more damage than the flood itself. Content recovery here means moving fast on inventory and drying before humidity and time turn a salvageable box of belongings into a total loss.

## Why Chesterfield Homes Lose More Contents Than They Should

Chesterfield's mix of older lakefront cottages and newer subdivisions built since the 1980s share one trait: finished or partially finished basements used as de facto storage rooms. A lot of homeowners keep seasonal furniture, holiday decorations, tools, and paper records in those lower levels. The township's proximity to Lake St. Clair keeps the water table high for much of the year, so basement seepage after heavy spring rain or a sump failure tends to spread across a wider footprint than it would in a home built on higher ground further inland. Once carpet padding, cardboard, and particleboard furniture sit in that moisture for more than a day or two, the contents start absorbing water faster than the structure around them dries.

## Our Content Recovery Process in Chesterfield

The first visit is almost always an inventory, not a cleanup. We photograph and log everything affected before it moves, which matters for the insurance claim and for your own peace of mind about what was actually in that basement. From there, items get triaged into three categories: things that dry in place, things that need off-site cleaning or ozone treatment, and things that are unfortunately beyond recovery. Upholstered furniture and electronics usually move to our facility for controlled drying, since trying to dry a couch or a server tower inside a damp Chesterfield basement just prolongs the moisture exposure. Paper documents and photographs get freeze-drying or air-drying depending on how saturated they are, and we keep a chain-of-custody log the whole time so nothing gets misplaced between pack-out and return.

## Chesterfield Insurance Coordination

Most homeowner policies in Macomb County cover contents damage differently than structural damage, and adjusters want to see a clear, itemized inventory before they approve replacement value on anything. We build that inventory as part of the job, not as an afterthought, which tends to speed up claim approval. If your policy has a sub-limit for basement contents (common in older Chesterfield lakefront properties that were insured before recent renovations), we flag that early so there aren't surprises when the claim is settled.

## Local note

One thing we watch for specifically in Chesterfield: homes built before the township's major subdivision growth often have cinder block basement walls with hairline cracks that only weep during high lake levels or after a hard spring thaw. Content damage in these basements can look minor at first glance, wet along one wall, but the humidity spike affects everything stored nearby within hours. We've learned to check moisture levels on adjacent boxes and furniture even when only one section of a basement looks visibly wet.
