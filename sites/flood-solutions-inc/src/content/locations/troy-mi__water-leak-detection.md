## Why Troy Properties See Hidden Leak Issues

Troy sits on a glacial lake plain where expansive clay soils shift with every frost cycle. That seasonal movement stresses supply lines, drain stacks, and the slab penetrations common in the split-level and ranch homes built heavily throughout the city during the 1960s and 1970s. Copper plumbing from that era is now past its typical service life in many of those homes, and pinhole corrosion leaks are a predictable result. The problem is compounded by finished basements, which Troy homeowners have long favored for added living space. Drywall and drop ceilings hide the mechanical systems behind them, so a weeping pipe fitting can saturate insulation and framing for an extended period before anyone notices a stain or a musty odor.

Troy also experiences hard winters where supply lines running through exterior walls or unheated crawl spaces are vulnerable to freezing and cracking. When temperatures rebound in late winter and early spring, those cracks begin to weep. Because the damage often appears weeks after the freeze event, homeowners frequently misattribute it to a new source rather than a pipe that fractured in January.

## Our Leak Detection Process in Troy

Accurate leak detection depends on non-destructive methods that pinpoint the source before any wall or slab is opened. The sequence matters:

**Pressure testing** isolates whether the loss is on the supply side or the drain side. A supply-side leak holds pressure differently than a drain leak, and that distinction shapes every step that follows.

**Acoustic listening equipment** amplifies the sound signature of water moving through or escaping a pipe. In Troy's slab-on-grade homes, this is often the first tool deployed, because the concrete transmits vibration in ways that allow a trained technician to triangulate the leak location within a narrow zone.

**Thermal imaging** reveals temperature differentials behind walls and beneath flooring. Wet insulation and saturated drywall hold temperature differently than dry material, making active leaks and recently dried areas visible on a thermal camera even when the surface looks normal.

**Moisture mapping** with calibrated meters then documents the full extent of saturation. This step is critical for insurance documentation and for making sure that any subsequent drying or repair scope covers the actual affected area, not just the visible damage.

Once the source is confirmed and mapped, the findings are documented with photographs and a written report that your plumber and insurance adjuster can both work from.

## Troy Insurance and Documentation

Most homeowner policies in Michigan cover sudden and accidental water discharge but exclude gradual leaks that went unreported. The distinction matters enormously, and it is one of the first things an adjuster will examine. A thorough leak detection report, one that documents when moisture readings were taken, what equipment was used, and what the findings showed, gives your carrier the evidence it needs to process a claim accurately. ## Local Note

Troy's split-level homes, which were built in large numbers during the late 1960s and into the 1970s, present a specific challenge for slab leak detection. The intermediate floor level in a split-level sits close to grade, and the concrete beneath it is often thinner than a full basement slab. Acoustic equipment picks up more ambient noise interference at that depth, particularly near mechanical rooms where furnace and water heater vibration competes with the leak signal. Technicians who work Troy regularly learn to take multiple listening points and cross-reference them with thermal imaging before committing to a location, rather than acting on a single acoustic reading.
