## Why Sterling Heights Properties See Flood Damage

The geology under Sterling Heights is largely glacial clay, which drains poorly and holds water near the surface long after a storm passes. During heavy spring rains or rapid snowmelt, that saturated ground has nowhere to put excess water except back through foundation cracks, window wells, and floor drains. Homes built across Sterling Heights from the 1960s through the 1980s were constructed with block or poured concrete basements that were never designed to handle the volume of water that increasingly intense Midwest storm events now deliver. Many of those basements have been finished over the decades, adding drywall, drop ceilings, and carpet over concrete floors, all of which absorb and trap flood water in ways that are not visible from the surface.

Sterling Heights also sits within a regional combined sewer system that serves a large portion of Macomb County. During significant rain events, that system can surcharge, pushing sewage-contaminated water back through floor drains. That distinction matters: a clean-water pipe burst and a sewer backup are two very different restoration problems, requiring different protective protocols, different drying strategies, and different documentation for insurance purposes.

## Our Flood Damage Restoration Process in Sterling Heights

The first step on any flood job is understanding what category of water entered the structure. Clean water from a supply line, gray water from an appliance, and black water from a sewer backup each require a different response, and misclassifying the source leads to incomplete remediation. Once the water source is identified and stopped, standing water is extracted using truck-mounted equipment capable of pulling hundreds of gallons quickly from finished and unfinished spaces alike.

After bulk extraction, the work shifts to what is not visible. Moisture meters and thermal imaging cameras map the spread of saturation through walls, subfloors, and framing. In Sterling Heights homes with block foundations, water frequently migrates laterally through the block cores before appearing on the interior face, meaning the wet zone often extends further than the visible water line suggests. Industrial air movers and dehumidifiers are positioned based on that moisture map, not just placed near the obvious wet areas. Readings are taken at each visit to track drying progress and adjust equipment placement until materials reach acceptable moisture levels.

Materials that cannot be dried in place, saturated carpet, compromised drywall, swollen baseboards, are removed carefully and documented before disposal. That documentation is not just good practice; it is what your insurance adjuster needs to process a contents and structural claim accurately.

## Sterling Heights Insurance Coordination

Flood damage claims in Sterling Heights often involve two separate policies: a standard homeowner's policy, which may cover sudden and accidental water damage from internal sources, and a separate flood insurance policy through the National Flood Insurance Program if the property carries one. Sewer backup coverage is frequently a rider that some homeowners have and others do not. Knowing which policy applies before the adjuster arrives matters, and having thorough documentation of the damage, photographs, moisture readings, and a written scope of affected materials, makes the difference between a smooth claim and a prolonged dispute. Flood Solutions Inc. documents losses in the format adjusters expect and communicates directly with carriers to keep the process moving.

## Local Note

Sterling Heights has a significant number of homes built with brick veneer exteriors over wood-framed walls. After a flood event that saturates the basement and lower floor system, that brick veneer can trap moisture against the sheathing behind it, slowing exterior drying considerably. Crews familiar with this construction pattern check moisture levels at the sheathing layer, not just the interior drywall face, because readings taken only from the inside can show acceptable numbers while the wall assembly behind the brick is still holding water. Missing that detail leads to callbacks and mold problems weeks after a job is closed.
