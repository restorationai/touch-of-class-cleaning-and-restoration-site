## Why Sterling Heights Properties See Water Damage Differently

The geology under Sterling Heights is the first thing that separates water damage here from losses in communities built on sandier ground. Macomb County's dense clay soil doesn't drain, it holds moisture against foundation walls and forces it toward any gap in a block or poured-concrete basement. After a heavy rain or a rapid thaw, that hydrostatic pressure builds quietly before it becomes visible. By the time water appears on a basement floor, it has often already been wicking into the base of drywall, the bottom plate of framing, and the underside of carpet padding for hours.

The era of construction matters too. Homes built in the 1960s and 1970s, common throughout Sterling Heights, frequently used fiberglass batt insulation in basement walls without a true vapor barrier behind it. That insulation holds water like a sponge and shows no outward sign of saturation until it's removed. Modern moisture meters and thermal imaging cameras reveal what the eye misses, which is why proper diagnostics matter before any drying equipment is placed.

## Our Water Damage Restoration Process in Sterling Heights

Every water loss is scoped before equipment is staged. A technician walks the affected area with a moisture meter and, where warranted, a thermal imaging camera to map the full boundary of the wet zone, not just the visible puddle. Standing water is extracted first using truck-mounted or portable extraction units capable of pulling water from carpet, padding, and hard flooring. Once bulk water is removed, the real drying work begins.

Structural drying in Sterling Heights homes requires attention to the building assembly. In a 1970s split-level with plaster-over-lath walls, for example, surface readings can show acceptable moisture levels while the lath behind the plaster stays saturated, because plaster releases moisture slowly and unevenly. Drying equipment is positioned to move air through wall cavities, not just across surfaces. Industrial-grade desiccant or refrigerant dehumidifiers pull moisture out of the air as evaporation occurs, and daily moisture readings track whether the drying curve is on target. Work isn't closed out until readings confirm the structure has returned to pre-loss moisture levels.

## Sterling Heights Insurance Coordination

Most homeowner policies in Michigan cover sudden and accidental water losses, a burst pipe, an appliance failure, an overflow, but exclude seepage and gradual leaks. The distinction matters because the cause of loss determines coverage, and documentation gathered in the first hours is what supports or undermines a claim later. Flood Solutions inc photographs affected materials, documents moisture readings, and prepares scope notes in a format that most major carriers recognize. That paperwork is handed to the homeowner and, when authorized, communicated directly to the adjuster so the process moves without the homeowner having to translate between the restoration crew and the insurance company.

## Local Note

In Sterling Heights, homes built on slab-on-grade foundations, particularly those constructed in the late 1970s and early 1980s in the city's western residential areas, present a specific challenge after a water loss: water that migrates under the slab is invisible to standard moisture meters reading from above. If flooring over a slab feels cool or shows efflorescence at grout lines after a loss, that's a signal to check sub-slab conditions before closing out the drying phase. Missing it means flooring goes back down over a wet slab, and the problem resurfaces within months as mold or buckled material.
