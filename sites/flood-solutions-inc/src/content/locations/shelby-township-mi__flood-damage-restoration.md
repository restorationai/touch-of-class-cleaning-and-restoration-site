## Why Shelby Township Properties See Flood Damage Issues

The dense glacial clay that underlies most of Shelby Township drains poorly. During heavy rain events, that clay becomes nearly impermeable, and surface water has little choice but to find the lowest point on your property, which is often a window well, a foundation crack, or a sump pit that is already overwhelmed. Homes built in the 1970s and 1980s, which make up a large share of Shelby Township's residential stock, were typically constructed with block-wall foundations rather than poured concrete. Block foundations are more porous by nature, and mortar joints that have aged 40-plus years can allow water intrusion even without a visible crack.

Macomb County also sits in a region where lake-effect moisture from Lake St. Clair can extend and intensify storm systems that might otherwise pass quickly. A rain event that drops two inches in an hour is not unusual in late spring or early fall, and that kind of intensity is exactly what overwhelms residential drainage infrastructure.

## Our Flood Damage Restoration Process in Shelby Township

Every flood cleanup starts with a thorough moisture assessment, not just of the visible standing water but of the materials that absorbed it. Water behind a finished basement wall or beneath a laminate floor can read dry at the surface while the subfloor or insulation behind it stays saturated. We use thermal imaging and calibrated moisture meters to map the actual wet zone before any drying equipment is placed.

From there, the process moves in a deliberate sequence:

- **Water extraction** using truck-mounted and portable extraction units to remove standing water from flooring, carpet, and low-lying cavities.
- **Controlled demolition** where necessary, removing wet drywall, insulation, or flooring that cannot be dried in place and would otherwise become a mold substrate.
- **Structural drying** using industrial air movers and dehumidifiers sized to the actual cubic footage of the affected space, not a one-size estimate.
- **Moisture monitoring** over the drying period to confirm materials reach acceptable levels before any reconstruction begins.
- **Documentation** of all affected materials, moisture readings, and equipment logs, a record that matters when you file an insurance claim.

## Shelby Township Insurance Coordination

Flood damage claims in Michigan can be complicated by the distinction between sewer backup coverage and standard homeowner's water damage coverage, they are often separate endorsements, and many Shelby Township homeowners do not realize which one applies until they are already in the middle of a claim. We document the loss thoroughly from the start: photographs of the water source and entry point, moisture readings at the time of arrival, and a written scope of affected materials. That documentation package is what carriers need to process a claim efficiently, and we provide it regardless of which insurer is involved.

## Local Note

Shelby Township's block-wall basements from the 1970s and 1980s have a particular drying challenge that does not apply to newer poured-wall construction. The hollow cores of concrete block can hold water inside the wall cavity itself, invisible to a surface moisture reading. If a block wall has been wet for more than a day or two, we probe the cores directly and factor that retained moisture into the drying plan. Skipping that step is one of the most common reasons a basement that appears dry still smells musty three weeks later.
