## Why Utica Properties See Flood Damage

The geology under Utica and the surrounding Macomb County corridor is largely dense glacial till, a soil type that sheds water rather than absorbing it. During heavy rain events, that runoff has nowhere to go quickly, and it finds the path of least resistance: window wells, foundation cracks, and floor drains connected to aging municipal laterals. Homes built in the 1970s and 1980s, which make up a large share of Utica's residential stock, often have block foundation walls rather than poured concrete. Block foundations are more porous, and water migrates through the mortar joints long before it shows up as a visible seam on the floor. By the time a homeowner notices standing water, the block cores and the framing above the sill plate may already be saturated.

Utica also sits within the Clinton River watershed. During significant storm events, the river system backs up tributary drainage, and neighborhoods near low-lying streets can experience flooding that has nothing to do with a failed sump or a broken pipe. That kind of flood carries silt and debris, which changes the cleanup scope compared to a clean-water appliance leak.

## Our Flood Cleanup Process in Utica

The first step on any flood job is understanding what category of water entered the structure. Clean water from a supply line behaves differently than gray or black water from a backed-up drain or a river overflow. That distinction drives every decision about what materials can be dried in place and what has to be removed.

After the water source is confirmed and stopped, extraction begins. Standing water is pulled with truck-mounted or portable extraction units, and then the slower work starts: identifying moisture that has wicked into wall cavities, subfloor assemblies, and insulation. In Utica's older ranch-style and split-level homes, the subfloor is often tongue-and-groove planking over floor joists, and that material holds water in the seams even after the surface reads dry on a pin-type meter. Thermal imaging and calibrated moisture meters together give a more complete picture of what is actually wet.

Drying equipment, including industrial air movers and refrigerant dehumidifiers, is placed based on the moisture map, not by a standard formula. Daily monitoring tracks whether the drying is progressing on schedule. Affected materials that cannot be dried to standard are removed, documented, and inventoried for the insurance claim.

## Utica Insurance Coordination

Flood damage claims in Michigan involve a distinction that catches homeowners off guard: standard homeowners policies typically exclude rising groundwater and surface flooding, while sudden and accidental discharge from a plumbing failure is usually covered. Knowing which cause of loss applies matters from the first hour, because the documentation strategy differs. Flood Solutions Inc. photographs affected areas, records moisture readings, and prepares scope documentation in a format that adjusters can work with directly. If a homeowner carries separate flood insurance through the National Flood Insurance Program, the documentation requirements follow NFIP guidelines, and the team is familiar with that process.

## Local Note

Utica's block-wall basements present a specific drying challenge that does not come up as often in communities with newer poured-concrete construction. The hollow cores in concrete masonry units trap water and release it slowly, which means a basement can appear to be drying on schedule while the block wall itself remains saturated well into the second week. Crews working in Utica account for this by extending monitoring intervals and positioning dehumidifiers to draw moisture out of the wall assembly, not just the air. Skipping that step is one of the more common reasons a Utica homeowner ends up with mold growth weeks after a flood cleanup that seemed finished.
