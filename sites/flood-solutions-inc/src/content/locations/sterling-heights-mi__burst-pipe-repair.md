## Why Sterling Heights Properties See Burst Pipe Issues

Macomb County's clay-heavy soil doesn't drain quickly, which means groundwater pressure against foundation walls stays elevated long after a storm or a rapid thaw. But burst pipes in Sterling Heights are typically an interior problem, not an exterior one. The city grew rapidly through the 1960s and 1970s, and a large share of the housing stock from that era uses copper supply lines that have now been in service for 50 or 60 years. Copper work-hardens over decades of thermal cycling. A line that has expanded and contracted through hundreds of Michigan winters eventually reaches a point where a single hard freeze is enough to split it.

Homes built in that era also tend to have plumbing routed through exterior walls and uninsulated garage ceilings, two locations where pipe temperatures drop fastest during a cold snap. When the break happens, water doesn't announce itself with a dramatic spray. It often runs silently behind drywall or into a drop ceiling before the pressure drop registers on a homeowner's water meter.

## What the Cleanup and Repair Process Looks Like

The first priority when we arrive is stopping active water movement. That means confirming the main shutoff has been closed and verifying no secondary supply lines are still pressurized. From there, the process follows a defined sequence:

**Extraction and containment.** Standing water is removed with truck-mounted extraction equipment. Any water that has migrated under flooring or into wall cavities is located using thermal imaging and moisture meters, surface readings alone miss the saturation that drives mold growth later.

**Controlled demolition.** Saturated drywall, insulation, and flooring that cannot be dried in place are removed. In older Sterling Heights homes with plaster-and-lath construction, this step requires more precision: plaster releases moisture more slowly than modern drywall, and cutting too conservatively leaves wet substrate behind.

**Structural drying.** Commercial dehumidifiers and air movers are placed to dry the structural framing to the IICRC S500 standard. In Macomb County winters, outdoor air is too cold and dry to assist, we rely entirely on equipment-driven drying, and monitoring logs are kept daily.

**Pipe repair coordination.** We coordinate with a licensed plumber for the actual pipe repair so that the water line is restored before we close any opened wall cavities. Reconstruction follows once moisture readings confirm the structure is dry.
