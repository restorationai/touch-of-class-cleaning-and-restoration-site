## Why Shelby Township Properties Experience Hidden Leak Problems

The township's housing stock spans several distinct eras, and each brings its own leak vulnerabilities. Subdivisions built through the 1970s and 1980s frequently used copper supply lines that are now reaching the end of their service life. Pinhole corrosion is common in copper that has spent decades in contact with Macomb County's moderately hard water, and those pinholes rarely announce themselves with a dramatic burst. Instead, water seeps behind a cabinet or into a wall cavity for months before a homeowner notices a spike on the DTE Energy bill or a musty smell in a finished basement.

Slab-on-grade construction, which is widespread in Shelby Township's ranch-style and split-level homes, adds another layer of complexity. A slab leak here does not just mean wet concrete. The clay substrate beneath the slab holds moisture and transmits hydrostatic pressure, which can accelerate the spread of water under the foundation and cause secondary damage to framing and insulation long before the leak is visible at the surface.

## How We Detect Leaks in Shelby Township Homes

Accurate detection starts with ruling out the obvious before touching a wall or cutting into a slab. The process typically begins with a pressure test on the supply system to confirm a leak is present and to isolate it to a specific zone. From there, the approach depends on what the home is telling us.

For slab leaks, electronic amplification equipment picks up the sound of water escaping under pressure through concrete. Thermal imaging adds a second layer of confirmation by mapping temperature differentials on the slab surface. Water moving through a crack or a corroded fitting carries heat, and that thermal signature is readable even when the slab looks completely dry.

For leaks inside walls, moisture meters and thermal cameras work together. A wall that reads dry at the surface can still show a thermal gradient that points to a wet stud bay or a saturated bottom plate. Pinpointing the source before opening a wall keeps the repair footprint small and the disruption manageable.

Shelby Township's finished basements deserve particular attention during any leak investigation.
