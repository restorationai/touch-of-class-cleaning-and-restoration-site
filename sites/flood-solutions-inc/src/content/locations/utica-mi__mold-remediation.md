## Why Utica Properties See Mold Issues

Macomb County's clay soil drains poorly compared to sandier profiles found further west in the state. After a heavy rain or snowmelt, that water has nowhere to go quickly, so it migrates toward the path of least resistance: basement walls, crawl spaces, and slab edges. Utica's housing stock includes a significant number of ranch-style and split-level homes built during the postwar boom of the 1950s through the 1970s. Many of those homes were built with block or poured concrete foundations that have aged past their original waterproofing lifespan. Hairline cracks, failing mortar joints, and deteriorated window-well drainage are common entry points for the chronic low-level moisture that mold needs to thrive.

Attic mold is another pattern worth knowing. Homes in this part of Macomb County often have soffit and ridge ventilation systems that were undersized by today's standards. When warm, humid interior air rises into an underventilated attic in winter, condensation accumulates on the sheathing, and black or green mold colonies can develop across the roof deck without any visible leak below.

## Our Mold Remediation Process in Utica

Every remediation starts with a thorough inspection, not a visual scan. Moisture meters and thermal imaging cameras reveal saturation behind walls and under flooring that looks completely dry at the surface. Once the scope is mapped, the affected area is sealed with physical containment barriers and placed under negative air pressure using HEPA-filtered air scrubbers. This prevents spores from migrating to unaffected rooms during the removal work.

Contaminated materials, whether drywall, insulation, or wood framing, are removed, bagged, and disposed of according to Michigan Department of Environment, Great Lakes, and Energy (EGLE) guidelines. Remaining structural surfaces are cleaned with EPA-registered antimicrobial solutions and allowed to dry to verified moisture levels before any reconstruction begins. Skipping that drying verification step is the single most common reason mold returns after a remediation, and it is a step that cannot be rushed.

## Local Note

One detail that comes up specifically in Utica's older ranch homes: the original vapor barriers installed in crawl spaces during the 1960s and 1970s were typically thin polyethylene sheeting, and decades of foot traffic and settling have left them torn and ineffective. When remediation crews open a crawl space in these homes, they frequently find mold on the floor joists above a barrier that looks intact from the access hatch but is actually fragmented underneath. A proper remediation in these properties includes replacing that vapor barrier entirely, not just treating the wood above it, because leaving a compromised barrier in place recreates the same moisture conditions within a season or two.
