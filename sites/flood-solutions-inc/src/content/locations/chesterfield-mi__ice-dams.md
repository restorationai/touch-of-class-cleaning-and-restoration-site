Lake-effect snow off Lake St. Clair doesn't just pile up in Chesterfield Township, it freezes and thaws in cycles that most inland Michigan communities never see. That freeze-thaw churn is exactly what builds ice dams along the eaves of ranch and split-level homes throughout the township, and once that ridge of ice forms, meltwater has nowhere to go but backward, under the shingles and into the attic insulation below.

## Why Chesterfield Properties See Ice Dam Issues

Chesterfield Township's proximity to the lake means heavier, wetter snow loads and more frequent thaw cycles than communities further from the shoreline. Add in the housing stock: a large share of homes built from the 1960s through the 1980s with shallow roof pitches and minimal attic ventilation by today's standards, and you get roofs that hold heat unevenly. Warm air escapes from the living space into the attic, melts the underside of the snowpack, and that water refreezes right at the eave where the roof overhangs unheated soffits. The result is a dam that backs water up under the shingle line, often before anyone notices a drip inside. Homes near the I-94 corridor and along the older side streets off Sugarbush and Baker Road tend to run into this more than newer construction with upgraded attic baffles and ridge venting, simply because the insulation and ventilation haven't been touched since the roof was first shingled.

## Our Ice Dam Process in Chesterfield

We start by getting the water stopped and the ice managed safely, usually with steam equipment rather than chipping or salt, both of which can damage shingles and gutters that are already stressed by the ice load. From there we move inside to check the attic and top-floor ceilings for water intrusion, since ice dam damage is often worse than it looks from the ground. Wet insulation gets pulled and the deck and rafters are dried in place using the same moisture-mapping approach we'd use on any water loss, because saturated wood framing left alone becomes a mold conversation by spring. Once the structure is dry, we document the ventilation and insulation gaps that let the dam form in the first place, which matters both for your repair plan and for your insurance adjuster.

That matters during a hard freeze week when several homes in the same subdivision can call within a day or two of each other, since the same storm system and the same lake-effect pattern hits everyone's roofline at once. ## Chesterfield Insurance Coordination

Most homeowner policies treat sudden ice dam water intrusion as covered water damage, but adjusters want to see clear documentation that the loss was sudden and not the result of long-term neglected maintenance. We photograph the ice formation, the point of entry, and the interior damage before any drying work starts, and we put together a written scope that separates storm-related damage from any pre-existing roof or ventilation issues. That distinction is often the difference between a smooth claim and a drawn-out back-and-forth with the carrier, so we build the paperwork to hold up from the first call.

## Local note

One thing we watch for specifically in this township: homes with finished bonus rooms over the garage, common in several of the split-level layouts built here in the 1970s, often have a ventilation gap right where the garage roofline meets the main house. That seam is a common spot for ice dams to form even when the rest of the roof looks fine, and it's easy to miss on a quick exterior look from the driveway.
