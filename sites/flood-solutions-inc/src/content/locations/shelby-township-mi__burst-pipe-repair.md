## Why Shelby Township Properties See Burst Pipe Damage

Shelby Township's housing stock spans several decades, and that range matters when a pipe breaks. Subdivisions developed in the 1970s and 1980s frequently used copper supply lines that have now accumulated decades of thermal cycling. Repeated freeze-thaw stress work-hardens copper over time, making pinhole failures and full splits more likely in older runs, particularly in sections that pass through exterior walls or above uninsulated garage spaces, which are common in the ranch and colonial layouts that dominate this part of Macomb County.

The township's clay-heavy soil also plays a role. Poor drainage around foundations means water from a broken outdoor line or a supply line near the foundation can saturate the ground against the slab or basement wall before it ever surfaces inside. Homeowners sometimes see the water damage indoors first and assume the source is internal, when the actual break is outside or in a crawl space.

Winter pipe failures here also tend to cluster after the first hard cold snap of the season, when pipes that were borderline all fall finally give way. A burst that happens overnight can mean four to eight hours of undetected flow before anyone is up and moving through the house.

## Our Burst Pipe Cleanup Process in Shelby Township

The first priority on any burst pipe call is stopping the water. If the homeowner has not already shut off the main, that happens before anything else. From there, the work breaks into distinct phases that cannot be rushed without creating bigger problems later.

Extraction comes first. Standing water is removed with truck-mounted or portable extraction equipment, depending on access. Shelby Township homes with finished basements, a very common configuration in the subdivisions along the township's western corridors, often require moving stored belongings and pulling carpet before extraction can reach the subfloor. We document everything before it moves.

After extraction, moisture mapping tells us where the water actually went, not just where it pooled. Thermal imaging and calibrated moisture meters read behind drywall, under flooring, and inside wall cavities. Water follows gravity and the path of least resistance, so the wet zone is almost always larger than it looks.

Drying is then set up with industrial air movers and dehumidifiers sized to the affected area. Readings are logged on a schedule until materials reach target moisture levels. Drywall that reads dry at the surface can still hold moisture at the paper backing or the stud behind it, we do not call a space dry until the meters confirm it at depth.

Once the structure is dry, the repair side of the work begins: replacing damaged drywall, subfloor sections, insulation, and any finish materials that could not be salvaged.

## Insurance Coordination for Shelby Township Homeowners

Most standard homeowner policies in Michigan cover sudden and accidental water damage from a burst pipe, but the language around what qualifies matters. A pipe that failed due to a slow freeze over several days can be characterized differently than one that split in a single cold event. We photograph the break, document the temperature record if relevant, and write a detailed scope of loss that gives your adjuster a clear picture of what happened and what it will take to restore the property.

We bill carriers directly and communicate with adjusters throughout the process so you are not managing paperwork while also managing a damaged home.

## Local Note

Shelby Township's newer subdivisions, particularly those built in the 1990s and early 2000s, often used PVC or CPVC for interior supply runs rather than copper. CPVC is more brittle at very low temperatures than copper, and when it fails, it tends to crack along longer sections rather than at a single point. That means the wet zone from a CPVC burst can be spread across more linear feet of wall cavity than a homeowner expects. If your home was built during that era and you have had a pipe break, plan for a wider moisture survey than a single-room inspection would suggest.
