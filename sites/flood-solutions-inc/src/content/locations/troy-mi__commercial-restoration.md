## Why Troy Commercial Properties Face Distinct Restoration Challenges

Oakland County's climate does real work on commercial buildings. Winter temperatures routinely drop far enough to freeze supply lines in unheated mechanical rooms, and the rapid warm-up that follows a cold snap is when burst-pipe calls spike. Flat-roof commercial construction, common across Troy's office parks and retail strips, is especially vulnerable: ponding water from ice dams or clogged drains can push hundreds of gallons into ceiling assemblies before a tenant notices a stain.

Fire risk has its own commercial pattern here. Many of Troy's older flex-industrial buildings along the M-59 and Big Beaver corridors were built before modern suppression systems were standard. When a fire does occur in one of those structures, the smoke and soot travel through shared HVAC ductwork and reach tenant suites that never saw a flame. Cleaning that secondary contamination is often more labor-intensive than addressing the origin room.

Building age also shapes mold exposure. Structures built in the 1970s and 1980s frequently used fiberglass batt insulation in exterior wall cavities, which holds moisture without showing obvious surface growth. A slow roof leak or a failed window seal can feed a mold colony for months before it becomes visible, and by then it has typically migrated into adjacent drywall and framing.

## Our Commercial Restoration Process in Troy

Every commercial loss starts with a scope assessment that accounts for occupancy. A retail tenant mid-season cannot be treated the same as a vacant office suite. The first step is containment and documentation: moisture mapping with thermal imaging and pin meters, air quality readings where smoke or mold is suspected, and a written damage inventory that your insurance adjuster can work from directly.

Drying and dehumidification in commercial spaces requires industrial-scale equipment, not the residential units that get carried in the back of a van. Large desiccant dehumidifiers, high-velocity air movers, and negative-air machines for containment zones are staged based on the building's square footage and construction type, not a one-size formula. Progress is logged daily so that the drying record is defensible if coverage is disputed.

For fire and smoke losses, content pack-out is often the right call for businesses with equipment, inventory, or documents that cannot be exposed to ongoing soot off-gassing. Structural cleaning follows a sequence: HVAC systems and ductwork first, then hard surfaces, then porous materials that may need replacement rather than cleaning.

Reconstruction is coordinated with your property manager and any relevant municipal permits through the City of Troy's Building Department. Getting inspections scheduled promptly matters, because a delayed inspection can hold up a certificate of occupancy and extend your business interruption claim.

## Local Note

Troy's commercial buildings from the late 1970s and early 1980s were frequently constructed with a vapor barrier configuration that made sense for the energy standards of that era but creates a problem during drying: moisture driven inward by dehumidification can become trapped between the barrier and the exterior sheathing. Crews working in those buildings need to account for that layer when placing equipment and reading moisture readings, because surface readings at the drywall face can show dry while the cavity behind it remains saturated. It is a detail that shows up repeatedly in Troy's older office and flex-industrial stock and one that changes the drying timeline meaningfully.
