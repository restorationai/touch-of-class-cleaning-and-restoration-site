## Why Rochester Hills Properties See Mold Issues

The clay-rich soils across much of Oakland County do not drain the way sandy or loam-based soils do. After a heavy rain or a rapid snowmelt, water pools against foundation walls and finds its way into block or poured-concrete basements through hairline cracks, mortar joints, and floor-wall seams. Rochester Hills has a substantial inventory of homes built between the late 1960s and the mid-1990s, many of which used fiberglass batt insulation in basement rim joists and crawl spaces. That insulation holds moisture against wood framing without ever showing visible wetness on the interior surface, creating exactly the dark, damp, low-airflow environment where mold thrives.

Attic mold is another pattern that shows up frequently in this area. Inadequate soffit-to-ridge ventilation, combined with bathroom exhaust fans vented into attic space rather than through the roof, drives humidity into the sheathing through the winter months. By spring, black or green staining on the OSB or plywood is often the first sign a homeowner notices.

## Our Mold Inspection and Testing Process in Rochester Hills

A thorough assessment starts before any sample is collected. A visual inspection of the full structure, including the attic, basement, and any crawl space, identifies moisture intrusion points, staining patterns, and areas where building materials show swelling, discoloration, or deterioration. Moisture meters and thermal imaging help locate hidden wet zones that look fine on the surface.

Once suspect areas are identified, air samples and surface samples are collected following established industry protocols. Air samples capture what occupants are actually breathing, comparing spore counts and species indoors against an outdoor baseline. Surface samples from staining or visible growth identify the specific genera present, which matters because different mold types carry different health implications and respond differently to remediation approaches.

All samples are sent to an accredited third-party laboratory. The written report documents findings, sample results, and affected areas in enough detail to support an insurance claim or guide a remediation contractor on exactly where work is needed and how to verify it is complete.

## Local Note: What Rochester Hills Homes Often Hide

Homes built in Rochester Hills during the 1970s and 1980s frequently used exterior foam board insulation sandwiched between the brick veneer and the wood-framed wall. When that cavity gets wet from a failed weep screed or a cracked mortar joint, the foam traps moisture against the sheathing and the wall stays wet for weeks without any interior sign. During a mold inspection in this type of construction, probing the wall cavity with a pin-type moisture meter at the base of exterior walls often reveals elevated readings that a non-invasive scan alone would miss. It is a detail that matters in this particular housing stock and shapes how samples are prioritized during the assessment.
