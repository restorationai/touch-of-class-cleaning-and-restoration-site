## Why Rochester Hills Properties See Significant Storm Damage

The glacially deposited clay soils common across Oakland County shed water rather than absorbing it, which means heavy rain events push runoff toward foundations and window wells faster than most homeowners expect. Roof systems on the ranch-style and colonial homes built throughout Rochester Hills during the 1970s and 1980s often have original or once-replaced decking that has absorbed decades of freeze-thaw cycles. When a storm drops a large limb or drives wind-driven rain under aging shingles, the decking can fail in ways that aren't visible from the attic until water has already tracked down interior wall cavities. Brick veneer construction, common in this part of Macomb and Oakland County, can mask moisture intrusion at the mortar line for days before it shows up as staining or efflorescence indoors.

## Our Storm Damage Restoration Process in Rochester Hills

The first priority after any storm event is stopping the bleeding. The crew begins with emergency tarping and board-up to close the structure against additional weather exposure, because a second rain event on an already-compromised roof can double the interior damage. Once the structure is stabilized, the team does a systematic moisture mapping of every affected area, using thermal imaging and pin-type meters to find water that has tracked beyond the visible damage zone. Wet insulation between floor joists and inside wall cavities reads dry at the surface long after it has saturated the framing behind it, so the inspection goes deeper than a visual walkthrough.

Debris removal comes next: storm-damaged materials, broken glass, compromised roofing, and any structural elements that cannot be dried in place. Industrial drying equipment is staged to move air through the building envelope efficiently, and readings are logged daily so the drying curve can be documented for your insurance carrier. Reconstruction planning starts while drying is still active, so there is no gap between mitigation and the repair work that returns your home to its pre-storm condition.
