## Why Utica Properties See Sewage Backup Issues

Much of Utica's residential development happened in the postwar decades, with a second wave of subdivision growth through the 1970s and 1980s. That means a significant portion of the housing stock is running on cast-iron or Orangeburg sewer laterals that are now 40 to 60 years old. These materials crack, root-intrude, and collapse in ways that PVC pipe simply does not. When a lateral fails, the backup typically hits the lowest drain in the house first, which in most Utica ranch and split-level homes means the basement floor drain or a first-floor laundry tub.

Macomb County's clay-heavy soil compounds the problem. Clay does not drain freely, so during a heavy rain event the ground around a sewer lateral becomes saturated quickly. That saturated soil increases hydrostatic pressure against the pipe, and any existing crack or offset joint becomes a point of failure. Septic systems in the less-sewered pockets of Utica face the same clay drainage limitation, with drain fields that can become overwhelmed after back-to-back wet weather.

## Our Sewage Cleanup and Sanitization Process in Utica

Sewage water is classified as Category 3 contamination, the most hazardous tier in the restoration industry's water damage classification system. That classification drives every decision we make on-site.

The process starts with containment. Before extraction begins, affected areas are isolated to prevent cross-contamination to unaffected parts of the home. Porous materials that have absorbed raw sewage, including drywall, carpet, and certain types of insulation, are removed rather than dried in place. Drying contaminated porous material is not remediation; it only concentrates the pathogens.

After bulk removal and extraction, the structural surfaces that remain, concrete block foundation walls, poured slab floors, floor joists, are treated with EPA-registered disinfectants rated for sewage contamination. We apply these in the correct dwell time and concentration, not as a surface rinse. Structural drying follows disinfection, with moisture readings taken at multiple depths to confirm the assembly is dry before any reconstruction begins.

For homes on Utica's municipal sewer system, we also document the backup point and recommend a licensed plumber perform a camera inspection of the lateral before the job is closed out. A cleaned basement is not a solved problem if the pipe that caused the backup is still compromised.

## Utica Insurance and Claim Coordination

Sewage backup coverage is one of the more misunderstood parts of a standard homeowner's policy. Most base policies in Michigan do not automatically cover sewer or drain backup; that protection typically requires a specific endorsement. If you are unsure whether your policy includes it, the declarations page will list it as a separate line item, often called "water backup" or "sewer backup" coverage.

We document every phase of a sewage cleanup job in a format that supports an insurance claim: photographs before removal, moisture readings, material quantities, and post-disinfection surface readings. That documentation goes to your adjuster directly, which reduces the back-and-forth that can delay a claim settlement.

## Local Note

One pattern we see consistently in Utica's older ranch-style homes is a floor drain in the basement utility room that was never fitted with a backflow preventer. These drains were standard practice in mid-century construction, but without a check valve or standpipe cap, they become the first and easiest path for a sewer backup to enter the living space. If your home was built before roughly 1985 and you have never had that drain inspected or fitted with a backflow device, it is worth asking a licensed plumber about it before the next heavy rain season. It is a low-cost fix relative to the cost of a sewage cleanup job.
