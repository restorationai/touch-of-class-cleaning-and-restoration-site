## Why Clinton Township Properties See Water Damage Issues

The geology here is the starting point. Macomb County's clay-dominant soil has a low permeability rate, it doesn't drain quickly, so saturated ground during a heavy spring thaw or a summer storm system pushes hydrostatic pressure against basement walls and floor slabs. Older ranch-style homes throughout Clinton Township, many built during the post-war suburban expansion of the 1950s through 1970s, often have block foundation walls that are especially vulnerable to that pressure. Mortar joints in block foundations can wick moisture even when there's no visible crack.

The sump pump is the first line of defense in most of these homes, and it's also the most common point of failure. A power outage during a storm, or simply a pump that has reached the end of its service life, can flood a finished basement in under an hour. Older plumbing in homes from that era, including galvanized steel supply lines, is also prone to pinhole leaks and sudden failures that go unnoticed inside wall cavities until the damage is significant.

## Our Water Damage Restoration Process in Clinton Township

The process starts with a thorough moisture assessment before any equipment is staged. Water behaves differently depending on what it's traveled through, a leak behind a plaster wall in an older Clinton Township home reads very differently on a moisture meter than water absorbed into modern OSB sheathing. Thermal imaging helps locate pockets of saturation that aren't visible at the surface, particularly in finished basements where water migrates under vinyl plank flooring or behind drop ceilings.

Once the scope is mapped, extraction comes first. Truck-mounted and portable extraction units pull standing water and pull it from carpet padding and subfloor materials before drying equipment is placed. Structural drying follows using commercial-grade dehumidifiers and air movers positioned to create a controlled drying environment. Moisture readings are logged at each visit so progress is documented, this matters both for drying decisions and for the insurance file. Affected materials that cannot be dried in place, such as saturated insulation or swollen wood framing, are removed to prevent secondary damage.

## Local Note

One pattern that comes up repeatedly in Clinton Township's older ranch homes: the basement floor drain. Many of these homes have floor drains that tie into the sanitary sewer, and during a municipal sewer backup event, which Macomb County does experience during heavy rain events that overwhelm the combined system, water can enter the basement through that drain rather than through the foundation wall. The source matters because it changes the water category for restoration purposes. Water from a sewer backup is categorically different from a clean supply line failure, and the cleaning and drying protocols differ accordingly. If you're not sure where the water came from, that's part of what the initial assessment is designed to determine.
