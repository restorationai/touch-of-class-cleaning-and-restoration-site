## Why Clinton Township Properties See Distinctive Fire Damage Patterns

The dominant housing stock across Clinton Township runs from postwar brick ranches to two-story colonials built through the 1970s and 1980s, with a significant wave of construction through the 1990s. That mix matters for fire damage in a few concrete ways.

Older homes often have plaster-and-lath wall systems rather than modern drywall. Smoke particulates and soot penetrate plaster differently, working into the porous surface and settling in cavities behind the lath. What looks like surface discoloration on a plaster wall can represent deep odor contamination that outlasts a simple wipe-down by weeks. Attached garages, common throughout Clinton Township's residential neighborhoods, create a direct pathway for smoke and combustion byproducts to reach living spaces through shared walls and HVAC systems.

Michigan's climate adds another layer. The region's humidity in spring and fall means that water used in firefighting does not evaporate quickly. Structural materials stay wet longer, and when soot-laden moisture sits in wall cavities or under flooring, secondary mold growth can begin within 48 to 72 hours of the fire event. Restoration that addresses only the visible fire damage without accounting for moisture intrusion leaves a property vulnerable to a second round of damage.

## Our Fire Damage Restoration Process in Clinton Township

The first step on any fire loss is a thorough assessment, not just of charred or visibly burned areas, but of the full smoke migration path. Smoke travels through HVAC ducts, rises into attic spaces, and settles into soft materials like carpeting and upholstered furniture far from the point of origin. We use air quality monitoring and thermal imaging alongside visual inspection to map where contamination has traveled.

Structural stabilization comes next when load-bearing elements have been compromised. In Clinton Township's older colonials, roof trusses and floor joists are often dimensional lumber rather than engineered wood, which behaves differently under heat stress. We document structural conditions thoroughly, both for accurate repair scoping and for insurance carrier review.

Odor removal in fire damage is a process, not a single treatment. Thermal fogging, hydroxyl generation, and ozone application each address different odor compounds in different materials. Soot on hard surfaces requires specific chemical agents matched to the surface type, because the wrong cleaner can permanently set staining rather than lift it. Porous materials, including insulation, carpet padding, and some cabinetry, often require removal rather than cleaning once smoke has fully penetrated them.

Final reconstruction brings the structure back to pre-loss condition, coordinating with local building requirements for any permitted work.

## Local Note

One pattern specific to Clinton Township's postwar brick ranches: the original interior walls in many of these homes were finished with a three-coat plaster system over metal lath rather than gypsum board. After a fire, the plaster itself may test structurally sound while the metal lath behind it has corroded from firefighting water and heat cycling. A wall that passes a visual inspection can still require full replacement once the lath condition is assessed. Skipping that step during restoration leads to callbacks months later when the plaster begins to separate. It is the kind of detail that only surfaces when you have worked in this housing stock long enough to know what to look for.
