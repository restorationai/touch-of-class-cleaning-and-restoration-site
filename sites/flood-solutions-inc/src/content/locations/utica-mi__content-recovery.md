When a pipe bursts in a Utica basement or a kitchen fire leaves soot on furniture and family photos, the instinct is to start throwing things away. Content recovery is the alternative: inventorying, cleaning, and restoring what water, smoke, or fire damage touched instead of writing it off. In a Macomb County city built around older ranch and split-level homes with finished basements, that distinction often means saving heirlooms, electronics, and documents that a homeowner assumed were gone.

## Why Utica Homes Generate Content Recovery Calls

Much of Utica's housing stock dates from the mid-century boom years, which means finished basements with wood paneling, carpet over concrete, and stored belongings sitting directly on the slab. When the Clinton River watershed floods after spring snowmelt or a summer downpour overwhelms an aging sump pump, those basements take on water fast, and everything stored down there, from holiday decorations to filing cabinets, gets soaked before anyone notices. Michigan's freeze-thaw winters add a second pattern: frozen and burst supply lines in unheated garages or crawlspaces, which soak stored tools, sporting equipment, and seasonal items that then sit wet for days if the home is a vacation property or the leak happens while residents are traveling.

## Our Content Recovery Process in Utica

We start with a room-by-room inventory, photographing and cataloging items before anything moves, which matters for insurance documentation and for the homeowner's peace of mind. Soft goods, upholstery, and clothing typically go through wet cleaning or specialized laundering depending on contamination level. Electronics get assessed separately since Michigan's humidity swings between seasons can accelerate corrosion once water reaches circuit boards, so timing matters more than people expect. Documents and photographs, when recoverable, are stabilized and either air-dried under controlled conditions or sent out for freeze-drying if the volume or water saturation calls for it. Furniture goes through structural assessment first: solid wood often survives with refinishing, while particleboard and MDF pieces common in newer furniture rarely do.

## Utica Insurance Coordination

Most homeowner policies in Macomb County distinguish between sudden water damage, like a burst pipe, and gradual seepage or flood damage from rising groundwater, which often requires separate flood coverage. We document the loss in a way that supports whichever claim type applies, itemizing recoverable versus non-recoverable contents so the adjuster has a clear basis for valuation. For finished basements specifically, we note pre-loss condition of flooring, paneling, and built-ins separately from personal contents, since those are usually handled under different line items on a homeowner's policy.

## Local note

One pattern we see often in Utica: basement storage shelving installed a few inches too low, sitting right at the height where floodwater from a sump failure or river-adjacent flooding typically crests. Homeowners who raise storage shelving even eight to ten inches off the slab cut their contents losses significantly in the events we've responded to across the area. It's a small detail, but in a city where finished basements double as storage and rec rooms, it's often the difference between a load of laundry and a full pack-out.
