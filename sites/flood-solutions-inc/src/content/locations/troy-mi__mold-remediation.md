## Why Troy Properties See Mold Issues

Oakland County's clay-heavy soil holds water close to the surface long after a rain event or snowmelt. That sustained ground saturation presses against slab edges and block foundations, particularly in subdivisions built during Troy's rapid residential expansion in the 1970s and 1980s. Homes from that era were often built with fiberglass batt insulation tucked against exterior walls, insulation that absorbs and holds moisture without showing visible damage until mold is already established inside the cavity.

Troy also sits in a zone where summer humidity regularly climbs above 70 percent for extended stretches. Attic spaces with inadequate ridge ventilation trap that humidity against roof sheathing, and mold can colonize OSB or plywood within 24 to 48 hours of a sustained moisture event. Finished basements, common in the ranch and colonial homes that make up much of Troy's housing stock, add another layer of risk: carpeted concrete slabs and drywall that extends to the floor give mold an easy foothold after even a minor water intrusion.

## The Mold Remediation Process in Troy

Effective mold abatement starts with a thorough moisture assessment, not just a visual inspection. Readings are taken at the wall cavity, subfloor, and ceiling plane using non-invasive meters, and any area reading above acceptable thresholds gets mapped before work begins. That map drives the containment plan.

Physical containment uses 6-mil poly sheeting and negative air pressure to isolate the work zone from the rest of the home. HEPA-filtered air scrubbers run continuously during abatement, capturing airborne spores before they can resettle in unaffected rooms. Contaminated materials, drywall, insulation, carpeting, are bagged and removed per EPA guidelines. Structural surfaces that can be saved are treated with an antimicrobial agent, then dried to confirmed low-moisture readings before any rebuild begins.

In Troy homes with finished lower levels, the process often involves removing the bottom portion of drywall to inspect the full wall cavity rather than treating only the visible surface. Mold that is cleaned at the face but left behind the wall will return.
