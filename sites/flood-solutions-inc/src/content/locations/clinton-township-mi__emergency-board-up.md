## Why Clinton Township Properties Need Emergency Board Up

Clinton Township's housing stock is mostly post-war: ranch and split-level homes built through the 1950s, 60s, and 70s, many still carrying original aluminum-frame sliding doors and single-pane windows along the back of the house. Those materials don't flex well under impact, whether it's a wind-driven branch during a Macomb County storm cell or a forced entry. Add to that the commercial strips along Groesbeck Highway and Gratiot Avenue, where storefronts rely on large plate-glass sections that shatter completely rather than cracking, and you get a steady pattern of board-up calls tied less to any one cause and more to how this township was built. Winters bring another variable: freeze-thaw cycling can stress already-weak window seals and door frames to the point that a hard freeze finishes what a summer storm started.

## Our Emergency Board Up Process in Clinton Township

We start by measuring the actual opening, not assuming a standard size, since older aluminum frames and newer vinyl replacements rarely share dimensions even in homes built the same year. Plywood is cut to overlap the frame by a few inches on each side, not just plugged into the hole, so wind pressure doesn't pop it loose overnight. For sliding doors with warped or bent tracks, a common issue in Clinton Township's older subdivisions, we anchor into the surrounding stud or masonry rather than the damaged track itself. Commercial storefronts along the Gratiot corridor typically need larger sheet counts and additional bracing given the span of the glass. Every board-up is photographed before and after for the insurance file, since this step often gets skipped in a rushed DIY fix and creates problems at claim time.

## Clinton Township Insurance Coordination

Most homeowner and commercial policies in Michigan treat emergency board-up as a mitigation cost, meaning the carrier expects the property owner to prevent further damage rather than wait for an adjuster to arrive first. We document the pre-existing condition, the cause of loss where it's visible (storm impact, forced entry, vehicle strike), and the materials used, then provide that file directly to the carrier or the property manager's insurance contact. For landlords managing multiple Clinton Township units, this paperwork trail matters more than the board-up itself when it comes time to reconcile the claim.

## Local note

A detail that catches a lot of DIY board-up attempts off guard: Clinton Township's ranch-style subdivisions often have sliding patio doors facing the backyard where the aluminum track has warped slightly over sixty-plus years of expansion and contraction. A plywood panel screwed straight to the track edge won't sit flush and will rattle loose in the next wind event. The fix is cutting the panel to bridge the gap and anchoring into the surrounding frame or siding instead, which takes a few extra minutes but holds through the next storm system instead of failing on the first one.
