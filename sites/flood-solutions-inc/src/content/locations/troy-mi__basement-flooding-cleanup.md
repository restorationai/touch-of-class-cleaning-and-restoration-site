## Why Troy Properties See Basement Flooding Issues

Troy's underlying geology is largely glacial lake clay, which drains slowly and pushes hydrostatic pressure against basement walls and floor slabs during and after heavy precipitation. That pressure finds the path of least resistance: cove joints where the floor meets the wall, tie-rod holes in poured concrete, and mortar joints in block foundations. Homes built across Troy's residential corridors in the 1960s through the 1980s often have block foundations that are especially susceptible to this kind of lateral seepage, because block is inherently more porous than poured concrete.

Troy also sits within the Clinton River watershed. When upstream tributaries run high, storm drains in lower-lying residential areas back up, and sump pits fill faster than undersized pumps can discharge. A single overnight storm can overwhelm a pump that worked fine all winter. Finished basements, which are common in Troy's mid-century and newer subdivisions, add complexity: water migrates under laminate flooring and behind stud walls before it becomes visible, meaning the visible puddle on the floor is rarely the full picture.

## Our Basement Water Removal Process in Troy

The first step on every job is a moisture mapping survey, not just a visual walk-through. Thermal imaging and calibrated moisture meters show where water has traveled behind finished surfaces, which matters especially in Troy homes where basements are often used as living space. Pulling up a wet carpet pad and leaving saturated drywall in place is a common mistake that leads to mold callbacks weeks later.

Once the scope is mapped, standing water is extracted using truck-mounted or portable extraction units sized to the volume present. Saturated carpet, pad, and any non-salvageable building materials are removed before drying equipment is placed, because drying over wet materials extends the timeline significantly and rarely achieves acceptable final moisture readings.

Drying is done with a calculated placement of refrigerant dehumidifiers and high-velocity air movers. In Troy's climate, where outdoor humidity in spring and summer can be high enough to work against the drying process, equipment selection and monitoring intervals matter. Moisture readings are logged at each visit so the drying curve is documented, not estimated.

## Troy Insurance Coordination for Basement Water Damage

Whether a basement flooding claim gets covered depends heavily on the cause of loss. In Troy, as elsewhere in Michigan, sudden and accidental discharge from a supply line or appliance is typically covered under a standard homeowner policy, while gradual seepage through foundation walls is often excluded. Sewer backup and sump pump failure coverage is usually a separate endorsement, and many homeowners in Troy discover they do not have it until after a loss.

Flood Solutions Inc documents the loss thoroughly: photographs of source conditions, moisture readings, affected materials, and a written scope. That documentation is what a claims adjuster needs to process the claim efficiently. We work with most major carriers and can bill directly in many cases, reducing the out-of-pocket burden while the claim is in process.

Troy's finished basements, particularly in subdivisions developed in the 1980s and 1990s, frequently used a drop-ceiling grid over mechanicals and a combination of wood-framed and metal-stud partition walls. When water gets into these spaces, the drop-ceiling tiles absorb moisture and mask wet conditions above the grid, and the metal track at the base of metal-stud walls wicks water laterally along the floor before it becomes visible at the wall face. On jobs in these homes, we lift ceiling tiles and probe above the grid as a standard step, not an afterthought, because leaving wet insulation above a drop ceiling is one of the most reliable ways to end up with a mold problem two months after the drying equipment is gone.
