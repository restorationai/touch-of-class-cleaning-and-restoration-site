## Why Chesterfield Properties See Hidden Leak Problems

Macomb County's clay soil doesn't drain the way sandy or loam-based ground does. After a heavy rain or a hard freeze, that clay expands and contracts, and the pipes buried in it flex along with it. Over time, copper supply lines develop stress fractures at fittings, and older galvanized pipe corrodes from the inside out. Chesterfield's housing stock includes a significant number of ranch-style homes and split-levels built during the 1970s and 1980s, many of which still have their original plumbing. Those homes are now at the age where pinhole leaks in copper are common, and slab penetrations that were sealed decades ago can begin to weep.

The township also sees a wide range of water table conditions depending on how close a property sits to Lake St. Clair's drainage basin. ## How We Locate Leaks in Chesterfield Homes

Accurate leak detection means finding the source without tearing open every wall or jackhammering every slab. The process starts with a pressure test on the supply system to confirm a leak is present and to isolate whether it is on the hot or cold side. From there, thermal imaging cameras scan floors and walls for temperature anomalies, since water moving through a pipe or pooling behind a surface creates a distinct thermal signature that shows up clearly even when the surface looks dry to the eye.

For suspected slab leaks, acoustic listening equipment picks up the sound of pressurized water escaping a pipe beneath concrete. That pinpoints the location to within inches, which matters enormously when the alternative is opening a trench across a finished floor. Once the leak is confirmed and mapped, the findings are documented with photos and a written report that can be submitted directly to your insurance carrier.
