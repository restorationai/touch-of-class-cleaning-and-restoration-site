Michigan winters put Troy roofs through a freeze-thaw cycle that most homeowners never think about until water starts dripping down an interior wall in January. Ice dams form when heat escaping through the attic melts snow on the upper roof, that meltwater runs down to the cold eaves, and refreezes into a ridge that backs water up under the shingles. In a city built largely on 1960s through 1990s colonial and split-level construction, with steep-pitched roofs and attics that were insulated to a much lower standard than today's code, that backup finds its way into ceilings, exterior walls, and window headers faster than most people expect.

## Why Troy Homes See Ice Dam Problems

Much of Troy's housing stock predates the insulation and ventilation standards used in new construction today. Attics in these subdivisions often have gaps around can lights, bath fans that vent into the attic instead of outside, and insulation that has settled or compressed over three or four decades. All of that lets warm air rise into the attic space, which is the single biggest driver of ice dam formation. Add in Oakland County's typical pattern of heavy lake-effect snow followed by a thaw-refreeze stretch in January and February, and the same roof can build and lose an ice dam several times in one winter. Homes with complex rooflines, dormers, and multiple valleys, common in Troy's larger subdivisions, tend to dam up first because those areas shed heat unevenly.

## Our Ice Dam Process in Troy

We start by identifying where water has actually entered, not just where the dam is visible from the driveway, because the two are often several feet apart. Steam is used to break down the ice ridge without damaging shingles, which matters on Troy roofs where architectural shingles are common and brittle in sub-freezing temperatures. Once the roof is clear, we move inside to check ceiling drywall, insulation, and top-plate framing for moisture, since water can travel along a rafter or truss chord before it shows up as a stain. Drying follows standard moisture-mapping practice so we know insulation and framing have actually released trapped water rather than just looking dry at the surface. Where the underlying cause is attic bypass air leakage rather than a one-time weather event, we'll flag it, because treating the water damage without addressing the heat loss just means doing this again next winter.

## Troy Insurance Coordination

Most homeowner policies in Michigan treat ice dam water intrusion as a covered peril, but carriers vary on what they'll pay toward the ice dam removal itself versus the interior water damage it caused. We document the ice formation, the point of entry, and the interior damage separately with photos and moisture readings, which gives the adjuster a clear record to work from rather than a single vague claim. If the loss involves a finished basement or a recently renovated kitchen below the damaged ceiling, as is common in Troy's updated 1970s and 80s ranch homes, we itemize those materials specifically so they aren't lumped into a generic drywall repair estimate.

## Local note

A detail worth knowing if you're dealing with this for the first time: Troy's building department typically requires a permit for structural drywall or framing repair tied to storm or water damage once the repair exceeds a cosmetic patch, even when the work is insurance-driven. We coordinate that paperwork as part of the repair scope so it doesn't hold up your claim timeline or leave you doing a follow-up call to the city after the crew has already left.
