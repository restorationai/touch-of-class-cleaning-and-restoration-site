## Why Shelby Township Homes Hold Smoke Longer

Macomb County's cold winters create a specific hazard after a fire: when crews ventilate a smoke-damaged home in January or February, the sharp temperature differential between the heated interior and the outdoor air causes smoke residue to condense and re-deposit on cold surfaces like exterior walls, window frames, and garage drywall. This is a pattern that shows up repeatedly in Michigan's northern suburban communities, and it means smoke residue cleanup in Shelby Township often requires a second pass on surfaces that looked clean on day one.

Skipping duct cleaning after a fire leaves an odor source that no amount of surface cleaning will resolve.

## Our Smoke Damage Restoration Process in Shelby Township

The first step on any smoke damage job is containment, isolating affected rooms so cross-contamination doesn't spread soot into unaffected areas while work is underway. From there, the process moves in a specific order:

**Dry soot removal first.** Wet-cleaning soot before dry removal drives particles deeper into porous surfaces. Dry sponges and HEPA-filtered vacuums pull loose carbon and ash before any liquid cleaning agent touches the surface.

**Surface cleaning by material type.** Brick, painted drywall, wood trim, and upholstery each require different chemistry. Alkaline cleaners work on protein smoke (kitchen fires, grease); acidic or neutral cleaners are appropriate for wood or synthetic smoke residue. Applying the wrong product to the wrong surface can permanently set a stain.

**Odor neutralization, not masking.** Thermal fogging and hydroxyl generation reach into wall cavities, subfloor gaps, and ductwork where spray-applied deodorizers cannot. The goal is molecular neutralization of odor compounds, not a fragrance layer over them.

**Clearance check.** Before the job closes, affected surfaces are re-inspected under controlled lighting to confirm residue removal is complete.
