Sterling Heights sits on the clay-heavy soils of Macomb County, and when the region gets a hard rain, that clay has nowhere to send the water fast enough. The result shows up in basements across the city as sewage backing up through floor drains, laundry tubs, and toilets, carrying everything the municipal sewer line was meant to carry away. Raw sewage exposure is not a cleanup-later problem. Pathogens including E. coli, hepatitis A, and norovirus survive on porous surfaces long after the visible mess is gone, and every hour the contamination sits, it works deeper into concrete, drywall, and subfloor material.

## Why Sterling Heights Homes See Sewage Backup So Often

Macomb County's combined sewer infrastructure in older sections of Sterling Heights was designed for a smaller population and lighter impervious surface coverage than the city carries today. When heavy rainfall overwhelms the system, the path of least resistance is back through the lowest fixture in the building. Homes built in the 1960s and 1970s, which make up a large share of the housing stock throughout the city, typically have cast-iron or Orangeburg drain lines that have had decades to corrode, root-intrude, and partially collapse. A partial blockage that causes no trouble during a dry summer can become a full sewage backup the first time a major storm pushes the municipal system past capacity.

Flat lot grading, common on the subdivisions that filled in during Sterling Heights' rapid post-war growth, also means surface water has little slope to carry it away from foundation walls. That water pressure accelerates the backup event and extends how long contaminated water sits before it drains naturally.

## Our Sewage Cleanup and Sanitization Process

The first step is containment. Sewage backup is classified as Category 3 water, the most hazardous category, and the affected area needs to be isolated before any material is moved. Our crew arrives with full PPE, establishes a contamination perimeter, and begins extracting standing sewage using truck-mounted and portable extraction units capable of handling solids-laden water.

Once standing water is removed, the focus shifts to what you cannot see. Porous materials that have absorbed sewage, including drywall below the flood line, carpet, carpet pad, and in some cases OSB subfloor, are removed and bagged for disposal per Michigan Department of Environment, Great Lakes, and Energy (EGLE) guidelines for contaminated waste. Concrete and block surfaces are cleaned with an EPA-registered disinfectant, allowed to dwell for the required contact time, and then rinsed. Structural cavities behind walls are inspected with moisture meters and thermal imaging to confirm contamination has not wicked higher than the visible damage line.

Drying equipment, including desiccant dehumidifiers and high-velocity air movers, is placed to bring the structural materials down to acceptable moisture content before any reconstruction begins. Documentation of moisture readings is kept throughout so that your insurance adjuster has a clear record of conditions at each stage.

## Coordinating with Insurance After a Sewage Backup in Sterling Heights

Most standard homeowner policies in Michigan cover sudden and accidental sewage backup only if the homeowner has added a sewer backup endorsement. Many Sterling Heights homeowners added this coverage after the region experienced significant flooding events in past years, but not everyone did. Before assuming coverage, pull your declarations page and look specifically for a water backup or sewer backup rider.

When coverage is confirmed, Flood Solutions documents the loss with photographs, moisture logs, and a written scope of work that aligns with the format most carriers and their preferred estimating platforms expect. That documentation reduces back-and-forth and helps move the claim toward approval without the homeowner having to interpret technical language on their own.

## Local Note

Homes in Sterling Heights that were built on slab foundations, a construction style that became common in the city's southern sections during the 1980s, present a specific challenge during sewage backup events. When sewage enters through a floor drain set into the slab, the water can travel laterally under the concrete before it surfaces, meaning the visible wet area at the drain understates the actual contamination footprint. Crews working slab-on-grade homes here probe the perimeter with moisture meters and lift flooring at the edges of the affected room to confirm the contamination boundary before assuming the cleanup is contained to what the eye can see.
