When a basement floods in Clinton Township, the water rarely stays contained to the carpet and drywall. Furniture, photo albums, seasonal storage bins, and the contents of finished rec rooms all sit in the path of rising water, and the longer those items sit wet, the more of them become unsalvageable. Content recovery is about triage as much as cleaning: knowing what can be dried and restored versus what needs to be documented and replaced, fast enough that mold doesn't make the decision for you.

## Why Clinton Township Homes See Content Recovery Needs

Much of Clinton Township's housing stock dates to the mid-20th century boom years, when brick ranch homes with full basements went up across Macomb County to house the region's growing auto industry workforce. Those basements were rarely built with finished living space or built-in moisture barriers in mind, so many homeowners later finished them for family rooms, home offices, or storage, filling them with furniture, electronics, and paper records that sit directly on the slab. When groundwater rises after a heavy spring thaw or a sewer backup pushes water up through a floor drain, those contents take the first hit. Proximity to Lake St. Clair also means the water table sits relatively high across parts of the township, so basements that flood once tend to flood again, and homeowners often have the same categories of contents at risk each time.

## Our Content Recovery Process in Clinton Township

The first step is always inventory before anything moves. We photograph and log affected items in place, noting condition, so the record matches what the insurance adjuster will later review. From there, contents are sorted into categories: items that can be cleaned and dried on-site, items that need to be packed out for specialized cleaning (upholstery, electronics, textiles), and items that are beyond recovery and need to be documented for claim purposes rather than restoration. Paper documents and photographs get special handling since standard air drying can cause pages to fuse or ink to bleed; we use controlled drying methods calibrated to the material. Electronics that have been exposed to water, even briefly, are not powered on until they've been inspected, since energizing a wet circuit board can cause damage that wasn't there to begin with.

## Local Note

A pattern we see often in Clinton Township's older ranch-style basements: finished rec rooms built in the 1970s and 80s frequently have carpet glued directly to the slab rather than installed over padding. When these basements flood, the carpet holds water against the concrete far longer than a padded install would, which means contents stored nearby, particularly cardboard boxes and wood furniture legs sitting on that carpet, wick moisture upward even after the visible water is gone. We check moisture levels at the base of stored items specifically for this reason rather than relying on surface appearance alone.
