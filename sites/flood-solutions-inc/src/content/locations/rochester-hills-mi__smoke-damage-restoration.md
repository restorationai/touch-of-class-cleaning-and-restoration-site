## Why Rochester Hills Homes Hold Smoke Residue Longer

The housing stock across Rochester Hills ranges from mid-century brick ranches to vinyl-clad colonials built through the 1990s and early 2000s. That mix matters for smoke remediation because different construction eras absorb and trap residue differently.

Brick and block construction, common in homes built before 1975, is highly porous. Smoke particles and the volatile organic compounds that carry that sharp, acrid odor can penetrate several millimeters into uncoated masonry. Surface wiping alone won't reach it. Homes built in the 1980s and 1990s often have fiberglass batt insulation in wall cavities; if smoke has migrated through outlet boxes or recessed lights, the insulation may need to be removed rather than cleaned.

Michigan's cold winters also play a role. Homes that have been sealed tight for heating season have less air exchange, which means smoke concentrations build faster and linger longer than in climates where windows stay open. After a winter fire event, the residue has often bonded to cold surfaces in ways that require chemical sponges and hydroxyl or thermal fogging rather than simple ventilation.

## The Smoke Damage Restoration Process We Use Here

Every smoke job starts with a room-by-room assessment before any cleaning begins. Smoke moves by pressure and temperature, so the visible char near the fire origin is rarely the only affected area. We use particle counters and thermal imaging to map where residue has traveled, including inside HVAC systems, which are a primary distribution path in forced-air homes common throughout Rochester Hills.

From there, the process typically follows this sequence:

1. **Containment and ventilation setup** to stop cross-contamination to unaffected areas.
2. **Dry soot removal** using HEPA vacuuming and chemical dry sponges on walls, ceilings, and structural surfaces before any wet cleaning is introduced. Applying wet cleaners to dry soot smears it deeper into the substrate.
3. **Wet cleaning and degreasing** of hard surfaces, cabinetry, and fixtures using pH-appropriate agents matched to the surface material.
4. **Duct inspection and cleaning** if smoke has entered the HVAC system, which it almost always has in whole-house events.
5. **Odor neutralization** using thermal fogging or hydroxyl generation, not masking agents. The goal is molecular-level odor elimination.
6. **Sealing** of porous surfaces like drywall and wood framing with an encapsulating primer before reconstruction begins.

Documentation runs throughout every phase, which matters when you're coordinating with a carrier.

## Insurance Documentation for Rochester Hills Claims

Smoke damage claims in Michigan are typically covered under the fire and smoke peril of a standard homeowner's policy, but the documentation burden falls on the policyholder to demonstrate scope. Carriers will often send their own adjuster, and the estimates adjusters produce don't always reflect the full extent of hidden residue in walls or ductwork.

We photograph and document affected materials room by room, note migration paths, and provide line-item scopes that align with Xactimate pricing, the estimating platform most Michigan carriers use. That parallel documentation gives your adjuster a complete picture and reduces the back-and-forth that delays claim settlement. ## Local Note

Homes in Rochester Hills that were built with the cathedral ceilings popular in 1990s colonials present a specific challenge after smoke events: the smoke rises and concentrates at the peak of those vaulted spaces, and the drywall up there is often textured or painted with flat finishes that absorb residue aggressively. Standard extension-pole cleaning doesn't always reach the geometry of those peaks evenly. We account for this in our equipment staging, bringing articulating lifts or scaffolding when ceiling height and geometry require it, rather than leaving high-angle surfaces undertreated and allowing odor to return weeks later when temperatures rise.
