When a burst pipe, sump failure, or storm surge leaves standing water on your floors, the clock starts immediately, not just on structural damage, but on the window before microbial growth takes hold in wall cavities and under subfloor materials. Flood Solutions Inc has been responding to water losses across Macomb and Oakland counties since 1996, and the conditions here demand a different approach than a dry-climate response.

## Why Rochester Hills Properties See Water Damage Differently

The geology beneath Rochester Hills is a significant factor that rarely gets mentioned until something goes wrong. Much of the city sits on glacial till, dense, low-permeability clay that doesn't drain the way sandy soils do. During a heavy rain event or a rapid thaw in late February or March, that clay acts like a bathtub: water has nowhere to go quickly, and it finds the path of least resistance into crawl spaces, basement window wells, and foundation cracks.

The housing stock adds another layer of complexity. Rochester Hills developed heavily through the 1970s, 1980s, and into the 1990s, meaning a large share of homes use poured-concrete or block foundations that were built before current waterproofing standards. Block foundations in particular are porous, water doesn't just seep through the mortar joints, it wicks through the block itself and can saturate the interior face long before you see visible pooling on the floor. Older sump pits in these homes were often sized for typical seasonal seepage, not for the sustained hydrostatic pressure that follows a multi-day rain event.

## Our Water Cleanup Process in Rochester Hills

The first priority on any water damage call is stopping the source and containing the spread. Once that's confirmed, the extraction phase begins, and in Rochester Hills homes, that often means addressing more than just the visible standing water. Water migrates under vinyl plank flooring, into the space between a concrete slab and carpet padding, and behind drywall that sits directly on a basement floor. Thermal imaging and moisture meters map where the water actually went, not just where it's visible.

Extraction equipment pulls the bulk of standing water quickly. After that, the drying phase is calibrated to the specific materials involved. Drywall dries at a different rate than the wood framing behind it, and both dry differently than concrete block. Dehumidifiers and air movers are positioned based on the moisture readings, not placed generically around the room. Readings are logged at each visit so the drying curve can be tracked and equipment adjusted, over-drying causes its own damage to hardwood and trim, and under-drying leaves moisture that feeds mold.

## Local Note

Homes built in Rochester Hills during the late 1970s and early 1980s frequently used a now-discontinued fiberglass batt insulation installed directly against basement block walls, sometimes faced with a thin vapor barrier, sometimes not. When that wall assembly gets wet, the insulation holds moisture against the block for weeks after the visible water is gone, and standard drying protocols that work fine on a framed wall with modern insulation will miss it entirely. If your home was built in that era and you've had water in the basement, it's worth asking specifically about the wall assembly before assuming the space is dry.
