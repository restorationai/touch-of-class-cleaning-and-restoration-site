When a sewer line backup sends raw sewage into a basement or crawl space, the clock starts immediately: Category 3 water, the classification covering raw sewage, begins contaminating porous materials within hours and can make a space genuinely hazardous to occupy. ## Why Clinton Township Properties See Sewage Backup Problems

Much of Clinton Township's residential development happened in waves from the 1950s through the 1980s, which means a significant portion of the housing stock sits above clay or cast-iron sewer laterals that are now well past their expected service life. Clay tile joints shift as the ground moves through Michigan's freeze-thaw cycles, and tree root intrusion through those joints is one of the most common causes of sewer line backup in the township. When the main line restricts, the path of least resistance is back through floor drains, toilets, and utility sinks.

Macomb County's combined sewer system in older sections of the township adds another layer of risk. During heavy rain events, storm water volume can overwhelm the system and force sewage back upstream into connected homes. Basements finished with carpet or drywall are particularly vulnerable because contamination absorbs quickly into those materials and is not always visible at the surface.

## Our Sewage Cleanup and Sanitization Process in Clinton Township

The process starts with containment. Before any extraction begins, affected areas are isolated to prevent cross-contamination from spreading to unaffected parts of the home. Personal protective equipment is on before anyone enters the space.

Extraction of standing sewage comes next, using truck-mounted or portable units depending on access. Clinton Township basements with low egress windows or finished drop ceilings sometimes require portable equipment staged inside, which affects setup time. After bulk removal, saturated materials, including flooring, baseboards, and lower sections of drywall, are assessed. Materials that cannot be effectively sanitized are removed and bagged for proper disposal.

Sanitization follows extraction. EPA-registered disinfectants are applied to all affected hard surfaces, framing, and concrete. Antimicrobial treatments are applied where appropriate. Drying equipment, including air movers and dehumidifiers, is then deployed and monitored with moisture meters until readings confirm the structure has returned to acceptable levels. Documentation of moisture readings is provided throughout, which matters for insurance claims.

Sewage backup coverage is not automatically included in a standard homeowner's policy.
