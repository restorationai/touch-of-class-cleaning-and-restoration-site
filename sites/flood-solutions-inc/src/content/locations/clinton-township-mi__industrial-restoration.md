## Why Clinton Township Industrial Facilities Face Distinct Restoration Risks

Macomb County sits in a climate band where ground temperatures can swing dramatically between November and March. For industrial buildings, that means roof drains and overhead sprinkler mains are exposed to repeated freeze-thaw stress. A fitting that holds through five winters can fail in the sixth, releasing hundreds of gallons across a concrete production floor before anyone arrives for the morning shift.

Older industrial parks in the township, particularly those developed in the 1970s and 1980s along the M-59 corridor, frequently have flat or low-slope roofing systems that have been patched and re-patched over decades. When those roofs fail during a heavy Lake Erie-influenced snow event, water doesn't just enter at one point; it migrates laterally under the membrane and surfaces in unexpected places inside the building, sometimes far from the visible ceiling stain. Tracing the actual moisture boundary in a 60,000-square-foot warehouse requires thermal imaging and moisture mapping, not a visual walk-through.

Fire losses in manufacturing environments carry an additional layer of complexity: smoke and soot from industrial materials, rubber, plastics, coatings, and lubricants, behave differently than smoke from a residential kitchen fire. The residues are oilier, they penetrate HVAC ductwork aggressively, and they can contaminate inventory and equipment far beyond the fire's origin point.

## Our Industrial Restoration Process in Clinton Township

Every industrial loss starts with a scope assessment that accounts for operational continuity. Before any equipment rolls in, the team identifies which areas of the facility can be isolated and which must stay accessible, a question that matters differently in a cold-storage warehouse than in an active stamping plant.

For water losses, the process follows a structured sequence: emergency extraction to remove standing water from drains, trenches, and low points; deployment of commercial-grade desiccant or LGR dehumidification equipment scaled to the cubic footage involved; and daily moisture mapping to track drying progress against a documented baseline. Concrete slabs in industrial settings hold moisture longer than wood-frame assemblies, and drying timelines are set accordingly rather than estimated by feel.

For fire and smoke losses, the scope includes a full inventory of affected materials, including contents, racking systems, and mechanical equipment, before any cleaning begins. Soot that has settled on machined surfaces or electrical panels requires a different protocol than soot on painted block walls, and those decisions are made room by room.

Throughout the project, documentation is maintained in a format that supports insurance carrier review and, where applicable, OSHA recordkeeping requirements for the facility.

## Insurance and Carrier Coordination for Industrial Losses

Commercial property policies for industrial facilities often involve higher deductibles, business interruption riders, and equipment floaters that interact in ways a standard residential claim does not. The documentation package Flood Solutions inc produces, moisture logs, thermal images, scope-of-loss reports, and equipment inventories, is structured to support the adjuster's review process and reduce back-and-forth that extends claim timelines.

If a loss involves tenant space within a larger industrial complex, coordinating between the building owner's carrier and the tenant's policy is part of the process, not an afterthought.
