Flood Solutions inc has been working through water damage, mold, and structural drying jobs in Macomb County since 1996. That's nearly three decades of responding when a pipe bursts at midnight, when a sump pump fails during a spring storm, when a homeowner pulls back drywall and finds something that's been growing there for months. The company was built around a straightforward premise: restoration work done correctly the first time costs less, in money, time, and stress, than work that has to be redone.

## What we do

Most days start with a phone call from someone who didn't plan on making it. A basement with standing water. A ceiling that came down after an ice dam. A smell that turned out to be mold behind the bathroom tile. The job is to show up, assess what's actually happening, not just what's visible, and move through the work in a sequence that prevents secondary damage. That means moisture mapping before any drying equipment goes in, documentation that holds up with an insurance adjuster, and communication with the homeowner at each step so nothing is a surprise.

Insurance claims are a significant part of what we handle. We photograph and document affected materials, write scopes that carriers recognize, and bill directly where possible so homeowners aren't left managing paperwork on top of an already difficult situation.

## Our certifications and licensure

The restoration industry has standards, the IICRC (Institute of Inspection, Cleaning and Restoration Certification) sets the technical benchmarks that define how water damage is categorized, how drying is validated, and what constitutes a completed remediation. Working to those standards matters because it's the difference between a structure that reads dry and one that actually is dry.

For homes built before 1978, lead paint is a real concern any time demolition or controlled demolition is involved. Federal law requires specific handling protocols for pre-1978 properties, and certification under the EPA's Renovation, Repair and Painting (RRP) rule governs how that work is done safely. Macomb County has substantial housing stock from the postwar decades, ranch homes, brick colonials, and Cape Cods built in the 1950s and 1960s, so this comes up regularly.

We'll be straightforward about what certifications we hold: if you need that documentation for an insurance file or a permit application, call us at (586) 580-0197 and we'll tell you exactly what we can provide.

## Where we work

Our base is Macomb, MI, and we work throughout Macomb County and the surrounding region, including communities like Sterling Heights, Clinton Township, Shelby Township, St. Clair Shores, Warren, and Chesterfield. Southeast Michigan's clay-heavy soils and high water table make basement water intrusion a recurring problem, not a rare one. Spring thaw and summer storm events push sump pumps hard, and older neighborhoods with aging municipal infrastructure see backups that newer subdivisions don't. We know the area, the housing stock, and the conditions that drive most of the calls we get.
