## Why Creve Coeur Properties See Elevated Storm Damage Risk

Creve Coeur's position in west St. Louis County places it directly in the path of storm tracks that funnel northeast from the Ozark Plateau. The city's mature tree canopy — one of its most attractive features — becomes a liability when straight-line winds exceed 60 mph. Large hardwoods with shallow root systems in the area's clay-heavy soils are prone to uprooting rather than snapping, meaning whole root balls can heave, taking sections of fence, driveway, and foundation landscaping with them.

The housing stock here spans several decades, with a notable concentration of mid-century ranch and split-level homes built in the 1950s through 1970s. Those roofs often carry original or once-replaced architectural shingles that have lost significant granule coverage over the years, making them more vulnerable to hail damage than their age alone would suggest. Older fascia boards and soffit materials on these homes also tend to absorb wind-driven rain quickly once a shingle or flashing is compromised, accelerating interior damage.

## Our Storm Damage Restoration Process in Creve Coeur

Every storm job starts with a thorough exterior inspection before a single interior wall is opened. Wind and hail damage is rarely limited to what's visible from the ground — ridge caps, pipe boots, and flashing at dormers and chimneys are common failure points that get missed in a quick visual pass. We document everything with photographs and written notes organized for insurance submission.

Once the scope is established, the sequence typically runs:

- **Emergency tarping and board-up** to stop active water intrusion at the roof, windows, or siding
- **Tree and debris removal** from the structure and immediate perimeter, coordinated with licensed tree crews when large canopy removal is involved
- **Moisture mapping** of the interior — even a few hours of water entry through a compromised roof deck can saturate insulation and begin wicking into ceiling drywall
- **Structural repair and replacement** of damaged roofing, siding, gutters, fascia, and windows
- **Final inspection and documentation** for insurance close-out

In Creve Coeur's older ranch-style homes, we pay particular attention to the attic space. Low-slope roof sections common on mid-century designs can pond water briefly during heavy rain, and once the decking is compromised, insulation becomes saturated quickly — a problem that doesn't always show up as a visible ceiling stain until days later.

## Local Note

Creve Coeur's clay-dominant soil profile is worth understanding before any post-storm foundation or drainage work begins. Heavy clay retains water long after a storm passes, and properties with grading that pitches even slightly toward the foundation can see water pooling against basement walls for days after the rain stops. When a storm event includes both roof damage and significant ground saturation, the basement moisture problem often shows up a week later — after the roof is tarped and the homeowner assumes the worst is over. We flag this pattern during our initial assessment so you're not caught off guard by a secondary claim.
