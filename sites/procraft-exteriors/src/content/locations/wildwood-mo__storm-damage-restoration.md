Wildwood sits at the western edge of St. Louis County where the terrain opens up into rolling oak woodland and wide suburban lots — and that geography makes it a reliable target for the kind of fast-moving severe weather that sweeps across the Missouri River valley each spring and fall. When a line of thunderstorms drops a large tree across a roof on a Tuesday afternoon, the damage clock starts immediately: water finds its way through fractured decking, wind-driven rain soaks insulation, and what looks like a surface problem can become a structural one within hours. ProCraft Exteriors has been responding to storm damage across this part of the county since 2005, and we know how quickly a Wildwood property can go from "minor damage" to a much larger repair scope.

## Why Wildwood Properties See Significant Storm Damage

The heavily wooded character of Wildwood is one of its defining qualities — and one of the primary reasons storm damage here tends to be more severe than in denser suburban areas closer to I-270. Mature oaks, hickories, and maples that have grown for decades alongside homes in established subdivisions develop root systems and canopy spreads that can dwarf the structures beneath them. When straight-line winds exceed 60 mph — a threshold that St. Louis County reaches several times a year — those trees don't just lose limbs. They fall whole, and they fall onto roofs, fences, detached garages, and vehicles.

Wildwood's housing stock adds another layer of complexity. Homes built in the 1980s and 1990s, which make up a significant portion of the city's residential inventory, often used roof decking and framing standards that predate current wind-load requirements. An impact from a falling tree limb can crack ridge boards, shift rafters, and compromise the sheathing in ways that aren't visible from the ground. Hail — which accompanies many of the same storm systems that produce tornado warnings across St. Louis County — accelerates granule loss on asphalt shingles and can leave a roof functionally compromised even when it appears intact from the street.

## Our Storm Damage Restoration Process in Wildwood

The first priority after any storm event is stopping additional damage from occurring. That means emergency tarping of breached roof sections, boarding of broken windows or doors, and a thorough inspection of the structure before any permanent repairs begin. We document everything photographically before we touch a surface — not as a formality, but because insurance carriers require it and because the documentation protects you if a dispute arises later about the scope of the original damage.

Once the structure is secured, we assess the full damage picture: roofing, siding, gutters, fascia, soffit, and any structural framing that may have been affected. In Wildwood, where many homes have steep-pitch roofs and significant attic space, we pay particular attention to whether wind or water has penetrated the attic insulation and decking. Wet insulation loses its R-value quickly and can harbor mold within days if not addressed. We remove damaged materials, dry the affected areas, and replace with materials that match the existing profile — important in neighborhoods where HOA covenants govern exterior appearance.

## Wildwood Insurance & HOA Coordination

Most homeowner policies cover sudden storm damage to roofing, siding, and structural components, but the claims process requires accurate, timely documentation. We work directly with adjusters, provide itemized damage reports, and can be present during the adjuster's inspection to walk through our findings. Many Wildwood subdivisions also have HOA architectural review requirements that govern replacement materials, colors, and installation methods. We're familiar with that layer of the process and can help you navigate it so your repair doesn't create a compliance issue after the fact.

Wildwood's lot sizes and setback distances mean that fallen trees often land at angles that make crane or heavy equipment access genuinely complicated — a long driveway, a slope, or a fence line can all affect how debris is staged and removed before structural repairs begin.
