Before you can fix a mold problem, you have to understand it: how far it has spread, what is feeding it, and whether the air in the home is actually affected. That is what a mold inspection and assessment is for. It answers the questions a homeowner cannot answer by looking, and it produces a written record you can act on, whether the next step is remediation, a real estate disclosure, or simply peace of mind.

## What a mold inspection and assessment covers

A proper assessment is more than spotting discoloration on a wall. It starts with a visual survey of the areas of concern and the places mold likes to hide, behind baseboards, under sink cabinets, in HVAC cavities, and around windows and rooflines that have taken on water. Because mold cannot grow without moisture, moisture meters and, where useful, thermal imaging are used to trace where water is now or has been. Where sampling is called for, air and surface samples are collected and sent to an accredited laboratory, alongside an outdoor baseline sample taken at the same time so the indoor results have something to be measured against. The output is a written report: what was found, what the lab results say, and what they mean.

## Why we bring in a third-party assessor and lab

Kenny holds a Florida Mold Assessor license, so this company knows the assessment side of the work in detail. But Veterans Remediation & Restoration also performs mold remediation, and when the same company would both test a property and be paid to remediate it, the testing can look, fairly or not, like it serves the sale. Florida's mold-related services standards draw a line between assessment and remediation for exactly that reason.

So our best practice is to use an independent third-party assessor and an accredited laboratory for the formal assessment and for any clearance testing. Kenny's license and experience mean the inspection is coordinated by someone who understands precisely what a sound assessment requires and can translate the lab findings into plain language for you. The independence of the testing means the results are objective, and you are never asked to take the word of the same crew doing the removal.

## A written, lab-backed report you can use

Every assessment produces documentation, not just a verbal "you have mold." The report describes the affected areas, the moisture conditions driving the problem, and the laboratory analysis of any samples taken. That record is what an insurer, an adjuster, a real estate agent, or a remediation crew needs to move forward on solid footing, and it is the baseline any later clearance test is measured against.

## Our process

The clock and the humidity both work against you here on the coast, so the goal is a clear answer quickly. We survey the property, identify and document the moisture source, coordinate independent sampling where it is warranted, and walk you through the lab results once they are back. If remediation is the right next step, we can perform that work, with the assessment and the final clearance kept independent so the outcome is verified rather than assumed.
