A storm doesn't announce when it's finished damaging your home. The wind stops, the rain clears, and the real work begins, finding the water that pushed under your roof decking, the insulation that soaked it up silently, the window frame that shifted just enough to let humidity in for weeks. Storm damage restoration is the process of stopping that hidden clock before what started as a shingle problem becomes a structural or mold problem.

## What Storm Damage Restoration actually involves

Storm damage is rarely one thing. A single severe weather event can leave a Freeport home dealing with wind-driven rain intrusion, impact damage from debris or fallen trees, compromised roof sheathing, broken windows, and flooding, sometimes all at once. Restoration work has to address each entry point and each affected material category, not just the most visible damage.

On the exterior, that means tarping and boarding to stop active water intrusion before anything else. On the interior, it means moisture mapping, using thermal imaging and pin-less meters to find water that has migrated behind drywall, under flooring, or into wall cavities where it reads dry at the surface. Structural drying equipment (commercial-grade dehumidifiers and air movers) runs until readings confirm the materials have returned to acceptable moisture content, not just until things feel dry to the touch.

Tree impact damage adds another layer: the tree is removed, but the framing underneath needs inspection for deflection, fastener pull-through, and any breach that let water travel further than the obvious puncture point.

Timeline matters here. The Gulf Coast's humidity means wet building materials can begin supporting mold colonization within 24 to 48 hours of saturation. Getting drying equipment in place quickly isn't a sales pitch, it's the difference between a drying job and a drying-plus-remediation job.

## Our process

1. **Emergency stabilization**, Tarps over breached roofing, plywood over broken windows and doors, and any immediate action needed to stop water from continuing to enter the structure. This happens before documentation or scoping, because every hour of open exposure adds to the loss.

2. **Damage assessment and moisture mapping**, A full walk of the property using thermal imaging cameras and calibrated moisture meters to document every affected area, including concealed cavities. Photos and readings are logged for the insurance claim file at this stage.

3. **Debris and tree removal**, Fallen limbs, branches through rooflines, and storm debris are cleared from the structure. For significant tree impacts, this step includes inspection of the framing beneath the impact zone for hidden damage before any patching begins.

4. **Structural drying and content protection**, Commercial dehumidifiers and air movers are placed to dry affected building materials in place where possible. Contents in the damage path are moved, protected, or documented. Drying logs are maintained daily and shared with the adjuster.

5. **Reconstruction scope and repair**, Once materials are confirmed dry, damaged roofing, siding, drywall, flooring, and framing are repaired or replaced. Work is scoped in writing before it starts.

## What separates a good storm damage response from a bad one

The most common failure in storm damage work isn't the repair, it's the assessment. Contractors who focus on what's visible (the missing shingles, the broken window) and miss what's hidden (the water that traveled six feet along a top plate before dropping into a wall cavity) leave homeowners with a patched roof and a mold problem three months later.

A few specific things experienced operators do differently:

- **Thermal imaging before drying.** Wet insulation behind intact drywall won't show on a surface moisture reading. Infrared cameras reveal the temperature differential that points to saturated cavities, without unnecessary demolition.
- **Drying validation, not assumption.** Equipment gets placed and then monitored. Readings are taken daily. Drying is not declared complete because the equipment has been running for three days, it's complete when the numbers say so.
- **Documenting wind-driven rain separately from flood.** Insurance adjusters treat these as different causes of loss. Mixing them in the documentation can complicate or delay a claim. Proper scoping keeps them distinct.
- **Checking the attic.** Wind-driven rain frequently enters through soffit vents, ridge vents, or around penetrations and saturates attic insulation without any visible ceiling damage below. It's one of the most missed areas in a storm inspection.

## What does Storm Damage Restoration cost?

Typical costs for storm damage restoration vary significantly based on the size of the affected area, the type of damage, and how quickly drying begins. Most homeowners pay somewhere between a few thousand dollars for a contained single-room loss and well into five figures for whole-roof failures with interior water intrusion. ## Seasonal and regional considerations

Freeport and the surrounding Panhandle sit in one of the most storm-active corridors in the country. Hurricane season runs June through November, but severe thunderstorms capable of producing tornado-force straight-line winds, large hail, and significant tree damage occur well outside that window. The region's high humidity baseline means that even modest water intrusion, a cracked soffit, a lifted shingle, can drive interior moisture levels high enough to support mold growth faster than in drier climates. Post-storm inspections after any named storm or severe weather event are worth scheduling even if damage isn't immediately obvious.
