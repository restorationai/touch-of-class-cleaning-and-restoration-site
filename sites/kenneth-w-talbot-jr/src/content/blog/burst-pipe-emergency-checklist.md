When a pipe bursts, the first 10 minutes matter more than anything that follows. Shut off the main water supply valve, cut power to any rooms with standing water at the breaker panel, and start documenting with your phone camera before you touch anything else. Everything after that, drying, repairs, insurance claims, depends on how well you handle those first few steps. This checklist walks you through the full response, from the moment you hear the rush of water to the point where your home is dry and structurally sound again.

## Step 1: Stop the Water and Protect Yourself

Your main shutoff valve is usually located where the water line enters the house, in a utility closet, under a sink near an exterior wall, or in a crawl space. Turn it clockwise until it stops. If you can't find it or it won't budge, call your local utility provider for an emergency street-side shutoff.

Once the water is off:

1. **Cut power to affected rooms** at the breaker panel. Water and live outlets are a serious hazard, especially if water is pooling near appliances or has reached a finished floor with outlets at baseboard level.
2. **Open faucets** throughout the house to relieve pressure and drain any remaining water in the lines.
3. **Move valuables and furniture** off wet flooring if you can do so safely. Wet upholstery and wood furniture will begin to swell and stain within hours.

Do not re-enter a room where water is touching electrical outlets, appliances, or a breaker panel until the power is confirmed off.

## Step 2: Document Everything Before You Clean Up

This step costs you nothing and protects you significantly when you file an insurance claim.

- Photograph the burst pipe itself, including the surrounding wall or ceiling material.
- Video-walk every room with visible water, wet flooring, or water staining on walls.
- Note the time the damage was discovered, insurers often ask.
- Photograph any damaged personal property (furniture, rugs, electronics) in place before moving it.

If you have a home inventory list or receipts for major items, pull those up now. You'll want them when you call your carrier.

One thing to know about Florida homes specifically: many older Freeport-area properties use CPVC or copper supply lines that can become brittle over time, especially in crawl spaces that see significant temperature swings between winter nights and summer heat. A pipe that looks intact from the outside can fail at a fitting or a spot where it was nicked during a previous renovation. The source of the break isn't always where the water appears.

## Step 3: Begin Controlled Water Removal, Carefully

If the volume of water is manageable and you have the right equipment, you can begin removing standing water yourself:

1. Use a wet/dry vacuum for smaller puddles on hard flooring.
2. Lay old towels or mop up what the vacuum can't reach.
3. Place box fans and open windows to begin air movement, but only if outdoor humidity is lower than indoor humidity. On a humid Florida afternoon, opening windows pulls more moisture in than it pushes out.
4. Lift area rugs and move them outside to dry flat, not folded.

What you cannot do with a shop vac and fans: dry the structure. Water wicks into drywall, subfloor, and wall cavities quickly. A surface that feels dry to the touch 24 hours after a burst pipe may still be holding significant moisture in the framing behind it. That hidden moisture is where mold colonization typically begins, often within 24 to 48 hours in a warm, humid environment like Northwest Florida.

If the water reached drywall, insulation, or subfloor, this is the point where professional water damage restoration equipment, dehumidifiers, air movers, and thermal imaging, becomes necessary rather than optional.

## What NOT to Do After a Pipe Bursts

Some of the most common responses to a burst pipe make the damage significantly worse:

- **Don't use a standard household fan as your only drying method.** Fans move air but don't remove moisture from it. Without a dehumidifier pulling water vapor out of the air, you're just circulating humid air around wet materials.
- **Don't assume the floor is fine because it looks dry.** Hardwood and laminate flooring can cup, buckle, and delaminate days after the visible water is gone, because the subfloor beneath is still wet.
- **Don't use a heat gun or space heater to speed drying.** Rapid surface drying traps moisture deeper in the material and can warp wood framing.
- **Don't delay calling your insurance company.** Most homeowner policies require prompt notification of a loss. Waiting several days to report can complicate your claim.
- **Don't discard damaged materials before your adjuster or a restoration crew has documented them.** Wet drywall, flooring, and insulation are evidence of the scope of loss.

## When to Call a Professional Restoration Company

Call a professional if any of the following apply:

- Water has been standing for more than a few hours
- The water reached drywall, insulation, or a wood subfloor
- The affected area is larger than a single small room
- You can smell a musty or earthy odor, that's a sign microbial activity may already be underway
- The water came from a source that may be contaminated (a supply line behind a toilet, near a dishwasher drain, or from an appliance with standing water inside it)
- You're not sure whether the structure is dry

Appliance leaks, a refrigerator water line, a washing machine supply hose, a dishwasher that overflowed, follow the same checklist, but the contamination risk can be higher depending on what the water contacted. ## The Longer Recovery: What Comes After the Water Is Gone

Once the visible water is removed, the recovery process is just beginning. Professional drying typically takes several days, not hours, and involves moisture readings taken at multiple points in the structure to confirm the materials have reached acceptable levels before any repairs begin.

After drying is confirmed:

1. **Damaged drywall and insulation** are removed and replaced, wet insulation does not dry out effectively and must come out.
2. **Subfloor and flooring** are assessed; some can be dried in place, others require replacement.
3. **The pipe itself** is repaired by a licensed plumber before any walls are closed.
4. **Reconstruction**, new drywall, paint, flooring, brings the space back to pre-loss condition.

If your home is older and the pipe failure involved lead-containing solder or older plumbing materials, your restoration contractor should be aware before any demolition begins.

A burst pipe is stressful, but a methodical response limits the damage significantly. Follow the steps above, document thoroughly, and don't let the structure sit wet while you decide what to do next, that window closes fast in Florida's climate.
