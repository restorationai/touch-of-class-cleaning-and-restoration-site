## Why Laurel Hill Properties See Mold Issues

Okaloosa County's inland communities, including Laurel Hill, experience a climate pattern that differs meaningfully from the beachside towns to the south. Afternoon humidity regularly climbs into the upper 80s and 90s percent during summer months, and the dense tree canopy common on residential lots here limits airflow and keeps exterior surfaces shaded and damp. Homes built in the 1970s and 1980s, which make up a significant portion of Laurel Hill's housing stock, often used paper-faced drywall and fiberglass batt insulation in wall cavities. Both materials are highly hospitable to mold colonization once moisture intrudes. Crawl space construction is also common in this part of the Panhandle, and ground moisture vapor rising through an unencapsulated crawl space can silently saturate floor joists and subfloor sheathing for months before anyone notices.

Roof penetrations are another recurring entry point. Older metal roofs and asphalt shingles that have lost their granule coating allow small amounts of water to migrate into attic spaces during heavy rain events, which are frequent here from late spring through early fall. Attic mold is particularly common in Laurel Hill homes precisely because those spaces are rarely inspected between rainy seasons.

## Our Mold Remediation Process in Laurel Hill

When a call comes in, the process starts with a thorough visual inspection and moisture mapping using thermal imaging and calibrated moisture meters. Surface readings alone can be misleading: a wall can feel dry to the touch while the framing behind it reads well above acceptable moisture content. Containment comes next. Affected areas are sealed with polyethylene sheeting and negative air pressure is established using HEPA-filtered air scrubbers to prevent spores from migrating to unaffected rooms during the removal work.

Contaminated porous materials, including drywall, insulation, and in some cases wood framing, are removed and bagged according to established remediation protocols. Structural surfaces that can be cleaned rather than removed are treated with EPA-registered antimicrobial agents. Drying equipment remains in place until moisture readings confirm the structure has returned to acceptable levels. The OSHA-trained crew at Veterans Remediation & Restoration follows documented procedures throughout so that the scope of work is clear and defensible for insurance purposes.

## Local Note

One pattern that comes up repeatedly in Laurel Hill's older ranch-style homes is mold growth along the bottom plate of exterior walls in rooms that were converted from screened porches or carports. These additions were often enclosed without a proper vapor barrier between the new slab and the framing above it. Ground moisture wicks upward through the concrete, saturates the bottom plate, and mold follows. The problem is easy to miss during a standard walkthrough because the affected framing is behind finished drywall, but a moisture meter held at baseboard level will often reveal elevated readings that point directly to the source.
