## Why Harold Properties See Water Damage Issues

The Panhandle's storm season runs longer than most homeowners expect, and Harold properties feel it directly. Tropical systems and slow-moving Gulf thunderstorms can drop several inches of rain in a matter of hours, overwhelming gutters, window seals, and any low-grade grading around a foundation. Because much of the housing stock in this part of Santa Rosa County is pier-and-beam or slab-on-grade construction built to older standards, water finds pathways that newer builds have engineered away.

Crawl space flooding is a recurring issue in the area. When groundwater or stormwater enters a crawl space, it saturates floor joists and subfloor sheathing from below while the surface of the flooring above reads perfectly dry. Standard consumer moisture meters often miss this entirely. Thermal imaging and professional-grade pin meters are the only reliable way to map the true extent of saturation in these conditions.

High ambient humidity compounds every water loss in Harold. Even after standing water is removed, relative humidity in an affected space can stay above 60 percent for days, which is enough to sustain mold colonization on organic materials. Structural drying here is not a passive process; it requires calculated equipment placement and daily moisture readings to confirm the building is actually drying, not just appearing to.

## Our Water Damage Restoration Process in Harold

The first step on every job is a thorough inspection using moisture meters and thermal imaging to map affected materials before a single piece of equipment is placed. That data drives the extraction and drying plan rather than guesswork.

Water extraction comes next. Truck-mounted and portable extractors pull standing water and surface moisture from carpet, hardwood, and subfloor materials. For crawl space flooding, submersible pumps handle bulk water before drying equipment is introduced to the enclosed space below.

Once extraction is complete, the drying phase begins. Commercial-grade desiccant and refrigerant dehumidifiers work alongside high-velocity air movers to create directed airflow through wall cavities, under cabinets, and across subfloor surfaces. Drying targets are set per the IICRC S500 standard, and readings are logged each visit so progress is documented, not assumed.

Materials that cannot be dried in place, including saturated insulation or swollen OSB sheathing, are removed to allow the structure behind them to dry completely. Leaving wet material in place to save time is the most common reason a water loss becomes a mold remediation job weeks later.
