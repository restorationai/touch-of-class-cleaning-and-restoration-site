## Why Hurlburt Field Properties Face Elevated Water Damage Risk

Hurlburt Field's position along the Okaloosa County coast means the water table sits close to grade across much of the area. During heavy rain events, that shallow table limits how quickly saturated soil drains, which puts pressure on slab foundations and can force groundwater through concrete seams and around utility penetrations. The region also sees tropical moisture from the Gulf for a significant portion of the year, keeping indoor relative humidity elevated even between storms. That combination accelerates the timeline for microbial growth once a leak or flood event occurs.

The housing stock near the installation includes a mix of concrete block construction and wood-frame homes built across several decades. Concrete block absorbs and holds moisture differently than wood framing: the block itself may test dry at the surface while the interior cavity and the furring strips behind drywall stay wet for days. Standard moisture meters read the surface; thermal imaging and penetrating probes are what actually tell the story inside those walls.

## Our Water Damage Restoration Process in Hurlburt Field

Every job starts with a full moisture assessment before a single extraction pump is placed. Thermal imaging cameras identify hidden wet zones, and calibrated moisture meters establish baseline readings in walls, subfloors, and ceiling assemblies. That data drives the drying plan rather than guesswork.

Water removal comes next. Truck-mounted and portable extraction units pull standing water from hard floors and carpet, and where water has migrated under flooring, targeted floor mat systems draw it out without requiring a full demolition. Once bulk water is gone, industrial air movers and low-grain refrigerant dehumidifiers are positioned according to the room geometry and the material types present. Daily moisture readings track whether the drying curve is on pace or whether the equipment layout needs adjustment.

Demolition is performed only where materials cannot dry in place. Wet drywall below the flood line, saturated insulation, and compromised flooring are removed, documented with photographs, and reported to your insurance carrier. The OSHA-trained crew handles all debris removal and containment.

## Local Note

Concrete block homes common to this part of Okaloosa County present a drying challenge that crews unfamiliar with Gulf Coast construction sometimes underestimate. The block wicks moisture laterally, meaning a water intrusion event that appears confined to one corner of a room can saturate block several feet beyond the visible wet line. Drying plans here typically require dehumidifier capacity sized for the full room rather than just the affected section, and monitoring points are placed at the perimeter of the block assembly, not just at the origin of the leak. Skipping that step is how a property gets cleared too early and ends up with mold behind the baseboard two weeks later.
