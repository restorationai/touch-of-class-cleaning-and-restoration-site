When water gets into a Paxton home, it rarely stays where it started. It follows wall cavities, soaks into subfloor sheathing, and hides behind baseboards long after the visible puddle is gone. ## Why Paxton Properties See Water Damage Issues

The Florida Panhandle receives some of the highest rainfall totals in the continental United States, and Walton County's inland communities like Paxton feel that in concentrated bursts. Summer thunderstorms can drop several inches in under an hour, overwhelming crawl space drainage and pushing water toward foundation sills. Homes in this part of the county frequently have pier-and-beam or block foundations rather than poured concrete slabs, which means groundwater intrusion can saturate floor joists from below while a homeowner is still trying to find the source of the moisture.

Older construction in the area also tends to use tongue-and-groove subfloor planking rather than modern OSB panels. That wood is durable under normal conditions, but it absorbs standing water aggressively and is slow to release it. Surface readings from a moisture meter can look acceptable while the deeper layers of the floor system are still holding significant moisture, which is exactly the environment where mold colonization begins, typically within 24 to 72 hours of initial saturation.

## Our Water Damage Restoration Process in Paxton

When we arrive, the first step is containment, not extraction. We use thermal imaging and calibrated moisture meters to map where water has actually traveled, not just where it is visible. In Paxton homes with pier-and-beam construction, that inspection always includes the crawl space, because water that enters through a crawl vent or a failed vapor barrier will wick upward into the floor system without any visible sign inside the living space.

Once the moisture map is complete, we begin water removal using truck-mounted and portable extraction units sized to the volume of standing water. Structural drying follows immediately, with industrial air movers and dehumidifiers positioned according to the room geometry and material types. We monitor drying progress with daily readings and adjust equipment placement as materials release moisture. Nothing is packed out or signed off until readings reach the dry standard for the material class, not just the surface.

For water cleanup in finished spaces, we remove only the material that cannot be dried in place. Selective demolition, cutting flood cuts at the appropriate height and removing saturated insulation, keeps reconstruction costs lower and the job scope honest.

## Local Note

In the inland Panhandle, homes that have been through even one prior flood event often have residual moisture trapped in wall cavities that was never fully dried, either because the original cleanup was incomplete or because the homeowner handled it without professional drying equipment. That latent moisture shows up on our thermal camera as a cool spot behind drywall that has no obvious current source. If we find it, we document it and discuss it with you before we begin, because addressing it now, while the walls are already open, costs far less than discovering active mold growth during a future inspection or sale.
