## Why DeFuniak Springs Properties See Water Damage Differently

The Panhandle's subtropical climate means DeFuniak Springs regularly absorbs heavy rainfall, particularly during late-summer storm season when slow-moving Gulf systems can drop several inches in a matter of hours. Unlike coastal communities with engineered drainage corridors, many residential streets and rural parcels in Walton County rely on natural grade and roadside swales. When those systems back up, water finds the path of least resistance: under doors, through crawl spaces, and into garages.

The local housing stock adds another layer of complexity. A significant portion of DeFuniak Springs' residential properties were built in the mid-twentieth century, when vapor barriers were thin or absent and subfloor materials were often tongue-and-groove pine rather than modern OSB. That wood is dense and slow to release moisture, it can read nearly dry at the surface while holding enough saturation below to feed mold growth within 48 to 72 hours of a loss event. Newer construction on the outskirts of town presents its own challenges: concrete block walls wick moisture laterally in ways that standard drywall readings can miss without deeper probing.

## Our Water Cleanup Process in DeFuniak Springs

When a crew arrives, the first priority is stopping active water intrusion if it hasn't been addressed, shutting supply valves, tarping roof breaches, or coordinating with the homeowner on utility shutoffs. From there, the process follows a structured sequence:

**Assessment and moisture mapping.** Thermal imaging and calibrated moisture meters establish where water has traveled, not just where it's visible. In older DeFuniak Springs homes, this step often reveals saturation behind plaster or beneath original hardwood flooring that looks undamaged on the surface.

**Extraction.** Truck-mounted and portable extractors pull standing water from floors, subfloor cavities, and carpet padding. In pier-and-beam homes, crews access the crawl space to remove pooled water and evaluate insulation that may be holding moisture against the subfloor.

**Drying and dehumidification.** Commercial air movers and refrigerant dehumidifiers are positioned based on the moisture map, not just placed at room perimeter. Drying logs are maintained throughout so the process is documentable for insurance purposes.

**Post-dry verification.** Before equipment is removed, readings are taken across all affected materials and compared against established dry standards. Nothing is closed up until the numbers confirm it.
