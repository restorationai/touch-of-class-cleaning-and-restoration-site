## Why DeFuniak Springs Properties See Severe Storm Damage

The Panhandle's geography concentrates risk in ways that set DeFuniak Springs apart from Central or South Florida. Storms that make landfall near Destin or Fort Walton Beach often retain enough energy to push 40–50 miles inland, arriving in Walton County as wind-driven rain events rather than the weakened remnants you'd expect further south. DeFuniak Springs also sits at a slightly elevated inland position compared to coastal communities, which means tornadoes embedded in tropical systems, a real and underappreciated hazard, can track through the area without the friction of dense coastal development to slow them.

The local housing stock adds another layer of vulnerability. A significant portion of homes in the DeFuniak Springs area were built in the mid-20th century, before Florida's post-Andrew building code reforms tightened roof-to-wall connection requirements. Older construction with cut-rafter roof framing and minimal hurricane strapping is far more susceptible to uplift damage than homes built to current Florida Building Code standards. When a ridge vent fails or a section of decking lifts, interior exposure to rain can be measured in gallons per minute, not drips.

## Our Storm Damage Restoration Process in DeFuniak Springs

Every storm job starts with a full exterior and interior assessment before any debris is moved. Once the structure is secured, we extract standing water, set commercial drying equipment, and begin the debris removal process. Tree impact damage, one of the most common storm damage scenarios in this part of Walton County, where mature longleaf and slash pines tower over older neighborhoods, often involves structural assessment before the tree can be safely removed from a roof or wall. Throughout drying, we monitor moisture levels in wall cavities, subfloor assemblies, and ceiling materials on a documented schedule.
