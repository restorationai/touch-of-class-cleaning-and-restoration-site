When a fast-moving storm tears through the installation's surrounding communities, the damage rarely stops at a broken shingle: wind-driven rain finds every gap in siding and soffit, standing water soaks into slab-on-grade foundations, and downed longleaf pines land on rooflines with enough force to compromise structural framing. ## Why Eglin AFB Properties Face Distinct Storm Damage Risks

The Panhandle's sandy soil drains quickly in open areas, but the dense tree canopy surrounding Eglin's residential zones creates a different problem: mature longleaf pines and water oaks grow tall and shallow-rooted in the sandy substrate. When sustained winds arrive with a tropical system or a strong inland squall, those trees topple or shed large limbs at a higher rate than in areas with heavier clay soils that grip root systems deeper. A single downed oak across a roofline can open several hundred square feet of interior to rain within minutes.

The base's proximity to Choctawhatchee Bay also means storm surge and tidal backflow are real considerations during named storms, not just inland flooding from rainfall. Properties on the bay-facing side can take on water from two directions simultaneously: rain coming through a compromised roof and water rising from below grade. That combination saturates wall cavities, subfloor assemblies, and insulation in ways that surface-level inspection misses entirely.

Florida's building code requires wind-rated construction in this region, but older housing stock in the communities adjacent to the base predates some of those standards. Structures built before the post-Andrew code reforms of the mid-1990s may have roof-to-wall connections and sheathing attachment patterns that perform differently under the same wind load than newer construction.

## Our Storm Damage Restoration Process at Eglin AFB

When we arrive on-site, the first priority is stopping active intrusion: tarping breached rooflines, boarding compromised windows and doors, and identifying any structural elements that make the building unsafe to enter. We document everything with photographs and written notes before a single piece of debris moves, because that documentation is what your insurance adjuster needs to process the claim accurately.

Once the structure is secured, we move into damage assessment. Moisture meters and thermal imaging cameras read conditions behind walls and under flooring that look dry to the eye but are already beginning to support microbial growth. In the Panhandle's heat and humidity, the window between a wet wall cavity and an active mold colony is short, often measured in days rather than weeks. We set drying equipment, extract standing water, and establish airflow designed for the specific materials involved, not a one-size approach.

Debris removal runs parallel to drying. Storm debris in this area frequently includes pressure-treated lumber, roofing materials with embedded fasteners, and sections of damaged insulation, all of which require sorted disposal rather than a single dumpster load.
