## Why Ponce de Leon Properties Are Especially Vulnerable to Storm Damage

Holmes County's landscape is dense with longleaf and slash pine, and that's both a feature and a liability. During high-wind events, mature pines along rural lots and highway corridors like US-90 are notorious for uprooting in saturated soil rather than snapping cleanly. A root-ball failure drops the entire trunk, which means roof penetrations are often larger and more irregular than a standard limb strike. Older wood-frame homes common to this part of the Panhandle also tend to have metal roofing that, once a fastener strip fails, can peel back in a single sheet and expose the entire decking to rainfall.

The region's sandy, well-drained soil feels like an advantage until a prolonged storm event saturates it fully. At that point, surface runoff has nowhere to go and begins pushing under crawl-space foundations and through low-set door thresholds. Homes built before modern vapor barrier standards are especially susceptible to that kind of ground-level intrusion.

## Our Storm Damage Restoration Process in Ponce de Leon

Every storm loss is different, but the sequence that prevents the most secondary damage follows a consistent logic. When we arrive on-site, the first priority is making the structure weather-tight: tarping compromised roof sections, boarding window openings, and securing any debris that poses an ongoing hazard. We document every affected surface photographically before anything is moved or removed, which matters when an insurance adjuster reviews the claim.

Once the envelope is stabilized, we assess moisture intrusion. Rain-driven water that enters through a roof breach or blown-in window doesn't stay where it lands. It follows framing cavities downward, pools behind insulation batts, and can reach floor systems within hours. We use thermal imaging and pin-type moisture meters to trace the full path of water movement before any drying equipment is placed. Structural drying in a Panhandle home with older wall assemblies takes longer than a surface reading suggests, and we account for that in the drying plan rather than calling a job complete based on surface numbers alone.

Debris removal, controlled demolition of unsalvageable materials, and coordination with roofing and structural trades follow once the drying baseline is established.
