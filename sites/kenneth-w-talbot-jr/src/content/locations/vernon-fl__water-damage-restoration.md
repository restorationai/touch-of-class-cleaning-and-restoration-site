## Why Vernon Properties See Water Damage Issues

Washington County's rainfall totals are among the highest in the Florida Panhandle, and Vernon's low-lying position near the Choctawhatchee River corridor puts it in the path of runoff that has nowhere fast to go. When heavy rain saturates the surrounding clay soils, groundwater pressure rises against foundations and crawl spaces, forcing moisture through concrete block walls and floor seams that were never designed to hold it back.

The housing stock in and around Vernon skews older, with many homes built in the mid-twentieth century using construction methods that predate modern vapor barriers and pressure-treated subfloor framing. Wood floor joists in crawl space homes absorb standing water quickly, and because the wood is often older-growth pine that has dried and checked over the decades, it wicks moisture deep into the grain. Surface readings from a pin-type moisture meter can look acceptable while the structural members underneath are still well above safe thresholds. Thermal imaging and deep-penetration moisture mapping are essential here, not optional add-ons.

Plumbing in homes of this era frequently runs copper or galvanized steel that has reached or exceeded its service life. Galvanized lines corrode from the inside out, and pinhole failures inside wall cavities can feed water into insulation and framing for days before a stain appears on the ceiling or a floor starts to feel soft underfoot.

## Our Water Damage Restoration Process in Vernon

When a call comes in, the first priority is stopping active water intrusion at the source. That means locating the failure point, whether a burst supply line, a failed water heater, or storm-driven entry at a foundation seam, and coordinating with the homeowner on shutoff before any equipment is staged.

Once the source is controlled, standing water is extracted using truck-mounted and portable extraction units. In Vernon's crawl space homes, that often means getting equipment into tight clearances under the floor to pull water from the ground surface and from the underside of the subfloor before it wicks further into the structure above.

After extraction, the drying phase begins. The Gulf Coast's naturally high ambient humidity works against evaporation, so commercial-grade desiccant and refrigerant dehumidifiers are positioned to create a controlled drying environment rather than simply moving ambient air around. Airflow is directed at cavities, not just surfaces. Moisture readings are logged at each visit so the drying curve is documented and the structure is not released until readings confirm it has returned to normal dry-standard levels.

Materials that cannot be dried in place, saturated insulation, compromised drywall below the flood line, or subfloor panels that have begun to swell and delaminate, are removed and disposed of properly before they become a mold substrate.

## Local Note

One thing that comes up regularly in Washington County's older homes is the presence of board-and-batten or tongue-and-groove interior wall sheathing beneath later layers of drywall or paneling. When water gets into these assemblies, the outer surface can feel and read nearly dry while the original wood sheathing behind it holds significant moisture. Crews working in Vernon need to probe and image through the finish layer, not just read the face of it, or a remediation that looks complete on paper can produce mold growth in the concealed cavity within a few weeks.
