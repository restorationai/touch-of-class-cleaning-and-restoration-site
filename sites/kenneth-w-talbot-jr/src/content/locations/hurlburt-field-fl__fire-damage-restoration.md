## Why Hurlburt Field Properties Face Distinct Fire Damage Challenges

The Gulf Coast climate is the defining variable in how fire damage behaves here. Relative humidity along this stretch of the Florida Panhandle routinely climbs above 80 percent, even in the cooler months. That moisture interacts with smoke residue in ways that accelerate deterioration: soot becomes sticky and harder to remove, odor molecules bind more deeply into drywall, insulation, and wood framing, and secondary water damage from firefighting efforts dries far more slowly than it would in a drier inland climate.

Homes in the Hurlburt Field area also tend to reflect the construction patterns common to post-war military-adjacent communities along the Panhandle. Many properties were built with concrete block exteriors, which resist flame spread but trap smoke odor inside wall cavities in ways that standard cleaning cannot reach without targeted thermal fogging or hydroxyl treatment. Crawl spaces and attic spaces in this region also accumulate ambient moisture year-round, which means fire-damaged structural members can begin supporting mold growth faster than national averages suggest.

## Our Fire Damage Restoration Process in Hurlburt Field

The first thing our crew does on arrival is assess structural safety and document every affected surface before anything is moved or cleaned. That documentation matters for your insurance claim and for understanding the full scope of damage, smoke travels further than most homeowners expect, infiltrating HVAC systems, closets, and rooms that appear untouched.

From there, the process moves through several distinct phases:

- **Board-up and tarping** to secure the structure against Okaloosa County's afternoon rain events, which can compound water intrusion into fire-damaged roof assemblies almost immediately after a loss.
- **Soot and smoke residue removal** using dry chemical sponges, HEPA vacuuming, and wet-cleaning methods matched to the specific surface type. Painted concrete block, wood trim, and drywall each respond to different techniques.
- **Odor neutralization** using thermal fogging and hydroxyl generators, which are particularly important in Gulf Coast homes where ambient humidity has already driven odor molecules deep into porous materials.
- **Structural drying and assessment** to identify whether framing, subfloor, or insulation needs removal before reconstruction begins.
- **Reconstruction coordination**, scoped to what the structure actually needs rather than a templated package.

Every step is documented with photographs and moisture readings, giving your insurance adjuster a clear, defensible record of the work performed.
