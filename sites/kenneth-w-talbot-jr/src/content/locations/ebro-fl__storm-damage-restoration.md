## Why Ebro Properties See Serious Storm Damage

Washington County's geography sets the stage for damage that surprises people who moved here from farther inland. Ebro and the surrounding rural corridor receive the tail end of Gulf tropical systems that have already dumped rain on the coast, but they also sit directly in the path of fast-moving inland tornadoes and severe squall lines that form over the Choctawhatchee River basin. The result is a pattern of wind-driven rain, large-diameter tree strikes, and flash flooding that can overwhelm even well-maintained properties.

The housing stock in this part of Washington County leans toward older wood-frame construction, manufactured homes, and metal-roofed agricultural buildings. Older wood-frame structures are particularly vulnerable because roof sheathing installed decades ago was often thinner and fastened with fewer nails per square foot than current Florida Building Code requires. When a tree limb punches through that kind of roof, the opening is rarely clean, and water moves into wall cavities and subfloor framing before the storm has even passed.

Sandy, fast-draining soil in this region does reduce some surface flooding risk, but it also means tree root systems are shallower than in clay-heavy soils. Longleaf and slash pines common throughout the county can topple in sustained winds that would not fell the same species in denser soil, making tree-strike damage one of the most frequent storm loss types in the area.

## Our Storm Damage Restoration Process in Ebro

Every storm job starts with a methodical damage assessment before any materials are moved or discarded. Our OSHA-trained team documents roof breaches, structural penetrations, and water intrusion points with photos and written notes, creating a clear record for your insurance carrier. That documentation matters from the first hour on-site.

Once the scope is established, the sequence follows a logical order:

- **Emergency tarping and board-up**: Open roof sections and broken windows are secured first to stop active water entry and protect the interior from additional weather exposure.
- **Water extraction and drying**: Any standing water is removed, and affected structural materials, flooring, and wall cavities are dried using commercial air movers and dehumidifiers calibrated to the ambient humidity conditions typical of a Panhandle summer.
- **Debris and tree removal coordination**: Large tree strikes often require coordination with a licensed tree service before structural repair can begin. We work alongside that process rather than waiting for it to finish before we start.
- **Structural repair and reconstruction**: Damaged sheathing, framing, roofing materials, drywall, and flooring are repaired or replaced to bring the structure back to pre-loss condition.

Throughout every phase, we keep communication open with you and with your insurance adjuster so there are no surprises when the scope changes, as it often does once walls are opened.

## Local Note

One pattern worth knowing in this part of Washington County: after a storm, metal roofs on older agricultural and residential buildings often show no visible exterior damage, but the fasteners along the ridge cap and eave flashing have lifted just enough to allow water to wick under the metal during the next rain. That hidden entry point can saturate insulation and ceiling framing for weeks before interior staining appears. If your property has a metal roof and you experienced high winds, it is worth having the fastener line inspected even if the roof looks intact from the ground.
