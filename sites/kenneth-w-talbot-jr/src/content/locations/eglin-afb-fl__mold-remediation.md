## Why Eglin AFB Properties See Mold Issues

The climate around Eglin is not just humid in the abstract. The base and its surrounding communities sit between Choctawhatchee Bay and the Gulf, which means ambient relative humidity stays elevated even between rain events. When that outside air infiltrates a building through gaps in the envelope, it meets cool interior surfaces created by air conditioning and deposits moisture. Attic spaces, wall cavities behind exterior-facing drywall, and the underside of elevated floor systems are the first places mold takes hold, often invisible until the musty odor becomes impossible to ignore.

The housing stock around Eglin adds another layer of complexity. Many residential units in the surrounding communities were built during or shortly after the base's major mid-century expansions, using construction methods that left limited vapor control between conditioned and unconditioned spaces. Crawl spaces with inadequate ground cover and minimal cross-ventilation are particularly common, and standing ground moisture in those spaces can sustain mold growth year-round without any single dramatic water event to trigger it.

## Our Mold Remediation Process in Eglin AFB

Every job starts with a visual inspection and moisture mapping. We use thermal imaging and calibrated moisture meters to find wet material that looks dry at the surface, because in Florida's climate, surface readings can be misleading. Mold behind drywall or inside wall cavities will not appear on a surface scan.

Once affected areas are identified, we establish negative-air containment using poly barriers and HEPA-filtered air scrubbers. This keeps spores from migrating to clean areas of the structure during removal. Affected porous materials, typically drywall, insulation, and organic-based flooring underlayment, are removed and bagged for disposal. Non-porous surfaces are treated with an EPA-registered antimicrobial agent. We then run HEPA air scrubbers through the clearance phase to reduce airborne spore counts before any reconstruction begins.

In Eglin's climate, we pay particular attention to the drying phase. Replacing materials before the underlying structure reaches acceptable moisture levels is one of the most common reasons mold returns. We verify structural wood and framing moisture content before signing off.

The Florida Panhandle's combination of sandy, well-draining soil and high water tables creates a specific crawl space problem that is easy to overlook. Even when surface drainage appears fine after a rain event, the water table beneath a slab or crawl space can rise enough to push moisture upward through the ground cover. Properties near the low-lying areas around Eglin that have older or inadequate vapor barriers in their crawl spaces can develop persistent mold on floor joists and subfloor sheathing without any visible leak above grade. If a musty smell is strongest near the floor, that is often the first sign to check below rather than above.
