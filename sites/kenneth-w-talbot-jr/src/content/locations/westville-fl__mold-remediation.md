## Why Westville Properties See Mold Issues

Holmes County's climate is the primary driver. Summer relative humidity in the Florida Panhandle regularly climbs above 80 percent, and Westville is no exception. When that outdoor air infiltrates a home through crawl space vents, aging window seals, or roof penetrations, it meets cooler interior surfaces and condenses. Mold colonization can begin within 24 to 48 hours of a moisture event, and in a crawl space with inadequate vapor barrier coverage, it can spread across floor joists for weeks before anyone notices.

The housing stock in and around Westville adds another layer of risk. Many homes in the area were built in the mid-20th century with pier-and-beam foundations and wood subfloors, construction methods that leave crawl spaces exposed to ground moisture year-round. Unlike poured concrete slab homes, pier-and-beam structures allow soil moisture to wick upward continuously. When a plumbing leak or roof intrusion adds to that baseline moisture load, the conditions for heavy mold growth arrive quickly.

Heavy rainfall events, which are common throughout the Panhandle from late spring through early fall, also push water into low-lying areas. Homes on lots with poor drainage or compacted clay soil can see groundwater intrusion at the foundation perimeter, keeping crawl spaces damp long after the rain stops.

## Our Mold Remediation Process in Westville

Every remediation starts with a thorough moisture and mold assessment. Before any physical work begins, the crew identifies the moisture source, because removing mold without stopping the water entry is a temporary fix at best. Once the source is addressed or isolated, containment is established using polyethylene sheeting and negative air pressure to prevent spores from migrating to unaffected areas of the home.

Affected materials are removed according to industry-standard protocols. Porous materials like drywall, insulation, and compromised wood framing that cannot be adequately cleaned are removed and disposed of properly. Non-porous surfaces are HEPA-vacuumed, treated with an EPA-registered antimicrobial, and dried to verified moisture levels using professional-grade dehumidifiers and air movers. In crawl spaces, which are a frequent problem area in Westville's older homes, encapsulation or vapor barrier replacement is often part of the scope.

Air scrubbers with HEPA filtration run throughout the project to capture airborne spores. The job isn't considered complete until moisture readings confirm the structure is dry and post-remediation conditions no longer support mold growth.
