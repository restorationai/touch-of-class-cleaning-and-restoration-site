## Why Paxton Properties See Serious Storm Damage

The Florida Panhandle experiences a long and active hurricane season, but named storms are not the only threat. Paxton and the surrounding Walton County area are also in the path of severe thunderstorm lines that produce straight-line winds, microbursts, and occasional tornadoes. These events are fast and localized, which means one property can take a direct hit while the house next door has nothing more than wet leaves on the driveway.

The housing stock in this part of rural Florida is a mix of older wood-frame homes and newer manufactured and modular construction. Both present specific challenges after a storm. Older wood-frame structures can absorb wind-driven rain into wall cavities, subfloor systems, and attic insulation before the damage is visible from inside. Manufactured homes, while built to HUD wind standards, are particularly vulnerable to roof membrane separation and skirting damage that allows water to pool beneath the structure. Either way, what looks like surface damage at first inspection often has a deeper moisture component that requires professional drying equipment, not just a shop vac and fans.

Sandy, well-drained soils common in this part of the Panhandle do help surface water move away from foundations faster than the clay-heavy soils further north, but that advantage disappears when a storm drops several inches of rain in a short window. Gutters overwhelmed by debris, downspouts pushed off-grade by fallen branches, and low-lying crawl spaces can all collect water faster than the ground can accept it.

## Our Storm Damage Restoration Process in Paxton

When we arrive after a storm, the first priority is a thorough assessment before any work begins. We document structural damage, roof penetrations, and water intrusion points with photographs and moisture readings. This documentation matters both for your insurance claim and for understanding the full scope of what needs to be addressed.

Tarping and board-up happen immediately if the structure is open to weather. A roof penetration left unprotected while adjusters schedule inspections can turn a manageable claim into a mold situation within 48 to 72 hours in Florida's humidity. Once the structure is secured, we begin water extraction and drying using commercial-grade dehumidifiers and air movers calibrated to the actual moisture readings in the affected materials, not a generic drying schedule.

Tree and debris removal is coordinated as part of the job, not handed off to a separate contractor with a different timeline. If a pine has punched through a roof deck, the debris removal and the structural protection need to happen together, not in two separate visits.

## Local Note

In the rural stretches of Walton County around Paxton, properties often sit on larger lots with mature longleaf pine and water oak. These trees are a genuine asset most of the year, but after a storm they become the most common source of roof damage in the area. Longleaf pine root systems can be shallow in the sandy soils here, making them more susceptible to windthrow than their size suggests. If you have large trees within falling distance of your structure, a post-storm inspection of the root zone is worth doing even when the tree looks intact, because a partially uprooted tree can shift days after the storm passes.
