## Why Destin Properties Are Especially Vulnerable to Storm Damage

Destin's building stock skews heavily toward vacation rentals, condominiums, and resort-adjacent townhomes, many of them constructed during the rapid coastal development of the 1980s and 1990s. Roofing systems from that era often predate Florida's post-Hurricane Andrew building code reforms, which means older shingle and flat-roof assemblies may lack the wind-uplift resistance now required in Okaloosa County. When a storm peels back a section of aged roofing membrane or drives water under deteriorated flashing, the interior can absorb significant moisture before anyone notices, especially on properties that sit vacant between rental seasons.

The bay-side of Destin adds another layer of complexity. Properties along Choctawhatchee Bay face storm surge risk from a direction many owners don't anticipate. While Gulf-front homes are built with surge in mind, bay-side structures sometimes have lower finished-floor elevations and fewer flood-hardening features. A fast-moving tropical system can push bay water inland well before the Gulf side sees its worst conditions.

Tree canopy is a factor too. Mature live oaks and slash pines throughout Destin's residential corridors are beautiful in calm weather and a liability when sustained winds arrive. A large limb or uprooted tree through a roof or exterior wall creates an immediate water intrusion pathway, and in Destin's climate, you have a narrow window before secondary damage compounds the primary loss.

## Our Storm Damage Restoration Process in Destin

The first priority after a storm is stopping additional water from entering the structure. Our crew performs emergency tarping and board-up as needed, then conducts a full moisture survey using thermal imaging and calibrated moisture meters, not just a visual walk-through. Water behind plaster or inside wall cavities won't show up on the surface, but it will register on instruments, and finding it early is the difference between a contained drying project and a mold remediation job three weeks later.

Once the scope is mapped, we extract standing water, remove saturated materials that can't be effectively dried in place, and deploy commercial-grade drying equipment sized to the actual moisture load, not a one-size estimate. In Destin's warm, humid climate, ambient conditions slow evaporative drying, so equipment selection and placement matter more here than in drier regions. We monitor drying progress with daily readings and adjust as needed until affected assemblies reach target moisture levels.

Structural repairs, framing, sheathing, roofing substrate, follow once the structure is confirmed dry. We document every phase with photographs and moisture logs, which your insurance adjuster will need for a complete claim file.
