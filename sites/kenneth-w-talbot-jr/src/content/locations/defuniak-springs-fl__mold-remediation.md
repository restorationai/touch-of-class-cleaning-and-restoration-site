## Why DeFuniak Springs Properties See Mold Issues

The Panhandle's climate is the first factor. DeFuniak Springs regularly sees summer relative humidity above 80 percent, and the region's afternoon thunderstorms are intense enough to drive water through aging window seals, soffit vents, and roof penetrations that look fine from the ground. Homes near the Circle Drive historic district, where some of the oldest residential structures in Walton County still stand, often have original wood framing, tongue-and-groove sheathing, and minimal vapor barriers. That combination holds moisture far longer than modern construction, giving mold a deep foothold before surface staining even appears.

Crawl spaces are another recurring problem. Much of DeFuniak Springs sits on sandy Panhandle soil that drains quickly after rain but allows ground vapor to rise freely into unencapsulated crawl spaces. Once that vapor contacts the cooler underside of a wood subfloor, condensation forms, and mold follows. Homeowners often discover the issue only when a musty odor reaches the living space or when flooring begins to feel soft underfoot.

## Our Mold Remediation Process in DeFuniak Springs

Every job starts with a thorough assessment, visible growth is documented, but the inspection goes further into wall cavities, attic decking, and crawl space joists where mold hides behind surfaces that read dry to the touch. Moisture mapping with calibrated meters identifies the source driving active growth; without addressing that source, any remediation is temporary.

Containment comes next. Poly sheeting and negative air pressure isolate the affected area so spores disturbed during removal don't migrate to clean rooms. HEPA-filtered air scrubbers run continuously throughout the work. Contaminated materials, drywall, insulation, wood framing that cannot be cleaned to standard, are bagged and removed. Salvageable structural wood is treated with EPA-registered antimicrobial agents and dried to verified moisture content before any rebuild work begins. The process follows IICRC S520 mold remediation principles, which set the industry standard for what "clean" actually means at the end of a job.

Older homes in the DeFuniak Springs area, particularly those built before central air conditioning was standard, were often constructed with wide roof overhangs and cross-ventilation in mind rather than tight building envelopes.
