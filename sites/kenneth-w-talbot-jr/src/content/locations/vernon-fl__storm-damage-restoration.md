## Why Vernon Properties See Storm Damage Differently

Washington County's landscape is heavily wooded, and Vernon's residential areas reflect that. Mature longleaf and slash pines tower over homes that in many cases were built decades ago with roof framing and siding that was not engineered for the wind loads now associated with Gulf-influenced storm systems. When a tree falls across a roofline, the breach is rarely clean: insulation gets compressed and wet, ceiling drywall can delaminate within hours, and if the structure has a crawl space, driven rain finds its way in through foundation vents before the storm even passes.

The clay-heavy soils common to this part of the Florida panhandle drain slowly. That matters because standing water around a foundation after a severe storm does not simply recede the way it would on sandier ground closer to the coast. Water can wick upward into subfloor materials and wall cavities while the yard still looks flooded, making the visible damage a poor guide to the actual scope of what needs to be dried out.

## Our Storm Damage Restoration Process in Vernon

Every storm loss is different, but the sequence of work follows a disciplined order regardless of what the storm left behind.

The first step is a thorough damage assessment. Before any debris is moved or tarps are placed, the affected areas are documented with photos and moisture readings. This baseline matters for your insurance claim and for understanding where water has already migrated.

Emergency stabilization comes next. That means tarping compromised roof sections, boarding openings where windows or doors have been breached, and extracting any standing water from interior spaces. The goal is to stop additional damage from rain, wind, or secondary intrusion while the full scope of repairs is being scoped.

Once the structure is stabilized, drying equipment is deployed. Industrial air movers and dehumidifiers are positioned based on moisture mapping, not guesswork. Readings are logged throughout the drying period so there is a documented record that materials have returned to acceptable moisture levels before any reconstruction begins.

Debris removal, structural repairs, and finish work follow in coordination with the drying timeline. Rushing reconstruction before materials are genuinely dry is one of the most common causes of mold problems in the weeks after a storm.

## Local Note

One thing that comes up repeatedly in Vernon and the surrounding Washington County area is the presence of older homes with metal roofing that has been re-screwed or patched over the years. When a storm lifts a section of that roofing, the damage pattern underneath is often more complex than a simple shingle loss: the decking may have absorbed moisture from previous small leaks that were never fully addressed, meaning the structural drying scope is larger than the visible roof opening would suggest. A moisture meter reading at the decking level, not just at the ceiling below, is essential before any repair estimate is finalized.
