## Why Wright Properties See Water Damage Issues

The Florida Panhandle receives some of the heaviest seasonal rainfall in the continental United States, and Wright is no exception. Tropical systems and afternoon thunderstorms can dump several inches of rain in a matter of hours, overwhelming gutters, grading, and crawl space drainage systems that were not designed for that kind of volume. Many homes in this part of Okaloosa County were built on slab foundations, which limits below-grade flooding but does not protect against water that enters through the building envelope, around windows, through soffit gaps, or along the wall-to-slab transition.

Plumbing failures are equally common. The high mineral content in local water supplies accelerates corrosion in older supply lines, and the temperature swings between winter cold fronts and summer heat stress copper and CPVC fittings over time. A pinhole leak behind a cabinet or inside a wall can saturate the framing and insulation for days before it shows up as a stain or a smell.

## Our Water Damage Restoration Process in Wright

When the call comes in, the first priority is stopping any active water source and getting eyes on the full scope of the damage, not just the visible wet area. Water travels. It follows framing members, runs along vapor barriers, and pools in the lowest accessible point of a structure. Thermal imaging and calibrated moisture meters let the crew map the actual wet zone, which is almost always larger than what a homeowner can see.

Once the scope is documented, water extraction begins using truck-mounted and portable extraction units capable of pulling standing water from carpet, hardwood, and concrete. After bulk water is removed, the drying phase starts. Industrial air movers and commercial-grade dehumidifiers are positioned to create a controlled drying environment. In Wright's climate, ambient humidity outside can exceed 80 percent on a typical summer afternoon, so the equipment load has to account for that vapor pressure, running undersized residential dehumidifiers in this environment will extend a drying project by days and risk secondary mold colonization, which IICRC guidance places at roughly 48 to 72 hours after initial saturation.

Moisture readings are logged daily. Nothing is signed off as dry until the readings return to baseline for the materials in question, not just surface readings, but deep-wall and subfloor readings as well.

## Local Note

One pattern that comes up repeatedly in Wright and the surrounding Okaloosa County communities is water damage that originates at the roofline during storm events and then travels inside the wall cavity to the slab level before it is discovered. Because slab-on-grade construction has no crawl space to inspect, that water often pools silently against the interior face of the slab and wicks into the bottom plate of the wall framing. By the time a homeowner notices a baseboard stain or a musty smell near the floor, the bottom plate and the lower courses of drywall may already be compromised. Thermal imaging on the exterior walls from floor level up is the only reliable way to catch this pattern early.
