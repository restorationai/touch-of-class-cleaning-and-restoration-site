## Why Crestview Properties Are Especially Vulnerable to Storm Damage

Crestview's position roughly 40 miles north of the Gulf Coast means it sits in the path of both tropical systems tracking northeast and severe inland thunderstorms that build over the Panhandle in spring and summer. The city's elevation and piney terrain create a specific hazard: mature longleaf and slash pines, common throughout residential areas, have shallow root systems that make them prone to uprooting in sustained winds. A toppled pine doesn't just punch through a roof, it can shear rafters, crack ridge beams, and leave the interior exposed to whatever rain follows.

The housing stock compounds the risk. A significant portion of Crestview's homes were built in the 1960s through 1980s, predating Florida's post-Hurricane Andrew building code overhaul. Older construction often features roof-to-wall connections that don't meet current strap and clip requirements, making them more susceptible to partial roof lifting during high-wind events. When those roofs fail even partially, water intrusion can be rapid and widespread, soaking attic insulation, wicking into wall cavities, and reaching subfloor sheathing before the storm has even passed.

## Our Storm Damage Restoration Process in Crestview

The first priority after any storm event is stabilization, preventing additional damage from an unsecured opening or a compromised structure. When we arrive, we assess the exterior envelope first: roof penetrations, broken windows, displaced siding, and any structural elements that could shift further. Emergency tarping and board-up happen before interior work begins, because drying out a space that's still open to the elements is a losing effort.

Once the structure is secured, we move to moisture mapping. Wind-driven rain behaves differently than a plumbing leak, it can penetrate at odd angles, travel along roof decking, and appear in wall cavities far from the obvious entry point. We use thermal imaging and moisture meters to trace the full extent of saturation before any materials are removed. That documentation also forms the foundation of your insurance claim, capturing affected square footage, material types, and moisture readings at the time of inspection.

Debris removal, including tree limbs, displaced roofing materials, and damaged contents, is handled alongside the structural assessment. In Crestview, where storm debris frequently includes large pine sections, we coordinate with appropriate equipment to clear safely before interior crews begin extraction and drying.
