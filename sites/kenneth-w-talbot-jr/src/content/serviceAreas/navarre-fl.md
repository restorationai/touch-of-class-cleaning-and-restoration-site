## Restoration emergencies common in Navarre

Navarre sits on the Santa Rosa County coastline, wedged between the Gulf of Mexico and Santa Rosa Sound, which means tropical systems and heavy summer thunderstorms are a regular part of life here rather than a rare event. Wind-driven rain finds its way through roof flashing and window seals during named storms, and the area's high water table pushes groundwater up through slab penetrations and crawlspace vents even when a storm never makes direct landfall. Salt air and humidity accelerate corrosion on HVAC lines, water heaters, and plumbing fittings, so slow leaks inside walls often go unnoticed until flooring or drywall starts to fail. Between storm season and the everyday humidity load, mold colonization can start within 24 to 48 hours of any unaddressed moisture intrusion, which is why timing matters as much as thoroughness once a leak is found.

Much of Navarre's housing stock was built from the 1970s onward, with a heavy wave of construction after Hurricane Opal and Hurricane Ivan reshaped building requirements along this stretch of coast.
