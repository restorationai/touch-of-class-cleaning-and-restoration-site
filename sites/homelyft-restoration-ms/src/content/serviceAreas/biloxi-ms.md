## Restoration emergencies common in Biloxi

Biloxi sits on a narrow peninsula between the Mississippi Sound and the Back Bay, which means the water table is rarely more than a few feet below grade. A slow pipe leak in a slab-on-grade home doesn't just wet the flooring — it can saturate the subbase and wick moisture into wall cavities for weeks before anyone notices the smell. The city's housing stock compounds this: a large share of Biloxi's residential properties were built between the 1950s and 1980s, before modern vapor barriers and hurricane-rated sheathing became standard. Many of those homes have been repaired, re-roofed, and re-sided multiple times since Hurricane Katrina, and each repair cycle can leave hidden seams where wind-driven rain finds a path inside.

The Gulf's humidity — routinely above 80 percent from May through September — means that mold can begin colonizing wet drywall in as little as 24 to 48 hours after a water intrusion. Biloxi also sits in a high-wind zone, so roof damage from named storms and pop-up squalls is a recurring driver of interior water damage, particularly in older neighborhoods where roofing underlayment has aged past its rated service life.

## Services we provide in Biloxi

### Water damage restoration

Water damage in Biloxi often starts at the roof or at grade — either wind-driven rain pushing under aging shingles or groundwater infiltrating through foundation cracks during heavy rainfall events. We extract standing water, set commercial drying equipment calibrated to the ambient humidity, and monitor moisture readings daily until the structure meets dry standard. We document the full scope and communicate directly with your insurance adjuster.

### Mold remediation

The combination of heat, humidity, and older construction makes Biloxi one of the more mold-active markets on the Gulf Coast. We contain affected areas, remove compromised materials, and treat surfaces according to established remediation protocols. We don't offer mold testing or clearance sampling — those are independent third-party functions — but we can refer you to qualified industrial hygienists operating in the area.

### Fire and smoke damage restoration

Smoke residue from a kitchen fire behaves differently in a humid coastal environment than it does in a dry climate. Soot particles absorb ambient moisture and bond more aggressively to porous surfaces like drywall, wood trim, and upholstery. We clean, deodorize, and where necessary remove and replace structural materials, working from a detailed scope so your insurer can see exactly what was done and why.

### Storm damage and emergency board-up

After a named storm or a severe line of thunderstorms rolls through the Sound, exposed roof decks and broken windows need to be secured before the next rain event causes a secondary loss. We provide emergency tarping and board-up services to limit further damage while the full restoration scope is being scoped and approved.

### Reconstruction

Once mitigation is complete, many property owners want a single contractor to carry the project through to finished condition — drywall, flooring, cabinetry, paint. We handle light-to-moderate reconstruction so you're not managing a separate general contractor during an already stressful process.

## Building stock, site conditions, and permits in Biloxi

Biloxi's older housing tends to sit on brick or masonry piers with a vented crawlspace, while the postwar and later homes are mostly slab-on-grade. That split matters: a crawlspace over sandy coastal soil stays humid year-round and rots band joists and subfloor from below, while a slab traps water under flooring and pushes it into the bottom plates. Mid-century homes here commonly still have galvanized supply lines and cast iron drains, both of which fail from the inside out, and 1980s work can hide polybutylene. Anything built before 1980 may carry asbestos floor tile, mastic, or ceiling texture, and pre-1978 paint gets treated as lead until proven otherwise.

Structural repair and rebuild permits come from the City of Biloxi's building department. Flood-zone rules and the substantial-improvement threshold can pull an elevation requirement into a rebuild, and coastal wind provisions govern roof and sheathing work.
