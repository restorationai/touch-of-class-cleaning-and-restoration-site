## Restoration emergencies common in Moss Point

Moss Point sits at the confluence of the Escatawpa and Pascagoula Rivers, which means the water table is rarely far below grade. After a significant rain event, that moisture doesn't just pool in yards — it migrates under slabs, saturates crawl spaces, and wicks into wall cavities before a homeowner notices the smell. The city's housing stock skews older, with a meaningful share of homes built before modern moisture barriers and vapor retarders were standard practice. Older cast-iron and galvanized supply lines in those homes are prone to pinhole leaks that go undetected for weeks.

The Gulf Coast's subtropical humidity — routinely above 70 percent even in mild months — means that any wet material left unaddressed for more than 24 to 48 hours is already in the window where mold colonization becomes likely. Tropical systems and named storms are a recurring reality here; Jackson County has seen repeated federally declared disaster events tied to wind-driven rain and storm surge. That combination of aging infrastructure, high ambient humidity, and storm exposure is why Moss Point property owners deal with water, mold, and storm damage at rates that consistently outpace drier inland markets.
