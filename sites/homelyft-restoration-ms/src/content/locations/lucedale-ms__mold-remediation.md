## Why Lucedale Properties See Mold Issues

The geography here works against homeowners in a few specific ways. Lucedale sits in a bowl-shaped section of George County where stormwater drains slowly after heavy rain events. Homes built on crawl spaces — which describes a large share of the residential stock throughout the city — trap ground-level moisture vapor underneath living areas year-round. When that vapor has nowhere to go, it condenses on floor joists, subfloor sheathing, and HVAC ductwork. The result is often a musty odor that homeowners notice first in bedrooms and closets before they ever see visible growth.

Older homes in and around Lucedale also frequently used kraft-faced insulation and untreated wood framing that provides a ready food source for Cladosporium, Penicillium, and Aspergillus species — the molds most commonly identified during our assessments in this area. Black mold (Stachybotrys chartarum) tends to appear where there has been a sustained moisture intrusion, such as a slow roof leak above a ceiling cavity or a supply-line pinhole that has been weeping inside a wall for months.

## Our Mold Remediation Process in Lucedale

Every job starts with a thorough visual inspection and moisture mapping using thermal imaging and calibrated moisture meters — not a quick walk-through. We document affected materials with photographs and measurements before any work begins, which matters both for your insurance claim and for confirming that the remediation scope is accurate.

Containment comes next. We seal off the affected area with poly sheeting and establish negative air pressure using HEPA-filtered air scrubbers so that disturbed spores cannot migrate into unaffected rooms. In pier-and-beam homes common throughout Lucedale, that sometimes means establishing a separate containment zone in the crawl space itself, which requires different staging than a slab-foundation home.

Affected porous materials — drywall sections, insulation batts, compromised subfloor panels — are removed, bagged, and disposed of per Mississippi DEQ guidelines. Remaining structural surfaces are HEPA-vacuumed, treated with an EPA-registered antimicrobial, and dried to confirmed moisture levels before containment is removed. We hold an EPA Lead-Safe certification, which is relevant on pre-1978 homes in Lucedale where disturbing building materials can expose lead-based paint during demolition work.

Final air clearance testing, conducted by an independent industrial hygienist rather than our own crew, confirms that airborne spore counts have returned to normal background levels before the job is closed out.
