## Our Carpet Cleaning Process in Glendale

A surface-level pass with a rental machine won't address what's actually happening in your carpet. The process starts with a dry pre-vacuum using commercial-grade equipment to pull out loose particulate before any moisture is introduced — skipping this step is the single most common reason DIY cleaning leaves residue. Next, a pH-balanced pre-treatment is applied to break down oil-based soils and loosen clay particulate from the fiber structure. Hot-water extraction then flushes the pile, backing, and upper pad layer, removing suspended soils along with cleaning solution. Extraction pressure and water temperature are calibrated to the carpet type — lower heat for older berber or wool-blend rugs common in some of Glendale's established residential areas, higher temperature for commercial-grade loop pile in office or retail settings.

After extraction, we apply a post-treatment rinse that neutralizes any alkaline residue, which is a step that matters in high-humidity environments because residue left in the fiber attracts soil faster and creates the sticky feel many homeowners notice after a poor cleaning job. Drying time is accelerated with air movers positioned to push airflow across the carpet surface — in Glendale's summer humidity, skipping this step can leave a damp pad for 24 hours or more, which is enough time for mildew to establish.

## Stain Removal and Carpet Sanitization

Not every stain responds to the same chemistry. Pet urine, red clay tracked in from Glendale's outdoor spaces, food-based stains, and mold-related discoloration each require a different treatment approach. Enzyme-based treatments are used for protein stains — pet waste, food, blood — because they break down the organic compound rather than masking it. Oxidizing agents handle tannin stains like coffee and red clay without bleaching the surrounding fiber. For carpets where odor is the primary concern, we apply a sanitizing treatment after extraction that addresses bacteria at the fiber and pad surface — this is particularly relevant in homes that have experienced any water intrusion, since wet pad is a rapid incubator for microbial growth. Our team holds IICRC certification, which means the cleaning protocols and drying standards we follow are grounded in the same technical framework used for water damage restoration — not a consumer-grade interpretation of what "deep clean" means.
