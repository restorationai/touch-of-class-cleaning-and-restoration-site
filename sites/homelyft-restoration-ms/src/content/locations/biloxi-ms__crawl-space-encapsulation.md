Biloxi sits just inches above the Gulf, and that proximity to water isn't only a concern during hurricane season — it's a year-round reality underneath your home. The combination of high ambient humidity, sandy coastal soils that drain poorly after heavy rain, and the region's long, humid summers creates crawl space conditions that accelerate wood rot, mold colonization, and structural deterioration faster than almost anywhere else in Mississippi. Crawl space encapsulation addresses those conditions at the source, sealing the ground and perimeter so that outside air and moisture stop cycling through the space beneath your floors.

## Why Biloxi Properties Are Especially Vulnerable Below the Foundation

Biloxi's climate sits in a category of its own even compared to other Gulf Coast cities. Average relative humidity hovers above 75% for much of the year, and the soil composition along the coastline — a mix of sand, silt, and fill material deposited over decades of development — holds moisture close to the surface long after a rainstorm passes. That standing ground moisture evaporates upward into an open crawl space, where it condenses on floor joists, insulation, and subfloor sheathing.

Older homes in Biloxi, particularly those built before the 1980s, were constructed with ventilated crawl spaces — a design standard that made sense in drier climates but actively works against homeowners here. Those vents pull in warm, humid Gulf air, which then meets the cooler surfaces inside the crawl space and deposits moisture directly onto structural wood. Within 48 to 72 hours of a significant rain event, that moisture can begin supporting mold growth. Over a season or two, the same conditions soften floor joists and compromise the integrity of the subfloor above.

Post-Katrina rebuilds and elevated slab construction have changed some of Biloxi's housing stock, but a large portion of the city's residential properties — particularly on the north side away from the waterfront — still sit on traditional pier-and-beam or block foundations with open crawl spaces that were never sealed.

## Our Crawl Space Encapsulation Process in Biloxi

Every encapsulation project starts with a thorough crawl space inspection — not a quick look with a flashlight, but a documented assessment of moisture readings, wood moisture content, existing insulation condition, and any signs of mold or pest activity. In Biloxi's environment, skipping that step means sealing problems in rather than out.

Once the space is assessed, we remove any deteriorated insulation, treat surfaces showing mold growth per IICRC AMRT standards, and address any standing water or drainage issues before a single inch of vapor barrier goes down. The encapsulation itself uses a reinforced polyethylene liner — typically 20-mil — that covers the entire ground surface and runs up the foundation walls, sealed at the seams and at every penetration point for pipes and posts. Foundation vents are closed and sealed as part of the process, converting the crawl space from a ventilated to a conditioned or semi-conditioned environment.

Depending on the size of the space and the severity of existing moisture intrusion, we may also install a crawl space dehumidifier sized for the square footage, with a drain line routed to daylight or a condensate pump. In Biloxi's climate, passive encapsulation alone is sometimes insufficient — active dehumidification is often what keeps relative humidity in the crawl space below the 60% threshold where mold stops thriving.
