Leaf sits in Jones County, deep in a part of Mississippi where mid-century construction was the norm and building codes evolved slowly — meaning a surprising share of the local housing stock was built or renovated during the decades when asbestos was standard in floor tiles, pipe insulation, roof shingles, and textured ceiling finishes. Asbestos fibers don't announce themselves, and in Jones County's humid climate — where summer moisture pushes into every seam and aging materials degrade faster than in drier regions — disturbing asbestos-containing material without a proper abatement plan puts everyone in the structure at risk.

## Why Leaf Properties See Asbestos Issues

Jones County's building boom ran hard through the 1950s, 1960s, and into the 1970s. Homes and small commercial buildings constructed during that window routinely used asbestos in ways that aren't always obvious: sprayed-on acoustic ceiling texture, vinyl floor tile adhesive (the mastic beneath the tile is often the culprit even when the tile itself tests negative), duct wrap on HVAC systems, and corrugated roofing panels on outbuildings. Mississippi's heat and humidity accelerate the deterioration of these materials. When asbestos-containing products become friable — meaning they crumble or release dust under light pressure — the risk of fiber release rises sharply. A roof patch, a bathroom remodel, or even a slow roof leak that softens ceiling drywall can disturb material that would otherwise have stayed stable for decades.

The region's climate also means renovation cycles happen more frequently. Moisture intrusion, storm damage from Gulf-influenced weather systems, and the general wear of hot summers on older roofing and siding push homeowners into repair projects that can unknowingly disturb asbestos. Before any demolition, renovation, or significant repair work on a structure built before 1980, an asbestos inspection is not just prudent — in many cases it's required under EPA and Mississippi Department of Environmental Quality guidelines.

## Our Asbestos Abatement Process in Leaf

Every project starts with a thorough inspection and bulk sampling of suspect materials. Samples are sent to an accredited laboratory — results typically come back within 24 to 72 hours for standard turnaround. If asbestos is confirmed, we develop a site-specific abatement plan before any material is touched.

Containment is built before removal begins. Negative air pressure units with HEPA filtration are set up to prevent fiber migration into adjacent rooms or the HVAC system. Workers wear full personal protective equipment, and the work area is sealed with poly sheeting at doorways, vents, and any penetrations. Asbestos-containing material is wetted during removal to suppress fiber release, then double-bagged in labeled, leak-tight containers per EPA disposal requirements. Waste is transported to a licensed disposal facility — not a general landfill.

Once removal is complete, a visual inspection and air clearance testing confirm the space is safe before containment comes down. The EPA Lead-Safe certification our team holds reflects the same disciplined approach to hazardous material handling that governs our asbestos work — careful containment, documented chain of custody, and no shortcuts on clearance.
