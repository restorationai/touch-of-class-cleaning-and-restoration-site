## Why Helena Properties See Water Damage Differently Than Other Mississippi Towns

Helena's position along the Mississippi River corridor creates a moisture environment that most inland Mississippi cities don't face in the same way. Flood-plain proximity means that even homes not directly in a flood zone can experience groundwater intrusion when the river stage rises and the water table follows. Older homes throughout the city — many built before modern vapor barriers and pressure-treated sill plates became standard — are particularly vulnerable to that kind of slow, silent wicking through foundation walls and crawl spaces.

The housing stock here also tends to feature original hardwood subfloors and plaster-and-lath interior walls. Both materials behave very differently from modern drywall and OSB when they get wet: they absorb moisture slowly, hold it longer, and require more drying time and more precise psychrometric monitoring to dry without warping or delamination. Rushing that process causes secondary damage — cupped floors, crumbling plaster, and mold colonization that can begin within 24 to 48 hours of initial saturation.
