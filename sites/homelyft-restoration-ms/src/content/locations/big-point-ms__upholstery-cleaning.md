## Why Big Point Homes See Upholstery Issues Sooner

Big Point sits close enough to the Mississippi Sound that ambient humidity rarely drops below what furniture manufacturers consider ideal storage conditions. That constant moisture load means upholstered furniture here absorbs airborne moisture even without a spill or flood event. Homes with window units instead of whole-house HVAC, common on older properties throughout this part of Jackson County, see this most: rooms that don't get consistent dehumidification let natural fibers like cotton and linen hold onto moisture, which is how mildew odor sets into a couch cushion long before anyone notices visible staining. Leather pieces fare differently but no better, they can dry out and crack when window units cycle cold, dry air directly across them for months at a time.

## Our Upholstery Cleaning Process in Big Point

We start with a fiber and dye test on an inconspicuous area, because rural Mississippi homes often have furniture that's moved through a few households and the original care tag is long gone. From there, fabric pieces get a low-moisture steam extraction that lifts embedded soil and odor without oversaturating cushion foam, which matters more here than in drier climates since foam that stays damp too long in a humid house is exactly how mildew gets started again. Leather upholstery gets a pH-balanced cleaning step followed by conditioning, since Gulf Coast humidity swings between saturated summer air and drier winter cold snaps, both of which stress leather's natural oils. We finish every job with fans positioned to accelerate drying time, because ambient air alone won't do it fast enough in this climate.

One thing that catches homeowners off guard: furniture stored in a garage, workshop, or detached structure for even a short stretch, common when a house is mid-renovation or between tenants, picks up moisture and odor faster than furniture that stayed in a conditioned living room.
