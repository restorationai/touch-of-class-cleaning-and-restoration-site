Hattiesburg's clay-heavy soils and the region's frequent heavy rainfall create a perfect setup for sewer line backups — saturated ground puts hydrostatic pressure on aging lateral lines, and when those lines fail, raw sewage doesn't just pool in a basement drain. It wicks into subfloor insulation, soaks into crawl space vapor barriers, and contaminates the framing that holds up older homes throughout the city. When that happens, the clock starts immediately: Category 3 contamination — the classification for raw sewage — can begin affecting porous building materials within hours, and the odor alone signals a health environment that needs professional intervention, not a mop and a bottle of bleach.

## Why Hattiesburg Properties See Sewage Backup Problems

The Hattiesburg metro sits in Forrest County on a mix of Oktibbeha and Susquehanna clay soils that expand when wet and shrink during dry spells. That seasonal movement stresses sewer lateral connections at the foundation wall — a problem that shows up repeatedly in neighborhoods with homes built before the 1980s, where original clay tile or cast iron pipe is still in service. When a heavy rain event follows a dry stretch, the ground shifts just enough to crack a joint, and tree root intrusion does the rest.

The city's combined age of housing stock also means many properties still rely on private septic systems on larger lots outside the main utility service area. Septic tank overflows introduce the same Category 3 contamination risk as municipal sewer backups, but the remediation scope often extends to the drain field and surrounding soil — not just the interior of the home. Homes on older lots near the Leaf River corridor are particularly susceptible after significant rainfall events push the water table upward.

## Our Sewage Cleanup and Sanitization Process in Hattiesburg

Raw sewage removal is not a one-step job. The process starts with containment — sealing off the affected area to prevent cross-contamination to clean zones of the home. From there, standing waste is extracted using truck-mounted equipment capable of handling solid-laden water that a standard wet vac cannot manage.

Once bulk removal is complete, affected materials are assessed individually. Saturated drywall, insulation, and subfloor sheathing that have absorbed Category 3 water are typically removed rather than dried in place — drying contaminated porous material does not eliminate the pathogens embedded in it. Structural framing and concrete that can be retained are treated with EPA-registered disinfectants applied at dwell times specified by the manufacturer, not wiped on and immediately wiped off.

Final air scrubbing with HEPA filtration runs throughout the job to capture aerosolized contaminants disturbed during demolition. As an IICRC Certified Firm with technicians holding Water Damage Restoration (WRT) credentials, the work follows the IICRC S500 and S520 standards — the same framework your insurance adjuster will reference when reviewing the claim.
