## Why Saucier Properties See Distinct Storm Damage Patterns

Saucier's position roughly 20 miles north of the Gulf puts it in a climate band that experiences both the full force of landfalling hurricanes and the inland tornado risk that comes from squall lines riding up the I-10 corridor. The local housing stock leans heavily toward wood-frame construction on pier-and-beam or shallow slab foundations, often with metal roofing that, while durable in moderate wind, can peel at seams when gusts exceed design thresholds in a major storm. Older homes in the area — many built before Mississippi adopted its current residential building code revisions — may lack hurricane straps or modern sheathing connections, meaning roof decking is more vulnerable to uplift damage than newer construction.

The sandy-loam and clay-mix soils common to this part of Harrison County also become a factor after heavy rain events: saturated ground loses load-bearing capacity quickly, which can shift pier foundations and cause doors and window frames to rack out of square — a structural signal that's easy to miss in the chaos of cleanup but important to document before repairs begin.

## Our Storm Damage Restoration Process in Saucier

Every storm job starts with a systematic exterior and interior assessment before any debris is moved. We photograph and document roof damage, siding breaches, soffit intrusions, and any points where water has entered the building envelope — that documentation matters both for structural repair planning and for the insurance claim that typically follows. Tarping and board-up happen on the same visit when needed, stopping ongoing weather intrusion before interior drying begins.

Inside, we use thermal imaging cameras and calibrated moisture meters to map where water has traveled — under flooring, inside wall cavities, above ceiling drywall. Saucier homes with blown-in attic insulation (common in energy-retrofit programs that ran through Harrison County in the 2000s) often trap moisture in ways that aren't visible from below; we pull readings from multiple depths rather than relying on surface readings alone. Structural drying follows IICRC S500 and S520 standards, with commercial-grade desiccant dehumidifiers and high-velocity air movers sized to the actual affected square footage — not a one-size-fits-all equipment drop.

## Saucier Insurance Coordination After a Storm

Most homeowners' policies in Mississippi cover sudden storm damage to the structure, but the documentation requirements can be demanding — especially when a single event causes both wind damage (covered under the primary policy) and flooding (which requires a separate NFIP or private flood policy). We photograph every affected area, generate moisture logs, and produce a written scope of damage that's formatted to work alongside your adjuster's estimate. If there's a coverage dispute over whether damage is wind-driven versus flood-origin, having detailed pre-remediation documentation is often the difference between a paid claim and a denied one. We bill carriers directly for covered work and walk you through what to expect on the deductible side before any work begins.

Harrison County's interior — including the Saucier area — has one of the densest concentrations of mature longleaf and loblolly pine in the region.
