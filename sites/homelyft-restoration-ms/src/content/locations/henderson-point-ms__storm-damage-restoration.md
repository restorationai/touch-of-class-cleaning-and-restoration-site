When a hurricane or severe squall rolls in off the Gulf, the combination of wind-driven rain, storm surge, and flying debris can leave a home looking like it absorbed the full force of the coast, because it often has. HomeLyft Restoration MS responds to storm damage calls across Henderson Point and the broader Hancock County shoreline, working to stabilize properties before secondary damage — mold, structural rot, compromised electrical — compounds the original loss.

## Why Henderson Point Properties See Disproportionate Storm Damage

Geography is the first factor. The peninsula's low elevation and proximity to open water mean surge events that might drop a foot of water on inland communities can push two to three feet across yards and into crawl spaces here. The second factor is housing stock. Much of the residential construction along the Henderson Point waterfront predates modern wind-load codes — rooflines, fascia boards, and older single-pane windows are the first casualties when sustained winds climb above 60 mph. Older homes in this area were frequently built on pier-and-beam foundations, which perform well in many respects but allow storm water and debris to collect underneath, creating persistent moisture problems that don't dry out on their own.

The third factor is the tree canopy. Mature live oaks and pines along the peninsula are beautiful until a storm snaps a limb onto a roof or drives a trunk through a wall. Tree damage cleanup is often the visible part of a storm loss, but the hidden damage — water intrusion through the breach point, insulation saturation, sheathing rot — is what drives the real restoration cost if it isn't addressed quickly.

## Our Storm Damage Restoration Process in Henderson Point

Every job starts with a documented scope assessment before a single piece of debris is moved. We photograph and log damage in a format that insurance adjusters recognize, which matters when you're filing a hurricane damage claim and the carrier wants to see proof of the pre-mitigation condition.

From there, the process follows a clear sequence:

1. **Emergency tarping and board-up** — open roof sections and broken windows are sealed to stop additional water entry.
2. **Water extraction and structural drying** — standing water is removed and drying equipment is deployed using IICRC S500 protocols. Our team holds IICRC WRT and ASD certifications, so drying plans are calculated, not guessed.
3. **Debris removal and tree damage cleanup** — fallen limbs, damaged siding, and compromised roofing materials are cleared and disposed of properly.
4. **Damage assessment for reconstruction** — once the structure is dry and stabilized, we document what needs repair or replacement and coordinate the rebuild phase.

## Henderson Point Insurance Coordination

Coastal Mississippi storm claims carry specific complexities. Wind damage and flood damage are typically covered under separate policies — homeowners insurance handles wind, while flood losses run through the National Flood Insurance Program. After a Gulf storm, both can be in play simultaneously, and the line between them matters enormously for what gets paid. We document damage in a way that clearly distinguishes wind-driven water intrusion from ground-level flood entry, which helps adjusters process claims accurately and reduces the chance of a disputed denial. We work directly with insurance carriers on documentation and billing so you're not managing two claims processes on your own while also living through the aftermath of a storm.

## Local Note

One thing that catches homeowners off guard in Henderson Point: after a storm, the humidity doesn't drop the way it might in an inland Mississippi city. The peninsula's air stays saturated for days, which means materials that look dry to the eye — subflooring, wall cavities, insulation — are still holding significant moisture. We've seen cases where a homeowner dried out visible surfaces, assumed the job was done, and discovered mold colonizing inside wall cavities within two weeks. Moisture mapping with thermal imaging and calibrated meters is the only reliable way to know what's actually dry, and it's a step we don't skip on coastal jobs.
