## Why Laurel Properties Face Repeated Board-Up and Tarping Needs

Laurel's housing stock skews older than the Mississippi average. Much of the city's residential fabric — particularly in the established neighborhoods near downtown — was built in the mid-twentieth century with wood-framed construction, single-pane windows, and rooflines that predate modern wind-load engineering standards. Those roofs perform well under normal conditions, but when straight-line winds exceed 60 mph or a tornado touches down nearby, the older fastener patterns and lighter sheathing can fail at the ridge or eave before the rest of the structure shows any damage. The result is a large, irregular opening that a standard pre-cut board won't cover cleanly — requiring on-site cutting, custom framing, and a tarp overlap that extends well past the damaged zone to shed water away from the interior.

Fire damage is the other common trigger. A kitchen fire or electrical fire in a Craftsman-era home can blow out windows from heat pressure and leave the roof vulnerable at the point where fire crews ventilated. Boarding those openings quickly prevents secondary water intrusion from the hoses used to suppress the fire — water that, left standing under flooring or inside wall cavities, can produce visible mold colonization within 48 to 72 hours.

## Our Emergency Board-Up and Tarping Process in Laurel

When HomeLyft Restoration MS is dispatched to a Laurel property, the crew arrives with a truck stocked for the full range of structural openings — not just standard window dimensions. The process runs in a consistent sequence:

**Assessment first.** Before a single sheet of plywood goes up, a technician walks the perimeter and documents every breach — photographing each opening against a measuring reference. That documentation feeds directly into the insurance claim file, which matters when adjusters review scope.

**Structural security.** Windows and door openings are covered with 5/8-inch CDX plywood, cut to fit and secured with exterior-grade screws into the surrounding framing — not just the trim. For fire-damaged openings where the surrounding wood is compromised, we anchor into adjacent structural members.

**Roof tarping.** We use commercial-grade polyethylene tarps rated for sustained wind exposure, weighted and secured at ridges and eaves with lumber battens rather than rope alone. Rope-only tarping fails quickly in the kind of gusty post-storm conditions Laurel sees in the hours after a cell passes through.

**Photo documentation close-out.** Every secured opening is photographed again before the crew leaves. The before-and-after set goes to the homeowner and, with authorization, directly to the insurance carrier.
