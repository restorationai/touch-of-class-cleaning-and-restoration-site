Greene County's humidity doesn't let up, and in Leakesville the combination of long, wet summers and clay-heavy soil means crawl spaces underneath older homes take a beating year after year. Moisture wicks up through bare dirt floors, condenses on floor joists, and creates exactly the conditions where wood rot and mold colonies establish themselves quietly — often for months before a homeowner notices a musty smell or soft spot in the subfloor. Crawl space encapsulation addresses that cycle at the source rather than treating the symptoms.

## Why Leakesville Homes Are Particularly Vulnerable

Leakesville sits in a part of Mississippi where annual rainfall regularly exceeds 60 inches, and the underlying soil composition shifts between sandy loam near creek drainages and expansive clay on higher ground. Clay soil holds water against foundation walls and under slabs long after a rain event ends, keeping relative humidity inside an unencapsulated crawl space well above the 60% threshold where mold begins to colonize wood surfaces — a process that can take fewer than 48 hours under the right conditions.

Many of the residential properties in and around Leakesville were built in the mid-20th century, when open-vent crawl space design was standard practice. That design made sense in theory — passive airflow was supposed to carry moisture out. In Greene County's climate, it does the opposite: warm, humid outdoor air flows in during summer, hits the cooler soil and framing, and deposits moisture rather than removing it. Homes built on pier-and-beam foundations are especially susceptible because the crawl space is deeper and less sheltered from ground-level moisture migration.

## Our Encapsulation Process, Calibrated to Greene County Conditions

Encapsulation is not simply rolling out a sheet of plastic. The process begins with a thorough inspection of the crawl space — checking for standing water, active wood decay, existing vapor barrier remnants, and any evidence of pest intrusion that would need to be addressed before sealing the space. In Greene County's climate, we pay particular attention to the rim joist area, where condensation tends to accumulate first and where insulation is frequently missing or deteriorated in older construction.

Once the space is cleared and any remediation completed, we install a heavy-duty reinforced polyethylene liner — typically 20-mil — across the ground surface and up the foundation walls, sealed at all seams and penetrations with moisture-resistant tape and mastic. Vents are sealed rather than left open, converting the crawl space from a semi-conditioned to a fully conditioned environment. Depending on the square footage and existing HVAC configuration of the home, we may recommend a dedicated crawl space dehumidifier sized to the space, which maintains relative humidity below 55% without relying on seasonal outdoor conditions.

Our team holds IICRC certification in water damage restoration and applied structural drying, which means the moisture science behind encapsulation — vapor pressure, dew point, psychrometrics — is the same discipline we apply to emergency drying work. That technical foundation matters when sizing a dehumidification system for a 1,200-square-foot crawl space under a Greene County home versus guessing at a unit from a home improvement store.
