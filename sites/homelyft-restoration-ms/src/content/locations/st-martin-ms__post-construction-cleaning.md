St. Martin sits in a stretch of Jackson County where coastal humidity doesn't take a day off — and that matters the moment a contractor wraps up a renovation or new build. Drywall dust settles into every horizontal surface, construction adhesive off-gasses in the heat, and fine silica particles from cutting concrete board work their way into HVAC returns before the first homeowner ever turns the system on. Post-construction cleanup here isn't a light sweep; it's a multi-phase process that accounts for Gulf Coast conditions from the first walk-through to the final air-out.

## Why St. Martin Properties Present Unique Post-Construction Cleaning Challenges

Jackson County's climate is the defining factor. Average annual humidity in this part of Mississippi hovers well above 70%, which means construction dust doesn't just sit — it bonds to surfaces that have absorbed ambient moisture during the build. Drywall compound residue, grout haze, and plaster overspray all behave differently here than they would in a drier climate. In homes close to the Back Bay corridor, salt-laden air during the build phase can leave a fine mineral film on windows and fixtures that standard construction wipe-downs miss entirely.

The local housing stock adds another layer. St. Martin has a mix of post-Katrina rebuilds and older wood-frame homes, and the two require different approaches. Post-Katrina construction often used fiber cement siding and engineered lumber — materials that trap fine particulate in their textured surfaces. Older homes may have existing lead paint on surfaces adjacent to renovation zones, which is why our team carries EPA Lead-Safe Certification. We treat every project with that awareness, not just the ones that flag it upfront.

## Our Post-Construction Cleaning Process in St. Martin

A final clean after construction isn't a single visit — it's a sequence. We begin with a rough clean: removing debris, pulling protective film off fixtures, and vacuuming framing cavities and ductwork openings before they're sealed. HEPA-filtered vacuums handle the bulk of airborne particulate so it doesn't recirculate through the home's ventilation system.

The detail phase follows. This is where grout haze comes off tile, paint overspray is removed from hardware, and construction adhesive residue is dissolved from flooring without damaging the finish. We use pH-neutral cleaners on natural stone — a common choice in higher-end St. Martin renovations — and solvent-based removers only where the substrate tolerates it.

The final phase is a light clean and inspection walk. Windows are polished, surfaces are wiped in sequence from ceiling to floor, and any punch-list items the general contractor flagged are addressed before the client's final walkthrough. Our IICRC-certified team documents the condition of the space at each phase, which matters when a builder or property manager needs sign-off documentation.

## Local Note

One thing that catches out-of-area cleaning crews in St. Martin: post-Katrina rebuilds in this part of Jackson County frequently used spray foam insulation in attic spaces and wall cavities. When a renovation disturbs those cavities — adding a recessed light, running a new wire — foam fragments shed a fine, sticky particulate that behaves nothing like standard drywall dust. It clings to HVAC grilles, light fixtures, and cabinet interiors, and it doesn't respond to dry vacuuming alone. We've learned to treat any project in a foam-insulated home with a damp-wipe protocol on all horizontal surfaces, not just the areas directly adjacent to the work zone. It's a small adjustment that prevents a callback two weeks later when the homeowner notices residue on shelving they thought was clean.
