## Why Pascagoula Carpets Face Tougher Conditions

The Jackson County coast sits in a subtropical humidity band that keeps indoor relative humidity elevated for most of the year. That persistent moisture creates a hospitable environment for dust mites, mold spores, and bacteria to settle into carpet backing — the layer most homeowners never see. When a storm pushes water under a door or a window seal fails during a heavy Gulf rain event, carpet absorbs that moisture quickly, and the backing can begin to support microbial growth within 24 to 48 hours if it isn't properly extracted and dried.

Pascagoula's housing stock adds another layer of complexity. Many homes in the area were built in the mid-20th century during the shipbuilding industry's peak, and older carpet installations sometimes sit over subfloor materials — particleboard, older plywood — that respond poorly to excess moisture. Deep cleaning methods that flood the carpet without controlling dwell time and extraction speed can saturate those substrates. That's why the cleaning approach here has to account for what's underneath, not just what's visible on top.

Commercial properties along the waterfront and near the Port of Pascagoula deal with a different challenge: heavy foot traffic from industrial workers, combined with tracked-in petroleum residue and fine metal particulate from nearby operations. Standard residential cleaning chemistry doesn't cut through those contaminants the way a commercial-grade pre-treatment and hot-water extraction process does.

## Our Carpet Cleaning Process in Pascagoula

Every job starts with a walkthrough — fiber type, pile density, stain history, and subfloor condition all factor into which pre-treatment chemistry and extraction pressure are appropriate. Wool and natural fiber rugs common in older Pascagoula homes require lower-pH solutions and gentler agitation than synthetic nylon or polyester installed in newer builds.

Pre-treatment is applied and given time to break down soil, grease, and biological residue before any machine contact. Hot-water extraction — often called steam cleaning, though the water temperature and pressure are the real workhorses — then pulls loosened debris and cleaning solution out of the pile. In Pascagoula's humid conditions, we pay close attention to moisture left in the carpet after extraction, because residual dampness in a high-humidity environment can extend drying time significantly and create the exact conditions that encourage odor and microbial growth.

For stain removal, the approach depends on the stain's chemistry: tannin-based stains (coffee, tea, certain Gulf seafood sauces that are a staple in this area) respond to acidic spotters, while protein-based stains need enzyme treatments. Attempting the wrong chemistry first can set a stain permanently, which is why we identify before we treat.

Sanitization is available as an add-on for homes with pets, young children, or any history of water intrusion — a common request in coastal Jackson County. The process uses EPA-registered disinfectants appropriate for soft surfaces and is applied after extraction so the sanitizing agent isn't immediately vacuumed out.
