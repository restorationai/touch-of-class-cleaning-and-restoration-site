Henderson Point sits at the tip of a narrow peninsula between the Bay of St. Louis and the Mississippi Sound, and that geography shapes every renovation project here in ways that a contractor working inland simply won't anticipate. Salt air accelerates corrosion on fasteners and flashing, seasonal storm surge keeps moisture levels elevated inside wall cavities long after the rain stops, and the tight lot lines along the waterfront mean material staging and crew access require real planning before a single board is pulled. Whether you're updating a Gulf-front cottage, rebuilding after storm damage, or reconfiguring a kitchen that's been the same since the 1970s, the work demands a general contractor who understands coastal Mississippi conditions — not just construction in the abstract.

## Why Henderson Point Properties Present Unique Renovation Challenges

The housing stock along this peninsula leans heavily toward mid-century pier-and-beam construction, with many homes built on elevated slabs or wood pilings to meet older flood-zone requirements that have since been revised upward by FEMA map amendments. That means a bathroom remodel or kitchen renovation often surfaces surprises: subfloor framing that's been exposed to decades of humidity cycles, galvanized plumbing that's well past its service life, and insulation that was never adequate for the thermal demands of a Mississippi Gulf Coast summer. Coastal Harrison County also sits in a high-wind zone, so any structural work — adding a room, replacing a roof deck, opening up a load-bearing wall — has to be engineered and permitted to meet Mississippi's windstorm standards. Our team holds FORTIFIED Building and Roofing certification for Mississippi windstorm, which means we can design and build to the Insurance Institute for Business & Home Safety's FORTIFIED standard, a credential that can meaningfully reduce homeowners' wind and hail premiums in this part of the state.

## Our Renovation and General Contracting Process in Henderson Point

Every project starts with a detailed scope walk — not a quick estimate call. On a peninsula where access is limited to a single road corridor, we coordinate material deliveries and dumpster placement early so we're not creating bottlenecks for neighbors or delaying the job mid-phase. After the scope is set and permits are pulled through Harrison County, we sequence the work to protect the structure from weather exposure during construction: Henderson Point's afternoon storm pattern from June through September means open roof decks and unsheathed exterior walls can't sit overnight without protection.

Interior remodels — kitchen layouts, bathroom conversions, open-concept reconfiguration — are planned with the home's original structure in mind. Pier-and-beam homes on this peninsula often have more flexibility for plumbing relocation than slab-on-grade construction, but the tradeoff is that subfloor access reveals conditions that need to be addressed before finish work begins. We build that discovery phase into the schedule rather than treating it as a surprise.

## Local Note: What the Salt Environment Does to Finish Materials

One thing that catches homeowners off guard on Henderson Point renovations is how quickly salt-laden air degrades finish materials that would hold up fine twenty miles inland. Standard interior-grade cabinet hardware, hollow-core exterior doors, and even certain paint formulations show accelerated corrosion and adhesion failure within a few seasons when the home is within a few hundred feet of open water. When we spec a kitchen remodel or bathroom renovation on this peninsula, we default to marine-grade or stainless hardware, solid-core exterior doors with fiberglass or aluminum cladding, and primers rated for high-humidity coastal environments. It adds a modest amount to material costs upfront and saves a full replacement cycle within five years — a trade most homeowners are glad they made.
