## Why Latimer Properties See Mold Issues

Harrison County's coastal climate is the primary driver. Latimer properties experience long stretches of high relative humidity — often above 70% for weeks at a time — which means building materials stay damp even without an active leak. Crawl spaces in this area are particularly vulnerable: the combination of warm ground temperature, limited airflow, and occasional flooding from Gulf-related weather events creates near-ideal conditions for mold to colonize floor joists and subfloor sheathing.

Older homes in the area frequently have pier-and-beam foundations rather than slab construction, which puts more exposed wood in direct contact with humid ground air. Vapor barriers in these crawl spaces degrade over time, and once they fail, moisture wicks upward into the structure. Many homeowners don't discover the problem until they notice a musty odor in the living area or see discoloration on baseboards — by which point remediation is more involved than a simple surface wipe-down.

Attic mold is another pattern worth knowing. When attic ventilation is inadequate — a common finding in homes that have had insulation added over the years without adjusting soffit and ridge venting — warm, humid air gets trapped and condenses on roof sheathing. That slow, consistent moisture feeds mold quietly for months before it becomes visible.

## Our Mold Remediation Process in Latimer

Every job starts with a thorough assessment: we identify the moisture source, map the affected areas, and take moisture readings before any material is touched. Skipping this step and going straight to cleaning is one of the most common reasons mold returns — if the moisture pathway isn't corrected, new growth follows within weeks.

Once the scope is clear, we establish containment using negative air pressure and polyethylene barriers to prevent spores from migrating to unaffected rooms during the remediation. HEPA-filtered air scrubbers run continuously throughout the process. Affected porous materials — drywall, insulation, compromised wood — are removed, bagged, and disposed of properly. Non-porous surfaces are cleaned and treated using EPA-registered antimicrobial agents.

Structural drying follows removal. Our team holds IICRC AMRT (Applied Microbial Remediation Technician) and IICRC ASD (Applied Structural Drying) certifications, which means the drying phase is measured and documented rather than estimated. We use calibrated desiccant dehumidifiers and air movers positioned based on psychrometric readings, not guesswork. Final clearance involves a post-remediation verification to confirm spore counts have returned to normal background levels before containment comes down.
