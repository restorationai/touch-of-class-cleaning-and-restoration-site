## Why Leaf Properties See Water Damage Issues

Jones County's soil composition — a mix of clay-heavy red clay and sandy loam depending on elevation — plays a significant role in how water behaves around and inside homes. Clay-dominant soil near lower-lying areas of Leaf drains slowly, which means prolonged saturation against foundation walls and crawl spaces after heavy rain events. Many homes in the Leaf area were built in the mid-twentieth century and feature pier-and-beam or crawl space foundations rather than modern sealed slabs, leaving them particularly vulnerable to groundwater intrusion and humidity migration from below.

Mississippi's storm season — which can produce significant rainfall from late spring through early fall — compounds the risk. A slow-moving storm system doesn't have to be a named hurricane to push two or three inches of rain onto a property in a short window, overwhelming gutters, window wells, and aging exterior caulking. Inside, galvanized or older copper supply lines that haven't been updated in decades are prone to pinhole leaks that go undetected until a ceiling stain or a spike in the water bill signals a problem.

## Our Water Damage Restoration Process in Leaf

The first step on any Leaf job is a thorough moisture assessment. Technicians use thermal imaging cameras and calibrated moisture meters to map hidden water migration — behind drywall, beneath vinyl plank flooring, and inside wall cavities — before any extraction equipment is placed. This matters especially in older Jones County homes where wall assemblies may include original wood lath or multiple layers of flooring material that retain moisture differently than modern construction.

Once the scope is mapped, water removal begins using truck-mounted or portable extraction units capable of pulling standing water and saturated carpet padding quickly. Structural drying follows the IICRC S500 standard, using industrial air movers and low-grain refrigerant or desiccant dehumidifiers sized to the affected area. Technicians return daily to log temperature, humidity, and material moisture readings, adjusting equipment placement until readings confirm materials have reached dry standard. Nothing is closed up — no new drywall, no flooring reinstallation — until the numbers support it.

For homes with crawl space foundations, which are common throughout the Leaf area, the drying protocol extends below the structure. Encapsulation barriers, crawl space air movers, and dehumidifiers address moisture that would otherwise continue wicking upward into floor joists and subfloor sheathing long after the visible water is gone.
