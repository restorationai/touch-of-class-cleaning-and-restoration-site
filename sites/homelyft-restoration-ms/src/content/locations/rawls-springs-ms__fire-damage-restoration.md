## Why Rawls Springs Properties Face Particular Fire Damage Challenges

Forrest County's housing stock skews older in the Rawls Springs area, and that matters for fire restoration in ways that aren't obvious at first. Homes built in the 1960s through 1980s commonly used oil-based interior paints, particleboard cabinetry, and insulation materials that produce a denser, stickier smoke residue than modern construction. That residue bonds differently to surfaces and requires chemical sponges and pH-adjusted cleaning agents rather than simple washing.

The regional climate compounds the problem. Rawls Springs sits in a zone where relative humidity regularly climbs above 80 percent from May through September. After a fire, crews open walls and ceilings to inspect for hidden char — and that exposure invites moisture intrusion almost immediately. Without active dehumidification running alongside smoke cleanup, secondary mold colonization can begin within 48 to 72 hours in these conditions. A restoration plan here has to treat fire damage and moisture management as a single, simultaneous problem rather than sequential steps.

Local utility infrastructure is another factor. Older service panels in the area sometimes lack arc-fault protection, and a fire that starts at an outlet or junction box may have been smoldering inside wall framing well before it became visible. That means structural inspection — not just surface cleaning — is a non-negotiable part of any honest scope of work here.

## Our Fire Damage Restoration Process in Rawls Springs

The process begins with a room-by-room assessment that maps the fire's travel path and documents every affected surface for the insurance file. Photographs, moisture readings, and air quality notes go into a single report that your adjuster can work from directly.

Demolition of non-salvageable materials comes next — charred framing, saturated insulation, melted fixtures. We work to the IICRC FSRT standard, which defines how far beyond the visible burn line materials need to be removed to eliminate embedded odor sources. In Rawls Springs homes with tongue-and-groove pine ceilings or original hardwood flooring, we take extra care during this phase because those materials are often worth salvaging with the right cleaning chemistry, even when they look beyond hope.

Odor neutralization follows structural cleaning. Thermal fogging and hydroxyl generation run concurrently in sealed areas to break down odor molecules rather than masking them. HVAC systems get a dedicated cleaning pass — duct fires and smoke infiltration into forced-air systems are a common secondary damage source that gets overlooked when a crew is focused only on the visible burn area.

Reconstruction — framing, drywall, insulation, painting, trim — closes out the project, and we document the completed work for permit sign-off where the scope of structural repair requires it under Forrest County's building code.
