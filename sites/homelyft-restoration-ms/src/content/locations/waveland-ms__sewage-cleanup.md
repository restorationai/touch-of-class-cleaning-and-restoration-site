## Why Waveland Properties Are Especially Vulnerable to Sewage Backups

The combination of aging municipal infrastructure and Waveland's post-Katrina rebuilding pattern creates a layered risk. Many homes rebuilt after 2005 sit on elevated slabs or new pier-and-beam systems, but the lateral sewer lines connecting those homes to the street often follow original easements that were damaged, shifted, or patched rather than fully replaced. Root intrusion from the area's mature live oaks and water oaks is another persistent factor — roots seek out the moisture around older clay and cast-iron pipe joints, and once a root mass establishes itself inside a line, partial blockages become full backups quickly.

Waveland also receives heavy seasonal rainfall, and the soil profile here — a mix of coastal sand and silty clay — drains poorly during sustained rain events. When the ground is already saturated, septic systems that might otherwise function adequately can overflow, and municipal sewer mains can surcharge, pushing sewage backward through the lowest fixtures in a home. Bathrooms and laundry rooms on ground-floor slabs are typically the first points of failure.

## Our Sewage Cleanup and Sanitization Process in Waveland

Raw sewage is classified as Category 3 water — the most contaminated classification in the IICRC S500 standard — and the cleanup protocol reflects that. When we arrive at a Waveland property, the first priority is containment: sealing off the affected area to prevent cross-contamination to clean living spaces. We photograph and document everything for insurance purposes before extraction begins.

Extraction of standing sewage is followed by the removal of any porous materials that cannot be decontaminated — this typically means saturated drywall, insulation, and flooring underlayment below the sewage line. In Waveland's coastal climate, where ambient humidity already runs high, leaving any contaminated organic material in place creates near-certain conditions for mold colonization within 24 to 48 hours. Once the structure is cleared to clean substrate, we apply EPA-registered antimicrobial and disinfectant treatments to all affected surfaces, including subfloor sheathing, floor joists, and wall cavities where splash contamination may have traveled. Industrial air movers and dehumidifiers run until moisture readings return to baseline for the region.

As an IICRC Certified Firm with technicians holding IICRC WRT and AMRT credentials, the team follows documented drying and sanitization protocols — not a rough approximation of them.
