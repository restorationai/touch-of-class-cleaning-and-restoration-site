**Salt air and heavy coastal humidity do more to fabric and leather in Bay St. Louis than most homeowners expect.** A sofa that looks fine in October can carry a musty undertone by summer, especially in homes that sit close to the water and run air conditioning hard for months at a time. Upholstery cleaning here is less about surface stains and more about getting moisture and odor out of the cushion core before mildew gets a foothold.

## Why Bay St. Louis Homes See Upholstery Issues

Hancock County's Gulf Coast climate keeps indoor humidity high for most of the year, and that moisture works its way into upholstery fabric even when a room feels dry to the touch. Homes closer to the water, especially older raised cottages built to handle storm surge, often run window units or older HVAC systems that cycle on and off rather than holding a steady humidity level. That inconsistency lets fabric fibers absorb ambient moisture between cooling cycles, which is why upholstery in coastal Mississippi tends to hold odor and soil differently than furniture further inland. Add hurricane season, when windows stay shut and homes seal up tight for days, and trapped humidity has nowhere to go but into the cushions.

## Our Upholstery Cleaning Process in Bay St. Louis

We start by identifying the fabric or leather type, because what works on a synthetic blend can discolor natural fiber or dry out leather. For fabric pieces, we use low-moisture hot water extraction that lifts soil and odor without oversaturating the cushion foam, since foam that stays damp too long in a humid house is exactly what invites mildew. Leather upholstery gets cleaned with a pH-balanced solution and reconditioned afterward, because salt air dries leather out faster than it does in drier climates. On every job we finish with targeted air movement and, when needed, a portable dehumidifier running near the piece for a few hours so the fabric drops back to a stable moisture level instead of sitting damp overnight.

## Equipment & Methods We Use for Upholstery

Our technicians work from the IICRC's cleaning framework, choosing extraction, dry foam, or encapsulation methods based on the fiber content and the condition of the piece rather than defaulting to one process for everything. Microfiber and synthetic blends common in newer furniture typically respond well to hot water extraction. Older natural-fiber pieces, which we see more often in longtime Bay St. Louis households, need a gentler approach and slower drying to avoid shrinkage or watermarking. For leather, we test in an inconspicuous spot first, since coastal sun exposure through south-facing windows can already have dried or faded the material before we ever get there.

## Local note

Homes on Bay St. Louis's older raised lots, especially pieces set on piers or pilings near the water, tend to have upholstered furniture positioned right against exterior walls where humidity is highest. We've found that cleaning alone doesn't solve the odor complaint in those rooms; the fix is pulling furniture a few inches off the wall after cleaning and running a fan behind it for the first day, which keeps the fabric from reabsorbing the same trapped moisture that caused the smell in the first place.
