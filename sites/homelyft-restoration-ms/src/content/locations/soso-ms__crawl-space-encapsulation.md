## Why Soso Properties See Crawl Space Moisture Problems

Jones County's soil profile is dominated by expansive clay that holds groundwater close to the surface long after rain events. During a wet spring or a stretch of heavy Gulf-driven storms, that saturated ground pushes vapor upward through the dirt floor of an open crawl space with nowhere else to go. Homes built in this part of Mississippi — many of them pier-and-beam construction from the mid-20th century — were designed with passive venting strategies that made sense in a drier era but actively draw humid outside air into the crawl space during the long summer months.

Once relative humidity inside the crawl space climbs above 60 percent, wood-destroying fungi can begin colonizing floor joists within days. Fiberglass batt insulation, a common choice in older Jones County homes, absorbs that moisture and sags away from the subfloor, eliminating its R-value and creating a dark, damp habitat that attracts pests. The combination of clay soil, high ambient humidity, and aging building stock makes crawl space encapsulation not a luxury upgrade here — it's straightforward preventive maintenance.

## Our Crawl Space Encapsulation Process in Soso

Every encapsulation project starts with a thorough inspection before a single roll of liner is opened. The team assesses standing water, existing vapor barrier condition (if any), wood moisture content using a calibrated pin meter, and the state of any HVAC equipment or ductwork sharing the crawl space. In Soso, we frequently find that older polyethylene sheeting — sometimes just 6-mil plastic laid loosely over the dirt — has torn, shifted, or been undermined by root intrusion, giving homeowners a false sense of protection.

Once the space is cleared and dried, the process moves through these stages:

- **Drainage and grading** — low spots in the crawl space floor are addressed so water has a path out rather than pooling against piers or the foundation wall.
- **Heavy-duty liner installation** — we use a reinforced, puncture-resistant vapor barrier (20-mil or heavier depending on conditions) that runs up the foundation walls and is mechanically fastened and sealed at all seams and penetrations. This is a closed-cell installation, not a loose lay.
- **Vent sealing** — existing foundation vents are sealed and insulated. This is the step that surprises many homeowners: closing the vents is counterintuitive, but it's the move that stops humid outside air from cycling through.
- **Conditioned air or dehumidification** — the now-sealed space needs a controlled source of dry air. Depending on the home's HVAC configuration, we either tie in a supply duct or install a dedicated crawl space dehumidifier with a condensate drain.
- **Final moisture verification** — wood moisture readings and relative humidity are documented before the job is closed out.

The IICRC-certified team at HomeLyft Restoration MS follows drying and encapsulation protocols consistent with industry standards, and the company holds EPA Lead-Safe certification — relevant in older Jones County homes where disturbing the crawl space area can mean contact with lead-based paint on piers or sill plates.

One pattern that comes up repeatedly in Jones County crawl space work: homes in this part of Mississippi often have HVAC ducts — sometimes older flex duct — running through the crawl space that were never properly supported or sealed at the joints.
