## Why Agricola Properties Present Unique Biohazard Challenges

Stone County's climate sits in a persistent high-humidity band for most of the year. That matters for biohazard remediation because porous building materials — subfloor planks, older hardwood, unpainted concrete block, and the tongue-and-groove wood paneling common in rural Mississippi homes built before the 1980s — absorb biological contamination more aggressively than modern sealed surfaces. Once absorbed, those materials require more aggressive treatment protocols and, in some cases, controlled removal rather than surface cleaning alone.

Agricola's housing stock skews toward single-family homes on larger lots, many with pier-and-beam or crawl-space foundations. Crawl spaces are a particular concern in biohazard situations: gravity and drainage patterns can carry contamination below the living area before anyone realizes the scope of the problem. Our team assesses subfloor and crawl-space exposure as a standard part of every site evaluation in this region — not an add-on.

## Our Biohazard Cleanup Process in Agricola

Every job begins with a contained site assessment. Our IICRC-certified technicians identify the full extent of affected materials before any work begins, which prevents cross-contamination to unaffected areas of the home. We use ATP testing and visual mapping to define the work zone, then establish containment barriers appropriate to the size and layout of the structure.

Decontamination follows a strict sequence: removal of unsalvageable porous materials, application of hospital-grade EPA-registered disinfectants, and verification testing before containment comes down. In Agricola's climate, we extend dwell times on disinfectants during high-humidity conditions, because surface moisture can dilute chemical efficacy if the application isn't calibrated to ambient conditions. All regulated biological waste is packaged and transported according to Mississippi State Department of Health guidelines — nothing is left curbside or in a standard waste stream.
