## Why Pass Christian Properties See Mold Issues

The Harrison County coastline experiences some of the highest average relative humidity readings in the continental United States, and Pass Christian's position along U.S. 90 — directly exposed to onshore Gulf winds — amplifies that effect. Homes here cycle through wet and warm conditions for most of the year, which means building materials stay at or above the moisture content threshold where mold colonies establish themselves.

The housing stock adds another layer of complexity. A significant portion of Pass Christian's residential properties were rebuilt or substantially repaired after Hurricane Katrina in 2005, which means many homes have a mix of new construction materials layered over older foundations, pier-and-beam substructures, or original masonry that was never fully dried before reconstruction began. That combination — new drywall over old framing that absorbed storm moisture — creates hidden reservoirs where mold grows inside wall cavities long before it becomes visible on a surface. Older cottages closer to the beachfront that predate modern vapor barriers are especially susceptible; without a continuous barrier under the slab or between the crawl space and the living area, ground moisture migrates upward year-round.

## Our Mold Remediation Process in Pass Christian

Every job starts with a thorough inspection using moisture meters and thermal imaging to map where water is still present inside building assemblies — because visible mold is almost never the whole picture. Once we understand the moisture profile of the structure, we establish negative-air containment using 6-mil poly barriers and HEPA-filtered air scrubbers to prevent spores from migrating to unaffected rooms during the remediation work.

Affected materials — drywall, insulation, wood framing where necessary — are removed and bagged in accordance with EPA guidelines and disposed of properly. Remaining structural surfaces are treated and HEPA-vacuumed, and the space is dried to target moisture content before any reconstruction begins. Our team holds IICRC AMRT (Applied Microbial Remediation Technician) certification, so the process follows a documented, defensible standard — which matters when your insurance adjuster or a future home inspector asks how the work was performed. We also carry EPA Lead-Safe certification, which is relevant for Pass Christian's older homes where lead-based paint may be present on surfaces being disturbed during remediation.
