## Why Latimer Properties Face Elevated Asbestos Exposure Risk

Harrison County's housing stock tells the story of several distinct construction eras. Homes built before 1980 frequently contain asbestos in places that aren't immediately obvious: floor tile adhesive, pipe insulation, attic insulation, roofing felt, and the textured "popcorn" ceilings that were popular through the 1970s. The Gulf Coast climate compounds the risk. Repeated cycles of high humidity, tropical storm moisture intrusion, and intense summer heat cause building materials to expand and contract over decades. That mechanical stress can fracture otherwise stable, non-friable asbestos materials — turning them friable, meaning fibers can become airborne with minimal disturbance.

Harrison County also saw significant post-storm rebuilding after major Gulf Coast weather events, and some of that work involved disturbing pre-existing materials without proper abatement. If your home was partially renovated in the years following a major storm, there's a real possibility that asbestos-containing materials were disturbed rather than properly removed.

## Our Asbestos Abatement Process in Latimer

Every project starts with a thorough inspection and bulk sampling of suspect materials. Samples are sent to an accredited laboratory — results typically return within a few business days, though rush analysis is available when a renovation timeline is pressing. If asbestos is confirmed, we develop a site-specific abatement plan that accounts for the material type, its condition, and the scope of the planned work.

Containment is established using negative air pressure enclosures with HEPA filtration — a critical step in older homes where HVAC systems can distribute disturbed fibers throughout the structure if not properly isolated. Our team is EPA Lead-Safe Certified and OSHA trained, which matters in Mississippi homes where lead-based paint and asbestos often coexist in the same pre-1978 construction. All removed materials are packaged, labeled, and transported to a licensed disposal facility in compliance with Mississippi Department of Environmental Quality (MDEQ) regulations. Final air clearance testing confirms fiber levels are within acceptable limits before containment is removed.

## Latimer Insurance and Regulatory Coordination

Homeowners insurance rarely covers elective asbestos abatement, but the picture changes when asbestos disturbance is the result of a covered loss — a roof failure from a storm, a burst pipe that required wall demolition, or fire damage that exposed insulation. In those cases, we document the asbestos-containing materials as part of the broader loss scope and communicate directly with your adjuster. Mississippi's renovation notification requirements under MDEQ and EPA NESHAP rules also apply to projects above certain square-footage thresholds; we handle that paperwork as part of the project, so you're not navigating state and federal forms on your own.

## Local Note

One pattern we've seen repeatedly in Harrison County homes built in the 1950s and 1960s is asbestos-containing floor tile installed directly over a concrete slab — a construction method common in the Gulf South because slab-on-grade foundations were the standard response to the region's high water table and expansive clay soils. That tile is often still intact beneath carpet or vinyl that was laid over it in later decades. Homeowners planning to install new flooring frequently don't know it's there until the carpet is pulled. If your Latimer home was built before 1975 and has never had a formal asbestos inspection, it's worth scheduling one before any flooring work begins — disturbing intact tile during a DIY project can release fibers that would otherwise remain safely encapsulated.
