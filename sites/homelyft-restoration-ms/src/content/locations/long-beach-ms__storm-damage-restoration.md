## Why Long Beach Properties Face Distinct Storm Damage Challenges

Long Beach's position along U.S. 90 and its proximity to the Mississippi Sound means properties here experience a specific combination of wind, surge, and rain-driven intrusion that differs from damage patterns even a few miles inland. Homes built before the post-Katrina building code revisions — many of which still stand throughout the city — were constructed to older wind-load standards and often lack the hurricane straps and reinforced roof decking now required under Mississippi's Residential Building Code. When a strong tropical system or a fast-moving squall line moves through Harrison County, those older connections are the first to fail.

The sandy, low-lying soil common in coastal Long Beach also drains more slowly than it appears to, and water that seems to sheet off the yard is often backing up under pier-and-beam foundations or pooling against slab edges. Salt air accelerates corrosion on exposed fasteners and flashing, meaning a roof that looked intact before a storm may have had compromised seals that only become obvious when wind-driven rain finds the gaps.

## Our Storm Damage Restoration Process in Long Beach

Every job starts with a documented damage assessment — photographs, moisture readings at multiple wall depths, and a written scope before any materials are removed. That documentation exists primarily for your insurance adjuster, but it also drives the work plan.

For wind and tree damage, the first priority is weather protection: emergency tarps or temporary roof coverings installed the same day to stop additional water intrusion. Once the structure is secured, we extract standing water using truck-mounted extraction units, set industrial air movers and desiccant or refrigerant dehumidifiers calibrated to the ambient humidity levels typical of the Gulf Coast, and monitor drying progress with daily moisture readings until materials reach their target moisture content per the IICRC S500 standard. Saltwater intrusion — common when surge reaches a property — requires additional steps: affected materials are treated differently than freshwater losses because salt residue is hygroscopic and will continue drawing moisture from the air even after the visible water is gone.

Tree impact damage often involves structural assessment before interior drying can begin. We coordinate with structural engineers when load-bearing members are compromised, and our team holds FORTIFIED Building Roofing and MS Windstorm certification, which matters when your insurer or a future buyer asks how the roof was restored.

Storm damage claims in Mississippi's coastal counties move through a specific process that's different from an interior-state water loss. Policies in Harrison County frequently involve separate wind and flood deductibles, and some carriers require a public adjuster or independent inspection before approving a full scope. ## Local Note

One pattern that shows up repeatedly in Long Beach storm work: homes with original 1960s and 1970s tongue-and-groove wood decking under later-added asphalt shingles. When wind lifts shingles and rain soaks through, that old decking absorbs water unevenly and can hold moisture for weeks even after the surface appears dry. Standard drying timelines built around modern OSB sheathing don't apply — the crew checks decking moisture separately and extends drying cycles when tongue-and-groove is present, which keeps the repair from trapping moisture under new shingles and creating a mold problem six months later.
