## Why Sharon Properties See Persistent Carpet Problems

Madison County's climate is the first factor. Sharon experiences an average of more than 55 inches of rainfall per year, and the months between May and October add intense humidity on top of that. Carpet in a home without a dehumidifier running consistently will hold ambient moisture, which accelerates the breakdown of carpet backing and creates the musty smell homeowners often mistake for an old carpet that needs replacing — when in reality a thorough extraction and sanitization cycle can restore it.

The second factor is soil composition. The reddish-brown clay loam common across this part of Madison County is fine-grained and clingy. It doesn't vacuum out cleanly the way sandy coastal soil does. Foot traffic grinds it into the pile, and over time it acts like sandpaper on fiber tips, dulling the carpet's appearance even after a standard cleaning. Addressing that requires a pre-treatment agitation step before hot-water extraction, not just a pass with a wand.

Older homes in Sharon — particularly those built in the 1970s and 1980s during the area's residential growth period — often have thicker, denser carpet padding underneath original or replacement carpet. That padding holds water and soil at a level that surface cleaning can't reach. Getting real results means adjusting dwell time on pre-conditioner and using high-CFM extraction equipment rated for that depth.

## Our Carpet Cleaning Process in Sharon

Every job starts with a walkthrough to assess fiber type, soiling level, and any staining that needs targeted treatment before the main clean. Wool and wool-blend carpets — found in some of the older, more established homes in the area — require pH-neutral chemistry that won't cause fiber swelling or color shift. Synthetic cut-pile carpet common in 1990s and 2000s construction tolerates a more aggressive alkaline pre-spray.

From there, the process follows a consistent sequence:

1. **Dry soil removal** — a commercial-grade vacuum pass to pull out loose particulate before introducing moisture.
2. **Pre-conditioning** — a dwell period with the appropriate chemistry, matched to fiber type and soil load.
3. **Agitation** — a counter-rotating brush or hand tool to break the clay-and-organic bond at the base of the pile.
4. **Hot-water extraction** — truck-mounted or portable high-pressure extraction pulling suspended soils and cleaning solution out of the carpet.
5. **Spot treatment** — targeted work on stains that didn't release during the main pass, including tannin stains from Mississippi's iron-rich well water common in rural Sharon addresses.
6. **Sanitization** — an EPA-registered antimicrobial applied where odor or biological contamination is present.
7. **Speed-dry setup** — air movers positioned to cut drying time, critical in Sharon's humid summer months when ambient air alone won't dry carpet adequately before mildew sets in.
