## Why Diamondhead Properties See Asbestos Risk

Diamondhead was developed primarily between the late 1960s and the mid-1980s — a window that aligns almost perfectly with peak asbestos use in American residential construction. Builders in that period routinely used asbestos-containing adhesives under vinyl floor tiles, wrapped HVAC ducts and hot-water pipes in asbestos insulation, and sprayed textured ceilings with products that could contain up to 10% chrysotile fiber by weight. Mississippi's Gulf Coast humidity compounds the risk: moisture cycling causes materials to expand and contract over decades, and what was once a tightly bonded floor tile can become friable — meaning it crumbles and releases fibers — long before anyone picks up a demolition tool.

The region also sits in a high-wind corridor, and homes that sustained roof or wall damage during hurricane seasons may have had repairs done quickly, sometimes without full inspection of what was disturbed beneath the surface. That history of storm-driven patch work means asbestos-containing materials can show up in unexpected places — inside wall cavities, under replacement flooring, or mixed into older joint compound that was re-coated rather than replaced.

## Our Asbestos Abatement Process in Diamondhead

Every project starts with a formal asbestos inspection conducted by a Mississippi-licensed inspector. Bulk samples are collected from suspect materials — floor tile and mastic, ceiling texture, pipe wrap, attic insulation — and sent to an accredited laboratory. Results typically return within a few business days, and we walk you through the report before any abatement scope is finalized.

When abatement is required, the work area is isolated with poly sheeting and negative-air pressure equipment to prevent fiber migration to adjacent rooms. Our EPA Lead-Safe Certified team removes regulated materials using wet methods that suppress airborne fiber counts, double-bags all waste in labeled, sealed containers, and disposes of it at a licensed Mississippi facility — the disposal documentation becomes part of your project file, which matters if you're selling the home or pulling a building permit.

For popcorn ceiling asbestos removal specifically, we wet the surface thoroughly before any scraping begins. Dry-scraping a popcorn ceiling that tests positive is one of the fastest ways to contaminate an entire living space; the wet method keeps fibers locked in the material until it's bagged and out of the building.
