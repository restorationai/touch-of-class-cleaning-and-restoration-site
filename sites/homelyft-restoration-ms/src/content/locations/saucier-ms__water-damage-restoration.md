## Why Saucier Properties Are Particularly Vulnerable to Water Damage

Saucier's geography puts it in a sweet spot for trouble. The community sits inland enough that it escapes the worst storm surge, but close enough to the Gulf that it absorbs the full force of tropical moisture. Annual rainfall in Harrison County regularly exceeds 60 inches, and much of that falls in concentrated bursts during hurricane season — June through November — when the ground is already saturated.

The local housing stock adds another layer of complexity. A significant portion of Saucier's homes were built in the 1970s and 1980s, when vapor barriers were inconsistent and crawl space ventilation standards were minimal. Those crawl spaces trap humidity year-round, and when a plumbing failure or roof leak adds liquid water to an already damp environment, mold colonization can begin within 24 to 48 hours. Older galvanized supply lines — still common in homes of that era — are also prone to pinhole leaks that go undetected for weeks, saturating wall cavities silently before the damage becomes visible.

Harrison County's clay-heavy soil is another factor. It drains slowly, which means water from a heavy rain event or a broken exterior hose bib tends to pool against foundations rather than percolate away. That hydrostatic pressure pushes moisture through block or poured-concrete foundations, and homeowners often discover the intrusion only after flooring or baseboards begin to buckle.

## Our Water Damage Restoration Process in Saucier

Every job starts with a thorough moisture assessment. We use thermal imaging cameras and calibrated moisture meters to map the full extent of saturation — including areas that look dry to the eye but are holding water inside wall assemblies or beneath flooring. That map drives every decision that follows.

Extraction comes next. Truck-mounted and portable extraction units pull standing water from hard surfaces and carpet before it migrates further. Once the bulk water is out, we set a calculated drying system: commercial-grade low-grain refrigerant dehumidifiers, high-velocity air movers positioned to create a directed airflow pattern, and — where necessary — desiccant dehumidification for particularly stubborn structural assemblies. Drying targets and daily moisture readings are documented throughout, following the IICRC S500 standard for water damage restoration.

For Saucier homes with crawl spaces, we treat that space as a separate drying zone. Crawl space drying in this climate requires its own dehumidification, and we inspect the vapor barrier for tears or gaps that may have contributed to the original moisture problem. Affected insulation is removed and replaced rather than dried in place — wet fiberglass batts in a Gulf Coast crawl space are a reliable mold incubator.

## Local Note

One pattern we see repeatedly in Saucier's older ranch-style homes: the original subfloor is tongue-and-groove pine rather than modern OSB or plywood. Pine subfloor holds up better to brief wetting than OSB — it swells less catastrophically and can often be dried in place if extraction begins quickly. But it also releases moisture more slowly during the drying phase, which means drying timelines in these homes routinely run a day or two longer than the industry average.
