## Why D'Iberville Properties See Mold Issues

The geography here works against homeowners in a few specific ways. D'Iberville is built largely on low-lying terrain near the Back Bay of Biloxi, and properties close to that shoreline experience persistent ground moisture that wicks upward through slab foundations and into wall assemblies. Homes with pier-and-beam construction — common in older sections of the city — are especially vulnerable because the open crawl space underneath becomes a humidity reservoir during the long Gulf Coast summer.

Housing stock in D'Iberville also includes a significant number of post-Katrina rebuilds from the mid-2000s. While those homes were built to updated wind codes, some were framed and enclosed quickly under contractor pressure, and any moisture trapped during construction never fully dried. Years later, that latent moisture becomes a mold problem behind drywall that looks perfectly fine on the surface. Thermal imaging during our inspection phase regularly reveals exactly this pattern.

Black mold (*Stachybotrys chartarum*) favors cellulose-rich materials — drywall paper, OSB sheathing, wood framing — kept consistently damp. In D'Iberville's climate, "consistently damp" is not a worst-case scenario; it's a baseline condition for any uncontrolled moisture source.

## Our Mold Remediation Process in D'Iberville

Every project starts with a thorough visual inspection and moisture mapping. We use thermal cameras and calibrated moisture meters to locate wet building materials that aren't visible to the eye — because in a high-humidity environment, the mold you can smell is rarely the full extent of the problem.

Once the scope is defined, we establish negative-air containment using 6-mil poly barriers and HEPA-filtered air scrubbers. This prevents cross-contamination to unaffected rooms, which matters especially in smaller ranch-style homes where HVAC returns can pull spores throughout the structure if the system isn't isolated. Our IICRC AMRT-certified technicians then remove contaminated materials — drywall, insulation, subfloor sections — down to clean, structurally sound substrate.

After removal, affected framing and cavities are HEPA-vacuumed, treated with an EPA-registered antimicrobial agent, and dried to manufacturer-specified moisture content before any reconstruction begins. We do not close walls until moisture readings confirm the assembly is dry — a step that gets skipped when timelines are rushed but that makes the difference between a permanent fix and a recurring problem.
