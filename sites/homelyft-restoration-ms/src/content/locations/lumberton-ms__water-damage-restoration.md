## Why Lumberton Properties See Water Damage Issues

Lamar County averages well over 60 inches of rainfall per year — one of the higher totals in Mississippi — and Lumberton's position along the Bogue Chitto River watershed means that even moderate rain events can saturate the ground faster than storm drains and yard grades can redirect water. When soil reaches saturation, hydrostatic pressure pushes moisture through block foundations and crawl space vents in ways that catch homeowners completely off guard.

Older homes in Lumberton — many built in the 1950s through 1970s — often have galvanized steel supply lines that have reached or exceeded their service life. Pinhole leaks in those pipes can seep behind walls for weeks before a homeowner notices discoloration on drywall or a soft spot in flooring. By that point, subfloor sheathing and floor joists are already compromised, and what started as a slow drip becomes a full remediation project.

Lumberton also sits close enough to the Gulf Coast corridor that tropical systems and named storms regularly push heavy rain bands inland through Lamar County. The 2020 hurricane season alone produced multiple rainfall events that overwhelmed residential drainage in communities throughout this part of Mississippi.

## Our Water Damage Restoration Process in Lumberton

Every job starts with a thorough moisture assessment using thermal imaging cameras and calibrated moisture meters — not a visual walk-through. Water migrates in ways that aren't visible to the eye, tracking along wall cavities, under vinyl flooring, and into insulation batts inside crawl spaces. We map the full extent of saturation before any equipment is placed.

From there, the process moves through:

- **Water extraction** — truck-mounted and portable extraction units pull standing water from hard surfaces, carpet, and subfloor assemblies. In pier-and-beam homes common in this part of Lamar County, we access the crawl space directly to extract pooled water and remove saturated insulation.
- **Structural drying** — industrial desiccant and refrigerant dehumidifiers, paired with high-velocity air movers, are positioned based on the moisture map, not guesswork. Drying is managed to the IICRC S500 standard with daily moisture readings logged until materials reach acceptable drying goals.
- **Content and material evaluation** — hardwood flooring, cabinetry, and drywall are assessed individually. Some materials can be dried in place; others need controlled removal to prevent secondary mold colonization, which can begin in as little as 24 to 48 hours in Lumberton's warm, humid conditions.
- **Documentation for insurance** — every affected surface is photographed, measured, and recorded in a format that aligns with what adjusters from major carriers need to process a claim.

## Local Note

One thing that comes up repeatedly on Lumberton jobs: pier-and-beam homes with original tongue-and-groove subfloor decking dry significantly more slowly than homes with modern OSB or plywood sheathing. The old-growth pine used in mid-century construction in this part of Mississippi is dense and holds moisture deep in the grain. Drying timelines that would be three to four days on a newer home can stretch to six or seven days on these older structures — and rushing the process by pulling equipment too early is one of the most common reasons secondary mold problems develop. We account for this from the start rather than discovering it midway through a job.
