## Why Moselle Homes See Heavier Upholstery Wear

Moselle sits in the Pine Belt region of south Mississippi, and that means two things for furniture: pine pollen and moisture. Spring pollen season coats porches and gets carried through screen doors on clothing and shoes, settling into fabric weave where it binds with body oils and household dust. Because pollen residue is resin-based, a quick vacuum or surface wipe doesn't remove it, it just redistributes it deeper into the cushion fill. Layer on the region's year-round humidity, and fabric sofas that sit near an exterior wall or a window unit often develop a faint mildew smell long before any visible staining shows up. Rural properties on well water add another variable. Iron and mineral content in well water can leave rust-toned rings on fabric arms and headrests if a damp cloth or DIY spot cleaner is used without the right pretreatment.

## Our Upholstery Cleaning Process

We start every job with a fiber and dye test, because Jones County homes mix everything from older cotton-blend sectionals to newer microfiber and genuine leather recliners, and each reacts differently to moisture and heat. For fabric upholstery, we pretreat with a citrus-based solvent that breaks down pollen resin and body oils before hot water extraction lifts the soil and most of the moisture back out in the same pass. Leather upholstery gets a different approach entirely: a pH-balanced cleaner followed by a conditioner that replaces the oils stripped during cleaning, which matters in a climate where leather can dry and crack faster between humid stretches and air-conditioned indoor air. We finish with controlled air movers to push drying time down, since leaving a cushion damp for more than a few hours in this humidity is exactly how a mildew odor gets started in the first place.
