## Why Moselle Properties See Sewage Backup Issues

Moselle sits in a part of Jones County where a significant portion of homes rely on private septic systems rather than municipal sewer connections. That distinction matters enormously when something goes wrong. Septic tanks that haven't been pumped on schedule — typically every three to five years — can back up through floor drains, toilets, and washing machine standpipes with very little warning. The problem compounds during wet seasons: saturated soil around the drain field loses its ability to absorb effluent, and the system has nowhere to push waste except back into the house.

The area's older housing stock adds another layer of complexity. Homes built in the 1960s and 1970s often have cast-iron drain lines that have corroded or shifted over decades, creating partial blockages that turn a minor backup into a full overflow. When raw sewage contacts the wood subfloor common in those homes, you're dealing with a Category 3 contamination event — the most serious classification — that requires more than mopping and bleach.

## Our Sewage Cleanup and Sanitization Process in Moselle

The first thing our crew does on arrival is assess the source and stop ongoing contamination. If a septic line is still actively backing up, we coordinate with a licensed plumber to clear the blockage before remediation begins — there's no point sanitizing a space that's still receiving waste. Once the source is controlled, we establish containment to prevent cross-contamination to unaffected rooms.

Extraction comes next. Standing sewage is removed with truck-mounted or portable extraction units, and any porous material — carpet, pad, drywall below the flood line, insulation — that absorbed Category 3 water is removed and bagged for disposal per Mississippi Department of Environmental Quality guidelines. What remains gets treated with EPA-registered antimicrobial agents applied to all affected hard surfaces, framing, and concrete. We don't consider a surface clean until it has been tested or visually confirmed free of residue and odor compounds.

Structural drying follows sanitization. We place industrial air movers and dehumidifiers calibrated to the square footage and material types present, and we monitor moisture readings daily until the structure reaches its dry standard. Because Moselle's summer humidity regularly pushes above 80 percent, drying timelines here can run a day or two longer than in drier climates — we account for that in our equipment placement rather than pulling gear prematurely.
