## Why St. Martin Properties Face Particular Fire Damage Challenges

The Gulf Coast climate creates a compounding problem after a structural fire. High ambient humidity — common across Jackson County from spring through fall — accelerates the way acidic smoke residue etches into drywall, wood trim, and HVAC ductwork. Within hours of a fire, soot begins to chemically bond to surfaces; within days, secondary damage from smoke and char moisture can discolor paint, pit metal fixtures, and permanently stain porous masonry.

St. Martin's housing stock adds another layer of complexity. The area includes a mix of ranch-style homes built in the 1970s and 1980s alongside newer construction, and the older homes frequently have original fiberglass insulation, aluminum wiring, and ductwork that can trap smoke residue in ways that modern builds do not. Restoration scopes on these properties often extend into the attic and crawl space, where smoke migrates through gaps in older framing and settles in insulation that would otherwise go uninspected.

## Our Fire Damage Restoration Process in St. Martin

When we arrive at a fire-damaged property in St. Martin, the first priority is a structured assessment — not a sales pitch. We document the extent of char, smoke penetration, and water damage left by suppression efforts, then establish a clear scope before any work begins.

The process follows a defined sequence:

- **Structural stabilization**: Board-up and roof tarping to prevent weather intrusion, which is especially important given Jackson County's tendency toward afternoon thunderstorms during storm season.
- **Debris and char removal**: Controlled demolition of materials that cannot be restored, with careful separation of salvageable items for contents cleaning.
- **Smoke and soot cleaning**: Dry chemical sponges, HEPA vacuuming, and wet cleaning agents matched to the surface type — painted drywall, raw wood, brick, and HVAC interiors each require a different approach.
- **Odor neutralization**: Thermal fogging and hydroxyl generation to address smoke odor at the molecular level, not just mask it. In the humid St. Martin climate, skipping this step means odor returns with the next rainy stretch.
- **Structural drying**: Suppression water left by firefighters can saturate subfloors and wall cavities; our IICRC WRT and ASD certified technicians monitor moisture levels until readings reach regional equilibrium — not just until surfaces feel dry.
- **Reconstruction**: From drywall and insulation replacement to full room rebuilds, we carry the project through to a finished, livable state.
