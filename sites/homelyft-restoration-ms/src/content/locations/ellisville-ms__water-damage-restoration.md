## Why Ellisville Properties See Water Damage Issues

Jones County's red clay soil is notoriously slow to absorb surface water, which means heavy rain events — common from March through June — can push water against foundation walls and through crawl space vents before a homeowner realizes there's a problem. Many homes in and around Ellisville were built in the 1960s through 1980s, a period when pier-and-beam and block foundations were standard in this part of Mississippi. Those foundations leave crawl spaces vulnerable to groundwater intrusion whenever the water table rises after prolonged rain. Older galvanized supply lines in homes of that era are also prone to pinhole leaks that go undetected behind walls for weeks, saturating insulation and framing long before a visible stain appears on drywall. The combination of aging housing stock and a climate that delivers both intense rain events and high ambient humidity creates conditions where water damage escalates faster here than in drier regions.

## Our Water Damage Restoration Process in Ellisville

The first priority on any job is stopping ongoing water entry and identifying every affected material — not just the obvious wet floor, but the wall cavity behind it, the subfloor beneath it, and the insulation above it. Our IICRC WRT and ASD certified technicians use thermal imaging cameras and calibrated moisture meters to map the full extent of saturation before a single piece of equipment is placed. That moisture map drives the drying plan.

Extraction comes next. Truck-mounted and portable extraction units pull standing water from hard floors, carpet, and subfloor assemblies. Once free water is removed, we set drying systems — commercial-grade desiccant or refrigerant dehumidifiers and high-velocity air movers positioned according to the IICRC S500 psychrometric drying standard — to draw moisture out of structural materials at a controlled rate. Daily moisture readings track progress and let us adjust equipment placement rather than guessing. When materials cannot be dried in place — saturated particleboard subfloor is a common example in Ellisville homes of this era — we document, remove, and photograph everything for the insurance file before disposal.
