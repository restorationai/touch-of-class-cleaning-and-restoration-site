## Why Wade Properties See Elevated Contents Damage Risk

Mississippi's Gulf Coast climate — high relative humidity through much of the year, intense summer thunderstorms, and the ever-present threat of tropical systems tracking inland — creates conditions where contents damage compounds fast. Wade's residential areas include a mix of older wood-frame homes and newer construction, and that older housing stock matters. Homes built before the 1980s often have less vapor-resistant wall assemblies, which means a roof leak or burst pipe doesn't just wet the floor — moisture migrates laterally into walls and subfloors, and the belongings sitting against those walls absorb ambient humidity before water even reaches them visibly.

Furniture, textiles, documents, and electronics are particularly vulnerable in this climate. A chest of drawers pushed against an exterior wall in a home without adequate vapor control can show mold colonization on the back panel within days of a water intrusion event — long before a homeowner notices any visible ceiling or floor damage. Recognizing that pattern early is part of what shapes how the pack-out process is prioritized on every job in this area.

## The Contents Pack-Out and Restoration Process

The process begins with a room-by-room contents inventory before anything is moved. Every item is photographed, catalogued by condition, and assigned a cleaning or restoration classification — salvageable with cleaning, salvageable with specialized treatment, or non-salvageable for insurance documentation purposes. That documentation matters when you're filing a claim, because a thorough inventory is the difference between a fair settlement and a dispute over what was actually in the home.

Once inventoried, items are carefully packed and transported to a climate-controlled storage facility. Smoke-damaged furniture goes through dry-soot removal first — vacuuming with HEPA-filtered equipment before any wet cleaning, because introducing moisture to dry soot without that step drives particles deeper into fibers. Textiles are assessed for dye stability before any chemical deodorization is applied. Hard surfaces — cabinetry, wood furniture, framed artwork — are cleaned with pH-appropriate solutions and then treated for odor using thermal fogging or hydroxyl generation depending on the material sensitivity.

The IICRC FSRT (Fire & Smoke Restoration) and IICRC AMRT (Applied Microbial Remediation) certifications held by HomeLyft Restoration MS inform the sequencing of that work — particularly when a single loss event involves both smoke and water damage, which is common when firefighting suppression soaks contents that were also exposed to combustion byproducts.

Contents restoration claims are often the most disputed portion of a property loss claim, because insurers and homeowners frequently disagree on whether an item is restorable or needs replacement. These materials respond differently to smoke and water than the composite woods used in modern furniture: they're more resistant to warping in some ways, but they also hold smoke odor deep in the grain and require longer ozone or hydroxyl treatment cycles to fully deodorize.
