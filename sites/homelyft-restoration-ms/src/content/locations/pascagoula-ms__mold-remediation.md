## Why Pascagoula Properties See Persistent Mold Issues

Jackson County's coastal climate is the primary driver, but the local housing stock adds a layer of complexity. A significant share of Pascagoula's residential neighborhoods were built in the post-WWII boom years when Ingalls Shipbuilding was expanding rapidly — meaning many homes are 60 to 70 years old, with pier-and-beam foundations, original wood subflooring, and crawl spaces that were never designed with today's understanding of vapor barriers and mechanical ventilation. Those crawl spaces trap ground moisture and funnel it directly into floor joists and wall cavities, creating ideal conditions for Cladosporium, Penicillium, and the Stachybotrys strains commonly called black mold.

Post-Katrina rebuilds introduced a different set of challenges. Homes reconstructed quickly after 2005 sometimes used materials that weren't fully dried before installation — drywall, OSB sheathing, and insulation that arrived on-site damp and were sealed in before anyone knew there was a problem. Years later, those hidden reservoirs of organic material can begin supporting mold growth with no visible water intrusion at all.

## Our Mold Remediation Process in Pascagoula

Every project starts with a thorough visual inspection and moisture mapping using thermal imaging and calibrated moisture meters — not a walk-through with a flashlight. In Pascagoula's older pier-and-beam homes, that means going under the structure to assess joist conditions, vapor barrier integrity, and any standing moisture near the foundation piers. We document findings with photographs before any work begins, which matters when an insurance claim is involved.

Once the scope is established, we establish negative-air containment using 6-mil poly barriers and HEPA-filtered air scrubbers to prevent cross-contamination to unaffected rooms. Affected porous materials — drywall, insulation, compromised wood — are removed and bagged per EPA guidelines. Structural surfaces are HEPA-vacuumed, treated with EPA-registered antimicrobial agents, and dried to manufacturer-specified moisture content before any rebuild begins. Because the IICRC S520 standard guides our process, the documentation we produce is recognized by most major insurance carriers.
