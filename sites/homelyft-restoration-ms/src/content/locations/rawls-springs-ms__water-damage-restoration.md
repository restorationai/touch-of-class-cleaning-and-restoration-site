## Why Rawls Springs Properties Face Distinct Water Damage Risks

Forrest County averages well over 60 inches of rainfall per year, and Rawls Springs — positioned on rolling terrain north of Hattiesburg — channels that runoff in ways flat-lot subdivisions don't. Graded lots that looked fine during a dry summer can funnel sheet flow directly against a slab or into a crawl space opening after a Gulf-driven storm system stalls overhead for two or three days. That's not a rare event in this part of Mississippi; it's a seasonal pattern.

The housing stock compounds the risk. A significant portion of Rawls Springs homes were built between the 1960s and 1990s, when vapor barriers under pier-and-beam construction were inconsistently installed and supply lines were often galvanized steel. Galvanized pipe corrodes from the inside out, so the first sign of a problem is frequently a wet subfloor or a stain on a ceiling — not a visible drip. By the time a homeowner notices, water has often been migrating through wall cavities for days.

## Our Water Damage Restoration Process in Rawls Springs

Every job starts with a thorough moisture mapping inspection using thermal imaging cameras and calibrated moisture meters. In Rawls Springs homes with pier-and-beam construction, that means checking not just the visible floor surface but the subfloor decking, the joists below, and the ground-level vapor barrier — or confirming one is absent. Water hides in places a visual inspection misses.

Once the scope is documented, extraction comes first. Truck-mounted and portable extraction units pull standing water from hard surfaces, carpet, and saturated insulation. After bulk water removal, the drying phase begins: industrial-grade desiccant or refrigerant dehumidifiers and high-velocity air movers are positioned according to the IICRC S500 standard, which governs drying chamber setup, equipment ratios, and daily moisture readings. Drying logs are maintained throughout — those records matter for your insurance adjuster and for confirming the structure is genuinely dry before any reconstruction begins.

For homes with older wood floors or tongue-and-groove subfloor planking — common in Rawls Springs properties built before 1980 — drying timelines run longer than in newer construction with OSB sheathing. We account for that in our equipment placement and daily monitoring rather than pulling equipment prematurely.
