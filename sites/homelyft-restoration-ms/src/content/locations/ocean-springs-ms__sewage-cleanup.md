## Why Ocean Springs Properties See Sewage Backup Issues

The Gulf Coast's subtropical humidity and frequent heavy rainfall are the backdrop, but the specific risk in Ocean Springs comes from a few compounding factors. A significant portion of the residential housing stock — particularly in the older sections closer to downtown and along the Back Bay — was built with cast-iron or clay-tile drain lines that are now 50 to 70 years old. Those materials corrode, root-intrude, and collapse in ways that PVC simply doesn't. When a line fails, it rarely fails cleanly: partial blockages cause slow drains for weeks before a full backup forces sewage up through floor drains, toilets, or washing machine standpipes.

Septic systems are equally common outside the city's sewer service area, and the sandy loam and coastal soil composition around Ocean Springs can reduce drain-field absorption after prolonged rain events. A system that functions fine in dry months can overflow onto a crawl space or yard after a multi-day soaking rain — the kind that rolls through Jackson County every spring and fall.

## Our Sewage Cleanup and Sanitization Process in Ocean Springs

Category 3 water — the classification for raw sewage — requires a structured response, not improvisation. Our process on every Ocean Springs job follows the IICRC S500 standard and includes these concrete steps:

1. **Containment and safety assessment** — We identify the contamination boundary, establish negative air pressure where needed, and ensure no cross-contamination reaches unaffected living areas.
2. **Extraction and solid waste removal** — Truck-mounted extraction equipment pulls standing sewage and saturated material. Solid waste is bagged and disposed of per Mississippi DEQ guidelines.
3. **Structural material evaluation** — Porous materials (drywall, insulation, subfloor sheathing) that have absorbed Category 3 water are removed rather than dried in place. There is no safe way to dry contaminated porous material back to a usable condition.
4. **EPA-registered disinfection** — All affected hard surfaces are treated with hospital-grade disinfectants. We pay particular attention to pier-and-beam crawl spaces, which are common in Ocean Springs and trap contaminated moisture against wood framing.
5. **Structural drying and clearance** — After sanitization, we deploy desiccant dehumidifiers and air movers calibrated to the ambient humidity typical of coastal Mississippi — not the drier inland settings some equipment is factory-tuned for.
6. **Documentation for insurance** — Every affected area is photographed, moisture-mapped, and logged before, during, and after remediation.

Sewage backup claims are handled differently from standard water damage claims under most homeowner policies. Many policies exclude sewer backup unless the homeowner purchased a specific rider — a detail that surprises people at the worst possible moment. Our team documents the loss thoroughly: moisture readings, photo logs, material inventories, and scope of work that adjusters and carriers need to process a claim.
