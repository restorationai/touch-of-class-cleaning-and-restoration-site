Pearlington sits at the edge of the Pearl River delta, where the land stays saturated for weeks after a heavy rain and the summer humidity rarely dips low enough to give a crawl space a chance to dry on its own. That combination — high water table, clay-heavy soil that holds moisture like a sponge, and Gulf Coast summers that push dew points into the low 70s — means an unencapsulated crawl space here doesn't just get damp occasionally. It becomes a slow-moving source of wood rot, mold colonization, and structural softening that works from the ground up through your floor joists before most homeowners notice anything wrong.

## Why Pearlington Properties Are Especially Vulnerable

The Pearl River has shaped this community in more ways than one. Floodwaters from major storm events have repeatedly pushed into low-lying areas throughout Hancock County, and the soil left behind is dense, poorly draining, and prone to holding groundwater close to the surface long after the visible flooding is gone. Homes built on pier-and-beam foundations — common throughout this part of the Mississippi Gulf Coast — have crawl spaces that sit directly in the path of that residual moisture. Warm, humid air from outside enters through foundation vents, meets the cooler soil and wood surfaces below, and condenses. Do that cycle daily for a few years and you get wood moisture content well above the 19% threshold where decay fungi begin to establish.

The region's proximity to tidal influence also means that even properties not directly on the river can experience groundwater fluctuations tied to rainfall events upstream. A crawl space that looks fine in March can be holding standing water by July without a single pipe having failed.

## Our Crawl Space Encapsulation Process in Pearlington

Every encapsulation job starts with a thorough moisture assessment — we measure wood moisture content at multiple joist locations, check for existing mold or fungal growth, and document any standing water or efflorescence on the foundation walls. In Pearlington's climate, skipping this step means installing a vapor barrier over a problem that will continue underneath it.

Once the crawl space is cleared and dried, we install a heavy-duty reinforced polyethylene liner — minimum 20-mil — across the floor and up the foundation walls, sealed at all seams and mechanically fastened at the perimeter. On Hancock County properties where the soil is particularly soft or uneven, we take extra care to lap the liner over any piers and seal around them individually, since those penetrations are common failure points in cheaper installations.

Vent sealing follows. Older Gulf Coast homes were built with open foundation vents based on building science that has since been revised — sealed crawl spaces with controlled conditioned air or a dedicated dehumidifier consistently outperform vented crawl spaces in humid climates like this one. We seal existing vents and, where appropriate, install a crawl space dehumidifier sized to the actual square footage and humidity load of the space. Finally, we document the completed installation with photographs and moisture readings so you have a baseline record.

## What Makes Encapsulation Different Here Than in Drier Climates

In a place like central Mississippi, a basic 6-mil poly sheet stapled to the joists might hold up for years. In Pearlington, that approach fails faster than most homeowners expect. The combination of high annual rainfall, prolonged humidity, and the occasional flood event demands materials and installation methods that account for real-world abuse — foot traffic during future inspections, water intrusion events, and the expansion and contraction that comes with extreme seasonal temperature swings.

The IICRC AMRT standard that guides our mold remediation work also informs how we approach encapsulation: if there is active mold present in the crawl space, it must be properly remediated before any barrier goes down. Sealing mold in does not stop it — it just removes your ability to monitor it.

## Local Note

One thing that comes up repeatedly on Pearlington jobs is the presence of older pier-and-beam homes where the crawl space clearance is very shallow — sometimes under 18 inches — because the original builders set the piers low to keep the structure close to grade. That tight clearance makes installation physically demanding and limits the equipment that can be used. It also means moisture problems tend to be more severe, because air circulation is almost nonexistent. When we quote a job in this area, we always ask about clearance upfront so we can plan the installation approach and give you an accurate scope rather than a number that changes once someone gets under the house.
