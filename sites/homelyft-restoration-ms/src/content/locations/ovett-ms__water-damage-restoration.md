## Why Ovett Properties See Water Damage Issues

The housing stock in the Ovett area skews older, with a significant share of homes built in the 1960s through 1980s on pier-and-beam foundations. That construction style has real advantages — it breathes in summer heat — but it also means the crawl space is the first place standing water collects and the last place a homeowner thinks to check. A slow plumbing leak under a pier-and-beam home can saturate the ground beneath the structure for weeks before anyone notices the soft spot in the kitchen floor.

Jones County's clay-heavy soil also creates a drainage problem that flat-slab and newer construction owners underestimate. After a heavy rain event, water that can't percolate fast enough pools against foundation walls and finds its way through any crack or gap in the sill plate. Homes along low-lying stretches of rural roads near Ovett are especially vulnerable because the roadside ditches — maintained by the county — can back up during sustained rainfall, pushing water toward structures rather than away from them.

Aging galvanized supply lines, still present in a number of homes in this part of Jones County, corrode from the inside out. The failure is rarely dramatic until it is — a pinhole becomes a split, usually in winter when overnight temperatures dip into the low 30s and water sitting in an uninsulated crawl-space line freezes.

## Our Water Damage Restoration Process in Ovett

The first priority on any water damage call is stopping the source and assessing how far moisture has traveled — two things that look simple but require calibrated equipment to do accurately. The team arrives with thermal imaging cameras and moisture meters to map the wet zone, including materials that look dry to the eye but are holding water inside.

Extraction comes next. Truck-mounted and portable extraction units pull standing water from flooring, subfloor assemblies, and crawl spaces. On pier-and-beam homes common in this area, that often means running hoses into a crawl space that has limited clearance — something the crew accounts for in staging.

Structural drying follows the IICRC S500 standard, using commercial-grade desiccant dehumidifiers and high-velocity air movers positioned to create a drying chamber around affected assemblies. Moisture readings are logged daily so the drying curve is documented — important for insurance claims and for confirming that framing has reached acceptable moisture content before any reconstruction begins. The team holds IICRC certifications in Water Restoration (WRT) and Applied Structural Drying (ASD), which means the drying plan is based on psychrometric science, not guesswork.

For homes with older building materials — tongue-and-groove subfloor, original plaster, or wood-paneled walls — drying timelines are extended and monitored more closely, because these materials release moisture more slowly than modern OSB and drywall.

## Local Note

In the Ovett area, pier-and-beam homes with original heart-pine subfloor present a drying challenge that doesn't show up on a standard moisture meter reading.
