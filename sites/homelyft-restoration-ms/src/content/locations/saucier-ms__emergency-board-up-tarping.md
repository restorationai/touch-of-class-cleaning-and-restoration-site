## Our Emergency Board-Up and Tarping Process in Saucier

When we arrive on a Saucier property, the first priority is a rapid perimeter walk to identify every compromised opening — not just the obvious ones. In homes with crawl-space foundations common to this part of Harrison County, a roof breach can drive water through ceiling cavities and into wall systems before it ever pools on the floor. We document each opening photographically before we cover it, which matters later when you're working through an insurance claim.

For roof tarping, we use heavy-duty polyethylene tarps secured with batten boards and mechanical fasteners rather than adhesive or rope alone. The humidity and intermittent rain that characterize Saucier's shoulder seasons — spring and fall especially — mean a tarp that's only edge-weighted will lift and channel water rather than shed it. We anchor the ridge line first, then work down both slopes so there's no low point where water can pool and add weight.

Board-up on windows and doors uses 5/8-inch plywood cut to fit the rough opening, secured into the surrounding framing rather than just the trim. On older homes with true-dimension lumber framing, we adjust our fastener length accordingly — the studs are thicker than modern dimensional lumber, and shorter screws won't bite deep enough to hold through a second storm.
