## Why Ocean Springs Properties See Mold Issues

The geography does most of the damage before a homeowner ever notices a smell. Ocean Springs is essentially a peninsula bracketed by the Back Bay of Biloxi to the north and the Mississippi Sound to the south, which means humidity rarely drops low enough for building cavities to dry on their own after a moisture event. Crawl spaces — common in the older neighborhoods clustered around Government Street and the arts district — trap ground vapor year-round. When an AC condensate line clogs, a wax ring fails under a toilet, or a storm pushes water under a door threshold, that moisture has nowhere to go.

The city's older wood-frame homes present an additional layer of complexity. Balloon-frame construction, used widely in Gulf Coast housing built before the 1970s, allows water to wick vertically inside wall cavities from the sill plate to the ceiling without leaving an obvious surface stain. By the time a homeowner smells the musty, earthy odor that signals active mold growth, the colony may already span several stud bays. Salt air accelerates the breakdown of caulk and flashing, creating entry points that aren't visible from a ladder.

## Our Mold Remediation Process in Ocean Springs

Every job starts with a thorough visual inspection and moisture mapping using thermal imaging and pin-type meters to locate moisture that hasn't yet produced visible growth. We document findings with photographs before anything is disturbed — this record matters for insurance claims and for establishing a baseline if a post-remediation test is ordered.

Containment comes next. We seal the affected area with 6-mil polyethylene sheeting and establish negative air pressure using HEPA-filtered air scrubbers so that spores disturbed during removal don't migrate to clean areas of the home. In Ocean Springs homes with open floor plans or central HVAC systems that lack return-air zoning, this step is especially important — a single unsealed register can spread airborne spores through the entire duct network.

Physical removal of contaminated porous materials (drywall, insulation, subflooring where warranted) follows IICRC AMRT guidelines. Non-porous surfaces are HEPA-vacuumed, wire-brushed where necessary, and treated with an EPA-registered antimicrobial. Structural drying equipment — desiccant or refrigerant dehumidifiers and high-velocity air movers — runs until moisture readings in the framing return to acceptable levels, typically between 14% and 19% for wood in this climate. We don't close walls until the numbers confirm it.
