## Why Escatawpa Properties Are Especially Vulnerable After a Fire

The housing stock along the Escatawpa River corridor leans heavily on wood-frame construction — many homes built in the mid-20th century with pier-and-beam foundations and tongue-and-groove interior sheathing. That building style burns fast and absorbs smoke deeply. Unlike poured-concrete or brick-veneer construction, the open cavities beneath these floors and inside the wall bays act as chimneys during a fire, pulling smoke into spaces that are invisible until a restoration crew opens them up.

Jackson County's subtropical humidity compounds the problem. Within hours of a fire being extinguished, moisture from suppression water and ambient air begins working into charred framing. Wet char is a near-perfect growth medium for mold, and in Escatawpa's climate, colonization can begin in as little as 48 to 72 hours. That timeline means the window between fire suppression and the start of professional drying and debris removal is narrow.

Natural gas and propane are both common utility sources in this part of the county, and utility coordination with Mississippi Power and local propane suppliers is a standard early step — the structure cannot be safely entered for assessment until service is confirmed off at the meter or tank.

## Our Fire Damage Restoration Process in Escatawpa

Every fire loss is different, but the sequence we follow is consistent and built around the IICRC S700 standard for fire and smoke restoration.

**Board-up and structural stabilization** come first. Exposed openings invite weather, and in Jackson County that means rain can arrive with little warning. We secure windows, doors, and roof penetrations before any interior work begins.

**Damage documentation** follows — photo and written documentation of every affected surface, structural member, and content item. This documentation package is what drives your insurance claim, and we build it to the level of detail adjusters require.

**Controlled demolition** removes unsalvageable materials: charred framing, melted insulation, smoke-saturated drywall. In pier-and-beam homes common to this area, we often pull subfloor sections to inspect the joist bays beneath — smoke travels down as readily as it travels up.

**Smoke and soot cleaning** addresses what's left. We use dry chemical sponges on porous surfaces, thermal fogging and hydroxyl generation for odor neutralization, and HEPA-filtered negative air machines to capture airborne particulate during the cleaning phase. The salt-humid air here means we run dehumidification simultaneously — you cannot effectively neutralize smoke odor in a structure that is still actively absorbing atmospheric moisture.

**Structural drying** closes the loop, using calibrated drying equipment and moisture mapping to confirm framing and subfloor materials have returned to acceptable moisture content before any rebuild begins.

## Local Note

In the older wood-frame homes common to the Escatawpa area, interior wall cavities were often insulated with cellulose — shredded newspaper product that was standard in mid-century construction across coastal Mississippi. Cellulose absorbs smoke compounds and moisture at a much higher rate than fiberglass batt, and it holds both long after the visible char has been removed. When we open walls in these homes, we always test the insulation separately before deciding whether cleaning is viable or full removal is necessary.
