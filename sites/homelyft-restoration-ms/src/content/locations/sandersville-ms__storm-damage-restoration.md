## Why Sandersville Properties Are Vulnerable to Storm Damage

Mississippi's interior counties experience a longer severe-weather season than the coast does — spring tornado outbreaks, summer convective storms, and the occasional inland remnant of a Gulf hurricane all take their turn. Sandersville and the surrounding Jones County area are no strangers to any of them. The local housing stock includes a meaningful share of older wood-frame homes built before modern wind-load standards were codified, and those structures — with their original roof sheathing nailed on wider spacing — are more susceptible to sheathing blow-off when gusts exceed 60 mph.

Large hardwood trees — oaks and sweet gums especially — are common on residential lots throughout this part of Mississippi. They're beautiful until a storm snaps a limb or uproots a root-ball, sending several tons of wood through a roof deck or onto a vehicle. Tree-impact damage is one of the most common calls we receive after a severe weather event in this region, and the structural assessment that follows is rarely simple: a limb that punches through sheathing can displace rafters, crack ridge boards, and allow water to travel horizontally inside wall cavities before it ever shows up as a ceiling stain.

## Our Storm Damage Restoration Process in Sandersville

The first priority after any storm impact is stopping additional water intrusion. Our crew arrives with heavy-duty polyethylene sheeting, structural lumber, and a lift capable of reaching most residential roof pitches to install emergency tarping over any breached roof sections. We document every affected area with photo and moisture readings before a single piece of debris is moved — that documentation is what your insurance adjuster will rely on when calculating the scope of loss.

Once the structure is secured, we move into a systematic damage assessment: roof decking, attic insulation, ceiling assemblies, wall cavities, and subfloor where applicable. Moisture mapping with calibrated meters tells us how far water has traveled from the point of entry. If the storm dropped enough rain to saturate insulation or wet OSB sheathing, we deploy desiccant or refrigerant dehumidifiers and axial air movers sized to the affected square footage. Drying is monitored daily and logged to IICRC S500 and S520 standards — the same documentation your carrier expects before authorizing reconstruction.

Reconstruction — framing repairs, sheathing replacement, roofing, interior drywall, and paint — is handled by our licensed crews under Mississippi contractor license RO 2728, so you're not coordinating a separate general contractor while your home is still exposed.
