## Why McLain Homes Present Unique Renovation Challenges

Greene County's climate is a study in extremes: summer heat indexes that regularly push past 105°F, followed by winters that occasionally dip below freezing and catch older homes off guard. That swing matters when you're renovating. Wood framing in homes built before the 1980s — and there are plenty of them in McLain — has often cycled through enough wet seasons to show subtle foundation settling, out-of-square door openings, and subfloor movement that a surface-level remodel will expose the moment you pull up old flooring.

Soil composition in this part of Mississippi also plays a role. The sandy loam and clay mix common to Greene County can shift seasonally, and slabs or pier-and-beam foundations in older homes sometimes need leveling or reinforcement before a kitchen or bathroom remodel can be finished correctly. ## Local Note: What McLain's Older Wood-Frame Homes Tell You Before Demolition

Contractors who work primarily in newer construction sometimes underestimate what a McLain-area farmhouse or older bungalow will reveal once demo begins. Balloon-frame construction — common in homes built before World War II in rural Greene County — runs wall studs continuously from foundation to roofline without the fire blocking you'd find in platform-frame homes. That affects how you run new electrical, how you insulate during a remodel, and how you handle any remediation work that needs to go inside those wall cavities.
