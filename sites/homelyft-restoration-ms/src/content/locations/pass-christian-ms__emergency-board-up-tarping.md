## Why Pass Christian Properties See Elevated Board-Up and Tarping Needs

Pass Christian's position on the western end of Harrison County places it among the most storm-exposed municipalities on the Gulf Coast. The city took a direct hit from Hurricane Katrina and has been rebuilt in layers since — some blocks feature post-2005 elevated construction with modern hurricane strapping, while others retain older slab-on-grade homes whose roof decks and window framing predate current wind-load codes. That mix matters for board-up work. A newer elevated home may have engineered trusses that accept standard plywood fasteners cleanly; an older pier-and-beam cottage near the waterfront may have softer, weathered framing that requires longer screws and backing boards to hold a panel securely without causing additional structural damage.

Salt air accelerates corrosion on exposed metal fasteners and weakens window glazing over time, which means broken glass events are more common here than in inland Mississippi cities. Fire damage board-up calls also spike after storm season, when overtaxed electrical systems and generator misuse lead to residential fires that leave walls and rooflines open to rain within hours of suppression.

## Our Emergency Board-Up and Tarping Process in Pass Christian

When you call, the first question we ask is what type of opening needs to be secured — roof deck, window, door, or a combination — because the materials and crew size differ. For roof tarping, we use reinforced polyethylene tarps rated for sustained wind exposure and anchor them with baton boards screwed into the decking rather than simply weighted at the edges. A tarp that blows off at 2 a.m. during a squall is worse than no tarp at all, because the false sense of security delays your call for help.

For broken window board-up and fire damage board-up, we cut plywood panels to fit the rough opening, use construction adhesive at the frame perimeter where nailing would cause cosmetic damage, and apply a weather-resistant barrier tape at the seams. Every opening gets photographed before and after installation — documentation your insurance adjuster will need when the claim is filed. Our team holds IICRC FSRT certification for fire and smoke response, so when a board-up follows a fire, we can assess secondary smoke and water intrusion at the same visit rather than scheduling a separate evaluation.
