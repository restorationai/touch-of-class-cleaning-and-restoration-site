## Why DeLisle Upholstery Holds Onto Moisture and Odor

DeLisle sits in Harrison County along the Wolf River corridor, close enough to the coast that salt air and summer humidity are a near-constant presence indoors, especially in homes without tight, newer HVAC systems. Fabric upholstery, especially cotton and cotton-blend sofas common in older coastal homes, absorbs ambient moisture even when nothing has been spilled. That moisture feeds odor-causing bacteria in the foam and batting long before mold becomes visible on the surface. Leather pieces have a different issue: the same humidity that softens fabric tends to dry out and crack leather finishes if it isn't conditioned regularly, particularly in rooms that get direct afternoon sun through south-facing windows.

## Our Upholstery Cleaning Process in DeLisle

We start with a fabric or leather identification step, since cleaning solutions and heat settings that work on a synthetic blend can damage natural fiber or top-grain leather. For fabric sofas and chairs, we use hot water extraction, sometimes called furniture steam cleaning, which lifts embedded soil and odor-causing residue out of the padding rather than just refreshing the surface fibers. Extraction is followed by controlled drying, because coastal humidity means fabric that air-dries on its own in a closed-up room can stay damp for a day or more, long enough for odor and mildew to return. For leather upholstery, we clean with pH-balanced leather-specific products and follow with a conditioning treatment, since skipping that step in a humid climate accelerates surface cracking. Pet odor, smoke residue, and general household soil each get pretreated differently before the main clean, which matters more on older, heavily used furniture than it does on a newer piece.
