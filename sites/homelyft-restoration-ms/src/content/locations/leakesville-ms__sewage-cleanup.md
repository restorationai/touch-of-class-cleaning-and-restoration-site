Greene County's high clay-content soils drain poorly after heavy Gulf-driven rainfall, and when that saturated ground pushes back against aging sewer laterals or overwhelmed septic systems, raw sewage doesn't just pool — it wicks into subfloor framing, crawl spaces, and wall cavities before most homeowners realize the scope of what they're dealing with. ## Why Leakesville Properties See Sewage Backup Issues

Leakesville sits in a part of Mississippi where a significant share of homes rely on private septic systems rather than municipal sewer connections. When those systems are undersized for the lot, installed decades ago without modern effluent filters, or simply overwhelmed by back-to-back rain events that saturate the drain field, the result is a septic overflow that surfaces in the yard, backs up through floor drains, or seeps into the crawl space beneath the house. Older homes in the area — many built in the mid-twentieth century with pier-and-beam foundations — are especially vulnerable because the crawl space sits close to grade and offers almost no barrier between saturated soil and the subfloor.

Municipal sewer customers in town aren't immune either. Tree root intrusion into clay tile sewer lines is a well-documented problem in Greene County's older residential streets, and a single root-blocked lateral can send sewage back through the lowest drain in the house — often a basement utility sink or a first-floor toilet — during a heavy rain event when the main is already running full.

## Our Sewage Cleanup and Sanitization Process in Leakesville

Every sewage job starts with containment. Before any extraction begins, the affected area is isolated with physical barriers and negative air pressure where the layout allows, so that aerosolized pathogens don't migrate into clean living spaces. From there, the process follows a structured sequence:

**Extraction and removal** — Standing sewage is removed with truck-mounted or portable extraction units. Porous materials that cannot be adequately disinfected — saturated insulation, compromised drywall, soaked carpet — are bagged and removed per Mississippi Department of Environmental Quality disposal guidelines for Category 3 waste.

**Surface disinfection** — Structural surfaces that remain (concrete, treated lumber, tile) are treated with EPA-registered disinfectants. For crawl spaces, this includes vapor barrier removal and replacement when the barrier has been compromised by sewage contact.

**Drying and dehumidification** — Because Leakesville's ambient humidity runs high through much of the year, passive drying is not adequate. We deploy desiccant or refrigerant dehumidifiers and air movers calibrated to the moisture readings taken at the start of the job. Drying is monitored with moisture meters until structural readings return to baseline.

**Post-remediation verification** — Before any reconstruction begins, affected areas are re-tested for moisture and surface contamination. Documentation of pre- and post-remediation conditions is provided for insurance purposes.
