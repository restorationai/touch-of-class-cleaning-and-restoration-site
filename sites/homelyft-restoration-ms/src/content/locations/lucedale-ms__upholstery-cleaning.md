## Why Lucedale Upholstery Sees Different Wear

Lucedale sits as the county seat of a largely rural, agricultural stretch of southeast Mississippi, and that shows up in how furniture ages. Pollen counts run high through spring, pet dander builds up fast in homes near wooded lots, and red clay soil tracked in from yards and gravel driveways settles deep into fabric weave rather than sitting on the surface. Leather pieces face their own challenge: the swing between air-conditioned indoor air and outdoor humidity dries and cracks hides faster than owners expect, especially on furniture positioned near windows or exterior doors. Mobile and manufactured homes, common throughout George County, also tend to run higher interior humidity than site-built houses, which means fabric upholstery in those homes can start holding a musty smell well before a stain ever becomes visible.

## Our Upholstery Cleaning Process for Lucedale Homes

We start with a fiber and dye test on an inconspicuous section, since Lucedale households mix everything from older cotton-blend sofas passed down through generations to newer microfiber sectionals, and each reacts differently to moisture and cleaning agents. For fabric pieces, we use low-moisture steam extraction that lifts embedded soil and allergens without oversaturating the padding underneath, which matters in a climate where anything left damp too long invites odor and mildew. Leather upholstery gets a pH-balanced cleaner followed by a conditioning step to replace oils lost to Mississippi's heat and AC cycling. We finish with a controlled drying pass using air movers, since ambient humidity here rarely dries furniture on its own the way it might in a drier region.

## Local note

One thing crews notice repeatedly in George County: furniture stored in detached garages, barns, or hunting camps for part of the year often comes back inside with mold spores already established in the padding, even when the fabric surface looks fine.
