## Why Saucier Properties See Asbestos Concerns

Harrison County's building boom in the 1950s through the 1970s left a significant footprint of structures that predate modern asbestos regulations. In Saucier specifically, many properties were built as working farmsteads or rural residential homes — often with multiple additions and renovations layered on top of original construction. That layering is where asbestos risk compounds: a 1960s floor tile gets covered with 1970s vinyl, which gets covered with 1990s laminate, and no one questions what's underneath until a renovation or a water intrusion event disturbs it.

The Gulf Coast's heat and humidity also stress building materials faster than in drier climates. Pipe wrap insulation, duct tape on HVAC systems, and roofing underlayment that were installed decades ago can become friable — meaning they crumble easily and release fibers into the air — well before a homeowner suspects a problem. Popcorn ceiling texture, a particularly common finish in homes built between 1955 and 1978, is one of the most frequently tested materials in older Saucier homes and one of the most likely to contain chrysotile asbestos.

## Our Asbestos Abatement Process in Saucier

Every abatement project begins with a proper inspection and bulk sampling — not assumptions. Suspect materials are collected by trained personnel following EPA sampling protocols and sent to an accredited laboratory. Results typically return within a few business days, giving you documented confirmation before any removal work begins.

Once asbestos-containing materials are confirmed, the work area is isolated with negative air pressure containment using HEPA-filtered air scrubbers. This prevents cross-contamination to the rest of the structure — a step that matters especially in Saucier's older homes, where open floor plans and uninsulated interior walls can allow airborne particles to travel quickly. Abatement technicians work in full PPE, and all removed material is double-bagged, labeled, and transported to a licensed disposal facility in compliance with Mississippi Department of Environmental Quality (MDEQ) regulations.

Final air clearance testing is conducted after the containment is broken down, confirming that fiber counts are within acceptable limits before the space is released for re-occupancy or continued renovation work. That documentation is provided to you in writing — something your contractor, real estate agent, or insurance adjuster may require.
