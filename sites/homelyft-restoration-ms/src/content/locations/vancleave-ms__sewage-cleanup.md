## Why Vancleave Properties See Sewage Backup Issues

Vancleave sits in a part of Jackson County where lot sizes are generous and septic systems are the norm rather than the exception. The region's soil profile — a mix of sandy loam near the surface and dense clay underneath — drains well in dry weather but becomes nearly impermeable after sustained rainfall. When the ground can't absorb effluent fast enough, pressure builds in the lateral lines and waste reverses course into the home.

Older homes in the area, particularly those built in the 1970s and 1980s when the community was growing rapidly, often have cast-iron or Orangeburg drain lines that have corroded or collapsed over decades. A partial blockage that seems minor in dry weather can become a full sewage overflow the first time a summer storm drops three inches in an afternoon — which is not unusual along the Gulf Coast between June and October. Homes on lower-elevation lots are especially vulnerable when the water table rises quickly.

## Our Sewage Cleanup and Sanitization Process in Vancleave

Raw sewage is classified as Category 3 — "black water" — by the IICRC S500 standard, meaning it carries pathogens that require full containment and professional-grade disinfection, not just surface cleaning. The process our IICRC-certified team follows reflects that.

First, we establish containment to prevent cross-contamination to unaffected areas of the home. Affected materials — flooring, baseboards, insulation, drywall — are assessed and, where saturation is confirmed, removed. Porous materials that have contacted raw sewage are not salvageable and must go. After extraction and removal, we apply EPA-registered disinfectants with documented dwell times, then run air movers and dehumidifiers calibrated to the structure's square footage and the ambient humidity, which in Vancleave's climate runs high even indoors. Moisture readings are logged throughout drying to confirm the structure is returning to normal levels before any rebuild begins.

Because many Vancleave properties have crawl spaces rather than slab foundations, we also inspect below the subfloor. Sewage that wicks into a crawl space is easy to miss and creates conditions for mold colonization — which can begin in as little as 24 to 48 hours in Mississippi's heat and humidity.
