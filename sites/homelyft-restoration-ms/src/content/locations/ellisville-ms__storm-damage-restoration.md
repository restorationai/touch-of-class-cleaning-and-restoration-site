## Why Ellisville Properties See Repeated Storm Damage

Jones County averages more than 60 thunderstorm days per year, and Ellisville's position along the US-11 corridor puts it in the path of both Gulf-spawned systems that track northeast after landfall and tornado-producing supercells that form along the Mississippi-Alabama line. That combination is harder on structures than a single hurricane season because the damage accumulates — a compromised ridge cap from one storm lets water in during the next, and by the time a homeowner notices staining on the ceiling, the decking underneath may already be soft.

The housing stock in and around Ellisville adds another layer of complexity. Many homes here were built between the 1950s and 1980s using construction methods that predate current Mississippi wind-load requirements. Roof-to-wall connections on those structures are often toe-nailed rather than hurricane-strapped, which means a strong straight-line wind event — not even a tornado — can lift roof sections partially free. When that happens, the interior is exposed to rainfall for however long it takes to get a tarp in place, and the clock on mold colonization starts immediately. Under humid inland Mississippi conditions in summer, visible mold growth can begin within 24 to 48 hours on wet organic materials like OSB and framing lumber.

## Our Storm Damage Restoration Process in Ellisville

Every job starts with a systematic exterior and interior assessment — we document structural breaches, map water intrusion paths, and identify secondary hazards like downed power lines or compromised load-bearing elements before any crew enters an unsafe area. Photographs and moisture readings are captured at this stage because they form the backbone of your insurance claim.

Emergency tarping and board-up follow immediately for any opening that exposes the interior to weather. We use reinforced poly sheeting secured to the decking, not just draped over the ridge, so the protection holds through aftershocks of the same storm system. Interior water extraction comes next — truck-mounted extractors pull standing water from hard floors and carpet, and we deploy desiccant or refrigerant dehumidifiers calibrated to the actual cubic footage of the affected space, not a one-size number. Structural drying is monitored with daily moisture readings until materials reach documented dry standards per the IICRC S500 and S520 frameworks.

Once the structure is dry and stable, reconstruction begins — roofing, siding, drywall, insulation, flooring — coordinated as a single project so you're not managing separate contractors.
