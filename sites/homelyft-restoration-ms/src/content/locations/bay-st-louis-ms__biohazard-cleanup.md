## Why Bay St. Louis Properties Present Unique Biohazard Challenges

The Gulf Coast climate is the defining factor. Temperatures regularly climb into the 90s from late spring through early fall, and relative humidity rarely dips low enough to slow microbial activity. When biological material is present in an enclosed space — a bedroom, a crawl space, a storage unit — decomposition and pathogen spread accelerate in ways that wouldn't occur in a drier or cooler climate. The window for safe, contained remediation is shorter here than in most of the country.

The housing stock adds another layer. Bay St. Louis has a mix of older pier-and-beam homes, many built before modern subfloor standards, and post-Katrina reconstruction builds. Older homes often have unfinished crawl spaces and porous wood framing that can absorb biological fluids below the visible surface. That means remediation isn't just about what you can see — it requires testing beneath flooring and inside wall cavities to confirm the scope before any restoration work begins.

Because Bay St. Louis is an incorporated city in Hancock County, biohazard waste disposal must follow Mississippi State Department of Health guidelines and applicable EPA standards for regulated medical and infectious waste. HomeLyft carries the certifications and trained personnel to handle transport and disposal through licensed channels — this is not a task for general cleaning crews or standard waste haulers.

## Our Biohazard Cleanup Process in Bay St. Louis

Every situation is different, but the framework is consistent. When you call, the first conversation is brief and private — we gather only what we need to mobilize the right team and equipment. We do not ask for more detail than necessary.

On arrival, the crew assesses the full scope before any work begins. In Gulf Coast homes, that assessment specifically includes checking subfloor decking, wall base plates, and any crawl space directly below the affected area — because in pier-and-beam construction, fluids travel downward through gaps that aren't always obvious. Containment barriers go up before any material is disturbed, and the team works in full personal protective equipment throughout.

All biologically contaminated materials — porous surfaces, textiles, flooring, drywall — are double-bagged in certified biohazard containers and transported by a licensed medical waste carrier. The space is then treated with hospital-grade disinfectants and, where indicated, ATP testing is used to verify that surfaces are free of biological residue before containment comes down. Our IICRC-certified team and OSHA-trained technicians follow protocols designed for exactly this environment.
