## Our Emergency Board-Up and Tarping Process in Leaf

Every job starts with a rapid visual assessment of the structure — identifying all breach points, checking for load-bearing concerns before any crew member goes onto a damaged roof, and photographing every affected area for your insurance documentation. That photo record matters: carriers want to see the pre-mitigation condition to validate the scope of loss.

For broken window board-up, we use minimum ⅝-inch plywood secured with structural screws rather than nails, which pull out under wind load. Edges are sealed with construction-grade foam tape to block wind-driven rain — a step that makes a real difference during the kind of overnight squalls Jones County sees in spring and early summer.

Roof tarping follows the IICRC FSRT guidelines for post-fire structural protection and uses reinforced polyethylene tarps rated for high-wind exposure. Tarps are anchored with weighted lumber battens screwed into the decking — not just draped and tied — so they hold through subsequent weather events while permanent repairs are scheduled.

## Local Note

One thing our crews have observed on older homes throughout the Jones County area: many wood-frame structures built before the 1980s used board-and-batten or tongue-and-groove sheathing rather than plywood decking beneath the roofing material. When a storm or fire damages the outer roofing layer on these homes, the underlying sheathing boards can have gaps between them — gaps that a standard tarp installation may not fully seal at the edges. We account for this by running an additional layer of 6-mil poly sheeting under the tarp on suspected older-sheathing roofs, which closes those gaps and prevents wind-driven rain from channeling through the deck while the permanent repair is pending.
