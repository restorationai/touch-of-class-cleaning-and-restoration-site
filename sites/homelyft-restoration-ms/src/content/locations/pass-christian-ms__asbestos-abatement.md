## Why Pass Christian Properties See Asbestos Concerns More Often Than Newer Communities

Pass Christian's housing stock tells the story of a community that rebuilt — and rebuilt again — after storms. Many of the homes that survived Camille in 1969 and were repaired rather than replaced carry original construction materials from the 1940s, '50s, and '60s. Asbestos was used heavily in that era as a fireproofing and insulation material precisely because it was durable, and durable is what Gulf Coast builders needed.

The materials most commonly found in these properties include popcorn ceiling texture (sprayed acoustical coatings were widely asbestos-laden through the mid-1970s), vinyl floor tiles and the mastic adhesive beneath them, pipe and duct insulation in crawl spaces and attics, and exterior transite siding or soffit panels. Coastal humidity is a compounding factor: moisture infiltration softens adhesives and causes materials to become friable — meaning they crumble easily and release fibers — faster than they would in a drier climate. A slow roof leak or a single flood event can accelerate that process significantly.

Mississippi also requires that asbestos abatement on regulated quantities of material be performed by contractors licensed through the Mississippi Department of Environmental Quality (MDEQ). ## Our Asbestos Abatement Process in Pass Christian

Every project begins with a bulk-sample inspection — a licensed inspector collects small physical samples from suspect materials (ceiling texture, floor tiles, insulation, siding) and submits them to an accredited laboratory. You receive a written report identifying which materials contain asbestos and at what percentage, which determines whether Mississippi's regulated-quantity thresholds apply and what abatement method is required.

Once a scope is confirmed, the work area is isolated with poly sheeting and negative air pressure is established using HEPA-filtered air scrubbers. This containment prevents fibers from migrating into living areas during removal. Depending on the material type, abatement technicians use wet methods — saturating the material before removal to suppress fiber release — or encapsulation where full removal isn't required or practical. All removed material is double-bagged in labeled, sealed asbestos waste bags and transported to an approved disposal facility; Mississippi does not permit asbestos debris to go to standard municipal landfills.

After removal, a final visual inspection and, when warranted, air clearance sampling confirm that fiber levels have returned to background before containment is dismantled and the space is released for renovation or reoccupancy.

## Local Note

One thing that comes up repeatedly in Pass Christian's older beachfront and near-beach homes is the combination of asbestos floor tile and the black cutback mastic adhesive used to bond it. The tile itself may test positive, but the mastic beneath it almost always does too — and it's the mastic that's trickier to address because it bonds directly to the concrete slab. In Mississippi's climate, that mastic softens in summer heat and can smear during removal attempts, increasing the risk of fiber release if the work isn't done wet and methodically. Encapsulation with a skim coat of self-leveling compound is sometimes the more practical solution than full removal, particularly when the slab is going to be covered with new flooring anyway. That call gets made based on the lab results and the renovation plan — not a one-size answer.
