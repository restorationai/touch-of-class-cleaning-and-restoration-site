## Why Ocean Springs Properties See Water Damage

Ocean Springs sits on the north shore of Biloxi Bay in Jackson County, and that coastal position shapes how water damage shows up here. Humidity off the Mississippi Sound keeps ambient moisture high most of the year, which slows natural drying even after a leak is stopped. A lot of the housing stock near the water is slab-on-grade or built up on piers, both common responses to a high local water table, and each construction type traps moisture differently. Slab homes push water sideways under baseboards and into wall cavities before anyone notices a puddle, while pier-and-beam homes let water collect underneath, soaking joists and subfloor out of sight. Add hurricane season and the sudden downpours that roll in off the Gulf, and a lot of Ocean Springs water intrusion starts as wind-driven rain working past flashing or window seals rather than a dramatic pipe failure.

## Our Water Extraction Process in Ocean Springs

We start with extraction, pulling standing water with truck-mount and portable units depending on how deep the water is and whether power is still on in the structure. Moisture mapping comes next: we check walls, subfloor, and crawlspace framing with meters, not guesswork, because surface-dry drywall or slab tile can hide a wet wall cavity or a saturated joist underneath. Air movers and dehumidifiers go in once we know the full extent, and we size that equipment to the humidity load, not just the square footage, since Gulf Coast air carries more moisture than drying plans built for drier climates. We monitor and log readings through the drying period and pull equipment only once materials test dry per IICRC S500 guidelines, not on a fixed calendar.

## Local note

One thing that catches a lot of Ocean Springs homeowners off guard: a pier-and-beam home can look completely dry from inside after a leak, while the crawlspace underneath stays saturated for days because ventilation down there is limited and Gulf Coast humidity won't let wood framing release moisture on its own. We check crawlspace moisture on every pier-built home we work, even when the visible flooring reads dry, because that hidden moisture is exactly what mold needs to get started within 24 to 48 hours.
