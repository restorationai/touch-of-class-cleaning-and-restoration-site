## Why Hurley Properties See Sewage Backup Problems

Hurley sits in a part of Jackson County where a significant portion of residential properties rely on private septic systems rather than municipal sewer connections. That distinction matters enormously when something goes wrong. A septic tank that hasn't been pumped on schedule, a drain field saturated after heavy Gulf Coast rainfall, or tree root intrusion into aging lateral lines can all send raw sewage back through floor drains, toilets, or washing machine connections with little warning.

The region's clay-heavy soil composition also plays a role. During extended wet seasons — and the Mississippi Gulf Coast sees plenty of those — clay soils can become so saturated that drain fields lose their absorption capacity entirely. When the ground can't take any more water, the path of least resistance is back into the house. Homes on larger rural lots, which are common throughout the Hurley area, often have older septic infrastructure that was sized for smaller households and hasn't been upgraded since installation.

## Our Sewage Cleanup and Sanitization Process in Hurley

Raw sewage is classified as Category 3 water — the most contaminated category recognized by the IICRC S500 standard — which means the cleanup protocol is more involved than a burst pipe or appliance overflow. When we arrive at a Hurley property, the first priority is containment: stopping the source if it hasn't already been addressed, then establishing a work perimeter to prevent cross-contamination to unaffected areas of the home.

Extraction of standing sewage comes next, using truck-mounted and portable extraction units capable of handling solids-laden water. Once liquid waste is removed, affected hard surfaces are cleaned with EPA-registered disinfectants, and porous materials — drywall, insulation, subfloor decking, carpeting — are assessed for salvageability. In most Category 3 losses, porous materials that have direct sewage contact cannot be dried and saved; they need to be removed and disposed of per Mississippi Department of Environmental Quality guidelines for biohazard waste.

After demolition and cleaning, we apply antimicrobial treatments to structural cavities before any drying equipment is placed. Structural drying in Hurley's climate requires careful monitoring because outdoor humidity levels often run high enough to slow evaporation significantly — our technicians adjust equipment settings and drying targets based on daily psychrometric readings rather than a fixed schedule.

Whether a sewage backup claim is covered depends heavily on the specific policy language.
