## Why Picayune Properties See Asbestos Concerns

Pearl River County's building boom in the 1950s through the 1970s coincided exactly with peak asbestos use in American construction. Builders in this part of Mississippi relied on asbestos-containing materials for their durability in the region's heat and their resistance to moisture — a practical choice given how aggressively Gulf Coast humidity attacks building materials. Siding shingles, duct tape on HVAC systems, joint compound, and vinyl floor tiles were all common carriers. Today, those same materials are aging, and any disturbance — a contractor cutting into a wall, a homeowner scraping a ceiling — can release fibers into the air.

The climate adds another layer of urgency. Picayune's combination of high annual rainfall and warm temperatures accelerates the deterioration of older building materials. Asbestos-containing materials that are crumbling or water-damaged are classified as "friable" — meaning fibers can become airborne with minimal disturbance. A roof leak or plumbing failure in a pre-1980 home here isn't just a water damage problem; it's a potential asbestos exposure event that requires testing before remediation crews begin any tear-out.

## Our Asbestos Abatement Process in Picayune

Every project starts with a thorough asbestos inspection and bulk sampling. Samples are sent to an accredited laboratory, and no abatement work begins until results are confirmed. This step protects you legally and ensures the scope of work is accurate — there's no value in removing materials that don't contain asbestos, and there's serious risk in missing materials that do.

Once a positive result is confirmed, the work area is isolated using negative air pressure containment with HEPA filtration units. All asbestos-containing material is wetted before removal to suppress fiber release, then double-bagged in approved waste containers and transported to a licensed disposal facility in compliance with Mississippi Department of Environmental Quality regulations. Final air clearance testing confirms fiber counts are below regulatory thresholds before containment is removed and the space is returned to you.

For popcorn ceiling asbestos removal specifically — one of the most common requests in older Picayune homes — the process requires careful ceiling-level containment and meticulous cleanup of horizontal surfaces below, since disturbed ceiling texture settles on flooring, furniture, and HVAC registers if the workspace isn't properly sealed.

## Local Note

One pattern that comes up repeatedly in Picayune's older residential areas: homes built in the late 1950s and 1960s often have asbestos-containing floor tiles installed directly over original hardwood. Homeowners planning to refinish or expose those hardwood floors sometimes don't realize the tile layer exists until demolition begins. In these cases, the tile adhesive — called "black mastic" — may also contain asbestos, and both materials need to be tested and potentially abated before any flooring work continues. Knowing this pattern ahead of time can save a renovation project from a costly mid-job stoppage.
