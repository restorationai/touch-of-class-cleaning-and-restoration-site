## Why Sandersville Properties Are Vulnerable After Sudden Damage

Jones County sits squarely in a corridor that sees frequent severe thunderstorm watches and the occasional tornado warning from late spring through early fall. That weather pattern means roof damage here often arrives fast and without much warning — a line of storms at 2 a.m. can strip shingles, crack ridge boards, and drive water into attic insulation before sunrise. Older wood-frame homes common throughout Sandersville and the surrounding rural stretches of Jones County are particularly susceptible: aging rooflines with worn flashing are the first to fail under wind-driven rain, and once water gets under the decking, the clock starts on mold colonization — typically 24 to 72 hours in Mississippi's humidity.

Fire damage compounds the exposure problem. A kitchen or electrical fire that chars window frames and melts glazing leaves openings that are immediately attractive to opportunistic entry. Board-up in that context is not just weather protection — it is a basic security measure that most insurance carriers expect to see documented within the first day of a loss.

## Our Board-Up and Tarping Process in Sandersville

When we arrive on site, the first step is a quick structural walk to identify every breach: missing or lifted shingles, cracked or absent skylights, blown-out windows, and any door frames that have been warped by heat or impact. We photograph each opening before we touch anything — that documentation becomes part of the insurance file.

For roof tarping, we use 6-mil polyethylene tarps rated for wind uplift and secure them with lumber nailer strips fastened into the decking, not just weighted at the edges. A tarp that peels off in the next storm is worse than no tarp at all because it gives a false sense of protection. For windows and doors, we cut OSB panels to fit flush with the frame, seal the perimeter with foam backer rod where gaps exist, and use exterior-grade screws into the structural framing — not just the trim. On homes where we identify lead-based paint on pre-1978 window trim (common in older Sandersville housing stock), our EPA Lead-Safe certified process governs how we handle and dispose of any disturbed material.

We carry our IICRC FSRT (Fire and Smoke Restoration Technician) certification into every fire board-up job, which means we are reading the structure for secondary hazards — compromised joists, active smoldering in wall cavities — not just covering holes.
