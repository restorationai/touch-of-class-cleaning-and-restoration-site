Pearlington sits at the western edge of Hancock County, pressed up against the Pearl River and the Louisiana state line — a community that knows better than most what it means to rebuild from the ground up. After Hurricane Katrina leveled much of the area, reconstruction here wasn't a single event; it's been an ongoing story of new builds, gut-rehabs, and phased renovations that continue today. When that construction work wraps, what's left behind — drywall dust settled into every horizontal surface, silica particles suspended in the air, adhesive smears on new flooring, caulk haze on windows — requires a different level of cleaning than a standard house cleaning service can deliver.

## Why Pearlington Properties Present Unique Post-Construction Challenges

The Pearl River corridor creates a humidity baseline that most inland Mississippi contractors don't have to account for. During and after construction, that ambient moisture causes drywall compound dust to partially rehydrate and bond to surfaces rather than simply wiping away. Sawdust and joint compound residue that might brush off a surface in a drier climate can instead form a thin, stubborn film on cabinetry, tile grout lines, and window tracks in Pearlington's climate.

Beyond humidity, the local building stock tells its own story. Many homes rebuilt after 2005 used engineered lumber, spray foam insulation, and composite materials that off-gas volatile organic compounds (VOCs) for weeks after installation. A proper final clean here isn't just about visible debris — it includes ventilating and surface-wiping to reduce that off-gassing load before a family moves back in. Older structures that survived the storm and received partial renovation often combine pre-Katrina materials with modern additions, which means a crew needs to recognize what they're cleaning and adjust accordingly.

## Our Post-Construction Cleaning Process in Pearlington

The work happens in a deliberate sequence. We start at the top of each room — scrubbing ceiling fans, light fixtures, and HVAC registers — and work downward so that dislodged dust falls onto surfaces we haven't cleaned yet. That top-down discipline matters especially in Pearlington homes with vaulted ceilings or open-rafter construction, where fine particulate can settle in layers.

Next comes the detail phase: cabinet interiors wiped down with appropriate cleaners matched to the cabinet material, window tracks cleared of compound residue, tile and grout scrubbed free of grout haze, and all hardware polished. We pay particular attention to door frames and baseboards, where painters' tape residue and overspray tend to concentrate. In homes where EPA Lead-Safe protocols apply — common in any structure with pre-1978 components, which is relevant in Pearlington given the mix of older and rebuilt housing — our team follows those procedures as certified under the EPA Renovation, Repair and Painting rule.

The final phase is a floor-by-floor inspection pass: hardwood buffed, tile mopped twice, carpet vacuumed with HEPA filtration, and a walkthrough with the contractor or homeowner to document that the space is ready for occupancy.

## Local Note

One thing that catches out-of-area cleaning crews in Pearlington: the combination of river-adjacent soil and post-storm slab construction means that concrete floors in newer builds often show efflorescence — white mineral deposits that migrate up through the slab — especially in the first year after construction. It looks like construction dust but doesn't respond to standard mopping. We identify it on sight and treat it with an appropriate mild acid wash rather than scrubbing it into the surface and making it worse.
