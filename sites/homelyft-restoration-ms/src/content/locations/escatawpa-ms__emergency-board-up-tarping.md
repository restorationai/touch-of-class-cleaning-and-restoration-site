## Why Escatawpa Properties Are Particularly Vulnerable After Storm or Fire Damage

Escatawpa sits at the intersection of two serious risk factors: proximity to the Gulf of Mexico's storm tracks and the humid subtropical climate that keeps relative humidity above 70% for much of the year. When a window gets blown out or a roof deck is exposed, that warm, wet air moves in fast. Mold colonies can begin establishing on wet wood and drywall within 24 to 48 hours under these conditions — not a week, not a month. A tarp or plywood panel installed the same day isn't just cosmetic; it's the difference between a repair bill and a remediation project.

The housing stock in and around Escatawpa also matters. Many homes in this part of Jackson County were built in the 1960s through 1980s, when construction standards for wind uplift were far less stringent than what Mississippi's current building code requires. Older roof sheathing — particularly the board-sheathing style common before OSB panels became standard — is more susceptible to wind damage along the edges and ridgeline, and it doesn't hold tarp anchors the same way modern decking does. Knowing how to secure a tarp to aging sheathing without creating additional damage is a detail that matters here in ways it simply doesn't in newer subdivisions.

## Our Emergency Board-Up and Tarping Process in Escatawpa

When a call comes in from the Escatawpa area, the first step is a rapid damage assessment — roof, walls, windows, and any structural compromise that could make the property unsafe to work on. Safety clearance comes before any material goes up.

For roof tarping, we use reinforced polyethylene tarps rated for wind loads appropriate to a Gulf Coast exposure zone, anchored with lumber battens screwed into solid framing rather than just stapled at the edges. A tarp that peels off in the next afternoon thunderstorm is worse than no tarp, because it gives a false sense of security while the interior continues to absorb moisture. We size the tarp to overlap the ridge by at least two feet on both sides when ridge damage is involved, and we seal the perimeter to limit wind catch.

For window and door board-up following fire damage or impact, we use 3/4-inch plywood cut to fit the rough opening and secured with exterior-rated screws into the framing — not just the trim. On older homes with non-standard window sizes, which are common in this area, we cut panels on-site rather than bringing pre-cut stock that won't fit. Every opening gets photographed before and after installation as part of the documentation package your insurance adjuster will need.

The team holds IICRC FSRT (Fire & Smoke Restoration) certification and the company carries EPA Lead-Safe certification — relevant on older Escatawpa homes where lead-based paint on window trim is a realistic possibility during board-up work.

## Coordinating with Insurance After Storm or Fire Damage in Jackson County

Most homeowner policies in Mississippi cover emergency protective measures — board-up and tarping — as part of the dwelling coverage, separate from the primary repair claim. The key is documentation: insurers want timestamped photos, a written scope of what was installed and where, and receipts for materials. We produce all of that as a standard part of every job, not as an add-on.

Jackson County properties that carry wind or named-storm riders (common given the area's FEMA flood zone designations) sometimes have specific requirements about who performs emergency mitigation in order for the cost to be reimbursable.
