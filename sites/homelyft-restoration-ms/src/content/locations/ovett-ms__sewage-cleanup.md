## Why Ovett Properties See Sewage Backup More Often Than You'd Expect

The clay-heavy soils common throughout Jones County don't drain well. When heavy rain saturates the ground — and Mississippi's Gulf-influenced weather delivers that regularly from spring through early fall — septic drain fields can become temporarily overwhelmed, pushing effluent back toward the tank and sometimes into the home through floor drains, toilets, or low-lying fixtures. This isn't a freak event; it's a pattern that repeats on properties where drain fields were sized for lighter loads or installed before modern soil-perc standards.

Older homes in the Ovett area also tend to have cast-iron or Orangeburg sewer laterals that have been in the ground for 50 or more years. Orangeburg pipe — a tar-and-paper composite used heavily through the 1970s — deteriorates from the inside out, collapsing in sections and causing sewer line backups that look like a simple clog until a camera inspection reveals the real problem. When that pipe fails, raw sewage doesn't just pool; it wicks into surrounding soil and, if the failure is under or near the home, into the crawl space structure.

## Our Sewage Cleanup and Sanitization Process

Raw sewage is classified as Category 3 water — the most contaminated classification in the IICRC S500 standard — which means the removal and sanitization process follows a stricter protocol than a burst supply line or roof leak.

When we arrive, the first priority is containment: establishing work zones to prevent cross-contamination into unaffected areas of the home. Affected porous materials — insulation, contaminated drywall, saturated subfloor sections — are removed and bagged for proper disposal rather than dried in place, because pathogens embedded in porous material cannot be reliably sanitized without removal. Hard surfaces, framing, and concrete are then treated with EPA-registered disinfectants formulated for Category 3 losses.

Drying follows sanitization, not the other way around. We use commercial-grade desiccant and refrigerant dehumidifiers alongside high-velocity air movers to bring structural moisture readings down to pre-loss levels. Moisture mapping with thermal imaging and pin/pinless meters documents the drying progress so there's a clear record for your insurance adjuster.

## Local Note: Crawl Space Conditions in This Part of Jones County

Homes in and around Ovett frequently have pier-and-beam construction with low crawl spaces — sometimes less than 18 inches of clearance. When a septic overflow or sewer line backup reaches a crawl space in these conditions, standard extraction equipment can't always reach the affected areas without modification. Our crews carry flexible hose extensions and low-profile equipment specifically because this building pattern is common in rural Jones County. We've also found that vapor barriers in these crawl spaces, when contaminated, are often stapled directly to floor joists — removing them without disturbing the structural framing requires deliberate technique, not just pulling and discarding. Sewage backup coverage in Mississippi is frequently a separate endorsement from standard homeowners' policies — many carriers exclude it from the base policy and require a rider that not every homeowner knows they're missing.
