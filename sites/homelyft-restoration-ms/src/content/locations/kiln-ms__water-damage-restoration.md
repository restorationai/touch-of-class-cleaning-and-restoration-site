## Why Kiln Properties See More Water Damage Than Most

Hancock County's geography works against homeowners in ways that aren't obvious until something goes wrong. Kiln and the broader unincorporated stretches along MS-43 and MS-603 receive some of the highest average annual rainfall in Mississippi — often exceeding 65 inches per year — and the soil here is a dense clay-loam mix that drains slowly. When that clay saturates during a multi-day rain event, hydrostatic pressure builds against crawl space walls and slab edges, forcing groundwater through the smallest gaps in a foundation. Pier-and-beam construction, common throughout this part of Hancock County, keeps living spaces elevated but leaves the underfloor cavity exposed to standing water and persistent moisture that wicks upward into joists and subfloor sheathing.

Gulf Coast tropical systems add another layer of risk. Even a weakened tropical storm making landfall near the Pearl River Delta can push 10 to 15 inches of rain into Kiln over 48 hours, overwhelming gutters, French drains, and sump systems that were sized for normal rainfall. Older homes — many built in the 1970s and 1980s with galvanized supply lines that have long since begun to corrode — face an elevated risk of sudden pipe failure during or just after a storm event, when pressure fluctuations stress aging plumbing.

## Our Water Damage Restoration Process in Kiln

Every job starts with a thorough moisture assessment using thermal imaging cameras and calibrated moisture meters. This step matters more in Kiln than in drier climates because elevated ambient humidity — regularly above 80% in summer — masks how far water has actually traveled. A wet wall cavity can read dry on the surface while the interior framing holds moisture well above the threshold for mold growth.

Once we've mapped the affected area, we begin water extraction using truck-mounted and portable extraction units capable of pulling hundreds of gallons from carpet, subfloor, and crawl spaces in a single session. Structural drying follows immediately: we place commercial-grade desiccant or refrigerant dehumidifiers and high-velocity air movers in a calculated configuration based on the room volume, material types, and the IICRC S500 standard for drying. In pier-and-beam homes, we deploy equipment into the crawl space as well, because drying only the living space while leaving standing water or saturated soil beneath the floor will cause the floor assembly to re-absorb moisture within days.

We monitor drying progress on every visit with the same instruments used at intake, documenting readings so that the drying log is available for your insurance adjuster. Work is not closed out until readings return to pre-loss baselines — not just "close enough."

One thing that catches homeowners off guard in this part of Mississippi: even after the visible water is gone and the floors feel dry underfoot, crawl spaces in Kiln-area homes can hold relative humidity above 90% for weeks following a water intrusion event.
