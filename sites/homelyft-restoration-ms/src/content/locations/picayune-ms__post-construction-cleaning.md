## Why Picayune Properties Present Unique Post-Construction Cleaning Challenges

Picayune sits in one of the most humidity-saturated corridors in Mississippi. Average relative humidity hovers above 70% for much of the year, and that moisture doesn't just make the summers uncomfortable — it accelerates the way construction residue behaves indoors. Drywall dust left on surfaces absorbs ambient moisture and hardens into a paste that bonds to flooring and cabinetry. Caulk and adhesive overspray becomes tacky and picks up airborne debris before it can be wiped away cleanly.

## Our Post-Construction Cleaning Process in Picayune

Post-construction cleaning is a sequenced process, not a single pass with a mop. The order matters because disturbing one layer of debris before the one above it is removed just redistributes the problem.

The process begins with rough-out cleaning: removing bulk debris, cardboard, packaging material, scrap lumber, and anything the construction crew left behind. From there, the work moves to detail cleaning — wiping down every surface from ceiling to floor in a deliberate top-down sequence so that loosened dust falls onto surfaces that haven't been cleaned yet. Window tracks, door hardware, light fixtures, cabinet interiors, and register covers all get individual attention.

For specialty cleaning needs — whether that's removing paint overspray from hardwood floors, cleaning tile grout haze left by installers, or degreasing kitchen surfaces before appliance installation — the methods shift to match the material. Grout haze on ceramic or porcelain tile, for example, requires a pH-appropriate cleaner that won't etch the tile surface; the wrong product can permanently dull the finish. Our IICRC-certified team brings the technical knowledge to match the cleaning agent to the substrate.

The final phase is a detailed inspection pass and air quality check. In Picayune's climate, fine particulate matter can remain suspended in indoor air long after surfaces look clean. We address this with HEPA air filtration and, where the scope calls for it, negative air pressure to capture airborne debris before it resettles.

## Local Note: What Picayune Contractors Often Miss at Final Clean

One pattern that comes up repeatedly on Picayune job sites is HVAC contamination that gets overlooked during the final clean. In Pearl River County's climate, HVAC systems often run during construction to manage humidity — which means the air handler has been pulling construction dust through the system for weeks or months. Registers get wiped, but the ductwork itself and the filter housing accumulate a layer of fine particulate that gets pushed back into the living space the moment the system cycles on after move-in. A thorough post-construction clean in Picayune should always include a duct inspection and, where needed, duct cleaning or at minimum filter replacement and register cleaning with the system off.
