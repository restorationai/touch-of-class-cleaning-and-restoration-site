## Why Big Point Properties See Water Damage Issues

Jackson County's coastal geography is the starting point. Big Point and the surrounding communities along the Back Bay of Biloxi corridor experience some of the highest relative humidity readings in Mississippi — routinely above 80% from May through October. That ambient moisture means building materials in this area never fully dry between rain events the way they might in a drier inland climate. Pier-and-beam homes, which are still common throughout the rural stretches of Jackson County, are especially vulnerable: the crawl space beneath the floor system traps ground moisture, and a single plumbing failure can saturate floor joists before a homeowner realizes anything is wrong.

Older construction in the area also tends to use tongue-and-groove hardwood subfloors rather than modern OSB panels. Hardwood absorbs water more slowly than OSB but holds it longer and is far more prone to cupping and crowning if drying is rushed. Pushing too much airflow too fast through hardwood subfloor assemblies can lock in a moisture gradient that causes permanent warping — a mistake that costs more to fix than the original water event.

## Our Water Damage Restoration Process in Big Point

Every job starts with a thorough moisture assessment using thermal imaging cameras and calibrated moisture meters.
