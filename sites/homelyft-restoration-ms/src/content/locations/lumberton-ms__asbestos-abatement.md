## Why Lumberton Properties See Asbestos Concerns

Lamar County's residential building boom ran hard through the 1950s, 1960s, and into the mid-1970s, which means a significant portion of Lumberton's single-family homes were constructed during the era when asbestos was standard in joint compound, floor adhesives, ceiling texture, duct insulation, and roofing felt. The region's heat and humidity accelerate the natural aging of these materials — what the industry calls "friable" asbestos, where fibers become airborne with minimal disturbance. A popcorn ceiling that looked fine last summer may have begun to crack and powder after another cycle of summer heat and winter cool-down, making asbestos testing a practical necessity before any ceiling scraping or remodel work.

Lumberton's older commercial buildings carry similar risk. Pipe wrap and boiler insulation in structures built before the EPA's 1989 partial asbestos ban are common finds during gut renovations. Mississippi's regulatory framework requires that any renovation or demolition project disturbing a threshold amount of asbestos-containing material follow NESHAP notification procedures with the Mississippi Department of Environmental Quality — a step that catches many property owners off guard when they're simply trying to update a bathroom or replace flooring.

## Our Asbestos Abatement Process in Lumberton

Every project starts with a thorough inspection and bulk sampling of suspect materials — popcorn ceilings, vinyl floor tiles, pipe insulation, attic vermiculite, and drywall joint compound are the most common culprits in Lumberton-area homes. Samples are sent to an accredited laboratory, and we walk you through the results before any abatement plan is written.

Once testing confirms asbestos-containing material, the work area is isolated using negative air pressure containment with HEPA-filtered air scrubbers. Regulated waste is wetted to suppress fiber release, double-bagged in labeled 6-mil poly, and transported to an approved disposal facility — Mississippi requires manifested disposal, and we handle that paperwork on your behalf. After abatement, air clearance sampling confirms fiber levels are below acceptable thresholds before containment is removed and the space is released for reconstruction. The process is methodical because the alternative — cutting corners on containment or skipping post-clearance testing — creates liability that follows a property through every future sale.

Because we hold EPA Lead-Safe Certification and are an IICRC Certified Firm, we're equipped to manage projects where asbestos and lead paint coexist — a common scenario in Lumberton homes built before 1978.
