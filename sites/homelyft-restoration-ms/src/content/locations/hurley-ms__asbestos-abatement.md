## Why Hurley Properties Are Prone to Asbestos Concerns

Jackson County's residential growth in the mid-twentieth century tracked closely with the broader Gulf Coast building boom, and Hurley was no exception. Homes constructed between roughly 1945 and 1980 in this area commonly incorporated asbestos in places that are easy to overlook: the 9×9 vinyl floor tiles in kitchens and utility rooms, the gray pipe wrap around HVAC ducts in crawl spaces, the spray-applied "popcorn" texture on bedroom and living room ceilings, and the joint compound used throughout interior walls.

The region's heat and humidity compound the risk. Seasonal moisture cycling — wet, swampy summers followed by drier winters — causes building materials to expand and contract repeatedly over decades. That movement can crack and friable asbestos-containing materials that were once stable, releasing fibers into the air inside the home. Crawl spaces in this part of Jackson County are especially worth scrutinizing: they tend to stay damp, which degrades pipe insulation and duct wrap faster than in drier climates.

If you're planning a kitchen remodel, bathroom update, or any work that touches flooring, ceilings, or ductwork in a home built before 1980, an asbestos inspection before demolition is not optional — it's the step that keeps your contractor, your family, and your neighbors safe.

## Our Asbestos Abatement Process in Hurley

Every abatement project starts with asbestos testing. We collect bulk samples from suspect materials — floor tiles, ceiling texture, pipe insulation, roofing felt — and submit them to an accredited laboratory. Results typically return within a few business days, and we walk you through what was found and what it means before any removal work begins.

When abatement is confirmed necessary, our crew establishes a regulated work area: plastic sheeting seals off the affected zone, negative air pressure units with HEPA filtration run continuously to prevent fiber migration into the rest of the structure, and all workers wear full personal protective equipment. Asbestos-containing materials are wetted to suppress fiber release during removal, then double-bagged in approved waste containers and transported to a licensed disposal facility — Mississippi Department of Environmental Quality (MDEQ) regulations govern the entire chain of custody.

After removal, the area undergoes a thorough HEPA vacuuming and wipe-down. Air clearance sampling confirms fiber counts are below regulatory thresholds before containment is removed and the space is released for reconstruction. The process is methodical by design — cutting corners on any step creates liability for the homeowner and real health risk for anyone in the building.

One pattern we see consistently in older Hurley-area homes is deteriorating pipe insulation in crawl spaces that was never flagged during previous home inspections — because many home inspectors are not licensed asbestos inspectors and are not required to identify it.
