## Why Wade Properties See Sewage Backup Issues

Many homes in and around Wade were built during the mid-twentieth century, when septic systems were sized for smaller households and plumbing vents were routed in ways that don't always meet today's load demands. Decades of root intrusion from the mature pine and hardwood trees common to this part of Forrest County gradually constrict lateral lines. Add a heavy rain event — and this region sees its share of them, especially during Gulf-driven storm systems that stall over south-central Mississippi — and the combination of saturated soil and overwhelmed drain fields can push raw sewage back through floor drains, toilets, and washing machine standpipes with very little warning.

Homes on private septic in rural Wade face a different but equally serious pattern: when a tank hasn't been pumped on schedule or the drain field is compromised, a single heavy-use day can trigger an overflow that spreads effluent across a yard, into a crawl space, or along a foundation perimeter. Either scenario — municipal sewer backup or septic overflow — classifies as Category 3 water loss under the IICRC S500 standard, meaning the contamination level requires full personal protective equipment, antimicrobial treatment, and in many cases, removal of porous materials that absorbed the waste.

## Our Sewage Cleanup and Sanitization Process in Wade

The first thing our crew does on arrival is establish containment. Sewage contamination spreads through foot traffic, HVAC airflow, and capillary action faster than most people expect, so we isolate the affected zone before any extraction equipment rolls in. From there, the process moves through several deliberate stages:

**Extraction and removal.** Standing sewage and saturated materials are removed using truck-mounted extraction units. Flooring, insulation, and drywall that have absorbed Category 3 contamination are bagged and disposed of per Mississippi DEQ guidelines — these materials cannot be dried in place.

**Antimicrobial application.** After extraction, affected surfaces are treated with EPA-registered disinfectants formulated for sewage pathogens, including bacteria, viruses, and fungi. We apply these in multiple passes, allowing appropriate dwell time rather than rushing through a single spray.

**Structural drying.** Once contaminated materials are out and surfaces are treated, commercial desiccant dehumidifiers and air movers are positioned to drive moisture out of subfloor assemblies and wall cavities. In Wade's humid summers, ambient moisture can slow drying significantly — our equipment is sized for those conditions, not for a climate-controlled lab environment.

**Post-remediation verification.** Before we close out, we document moisture readings and surface conditions. Our team holds IICRC AMRT and WRT credentials, so the drying protocol isn't guesswork — it follows a measurable standard.
