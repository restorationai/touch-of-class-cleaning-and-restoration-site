## Why Gulf Hills Properties Present Unique Post-Construction Cleanup Challenges

The Gulf Coast's subtropical climate creates a tight window between project completion and the moment ambient humidity starts affecting unprotected surfaces. In Gulf Hills, where many homes are situated on wooded lots with limited airflow around the structure, fine construction dust — drywall particulate, wood fiber, insulation fragments — settles into HVAC returns, window tracks, and subflooring gaps faster than in drier inland climates. That dust isn't just cosmetic. Left in ductwork, it circulates through the home every time the air handler runs.

Gulf Hills also sits in a coastal zone where post-storm renovation activity spikes after hurricane seasons. Homes rebuilt or significantly repaired under Mississippi's FORTIFIED building standards — which HomeLyft's team is certified to work alongside — often involve new roofing systems, reinforced sheathing, and upgraded windows. Each of those trades leaves its own debris signature: roofing granules tracked through interior spaces, sealant residue on glass, silica dust from masonry work. A standard janitorial crew isn't equipped to address that combination systematically.

## Our Post-Construction Cleaning Process in Gulf Hills

The work happens in phases, and the sequence matters. Rough cleaning comes first: bulk debris removal, disposal of scrap materials, and vacuuming of all horizontal surfaces with HEPA-filter equipment before any wet cleaning begins. Applying water or cleaning solutions to surfaces still coated in fine particulate just turns dust into a paste that's harder to remove.

Once surfaces are dry-cleaned, the detail phase addresses what rough cleaning can't reach — paint overspray on hardware, caulk smears on tile grout lines, adhesive residue on flooring, construction film on windows and mirrors. For Gulf Hills homes with new hardwood or LVP installations, we use pH-neutral solutions appropriate to the finish manufacturer's specs rather than general-purpose degreasers that can dull or streak the surface.

Final clean — the phase that happens just before the homeowner walks through — covers light fixtures, cabinet interiors, appliance surfaces, and a full floor-to-ceiling wipe-down of every room. We document the condition with photographs before and after, which matters if a contractor's punch list is still open or if there's a dispute about what the build team left behind.

## Equipment and Methods Calibrated to Coastal Conditions

Standard shop vacuums recirculate fine particles back into the air. On Gulf Hills job sites, we run commercial HEPA-rated vacuums that capture particles down to 0.3 microns — relevant when drywall dust and insulation fibers are present, particularly in homes where the HVAC system was running during construction. For window cleaning on coastal properties, we account for salt-air film that accumulates on exterior glass even during a short build timeline; standard glass cleaner alone doesn't cut through that layer.

Where a renovation involved older materials — and Gulf Hills has a mix of mid-century and newer construction — we follow EPA Lead-Safe protocols during cleanup if there's any possibility of disturbed lead-based paint. HomeLyft holds EPA Lead-Safe Certification, so that determination and the corresponding work practices are built into our process rather than treated as an afterthought.
