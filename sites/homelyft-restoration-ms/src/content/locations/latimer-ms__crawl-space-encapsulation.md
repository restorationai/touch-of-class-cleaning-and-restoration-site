Latimer sits in Harrison County where the Gulf Coast's subtropical humidity doesn't just linger in the summer air — it works its way into the ground beneath your home year-round. Crawl spaces here face a particular combination of warm, moisture-laden soil, seasonal heavy rainfall, and the kind of clay-heavy earth that holds water long after a storm passes. That persistent ground moisture is what turns an unprotected crawl space into a slow-moving problem: wood rot, elevated indoor humidity, mold colonies forming on floor joists, and energy bills that creep upward as conditioned air fights a losing battle against a damp subfloor.

## Why Latimer Properties Are Prone to Crawl Space Moisture Problems

Harrison County's climate is not forgiving to open crawl spaces. Average annual rainfall along this stretch of the Mississippi Gulf Coast exceeds 60 inches, and much of it falls in intense bursts that saturate the soil quickly. Clay-dominant soil common to this area doesn't drain efficiently — water pools at grade and then wicks upward through the ground into the crawl space environment beneath your living space.

Many homes in the Latimer area were built during periods when vapor barriers were either thin poly sheeting or absent entirely. Over time, that original barrier — if there was one — degrades, tears, or shifts, leaving bare soil exposed to the crawl space air. Once relative humidity in a crawl space climbs above 60 percent consistently, wood-decaying fungi and mold have everything they need to establish. Insulation batts absorb moisture, lose their R-value, and eventually fall from between joists. HVAC ducts routed through the crawl space sweat in summer, adding condensation to an already damp environment.

## Our Crawl Space Encapsulation Process in Latimer

Encapsulation is not simply laying plastic sheeting on the ground. The process we follow is calibrated to the conditions we actually find under Harrison County homes.

We begin with a thorough inspection — measuring moisture content in the structural wood, checking for standing water or evidence of prior flooding, and assessing whether existing vapor barriers have failed. Any standing water is extracted and the space is dried before encapsulation materials go in. If mold is present on joists or the subfloor, remediation happens first; our team holds IICRC AMRT certification, so mold work and encapsulation can be coordinated under one project rather than handed off between contractors.

Once the space is dry and clean, we install a heavy-mil reinforced polyethylene barrier — typically 20-mil — across the entire crawl space floor and up the foundation walls, sealed at all seams and penetrations with moisture-resistant tape. Piers and columns are wrapped individually. Crawl space vents, which actually allow humid Gulf Coast air to enter rather than ventilate the space, are sealed and conditioned air is introduced to maintain a controlled environment. A crawl space dehumidifier sized to the square footage is installed and connected to a drain line so it operates without manual emptying.

## Equipment and Methods We Use for Crawl Space Encapsulation

The materials matter as much as the method. Thin 6-mil poly sheeting, common in older installations, punctures easily during routine crawl space access and degrades within a few years under Gulf Coast conditions. We use reinforced 20-mil barrier material rated for long-term ground contact. Seams are double-taped with butyl-based tape that maintains adhesion in high-humidity environments.

For humidity control, we install commercial-grade dehumidifiers with built-in humidistats — not consumer units from a hardware store. These are sized by the cubic footage of the crawl space and the moisture load we measure during inspection. In Latimer's climate, undersizing a dehumidifier is one of the most common reasons encapsulation projects underperform within the first year.

All work is documented with before-and-after photos and moisture readings, which serves both your records and any insurance or real estate disclosure needs down the line.

## Local Note

One pattern we see consistently in Harrison County crawl spaces: homes built on sloped lots where one side of the foundation is partially below grade tend to accumulate water at that low corner even when the rest of the crawl space is relatively dry. The clay soil channels water toward the lowest point of the foundation perimeter. In these situations, encapsulation alone isn't always sufficient — a French drain or interior perimeter drain channel needs to be installed first to redirect water before the barrier goes down. Skipping that step and encapsulating over a wet corner is a mistake that shows up within one rainy season. We assess drainage conditions before any material goes in, not after.
