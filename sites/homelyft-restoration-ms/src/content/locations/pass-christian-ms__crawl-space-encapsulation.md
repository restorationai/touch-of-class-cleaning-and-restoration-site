## Why Pass Christian Homes Are Particularly Vulnerable

Harrison County's coastal climate is unforgiving to building assemblies that weren't designed with today's humidity science in mind. Pass Christian averages relative humidity levels that regularly exceed 70 percent through the spring and summer months, and the sandy-loam soils common along this stretch of the coast drain slowly after heavy Gulf rain events. Water doesn't disappear — it migrates upward through evaporation, and an open or inadequately vented crawl space draws that moisture directly into floor joists, subfloor sheathing, and insulation batts.

Many homes in Pass Christian were built before modern building science recognized vapor intrusion as a structural threat. Older pier-and-beam homes — common throughout the city — often have nothing between the ground and the floor system except fiberglass batt insulation that sags, traps moisture, and loses its R-value within a few seasons. After Hurricane Katrina, a significant portion of the housing stock was rebuilt or substantially repaired, but even post-2005 construction can develop crawl space moisture problems when Gulf weather patterns shift.

## Our Crawl Space Encapsulation Process in Pass Christian

Encapsulation here isn't a one-size product — it's a sequenced process calibrated to what the crawl space actually contains when we open the access hatch.

The first step is a thorough inspection: we document standing water, active mold colonies, deteriorated insulation, compromised joists, and any signs of pest activity before a single roll of liner is placed. If mold is present, remediation comes first — encapsulating over active growth seals the problem in rather than solving it, and our IICRC-certified mold remediation team (AMRT credential) handles that phase before the encapsulation crew moves in.

Once the space is clean and dry, we install a heavy-mil reinforced polyethylene vapor barrier across the entire ground surface and up the foundation walls, sealed at seams and penetrations with specialty tape rated for below-grade environments. Depending on the crawl space's configuration and the home's HVAC setup, we may also seal foundation vents and introduce a dedicated dehumidifier sized to the cubic footage of the space — a step that's particularly important in Pass Christian's climate, where passive ventilation strategies that work in drier climates simply push humid outside air into the crawl space rather than exhausting moisture out.

Final readings with a calibrated moisture meter confirm that wood moisture content in the floor system is trending toward safe levels before we close out the job.
