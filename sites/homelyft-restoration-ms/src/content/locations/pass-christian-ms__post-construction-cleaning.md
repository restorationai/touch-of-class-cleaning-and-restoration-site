## Our Post-Construction and Specialty Cleaning Process in Pass Christian

Every final clean starts with a walk-through to document what trades have finished and what surfaces are still curing or off-limits. From there, the process moves in a deliberate top-to-bottom sequence:

**Rough debris removal** — Construction scraps, packaging, and staging materials are sorted and hauled. Anything that touched lead-painted surfaces is bagged and disposed of under EPA Lead-Safe protocols.

**HEPA vacuuming and air scrubbing** — Fine construction dust is the invisible problem on every job. Industrial HEPA vacuums capture particles down to 0.3 microns, and where dust has migrated into HVAC returns — common in Gulf Coast homes where systems run almost year-round — we vacuum registers and recommend filter replacement before the system is turned back on.

**Surface detailing** — Windows, sills, cabinetry interiors, light fixtures, and hardware all get hand-wiped. Coastal salt residue on exterior-facing glass requires a different cleaning solution than standard construction dust, and skipping that step leaves a haze that shows up the first time afternoon sun hits the panes.

**Floor protection removal and floor finishing clean** — Protective paper and plastic come up carefully to avoid scratching new flooring. Hard surfaces get a final damp mop; any new grout joints are inspected for haze and treated with appropriate grout-haze remover rather than general-purpose cleaners that can etch tile.

**Final inspection and photo documentation** — The site is photographed room by room before the crew leaves, giving contractors, homeowners, and property managers a timestamped record of the completed clean.

## Local Note: Humidity Timing Matters for Interior Detailing

One thing that catches out-of-area cleaning crews in Pass Christian is the dew point. On humid mornings — which describes most of the year here — opening windows and doors to air out a freshly cleaned space can actually pull moisture back into surfaces that were just dried and detailed. The HomeLyft crew schedules interior detailing and final wipe-downs for mid-morning to early afternoon when possible, after ambient humidity has dropped from its overnight peak, and uses dehumidification equipment on-site when the project timeline doesn't allow for waiting on the weather. It's a small operational detail that makes a real difference in whether a new hardwood floor stays haze-free or blooms with a faint film two days after the clean.
