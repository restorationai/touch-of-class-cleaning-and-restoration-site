## Why Lucedale Properties See Elevated Storm Damage Risk

George County's housing stock skews older, with a significant share of homes built before modern wind-load requirements were codified in Mississippi's residential building code. Pier-and-beam foundations — common in rural Lucedale — can shift when saturated soil moves beneath them, meaning a single severe storm can cause structural settling on top of roof and siding damage. The area also sits within a recognized tornado risk zone; the National Weather Service regularly issues tornado watches for George and surrounding counties during spring squall lines and late-season Gulf systems.

Roofing materials matter here, too. Older three-tab shingles — still prevalent on homes built in the 1970s and 1980s throughout the county — begin lifting at sustained winds well below what a modern architectural shingle can handle. Once a shingle field is compromised, interior water intrusion starts within minutes, not hours. In Mississippi's average summer humidity, wet insulation and wet OSB sheathing can begin supporting mold colonization in as little as 48 to 72 hours.
