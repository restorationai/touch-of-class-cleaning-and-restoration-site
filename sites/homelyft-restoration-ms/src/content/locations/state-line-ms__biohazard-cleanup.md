## Why State Line Properties Present Unique Biohazard Considerations

The Greene and Marion County corridor that State Line straddles has a housing stock that skews older, with a meaningful number of homes built in the 1960s through 1980s on pier-and-beam or slab foundations. That construction style matters in biohazard work: pier-and-beam floors can allow fluids to migrate beneath subfloor sheathing and into the crawl space before anyone realizes the extent of the contamination. Crawl spaces in this part of Mississippi also tend to retain humidity year-round — the region's long warm season and frequent summer rainfall push ambient moisture levels high, which accelerates the growth of secondary biological hazards if contaminated material is not removed and treated promptly. A situation that might be contained to a single room in a drier climate can involve the substructure within 24 to 48 hours here.

Properties that have been unoccupied — a common situation in rural Greene and Marion County — present an additional layer of complexity. Vacant homes attract unauthorized occupants, and with that comes the possibility of discarded sharps, drug paraphernalia, and other infectious material scattered across multiple rooms or outbuildings. Our OSHA-trained team treats the full structure, not just the visible surface.

## Our Biohazard Cleanup Process in State Line

Every job begins with a contained assessment — our technicians arrive in unmarked vehicles, in plain protective gear that does not draw attention from neighbors, and conduct a thorough evaluation before any remediation begins. We identify all affected surfaces, materials, and structural cavities, then establish containment boundaries to prevent cross-contamination during the work.

Remediation follows a strict sequence: removal of non-salvageable porous materials (flooring, insulation, soft goods), treatment of hard surfaces with EPA-registered disinfectants, and verification testing to confirm pathogen levels meet regulatory clearance thresholds. All biohazardous waste is packaged and transported in compliance with Mississippi State Department of Health regulations and federal DOT standards for infectious material — this is not curbside disposal, and the documentation trail matters for your insurance claim and your peace of mind.

Because our team holds IICRC certification and EPA Lead-Safe credentials, we can also address the complicating factors common in older State Line homes: lead-based paint disturbed during material removal is handled under a separate protocol that keeps the job legally clean and protects your family.
