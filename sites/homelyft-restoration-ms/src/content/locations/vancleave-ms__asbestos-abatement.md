## Why Vancleave Properties See Asbestos Risks

Jackson County's residential building boom ran hard through the 1950s, 1960s, and 1970s, and Vancleave was no exception. Homes built during that window routinely used asbestos in floor tiles, pipe insulation, attic insulation, roofing felt, joint compound, and the spray-applied ceiling texture that was nearly universal in that era. The coastal humidity here doesn't just feel heavy — it works on building materials constantly, causing expansion and contraction cycles that can crack and friable older tile or ceiling texture over time, releasing fibers into living spaces.

Unlike urban markets where older housing stock gets flipped and renovated quickly, many Vancleave properties have been in families for decades and haven't had a professional inspection. That's not a criticism — it's simply the pattern in rural Jackson County, and it means asbestos-containing materials are often still in place, undisturbed, until a renovation or storm damage event brings them into play. Mississippi's Gulf Coast also sees its share of hurricane and tropical storm damage, and storm repairs that disturb pre-1980 materials without prior testing can create an exposure risk that's entirely avoidable.

## Our Asbestos Abatement Process in Vancleave

Every project starts with a thorough asbestos inspection and bulk sample collection. Samples are sent to an accredited laboratory — results typically come back within a few business days — and we review findings with you before any abatement work begins. If asbestos-containing materials are confirmed, we develop a scope of work that follows Mississippi DEQ notification requirements and EPA NESHAP standards, which apply to renovation and demolition projects of a certain scale.

On-site, the affected area is sealed with negative air pressure containment using 6-mil polyethylene sheeting and HEPA-filtered air scrubbers. Workers use full respirator protection and disposable coveralls. Asbestos-containing materials are wetted to suppress fiber release, carefully removed, double-bagged in labeled 6-mil poly bags, and transported to a licensed disposal facility. After removal, a final visual inspection is conducted and air clearance samples are collected to confirm the space is safe before containment comes down. Nothing is rushed — the process is methodical because the margin for error is zero.

## What's Different About Asbestos Work in Vancleave

The climate factor matters more here than in many parts of Mississippi. Vancleave's year-round humidity means that older floor tiles and pipe wrap are often in a more advanced state of degradation than equivalent materials in drier climates. Friable asbestos — material that crumbles easily by hand pressure — requires more aggressive containment protocols than intact, non-friable materials, and our inspectors assess friability on-site rather than assuming based on material type alone.

Jackson County also has a significant number of properties on private well water and septic systems, which means decontamination water from abatement work requires careful management so it doesn't enter the drain field. We account for that in our site planning, which is a detail that matters in rural Vancleave in a way it simply doesn't in a city with municipal sewer.
