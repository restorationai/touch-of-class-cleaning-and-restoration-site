Contents restoration and storage is the part of disaster recovery most homeowners don't think about until they're standing in a waterlogged living room watching a grandmother's armchair absorb what used to be clean water. Getting those belongings out of the damage zone quickly, catalogued properly, and restored or stored safely is often the difference between a full recovery and a total loss.

## Why Hurley Properties See Contents Damage Differently Than Other Areas

Jackson County's climate doesn't give contents much grace time. Average relative humidity in this part of Mississippi hovers above 70 percent for most of the year, which means wet upholstery, wood furniture, and paper goods begin to show mold colonization within 24 to 48 hours of a water event — faster than in drier inland climates. That compressed window makes the pack-out step urgent, not optional.

Hurley's housing stock skews toward older ranch-style and pier-and-beam construction, many built before modern vapor barriers were standard. When water intrudes — whether from a plumbing failure, a roof leak, or storm surge creeping up from low-lying lots near the Escatawpa River corridor — it tends to wick into wood subflooring and wall cavities and migrate to contents stored low to the ground. Books, photo albums, and the kinds of keepsakes families keep in bottom cabinets or on lower shelves are often the first casualties. We account for that when we assess a home and prioritize what gets packed out first.

Fire and smoke events add another layer. Older homes with wood-framed interiors and minimal insulation allow smoke to penetrate deeply into soft goods — curtains, mattresses, upholstered furniture — and the humid air that follows a fire suppression effort accelerates secondary damage. Soot that sits on a fabric surface for more than a few hours begins to set chemically, making restoration harder and sometimes impossible.

## Our Contents Restoration and Storage Process in Hurley

When we arrive at a Hurley property, the first step is a room-by-room contents inventory before anything is moved. Every item is photographed, logged by category and condition, and assigned a restoration priority. This documentation serves two purposes: it gives your insurance adjuster a complete loss record, and it gives you a clear picture of what can be restored versus what needs to be claimed as a replacement.

After inventory, affected contents are carefully packed out and transported to our secure climate-controlled storage facility. In Hurley's humidity, storing damaged goods in a standard warehouse or garage would continue the damage — temperature and humidity control during storage is not a luxury, it's a requirement for successful restoration.

Cleaning and restoration methods are matched to the material. Hard surfaces — furniture frames, lamps, ceramics, electronics — go through ultrasonic or hand-cleaning processes depending on the type of residue. Soft goods — upholstery, rugs, clothing, linens — are evaluated for ozone treatment, thermal fogging, or professional laundering. Documents and photographs receive specialized freeze-drying or air-drying treatment when water damage is involved. Items that cannot be restored are documented for your insurance claim rather than quietly discarded.

Once your home is cleared for re-entry and the structure has been dried and repaired, contents are returned, placed, and checked against the original inventory.
