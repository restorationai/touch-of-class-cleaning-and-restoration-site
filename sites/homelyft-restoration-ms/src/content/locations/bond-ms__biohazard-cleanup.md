## Why Bond Properties Present Unique Biohazard Considerations

Marion County's housing stock includes a substantial number of older rural homes — many built before modern subfloor and ventilation standards — where porous materials like untreated wood, aged concrete, and plaster-composite walls absorb biological contaminants more deeply than newer construction. Unlike urban areas with standardized slab foundations, many Bond-area properties sit on pier-and-beam frames, which means fluids can migrate beneath the floor deck and into crawl spaces before anyone realizes the extent of the contamination. Mississippi's humid subtropical climate accelerates that spread: in summer conditions, biological material begins to break down and create secondary hazards within hours, not days.
