## Why Long Beach Properties Face Elevated Asbestos Risk

Mississippi's Gulf Coast saw significant residential and commercial construction between the 1940s and late 1970s — the exact window when asbestos was most heavily used in building products. Long Beach properties from that era commonly contain asbestos in floor tiles, pipe insulation, roofing felts, duct wrap, joint compound, and the textured "popcorn" ceilings that were standard in homes built before the EPA's 1978 guidance. The region's high humidity and the storm damage cycles that have repeatedly stressed older structures — cracking slab foundations, warping subflooring, loosening ceiling texture — create conditions where previously stable, non-friable asbestos can become friable and airborne.

Post-storm repair work is a particular trigger. Homeowners who took on repairs themselves after hurricane seasons sometimes unknowingly sanded, drilled, or demolished materials that contained asbestos. If your Long Beach property has had patchwork renovations over the decades without a formal inspection, there's a meaningful chance undocumented asbestos-containing materials are still present.

## Our Asbestos Abatement Process in Long Beach

Every project begins with a formal asbestos inspection and bulk sampling — we collect material samples from suspect areas and send them to an accredited laboratory before any removal begins. This isn't optional under Mississippi Department of Environmental Quality (MDEQ) regulations, and skipping it exposes property owners to significant liability.

Once lab results confirm the presence and type of asbestos, our EPA Lead-Safe Certified and OSHA-trained team establishes a regulated work area: negative air pressure containment using poly sheeting, HEPA air filtration units running continuously, and full PPE for all personnel on site. Wet methods are used throughout removal to suppress fiber release. Waste is double-bagged in labeled, sealed containers and transported to a licensed disposal facility — Mississippi requires asbestos waste to go to approved Class I or Class II landfills, and we handle all manifesting and documentation.

After removal, air clearance testing is conducted before containment is broken. You receive a full written report documenting the scope of work, lab results, disposal manifests, and clearance data — the paperwork your contractor, insurer, or local building department will ask for.
