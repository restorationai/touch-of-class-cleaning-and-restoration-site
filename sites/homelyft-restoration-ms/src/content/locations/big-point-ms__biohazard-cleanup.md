## Why Big Point Properties Present Unique Biohazard Challenges

Jackson County's coastal plain climate is not forgiving to property owners dealing with a biohazard event. Relative humidity in this region regularly exceeds 80 percent through much of the year, which means porous materials — wood subfloors, drywall, upholstered surfaces — can absorb and retain infectious material faster than in drier climates. Many homes in the Big Point area were built in the mid-twentieth century, when construction relied heavily on tongue-and-groove pine flooring and plaster-over-wood-lath walls. Both materials are highly absorbent and require more aggressive treatment protocols than modern concrete board or vinyl plank.

Septic systems are also common in this rural stretch of Jackson County, and a biohazard event that involves plumbing or sewage overlap adds a secondary layer of contamination that demands careful sequencing — infectious material must be addressed before any water intrusion work begins, not alongside it.
