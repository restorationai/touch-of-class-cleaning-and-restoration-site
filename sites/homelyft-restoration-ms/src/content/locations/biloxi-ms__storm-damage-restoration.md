## Why Biloxi Properties Are Especially Vulnerable to Storm Damage

The geography alone creates risk that most homeowners don't fully appreciate until they're standing in a wet living room. Biloxi's low elevation and proximity to open water means storm surge can push water inland even during storms that don't make direct landfall. Wind speeds along the beachfront corridor routinely exceed those recorded just a few miles north, and the salt-laden air that follows a storm accelerates corrosion on exposed metal roofing components, gutters, and HVAC equipment.

Biloxi's housing stock adds another layer of complexity. Many older homes along the Coast were built before modern wind-load requirements were codified — requirements that were significantly strengthened after Hurricane Katrina reshaped the regulatory landscape in Mississippi. These older structures often have roof decking attached with smooth-shank nails rather than ring-shank fasteners, making them more susceptible to sheathing loss during high-wind events. When the roof deck goes, interior water intrusion follows within minutes, not hours.

Mississippi's climate also means that the window between a storm passing and mold beginning to colonize wet materials is short — in Biloxi's summer humidity, 24 to 48 hours is a realistic threshold for mold growth to begin on saturated drywall and insulation. Speed of response and speed of drying are not just selling points; they are the difference between a restoration job and a gut-and-rebuild.

## Our Storm Damage Restoration Process in Biloxi

Every storm damage response starts with a thorough damage assessment before any equipment is staged. In Biloxi, that assessment has to account for both wind damage and moisture intrusion simultaneously — a tree through a roof is obvious, but the water that has been wicking into wall cavities for six hours since the impact is what drives long-term damage costs.

Our process moves through these phases:

**Structural stabilization and tarping.** Open roof sections and broken windows are covered first to stop ongoing water entry. We document all damage photographically before tarping, which matters when your insurance adjuster reviews the claim.

**Water extraction and moisture mapping.** Standing water is extracted, then thermal imaging and moisture meters map how far water has traveled through floors, walls, and ceiling assemblies. In Biloxi's older pier-and-beam homes, water under the subfloor is a common hidden finding.

**Structural drying.** Industrial air movers and desiccant or refrigerant dehumidifiers run until moisture readings return to pre-loss baselines. Drying is documented daily — a requirement of the IICRC S500 standard and something most insurance carriers expect to see in the claim file.

**Debris removal and reconstruction coordination.** Tree limbs, damaged roofing materials, and compromised structural members are removed and the site is prepped for repairs. As an IICRC Certified Firm with FORTIFIED Building Roofing and MS Windstorm certification, we understand what rebuilt roof assemblies need to meet current Mississippi wind-resistance standards.
