## Why Wiggins Properties See Water Damage Issues

The regional climate is the starting point. Wiggins averages well over 60 inches of rainfall annually — among the highest totals in Mississippi — and the town sits close enough to the Gulf Coast that tropical moisture from named storms and unnamed tropical disturbances pushes heavy rain events inland with regularity. That volume of water, combined with the area's red clay subsoil, means drainage around foundations is a persistent challenge. Water that can't move away from a structure finds gaps in slab edges, crawl space vents, and aging mortar joints.

The housing stock in the Wiggins area also plays a role. A significant share of homes in Stone County were built in the 1970s and 1980s, when vapor barriers in crawl spaces were inconsistent and subfloor materials were often particleboard rather than plywood. Particleboard swells rapidly when wet and loses structural integrity faster than solid wood — meaning a slow crawl space leak that goes unnoticed for two weeks can cause far more structural damage here than the same leak in a newer home with engineered lumber and a sealed vapor barrier.

## Our Water Damage Restoration Process in Wiggins

Every job starts with moisture mapping. Technicians use thermal imaging cameras and calibrated moisture meters to trace where water has traveled — not just where it's visible. In Wiggins homes with crawl spaces, that often means getting under the structure to assess subfloor saturation and check whether insulation batts have absorbed and held water against the wood above them.

Once the scope is established, water extraction comes first. Truck-mounted extractors pull standing water from flooring, and specialized tools address water trapped beneath hardwood or laminate. After extraction, the drying phase begins using commercial-grade desiccant or refrigerant dehumidifiers and high-velocity air movers positioned according to the IICRC S500 standard for water damage. Drying logs are recorded daily — documentation that matters both for confirming the structure is genuinely dry and for supporting an insurance claim.

Materials that cannot be dried in place — saturated drywall below the flood line, swollen particleboard subfloor sections, soaked insulation — are removed and properly disposed of before drying equipment is set. Leaving wet material inside a sealed drying chamber defeats the process and accelerates mold colonization, which can begin within 24 to 48 hours in Wiggins's humidity levels.

## Wiggins Insurance Coordination

Water damage claims in Mississippi are handled differently depending on the cause. Sudden and accidental losses — a burst pipe, an appliance failure, a roof breach from storm debris — are typically covered under standard homeowners policies. Gradual leaks and flood damage from rising surface water are treated separately, and flood losses generally require a separate NFIP or private flood policy.

HomeLyft technicians document losses thoroughly: moisture readings at intake, photographs of all affected materials before removal, and a written scope of work that aligns with the format most adjusters expect. That documentation reduces back-and-forth with carriers and helps ensure the claim reflects the actual scope of damage rather than a visual estimate made before walls were opened.

## Local Note

In Stone County's older ranch-style homes — common throughout the Wiggins area — interior walls are frequently insulated with fiberglass batts that were installed without a facing vapor barrier. When a supply line leaks inside an exterior wall cavity, those batts wick moisture upward and hold it against the wall sheathing long after the visible water is gone. Standard drying timelines assume the cavity will dry within three to five days; in these homes, we routinely find elevated moisture readings at day six or seven, which means drying equipment needs to stay in place longer than the initial estimate. Pulling equipment too early in this housing type is one of the most common reasons mold appears weeks after a "completed" restoration.
