## Why Gulfport Properties Present Unique Biohazard Challenges

Gulfport sits on the Mississippi Gulf Coast, where the combination of salt air, high ambient humidity, and a housing stock that includes everything from post-Katrina rebuilds to older pier-and-beam structures creates a complicated backdrop for biohazard remediation. Older homes — particularly those that predate the widespread use of sealed concrete subfloors — can allow biological material to migrate into crawl spaces or beneath flooring in ways that aren't immediately visible. Porous surfaces absorb more than they appear to, and in Gulfport's heat, that absorption accelerates.

The coastal environment also means that properties may already carry elevated baseline mold spore counts. When a biohazard event occurs, the affected area becomes a compounding problem: the remediation team isn't just addressing the immediate contamination but also working to prevent a secondary mold event in the days that follow. That's a distinction that matters in Harrison County in a way it simply doesn't in cooler, drier climates.

Gulfport's post-Katrina rebuild era produced a significant number of properties with spray-foam insulation in crawl spaces and attics — a practice that became common after 2005 as builders prioritized moisture resistance.
