## Our Emergency Board-Up and Tarping Process in Eastabuchie

Every job starts with a rapid site assessment — we photograph and document all breach points before touching anything, creating a visual record that supports your insurance claim from the first moment. For roof tarping, we use reinforced polyethylene tarps anchored with wood battens screwed into sound decking rather than simply weighted at the edges. In Jones County's wind environment, a tarp that isn't mechanically fastened can become a sail in the next thunderstorm and cause additional damage.

For broken window board-up and fire damage board-up, we cut OSB panels to fit each opening, secure them with structural screws into the framing, and apply weatherproof tape at the seams. On homes with older wood-frame construction — common throughout rural Jones County — we take extra care to locate solid framing members rather than relying on stud spacing assumptions that apply to modern platform-framed houses. Where fire has compromised framing integrity, we work around sound structure and note compromised areas in our documentation.

We hold IICRC FSRT (Fire and Smoke) and IICRC WRT (Water) certifications, and our team is FORTIFIED Building Roofing MS Windstorm Certified — a credential that's directly relevant when tarping storm-damaged roofs in Mississippi's high-wind zones. We're also EPA Lead-Safe Certified, which matters on older Eastabuchie homes where disturbing painted surfaces during board-up requires lead-safe work practices.

## Local Note: Pine Debris and Roof Tarp Anchoring in Jones County

One thing that comes up repeatedly on Eastabuchie jobs is the presence of pine needle and debris buildup in roof valleys and around penetrations. When a tree strike or wind event damages a roof, that accumulated debris is often saturated and heavy, sitting against the decking under the tarp. If it isn't cleared before tarping, it holds moisture against the wood and accelerates rot even while the tarp is in place. On Jones County jobs, we make a point of clearing debris from the immediate damage area before we lay the tarp — it takes an extra few minutes but meaningfully extends how well the tarp protects the structure while repairs are being scheduled.
