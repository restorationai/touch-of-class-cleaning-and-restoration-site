## Our Water Damage Restoration Process in D'Iberville

The first step on every job is a thorough moisture mapping of the affected area using thermal imaging cameras and calibrated moisture meters. Water follows the path of least resistance — it travels along wall cavities, under flooring, and through insulation before it shows up visually. Knowing exactly where moisture has migrated lets the crew place extraction equipment and drying systems where they matter, not just where the damage is obvious.

After extraction, the team establishes a drying system using commercial-grade low-grain refrigerant dehumidifiers and high-velocity air movers positioned to create a directed airflow across wet surfaces. Drying targets are set using the IICRC S500 standard as the baseline, then adjusted for D'Iberville's ambient conditions — in summer months, outdoor dew points here regularly exceed 70°F, which means the dehumidifiers are working harder than they would in a drier climate. Daily moisture readings track progress and document that structural materials are reaching acceptable dry standards before any reconstruction begins.

Because the team holds IICRC Water Restoration Technician and Applied Structural Drying certifications, the drying plan is defensible to an insurance adjuster — every reading is logged with date, time, and location, creating a paper trail that supports your claim.
