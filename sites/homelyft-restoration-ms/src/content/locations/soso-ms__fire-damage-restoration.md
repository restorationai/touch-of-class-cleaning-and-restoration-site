## Why Soso Properties Face Particular Challenges After a Fire

Jones County's housing stock skews older, and many homes in and around Soso were built with materials that react to fire and smoke differently than modern construction. Tongue-and-groove pine subfloors, plaster-and-lath interior walls, and uninsulated crawl spaces are common in homes built before the 1970s. After a fire, smoke particulates settle into the grain of pine flooring and the porous face of plaster in ways that standard cleaning misses entirely. The result is a home that looks cleaned but still smells like a fire weeks later.

Mississippi's climate adds another layer. Even in the cooler months, relative humidity in Jones County rarely drops low enough for smoke-damaged materials to off-gas and dry on their own. Soot that might stay dry and powdery in an arid climate becomes a sticky, acidic paste here — one that etches metal fixtures, discolors grout, and accelerates corrosion on appliances if it isn't neutralized quickly. The longer smoke residue sits in humid air, the more secondary damage compounds.

Electrical fires are also a recurring pattern in older rural homes throughout this part of Mississippi, where knob-and-tube or aluminum wiring in mid-century construction can fail silently before producing visible flame. Structural fire damage from these slow-burning starts often hides inside wall cavities, making a thorough scope of work essential before any restoration begins.

## Our Fire Damage Restoration Process in Soso

Every fire loss is different, but the sequence we follow is deliberate. The first step on-site is a full structural assessment — identifying load-bearing members compromised by heat, locating hidden char inside wall cavities, and confirming that the building is safe for our crew and for the homeowner to re-enter.

From there, we move into controlled demolition of unsalvageable materials. In Soso's older homes, that often means removing plaster sections or original hardwood that has absorbed too much smoke to restore economically. We document everything photographically before removal, which matters when your insurance adjuster is reviewing the claim.

Smoke and odor treatment follows a layered approach: dry sponge cleaning for loose soot, chemical sponge and HEPA vacuuming for embedded particulates, and hydroxyl or thermal fogging for odor neutralization in porous materials. Our team is IICRC FSRT certified, meaning the process follows a defined standard rather than a technician's best guess. EPA Lead-Safe certification also matters here — disturbing painted surfaces in pre-1978 homes during fire cleanup requires specific handling protocols that protect your family and our crew.

Structural repairs, content pack-out, and coordination with your insurance carrier round out the process before the home is returned to you.
