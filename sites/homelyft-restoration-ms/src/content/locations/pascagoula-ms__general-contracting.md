## Why Pascagoula Properties Present Unique Renovation Challenges

The Jackson County coastline isn't just scenic — it's demanding on building materials. Homes within a few miles of the Pascagoula River delta and the Port of Pascagoula industrial corridor deal with persistent humidity levels that can hover above 70% for months at a stretch. That kind of sustained moisture exposure means subfloor systems, window framing, and exterior sheathing age faster here than the manufacturer's warranty assumes. Older homes — particularly those built before the 1980s — frequently contain lead-based paint in trim work and window sills, which is why EPA Lead-Safe certification isn't optional on Pascagoula renovation projects; it's a legal and practical necessity.

Post-Katrina reconstruction also left a complicated legacy. Some homes rebuilt after 2005 used materials that met the code of that era but fall short of today's FORTIFIED Home standards, which Mississippi insurers increasingly expect. A bathroom remodel that opens up an exterior wall can reveal framing that needs to be brought up to current wind-load requirements before new finishes go in — something a contractor unfamiliar with coastal Mississippi building codes might not catch until it's a problem.
