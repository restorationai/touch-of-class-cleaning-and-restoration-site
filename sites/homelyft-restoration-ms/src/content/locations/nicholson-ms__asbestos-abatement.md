## Why Nicholson Properties See Asbestos Concerns

Pearl River County's housing stock reflects decades of Gulf South construction norms. Homes and outbuildings built before 1980 in and around Nicholson frequently contain asbestos in places that aren't obvious at first glance: floor tile mastic, pipe insulation on older HVAC and plumbing runs, attic insulation, exterior siding panels, and — especially common in this region — the textured "popcorn" ceiling finishes applied through the 1970s. That ceiling texture was a popular choice across coastal Mississippi because it was inexpensive and hid imperfections, and it was routinely mixed with chrysotile asbestos fibers for added durability.

The problem compounds when storm damage enters the picture. Pearl River County properties that took wind or water damage — whether from a named storm or a severe local weather event — sometimes have compromised rooflines or siding that exposes underlying materials. Once those materials are cracked, broken, or wet, the asbestos abatement process becomes more urgent and more complex than a routine pre-renovation inspection.

## Our Asbestos Abatement Process in Nicholson

Every project begins with a formal asbestos inspection and bulk sampling before any removal work starts. Samples are sent to an accredited laboratory, and we don't proceed with abatement until results confirm what materials are present and in what concentration. That sequencing protects you legally and protects our crew.

Once confirmed, the affected area is isolated using negative air pressure containment — plastic sheeting, HEPA-filtered air scrubbers running continuously, and sealed HVAC penetrations so fibers cannot migrate to unaffected rooms. Removal follows EPA and Mississippi Department of Environmental Quality (MDEQ) protocols, with all asbestos-containing material wetted during removal to suppress fiber release. Waste is double-bagged in labeled, 6-mil poly bags and transported to a licensed disposal facility — disposal documentation is provided to you at project close.

Final air clearance testing by an independent party confirms the space is safe before containment comes down. That step isn't optional on our projects; it's how we close out every abatement job regardless of scope.
