## Why Glendale Properties Are Vulnerable After Storms and Fires

Glendale sits in a region of Mississippi where late-spring and early-fall storm systems can produce straight-line winds, hail, and the occasional tornado touchdown with very little warning. That weather pattern means roof damage — missing shingles, lifted decking, cracked ridge caps — is one of the most common calls we handle. When a fire also compromises a structure, the roof and window openings left behind are immediate entry points for rain, wildlife, and unauthorized access.

The housing stock in this part of Mississippi includes a meaningful number of older wood-frame homes with original roof sheathing that was never designed to handle prolonged exposure. ## Our Board-Up and Tarping Process in Glendale

When we arrive on site, the first priority is a quick structural safety check before anyone goes up on a roof or into a fire-damaged structure. We identify all breach points — broken windows, compromised doors, open roof sections — and photograph everything for insurance documentation before a single board goes up.

For window and door board-up, we use minimum ½-inch OSB fastened into the structural framing, not just the window casing, so the panel actually holds against wind pressure rather than pulling out with the first gust. For roof tarping, we use heavy-duty polyethylene tarps secured with cap nails and lumber battens along the edges — a method that keeps the tarp from peeling back in wind rather than just draping it loosely over the opening. Because HomeLyft holds a FORTIFIED Building Roofing MS Windstorm Certification, our crews understand wind-load principles that inform how we anchor temporary protection, not just how we install permanent roofing.

All breach points are documented with time-stamped photos and measurements, which become part of the loss file we hand directly to your adjuster.

## Local Note

One thing that comes up specifically in older Glendale-area homes is the combination of wood-shake or aged asphalt roofing over skip sheathing — a traditional construction method where roof boards are spaced rather than laid solid. When a section of that roof is breached, a standard tarp installation can actually bridge over the gaps in the sheathing rather than lying flat, creating pockets where wind can catch and lift the tarp. Our crews probe the sheathing pattern before anchoring so the batten placement hits solid wood, not air.
