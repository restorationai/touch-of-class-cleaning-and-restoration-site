Pearlington sits at the southwestern edge of Hancock County, tucked between the Pearl River and the Louisiana state line — a geography that makes it one of the most flood-prone corners of Mississippi. When rising water, a kitchen fire, or a sudden roof failure damages a home here, the contents inside often suffer just as badly as the structure. Furniture warps in the humidity, smoke odor embeds itself into upholstery and wood grain, and mold can begin colonizing porous materials within 48 hours of a water event. ## Why Pearlington Homes Face Elevated Contents Damage Risk

Pearlington's position along the Pearl River delta means the soil stays saturated for extended periods after heavy rain, and interior humidity levels climb fast when a structure is breached. Older homes in this part of Hancock County — many built before modern vapor barriers became standard — draw that ground moisture upward through slab and crawl space alike. The result is that a single plumbing failure or storm-driven roof leak doesn't stay contained; it spreads laterally through flooring and wicks into furniture legs, baseboards, and stored items in closets or under beds before a homeowner even realizes the extent of the problem.

Katrina reshaped much of the housing stock here, and a significant number of rebuilt homes use engineered wood products and composite cabinetry that respond to prolonged moisture exposure differently than solid wood — they swell faster and delaminate if not dried quickly. That means the window for salvaging furniture and cabinetry contents is shorter than it might be in drier inland communities, and prompt pack-out is not just convenient but often the deciding factor between restoration and replacement.

## Our Contents Restoration and Storage Process in Pearlington

The process begins with a room-by-room inventory before anything is moved. Every item is photographed, catalogued by condition, and assigned a priority based on salvageability — antiques, electronics, documents, and sentimental items are flagged separately from general household goods. This documentation also forms the foundation of your insurance claim file.

Pack-out uses padded moving blankets, sealed plastic bins, and climate-controlled transport to protect items during the drive to our Gulfport facility. Once there, contents are sorted into cleaning streams based on the type of damage:

- **Water-damaged items** go through controlled drying, antimicrobial treatment, and deodorization before any surface cleaning.
- **Fire and smoke-affected items** are processed using dry-sponge wiping, ultrasonic cleaning for hard surfaces and non-porous objects, and ozone or hydroxyl treatment for odor neutralization in textiles and upholstered pieces.
- **Mold-affected contents** are assessed individually — porous materials that cannot be cleaned to a safe standard are documented for your insurer rather than returned.

Items that pass cleaning are stored in a climate-controlled environment until your Pearlington home is ready to receive them. We coordinate the return delivery to align with your contractor's schedule so furniture is not sitting on a freshly refinished floor for days before it can be properly placed.

## Insurance Coordination for Pearlington Contents Claims

Contents losses in Hancock County frequently involve both homeowner's policies and, for properties in designated flood zones along the Pearl River corridor, separate NFIP flood policies. These two coverage streams have different documentation requirements, and mixing them up during the claims process is a common source of delays. Our team prepares itemized loss inventories with photographs, replacement cost estimates, and cleaning cost breakdowns formatted to satisfy both policy types. We work directly with adjusters and can provide supplemental documentation if a claim is initially underpaid based on an incomplete contents assessment.

One pattern that comes up specifically in Pearlington and the surrounding Pearl River communities: homes that flooded during Katrina and were subsequently elevated on piers often have enclosed storage areas beneath the elevated floor — sometimes finished, sometimes not — where homeowners keep seasonal items, tools, and furniture overflow. When we do a pack-out in this area, we specifically walk those under-home storage spaces before closing out the inventory, because missing those items can leave significant value off the insurance claim.
