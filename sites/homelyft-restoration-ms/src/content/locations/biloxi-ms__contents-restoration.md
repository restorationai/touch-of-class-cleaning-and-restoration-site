Biloxi's proximity to the Gulf means that when a storm surge, hurricane-driven flood, or even a sudden pipe failure hits a home, the damage rarely stops at the walls — it soaks into furniture, saturates clothing, and leaves smoke or mold odors embedded in upholstery long after the structure dries. Contents restoration and storage is the part of recovery most homeowners don't think about until they're standing in a ruined living room wondering whether their grandmother's armoire or their children's keepsakes can be saved. The answer, in most cases, is yes — but timing and proper handling make the difference.

## Why Biloxi Properties Face Elevated Contents Damage Risk

Biloxi sits in one of the most humidity-intensive corridors on the Gulf Coast. Average relative humidity hovers near 75–80% for much of the year, and summer dew points routinely push into the low 70s — conditions that accelerate mold colonization on porous materials like fabric, wood, and paper within 24 to 48 hours of a moisture event. That window is shorter here than in most inland Mississippi cities.

The housing stock adds another layer of complexity. Many Biloxi homes were rebuilt or substantially renovated after Hurricane Katrina, meaning you'll find a mix of newer elevated construction and older pre-storm structures with original hardwood floors, plaster-and-lath interior walls, and antique furnishings that carry real sentimental and monetary value. Elevated homes also create staging challenges — moving large furniture down exterior stairs or through narrow doorways requires careful inventory and wrapping before any item leaves the property.

Salt air is a factor that rarely gets mentioned but matters enormously for metal hardware, electronics, and leather goods. Corrosion from salt-laden coastal air can accelerate on items that have been wetted and then left in an uncontrolled environment, which is why getting belongings into a climate-controlled facility quickly is especially important for Biloxi properties near the waterfront.

## Our Contents Restoration and Storage Process in Biloxi

The process begins with a detailed pack-out inventory. Every item that leaves your home is photographed, catalogued, and assigned a condition rating before it's wrapped and loaded. This documentation serves two purposes: it protects your belongings during transit and gives your insurance adjuster a clear, itemized record of what was affected and how.

Once at our climate-controlled storage facility, items are sorted by damage type and material. Textiles go through ozone or hydroxyl treatment to neutralize smoke and mold odors without the chemical residue that some cleaning methods leave behind. Hard surfaces — furniture frames, cabinetry, decorative items — are cleaned using methods matched to the material, whether that's ultrasonic cleaning for small valuables and electronics components, or hand-cleaning with appropriate solvents for finished wood. Documents and photographs receive freeze-drying or air-drying treatment depending on the extent of saturation.

Throughout the process, our IICRC-certified team follows established standards for fire and smoke restoration (FSRT) and mold remediation (AMRT), so the cleaning methods applied to your belongings are the same protocols used on the structure itself — not an afterthought.

When your home is ready, we coordinate return delivery and help you place items back in the correct rooms. Nothing is returned until the structure has passed drying verification.
