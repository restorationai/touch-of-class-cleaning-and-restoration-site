## Why St. Martin Properties Face Persistent Water Damage Risks

Jackson County averages more than 60 inches of rainfall per year — roughly double what most of the continental U.S. sees — and St. Martin's relatively flat terrain gives that water nowhere fast to go. Crawl space flooding is one of the most common calls we handle here. Homes built on pier-and-beam foundations, which are common throughout this part of the Mississippi Gulf Coast, are especially vulnerable: when groundwater rises after a prolonged rain event, it pools under the structure and raises the relative humidity inside the crawl space to levels that accelerate wood rot and mold growth even when the living area looks completely dry.

The area also sits in a wind and storm surge corridor. A tropical system that makes landfall near the mouth of the Pascagoula River can push water inland faster than interior drainage systems can handle, and that surge can enter homes through foundation vents, garage doors, and even HVAC penetrations. Older vinyl and aluminum-sided homes in this part of Jackson County often have less weather-resistant sheathing behind the cladding, meaning water that gets past the exterior skin can travel silently inside wall cavities for days before it shows up as a stain or a soft spot in drywall.

## Our Water Damage Restoration Process in St. Martin

Every job starts with a thorough moisture mapping inspection. Our technicians use thermal imaging cameras and calibrated moisture meters to locate water that has migrated beyond the visible damage area — under flooring, inside wall assemblies, and in ceiling cavities. This step matters more in Gulf Coast homes than in drier climates because high ambient humidity masks evaporation, making it easy to underestimate how far water has actually traveled.

Once we understand the full scope, we move into water extraction. Industrial-grade truck-mount and portable extraction units pull standing water and saturated material moisture out of carpeting, hardwood subfloors, and crawl space soil. After extraction, we set a calculated drying system — high-capacity LGR dehumidifiers paired with axial and centrifugal air movers — sized to the affected square footage and the specific materials involved. Drying is monitored daily against psychrometric targets set by the IICRC S500 standard, and we don't close out a job until readings confirm materials have returned to acceptable moisture levels for this climate.

If building materials — drywall, insulation, flooring — are too saturated to dry in place, we perform controlled demolition to remove them before they become a mold substrate. We document everything photographically before, during, and after, which matters when you're filing a homeowner's insurance claim.
