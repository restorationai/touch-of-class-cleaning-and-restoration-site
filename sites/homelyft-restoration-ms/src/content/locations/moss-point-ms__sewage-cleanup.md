## Why Moss Point Properties See Sewage Backup More Often

Jackson County's coastal plain is built on sandy, low-permeability soils that drain poorly after heavy rain events — and Moss Point gets roughly 65 inches of rainfall annually, well above the national average. During sustained downpours, municipal sewer mains serving older sections of the city can become overwhelmed, pushing wastewater back through floor drains, toilets, and washing-machine standpipes. Homes built before the 1980s — and Moss Point has a significant share of them — frequently have cast-iron or clay sewer laterals that have corroded, cracked, or shifted over decades of soil movement near the river corridors. A hairline crack in a lateral is invisible until a heavy rain event turns it into a full sewage backup in your bathroom or utility room.

Septic systems serving properties on the city's edges face a related problem: the high water table limits the effective drain-field depth, and a saturated field during a wet spring can force effluent back toward the tank and eventually into the structure. If you've noticed a sulfur odor near floor drains after a rainstorm, that's often the first warning before a full overflow occurs.

## Our Sewage Cleanup and Sanitization Process in Moss Point

Category 3 water — the classification for raw sewage — requires a different response than a clean-water pipe burst. Every material it contacts is presumed contaminated with bacteria, viruses, and parasites until proven otherwise by testing or removal.

Here's how the process works on a Moss Point job:

1. **Containment and safety assessment** — technicians establish a contamination perimeter, ventilate the space, and confirm the sewage source has been shut off or isolated before anyone enters the affected area without full PPE.
2. **Extraction and solid waste removal** — truck-mounted and portable extraction units remove standing sewage. Solid waste is bagged and disposed of in accordance with Mississippi Department of Environmental Quality guidelines.
3. **Controlled demolition if needed** — porous materials like drywall, insulation, and carpet that absorbed Category 3 water cannot be dried and saved; they are removed and documented for your insurance claim.
4. **EPA-registered disinfection** — affected structural surfaces are treated with hospital-grade antimicrobials. Because Moss Point's climate keeps interior humidity elevated, we run commercial dehumidifiers alongside the sanitization process to prevent residual moisture from feeding secondary mold growth within the 24–48 hour window mold needs to colonize.
5. **Air quality monitoring and clearance** — before any reconstruction begins, moisture readings and air samples confirm the space is dry and microbially safe.

Properties near the Moss Point riverfront and low-lying areas closer to the Escatawba tend to have older cast-iron plumbing that has never been lined or replaced.
