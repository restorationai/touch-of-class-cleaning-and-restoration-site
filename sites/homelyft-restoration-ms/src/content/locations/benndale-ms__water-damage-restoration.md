## Why Benndale Properties See Water Damage Issues

George County averages well over 60 inches of rainfall annually — one of the higher totals in Mississippi — and Benndale's position in the county means it catches a meaningful share of that moisture. When tropical systems push inland from the Gulf, even a modest storm can dump several inches in a matter of hours. The red-clay subsoil common throughout this part of the state does not drain quickly, so water pools against foundations, works into crawl spaces, and finds its way through any gap in a pier-and-beam or slab perimeter.

The region's housing stock adds another layer of complexity. Many homes in and around Benndale were built in the mid-twentieth century, when vapor barriers were minimal or absent entirely. Crawl spaces in those older structures tend to hold humidity year-round, which means a burst supply line or a roof leak during a storm doesn't just wet the interior — it saturates framing members that were already operating at elevated moisture content. Mold colonization can begin within 24 to 48 hours under those conditions, which is why the timeline between the first call and the start of extraction matters.

## Our Water Damage Restoration Process in Benndale

The process starts with a thorough moisture assessment — not just the visible wet area, but the wall cavities, subfloor, and crawl space beneath it. Thermal imaging and calibrated moisture meters map where water has migrated so nothing is left to dry on its own behind a wall.

Extraction comes next. Truck-mounted and portable extraction units pull standing water from flooring, carpet, and subfloor assemblies. In pier-and-beam homes common to this part of George County, that often means getting equipment into a crawl space to pull water from the ground and from the underside of the floor deck — a step that's easy to skip and costly to ignore.

Once bulk water is removed, the drying phase begins. Industrial desiccant and refrigerant dehumidifiers, paired with high-velocity air movers, are positioned to create a controlled drying environment calibrated to the IICRC S500 standard. Moisture readings are logged daily so the drying curve is documented — something insurance adjusters and carriers require before closing a structural drying claim.

If building materials have absorbed enough water that they cannot be dried in place within an acceptable window, selective demolition — removing baseboards, cutting drywall above the flood line, pulling damaged flooring — protects the structure from secondary damage and speeds the overall timeline.

## Local Note

One pattern our technicians have noticed in older George County wood-frame homes is that tongue-and-groove pine subfloors — common in construction from the 1940s through the 1960s — behave very differently from modern OSB or plywood during a water event. Pine subfloor planks swell tightly against one another when wet, which actually slows surface extraction because water has nowhere to wick upward. The moisture is trapped below the surface and continues to saturate the floor joists long after the top of the floor feels dry to the touch. In these homes, we extend the drying phase and take moisture readings from the joist bays directly rather than relying solely on surface readings — otherwise a floor that tests dry at the surface can still be harboring enough moisture in the framing to support mold growth within a week.
