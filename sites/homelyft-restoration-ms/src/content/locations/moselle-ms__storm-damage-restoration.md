## Why Moselle Properties Are Especially Vulnerable to Storm Damage

Jones County's clay-heavy soil is notoriously slow to drain. After a significant rain event, water doesn't just run off — it pools against foundations, saturates crawl spaces, and finds every gap in a home's envelope. Moselle's housing stock skews toward pier-and-beam construction, which is well-suited to the region's movement-prone soil but leaves crawl spaces exposed to wind-driven rain and debris intrusion. When a storm pushes water under a home, it can sit for days before a homeowner realizes the subfloor is wet.

Moselle also sits close enough to the Piney Woods transition zone that large hardwoods — pines, water oaks, sweet gums — are common in yards and along fence lines. These trees are beautiful until a storm cell drops wind speeds above 60 mph, at which point root systems in saturated soil give way and canopies come down on roofs, carports, and power lines. Tree-impact damage is one of the most complex storm scenarios we handle because it combines structural breach, interior water intrusion, and sometimes live utility hazards all at once.

## Our Storm Damage Restoration Process in Moselle

Every job starts with a full exterior and interior assessment before a single piece of equipment is staged. We photograph and document the loss systematically — roof decking, wall penetrations, attic insulation, flooring, and contents — because that documentation is what your insurance adjuster needs to process the claim accurately.

Once the scope is clear, we move in sequence:

1. **Emergency stabilization** — tarping breached roofs, boarding windows, and removing debris that poses an immediate threat to the structure.
2. **Water extraction and drying** — any rain intrusion is treated as a water damage event. We use truck-mounted extractors and desiccant dehumidifiers calibrated to Mississippi's high ambient humidity, drying to IICRC S500 standards before any reconstruction begins.
3. **Structural assessment** — in pier-and-beam homes common to the Moselle area, we check rim joists, girders, and subfloor panels for saturation and storm-related movement.
4. **Reconstruction** — from replacing damaged roof sheathing and shingles to reframing walls and restoring interior finishes, the crew that does the mitigation can carry the job through to completion.
