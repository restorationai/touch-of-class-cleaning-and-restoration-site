## Why Laurel's Climate Puts Extra Wear on Upholstery

Jones County sits in the humid subtropical band that covers most of south Mississippi, which means upholstered furniture rarely gets a real chance to dry out between spring storms and summer afternoons. Fabric that looks clean can still hold enough ambient moisture to feed mildew growth inside the cushion core, especially in homes without central air running consistently or in rooms near exterior walls that stay a few degrees cooler. Older homes around Laurel, many built well before central HVAC was standard, tend to have less consistent airflow room to room, so a sofa against an interior wall in one room might dry fine while a chair in a sunroom or converted porch stays damp for days. Pollen season adds another layer: heavy pine and oak pollen drifts through open windows and settles into fabric weave, which is why upholstery that looks fine can still trigger allergy symptoms.

## Our Upholstery Cleaning Process in Laurel

We start every job with a fabric and fill assessment, because a leather sectional and a cotton-blend armchair need different chemistry and different drying expectations. For fabric upholstery, we use hot water extraction calibrated to the fiber type, pulling embedded soil and moisture out of the cushion core rather than just refreshing the surface. Leather gets a separate conditioning step after cleaning, since stripping natural oils without replacing them leaves leather stiff and prone to cracking in Mississippi's dry winter cold snaps. On pieces with visible mildew staining or a musty smell, we test the affected area before committing to a full clean, because some staining sits below the surface in the padding and needs a different approach than a standard steam clean. Drying time matters more here than in drier climates, so we bring air movers to speed evaporation and prevent the piece from sitting damp long enough for mold to take hold, generally a risk that starts within 24 to 48 hours in humid conditions.
