## Why Henderson Point Properties Face Compounding Fire Damage Risks

The Gulf Coast climate is the defining factor here. Relative humidity along this stretch of Harrison County routinely sits above 70 percent, even in cooler months. After a fire, firefighting water saturates walls, subfloors, and insulation — and in Henderson Point's humid air, that moisture has nowhere to go quickly. The result is a layered problem: smoke odor embedded in porous surfaces, water damage from suppression efforts, and an accelerated window for mold colonization that can begin in as little as 24 to 48 hours in these conditions.

Many homes along the Point were built during the post-war construction boom of the 1950s through 1970s, when pier-and-beam foundations and wood-frame construction were standard. Those older structures often contain materials — tongue-and-groove pine paneling, original hardwood floors, plaster-over-lathe walls — that absorb smoke and char gases deeply. Salvaging them requires careful chemical sponge work and thermal fogging rather than simple surface cleaning, and the decision to restore versus replace has to be made quickly before secondary damage escalates costs.

## Our Fire Damage Restoration Process in Henderson Point

Every fire loss is different, but the sequence that produces the best outcomes here follows a consistent structure.

**Emergency board-up and tarping** comes first. Gulf weather doesn't pause for property damage — an open roof or broken window in Henderson Point can let in a rain band or afternoon thunderstorm that doubles the loss. We secure the structure before any interior work begins.

**Controlled demolition of unsalvageable material** follows. Charred framing, compromised insulation, and fire-damaged drywall are removed systematically, with documentation photographed at each stage for your insurance adjuster.

**Smoke and soot removal** is where IICRC FSRT (Fire and Smoke Restoration Technician) training matters most. Different combustion byproducts — wet smoke from slow-burning fires, dry smoke from fast-burning ones, protein residue from kitchen fires — require different cleaning agents and techniques. Applying the wrong chemistry to the wrong residue type can permanently set a stain or odor.

**Structural drying** runs concurrently. Industrial dehumidifiers and air movers are calibrated to the moisture readings we take on arrival, and readings are logged daily until the structure reaches its drying goal — a process guided by the IICRC S500 and S700 standards.

**Odor neutralization** closes the remediation phase. Thermal fogging and hydroxyl generation penetrate the same pathways smoke traveled, breaking odor compounds at the molecular level rather than masking them.

Reconstruction — framing, drywall, painting, flooring — follows once the structure passes clearance.
