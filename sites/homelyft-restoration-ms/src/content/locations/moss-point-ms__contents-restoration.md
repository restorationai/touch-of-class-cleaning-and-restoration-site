Moss Point sits at the confluence of the Escatawba and Pascagoula rivers, which means humidity rarely dips low enough for belongings to dry on their own after a flood or fire. When a pipe bursts or a kitchen fire leaves smoke residue baked into upholstered furniture, the Gulf Coast air — thick and warm for most of the year — accelerates the damage to fabrics, wood finishes, and electronics if those items stay on-site. Contents restoration and pack-out services exist precisely for this situation: getting your belongings out of the damaged environment quickly, cleaning and restoring them under controlled conditions, and storing them securely until your home is ready.

## Why Moss Point Properties See Elevated Contents Damage

The Moss Point area's proximity to tidal waterways creates a baseline indoor humidity that can hover above 70% for weeks at a stretch, especially in the months between spring storms and late-summer heat. That moisture load means that after any water intrusion — a roof leak from a Gulf storm, a slow drain line failure, or a washing machine overflow — fabrics, paper documents, and wood furniture begin absorbing ambient moisture even after the visible water is gone.

Moss Point also has a significant stock of older homes, many built in the postwar decades when solid-wood furniture and natural-fiber upholstery were standard. Those materials respond differently to smoke and soot than modern composite furniture does. Smoke residue bonds more deeply into porous wood grain, and natural-fiber textiles like wool and cotton hold odor compounds longer than synthetic blends. That means the cleaning process here often requires more aggressive odor counteractants and longer drying cycles than a comparable job in a newer subdivision.

## Our Contents Pack-Out and Restoration Process

The first step is a room-by-room inventory before anything leaves your home. Every item is photographed, tagged, and logged — this documentation matters both for your insurance claim and for ensuring nothing gets misplaced during transport. Items are sorted on-site into three categories: restorable, questionable (needing lab-level assessment), and non-restorable. That sorting happens before the truck is loaded, not after.

From Moss Point, contents travel to our climate-controlled facility where temperature and relative humidity are held to IICRC standards throughout the restoration process. Smoke-affected items go through ultrasonic cleaning or dry-ice blasting depending on the material — ultrasonic tanks are effective on hard surfaces like ceramics, metals, and glass, while dry-ice blasting removes char residue from wood without introducing additional moisture. Soft contents — upholstery, bedding, clothing — are processed through ozone and hydroxyl treatment chambers to break down odor molecules rather than mask them.

Once restored, items move into secure, climate-controlled storage until your property is ready. You receive an itemized inventory with photos so you always know exactly what is in storage and in what condition.

## Coordinating with Insurance in Moss Point

Most homeowner policies in Mississippi cover contents restoration as part of a broader property loss claim, but the documentation requirements vary by carrier. We work directly with adjusters, providing line-item inventories, before-and-after photographs, and restoration cost estimates in a format most carriers accept. For total-loss items — things that cannot be economically restored — we provide replacement cost documentation to support your claim.

If your policy includes additional living expenses (ALE) coverage, the cost of contents storage during your home's reconstruction may be reimbursable. We can provide the storage invoices in whatever format your adjuster requests. Our license number is RO 2728, which some carriers require on contractor documentation before approving payment.

## Local Note

One pattern we see specifically in Moss Point and the surrounding Pascagoula River basin: homes that have experienced any previous flood event — even a minor one — often have residual moisture trapped inside wall cavities and under flooring. When a second loss event occurs, belongings in those rooms absorb moisture faster than expected because the ambient air inside the home is already saturated at the wall level.
