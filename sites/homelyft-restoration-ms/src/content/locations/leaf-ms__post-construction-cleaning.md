## Why Post-Construction Cleanup in Leaf Requires More Than a Broom

Leaf sits in a part of Mississippi where warm, moist air is the baseline — not the exception. During and after construction, that ambient humidity accelerates the way fine particulates behave. Drywall dust absorbs moisture and becomes paste-like on window tracks and HVAC registers. Sawdust from pine framing (still common in Jones County residential builds) swells slightly and packs into corners and subfloor gaps. If a project ran through the summer months, there's a real chance that construction debris has already begun to attract mold spores on any surface that stayed damp for more than 48 hours.

Older homes in the Leaf area — particularly those built in the 1960s and 1970s — often have original hardwood floors and plaster-over-lathe walls in sections that weren't part of the renovation. Those surfaces require gentler, targeted cleaning methods to avoid damage. We assess each room before touching it, separating the newly constructed zones from the original structure so we're not applying the same aggressive scrubbing to a 60-year-old pine floor that we'd use on fresh tile.

## Our Post-Construction Cleaning Process in Leaf

A true post-construction clean happens in phases, not one pass. Here's how we approach it on Leaf-area properties:

**Rough clean first.** We remove bulk debris — scrap lumber, drywall offcuts, plastic sheeting, packaging materials — and bag or haul it before any surface cleaning begins. Skipping this step and going straight to mopping just redistributes the mess.

**Mechanical dust removal.** HEPA-filter vacuums pull fine particulate from surfaces, light fixtures, window sills, and inside cabinet boxes before any wet cleaning. In Leaf's climate, introducing moisture too early in the process turns dust into a smear problem.

**Detail cleaning.** This is where post-construction cleaning earns its cost: cleaning inside drawer slides, wiping down every interior cabinet surface, scrubbing grout haze from tile, removing paint overspray from hardware, cleaning window glass inside and out, and polishing fixtures. We use appropriate agents for each surface type — alkaline cleaners for concrete, pH-neutral solutions for hardwood, non-abrasive options for new stone countertops.

**HVAC and register flush.** Construction debris migrates into ductwork. We clean registers and return-air grilles and, where accessible, wipe down the visible duct interiors so the first time the system runs, it isn't redistributing drywall dust through a freshly cleaned space.

**Final inspection walk.** We do a room-by-room check against a punch list before we call the job complete.

## Local Note

One thing that catches contractors and homeowners off guard in the Leaf area: Jones County's clay-heavy soil tracks into a construction site differently than sandy coastal soil does. It dries into a hard, pale crust on concrete slabs and new flooring that looks like dried grout haze but doesn't respond to grout haze removers. We've learned to identify it early and treat it with the right mechanical and chemical combination before it's mistaken for a flooring defect. If your builder or subcontractors were working during a wet stretch, expect this on any slab or hard-surface floor — it's a Leaf-area pattern, not a product problem.
