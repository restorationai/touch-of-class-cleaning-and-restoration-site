## Why Sharon Properties See Stubborn Post-Construction Residue

Madison County's clay-heavy soil is tracked in constantly during active construction — it dries into a fine, abrasive powder that embeds in grout lines, HVAC returns, and window tracks. Sharon's proximity to the Ross Barnett Reservoir corridor also means elevated ambient humidity for much of the year, which slows the off-gassing of VOC-heavy adhesives and caulks used in new construction. Those compounds leave a greasy film on glass, cabinetry, and light fixtures that standard cleaning products smear rather than remove.

Older homes in the Sharon area that have undergone renovation face a layered problem: original hardwood floors and plaster-and-lath walls from mid-century builds absorb construction dust differently than modern drywall. Dust works into wood grain and plaster pores, and a single pass with a microfiber mop won't reach it. Our process accounts for substrate type before we ever unload equipment.

## Our Post-Construction Cleaning Process in Sharon

Every job starts with a scope walk — we photograph and document each room, noting substrate types, HVAC configuration, and any surfaces flagged by the general contractor. That documentation protects both the property owner and the builder if punch-list disputes arise later.

**Phase 1 — Rough clean:** We remove all visible debris, tape scraps, packaging, and cut-off materials. Drywall dust is HEPA-vacuumed from every horizontal surface before any wet cleaning begins. Skipping this step and going straight to wet mopping turns dust into a paste that scratches flooring and clogs drains.

**Phase 2 — Detail clean:** Window frames, sills, door hardware, cabinet interiors, light fixtures, and outlet covers are wiped individually. In Sharon's climate, adhesive residue on glass — from protective film left on windows during construction — has often baked on by the time we arrive. We use appropriate solvents matched to the glass coating to avoid streaking.

**Phase 3 — Final clean (white-glove):** This is the stage before keys change hands. Floors are cleaned by surface type — tile grout is scrubbed, hardwood is cleaned with pH-neutral product, LVP is treated per manufacturer spec. Air vents are vacuumed and wiped. The space is inspected under raking light to catch any haze or smear missed in earlier passes.

For specialty cleaning requests — post-renovation deep cleans, pressure washing of exterior flatwork, or cleaning after tenant buildouts in commercial spaces — we scope those individually and schedule accordingly.

## Equipment and Methods for Post-Construction Cleaning

Construction dust contains silica, fiberglass fibers, and chemical particulates that a standard vacuum recirculates into the air. We use HEPA-filtered vacuums rated for fine particulate capture so that what gets pulled off surfaces stays out of the air column. For interior glass cleaning, we use two-bucket systems and lint-free microfiber to eliminate the streaking that plagues post-construction glass work. On tile and grout, rotary scrubbers with appropriate brush attachments reach into grout lines that hand-scrubbing misses.

For exterior flatwork — driveways, patios, walkways — surface preparation after construction often means concrete splatter, mortar drips, and form-release compound residue. Pressure washing alone doesn't always address chemical residue; we assess whether a pre-treatment is needed before washing.

## Local Note

One thing that comes up consistently on Sharon-area renovation projects: Madison County's clay soil means construction crews track in a reddish-brown residue that stains concrete and light-colored grout if it dries and is then wet-mopped without a dry extraction step first.
