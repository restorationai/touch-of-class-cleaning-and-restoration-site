## Why Soso Properties See Sewage Backup Issues

Soso sits in a part of Jones County where a large share of residential properties rely on private septic systems rather than municipal sewer connections. That matters because septic systems have a finite capacity and a finite lifespan. Drain fields in this region are frequently installed in heavy clay-loam soils that don't percolate well, especially after a wet season. When the ground is already saturated — common during Mississippi's spring rainfall cycles — a septic system that was functioning adequately in dry conditions can back up into the home through the lowest fixture in the house: usually a floor drain, a washing machine drain, or a ground-level toilet.

Older homes on larger rural lots in the Soso area also tend to have cast-iron or Orangeburg sewer laterals that have been in the ground for decades. These materials corrode, collapse, or root-intrude in ways that PVC lines don't, and a partial blockage in a lateral can send raw sewage back into the structure before a homeowner realizes anything is wrong. The combination of aging infrastructure and soil conditions that resist drainage makes sewage backup a recurring problem here — not a freak event.

## Our Sewage Cleanup and Sanitization Process in Soso

Category 3 water — the classification for raw sewage — requires a different response than a clean-water pipe burst. Every porous material that absorbed contaminated water is treated as a biohazard. Here's how the process unfolds on a Soso job:

**Containment first.** Before extraction begins, the affected area is isolated to prevent cross-contamination into clean living spaces. In homes with open floor plans or HVAC systems that could circulate airborne particles, this step is non-negotiable.

**Extraction and removal.** Truck-mounted extractors pull standing sewage from floors. Saturated materials — carpet, pad, drywall below the flood line, insulation — are removed and bagged for disposal. In pier-and-beam homes common in rural Jones County, the crawl space is inspected and, if affected, treated separately.

**Sanitization and antimicrobial treatment.** Hard surfaces, framing lumber, and subfloor decking that can be dried and retained are treated with EPA-registered disinfectants. This isn't a surface wipe-down — it's a thorough application calibrated to contact time requirements that actually kill pathogens.

**Structural drying.** Commercial desiccant or refrigerant dehumidifiers and high-velocity air movers are positioned based on moisture readings taken with thermal imaging and pin meters. Drying logs are documented daily.

**Post-remediation verification.** Before reconstruction begins, moisture readings confirm the structure has returned to acceptable levels. Documentation is provided for insurance and for any required county or local inspections.
