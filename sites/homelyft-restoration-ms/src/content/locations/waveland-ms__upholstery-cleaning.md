## Why Waveland Furniture Wears Differently

Waveland sits low and close to the water, and most of the housing stock here was rebuilt or substantially renovated after Hurricane Katrina, which means a lot of homes are newer but still sit in flood-prone zones with elevated foundations, open floor plans, and windows that stay open for cross-ventilation much of the year. That constant coastal airflow brings in moisture and fine salt particulate that settles into fabric weave and leather grain over time. Furniture placed near sliding doors or screened porches, which is common in Hancock County's indoor-outdoor layouts, tends to pick up mildew odor and fiber breakdown faster than the same piece would inland. Add in sand tracked in from the beach and pollen from the pine flatwoods just north of town, and upholstery here accumulates a different mix of grit than furniture in a drier, inland climate.

## Our Upholstery Cleaning Process in Waveland

We start with a fabric and fiber identification step, since synthetic blends common in newer post-Katrina furnishings react differently to moisture and heat than natural fiber weaves or genuine leather. From there we vacuum with a HEPA-filtered upholstery tool to lift sand and dry soil before any liquid touches the piece, because working wet sand into fabric first only grinds it deeper into the weave. Depending on the material, we use low-moisture encapsulation cleaning or hot water extraction, always calibrated to the humidity in the room that day, since Waveland's indoor humidity can swing noticeably between an air-conditioned interior and a home that's been sitting closed up. Leather pieces get a separate conditioning step to replace oils lost to salt air exposure. We finish with a controlled drying pass using air movers, because in this climate, furniture left to air-dry on its own can stay damp long enough to invite mildew growth in the padding.
