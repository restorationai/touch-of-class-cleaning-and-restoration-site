## Why Pass Christian Properties Are Especially Vulnerable to Storm Damage

Pass Christian's position on the Mississippi Sound means it absorbs the northeast quadrant of Gulf storms almost head-on. That geography matters for restoration work. Wind speeds at landfall consistently run higher here than they do even a few miles north along the I-10 corridor, and storm surge has historically pushed water well inland across the low-lying areas near the beachfront.

The housing stock adds another layer of complexity. Pass Christian has a significant number of raised Creole cottages and older wood-frame homes built before modern wind-load requirements. Roof decking on these structures is often planked rather than plywood-sheathed, which means wind uplift can peel sections away faster and expose interior framing to rain intrusion within minutes of a storm's peak. Once water enters through a compromised roof, it follows the path of least resistance — soaking insulation, running down interior wall cavities, and pooling in subfloor spaces that are difficult to reach with standard drying equipment.

Salt air accelerates corrosion on metal fasteners and flashing, so even a roof that looks intact after a storm may have failing connections that won't hold through the next weather event. That's why our assessments here go beyond surface inspection.

## Our Storm Damage Restoration Process in Pass Christian

The first priority after any major storm is stopping active water intrusion. Our crews perform emergency tarping and board-up on the same visit as the initial assessment — we don't schedule those as separate trips. Once the structure is stabilized, we document every affected area with moisture mapping and photographic evidence, which your insurance adjuster will need for the claim.

Water extraction comes next. In homes with pier-and-beam foundations — common throughout Pass Christian's older residential blocks — standing water under the structure is often overlooked until it drives mold colonization in the subfloor. We use truck-mounted extraction units combined with low-profile equipment designed to work in crawl spaces. Structural drying follows the IICRC S500 standard, with drying logs maintained throughout the process so there's a documented record of conditions at every stage.

For wind damage, we coordinate temporary roofing and siding repairs alongside the water mitigation work. Because HomeLyft holds FORTIFIED Building Roofing certification for Mississippi windstorm work, we're positioned to advise on whether a repair qualifies for FORTIFIED designation — which can affect your insurance premium going forward.

Fire and smoke damage from lightning strikes is also part of our scope. The IICRC FSRT certification on our team means smoke residue in attic framing or HVAC systems gets treated with the same rigor as the visible structural damage.

## Pass Christian Insurance Coordination

Mississippi Gulf Coast claims after named storms involve a specific set of considerations that don't apply in most other markets. Wind versus water coverage disputes — where insurers disagree on whether damage was caused by wind (covered under homeowners) or surge (covered under NFIP flood policies) — are common after Gulf storms and can significantly delay settlements.

We document damage in a way that distinguishes wind-driven rain intrusion from ground-level surge intrusion, which is the evidence your public adjuster or attorney will need if a coverage dispute arises. We bill most major carriers directly and can work alongside your adjuster on-site during the inspection.
