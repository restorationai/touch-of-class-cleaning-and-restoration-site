Gulfport sits at the intersection of Gulf Coast humidity and a clay-heavy soil profile that makes crawl spaces behave differently than almost anywhere else in Mississippi. The combination of salt-laden air blowing in off the Sound, ground moisture that never fully evaporates between rain events, and summer relative humidity that routinely pushes above 90% creates conditions where an unencapsulated crawl space isn't just uncomfortable — it's actively degrading your floor joists, insulation, and indoor air quality season after season. If you've noticed musty odors rising through your floors or felt soft spots developing underfoot, the crawl space is almost certainly where the problem started.

## Why Gulfport Properties Are Especially Vulnerable

The Gulf Coast climate doesn't give crawl spaces a dry season to recover. Even in January, overnight dew points in Gulfport rarely drop low enough to let exposed dirt subfloors dry out completely. That persistent ground moisture feeds two problems simultaneously: wood rot along the sill plates and rim joists that sit closest to the soil, and mold colonization that can establish itself within 48 to 72 hours of a moisture event — whether that's a heavy rain, a plumbing drip, or simply a stretch of high-humidity days.

Gulfport's housing stock adds another layer of complexity. A significant portion of the city's residential neighborhoods contain homes built in the 1950s through the 1970s, when vapor barriers were either thin polyethylene sheeting stapled loosely to joists or absent entirely. Those original barriers — where they still exist — have typically degraded into brittle fragments that provide almost no meaningful moisture control. Homes closer to the Back Bay of Biloxi watershed can also experience periodic groundwater intrusion after heavy rainfall, which overwhelms any partial barrier that might be in place.

The clay soils common across Harrison County retain moisture for extended periods after rain events, meaning the ground beneath your crawl space is releasing vapor upward long after the rain stops. That slow, steady off-gassing is exactly what a properly installed encapsulation system is designed to interrupt.

## Our Crawl Space Encapsulation Process in Gulfport

Every encapsulation project starts with a thorough inspection of what's already down there. Before any liner goes in, the crew assesses existing moisture levels with calibrated meters, checks wood members for rot and mold, looks for evidence of pest activity, and identifies any standing water or drainage issues that need to be resolved first. Skipping this step and laying liner over an active problem is one of the most common mistakes in the industry — and one that leads to callbacks.

Once the crawl space is cleared and prepped, we install a heavy-duty reinforced polyethylene liner — typically 20-mil — across the entire floor and up the foundation walls, sealed at all seams and penetrations with moisture-resistant tape. The liner is mechanically fastened to the walls and sealed at the top to create a continuous barrier. Vents are sealed to stop humid outdoor air from cycling through the space, and a properly sized dehumidifier is installed and set to maintain relative humidity below 55% year-round.

For Gulfport homes where we identify existing mold on wood members, that remediation happens before encapsulation — not after. Our team holds IICRC AMRT certification for mold remediation, and we follow established drying protocols calibrated to the actual conditions under your home, not a generic national standard.

## Equipment and Materials Matched to Gulf Coast Conditions

Not all encapsulation materials perform equally in a coastal environment. Standard 6-mil poly sheeting — still sold by some contractors — tears easily during installation and degrades faster in high-humidity conditions. The reinforced 20-mil liner we use is rated for long-term performance in exactly the kind of sustained moisture environment that defines Gulfport's climate. Seam tape selection also matters: standard HVAC tape loses adhesion in high-humidity subfloor conditions, so we use tape specifically rated for below-grade and crawl space applications.

Dehumidifier sizing is calculated based on the actual square footage and cubic volume of your crawl space, not a one-size-fits-all unit. An undersized unit runs continuously and still fails to control humidity; an oversized unit short-cycles and wears out prematurely. We size to the space and the local climate load.

One pattern we see repeatedly in Gulfport homes that were repaired or rebuilt after Hurricane Katrina: the crawl space work done during that reconstruction period was often completed quickly and under significant pressure, which sometimes meant vapor barriers were installed without proper seam sealing or without addressing pre-existing drainage issues beneath the slab.
