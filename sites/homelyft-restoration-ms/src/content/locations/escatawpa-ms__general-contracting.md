## Why Escatawpa Properties Present Unique Renovation Challenges

Jackson County's proximity to the Gulf means that relative humidity rarely drops below 60% even in winter, and during the long summer stretch it routinely sits above 80%. That baseline moisture level has real consequences for renovation work. Adhesives, grout, and paint all cure differently in high-humidity environments. Wood framing that looks structurally sound can harbor hidden moisture content that only a moisture meter reveals — and in older Escatawpa homes with pier-and-beam foundations, subfloor moisture intrusion is common enough that we check it as a matter of course before any flooring installation begins.

Sandy coastal soils also shift more than the clay-heavy soils further inland, which means foundation settling is a recurring factor in homes throughout this part of Jackson County. Doors that stick, floors that slope slightly toward an exterior wall, and tile that cracks along grout lines are often early signs of differential settling rather than cosmetic wear. A remodel that ignores those signals and simply installs new materials over an unstable substrate is a remodel that will need redoing. We assess the structural picture before the finish work begins.

For homes built before 1978 — and there are many in this area — EPA Lead-Safe certification governs how we handle painted surfaces during demolition and renovation. Our team carries that certification, which matters both for the safety of occupants and for compliance with Mississippi contractor regulations.

One thing that catches out-of-area contractors working in Escatawpa and the surrounding Jackson County communities: the combination of high ambient humidity and unvented or poorly vented crawl spaces means that new flooring — particularly engineered hardwood and laminate — needs to acclimate on-site for longer than manufacturer specs written for drier climates suggest.
