## Why Hattiesburg Properties See Mold Issues

The Pine Belt's climate is the core driver. Hattiesburg sits in a region where annual rainfall exceeds 60 inches and summer dew points routinely reach the low 70s — conditions that make moisture management inside a structure genuinely difficult. Older homes throughout the city, many built with pier-and-beam foundations common to mid-century Mississippi construction, have crawl spaces that act as humidity reservoirs. When ground moisture vapor migrates upward through an unencapsulated crawl space, it saturates floor joists and subfloor sheathing from below, often without any visible sign on the finished floor above. By the time a musty odor becomes noticeable in a living room, mold colonies on the underside of the subfloor may already be well established.

Homes closer to the University of Southern Mississippi corridor tend to be older rental stock that has cycled through multiple tenants and deferred maintenance cycles — conditions that compound moisture risk. Window unit air conditioners, still common in older rental homes, create condensation points that drip into wall cavities if the unit is even slightly tilted inward. That slow, intermittent moisture source is exactly the kind mold thrives on because it never fully dries between events.

## Our Mold Remediation Process in Hattiesburg

Remediation begins with a thorough moisture mapping inspection using thermal imaging cameras and calibrated moisture meters. We identify not just where mold is visible but where moisture readings indicate hidden colonization behind drywall, under flooring, or inside wall cavities. This step matters especially in Hattiesburg's older housing stock, where original plaster-and-lath construction or tongue-and-groove pine subfloors can trap moisture in ways that standard visual inspection misses.

Once the scope is confirmed, we establish negative air pressure containment using 6-mil poly barriers and HEPA-filtered air scrubbers. This keeps spores from migrating to unaffected areas of the home during the physical removal process. Affected porous materials — drywall, insulation, compromised wood framing — are removed, bagged, and disposed of per EPA guidelines. Non-porous surfaces are HEPA-vacuumed and treated with an EPA-registered antimicrobial agent. Structural drying equipment (desiccant or refrigerant dehumidifiers, air movers) then brings the remaining materials to target moisture content before any reconstruction begins, following the IICRC S520 standard for mold remediation.

For crawl space mold, we address the source — typically ground moisture vapor — with encapsulation after remediation, because treating mold without correcting the moisture pathway means the problem returns.
