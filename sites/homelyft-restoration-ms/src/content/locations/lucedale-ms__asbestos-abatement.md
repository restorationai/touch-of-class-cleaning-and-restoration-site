## Why Lucedale Properties See Asbestos Concerns

George County sits in a region where the construction trades historically relied on asbestos-containing materials long after coastal metros had begun phasing them out. Rural supply chains meant older materials stayed in circulation, and many Lucedale homes were built or renovated well into the late 1970s using products that would later be regulated. The persistent heat and humidity here — summers that routinely push past 90°F with high dew points — accelerate the deterioration of older building materials. Pipe wrap and duct insulation that might have stayed intact for decades in a drier climate can become brittle and friable in attics and crawl spaces that cycle through extreme heat and moisture season after season. Friable asbestos — material that crumbles when handled — is the condition that triggers mandatory abatement under Mississippi Department of Environmental Quality (MDEQ) regulations, and it's exactly what inspectors find in aging mechanical systems throughout this area.

Flood events and storm damage complicate things further. When water intrudes into a structure and disturbs old flooring, ceiling tiles, or insulation, it can release fibers that would otherwise have remained safely encapsulated. George County sees its share of severe weather, and post-storm renovation is one of the more common situations that surfaces an asbestos problem no one knew they had.

## Our Asbestos Abatement Process in Lucedale

Every project starts with a thorough inspection and bulk sampling — we collect material samples from suspect areas and send them to an accredited laboratory for polarized light microscopy (PLM) analysis. You get written results before any abatement work is scoped or priced. If testing confirms asbestos-containing materials, the removal process follows a defined sequence that meets Mississippi's MDEQ notification and work-practice requirements.

Containment comes first. Affected areas are sealed with polyethylene sheeting and negative air pressure is established using HEPA-filtered air scrubbers, preventing fiber migration into the rest of the structure. Workers wear full personal protective equipment and follow strict decontamination protocols entering and leaving the work zone. Regulated waste is wetted, double-bagged in labeled 6-mil poly bags, and transported to a licensed disposal facility — Mississippi requires manifest documentation for every load, and we handle that paperwork on your behalf. Once removal is complete, a final air clearance test using phase contrast microscopy (PCM) confirms fiber levels are within acceptable limits before containment is removed and the space is returned to you.

Our team holds EPA Lead-Safe Certification, and the broader HomeLyft Restoration MS operation is an IICRC Certified Firm — credentials that matter when your insurance carrier or a prospective buyer's inspector is reviewing the job documentation.
