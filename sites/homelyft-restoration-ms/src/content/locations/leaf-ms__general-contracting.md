## Why Leaf Homes Present Unique Renovation Challenges

Jones County's climate is the starting point for almost every renovation conversation in Leaf. Summers push heat and humidity into the 90s for months at a stretch, and the area sees enough rainfall annually to keep soil moisture levels high year-round. That moisture works its way into crawl spaces, pier-and-beam foundations, and wall cavities — especially in homes built before modern vapor barriers and house wraps became standard practice.

A lot of the housing stock in and around Leaf dates to the mid-20th century. These homes were built well, but they weren't built with today's energy codes or moisture management strategies in mind. When you open up a wall for a kitchen remodel or a bathroom renovation, it's common to find original insulation that has compacted over decades, subflooring with soft spots from years of minor moisture exposure, or wiring that predates current electrical code. None of that is a reason to avoid the project — it's just a reason to work with a contractor who won't be surprised by it.

Mississippi's residential building code also requires permits for most structural work, electrical changes, and plumbing modifications. Jones County enforces these requirements, and skipping the permit process creates real problems at resale. Every remodel HomeLyft manages in Leaf is permitted and inspected properly.
