## Why Sandersville Properties See Water Damage Issues

Jones County's red clay soil is notoriously slow to absorb surface water. During heavy rainfall events — and this region averages over 55 inches of rain annually — that clay acts almost like a bowl, directing runoff toward the lowest point on a property, which is often a crawl space or basement perimeter. Homes in Sandersville that were built before modern vapor barrier standards are especially vulnerable: older pier-and-beam construction allows ground moisture to wick upward into subfloor assemblies, and by the time a homeowner notices soft spots or a musty smell, the damage has often been progressing for weeks.

Beyond soil and rainfall, aging plumbing is a consistent culprit. Many homes in the Sandersville area were built during mid-century construction booms when galvanized steel supply lines were standard. Those pipes corrode from the inside out, and a pinhole leak inside a wall cavity can saturate insulation and framing for months before it becomes visible. Municipal water pressure fluctuations — common during high-demand summer months — can accelerate that failure.
