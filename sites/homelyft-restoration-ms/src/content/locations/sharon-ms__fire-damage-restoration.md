## Why Sharon Properties See Distinct Fire Damage Challenges

Madison County sits in Mississippi's humid subtropical climate belt, and that humidity becomes an enemy the moment a fire is extinguished. Water from suppression — whether from a garden hose, a sprinkler system, or a fire department pumper — soaks into framing, insulation, and subfloors. In Sharon's summer months, ambient humidity can push 85–90%, which means wet, smoke-saturated materials don't just dry on their own. They swell, warp, and begin supporting secondary mold growth within 24 to 48 hours if no drying equipment is deployed. That overlap of fire damage and moisture intrusion is one of the most common complications we see on Sharon jobs, and it's why fire restoration here requires both FSRT and structural drying expertise working in tandem.

Older homes in the Sharon area — particularly those built before 1980 — often contain materials that complicate post-fire cleanup in ways newer builds don't. Vermiculite insulation, older asbestos-containing floor tiles, and lead-based paint are all possibilities in homes of that era. Disturbing fire-damaged materials without testing first can turn a restoration project into a regulatory problem. HomeLyft holds EPA Lead-Safe certification and coordinates pre-demolition testing when the age of the structure warrants it, keeping the project compliant with Mississippi DEQ guidelines before a single piece of drywall comes down.

## Our Fire Damage Restoration Process in Sharon

Every fire loss is different, but the sequence of work follows a proven framework calibrated to what we actually find on-site.

**Emergency board-up and stabilization** comes first. Broken windows, compromised roof decking, and open wall cavities need to be secured before anything else — both to protect the structure from weather and to prevent unauthorized entry. In Sharon's summer storm season, an unsecured roof opening can admit enough rain overnight to double the scope of damage.

**Smoke and soot mapping** follows. Soot travels farther than most homeowners expect — HVAC systems pull fine particles into ductwork and redistribute them to rooms that never saw flames. We use thermal imaging and air sampling to trace the full extent of contamination before any cleaning begins, so nothing gets missed behind a closed closet door or inside a return-air chase.

**Structural assessment** determines what can be cleaned and restored versus what needs to come out. Char depth, char type (surface vs. deep-set alligatoring), and material composition all factor into that decision. Salvaging original framing lumber where structurally sound is both cost-effective and — in older Sharon homes — sometimes preferable because matching period materials exactly isn't always possible.

**Odor neutralization** is the step homeowners feel most acutely. Hydroxyl generators and thermal fogging penetrate porous surfaces — wood, drywall, soft furnishings — to break down odor-causing compounds at the molecular level rather than masking them. A home that smells clean after restoration is the real test of whether the job was done correctly.

**Reconstruction** closes the project. Our team handles framing, drywall, painting, flooring, and finish carpentry under one roof, so you're not coordinating a separate general contractor after the remediation crew leaves.
