## Why St. Martin Properties See Sewage Backup Issues

Much of St. Martin's residential development spread rapidly in the decades following Hurricane Katrina, but the area also retains older housing stock with cast-iron drain lines that have corroded over time. Cast iron is prone to root intrusion and scale buildup — two of the most common causes of sewer line backups in the area. Compounding the problem, Jackson County's sandy-loam soil shifts seasonally, and that movement can stress lateral sewer lines where they connect to municipal mains along the lower-lying roads near the bayou corridors.

Septic systems are also common throughout St. Martin's less-densely served streets. After prolonged rain events — and the Gulf Coast averages well over 60 inches of rain annually — saturated drain fields lose their ability to absorb effluent, pushing raw sewage back through floor drains, toilets, and tub drains. Homeowners often discover the problem only after the damage is already spreading across bathroom tile and into adjacent hallways.

## Our Sewage Cleanup and Sanitization Process in St. Martin

Raw sewage is classified as Category 3 water — the most hazardous tier in the IICRC S500 standard — and the cleanup protocol reflects that. Our IICRC-certified team arrives with personal protective equipment, containment barriers, and truck-mounted extraction units before any assessment is complete. Here's how the process unfolds on a typical St. Martin job:

**Containment and hazard assessment.** We isolate the affected area to prevent cross-contamination to clean rooms. If the backup has reached HVAC registers or crawl space vents, those pathways are sealed immediately.

**Extraction and removal.** Standing sewage is extracted using high-capacity pumps. Porous materials — drywall, insulation, carpet, subfloor — that have contacted Category 3 water are removed rather than dried in place. There are no shortcuts here; the IICRC standard does not allow porous materials contaminated with raw sewage to be dried and retained.

**Antimicrobial treatment.** Once the structure is cleared of contaminated material, all affected surfaces are treated with EPA-registered disinfectants. We apply these in multiple passes, allowing appropriate dwell time between applications rather than rushing to a single spray-and-wipe.

**Structural drying.** After sanitization, our IICRC ASD-credentialed technicians deploy desiccant dehumidifiers and air movers calibrated to the ambient humidity levels typical of a coastal Mississippi interior — which run significantly higher than inland standards and require adjusted drying targets.

**Clearance documentation.** Before we close out, we provide written documentation of the affected materials, the scope of removal, and the sanitization methods used — records your insurance adjuster will need and that protect you if questions arise later.
