## Why Ellisville Homes Present Unique Renovation Challenges

Jones County's climate is not gentle on structures. Summers are long and wet, with relative humidity regularly pushing into the 80–90% range. That moisture works its way into crawl spaces, softens subfloor sheathing, and causes wood-framed walls to rack subtly over years. Older homes in Ellisville — many built in the 1950s through 1970s — were constructed with materials and techniques that predate modern moisture management standards. Pier-and-beam foundations, common in this part of Mississippi, can settle unevenly as the clay-heavy soil expands and contracts through wet and dry cycles.

For renovation work, that means you can't simply rip out a kitchen or bathroom and start fresh without first assessing what the structure underneath has been doing. Subfloor leveling, vapor barrier upgrades in crawl spaces, and updated flashing details around windows and rooflines are frequently part of a renovation scope here — not optional add-ons, but prerequisites for work that will actually hold up.
