## Why Bay St. Louis Properties Face Compounding Storm Damage

Hancock County's coastal position means Bay St. Louis regularly experiences conditions that inland Mississippi communities rarely see: sustained tropical-force winds, storm surge that can push several feet of saltwater across low-lying streets, and rainfall rates that overwhelm older drainage infrastructure in a matter of hours. That saltwater exposure is a detail that matters for restoration — salt residue left in wall assemblies and subfloor systems accelerates corrosion of fasteners and wiring, and it has to be flushed and neutralized, not just dried.

The housing stock here adds another layer of complexity. A significant share of Bay St. Louis homes were built before modern wind-resistance codes, with roof-to-wall connections that weren't engineered for the uplift pressures a Category 2 or 3 storm generates. Post-Katrina rebuilds are generally better anchored, but even those structures can suffer sheathing failures when a fast-moving outer band drops a large oak or pine across a ridgeline. Tree damage cleanup in this area routinely involves not just debris removal but assessment of the structural envelope beneath — because what looks like a surface puncture can conceal rafter damage, broken ridge boards, or compromised ceiling joists.

## Our Storm Damage Restoration Process in Bay St. Louis

The first step on every job is a methodical exterior and interior inspection — roof, fascia, soffits, windows, doors, and foundation perimeter — before any interior work begins. Temporary protective measures (tarping, board-up, emergency waterproofing) go up fast to stop active water intrusion while the full scope is being documented.

Once the envelope is stabilized, the interior assessment begins. Moisture mapping with thermal imaging and calibrated meters identifies water migration that isn't visible to the eye — saturated insulation behind intact drywall, wet subfloor under undamaged flooring, water tracking along ceiling joists from a single entry point. That documentation also becomes the foundation of the insurance claim file.

Structural drying follows the IICRC S500 and S520 standards, using commercial desiccant and refrigerant dehumidifiers sized to the actual cubic footage of the affected space, not a one-size approach. Affected materials that cannot be dried in place — saturated insulation, buckled sheathing, compromised flooring — are removed and documented before reconstruction begins. As a FORTIFIED Building–Roofing MS Windstorm Certified contractor, the rebuild phase can incorporate wind-resistance upgrades that may qualify the home for reduced insurance premiums going forward.
