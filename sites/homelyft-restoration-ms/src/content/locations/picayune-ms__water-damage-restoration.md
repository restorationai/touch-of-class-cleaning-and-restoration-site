Picayune sits in Pearl River County at the edge of the Pearl River basin, where the combination of Gulf-influenced humidity, heavy seasonal rainfall, and a water table that rises quickly after storms creates conditions that turn a slow leak into a structural problem faster than most homeowners expect. When water gets into a Picayune home — whether from a burst supply line, a backed-up drain, or storm-driven intrusion through a crawl space — the clock starts immediately. Mold can begin colonizing wet framing within 24 to 48 hours in Mississippi's heat, and standing water under flooring warps subfloor panels in days, not weeks.

## Why Picayune Properties See Water Damage Issues

Pearl River County's clay-heavy soils don't drain the way sandier coastal soils do. After a significant rain event, that clay holds saturation close to the surface, and hydrostatic pressure builds against crawl space piers and slab edges. Picayune's housing stock includes a large number of pier-and-beam homes built between the 1950s and 1980s — construction that was practical for the region's wet seasons but leaves crawl spaces vulnerable to groundwater intrusion when vapor barriers fail or footings settle. In those older homes, the first sign of a water problem is often a musty smell rising through the floor, not a visible puddle.

The city also sits along several low-lying corridors that collect runoff from US-11 and MS-43 during heavy rain. Properties near these drainage corridors can see water enter through foundation vents or around door thresholds even when the roof is intact. That kind of intrusion is easy to underestimate — the entry point looks minor, but water travels horizontally under vinyl flooring and behind baseboards before it's ever noticed.

## Our Water Damage Restoration Process in Picayune

The first step on every job is a moisture mapping inspection — we use thermal imaging cameras and calibrated moisture meters to trace where water has traveled, not just where it's visible. In Picayune's pier-and-beam homes, that often means inspecting the crawl space before touching anything inside the living area, because subfloor saturation from below is frequently worse than what's visible from the room above.

Once the scope is documented, water extraction begins with truck-mounted extraction units capable of pulling hundreds of gallons from carpet, padding, and subfloor assemblies. Structural drying follows using commercial-grade desiccant and refrigerant dehumidifiers paired with high-velocity air movers positioned to create directed airflow through wall cavities and under flooring. Drying is monitored with daily moisture readings and logged to IICRC S500 standards — the same documentation your insurance adjuster will want to see. HomeLyft Restoration MS holds IICRC certifications in Water Restoration Technician (WRT) and Applied Structural Drying (ASD), which means the drying plan is engineered, not estimated.

In homes with older construction, we also check for lead paint before any demolition of wet drywall or plaster — our EPA Lead-Safe certification means that step is handled correctly if the testing comes back positive.

## Picayune Insurance Coordination

Most homeowner policies in Mississippi cover sudden and accidental water damage, but the documentation requirements matter. ## Local Note

One thing that comes up repeatedly in Picayune's older neighborhoods is the presence of tongue-and-groove pine subfloor — a material common in pre-1970 construction across Pearl River County. Unlike modern OSB or plywood, solid pine subfloor doesn't delaminate, but it does cup and crown when saturated, and it releases moisture very slowly. Drying timelines for these assemblies routinely run 30 to 50 percent longer than a standard plywood subfloor, and aggressive heat-drying can cause additional cupping. We adjust airflow and dehumidification targets specifically for this material rather than applying a one-size drying protocol — otherwise the floor looks dry on the surface while moisture is still locked in the wood grain below.
