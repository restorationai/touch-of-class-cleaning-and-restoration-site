## Why Sharon Properties See Contents Damage Compound Quickly

Madison County's housing stock includes a meaningful number of older farmhouse-style homes and mid-century builds where interior finishes — solid wood cabinetry, hardwood floors, plaster-and-lath walls — absorb smoke and water differently than modern construction. When a fire or flood event hits one of these homes, the porous materials act like sponges, pulling contaminants deeper into furniture joints, fabric fibers, and decorative items. Smoke residue from a kitchen fire doesn't stay on the surface; within hours it migrates into upholstery foam and the grain of unfinished wood. In Sharon's climate, that process accelerates because warmth keeps odor compounds volatile and active longer.

Soil composition in this part of Madison County also matters. The heavy clay soils common to central Mississippi retain groundwater close to the surface after heavy rainfall, which means crawl-space flooding can push moisture up through subfloor structures and into the contents sitting on ground-level floors — rugs, furniture legs, storage boxes — before a homeowner even realizes the crawl space is wet.

## Our Contents Pack-Out and Storage Process in Sharon

When the HomeLyft Restoration MS team arrives at a Sharon property, the first priority is a room-by-room contents assessment.
