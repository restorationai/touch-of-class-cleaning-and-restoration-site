## Why McLain Properties See Water Damage Issues

Greene County's clay-heavy soils don't drain quickly. After a heavy rain event — and this region sees significant rainfall totals, especially during spring and late-summer storm seasons — that slow drainage puts hydrostatic pressure against foundation walls and slab edges. Homes built on pier-and-beam foundations, which are common throughout rural Greene County, are particularly vulnerable: water pools beneath the crawl space, humidity climbs, and subfloor materials begin to swell and delaminate before a homeowner even notices a problem upstairs.

Older housing stock in and around McLain often features original hardwood flooring and plaster-over-lath wall construction. Both materials absorb water at different rates than modern drywall and engineered wood, which means moisture mapping during the assessment phase is especially important here — a moisture meter reading that looks acceptable at the surface can mask saturation deeper in the wall assembly.

Private well and septic systems are common in this part of Greene County as well. A septic backup that introduces water into a home carries a different contamination category than a clean water supply-line break, and the remediation protocol — including the personal protective equipment used and the materials that must be removed rather than dried in place — changes accordingly.

## Our Water Damage Restoration Process in McLain

Every job starts with a thorough moisture assessment using thermal imaging cameras and calibrated moisture meters to find water that has migrated beyond the visible wet area. In Greene County homes with pier-and-beam construction, that often means inspecting the crawl space as a separate affected zone, not just the rooms above.

Once the scope is documented, water extraction begins using truck-mounted and portable extraction units capable of pulling standing water from carpet, subfloor gaps, and low-clearance crawl spaces. Structural drying follows immediately — desiccant and refrigerant dehumidifiers are positioned based on the square footage and the building's construction type, and air movers are placed to create the directional airflow patterns the IICRC S500 standard calls for.

Drying progress is logged daily with psychrometric readings. In McLain's climate, ambient outdoor humidity can work against the drying process if equipment is undersized or improperly staged, so we calibrate dehumidifier capacity to the actual grain depression needed — not just the room size. When materials cannot reach drying goals within the standard timeframe, the documentation supports a clear conversation with your insurance adjuster about what needs to come out.

## Local Note

In Greene County homes with pier-and-beam construction — a building style you see frequently in and around McLain — the crawl space vapor barrier condition matters as much as the drying equipment placed upstairs. If the existing vapor barrier is torn, missing in sections, or was never installed, ground moisture will continuously feed the crawl space environment and make it nearly impossible to hit drying targets in the subfloor above.
