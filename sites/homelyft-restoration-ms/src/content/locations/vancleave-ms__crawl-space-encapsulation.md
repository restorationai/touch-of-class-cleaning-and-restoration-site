## Why Vancleave Properties See Crawl Space Moisture Problems

Jackson County averages over 60 inches of rainfall annually, and Vancleave's position inland doesn't insulate it from the humidity that follows Gulf storms northward. Homes built on pier-and-beam foundations — a common construction method throughout this part of Mississippi — leave the underside of the house exposed to ground vapor that rises constantly from the soil. When outdoor humidity climbs above 70 or 80 percent in summer (which happens most days from May through September), that vapor has nowhere to go except into the wood framing above it.

The clay-heavy soils found in lower-lying areas near Vancleave also retain water longer after heavy rain events. That means the ground beneath a crawl space can stay saturated for days after a storm, pushing vapor upward well after the rain has stopped. Homeowners often notice the first signs not in the crawl space itself but in the living area above: floors that feel soft underfoot, doors that stick in summer, or a musty smell that no amount of cleaning seems to fix.

## Our Crawl Space Encapsulation Process in Vancleave

Every encapsulation job starts with a thorough inspection of the existing crawl space — not a quick glance from the access hatch, but a full walk-through (or crawl-through) to assess standing water, existing vapor barriers, insulation condition, wood moisture content, and any signs of mold or pest activity. Moisture readings are taken at multiple points across the floor joists before any materials go down.

Once the space is cleared and dried, we install a heavy-duty polyethylene liner — typically 20-mil reinforced material — across the entire ground surface and up the foundation walls, sealed at all seams and penetrations with moisture-resistant tape. In Vancleave's climate, leaving any gap in coverage is the same as not encapsulating at all; vapor will find the path of least resistance. Depending on the home's existing ventilation setup, we may recommend closing off crawl space vents entirely and conditioning the space with a dedicated dehumidifier, which is the approach most aligned with current building science for hot-humid climates like coastal Mississippi.

Our team is IICRC certified in water damage restoration and applied structural drying, which means the moisture assessment phase isn't guesswork — it follows documented standards for measuring and verifying dryness before encapsulation materials are sealed in place.

One thing that comes up specifically in Vancleave and the surrounding Jackson County areas: homes that were built in the 1970s and 1980s often have fiberglass batt insulation stapled between the floor joists rather than against the foundation walls.
