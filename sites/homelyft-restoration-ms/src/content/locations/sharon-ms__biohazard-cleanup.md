## Why Sharon Properties Present Unique Biohazard Challenges

Sharon sits in a part of central Mississippi where summer heat and persistent humidity are facts of life from April through October. That climate creates conditions where biological material degrades quickly, odors penetrate porous surfaces faster, and secondary contamination risks rise with every passing hour. Many homes in this part of Madison County were built decades ago, with wood-framed construction, older HVAC systems, and materials like subflooring, insulation, and plaster that absorb and hold contamination far more stubbornly than modern composites.

Older construction also means crawl spaces and pier-and-beam foundations are common in the area. When a biohazard event occurs on an upper floor or in a bathroom, gravity and structure can carry contamination downward into spaces that are difficult to access and harder to verify as clean without professional equipment. A visual inspection alone is never sufficient in these cases.

## Our Biohazard Cleanup Process in Sharon

Every job begins with a thorough assessment before any cleaning starts. Technicians identify the full scope of affected materials — visible and non-visible — and establish a containment perimeter to prevent cross-contamination to unaffected areas of the home or building. Personal protective equipment, negative air pressure where appropriate, and hospital-grade EPA-registered disinfectants are standard on every job, not optional upgrades.

Affected porous materials — carpet, padding, drywall, subfloor — are carefully removed, bagged, and transported as regulated biological waste in compliance with Mississippi Department of Environmental Quality guidelines. Disposal is not handled at a standard landfill; it follows the regulated medical and biohazardous waste stream. Once removal is complete, all surfaces are treated with appropriate antimicrobial agents, and the space is documented with photographs before any restoration work begins. Our team holds IICRC certification and is OSHA trained, which means the process follows recognized industry standards rather than improvised methods.
