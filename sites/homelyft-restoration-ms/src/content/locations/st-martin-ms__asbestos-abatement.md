## Why St. Martin Properties Face Elevated Asbestos Risk

The Gulf Coast climate is hard on buildings. Seasonal humidity cycles cause materials to expand and contract repeatedly, and in structures built between the 1940s and late 1970s, that stress lands directly on asbestos-containing materials that were never designed to flex indefinitely. St. Martin's housing stock includes a meaningful number of ranch-style and split-level homes from the 1960s and 1970s — exactly the era when asbestos use in residential construction peaked nationally. Popcorn ceilings applied with asbestos-bearing texture compound, vinyl floor tiles bonded with asbestos-containing mastic, and duct insulation wrapped in chrysotile are common finds during pre-renovation inspections in this area.

Post-hurricane repairs compound the problem. When a storm peels back roofing or forces water into wall cavities, it often disturbs materials that had been stable for decades. A roof repair that looks straightforward can become a regulated abatement project once a sample comes back positive — and in Jackson County, skipping that step isn't just a health risk, it's a liability.

## Our Asbestos Abatement Process in St. Martin

Every project starts with a formal inspection and bulk sampling before any removal work begins. Samples are sent to an accredited laboratory, and we don't proceed with abatement until results are in hand — not because it's required, but because guessing wrong creates more problems than it solves. Once confirmed, the affected area is isolated with negative air pressure containment using HEPA-filtered air scrubbers. Workers wear full PPE throughout, and all removed material is wetted, double-bagged in 6-mil poly, labeled per EPA requirements, and transported to a permitted disposal facility.

For popcorn ceiling asbestos removal — one of the more common requests we handle in this area — the containment setup is especially important because disturbed texture compound releases fibers easily. We work in sections, keep surfaces wet, and conduct a thorough visual inspection before clearance air sampling is performed. The EPA Lead-Safe certification our team carries reflects the same careful approach we apply to asbestos work: regulated materials require regulated processes, and shortcuts don't stay hidden.
