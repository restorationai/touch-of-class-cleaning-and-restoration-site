## Why Biloxi Upholstery Wears Differently

Harrison County sits in a humid subtropical zone with a long cooling season that keeps indoor air damp for much of the year, especially in homes and condos that rely on window units or older central systems rather than dedicated dehumidification. Fabric upholstery absorbs that ambient moisture, which is part of why musty odors show up in couches and recliners even when there's been no obvious spill. Add in fine beach sand that works into seams and cushion crevices, plus salt-laden air that can dull leather finishes over time, and it's a different wear pattern than you'd see in a drier inland climate. Vacation rentals and second homes near the beachfront see this fastest, since furniture often sits closed up between guest turnovers with little airflow.

## Our Upholstery Cleaning Process in Biloxi

We start by identifying the fabric or leather type, since a coastal home's furniture has often been reupholstered or replaced more recently than the national average due to storm and humidity wear, and that affects what cleaning method is safe. For fabric pieces, we pre-treat embedded sand and grit before hot water extraction, because pushing moisture through sand-laden fibers without loosening it first just grinds grit deeper into the weave. Leather gets a pH-balanced cleaner and conditioner suited to the salt exposure common on the coast, since standard household cleaners can strip the finish faster here than in drier climates. Because ambient humidity slows natural drying, we follow every job with controlled airflow, sometimes supplemented with a portable dehumidifier, so the piece isn't sitting damp for a day or two afterward inviting mildew.

## Equipment & Methods We Use for Upholstery

Hot water extraction remains our default for most fabric sofas and sectionals, since it pulls embedded soil and odor-causing residue out of the padding rather than just cleaning the surface. For delicate or older fabrics common in Biloxi's pre-storm-era homes, we use a lower-moisture steam method to avoid over-wetting a foam core that's already retaining coastal humidity. Leather upholstery, increasingly common in newer coastal builds, gets cleaned and conditioned rather than steamed, since heat and excess moisture can crack a finish that's already stressed by salt air.
