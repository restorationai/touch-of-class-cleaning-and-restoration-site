## Why State Line Properties See Distinct Storm Damage Patterns

The Greene County and Wayne County corridor that frames State Line carries a specific mix of older wood-frame homes, manufactured housing, and newer stick-built construction — each responding to severe weather differently. Older homes in the area often have original roof sheathing fastened with smooth-shank nails rather than ring-shank or screws, which means high-wind events can lift decking before the shingles show any visible distress. That's a problem homeowners don't discover until water is already inside the wall cavity.

The region also sits in a corridor that channels tornado-producing supercells tracking northeast out of Louisiana and southern Mississippi. Tree canopy is dense here — mature pines and hardwoods are common on residential lots — and when those trees fail in a wind event, the damage tends to be concentrated and severe rather than widespread and shallow. A single large pine through a roof can expose hundreds of square feet of interior to rain within minutes.

Soil saturation is another factor. The clay-heavy soils common in this part of Mississippi don't drain quickly after heavy rain, which means water pools against foundations and crawl space vents longer than it would in sandier coastal soils. That sustained contact accelerates moisture intrusion into subfloor assemblies.

## Our Storm Damage Restoration Process in State Line

When we arrive on a storm-damaged property in State Line, the first priority is stopping active intrusion before assessment begins. That typically means emergency tarping of compromised roof sections and boarding windows or doors that have been breached — work that needs to happen before any documentation or drying equipment is staged.

Once the structure is stabilized, we conduct a full moisture mapping survey using thermal imaging and calibrated moisture meters. In homes with crawl spaces — common throughout this part of Wayne County — we check subfloor moisture levels independently from the living space above, because water that enters through foundation vents during a storm can saturate floor joists without leaving any visible sign on interior flooring.

Structural drying follows the IICRC S500 and S520 standards, using commercial-grade desiccant and refrigerant dehumidifiers paired with high-velocity air movers. We log temperature, relative humidity, and material moisture readings at each visit so the drying record is complete for your insurance file. If tree impact has compromised structural members, we coordinate with licensed contractors for framing repair before closing out the restoration scope.
