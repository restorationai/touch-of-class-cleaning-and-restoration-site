## Our Emergency Board-Up and Tarping Process in Latimer

When we arrive at a Latimer property, the first step is a rapid exterior assessment — we walk the roofline, check every ground-floor and accessible upper-floor opening, and photograph the full scope before touching anything. That documentation matters for insurance claims and for our own sequencing decisions.

For roof tarping, we use heavy-duty polyethylene tarps rated for wind resistance, secured with 2x4 cap strips screwed into the decking rather than simply draped and weighted. This method holds under the kind of gusts that Harrison County sees during a fast-moving storm cell and satisfies the requirements of Mississippi's FORTIFIED building standards — a program HomeLyft is certified under for windstorm work. For fire-damaged or structurally compromised roofs, we assess load-bearing capacity before any crew member steps onto the surface.

Board-up on windows and doors uses 5/8-inch plywood cut to fit each opening, secured with structural screws into the framing rather than the siding or brick veneer. On older homes common to this part of Harrison County, we take extra care to locate the actual stud and header locations before driving fasteners — the framing layout in mid-century construction isn't always where you'd expect it.

Once the structure is secured, we provide written documentation of every opening addressed, the materials used, and the installation method — formatted to support a standard homeowner's insurance claim.

## Local Note

One thing we've observed on Harrison County properties with older wood-shake or dimensional-shingle roofs: the decking underneath is sometimes spaced — boards with gaps between them rather than continuous plywood — which was standard practice in Mississippi construction through the mid-twentieth century.
