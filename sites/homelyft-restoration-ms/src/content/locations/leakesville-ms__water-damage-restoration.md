## Why Leakesville Properties See Water Damage Issues

Leakesville's position in the Chickasawhay River watershed means the surrounding land holds moisture long after a storm passes. Properties on lower-lying lots — common throughout Greene County — can experience groundwater intrusion even when the source isn't a plumbing failure. Saturated soil presses against foundation walls and crawl space vapor barriers, and older homes built on pier-and-beam foundations (a standard construction method across much of rural Mississippi through the mid-20th century) are especially vulnerable. Crawl spaces in these homes often lack modern encapsulation, and standing water underneath a structure can wick upward into subfloor sheathing and floor joists for weeks before a homeowner notices soft spots underfoot or a persistent musty smell.

The region also experiences intense convective thunderstorms from late spring through early fall, and the combination of high wind, heavy rainfall, and aging roof stock means water intrusion from above is just as common as plumbing failures. A compromised roof flashing or a cracked soffit can direct hundreds of gallons into wall cavities during a single storm.

## Our Water Damage Restoration Process in Leakesville

Every job begins with a thorough moisture mapping inspection — we use thermal imaging cameras and calibrated moisture meters to trace water migration beyond what's visible to the eye. Water follows framing cavities, travels along subfloor decking, and pools in areas that look dry from the surface. Once we've mapped the affected zones, we extract standing water using truck-mounted and portable extraction units before positioning drying equipment.

Structural drying in Greene County's climate requires more aggressive dehumidification than in drier regions. We calculate the number and placement of commercial-grade desiccant and refrigerant dehumidifiers based on the specific cubic footage and material types in your home — not a one-size number. Air movers are positioned to create directed airflow across wet surfaces, and we monitor temperature, relative humidity, and material moisture content daily until readings meet the drying goals established by the IICRC S500 standard. For homes with pier-and-beam construction, we treat the crawl space as a separate drying chamber, which often requires dedicated equipment positioned below the floor system.
