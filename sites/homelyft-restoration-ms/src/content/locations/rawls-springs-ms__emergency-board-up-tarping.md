## Why Rawls Springs Properties Are Especially Vulnerable After Structural Damage

Forrest County sits squarely in Mississippi's severe-weather corridor. Thunderstorm cells that spin up along the Pine Belt can drop baseball-sized hail, produce straight-line winds above 70 mph, and move on within 20 minutes — leaving roofs open to a sky that may dump another inch of rain before midnight. The housing stock in and around Rawls Springs leans toward wood-frame construction, much of it built in eras before modern sheathing standards, which means a single missing section of roof decking can expose tongue-and-groove boards or older skip sheathing directly to the elements. Those materials absorb water at a rate that modern OSB simply doesn't, and once they're saturated, drying timelines stretch significantly.

Fire damage compounds the problem. A kitchen fire that vents through a soffit or attic can leave a home structurally sound but with a gaping hole that invites both weather and opportunistic entry. Broken windows from storm debris or a vehicle impact create similar exposure. Every hour that opening stays unprotected is an hour that rain, insects, and unauthorized entry can deepen the loss.

## Our Board-Up and Tarping Process in Rawls Springs

When we arrive at a property, the first step is a rapid exterior assessment — identifying every breach, ranking them by exposure risk, and sequencing the work so the largest openings are sealed first. For roof tarping, we use commercial-grade polyethylene tarps (minimum 6-mil, typically heavier for Forrest County storm work) anchored with lumber battens screwed into sound decking rather than simply draped and weighted. A tarp that lifts in the next wind event is not a solution.

Window and door board-up uses 3/4-inch plywood cut to fit each opening, secured with structural screws into the surrounding framing — not just the trim. On older homes common to this part of Forrest County, we probe the surrounding framing before fastening to confirm it can hold load; if the fire or impact has weakened the adjacent structure, we adapt the anchor points accordingly. Every opening is photographed before and after closure for your insurance documentation.

For fire-damaged properties, we coordinate the board-up with the sequence of smoke and soot work that follows — meaning we don't seal interior spaces in ways that trap odor or prevent ventilation needed for the next phase of remediation. The team holds IICRC FSRT certification, so the board-up decisions are made with the full restoration picture in mind, not just the immediate closure.

## Local Note

One pattern we've seen repeatedly on wood-frame homes in the Rawls Springs area: older roof structures often use lighter rafter sizing than modern code requires, and when a storm removes multiple shingles in a concentrated area, the exposed decking can begin to cup or warp within 24 to 48 hours in summer heat.
