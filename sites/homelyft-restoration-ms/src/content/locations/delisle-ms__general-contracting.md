## Why DeLisle Homes Have Distinct Renovation Needs

Harrison County's Gulf Coast climate is the defining factor in almost every renovation decision made in DeLisle. Average annual humidity regularly pushes above 75%, and the area sits within a high-wind zone that influences both material selection and code compliance. Homes built before the 1990s — and there are many in this part of the county — often predate modern moisture-resistant sheathing standards, meaning that once you open up a wall for a remodel, you're likely to find original framing that has absorbed decades of coastal air. That's not a dealbreaker; it's a planning factor.

Soil movement is another consideration that's easy to overlook until a slab or pier-and-beam foundation starts showing its age. The sandy, poorly consolidated soils common in this stretch of Harrison County can shift subtly after heavy rain events, and those shifts show up as sticking doors, cracked tile grout, and windows that no longer seal cleanly — all of which become visible the moment you start a bathroom or kitchen remodel. Addressing the symptom without understanding the cause is how renovations get expensive fast.

Post-2005 construction in the area also reflects post-Katrina code revisions, which means homes built or substantially rebuilt after that period have different structural anchoring requirements, elevated floor heights in some flood zones, and wind-load specifications that affect everything from roofing materials to window selection. Knowing which code era a home falls into before the first nail is pulled saves significant time and money.

## Local Note: Coastal Framing and the Humidity Window

One thing that catches out-of-area contractors in DeLisle is the narrow window for certain finish work during the summer months. When outdoor humidity is consistently above 80% — which is most of June through September here — interior paint, caulk, and some adhesives cure more slowly and are more prone to adhesion failure if applied before the substrate has been properly conditioned. Experienced local crews know to run dehumidification inside the work area before finish coats go on, even when the space looks and feels dry. It's a small step that prevents callbacks and protects the quality of the finished product in a climate that doesn't forgive shortcuts.
