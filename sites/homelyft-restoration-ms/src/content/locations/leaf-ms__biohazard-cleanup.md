## Why Leaf Properties Present Specific Biohazard Remediation Challenges

Jones County's climate is not forgiving. Summer humidity regularly pushes into the upper 80th percentile, and even in cooler months, interior surfaces in older homes stay damp enough to accelerate the spread of biological material into porous substrates — subfloor planking, plaster, insulation, and wall cavities. Biohazard contamination does not stay on the surface it lands on. In homes built before the 1980s, which make up a significant portion of Leaf's residential housing stock, materials like tongue-and-groove hardwood flooring and older drywall compounds absorb fluids quickly and deeply.

That absorption is the central challenge. A situation that looks contained on the surface often requires careful assessment of what lies beneath — and in Jones County's older homes, "beneath" can mean crawl spaces with minimal vapor barriers, subflooring that has already seen decades of humidity cycling, and insulation that was never designed to resist biological infiltration. Proper remediation means addressing all of it, not just what is visible.

## Our Biohazard Cleanup Process in Leaf

Every job begins with a thorough assessment before any cleaning starts. Our IICRC-certified team evaluates the full scope of affected materials — surfaces, porous substrates, HVAC pathways, and adjacent areas that may have been impacted.
