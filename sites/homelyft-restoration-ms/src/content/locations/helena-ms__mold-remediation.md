## Why Helena Properties See Mold Issues

The Delta's climate is the dominant factor. Helena experiences hot, humid summers with average relative humidity that keeps building materials damp far longer than in drier climates — and mold colonizes porous materials like wood framing, drywall paper, and insulation when moisture content stays elevated for more than a day or two. Many homes in Helena were constructed in the mid-20th century or earlier, with pier-and-beam foundations and crawl spaces that have limited ventilation. Those crawl spaces trap ground moisture, and without proper encapsulation, that vapor migrates upward into living areas.

Flood-prone low-lying areas of Helena near the Mississippi River add another layer of risk. Even after surface water recedes, sub-floor cavities and wall cavities can hold moisture for weeks. Helena's older housing stock also tends to have original plaster walls in interior rooms — plaster absorbs water more slowly than modern drywall but releases it even more slowly, creating a long window during which mold can establish itself deep inside the wall assembly before any visible sign appears on the surface.

## Our Mold Remediation Process in Helena

Every project begins with a thorough visual inspection and moisture mapping. We use thermal imaging cameras and calibrated moisture meters to find hidden wet zones — especially important in Helena homes where plaster walls and original hardwood subfloors can mask elevated moisture readings that a surface check would miss.

Once we've identified the affected area, we establish negative-air containment using 6-mil poly barriers and HEPA-filtered air scrubbers. This keeps mold spores from migrating to unaffected rooms during the removal process — a step that matters especially in open-floor-plan renovations of older Helena properties where there may be fewer natural barriers between spaces.

Contaminated materials are removed, bagged, and disposed of per Mississippi Department of Environmental Quality guidelines. Structural surfaces are then treated and cleaned using EPA-registered antimicrobial agents. Our IICRC-certified team (holding the Applied Microbial Remediation Technician credential) dries the affected cavity to confirmed low-moisture readings before any reconstruction begins. We do not close walls until moisture content is at an acceptable baseline — a step that prevents the cycle of remediation followed by rapid re-growth that some Helena homeowners have experienced after less thorough prior work.
