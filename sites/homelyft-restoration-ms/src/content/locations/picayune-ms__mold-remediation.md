## Why Picayune Properties See Mold Issues

Pearl River County's climate is the primary driver. Picayune averages more than 60 inches of rainfall per year, and the soil here — a mix of sandy loam and clay — drains unevenly. After heavy rain, water pools against foundations and wicks into crawl spaces, especially in the ranch-style and pier-and-beam homes that make up a significant portion of the city's residential housing stock. Many of these homes were built in the 1960s through 1980s, before modern vapor barriers and moisture-resistant building materials were standard practice. Fiberglass batt insulation in those older crawl spaces acts like a sponge once humidity climbs, and once mold takes hold in insulation, the material typically has to come out entirely.

Air conditioning systems also play a role. In Picayune's heat, HVAC units run hard for seven or eight months a year. ## Our Mold Remediation Process in Picayune
