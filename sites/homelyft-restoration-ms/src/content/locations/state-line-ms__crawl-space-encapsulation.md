## Why State Line Properties See Crawl Space Moisture Problems

Greene County's clay-heavy soil holds water long after a rain event passes. That retained moisture evaporates slowly upward through unprotected crawl space floors, a process called vapor drive, and it doesn't need a plumbing failure or a flood to cause real damage. Over months and years, that steady vapor load raises wood moisture content, invites mold colonization — which can begin within 48 to 72 hours on wet framing — and degrades insulation batts until they sag and lose R-value entirely.

The region's warm, humid summers compound the issue. When outdoor air at 90°F and 80% relative humidity enters a vented crawl space, it meets cooler surfaces and deposits condensation directly on joists, subfloor sheathing, and any ductwork running through the space. Homes built before modern building codes were widely adopted in rural Mississippi are especially vulnerable because many were constructed with minimal vapor barriers — or none at all — and rely on passive foundation vents that actually worsen moisture intrusion under humid-climate conditions.

## Our Crawl Space Encapsulation Process in State Line

Every encapsulation project starts with a thorough crawl space inspection: moisture readings at multiple points across the floor system, a check for standing water or evidence of past intrusion, assessment of existing insulation and any HVAC equipment in the space, and documentation of current vent configuration. That baseline shapes every decision that follows.

Once the space is prepped — debris removed, any existing damaged insulation pulled — we install a heavy-mil reinforced polyethylene liner across the ground and up the foundation walls, sealed at seams and at penetrations like piers and plumbing runs. The liner is mechanically fastened and tape-sealed so it functions as a continuous barrier, not a patchwork. Depending on the home's layout and the severity of the moisture load, we may also recommend a crawl space dehumidifier sized to the cubic footage of the space, which actively manages humidity year-round rather than relying on passive ventilation that doesn't work in Mississippi's climate.

Foundation vents are sealed as part of the process when we convert to a conditioned or semi-conditioned crawl space model — a step that's particularly effective in this region because it eliminates the pathway for humid outdoor air to enter. The result is a clean, dry space that protects your floor system, your insulation, and the air quality throughout the home above.

## Equipment and Methods We Use for Crawl Space Encapsulation

The liner material matters significantly in a high-humidity climate. We use a reinforced, puncture-resistant polyethylene membrane rated for long-term ground contact — not the thin sheeting available at hardware stores. One thing our crews have observed in older homes throughout the State Line corridor: many were built with pier-and-beam foundations that have settled unevenly over decades, leaving low spots in the crawl space floor where water pools after heavy rain rather than draining away.
