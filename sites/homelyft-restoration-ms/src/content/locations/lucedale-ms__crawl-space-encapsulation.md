## Why Lucedale Properties See Crawl Space Moisture Problems

George County sits in the humid subtropical belt where annual rainfall routinely exceeds 60 inches. That moisture doesn't just run off — a significant portion percolates into the sandy, well-drained soils common throughout the Lucedale area and then evaporates upward through exposed crawl space dirt floors. Older homes built before modern vapor barrier codes became standard are especially vulnerable: the original builders often left crawl spaces vented and open, which worked reasonably well in drier climates but actively draws humid outdoor air into the space during Mississippi summers.

Wood-destroying organisms — fungal rot, mold colonies, and wood-boring insects — thrive in the 70–90% relative humidity levels that unencapsulated crawl spaces in this region routinely reach between May and September. By the time a homeowner notices soft spots in the floor or a musty smell drifting up through HVAC returns, the damage has often been progressing for months. Early encapsulation is almost always cheaper than the structural repairs that follow delayed action.

## Our Crawl Space Encapsulation Process in Lucedale

Every encapsulation job starts with a crawl space inspection that documents current moisture readings, visible mold or rot, pest evidence, and the condition of any existing vapor barrier. In Lucedale homes, we frequently find partial or deteriorated plastic sheeting that was installed decades ago — material that has torn, shifted, or simply degraded to the point where it provides almost no protection.

Once we have a clear picture, the work follows a defined sequence:

1. **Remove and dispose of old vapor barrier material** and any debris that has accumulated on the crawl space floor.
2. **Address standing moisture sources** — if there is active water intrusion or drainage issues, those are corrected before any barrier goes down.
3. **Install a heavy-duty reinforced polyethylene encapsulation membrane** (typically 12–20 mil) across the floor and up the foundation walls, sealed at all seams and penetrations.
4. **Seal foundation vents** where the encapsulation strategy calls for a closed system, reducing the exchange of humid outdoor air.
5. **Install or assess a crawl space dehumidifier** sized to the cubic footage of the space, with a drain line routed to daylight or a condensate pump.
6. **Final moisture readings** confirm the system is performing before we close out.

In George County's climate, we consistently recommend the closed-system approach over traditional vented crawl spaces — the science on this has shifted considerably in the last decade, and Mississippi's humidity levels make vented systems difficult to manage effectively.
