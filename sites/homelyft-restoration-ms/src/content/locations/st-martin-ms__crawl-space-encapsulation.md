St. Martin sits in a stretch of Jackson County where the Gulf Coast humidity doesn't just hang in the air — it works its way into the ground beneath your home. Crawl spaces here face a combination of high ambient moisture, clay-heavy soil that holds water after heavy rain, and warm temperatures that rarely drop low enough to dry things out on their own. If you've noticed a musty smell drifting up through your floors, or if your hardwood is starting to cup or squeak, the problem is almost certainly brewing below the subfloor, not above it.

## Why St. Martin Properties Are Especially Vulnerable

The Gulf Coast climate doesn't give crawl spaces much of a break. St. Martin averages over 60 inches of rainfall per year, and the soil composition in this part of Jackson County tends to retain that moisture rather than drain it away. When saturated ground sits directly beneath an open crawl space, evaporation pushes that humidity upward into floor joists, insulation, and eventually your living space.

The housing stock in St. Martin adds another layer of complexity. Many homes in this area were built in the 1970s through 1990s with minimal crawl space ventilation — a design approach that made sense in drier climates but creates a slow-motion moisture problem here. Fiberglass batt insulation stapled between joists acts like a sponge in these conditions, losing its R-value and becoming a surface where mold can take hold within 48 to 72 hours of sustained dampness. An IICRC AMRT-certified assessment can identify exactly how far that process has progressed before encapsulation work begins.

## Our Crawl Space Encapsulation Process in St. Martin

Encapsulation in this climate isn't just laying plastic sheeting on dirt. The process starts with a thorough inspection of the crawl space — measuring moisture levels in the wood, checking for standing water or soil saturation, and identifying any existing mold growth that needs to be addressed before sealing the space.

Once the space is cleared and dried, we install a reinforced vapor barrier — typically a 20-mil liner — across the floor and up the foundation walls, sealed at every seam and penetration point. In St. Martin's conditions, we also evaluate whether a crawl space dehumidifier is warranted to manage residual humidity after encapsulation. In many cases here, it is. Vents are sealed to prevent outside humid air from re-entering, and drainage matting may be added in areas where seasonal groundwater intrusion is a known pattern. The goal is a sealed, conditioned environment that stops the moisture cycle rather than just slowing it.

## What Encapsulation Costs and Whether Insurance Applies

Crawl space encapsulation is generally considered a preventive improvement rather than a covered loss event, which means most standard homeowners' policies in Mississippi won't pay for it outright. However, if moisture damage or mold in your crawl space resulted from a covered peril — a plumbing leak, storm-driven water intrusion, or a failed sump — the remediation portion of the work may be claimable. Our team documents conditions thoroughly, photographs affected materials, and can provide the scope-of-work detail your adjuster needs to evaluate what's covered.
