## Why Sharon Properties Are Particularly Vulnerable

Madison County's soil profile leans heavily toward expansive clay, which holds moisture rather than draining it away. After a heavy rain — and Sharon gets its share, typically 55 to 60 inches annually — that saturated clay sits against foundation walls and beneath crawl space floors for days. Vented crawl spaces, which make up the majority of the housing stock in this area, were designed for a drier climate logic that simply doesn't apply here. When outdoor air at 90 percent relative humidity is vented into a crawl space, it cools against the concrete block walls and condenses. You end up with more moisture inside the crawl space than outside.

Older homes in the Sharon area — many built in the 1960s through 1980s with pier-and-beam or block foundations — often have original vapor barriers that have degraded into torn plastic sheeting that does almost nothing. If your home is in that era, there's a reasonable chance whatever barrier exists is no longer functioning as one.

## Our Crawl Space Encapsulation Process in Sharon

Encapsulation in Sharon isn't a one-size process. Before any material goes down, the crawl space gets a thorough inspection: we assess standing water or moisture intrusion points, check for existing mold or wood rot on joists and beams, and evaluate whether the existing vents should be sealed as part of the project. In Madison County's climate, closing off foundation vents and conditioning the crawl space is often the more effective long-term approach, but that decision depends on your specific foundation type and HVAC configuration.

Once the space is prepped and any remediation needs are addressed, we install a heavy-duty reinforced polyethylene liner — typically 20-mil — across the floor and up the foundation walls, sealed at all seams and penetrations. Drainage matting goes down first in areas where moisture intrusion from the soil is active. A properly installed encapsulation system also includes a dehumidifier sized to the cubic footage of your specific crawl space, because a sealed space without active humidity control can still accumulate moisture from internal sources.

The IICRC AMRT certification our team carries means we're trained to identify mold conditions before they get sealed under a liner — a step that matters enormously in a climate like Sharon's, where mold can colonize wood framing within 48 to 72 hours of a moisture event.
