## Why Wiggins Homes See Different Upholstery Challenges

Wiggins sits along the US 49 corridor between Hattiesburg and the coast, in a stretch of Stone County that runs up against the De Soto National Forest. That proximity to timberland means a lot of households deal with pine pollen in spring, red clay and mud tracked in from wooded yards, and pet dander from working and hunting dogs that spend real time outdoors. All of that settles into fabric weave and foam padding faster than it would in a purely suburban setting. Add the region's typical summer humidity, and upholstery here holds onto moisture, odor, and allergens longer than furniture in a drier climate would. Older frame homes around the county, many built before central air was standard, also tend to have less consistent airflow through living spaces, which slows natural drying between cleanings.

## Our Upholstery Cleaning Process in Wiggins

We start by identifying the fabric or leather type, because Stone County households run the gamut from farmhouse-style cotton blends to leather recliners that have seen a lot of use. Microfiber and cotton blends typically get a low-moisture steam extraction that lifts embedded soil and dust without oversaturating foam that's already fighting humidity. Leather upholstery gets a different approach entirely: a pH-balanced cleaner followed by conditioning, since leather that dries out in this climate's temperature swings is prone to cracking at the seams. For pet-heavy households, which are common here, we pretreat for dander and odor before extraction rather than trying to mask it afterward. Drying time matters more in Wiggins than in drier parts of the state, so we use airflow equipment to speed evaporation and keep cushions from sitting damp overnight.
