Rawls Springs sits in Forrest County where the Gulf Coast's humid subtropical climate collides with the inland hill country, producing a storm season that runs longer and hits harder than many Mississippi communities expect. Supercell thunderstorms roll up from the south, tornadoes track through the Hattiesburg metro corridor, and late-season tropical systems push rain bands deep inland — leaving roofs, siding, and crawl spaces saturated long after the skies clear. When a storm tears through your property, the clock starts immediately: standing water under a home's pier-and-beam foundation or inside a compromised attic can begin supporting mold growth within 24 to 48 hours.

## Why Rawls Springs Properties See Elevated Storm Damage Risk

Forrest County's red-clay soils drain slowly, which means water from a heavy storm event doesn't disappear — it pools against foundation walls, seeps under slab edges, and backs up into low-lying crawl spaces. Many homes in the Rawls Springs area were built during the postwar construction boom of the 1950s through 1970s, a period when roofing materials and wall sheathing were not engineered to the wind-load standards that Mississippi now enforces under the International Residential Code. That older building stock means a single 70-mph gust can lift ridge caps, crack soffit boards, and drive water into wall cavities that have no modern vapor barrier to slow the intrusion.

The tree canopy here is another factor. Mature hardwoods — water oaks, sweetgums, and loblolly pines — are beautiful until a tornado-warned storm snaps one at the trunk. Tree strikes on roofs and exterior walls are among the most common calls we receive after a Forrest County severe weather event, and they compound quickly: a breached roof deck exposed to Mississippi humidity deteriorates structurally within days, not weeks.

## Our Storm Damage Restoration Process in Rawls Springs

Every job starts with a systematic damage assessment before a single tool is unloaded. We photograph and document every affected surface — roof decking, wall assemblies, flooring, insulation — using moisture meters and thermal imaging cameras to find water that has already migrated beyond the visible breach. That documentation matters for your insurance claim as much as it matters for our scope of work.

Once the scope is set, emergency tarping and board-up secures the structure against additional weather intrusion. For tree-strike damage, we coordinate debris removal and temporary structural shoring before moving to interior drying. Industrial air movers and desiccant dehumidifiers are deployed in a configuration calibrated to the specific materials involved — the older plank sheathing common in mid-century Rawls Springs homes holds moisture differently than OSB, and drying protocols are adjusted accordingly. Our team holds IICRC Water Damage Restoration Technician and Applied Structural Drying certifications, and all drying is tracked against the IICRC S500 standard so there is a defensible, documented record for your insurer.

Reconstruction — from replacing storm-damaged roofing to reframing a wall section — is handled under the same project umbrella, which eliminates the coordination gap between mitigation and rebuild that often delays homeowners working with two separate contractors.
