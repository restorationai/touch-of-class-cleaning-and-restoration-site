## Why Latimer Properties See Sewage Backup Issues

Harrison County's soil composition plays a bigger role in sewer line health than most homeowners realize. The expansive clay that underlies much of the Latimer area swells when saturated and contracts during dry spells, creating lateral movement that stresses older clay-tile and cast-iron drain lines over time. When those pipes crack or belly — meaning they sag and hold standing waste instead of draining — backups into the lowest fixture in the home become a recurring problem rather than a one-time event.

Latimer also sits in a region where Gulf Coast rainfall can be intense and concentrated. A fast-moving storm can overwhelm municipal collection systems, creating surcharge conditions where the public sewer main actually pushes back through private laterals. Homes on lower-lying lots or those whose cleanout access points have settled below grade are especially vulnerable. Septic systems serving properties on the outskirts of the Latimer area face their own pressure: saturated drain fields during wet seasons lose absorption capacity, and when the field can't accept effluent, it has nowhere to go but back toward the structure.

## Our Sewage Cleanup and Sanitization Process in Latimer

Raw sewage is classified as Category 3 — "black water" — under the IICRC S500 standard, meaning it carries pathogens that require a different response than a clean-water pipe burst. The process is methodical and can't be rushed without leaving contamination behind.

First, the source is confirmed and isolated. If the backup originated from a sewer line failure, we coordinate with the property owner to have the line inspected or cleared before cleanup begins — reintroducing effluent mid-job defeats the work. Once the source is controlled, standing sewage is extracted using truck-mounted and portable extraction units capable of handling solids-laden water that would clog standard wet-vac equipment.

All porous materials that absorbed contaminated water — drywall below the flood line, carpet, pad, insulation — are removed and bagged for disposal per Mississippi DEQ guidelines. Hard surfaces are cleaned in multiple passes with EPA-registered disinfectants, and structural cavities are treated before any drying equipment is placed. HEPA air scrubbers run continuously during the remediation phase to capture aerosolized contaminants. The team holds IICRC AMRT certification, which matters here because sewage events frequently trigger secondary mold colonization within 24 to 48 hours in Mississippi's humidity — catching that risk early is part of the scope, not an add-on.
