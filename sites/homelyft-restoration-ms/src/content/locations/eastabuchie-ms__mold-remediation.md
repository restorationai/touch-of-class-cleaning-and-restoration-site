## Why Eastabuchie Properties See Mold Issues

Jones County's climate is unforgiving when it comes to indoor moisture control. Eastabuchie's tree canopy and shaded lots — common on rural residential parcels off Highway 11 — limit sunlight exposure to exterior walls and crawl spaces, keeping building materials damp longer after rain. Many homes in this area were built in the 1960s and 1970s with pier-and-beam foundations, which means the crawl space underneath is a direct pathway for ground moisture to migrate into floor joists and subfloor sheathing. Once that wood reaches a sustained moisture content above 19%, mold has everything it needs.

Roof penetrations from satellite dishes, aging HVAC ductwork that sweats in summer, and bathroom exhaust fans that vent into attic spaces rather than outside are recurring contributors we find on service calls in this part of Jones County. These aren't code violations in older construction — they're simply how homes were built — but they create chronic moisture loading that feeds long-term mold growth.

## Our Mold Remediation Process in Eastabuchie

Every job starts with a moisture mapping inspection. We use thermal imaging cameras and calibrated moisture meters to find wet building materials behind walls and under floors before a single piece of drywall is removed. This step matters especially in pier-and-beam homes, where moisture often tracks horizontally through floor framing well beyond the visible stain.

Once we've mapped the affected area, we establish negative-air containment — plastic sheeting, zipper doors, and HEPA-filtered air scrubbers running continuously — so that disturbing moldy materials doesn't spread spores to unaffected rooms. Our IICRC AMRT-certified technicians then remove contaminated porous materials (drywall, insulation, affected wood) following the IICRC S520 standard, treat structural framing with EPA-registered antimicrobial agents, and dry the cavity to documented moisture targets before reconstruction begins. We are also EPA Lead-Safe Certified, which is relevant in Jones County homes built before 1978, where disturbing walls can expose lead-based paint alongside mold.

Final clearance involves a post-remediation verification — surface and air sampling to confirm spore counts have returned to normal background levels — before we sign off and close the containment.
