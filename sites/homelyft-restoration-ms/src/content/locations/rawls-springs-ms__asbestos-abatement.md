## Why Rawls Springs Properties Are More Likely to Contain Asbestos

Mississippi's Gulf South building culture leaned heavily on materials that were cheap, fire-resistant, and humidity-tolerant — and asbestos checked every box. Homes built between roughly 1940 and 1980 in communities like Rawls Springs frequently incorporated asbestos in vinyl floor tiles (especially the 9×9-inch squares common in kitchens and utility rooms), duct insulation around central HVAC systems, joint compound, and the spray-applied acoustic texture on ceilings that many homeowners know as popcorn ceilings. The region's persistent heat and humidity also meant that pipe wrap and boiler insulation in older homes were often asbestos-reinforced to resist condensation and thermal cycling — materials that can look perfectly intact but become friable when disturbed during a remodel or after water intrusion.

Forrest County's housing stock skews older than statewide averages in many pockets, and Rawls Springs is no exception. That means a bathroom gut, a window replacement, or even aggressive sanding of old floor adhesive can release fibers without any visible warning sign. The safest first step is always a professional asbestos inspection before demolition work begins.

## Our Asbestos Abatement Process in Rawls Springs

Every abatement project starts with sampling, not assumptions. Our team collects material samples from suspected areas — ceiling texture, floor tile and mastic, drywall joint compound, pipe insulation, attic vermiculite — and submits them to an accredited laboratory. Results typically return within a few business days, and we walk you through what was found in plain language before any remediation plan is written.

When abatement is confirmed, we establish a regulated work area using polyethylene sheeting and negative air pressure units equipped with HEPA filtration. All personnel working inside the containment zone wear full PPE including respirators rated for asbestos fibers. Asbestos-containing materials are wetted to suppress fiber release, carefully removed, double-bagged in labeled waste containers, and transported to a licensed disposal facility — Mississippi DEQ requirements govern every step of that chain of custody.

For popcorn ceiling asbestos removal specifically, we take extra care with overhead work because gravity pulls disturbed material downward and can spread contamination across a room quickly if containment is not sealed correctly. After removal, a post-abatement air clearance test confirms fiber levels have returned to acceptable thresholds before we release the space.
