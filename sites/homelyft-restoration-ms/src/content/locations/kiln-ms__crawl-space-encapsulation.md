Kiln sits in the piney woods of Hancock County where Gulf Coast humidity doesn't just visit — it settles in. Crawl spaces beneath homes here absorb that moisture year-round, and the combination of sandy, poorly draining soil, warm winters that never fully dry out the ground, and the occasional tropical system pushing water inland from the Pearl River basin creates conditions where vapor intrusion and wood decay can take hold faster than most homeowners realize. Crawl space encapsulation is one of the most effective ways to interrupt that cycle before it reaches your floor joists, insulation, and living space.

## Why Kiln Homes Are Particularly Vulnerable to Crawl Space Moisture

Hancock County's soil composition is a significant part of the problem. The sandy loam and clay pockets common across the Kiln area retain moisture close to the surface and release it slowly as vapor — exactly what an open or poorly vented crawl space draws in. Homes built in the 1970s and 1980s, which make up a large portion of the residential stock in this part of the county, were typically constructed with fiberglass batt insulation stapled between floor joists and no vapor barrier beyond a thin sheet of 6-mil poly, if that. Over time, that poly degrades, sags, and gaps, leaving bare soil exposed to the crawl space air.

When relative humidity inside a crawl space climbs above 70% — a routine occurrence here from April through October — wood-destroying fungi can begin colonizing structural members within weeks. Homeowners often notice the problem first through soft spots in flooring, a musty odor that rises into the living area, or higher-than-expected energy bills as conditioned air escapes through gaps in compromised subfloor assemblies. By the time those signs appear, the crawl space has usually been struggling for months.

## Our Crawl Space Encapsulation Process in Kiln

Every encapsulation project starts with a thorough crawl space inspection — not a quick glance from the access hatch, but a full assessment of the entire footprint. We document existing moisture readings, check for standing water or evidence of past flooding, evaluate the condition of existing vapor barriers and insulation, and look for signs of wood decay or mold colonization before any materials go in.

Once we have a clear picture, the process moves through several deliberate stages:

- **Debris removal and prep**: Old, degraded poly sheeting, saturated insulation, and any organic debris are removed and disposed of properly.
- **Drainage and grading**: If low spots are collecting water, we address ground grading or install a drainage mat before sealing anything in.
- **Heavy-duty liner installation**: We install a reinforced polyethylene liner — typically 20-mil — across the entire crawl space floor and up the foundation walls, sealed at seams and termination points with moisture-resistant tape and mechanical fasteners. This is a meaningful step up from the thin sheeting many older Kiln homes have.
- **Vent sealing**: Open foundation vents, which were standard practice for decades, are sealed. Research and field experience consistently show that sealed crawl spaces with controlled conditioning outperform vented ones in humid climates like Hancock County's.
- **Dehumidification**: A crawl space-rated dehumidifier is installed and set to maintain relative humidity below 55%, with a drain line routed to daylight or a condensate pump.
- **Final documentation**: Moisture readings are taken post-installation and photographed for your records and any insurance or real estate documentation needs.

Our team holds IICRC certification in Applied Structural Drying and Mold Remediation, which informs how we approach moisture control at the structural level — not just surface treatment.
