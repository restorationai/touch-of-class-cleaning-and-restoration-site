When heavy rainfall backs up the Pearl River system or saturates the sandy, low-lying soil around town, residential sewer lines and septic systems don't just slow down — they reverse. Raw sewage can surface in a bathroom, a crawl space, or a utility room within minutes, and every minute it sits, the contamination spreads deeper into subfloor materials and wall cavities. ## Why Pearlington Properties Are Especially Vulnerable to Sewage Backups

The Pearl River delta soil around Pearlington is predominantly sandy and poorly drained. During sustained rainfall events — the kind that roll in from the Gulf between June and October — the water table can rise fast enough to hydraulically overload both municipal sewer laterals and private septic drain fields simultaneously. Homes built on slab foundations common throughout this part of Hancock County have almost no buffer between the finished floor and the ground, so a sewer backup that might stay contained in a basement elsewhere spreads immediately across living space here.

Older homes in the area frequently rely on aging cast-iron drain lines that have corroded at joints over decades of exposure to the region's humid, acidic soil conditions. Root intrusion from the dense pine and hardwood tree cover common along the Pearl River corridor accelerates that joint failure. The result is that a single heavy storm can trigger backup calls across multiple properties in the same evening — not because of one isolated clog, but because the entire underground system is under simultaneous pressure.

## Our Sewage Cleanup and Sanitization Process in Pearlington

Raw sewage is classified as Category 3 water — the most hazardous category defined by the IICRC S500 standard — because it contains bacteria, viruses, and parasites that can cause serious illness. Our process reflects that classification at every step.

We begin with full personal protective equipment and immediate containment: plastic sheeting isolates the affected area from the rest of the structure before any extraction begins. Truck-mounted extraction units pull standing sewage and contaminated water from the space, and all porous materials — carpet, pad, drywall below the flood line, insulation — that absorbed Category 3 water are removed and bagged for disposal per Mississippi Department of Environmental Quality guidelines. No porous material that contacted raw sewage is dried in place and left; that shortcut creates long-term microbial problems.

Once the space is cleared, we apply EPA-registered disinfectants to all hard surfaces, subfloor, and framing, with appropriate dwell times. Structural drying then follows using industrial air movers and dehumidifiers sized to the cubic footage of the affected space. Our team holds IICRC AMRT certification, which means mold risk during the drying phase is actively monitored — a critical step in Pearlington's high-humidity climate where ambient moisture levels already push mold colonization timelines shorter than in drier parts of the country.
