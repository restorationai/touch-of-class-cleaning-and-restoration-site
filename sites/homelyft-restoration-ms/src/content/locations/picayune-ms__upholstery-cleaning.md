## Why Picayune Homes See More Upholstery Wear

Pearl River County sits close enough to the Gulf that relative humidity stays high most of the year, and a lot of Picayune housing stock, from older wood-frame homes near downtown to newer construction on the outskirts, relies on window units or older central air in secondary rooms like dens and sunrooms. Those rooms run warmer and more humid than the main living space, and upholstered furniture placed there absorbs moisture slowly and releases it just as slowly. Add in pollen from the surrounding pine forest that drifts in through open windows and door traffic, and fabric weaves trap allergens and fine particulates that a vacuum alone won't lift. Leather furniture has a different problem: without conditioning, Gulf Coast humidity swings cause the surface to dry out between damp spells, which is what leads to that tacky, worn feel long before the piece is actually old.

## Our Upholstery Cleaning Process in Picayune

We start every job with a fabric and fiber assessment, because a microfiber sectional, a cotton-blend armchair, and a leather sofa all need different chemistry and different heat settings. For fabric upholstery, we use low-moisture hot water extraction calibrated to pull cleaning solution and soil back out immediately, which matters here specifically because Picayune's ambient humidity slows natural drying time. Leaving a piece too wet in a humid Mississippi den is how you end up with a mildew smell a week later instead of a clean couch. For leather, we clean with a pH-balanced solution suited to the leather type, then condition it to replace oils the climate strips away. Steam cleaning is used selectively, mostly on durable synthetic fabrics that can handle higher heat without shrinking or watermarking.

## Local note

One thing we watch for on Picayune jobs specifically: furniture kept in sunrooms or converted porches, which are common additions on older lots around town, tends to have UV-faded fabric on one side and humidity-darkened fabric on the other. Cleaning both evenly requires testing solution on the faded side first, since sun-damaged fibers can react differently to extraction chemistry than fabric that's stayed out of direct light.
