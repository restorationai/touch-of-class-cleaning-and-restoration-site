## Why Lumberton Properties See Elevated Storm Damage Risk

Lumberton's position along the US-98 corridor places it at the convergence of two distinct weather patterns: Gulf moisture pushing northeast and cold fronts dropping out of the Mississippi interior. That collision zone generates some of the most intense localized convective storms in the state — the kind that produce brief but violent tornado touchdowns and microbursts capable of snapping 60-foot pines at mid-trunk. The surrounding timber country means falling trees are the single most common cause of catastrophic roof damage here, not just shingle loss.

The housing stock adds another layer of complexity. Many homes in the Lumberton area were built in the 1960s through 1980s using construction practices that predate modern wind-load requirements. Roof-to-wall connections on those structures rely on toenailing rather than hurricane straps, which means a sustained gust that a newer home might shed can partially lift a roof deck on an older property. Once the deck is compromised, rain intrusion begins immediately — and in a Mississippi summer, 80-degree attic temperatures and high ambient humidity can push mold colonization to begin within 24 to 48 hours of the initial breach.

## Our Storm Damage Restoration Process in Lumberton

The first priority on any storm call in Lumberton is stabilization — stopping the bleeding before the assessment even begins. That means emergency tarping of breached roof sections, boarding compromised windows and doors, and if a tree is still bearing load on the structure, coordinating safe removal before interior work starts. We document every affected surface photographically before anything is touched, which matters enormously when your insurance adjuster reviews the claim.

Once the envelope is secure, the process moves to moisture mapping. Even a small roof breach during a heavy rain event can push gallons of water into wall cavities and subfloor assemblies. We use thermal imaging and calibrated moisture meters to trace exactly where water traveled — not just where it pooled visibly. Structural drying follows the IICRC S500 standard, with commercial desiccant and refrigerant dehumidifiers sized to the actual wet volume, not a generic room count. Affected materials that cannot be dried in place — saturated insulation, compromised OSB sheathing, water-logged drywall — are removed and documented before reconstruction begins.

For Lumberton properties that qualify, that upgrade can translate directly to lower wind-peril premiums — a meaningful offset in a county where storm coverage isn't cheap.
