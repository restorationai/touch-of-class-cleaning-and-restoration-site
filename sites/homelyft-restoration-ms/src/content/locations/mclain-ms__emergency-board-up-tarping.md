## Why McLain Properties Are Vulnerable After a Storm or Fire

Greene County's housing stock skews older, and many homes in and around McLain were built in an era when roof sheathing was thinner and window framing was single-pane wood — construction that holds character but gives way faster under the lateral wind loads that accompany Gulf-tracking tropical systems. The region also sits in a corridor where named storms weaken but don't die — they push north from the Gulf Coast and still carry enough wind at Greene County elevations to lift unsecured tarps, scatter debris, and re-expose already-damaged areas. ## Our Emergency Board-Up and Tarping Process in McLain

When a call comes in from the McLain area, the first question we ask is what type of opening we're dealing with — roof breach, broken window, compromised door frame, or a combination after a fire. Each requires a different approach.

For **roof tarping**, we use reinforced polyethylene tarps rated for high-wind exposure, secured with wood battens screwed into undamaged decking rather than simply weighted at the edges. In Greene County's climate, a tarp that isn't mechanically fastened is a temporary fix that fails before morning. We overlap tarp edges at least 18 inches past the damaged section and run the material to the ridge when possible to shed water away from the breach rather than pooling it.

For **window and door board-up**, we cut plywood to fit the rough opening, pre-drill to avoid splitting aged window casings, and secure with exterior-grade screws rather than nails — both for holding strength and so the panels can be removed cleanly once permanent repairs begin. On older homes with irregular window dimensions common in this part of Greene County, we carry a range of sheet sizes and cut on-site.

For **fire-damaged structures**, we assess load-bearing integrity before anyone goes to the roofline. If the fire has compromised rafters or joists, we work from ladders and scaffolding rather than walking the deck — a step that matters both for crew safety and for preserving evidence for the insurance adjuster's inspection.
