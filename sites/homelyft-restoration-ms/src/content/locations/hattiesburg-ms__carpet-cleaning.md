## Why Hattiesburg Properties See Persistent Carpet Problems

The combination of high annual rainfall and the region's distinctive reddish-brown soil is hard on carpeting in ways that standard vacuuming simply cannot address. Moisture acts as a binding agent, locking fine clay particles deep into carpet backing where they abrade fibers from the inside out. Over time, high-traffic areas — hallways, living rooms, entryways — develop a dull, matted appearance that looks like wear but is actually embedded particulate. Older homes with pier-and-beam foundations, common in many of Hattiesburg's established residential areas, also allow humidity to rise from below, keeping carpet padding slightly damp even when the surface feels dry. That slow, chronic moisture is exactly the environment where odor-causing bacteria and allergens thrive.

Commercial properties face a different version of the same problem. Offices, retail spaces, and rental units that see heavy foot traffic during rainy months accumulate soiling faster than their cleaning schedules account for. A professional deep clean — not a rental machine from a grocery store — is the only way to reset the fiber and restore appearance between lease turnovers or seasonal high-traffic periods.

## Our Carpet Cleaning Process in Hattiesburg

Every job starts with a fiber and soiling assessment. Carpet types common in Mississippi homes — nylon, polyester, and older wool blends in historic properties — each respond differently to heat and cleaning chemistry. We identify the fiber, check for pre-existing damage or delamination, and select the appropriate method before any equipment touches the floor.

For most residential carpet, we use truck-mounted hot water extraction, which generates significantly more suction and heat than portable units. The process:

1. **Pre-treatment** — A pH-balanced pre-conditioner is applied and allowed to dwell, loosening clay-based soiling and breaking down organic stains common in Mississippi households (pet accidents, food, mildew).
2. **Agitation** — A grooming tool works the solution into the pile, lifting compacted fibers.
3. **Hot water extraction** — High-temperature water flushes the loosened soil out of the fiber and into the waste tank. Rinse water is pH-neutral to prevent residue buildup that attracts re-soiling.
4. **Post-treatment** — Stubborn stains receive targeted spot treatment. High-traffic lanes may receive a fiber protectant application.
5. **Speed drying** — In Hattiesburg's humid conditions, we position air movers to accelerate drying and reduce the window during which damp carpet can develop mildew odor. This step matters more here than in arid climates.

For commercial carpet cleaning — offices, medical suites, apartment common areas — we schedule around business hours and use low-moisture encapsulation methods when faster dry times are required.

## Equipment & Methods We Use for Carpet

Truck-mounted extraction units are the industry standard for a reason: they heat water to temperatures that portable machines cannot reach and generate vacuum pressure that pulls moisture back out of the carpet rather than leaving it behind. Our equipment is maintained and calibrated to IICRC standards, and our technicians are trained to adjust dwell times and water temperature based on fiber type and soiling level rather than running a fixed program on every job.
