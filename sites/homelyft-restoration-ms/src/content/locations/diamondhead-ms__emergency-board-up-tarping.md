## Why Diamondhead Properties Face Particular Board-Up and Tarping Demands

Diamondhead was developed largely in the 1970s and 1980s as a planned community, which means a significant portion of the housing stock is now 40 to 50 years old. Roof decking from that era — often thinner plywood or even board sheathing — is more likely to delaminate or crack under impact, making a clean tarp anchor harder to achieve than on newer construction. When a tree limb punches through an older deck or a fire vents through a soffit, the damaged area tends to be larger than it first appears once we pull back the surface material.

The Gulf Coast climate adds a second layer of urgency. Diamondhead averages more than 60 inches of rainfall annually, and summer afternoons can bring fast-moving thunderstorms with little warning. A roof opening left unsecured overnight in July or August is almost guaranteed to take on water. That moisture migrates into attic insulation, ceiling joists, and eventually drywall — turning a board-up job into a water damage claim on top of the original loss. Acting quickly closes that window.

## Our Emergency Board-Up and Tarping Process in Diamondhead

When we arrive on site, the first step is a rapid exterior assessment — we walk the roofline, check fascia and soffit integrity, and photograph every opening before touching anything. That documentation matters for your insurance adjuster and establishes a clear baseline of pre-mitigation conditions.

For roof tarping, we use 6-mil reinforced poly tarps secured with 2×4 cap boards screwed into undamaged decking, not just weighted at the edges. In Diamondhead's wind environment, a tarp that isn't mechanically fastened will lift in the next afternoon storm. We size the tarp to overlap the ridge by at least two feet and extend well past the damaged zone on all sides.

Window and door board-ups use 5/8-inch OSB cut to fit each opening, secured with structural screws rather than nails — nails back out under repeated wind pressure. If the frame itself is compromised by fire or impact, we sister in temporary framing before hanging the panel so the board actually holds. Every opening gets a HomeLyft placard with our contact number so that neighbors, utility workers, or your insurance adjuster know who secured the property.

Diamondhead is a deed-restricted community with an active property owners' association, and exterior work — even emergency protective measures — can draw HOA attention.
