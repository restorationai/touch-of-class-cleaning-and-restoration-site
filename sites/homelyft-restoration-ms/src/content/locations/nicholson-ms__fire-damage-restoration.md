## Why Nicholson Properties Face Distinct Fire Damage Challenges

Pearl River County's housing stock skews older, and Nicholson is no exception. Many homes in the area were built with pine-board framing and tongue-and-groove interior walls — materials that absorb smoke deeply and char in ways that modern OSB and drywall do not. When fire moves through pine framing, the char layer insulates the interior of the beam, meaning structural damage can extend further than a visual inspection suggests. Smoke also travels aggressively through gaps in older construction: into attic cavities, wall chases, and crawl spaces that were never sealed to modern standards.

The Gulf Coast humidity compounds this. Within 24 to 48 hours of a fire being extinguished, moisture from firefighting efforts — combined with Nicholson's naturally high relative humidity — can begin driving secondary mold growth inside smoke-damaged wall cavities. That overlap between fire damage and moisture intrusion is one of the most common complications we encounter on Pearl River County jobs, and it requires a coordinated response that addresses both simultaneously.

## Our Fire Damage Restoration Process in Nicholson

Every fire job starts with a thorough structural and smoke assessment before any cleaning or reconstruction begins. In Nicholson's older homes, that means probing framing members for char depth, checking crawl spaces for heat damage to floor joists, and mapping smoke migration through the structure with thermal imaging where appropriate.

From there, the process moves through several defined phases:

- **Board-up and tarping** to secure the structure and prevent weather intrusion — critical in Pearl River County where afternoon thunderstorms can push rain into a fire-opened roof within hours.
- **Soot and smoke residue removal** using dry chemical sponges, HEPA vacuuming, and wet cleaning agents calibrated to the surface type. Pine and older painted wood require different chemistry than modern drywall.
- **Odor neutralization** using thermal fogging and hydroxyl generation to reach cavities that surface cleaning cannot access.
- **Structural drying** when firefighting water is present, following IICRC S500 drying protocols to document moisture levels before any reconstruction begins.
- **Reconstruction coordination**, from framing repairs through drywall, paint, and finish work, handled under Mississippi contractor license #RO 2728.

Because HomeLyft holds both IICRC FSRT (fire and smoke) and IICRC AMRT (mold) certifications, the team can manage the full scope without handing off to a second contractor mid-job.
