## Why Sharon Properties See Sewage Backup Issues

Sharon sits in a part of Madison County where the soil profile shifts between heavy clay and sandy loam depending on how close a property is to the creek drainages that feed into the Natchez Trace corridor. Clay-heavy soils don't absorb groundwater quickly, which means a heavy rain event can saturate the ground around a septic drain field faster than the system can handle — and when that happens, sewage finds the path of least resistance back through floor drains and low-lying fixtures.

Older homes in the Sharon area — many built in the mid-20th century with cast-iron or Orangeburg sewer laterals — are especially vulnerable. Orangeburg pipe, a tar-paper composite used widely from the 1940s through the 1970s, deteriorates from the inside out and eventually collapses under soil pressure. A slow drain that suddenly becomes a full backup is often the first sign the lateral has failed. Replacing the pipe is a plumber's job; cleaning and sanitizing what backed up into your home is ours.

Mississippi's warm, wet springs also accelerate the timeline for secondary damage. Mold can begin colonizing porous materials within 24 to 48 hours of a sewage event if moisture isn't addressed — a fact that makes the extraction and drying phase every bit as important as the disinfection work.

## Our Sewage Cleanup and Sanitization Process in Sharon

Every sewage job follows a structured sequence because skipping steps — or doing them out of order — leaves contamination behind.

**1. Containment and safety assessment.** Before extraction begins, the affected area is isolated to prevent cross-contamination to clean zones. Affected materials are categorized by salvageability.

**2. Extraction and removal.** Standing sewage and saturated materials are removed using truck-mounted extraction equipment. Porous materials — carpet, pad, drywall below the flood line, insulation — that have direct contact with black water are typically non-salvageable and are removed and bagged for proper disposal per Mississippi DEQ guidelines.

**3. Disinfection.** All hard surfaces in the affected area are treated with EPA-registered disinfectants rated for Category 3 (black water) contamination. This includes subfloor decking, wall framing, and concrete slab surfaces. Our team is IICRC AMRT certified, which means the mold risk that follows a sewage event is part of our assessment, not an afterthought.

**4. Structural drying.** Commercial desiccant dehumidifiers and air movers are deployed to bring structural assemblies to target moisture content. Readings are logged daily using calibrated moisture meters so you have a documented drying record — something insurance adjusters and future buyers both want to see.

**5. Post-remediation verification.** Before equipment is removed, moisture readings are taken at multiple points and compared against dry-standard baselines for the material types present.
