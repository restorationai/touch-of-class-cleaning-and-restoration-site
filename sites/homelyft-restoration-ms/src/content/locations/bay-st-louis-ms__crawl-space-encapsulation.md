Bay St. Louis sits right at the edge of the Mississippi Sound, where Gulf humidity doesn't just hang in the air — it works its way into the soil beneath your home's foundation. Crawl spaces here face a combination of high ambient moisture, sandy coastal soils that shift with seasonal saturation, and the kind of persistent ground-level dampness that turns bare-dirt crawl spaces into breeding grounds for mold, wood rot, and pest activity. Encapsulation isn't a luxury upgrade in this environment; for most Bay St. Louis homes, it's the difference between a stable structure and one that quietly deteriorates season after season.

## Why Bay St. Louis Properties See Crawl Space Problems

The Gulf Coast climate is relentless on below-grade spaces. Bay St. Louis averages over 60 inches of rainfall per year, and the water table in low-lying coastal areas rises noticeably after significant rain events or storm surge. Crawl spaces that were never sealed — or were sealed with inadequate vapor barriers years ago — absorb that ground moisture continuously. You'll often notice the signs before you ever look under the house: musty odors in the living room, wood floors that feel slightly spongy, or HVAC systems that run longer than they should because conditioned air is fighting the humidity rising from below.

Many homes in the Bay St. Louis area were built in the mid-20th century, a period when crawl space ventilation was considered sufficient protection. We now know that in a hot, humid climate like Hancock County's, passive vents can actually make the problem worse — pulling in warm, moisture-laden outside air that condenses on cooler crawl space surfaces. Encapsulation seals that cycle off entirely.

## Our Crawl Space Encapsulation Process in Bay St. Louis

Every encapsulation project starts with a thorough crawl space inspection — not a quick look with a flashlight, but a methodical assessment of the liner condition (if any exists), wood moisture readings on joists and beams, signs of standing water or previous flooding, and any active mold growth. Our team holds IICRC AMRT certification for mold assessment and remediation, so if we find microbial growth during the inspection, we can address it as part of the same project rather than sending you to a separate contractor.

Once the space is prepped and any remediation is complete, we install a heavy-mil polyethylene liner across the entire crawl space floor and up the foundation walls, sealed at all seams and penetrations. For Bay St. Louis homes, we pay particular attention to pier foundations, which are common in the area — liner transitions around concrete piers require careful detailing to prevent moisture from wicking up through gaps. We also assess whether a crawl space dehumidifier is warranted; in most cases along the Gulf Coast, it is.

## Equipment & Methods We Use for Crawl Space Encapsulation

The liner itself matters more than most homeowners realize. We use a minimum 20-mil reinforced poly barrier — significantly thicker than the 6-mil sheeting sold at hardware stores and common in older installations. Thicker liner resists puncture from foot traffic during future inspections and holds up against the occasional rodent activity that's common in coastal areas.

For homes where moisture readings on structural wood are elevated, we bring in commercial-grade desiccant or refrigerant dehumidifiers calibrated to the space's cubic footage. We don't size equipment by guesswork — readings are taken before and after to confirm the crawl space is reaching and holding target relative humidity levels. This documentation also matters if you're filing a homeowner's insurance claim or disclosing the work during a home sale.

One pattern we see consistently in Bay St. Louis and the surrounding Hancock County coastline: homes that were elevated or rebuilt after Hurricane Katrina sometimes have crawl spaces that look structurally sound but have vapor barriers that were installed hastily during post-storm reconstruction and have since degraded or shifted.
