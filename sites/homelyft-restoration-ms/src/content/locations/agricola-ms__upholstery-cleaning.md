## Why Agricola Upholstery Takes More Than a Quick Clean

Agricola sits far enough inland that afternoon storms roll through fast and often, and the air stays heavy with humidity most of the year. That combination is hard on furniture. Fabric upholstery pulls moisture out of the air even when nothing has spilled on it, which is why cushions in homes without central air running constantly can feel damp to the touch by midsummer. Add in the number of properties out here on acreage with pole barns, garages, and gravel drives, and you get a specific pattern: sand, red clay dust, and pet dander ground deep into seat cushions and armrests from people coming in and out after yard work, hunting, or working on trucks. Standard vacuuming moves the surface debris but leaves the embedded soil and the trapped humidity right where they started.

## Our Upholstery Cleaning Process in Agricola

We start every job with a fabric and fill assessment, because a natural-fiber sofa, a microfiber sectional, and a leather recliner all call for different chemistry and different pressure. For fabric upholstery, we pre-treat soiled areas, then use hot water extraction to lift oils, dust, and odor-causing residue out of the padding, not just the top layer of the weave. Leather upholstery gets cleaned and conditioned separately, since leather that dries out in this climate cracks faster than people expect, especially on pieces that sit near a window getting Gulf Coast sun for part of the day. After extraction, we bring in air movers to push drying time down, because in Agricola's ambient humidity a couch left to air-dry on its own can stay damp at the seams for a day or more, which is exactly the window mildew needs to take hold.
