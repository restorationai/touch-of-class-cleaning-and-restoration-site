## Why Glendale Properties See Asbestos Concerns

Mississippi's Gulf Coast region, including Glendale and the surrounding Harrison County corridor, experienced significant residential and light-commercial construction from the 1940s through the mid-1970s. During those decades, asbestos was woven into dozens of common building products: floor tile adhesive, pipe wrap insulation, roofing felt, attic insulation, joint compound, and the spray-applied acoustic texture that became synonymous with that era's interior finishes. The region's subtropical humidity — regularly above 70% for months at a stretch — puts steady mechanical stress on these materials. Tile adhesive softens and cracks. Pipe insulation swells and crumbles. When that happens, previously non-friable asbestos becomes airborne and breathable.

Homes built before 1980 in Glendale are the most likely candidates for asbestos-containing materials, but post-1980 construction isn't automatically clear either — some products remained in use into the early 1990s. If your property is being renovated, sold, or has sustained water or fire damage that disturbed original building materials, an asbestos inspection is the responsible first step before any demolition or repair work proceeds.

## Our Asbestos Abatement Process in Glendale

Every project starts with bulk sampling and laboratory analysis — no visual inspection alone can confirm asbestos content. A certified technician collects material samples from suspected areas (floor tile, ceiling texture, pipe insulation, roofing material) following EPA sampling protocols, and those samples go to an accredited lab. You get written results, not a verbal estimate.

If asbestos-containing materials are confirmed, the abatement scope is designed around Mississippi Department of Environmental Quality (MDEQ) regulations and EPA National Emission Standards for Hazardous Air Pollutants (NESHAP) requirements. The work area is sealed with poly sheeting and placed under negative air pressure using HEPA-filtered air scrubbers. Workers wear full protective equipment. Materials are wetted before removal to suppress fiber release, then double-bagged in labeled, sealed containers for transport to a licensed disposal facility — no curbside disposal, no cutting corners on chain-of-custody documentation.

For popcorn ceiling asbestos removal specifically, the process involves careful wet application to the texture before any scraping begins. This step is non-negotiable and is one of the most common places DIY attempts go wrong. Once removal is complete, a final air clearance test confirms fiber levels are within acceptable limits before the containment is broken down and the space is returned to you.

## Local Note

One pattern that comes up repeatedly in older Glendale-area homes is layered flooring — original 9×9 vinyl asbestos tile (VAT) from the 1950s or 1960s covered by a second layer of 1970s or 1980s sheet vinyl, which is itself sometimes covered by modern laminate or hardwood. Homeowners and even some contractors assume the top layer is all that matters. In practice, the original VAT underneath is the layer most likely to contain asbestos, and cutting or grinding through any of those upper layers to reach subfloor can disturb it. An inspection that accounts for all flooring layers — not just what's visible on the surface — is essential before any flooring project in a home of that era.
