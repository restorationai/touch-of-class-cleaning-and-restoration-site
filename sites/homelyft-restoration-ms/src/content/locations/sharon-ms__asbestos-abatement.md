## Why Sharon Properties Face Elevated Asbestos Risk

Madison County's housing stock tells the story clearly. Sharon and the surrounding rural corridor developed steadily through the mid-twentieth century, and the construction materials of that era — floor tiles, pipe insulation, roofing felt, textured ceiling coatings, and joint compounds — routinely incorporated asbestos as a fire retardant and binder. Many of those homes were built well before federal regulations began restricting asbestos use in the late 1970s, meaning original materials may still be in place under layers of renovation work.

Mississippi's climate adds another layer of concern. The humidity and heat cycling that Sharon experiences every summer causes building materials to expand and contract repeatedly over decades. That mechanical stress can cause asbestos-containing materials to crack, crumble, or delaminate — a condition inspectors call "friable" — which is precisely when fibers become airborne and dangerous. A popcorn ceiling that looked intact for thirty years can begin releasing fibers after a roof leak or a single season of attic condensation.

## Our Asbestos Abatement Process in Sharon

Every project begins with a thorough asbestos inspection before any demolition or renovation work starts. A trained technician collects bulk samples from suspect materials — ceiling texture, floor tile adhesive, pipe wrap, siding — and submits them to an accredited laboratory. Results typically return within a few business days, and we walk you through what was found and what it means before any removal plan is drafted.

Once testing confirms asbestos-containing materials, the containment phase begins. We seal the work area with polyethylene sheeting and establish negative air pressure using HEPA-filtered air scrubbers, preventing fibers from migrating to adjacent rooms. Removal is performed wet — dampening materials before disturbance dramatically reduces fiber release — and all debris is double-bagged in labeled, sealed containers for transport to a licensed disposal facility in accordance with Mississippi Department of Environmental Quality (MDEQ) requirements.

Final air clearance testing is conducted after removal and cleanup. We do not consider a job complete until post-abatement air samples confirm that fiber counts have returned to background levels. That documentation matters whether you're satisfying a lender, a buyer's inspector, or your own peace of mind.
