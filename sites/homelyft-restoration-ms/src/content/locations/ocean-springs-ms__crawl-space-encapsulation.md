Ocean Springs sits on a narrow peninsula bracketed by the Back Bay to the north and the Gulf of Mexico to the south, and that geography does something predictable to the air under your house: it keeps it wet. The Gulf Coast's subtropical humidity — regularly pushing 90 percent or higher through the long Mississippi summer — seeps into unencapsulated crawl spaces and stays there, feeding wood rot, mold colonies, and the kind of musty smell that greets you every time you open the front door. Crawl space encapsulation in Ocean Springs isn't a luxury upgrade; for most homes in this city, it's the difference between a structurally sound house and one that's quietly deteriorating from the ground up.

## Why Ocean Springs Homes Are Especially Vulnerable

The soil throughout Jackson County holds moisture long after rain events, and Ocean Springs gets its share — the city averages around 65 inches of rainfall per year, well above the national mean. Older homes on the east side of town, many built in the mid-twentieth century on pier-and-beam foundations, were constructed before vapor barriers were standard practice. That means their crawl spaces are open to ground moisture rising directly into floor joists and subfloor sheathing year after year.

The proximity to the Back Bay compounds the problem. Properties within a few blocks of the waterfront experience tidal humidity fluctuations that push moisture levels in crawl spaces far beyond what a simple plastic sheet can manage. After major storm events — and Ocean Springs has seen its share — standing water in crawl spaces can persist for days, and if that moisture isn't addressed within 24 to 48 hours, mold begins colonizing wood surfaces. Once established, that growth is expensive to remediate and will return if the underlying moisture problem isn't sealed.

Homes on the western edge of the city near the US-90 corridor also deal with drainage challenges from impervious surfaces and older municipal storm infrastructure that can push groundwater toward foundations during heavy rain.

## Our Encapsulation Process, Calibrated to Gulf Coast Conditions

Encapsulation in a high-humidity coastal environment requires more than rolling out a standard 6-mil poly sheet. Here's how the work actually unfolds on an Ocean Springs property:

**Moisture mapping and inspection first.** Before any material goes down, the crawl space is inspected for standing water, existing mold, wood damage, and HVAC duct condition. Moisture readings are taken at multiple points across the floor joists. If active mold is present, remediation happens before encapsulation — sealing mold in is not a solution.

**Ground cover and wall liner installation.** We install a reinforced 20-mil polyethylene vapor barrier across the crawl space floor, running it up the foundation walls and sealing it to the rim joist with closed-cell spray foam and tape rated for high-humidity environments. Seams are overlapped and sealed — not just butted together — because Gulf Coast humidity will find any gap.

**Ventilation or conditioning.** In Ocean Springs, vented crawl spaces often make moisture problems worse, not better, because the humid outdoor air simply replaces what you're trying to remove. Depending on the home's configuration, we either seal foundation vents and condition the crawl space as part of the home's envelope, or install a dedicated crawl space dehumidifier sized for the square footage and local dew point conditions.

**Drainage and sump if needed.** Properties with recurring water intrusion may need an interior perimeter drain channel and sump pump before encapsulation. We assess this during the initial inspection.

## Local Note: What Pier-and-Beam Homes Here Actually Need

One thing that comes up repeatedly on older Ocean Springs properties: the original pier-and-beam construction often leaves crawl space heights that vary significantly from one end of the house to the other — sometimes as little as 18 inches in a corner, which makes proper liner installation genuinely difficult without the right low-profile equipment. A contractor unfamiliar with this housing stock may underestimate labor or skip sealing the low sections entirely, which defeats the purpose. These homes also frequently have original brick piers that need to be wrapped and sealed individually rather than treated as part of a continuous wall surface.
