## Why Lumberton Properties See Sewage Backup Issues

Lumberton sits in a part of Mississippi where the soil profile shifts between sandy loam near creek corridors and dense clay further inland. That clay-heavy ground expands and contracts with seasonal moisture swings, gradually shifting the pitch of buried sewer laterals and septic drain fields. When a line loses its slope, solids accumulate, and the next heavy rain — or even a high-use weekend — can trigger a full backup into the home. Older homes in Lumberton built in the 1960s through 1980s are especially susceptible: cast-iron and Orangeburg pipe from that era corrode and deform over time, and tree root intrusion through cracked joints is common on wooded residential lots throughout the city. Septic systems on larger parcels outside the city's sewer service area face their own pressure: drain fields that were sized for smaller households can become overwhelmed, particularly after extended wet periods.

## Our Sewage Cleanup and Sanitization Process in Lumberton

The first priority on any sewage call is stopping ongoing exposure. That means identifying whether the source is a municipal sewer line backup, a failed septic system, or a broken lateral — each carries different remediation requirements and, in Mississippi, different reporting considerations. Once the source is controlled, the extraction phase begins. Standing sewage is removed with truck-mounted extraction units, and porous materials — carpet, pad, drywall below the flood line, insulation — are assessed for salvageability. In most raw sewage losses, soft goods and contaminated drywall are removed rather than dried in place; the bacterial and viral load in Category 3 water makes in-place drying an inadequate response.

After extraction and material removal, every affected surface is treated with EPA-registered disinfectants formulated for Category 3 contamination. Structural drying follows using calibrated air movers and dehumidifiers, with moisture readings logged daily against the IICRC S500 standard to confirm the structure is returning to normal moisture levels. Final clearance involves a post-remediation inspection before any reconstruction begins — a step that matters both for your family's safety and for insurance documentation.
