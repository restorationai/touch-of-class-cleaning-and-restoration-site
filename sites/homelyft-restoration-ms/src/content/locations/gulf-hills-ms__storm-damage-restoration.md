## Why Gulf Hills Properties Face Elevated Storm Damage Risk

The Gulf Hills area developed largely in the mid-to-late twentieth century, meaning a significant portion of the housing stock predates Mississippi's post-Katrina building code revisions. Older roof decks were often fastened to a standard that modern wind-load requirements have since surpassed. When a tropical system or fast-moving tornado-warned thunderstorm passes through Jackson County, those decks are more likely to experience partial uplift — not catastrophic failure, but enough separation at the ridge or eave to let wind-driven rain penetrate attic insulation and ceiling assemblies before a homeowner even realizes the storm has passed.

Compounding this is the soil composition along the low-lying stretches near the Gulf Hills community. Clay-heavy soils that are already saturated from seasonal rainfall don't absorb runoff quickly. Water pools against foundations and crawl-space vents, and interior moisture levels climb even in rooms that never saw a drop of rain come through the ceiling. That secondary moisture intrusion is easy to miss and fast to become a mold problem — IICRC research puts the colonization window at 24 to 72 hours under warm, humid conditions like those Gulf Hills experiences from May through October.

## Our Storm Damage Restoration Process in Gulf Hills

Every job starts with a documented scope, not a guess. When crews arrive, the first priority is safety — identifying structural instability, live electrical hazards from downed lines, and any gas odors before any equipment is staged. From there, the process follows a deliberate sequence:

**Exterior stabilization** comes first. Tarping exposed roof decks, boarding compromised windows, and securing any fallen tree debris that is still in contact with the structure. This stops the active loss before interior work begins.

**Moisture mapping** follows immediately. Using thermal imaging and calibrated moisture meters, technicians trace water migration through wall cavities, subfloor assemblies, and insulation — not just the visibly wet surfaces. In Gulf Hills homes with pier-and-beam construction, that often means getting under the structure to assess crawl-space saturation.

**Controlled drying** is then set up using commercial-grade desiccant or refrigerant dehumidifiers and high-velocity air movers, positioned and monitored to meet the IICRC S500 drying standard. Daily moisture readings track progress and determine when materials have returned to acceptable levels before any reconstruction begins.

**Reconstruction coordination** closes the loop — from roof decking and sheathing to drywall, insulation, and interior finishes — so the property is returned to pre-loss condition without the homeowner managing multiple separate contractors.
