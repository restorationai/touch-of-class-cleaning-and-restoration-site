## Why Escatawpa Properties See Asbestos Concerns

Jackson County's housing stock along the Alabama border includes a significant share of mid-century construction — ranch homes, pier-and-beam cottages, and older commercial buildings that were built when asbestos was still considered a practical insulating material. Textured "popcorn" ceilings, floor tile mastics, pipe insulation, and roofing felts from that era routinely contain chrysotile or amosite asbestos. In Escatawpa specifically, the proximity to industrial corridors near the Pascagoula River basin means some properties also carry legacy contamination from industrial-era building practices that were common in Gulf Coast communities tied to shipbuilding and manufacturing.

The region's climate compounds the challenge. High ambient humidity causes building materials to swell and contract seasonally, which can crack or crumble older asbestos-containing materials — a condition called friability — even without any renovation work. A friable material releases fibers far more easily than one that's intact, which is why a routine inspection matters before any remodel, re-roofing, or HVAC replacement in a home built before 1980.

## Our Asbestos Abatement Process in Escatawpa

Every project begins with a formal asbestos inspection and bulk sampling by a qualified inspector before any abatement work is planned. Samples are sent to an accredited laboratory, and results typically return within a few business days. Once a material tests positive, Mississippi DEQ regulations and EPA NESHAP guidelines govern how it must be removed, contained, and disposed of — there is no shortcut that keeps a contractor in compliance.

For the actual removal, the work area is isolated with polyethylene sheeting and negative air pressure units that exhaust through HEPA filtration, preventing fibers from migrating to unaffected rooms. Workers wear full-face respirators and disposable protective suits. Wetted removal techniques keep disturbed material from becoming airborne during extraction. All waste is double-bagged in labeled, sealed containers and transported to a licensed disposal facility — Mississippi requires asbestos waste to go to a permitted landfill that accepts regulated material, and we handle that chain of custody documentation.

After removal, an independent air clearance test confirms fiber counts are below acceptable thresholds before containment barriers come down. That third-party verification protects you as the property owner and satisfies insurance carriers and local permitting offices.
