## Why Lumberton Properties See Stubborn Carpet Soiling

Lamar County's climate sits in a humid subtropical band that keeps indoor relative humidity elevated for much of the year — often above 60% even with air conditioning running. High ambient moisture means carpet backing and padding hold onto particulates longer, and organic stains (pet accidents, food spills, mildew tracking in from porches) bond more aggressively with synthetic and natural fibers alike. The red-clay subsoil common across this stretch of south Mississippi is particularly unforgiving: it carries a fine iron oxide dust that, once dry, acts almost like a dye on light-colored carpet. Routine vacuuming moves it around more than it removes it. Deep extraction is the only reliable way to lift it out.

Older homes in Lumberton — many built in the 1970s and 1980s during the county's post-highway growth period — frequently have original or once-replaced carpet over concrete slab foundations. Slab construction transfers ground moisture upward, which can keep carpet padding slightly damp for extended periods after rain events. That slow, low-level dampness creates ideal conditions for odor-causing bacteria and, over time, mold colonization in the pad itself. Catching the problem at the cleaning stage, before it reaches remediation territory, saves homeowners significant cost.

## Our Carpet Cleaning Process in Lumberton

Every job starts with a fiber and soiling assessment — not every carpet responds the same way to hot-water extraction, and older loop-pile or Berber styles common in Lumberton's ranch-style homes require adjusted dwell times and lower agitation to avoid unraveling. From there, the process moves through four stages:

**Pre-treatment** — A pH-balanced conditioning agent is applied to loosen the clay-based and organic soils specific to this area. High-traffic lanes and stain clusters get a targeted pre-spray formulated for the stain type (pet urine, grease, tannin, or iron-based soil).

**Hot-water extraction** — Truck-mounted equipment operating at 200°F+ water temperature drives cleaning solution into the pile and immediately vacuums it back out at high negative pressure. This is the method the IICRC S100 standard identifies as most effective for deep fiber cleaning, and it's what separates professional results from portable machine rentals.

**Spot and stain treatment** — Residual stains get individual attention after the main extraction pass. Red clay staining, a frequent problem in Lumberton, often requires a reducing agent applied carefully to avoid fiber damage.

**Accelerated drying** — Given the local humidity, we deploy air movers after extraction to bring drying time down to 4–6 hours rather than the 12–24 hours that still air would require. Faster drying means less time for re-soiling and dramatically reduces the risk of mildew developing in the pad before the carpet is fully dry.
