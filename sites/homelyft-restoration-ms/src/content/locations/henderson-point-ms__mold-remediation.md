When a slow roof leak, a crawl space seep, or a malfunctioning HVAC drain line goes unnoticed for even 48 to 72 hours in this climate, mold doesn't just appear — it colonizes. ## Why Henderson Point Properties See Mold Issues

The peninsula's position between the bay and the Gulf means relative humidity rarely drops low enough, even in winter, to let a damp wall cavity dry on its own. Summer dewpoints regularly sit in the upper 70s — air that is essentially pre-saturated before it ever contacts a cool interior surface. That combination creates ideal conditions for Cladosporium, Aspergillus, and the Stachybotrys strains most people call black mold to take hold behind baseboards, under subflooring, and inside wall cavities where you won't see or smell them until the colony is well established.

Housing stock along this stretch of the Mississippi Gulf Coast adds another layer of complexity. Many homes here were built in the post-Camille and pre-Katrina decades using construction methods that didn't account for the sustained moisture loads the peninsula generates. Crawl spaces with inadequate vapor barriers, pier-and-beam foundations that allow ground moisture to wick upward, and older fiberglass batt insulation that holds water rather than shedding it — these are patterns we encounter repeatedly in coastal Harrison County properties. Homes that were repaired or rebuilt after Katrina sometimes have hidden moisture intrusion points where repairs met original structure, and those junctions are among the first places we inspect.

## Our Mold Remediation Process in Henderson Point

Every job starts with a thorough visual inspection and moisture mapping using thermal imaging and calibrated moisture meters. In a coastal environment like Henderson Point, the source of moisture is rarely just one thing — we trace every wet reading back to its origin before any physical remediation begins, because removing mold without eliminating the moisture pathway guarantees recurrence.

Containment comes next. We seal affected areas with poly sheeting and establish negative air pressure using HEPA-filtered air scrubbers, which prevents spores from migrating to unaffected rooms during removal. Porous materials — drywall, insulation, carpet padding — that have active mold growth are removed, bagged, and disposed of per Mississippi DEQ guidelines. Hard surfaces are treated with EPA-registered antimicrobial agents and physically cleaned rather than simply sprayed. Our team holds IICRC AMRT (Applied Microbial Remediation Technician) certification, and remediation follows the IICRC S520 standard throughout.

Structural drying follows removal. Given Henderson Point's ambient humidity, we run commercial-grade desiccant or refrigerant dehumidifiers longer than we would in a drier inland climate — the goal is verified dry standard, confirmed by meter readings, not a set number of days. We document moisture readings at every stage so you have a clear record for insurance or future sale disclosure.

One thing that surprises homeowners on the Henderson Point peninsula is how quickly mold can establish in HVAC ductwork. The combination of cool conditioned air inside metal ducts and the extremely humid outdoor air that infiltrates around duct joints creates persistent condensation surfaces — particularly in homes where duct systems run through unconditioned crawl spaces or attic spaces. We inspect duct interiors as part of every mold assessment here, because a remediated wall cavity will re-inoculate within a season if the duct system is actively distributing spores through the living space.
