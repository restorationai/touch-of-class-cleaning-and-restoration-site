## Why Pascagoula Properties See Severe Storm Damage

Jackson County's geography makes Pascagoula one of the most storm-exposed cities on the Mississippi Gulf Coast. The city's low elevation — much of it only a few feet above sea level — means that even a Category 1 landfall can push surge water several blocks inland. That water doesn't drain quickly because the clay-heavy soils common along this stretch of coast become nearly impermeable once saturated. Rain that falls during the storm has nowhere to go, so it sits against foundation slabs, seeps under doors, and wicks up through slab-on-grade construction that was common in residential builds here through the 1980s.

The local housing stock adds another layer of complexity. A significant portion of Pascagoula's neighborhoods were built before Mississippi adopted its post-Katrina residential building codes in 2006. Older wood-frame homes in this area often have roof-to-wall connections that don't meet current strap requirements, making them more vulnerable to partial roof loss in tropical-force winds. When a tree comes down on a home built in the 1960s or 1970s, the structural damage frequently extends well beyond the visible puncture — rafters, ridge boards, and ceiling joists absorb the lateral load in ways that aren't obvious until a trained eye is on-site.
