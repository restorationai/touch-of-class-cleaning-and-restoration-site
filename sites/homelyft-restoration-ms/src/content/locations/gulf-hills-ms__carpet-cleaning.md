## Why Gulf Hills Carpets Take a Harder Hit

The Gulf Coast climate doesn't give carpet much of a break. Relative humidity in Jackson County regularly runs above 70 percent through the summer months, which means carpet padding stays damp longer after any moisture event — a spilled drink, a wet dog, shoes tracked in from a rain-soaked yard. That sustained dampness is exactly the condition mold and mildew need to colonize fiber backing, often before you can smell anything wrong.

Coastal soil in this part of Mississippi is also fine-grained and silty. It doesn't vacuum out the way heavier clay soils do. Instead it works down through the pile and abrades the fiber from below, dulling the surface and locking in odors. Homes closer to the water — where outdoor-indoor living is part of the lifestyle — tend to accumulate this grit faster than properties further from the shoreline. Add the salt particulate that settles on everything near the Gulf, and you have a carpet environment that standard rental machines simply aren't built to address.

## Our Carpet Cleaning Process in Gulf Hills

Before any water touches the carpet, a technician does a fiber and backing assessment. Gulf Hills homes range from newer construction with synthetic Berber to older residences with wool-blend or cut-pile carpet that reacts poorly to high-pH detergents. Choosing the wrong chemistry causes browning, shrinkage, or wicking — where a stain disappears wet and reappears as the carpet dries. We match the cleaning agent to the fiber, not to a one-size formula.

The core method is truck-mounted hot water extraction — water heated to the temperature range that breaks down the oily residue left by salt air and coastal soil, then immediately vacuumed back out at high negative pressure. Dwell time for the pre-conditioner is timed to the soil load, not guessed. Heavily trafficked areas like hallways and living rooms get a secondary agitation pass with a counter-rotating brush tool before extraction.

Because Gulf Hills humidity slows evaporation, we don't simply leave cleaned carpet to air-dry. High-velocity air movers are positioned to accelerate drying and prevent the rebound moisture that lets mildew re-establish in the backing. In most residential settings, carpet is dry and safe to walk on within four to six hours.

## Stain Removal and Carpet Sanitization

Not every stain responds to extraction alone. Tannin stains from Gulf Coast red clay mud, pet urine that has wicked into the pad, and mold-adjacent discoloration each require a targeted treatment applied before the main cleaning pass. We identify stain type first — applying the wrong spotter can set a stain permanently.

Sanitization is a separate step from cleaning and matters particularly in Gulf Hills homes with pets, young children, or any history of flooding. An EPA-registered sanitizer applied post-extraction reduces bacteria and odor-causing microorganisms in the fiber. This is not the same as deodorizing spray; it addresses the biological source rather than masking it.
