## Why Leakesville Properties See Asbestos Risks

Leakesville sits in one of Mississippi's more rural corners, and that geography has a direct effect on its building stock. Because large-scale redevelopment never swept through the way it did in faster-growing metros, a significant share of the housing inventory was built during the 1950s, 1960s, and 1970s — the peak era for asbestos-containing materials in residential construction. Popcorn ceilings were sprayed with asbestos-laden texture compound well into the late 1970s. Floor tiles in kitchens and bathrooms from that period almost universally used chrysotile asbestos as a binder. Pipe wrap on older heating systems is another common source.

Greene County's heat and humidity accelerate the aging of building materials. Repeated expansion and contraction cycles cause older floor tiles to crack and adhesive to crumble — a process that can release fibers without any renovation work at all. When a roof leak or plumbing failure introduces moisture into a structure with intact asbestos-containing materials, the combination of water damage and disturbed insulation creates a situation where two hazards need to be managed at the same time.

## Our Asbestos Abatement Process in Leakesville

Every project begins with a thorough inspection of the affected areas. Suspect materials are sampled and sent to an accredited laboratory — results typically return within 24 to 48 hours. Once the presence of asbestos is confirmed, the work area is sealed with heavy-gauge polyethylene sheeting and placed under negative air pressure using HEPA-filtered air scrubbers. This containment prevents cross-contamination to adjacent rooms and keeps fibers from migrating through the HVAC system.

Abatement technicians work in full personal protective equipment and follow Mississippi Department of Environmental Quality (MDEQ) regulations governing the handling, packaging, and disposal of asbestos waste. All removed material is double-bagged in labeled, leak-tight containers and transported to a licensed disposal facility — not a general landfill. After removal is complete, a final air clearance test confirms fiber levels are below the threshold before containment is broken and the space is returned to use.
