## Why Soso Properties See Water Damage Issues

Jones County averages over 55 inches of rainfall per year — well above the national average — and Soso properties feel that in real, structural ways. The region's clay-dense soil expands when wet and contracts in dry spells, which puts constant lateral pressure on crawl space walls and slab edges. Over time, that movement opens small gaps where groundwater and surface runoff find their way inside.

Older homes in the Soso area — many built in the mid-20th century with pier-and-beam construction — sit above crawl spaces that were never designed with modern vapor barriers or drainage tile in mind. When heavy rain follows a dry stretch, those crawl spaces can accumulate several inches of standing water within hours. Left unaddressed for more than 24 to 48 hours, that environment becomes a reliable incubator for mold colonies, which is why fast water extraction matters here more than in drier climates.

Summer thunderstorms in Jones County also arrive with little warning and can drop two to three inches of rain in under an hour, overwhelming gutters, downspouts, and yard grading that was adequate for normal rainfall. The result is water intrusion through window wells, garage slabs, and crawl space vents — often all at once.

## Our Water Damage Restoration Process in Soso

Every job starts with a thorough moisture assessment. Our technicians use thermal imaging cameras and calibrated moisture meters to map the full extent of water migration — because in pier-and-beam homes common to this part of Jones County, water travels along subfloor joists and into wall cavities well beyond the visible wet area. What looks like a contained puddle near a water heater can mean saturated insulation two rooms away.

Once the scope is mapped, we begin water extraction using truck-mounted and portable extraction units rated for high-volume removal. Standing water in crawl spaces gets pumped first; then we address surface water on flooring. After extraction, we establish a drying system calibrated to the specific materials involved — hardwood floors, OSB subfloor, drywall, or plaster — using commercial-grade desiccant and refrigerant dehumidifiers alongside high-velocity air movers. Drying is monitored daily against IICRC S500 standard drying goals, and we don't close out a job until moisture readings return to regional equilibrium levels appropriate for Jones County's ambient humidity.

If building materials have absorbed enough water to warrant removal — saturated insulation, swollen baseboards, compromised drywall — we handle controlled demolition and document everything for your insurance claim.
