That combination turns an unencapsulated crawl space into a slow-motion problem: wood rot, elevated indoor humidity, musty odors migrating up through subfloors, and eventually mold colonies that don't announce themselves until the damage is already expensive. ## Why Rawls Springs Properties See Crawl Space Problems

The geology here matters more than most homeowners realize. Forrest County's clay-heavy soil expands when wet and contracts when dry, which means crawl space vents — standard in homes built before modern encapsulation practices became common — cycle through wet and dry air constantly rather than staying at a stable moisture level. Many homes in the Rawls Springs area were built in the mid-20th century when open-vent crawl spaces were code-compliant and considered adequate. They weren't designed for the vapor loads that Mississippi summers deliver.

When outdoor dew points climb into the upper 70s — which happens regularly from June through September in this region — outside air drawn into a vented crawl space is actually wetter than the air inside. Venting, counterintuitively, makes the problem worse. That moisture condenses on cooler structural wood, insulation, and ductwork. Over a single summer, wood moisture content can rise high enough to support mold growth and begin softening floor joists.

## Our Crawl Space Encapsulation Process in Rawls Springs

Every project starts with a thorough inspection of the crawl space before any material goes in. We check for standing water, existing mold or wood rot, damaged vapor barriers (often the thin 6-mil poly sheeting installed decades ago that has since torn or shifted), and the condition of floor joists and sill plates. If there is active moisture intrusion or mold present, that gets addressed first — encapsulation over an active problem seals the damage in rather than solving it.

Once the space is clean and dry, we install a heavy-duty reinforced polyethylene liner — typically 20-mil — across the entire crawl space floor and up the foundation walls, sealed at seams and at wall penetrations with moisture-resistant tape. We seal or condition the vents depending on the home's configuration, and in most Rawls Springs installations we add a crawl space dehumidifier sized to the square footage of the space. That dehumidifier runs continuously and drains automatically, keeping relative humidity in the crawl space below the threshold where mold can establish itself regardless of what the weather outside is doing.

Because many homes in this area have ductwork running through the crawl space, we also inspect duct connections for gaps — conditioned air leaking into an unencapsulated crawl space is a major driver of moisture problems and energy loss.

## Equipment and Methods for Mississippi's Climate

Standard encapsulation products designed for drier climates underperform in the Pine Belt. We use liner systems and dehumidification equipment rated for high-humidity environments. The dehumidifiers we install are commercial-grade units — not the box-store models that cycle on and off and struggle to keep up in August. They're sized using the crawl space square footage, ceiling height, and the home's typical moisture load, not a one-size estimate.

For homes with existing fiberglass batt insulation between the floor joists, we evaluate whether it needs to come out before encapsulation. Wet fiberglass loses its R-value and becomes a substrate for mold; leaving it in place under a new liner traps the problem. In many Rawls Springs homes we've worked in, removing that insulation and replacing it after encapsulation is part of the scope.

The IICRC AMRT certification our team holds means the same technicians who understand mold remediation are assessing your crawl space — not a sales rep working from a checklist.

## Local Note

Homes in the Rawls Springs area that were built on pier-and-beam foundations — common in this part of Forrest County through the 1970s — often have crawl spaces with irregular heights and multiple access points that complicate liner installation. Low-clearance sections require hand-fitting liner panels rather than rolling them out in long runs, and sealing around the base of piers takes extra attention because those are the spots where vapor barriers most often fail over time. If your home has this foundation type, plan for the encapsulation work to take longer than a standard job — cutting corners on pier seals is where cheaper installations break down within a few years.
