## Why Hattiesburg Properties Take a Hard Hit in Severe Weather

Forrest County's combination of dense tree canopy, clay-heavy soils, and aging residential neighborhoods creates a specific storm-damage profile that differs from coastal Mississippi. The same mature hardwoods and longleaf pines that give Hattiesburg its character become projectiles when sustained winds exceed 60 mph. Root systems in the area's expansive clay soil can heave and destabilize trees that look healthy from the street — meaning a storm that barely registers on the news can still drop a 70-foot tree across a roofline.

Older housing stock throughout the city adds another layer of complexity. Homes built before the statewide adoption of post-Katrina wind-load codes often have roof-to-wall connections that rely on toe-nailing rather than hurricane straps, making them more vulnerable to uplift damage even in a sub-hurricane-force event. When a ridge board or rafter tail is compromised, water follows the framing cavity downward fast, saturating insulation and wall cavities in ways that a surface inspection won't reveal.

## Our Storm Damage Restoration Process in Hattiesburg

Every job starts with a full exterior and interior assessment — not just the obvious breach point. Roof penetrations, soffit separations, and window-frame gaps all get documented with photos and moisture readings before any material is moved. That documentation matters: carriers writing policies in Mississippi increasingly require a clear chain of evidence linking the storm event to each line item on the estimate.

Once the scope is confirmed, the sequence is stabilize, dry, then restore. Emergency tarping or board-up stops active water intrusion. Industrial drying equipment — desiccant dehumidifiers and high-velocity air movers — gets placed based on psychrometric readings, not guesswork. Because Hattiesburg's ambient humidity routinely sits above 70 percent in summer, we run equipment longer than the IICRC S500 standard's baseline drying goals require; the outdoor air working against the drying process means cutting equipment too early is one of the most common causes of mold callbacks in this climate.

Structural repairs — framing, sheathing, roofing, drywall — follow only after moisture content in affected assemblies reaches acceptable levels. Rebuilding over wet wood is a short-term fix that creates a long-term mold problem.
