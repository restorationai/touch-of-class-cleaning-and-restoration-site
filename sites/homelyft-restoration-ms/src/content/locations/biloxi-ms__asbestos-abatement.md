Biloxi's Gulf Coast humidity and its deep inventory of mid-century construction make asbestos abatement a more urgent conversation here than in many Mississippi cities. Homes and commercial buildings built before 1980 — and Biloxi has plenty of them, from the beachside cottages along the Back Bay corridor to the older residential blocks inland — frequently contain asbestos in floor tiles, pipe insulation, roofing felt, and the textured "popcorn" ceilings that were standard finish work for decades. Salt air accelerates the deterioration of building materials, which means asbestos-containing products that might stay intact in a drier climate can become friable faster here, releasing fibers into living spaces without any obvious warning sign.

## Why Biloxi Properties Are Prone to Asbestos Hazards

The Gulf Coast building boom that followed World War II left Biloxi with a substantial stock of structures constructed between the late 1940s and the mid-1970s — precisely the window when asbestos use in residential and commercial construction peaked. Roofing shingles, duct insulation, joint compound, and vinyl floor tiles from that era routinely contained asbestos. Post-Katrina renovation activity added another layer of complexity: some properties were partially rebuilt or patched rather than fully gutted, leaving original asbestos-containing materials sandwiched between newer finishes. That layering is easy to miss during a casual inspection and requires systematic sampling rather than a visual guess.

The coastal climate compounds the risk. High relative humidity — Biloxi averages above 75% humidity for much of the year — causes repeated expansion and contraction in older building materials. Over time, that cycling degrades binders in floor tiles and ceiling textures, increasing the likelihood that asbestos fibers become airborne during routine maintenance, a renovation, or even a hard storm season.

## Our Asbestos Abatement Process in Biloxi

Every project starts with a formal asbestos inspection and bulk sampling before any removal begins. Suspected materials are collected following EPA sampling protocols and sent to an accredited laboratory. Once results confirm the presence of asbestos, the work scope is defined — whether that means a full abatement of floor tile throughout a home, targeted removal of pipe wrap in a crawl space, or popcorn ceiling removal in a single room.

Containment is established using negative-air pressure enclosures with HEPA filtration so fibers cannot migrate to unaffected areas of the building. Wetted removal techniques minimize fiber release during the actual extraction. All waste is sealed in labeled, double-bagged containers and transported to a licensed disposal facility in compliance with Mississippi Department of Environmental Quality (MDEQ) regulations — the same state agency that governs disposal for Gulf Coast counties. A final air clearance test by an independent industrial hygienist confirms the space is safe before containment is removed and the area is released for reoccupancy or renovation.
