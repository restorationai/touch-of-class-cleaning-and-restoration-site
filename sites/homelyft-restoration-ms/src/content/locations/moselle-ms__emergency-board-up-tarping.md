## Our Emergency Board-Up and Tarping Process in Moselle

Every job starts with a rapid visual assessment of the full envelope — roof, walls, windows, and any compromised entry points. We photograph every damaged opening before we touch anything, both for your insurance documentation and to establish a baseline for the repair scope.

For roof tarping, we use heavy-duty polyethylene tarps rated for high-wind conditions, secured with dimensional lumber battens screwed directly into sound decking — not just weighted down at the edges, which fails in the first gust. On older Moselle homes where the decking may be 1×6 skip sheathing rather than modern plywood, we adjust our fastening pattern to hit solid material rather than gaps.

Window and door board-up uses 5/8-inch plywood cut to fit each opening, secured with structural screws into the framing — not staples, not tape. We mark every panel with the date, our contact information, and the claim number if one has been assigned, which adjusters and inspectors expect to see when they arrive on site.

All work is documented with timestamped photos delivered to you and, with your authorization, shared directly with your insurance carrier.

## Local Note

One thing worth knowing if you own an older wood-frame home in the Moselle area: the roof decking on many houses built before the mid-1980s is skip-sheathing — individual boards with deliberate gaps between them, rather than a continuous plywood sheet. When a tarp is laid over skip-sheathed decking without proper battening, wind gets underneath and lifts it almost immediately. We've seen improperly tarped roofs in Jones County sustain more water intrusion *after* the initial storm than during it, simply because a loose tarp acts like a sail and peels away. Our crews are specifically trained to identify skip-sheathing on contact and adapt the fastening approach accordingly — it takes a little longer, but the tarp stays put through the next round of weather.
