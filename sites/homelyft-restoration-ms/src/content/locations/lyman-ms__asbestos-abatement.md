## Why Lyman Properties Face Elevated Asbestos Risk

Harrison County's building boom in the mid-twentieth century coincided almost exactly with peak asbestos use in American construction. Contractors throughout the region — including communities like Lyman — routinely used asbestos-containing materials because they were cheap, fire-resistant, and widely available through Gulf Coast supply chains. That history means a significant portion of the local housing stock was built during the years when asbestos appeared in nine or more building components simultaneously: joint compound, ceiling texture, vinyl floor tiles, duct insulation, exterior siding, and more.

The Gulf Coast climate compounds the problem. High humidity and the occasional severe storm — the kind that peels back roofing or floods crawl spaces — can disturb materials that were otherwise stable. Friable asbestos, meaning material that crumbles or breaks apart easily, releases microscopic fibers into the air. A roof damaged in a storm or a crawl space disturbed during flood remediation can suddenly turn a contained asbestos issue into an active exposure risk. That's why abatement and disaster restoration often go hand in hand on Mississippi properties.

## Our Asbestos Abatement Process in Lyman

Every project starts with a thorough inspection and material sampling — no assumptions, no shortcuts. Suspect materials are collected by a trained technician and sent to an accredited laboratory for analysis. Once results confirm the presence of asbestos-containing materials, the work plan is built around the specific materials identified, their location, and their condition.

Containment comes next. Affected areas are sealed with heavy-gauge polyethylene sheeting and placed under negative air pressure using HEPA-filtered air scrubbers. This prevents fibers from migrating into living spaces during removal. Workers wear full personal protective equipment and follow removal protocols consistent with EPA and Mississippi Department of Environmental Quality (MDEQ) requirements for asbestos waste handling and disposal.

Waste is wetted, double-bagged in labeled asbestos disposal bags, and transported to a licensed disposal facility — not a standard landfill. After removal, a final visual inspection and air clearance test confirm the space is safe before containment is dismantled. The team holds EPA Lead-Safe certification and OSHA training, which matters when abatement intersects with other hazardous materials common in older Gulf Coast homes.

## Local Note

One pattern that comes up repeatedly on older Lyman properties: popcorn ceilings that were painted over at some point in the 1980s or 1990s. Paint can actually stabilize asbestos-containing texture temporarily, but it also makes testing trickier — the lab sample has to penetrate the paint layer to reach the texture material underneath. Homeowners who've been told their ceilings "tested negative" should ask whether the sample was collected correctly, because a surface scrape of paint alone won't give you an accurate result. If you're planning to sand, scrape, or remove that ceiling before a repaint or renovation, get a proper sample collected by someone who knows what they're looking at.
