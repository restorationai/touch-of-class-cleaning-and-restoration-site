## Why Bond Properties See Water Damage Issues

The climate in this part of Mississippi is unforgiving when it comes to moisture. Bond receives a significant share of the state's annual rainfall, and the region's warm, humid air means that even after visible water is removed, ambient humidity keeps building materials wet far longer than in drier climates. Crawl space flooding is a recurring problem here — many homes in the Bond area were built on pier-and-beam foundations, and when storm runoff or plumbing failures push water underneath the structure, it can saturate floor joists and subfloor sheathing before a homeowner even notices the problem above.

Older residential construction in the area also tends to use wood framing and older pipe materials that are more susceptible to freeze-thaw stress during the occasional hard winter freeze Mississippi sees — a burst pipe in an uninsulated interior wall can release dozens of gallons before the water reaches a floor drain or a visible surface. The combination of aging infrastructure and a climate that accelerates microbial growth makes fast response and thorough structural drying essential, not optional.

## Our Water Damage Restoration Process in Bond

Every water damage job in Bond starts with a thorough moisture assessment — not just the wet floor you can see, but the wall cavities, subfloor, and ceiling assemblies that hide saturation. We use thermal imaging and calibrated moisture meters to map the full extent of the damage before any equipment is placed.

From there, the process follows the IICRC S500 standard for water damage restoration:

- **Water extraction**: Truck-mounted and portable extractors pull standing water from hard surfaces, carpet, and crawl spaces.
- **Controlled demolition**: Saturated drywall, insulation, or flooring that cannot be dried in place is removed to expose wet framing — this is not optional when readings exceed safe thresholds.
- **Structural drying**: Industrial air movers and LGR dehumidifiers are positioned based on a drying plan calibrated to the room volume, material types, and ambient humidity levels typical of Bond's climate.
- **Daily monitoring**: Moisture readings are logged each day. Equipment is adjusted or repositioned as materials dry. The job is not complete until readings return to regional equilibrium moisture content — not just until surfaces feel dry to the touch.
- **Documentation**: Every reading, every photo, and every equipment log is saved for your insurance carrier.
