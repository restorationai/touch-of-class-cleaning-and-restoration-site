## Why Saucier Properties Are Especially Vulnerable to Crawl Space Moisture

Harrison County's clay-heavy soils hold water long after a rain event passes. Unlike sandy coastal soils that drain quickly, the red clay common throughout the Saucier corridor stays saturated for days, and that moisture vapor migrates upward through exposed crawl space dirt. Homes built in the 1960s through the 1980s — a common vintage in this part of the county — were typically constructed with vented crawl spaces, a design that made sense in theory but in practice pulls humid outdoor air directly under living spaces during Mississippi summers. When outdoor air at 88°F and 85% humidity enters a cooler crawl space, it hits the dew point and condenses on floor joists, insulation batts, and ductwork. Over a single season, that cycle can cause insulation to sag and fall, floor joists to soften, and HVAC efficiency to drop noticeably as ducts sweat.

Saucier also sits in a region that sees significant rainfall from Gulf-driven weather systems, including tropical moisture events that can dump several inches of rain in a short window. Crawl spaces without proper drainage or vapor barriers absorb that ground surge, and homes on slightly sloped lots can experience lateral water intrusion at the foundation perimeter.

## Our Crawl Space Encapsulation Process in Saucier

Every encapsulation job starts with a thorough crawl space inspection — not a quick flashlight sweep, but a systematic assessment of moisture levels at the soil surface, wood moisture content readings on floor joists and sill plates, and a check for existing mold colonization or pest activity. We document everything with photos before any work begins, which matters both for your records and for insurance purposes if moisture damage is part of a broader claim.

Once the crawl space is cleared of debris and any damaged insulation is removed, we install a heavy-duty polyethylene vapor barrier — typically a 20-mil reinforced liner — across the entire ground surface and up the foundation walls, sealed at seams and mechanically fastened at the perimeter. In Saucier's climate, we almost always recommend closing off existing foundation vents rather than leaving them open; the old "cross-ventilation" approach simply doesn't work when outdoor air is more humid than the air you're trying to protect against. Depending on the space's size and existing moisture load, we may also install a dedicated crawl space dehumidifier with a condensate drain line, and in some cases a sump pit if the lot grading creates drainage concerns.

All drying and encapsulation work follows IICRC S500 and S520 standards, and because many Saucier homes were built before 1978, our EPA Lead-Safe Certified team takes the additional precautions required when disturbing older painted surfaces during crawl space access work.
