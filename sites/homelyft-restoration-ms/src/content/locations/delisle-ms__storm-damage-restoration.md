## Why DeLisle Properties Are Particularly Vulnerable to Storm Damage

The geography and building stock of DeLisle create a specific set of vulnerabilities that differ from, say, a newer subdivision closer to the coast. Much of the residential housing in this area was built during eras when windstorm standards were less rigorous, meaning roof decking attachment and wall sheathing don't always meet the performance thresholds now required under Mississippi's post-Katrina building codes. When a storm pushes sustained winds above 60 mph — well within the range of a strong tropical storm — older roofs can experience partial or full decking loss before the storm even reaches hurricane classification.

The soil composition in this part of Harrison County also matters. The sandy, loamy soils common around DeLisle drain poorly in concentrated rainfall events, which means water doesn't just fall on a structure — it pools against foundations, infiltrates crawl spaces, and wicks upward into floor systems. A tree strike that opens a roof during a storm isn't just a roofing problem; within 24 to 48 hours, the interior framing and insulation are absorbing moisture, and mold colonization can begin in as little as 72 hours under the humidity conditions typical of a Mississippi summer.

## Our Storm Damage Restoration Process in DeLisle

Every job starts with a structured damage assessment — not a quick visual walk-through, but a systematic inspection that documents wind damage, water intrusion points, and structural compromise with photographs and moisture readings. This documentation matters both for the repair scope and for the insurance claim file.

Once the assessment is complete, the work moves in a deliberate sequence:

1. **Emergency stabilization** — tarping compromised roofs, boarding openings, and removing hazardous debris like hanging limbs or partially detached structural members that could cause additional damage or injury.
2. **Water extraction and drying** — any rain intrusion is treated as an active water damage event. We deploy commercial-grade desiccant dehumidifiers and high-velocity air movers calibrated to the actual moisture load, not a one-size estimate. Structural drying follows the IICRC S500 standard.
3. **Debris and tree removal coordination** — we work alongside licensed tree services when large canopy removal is needed before interior work can proceed safely.
4. **Structural and finish repairs** — roof decking, sheathing, framing, drywall, insulation, and exterior cladding are restored to current code, which in Mississippi includes windstorm provisions that are more stringent than they were even a decade ago.

Our team holds IICRC certifications in Water Restoration, Structural Drying, and Fire and Smoke Restoration, and we carry FORTIFIED Building Roofing and MS Windstorm certification — a credential specifically relevant to Gulf Coast storm repair work.

One pattern we've observed repeatedly in DeLisle and the surrounding Harrison County rural corridor: homes on pier-and-beam foundations — common in this area — are especially prone to moisture migration after storm events because the crawl space acts as a collection basin for wind-driven rain that enters through foundation vents.
