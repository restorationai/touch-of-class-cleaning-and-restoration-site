## Why Wade Properties See Asbestos Concerns

Forrest County's housing stock skews older in many of its unincorporated communities, and Wade is no exception. Homes constructed before 1980 frequently contain asbestos-containing materials (ACMs) in places owners don't expect: the mastic adhesive beneath vinyl floor tiles, the joint compound on drywall seams, the insulation wrapped around HVAC ducts in crawl spaces, and the blown-in attic insulation that was common before fiberglass alternatives took over. Mississippi's humid subtropical climate adds another layer of complexity — repeated cycles of heat and humidity cause building materials to expand and contract, which can degrade the binders that keep asbestos fibers locked in place. A material that was once "non-friable" and relatively stable can become friable over years of thermal stress, meaning fibers are more easily released into the air during disturbance.

Storm events also matter here. When a roof is compromised by wind or hail — something Forrest County residents know well — roofing underlayment and attic insulation can be disturbed in ways that release fibers before anyone realizes there's a hazard. Addressing asbestos before or immediately after storm-related repairs is not just good practice; Mississippi state regulations require it when renovation or demolition work is involved.

## Our Asbestos Abatement Process in Wade

Every project starts with a proper inspection and bulk sampling — not a visual guess. A certified inspector collects material samples from suspect areas, which are then analyzed by an accredited laboratory. That report tells us exactly what we're dealing with before a single tool touches the surface.

Once ACMs are confirmed, the abatement work follows a defined protocol: the affected area is isolated with polyethylene sheeting and placed under negative air pressure using HEPA-filtered air scrubbers so that disturbed fibers cannot migrate into living spaces. Workers wear full personal protective equipment, and all removed material is wetted during extraction to suppress fiber release. Waste is double-bagged in 6-mil poly bags, labeled per EPA requirements, and transported to a licensed disposal facility — Mississippi has specific manifest requirements for asbestos waste that we handle from start to finish.

For popcorn ceiling asbestos removal specifically, we wet the texture thoroughly before scraping to prevent airborne release, then clean all surfaces with HEPA vacuums before final air clearance testing confirms the space is safe for re-occupancy.
