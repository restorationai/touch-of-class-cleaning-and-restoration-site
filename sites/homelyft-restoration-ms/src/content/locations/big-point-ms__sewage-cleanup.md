## Why Big Point Properties See Sewage Backup Issues

Jackson County's combination of high seasonal rainfall and relatively flat coastal topography creates drainage conditions that stress both municipal sewer connections and private septic systems. Many homes in the Big Point area rely on septic tanks rather than centralized sewer infrastructure, and those tanks — especially on lots with compacted clay soil — can saturate and reverse-flow during prolonged wet periods. A slow drain that seems like a minor nuisance on a dry October afternoon can become a full sewage overflow by the time a multi-day Gulf rain event ends.

Older homes in the area, many built before modern plumbing codes standardized pipe diameter and backflow prevention requirements, are particularly vulnerable. Cast iron drain lines corrode from the inside out over decades, narrowing the effective bore and catching grease, roots, and debris until a blockage forces sewage back through the lowest fixture in the house — usually a floor drain, a tub, or a utility sink in the lowest level of the home.

## Our Sewage Cleanup and Sanitization Process in Big Point

Raw sewage is classified as Category 3 water — the most contaminated classification in the IICRC S500 standard — and it requires a structured removal and sanitization process, not just cleanup. Here is how that process runs on a typical Big Point job:

**Containment and assessment.** We identify the source of the backup, confirm the line is cleared or capped, and establish containment to prevent cross-contamination to unaffected areas of the home.

**Extraction.** Truck-mounted and portable extractors remove standing sewage water from hard floors, carpeting, and subfloor cavities. In crawl space situations — common in this part of Jackson County — we deploy confined-space-rated equipment to pull contaminated water from beneath the structure.

**Removal of porous materials.** Carpet, pad, drywall, and insulation that have absorbed Category 3 water cannot be safely dried and reused. We remove and bag these materials according to Mississippi solid waste guidelines before they can off-gas or spread contamination further.

**EPA-registered disinfection.** All affected hard surfaces — concrete, subfloor plywood, wall framing, floor joists — are treated with hospital-grade, EPA-registered antimicrobials. We do not skip this step on "minor" backups; even a small sewage event introduces fecal coliform and other pathogens that require chemical intervention.

**Structural drying.** Once sanitized, affected structural materials are dried using calibrated desiccant or refrigerant dehumidifiers and high-velocity air movers. Our IICRC ASD-certified technicians set drying targets based on material type and ambient conditions, not guesswork.

**Post-remediation documentation.** We photograph and document every phase, which matters when you are filing a homeowner's insurance claim for a sewer line backup or septic overflow event.
