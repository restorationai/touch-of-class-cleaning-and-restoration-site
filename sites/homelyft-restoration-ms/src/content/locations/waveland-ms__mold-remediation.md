## Why Waveland Properties See Elevated Mold Risk

The Gulf Coast climate is the primary driver. Waveland averages humidity levels that routinely exceed 70 percent, even on days without rain. When that exterior moisture meets air-conditioned interiors, condensation forms inside wall cavities, under flooring, and in crawl spaces — often without any visible leak. Homes that took on water during past hurricane seasons carry another layer of risk: residual moisture trapped behind original finishes that were patched rather than fully dried can quietly feed mold colonies for years.

The local housing stock compounds this. Many homes in the Waveland area were built before modern vapor barriers and moisture-resistant sheathing became standard. Older wood-framed construction, pier-and-beam foundations common near the shoreline, and original plaster or textured drywall all absorb and hold moisture differently than contemporary materials. That means mold isn't always where you expect it — it may be behind a wall that looks and feels dry on the surface.

Post-Katrina rebuilds added a different variable: some homes were reconstructed quickly with materials that weren't fully acclimated to Gulf Coast humidity before installation, leaving micro-gaps in the building envelope that let moist air in over time.

## Our Mold Remediation Process in Waveland

Every job starts with a thorough inspection — not a visual scan, but a moisture mapping exercise using thermal imaging and calibrated meters to find hidden wet zones before any remediation work begins. In Waveland's climate, skipping this step means missing the source, and mold will return.

Once the scope is established, the affected area is sealed off with negative air pressure containment. This is especially important in Gulf Coast homes where HVAC systems often run continuously and can spread spores through ductwork if the space isn't properly isolated. HEPA-filtered air scrubbers run throughout the remediation to capture airborne particulate.

Our team — holding IICRC AMRT (Applied Microbial Remediation Technician) certification — removes affected porous materials according to industry protocol, treats structural surfaces, and applies antimicrobial agents appropriate for the material type. Because Waveland homes often have older wood framing, we pay particular attention to sill plates and floor joists in crawl spaces, which are frequently the last place a homeowner looks and the first place mold takes hold after a flood event.

Drying equipment stays on-site until moisture readings return to acceptable baselines — not a fixed number of days, but until the data says the structure is ready. In high-humidity coastal conditions, that process takes longer than it would in a drier climate, and we account for that in every job plan.
