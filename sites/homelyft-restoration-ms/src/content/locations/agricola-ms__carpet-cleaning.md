## Why Agricola Properties See Carpet Soil and Staining Issues

George County's soil profile runs heavy with iron-rich red clay and sandy loam, and both types bond stubbornly to synthetic carpet fibers. A single rainy week in late spring or early fall can mean repeated tracking of that rust-colored clay across entryways, hallways, and living areas before it dries and sets. Unlike the lighter silica-based soils common in coastal Gulfport neighborhoods, George County clay particles are finer and wedge deeper into carpet pile with each footstep, making surface vacuuming almost ineffective once the soil has migrated past the face fibers.

Homes in the Agricola area also tend toward older construction with pier-and-beam or crawl-space foundations, which means ground moisture migrates upward more readily than in slab-on-grade builds. Carpet installed over wood subfloors in these homes can trap humidity at the backing layer — a condition that accelerates the growth of mildew and the breakdown of adhesive in glue-down commercial carpet installations. If you have noticed a musty undertone that lingers even after vacuuming, that is often moisture at the backing, not just surface soil.

## Our Carpet Cleaning Process in Agricola

Every job starts with a fiber and backing assessment before any water or chemistry touches the carpet. Wool blends and older nylon installations common in homes built in the 1970s and 1980s react differently to alkaline pre-sprays than the solution-dyed polyester found in most carpet installed after 2005 — getting that wrong causes color shift or shrinkage that no amount of re-cleaning will fix.

Once fiber type is confirmed, we apply a pH-balanced pre-conditioner calibrated to the soil load. For heavy clay-tracked areas we use an agitation step with a counter-rotating brush machine to break the clay's bond with the fiber before extraction begins. Extraction runs through truck-mounted hot-water equipment operating at temperatures between 180 and 230 degrees Fahrenheit, which sanitizes while it cleans — relevant in a climate where dust mites and mold spores are year-round concerns rather than seasonal ones. Drying time in Agricola's humid summers is managed with high-velocity air movers and, when indoor humidity is above 60 percent, a dehumidifier staged in the room to pull vapor out of the carpet backing before it can re-deposit soil or encourage microbial growth.

For commercial carpet — offices, churches, or any high-traffic space — we offer low-moisture encapsulation cleaning as an alternative when extended drying time is not practical, followed by a hot-water extraction pass on a longer maintenance cycle.
