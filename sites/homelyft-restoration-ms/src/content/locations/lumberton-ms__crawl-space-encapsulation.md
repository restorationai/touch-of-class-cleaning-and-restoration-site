Lumberton sits in the heart of Lamar County, where the humid subtropical climate pushes ground moisture levels high enough that an unprotected crawl space can turn into a slow-motion problem long before a homeowner notices. The combination of heavy seasonal rainfall, clay-heavy soils that hold water close to the surface, and older wood-framed construction common throughout the area creates near-perfect conditions for moisture intrusion, wood rot, and mold colonization beneath the floor. Crawl space encapsulation addresses that problem at the source — sealing the space so outside humidity and ground vapor can no longer migrate upward into your living areas.

## Why Lumberton Properties Are Especially Vulnerable to Crawl Space Moisture

Lamar County averages well over 60 inches of rainfall per year, and Lumberton's position along the Bogue Homa Creek drainage corridor means that even properties that sit above the floodplain can experience elevated soil saturation for weeks after a heavy rain event. Clay soils common in this part of Mississippi don't drain quickly — they hold moisture against foundation piers and beneath floor systems, keeping relative humidity in unencapsulated crawl spaces persistently above 70 percent. At that level, wood floor joists begin to soften, insulation loses its effectiveness, and mold can colonize within 48 to 72 hours of a moisture event. Many of the homes in and around Lumberton were built in the 1960s through the 1980s with ventilated crawl spaces that followed the building codes of the time — codes that have since been updated because the vented approach actually worsens moisture problems in humid Southern climates by drawing in warm, moist outside air.

## Our Crawl Space Encapsulation Process in Lumberton

Every encapsulation project starts with a thorough inspection — measuring moisture content in the floor framing, checking for existing mold growth, and identifying any standing water or drainage issues that need to be resolved before sealing the space. In Lumberton's climate, skipping that diagnostic step and simply laying a vapor barrier over an already-compromised crawl space is a common mistake that leads to callbacks.

Once the crawl space is confirmed dry and structurally sound, the process moves through several concrete steps:

- **Ground cover installation**: A heavy-duty reinforced polyethylene liner — typically 20-mil — is laid across the entire floor and sealed at seams with vapor-barrier tape. In Lumberton's high-moisture environment, thinner liners are a false economy.
- **Wall lining**: The liner is run up the foundation walls and mechanically fastened, creating a continuous sealed envelope rather than just a floor cover.
- **Pier wrapping**: Foundation piers are wrapped and sealed to eliminate moisture pathways around the vertical supports.
- **Drainage and dehumidification**: Where soil saturation warrants it, a drainage mat or interior drain tile is installed before the liner. A crawl space dehumidifier sized to the square footage is then added to maintain relative humidity below 55 percent year-round — critical in Lamar County's climate.
- **Vent sealing**: Existing foundation vents are closed and sealed, shifting the crawl space from a ventilated to a conditioned or semi-conditioned environment.

Our team holds IICRC AMRT certification in mold remediation and EPA Lead-Safe certification, which matters on older Lumberton homes where disturbing existing materials can create secondary hazards.
