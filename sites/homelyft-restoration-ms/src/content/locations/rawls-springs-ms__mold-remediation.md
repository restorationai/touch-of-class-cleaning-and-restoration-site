## Why Rawls Springs Properties See Mold Issues

Forrest County's humidity averages don't drop far enough in winter to give homes a true drying-out period. That means moisture that accumulates in attic sheathing, wall cavities, or under-slab spaces during summer storm season often lingers well into fall. Mold colonies can begin forming on organic materials — wood framing, paper-faced drywall, OSB sheathing — within 24 to 48 hours of sustained moisture contact. By the time a faint earthy odor is noticeable, growth may already be widespread behind walls or beneath flooring.

Rawls Springs also has a mix of housing stock that matters for remediation planning. Homes built in the 1960s through the 1980s frequently used paper-faced fiberglass batt insulation in crawl spaces, which acts as a sponge when ground moisture rises. That insulation often has to be removed entirely and replaced as part of a thorough mold abatement scope. Newer construction is not immune — tighter building envelopes can trap interior humidity if HVAC systems are undersized or if vapor barriers were installed incorrectly during the original build.

## Our Mold Remediation Process in Rawls Springs

Every job starts with a moisture mapping inspection. Technicians use thermal imaging and pin-type moisture meters to locate wet zones that aren't visible to the naked eye — including inside wall cavities and beneath subfloor decking. This step is especially important in Rawls Springs homes with pier-and-beam foundations, where ground moisture migration can affect a much wider area than the visible damage suggests.

Once the scope is confirmed, containment barriers go up using 6-mil poly sheeting and negative air pressure machines equipped with HEPA filtration. Negative pressure keeps disturbed mold spores from migrating to unaffected rooms during removal. Contaminated materials — drywall, insulation, wood framing where warranted — are bagged and removed per EPA guidelines. Remaining structural surfaces are HEPA-vacuumed, treated with an EPA-registered antimicrobial solution, and allowed to dry to verified moisture content before any reconstruction begins. The team holds IICRC AMRT credentials and follows the IICRC S520 standard for mold remediation throughout.

Whether mold coverage applies depends heavily on the documented cause of moisture. Most homeowner policies in Mississippi cover mold remediation when it results directly from a covered peril — a burst pipe, storm-driven water intrusion, or appliance failure — but exclude mold that developed from long-term seepage or deferred maintenance.
