## Why Ovett Carpets Face a Tougher Fight

Jones County's climate sits in a humidity band that rarely dips below 60% relative humidity, even indoors with air conditioning running. That persistent moisture does two things to carpet: it keeps fibers slightly damp long enough for organic soils to bond deeply into the pile, and it creates the low-level conditions that allow odors to linger well after a visible stain has dried. The red-clay soil common throughout this part of Mississippi is a particular challenge — its fine particles penetrate carpet backing and resist standard vacuuming because the particles carry a slight electrostatic charge that makes them cling.

Older rural homes in this area, many built with pier-and-beam foundations, also experience more airflow variation under the subfloor. That movement pulls dust and allergens upward through carpet pads over time, contributing to a soiling pattern that originates from below rather than just from foot traffic above. Recognizing that pattern changes how we pre-treat and extract.

## Our Carpet Cleaning Process in Ovett

Every job starts with a fiber identification walk — wool, nylon, polyester, and olefin each respond differently to heat and cleaning agents, and getting that wrong damages the carpet permanently. Once fiber type and soiling level are confirmed, we apply a pH-balanced pre-conditioner formulated for clay-heavy soils, allowing it to dwell long enough to break the electrostatic bond between clay particles and the fiber.

Hot-water extraction follows, using truck-mounted equipment capable of reaching temperatures that sanitize while still protecting fiber integrity. Extraction pressure and temperature are dialed back for older, more delicate pile constructions common in homes that have been in families for decades. After extraction, we apply a post-treatment rinse to neutralize any residual alkalinity — skipping this step in a high-humidity environment like Ovett's leaves fibers tacky and re-soiling accelerates dramatically. Drying time is managed with air movers positioned to account for the ambient humidity, not just the carpet surface.

## Stain Removal and Carpet Sanitization

Red clay, pine sap, and pet accidents are the three calls we get most often from Jones County properties. Clay stains require a specific tannin-based spotter applied before heat — applying heat first sets the iron compounds in the clay and makes the stain permanent. Pine sap, tracked in from the surrounding timber land, responds to a solvent pre-spray but must be fully emulsified before extraction or it migrates deeper into the backing.

For sanitization — particularly relevant in homes with pets, young children, or anyone with respiratory sensitivities — we use an EPA-registered antimicrobial applied after the primary extraction pass. This step targets bacteria and odor-causing organisms that survive standard cleaning, and it's especially useful in Ovett's climate where residual moisture in pad and backing can otherwise allow microbial populations to rebound within days of cleaning.
