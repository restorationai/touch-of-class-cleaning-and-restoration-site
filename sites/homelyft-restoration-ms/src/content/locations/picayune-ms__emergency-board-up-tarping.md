## Why Picayune Properties Face Elevated Board-Up and Tarping Needs

Pearl River County's position along the I-59 corridor puts Picayune directly in the path of tropical moisture that funnels north from the Gulf. Hurricanes and named tropical storms don't have to make landfall nearby to cause serious structural damage here — outer bands routinely produce 70-plus-mph gusts that lift shingles, crack soffits, and push tree limbs through windows. The region averages more than 60 inches of rainfall annually, which means any unprotected breach in a roof or wall can introduce hundreds of gallons of water in a single rain event.

The housing stock adds another layer of complexity. Picayune has a significant number of wood-frame homes built in the 1950s through 1970s, many with original single-pane windows and older roofing systems that were never engineered to today's wind-load standards. Fire damage is also a recurring driver of board-up calls — a kitchen fire that chars a wall or blows out a window leaves the interior exposed to both weather and opportunistic entry. In these older structures, even a short exposure window can mean swollen framing, compromised insulation, and the beginning of mold colonization within 24 to 48 hours.

## Our Board-Up and Tarping Process in Picayune

When a crew arrives at a Picayune property, the first step is a rapid exterior assessment — identifying every breach point, not just the obvious one. A storm that breaks a front window often damages soffit vents or ridge caps that aren't visible from the driveway. Missing those secondary openings is how water finds its way into wall cavities and attic space undetected.

For roof tarping, we use reinforced polyethylene tarps rated for wind and UV exposure, secured with baton strips and fasteners driven into the structural decking rather than the damaged sheathing. This matters on older Picayune homes where the decking itself may be board-sheathed rather than plywood — a different fastening pattern is required to get a hold that won't pull free in the next rain. For window and door openings, we cut and install OSB panels to fit the rough opening, secured with structural screws, and we can add plywood backing on larger breaches or where the frame itself has been compromised by fire heat.

All work is documented with timestamped photographs before, during, and after installation — a record that your insurance adjuster will need and that satisfies Mississippi's requirements for emergency mitigation documentation. HomeLyft holds IICRC FSRT (Fire and Smoke Restoration) certification and is a FORTIFIED Building/Roofing MS Windstorm Certified contractor, which means our crews understand the structural context behind the damage, not just the surface opening.
