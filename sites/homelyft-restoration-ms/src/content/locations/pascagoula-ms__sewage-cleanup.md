## Why Pascagoula Properties See Sewage Backup Issues

The combination of coastal elevation, clay-heavy Gulf Coast soil, and a housing stock that includes a significant number of mid-century and older homes creates conditions that make sewage backups more common here than in inland Mississippi cities. Clay soil expands when saturated — and after any significant rain event along the coast, it expands fast. That soil movement puts lateral pressure on older clay or cast-iron sewer pipes, accelerating joint separation and root intrusion. When those compromised lines meet a surge from the municipal system, the path of least resistance is back into the home.

Pascagoula's proximity to tidal waterways also means the water table sits unusually high in many residential areas. Septic systems in older neighborhoods can be pushed to capacity during wet seasons, and septic overflow onto a yard or into a crawl space is a sanitation event that requires professional remediation — not just a garden hose. The warm, humid Mississippi Gulf Coast climate means that bacterial growth and odor development accelerate quickly once sewage contacts wood framing, insulation, or subfloor material.

## Our Sewage Cleanup and Sanitization Process in Pascagoula

Sewage cleanup is not a single step — it is a sequenced process, and skipping any phase leaves contamination behind that will resurface as odor, mold, or a failed inspection.

**Containment and safety assessment** come first. Technicians establish a contamination perimeter, shut off HVAC if it is drawing air through the affected zone, and identify whether the source is a municipal sewer backup, a failed septic system, or a broken drain line. That distinction matters for both the remediation plan and the insurance claim.

**Extraction and solid waste removal** follow. Commercial truck-mounted extractors remove standing sewage, and solid waste is bagged and disposed of in compliance with Mississippi Department of Environmental Quality guidelines for biohazardous material.

**Structural assessment and controlled demolition** address materials that cannot be cleaned. Drywall, insulation, and flooring that have absorbed Category 3 water are removed to clean structural lines. In Pascagoula's older homes, this sometimes means encountering original hardwood subfloors or pier-and-beam framing that requires careful evaluation before a demolition decision is made.

**Antimicrobial treatment and drying** close the remediation phase. EPA-registered disinfectants are applied to all affected structural surfaces, and commercial desiccant or refrigerant dehumidifiers — sized to the actual cubic footage of the space — run until moisture readings confirm the structure has returned to normal levels per the IICRC S500 standard.
