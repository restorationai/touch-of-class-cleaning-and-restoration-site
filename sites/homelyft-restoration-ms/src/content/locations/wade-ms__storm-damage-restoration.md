## Why Wade Properties See Serious Storm Damage

Forrest County's mix of mature hardwood and pine stands means tree strike is one of the most common storm damage scenarios in this area. Older homes throughout the Wade community often feature wood-framed construction with pier-and-beam foundations, which can shift when saturated soil moves after a heavy rain event. That movement creates gaps at sill plates and rim joists — entry points for water that wouldn't exist in a slab-on-grade home.

Mississippi's Gulf-influenced climate also means that even a storm that doesn't make headlines can push sustained winds above 60 mph, enough to lift ridge cap shingles, compromise soffit venting, and force wind-driven rain horizontally into attic spaces. Once moisture reaches attic insulation, mold colonization can begin within 24 to 48 hours under summer humidity conditions — a timeline that makes fast assessment critical, not optional.

Because Wade is served by rural electric cooperative infrastructure rather than a large municipal grid, power restoration after a significant storm can lag behind more urbanized areas. That extended outage window affects sump pump operation and HVAC dehumidification — two systems that normally work in your favor when drying a structure.

## Our Storm Damage Restoration Process in Wade

Every job starts with a systematic damage assessment before any debris is moved. Photographing the loss in its original state matters for insurance documentation, and moving materials prematurely can obscure the true scope of structural damage. The team walks the exterior and interior, probing for hidden moisture with thermal imaging and pin-type meters, and maps the affected zones before writing a scope of work.

Debris removal — fallen limbs, displaced roofing materials, shattered glass — comes next, followed by emergency tarping or board-up to stop ongoing water intrusion. For roof penetrations, we use reinforced polyethylene sheeting secured with battens, not loose tarps that the next wind event will strip away.

Structural drying follows IICRC S500 and S520 protocols. Commercial-grade desiccant dehumidifiers and high-velocity air movers are positioned based on the psychrometric readings taken at assessment, not by guesswork. Moisture logs are recorded daily so the drying curve is documented — useful both for confirming the structure is dry and for supporting your insurance claim.

Reconstruction — framing repairs, sheathing replacement, drywall, roofing — is handled in-house. One contractor managing the full scope means fewer handoffs and a single point of accountability from first call to final walkthrough.
