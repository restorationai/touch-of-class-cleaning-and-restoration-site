## Why Poplarville Properties See More Water Damage Than Homeowners Expect

Pearl River County averages well over 60 inches of rainfall per year — significantly above the national average — and Poplarville's position in the piney woods region means the clay-heavy soils beneath many homes absorb water slowly and hold it longer. When the ground becomes saturated after multi-day rain events, hydrostatic pressure builds against foundation walls and crawl space enclosures. Older pier-and-beam homes common throughout the area are particularly vulnerable: the open crawl space that gives these houses their character also gives standing water a place to pool invisibly beneath the subfloor, warping joists and soaking insulation before a homeowner notices any sign from inside.

Beyond rainfall, aging plumbing infrastructure in homes built in the 1960s through 1980s — a common vintage in Poplarville's residential neighborhoods — means galvanized supply lines and cast-iron drain pipes that are reaching or exceeding their service life. A slow pinhole leak inside a wall cavity can go undetected for weeks, quietly saturating wall cavities and floor systems. By the time a stain appears on drywall or a floor starts to feel soft underfoot, the moisture has typically spread well beyond what's visible.

## Our Water Damage Restoration Process in Poplarville

Every job starts with a thorough moisture inspection using thermal imaging cameras and calibrated moisture meters — tools that find water in wall cavities, under flooring, and inside ceiling assemblies that a visual check would miss entirely. Once the full scope is mapped, we extract standing water using truck-mounted and portable extraction units, then transition to the drying phase.

Structural drying in Poplarville's climate requires careful calibration. High ambient humidity — especially in summer months when outdoor dew points regularly exceed 70°F — means drying equipment has to work against the environment, not just the structure. We deploy commercial-grade desiccant or refrigerant dehumidifiers sized to the affected area, paired with high-velocity air movers positioned to create the airflow patterns the IICRC S500 standard requires for effective evaporative drying. Moisture readings are logged daily so you have a documented drying record — something most insurance carriers require before closing a claim.

For pier-and-beam homes, crawl space drying is a separate phase that often gets skipped by less thorough contractors. We treat the crawl space as a distinct drying zone, addressing soil moisture and vapor intrusion alongside the structural members above it.
