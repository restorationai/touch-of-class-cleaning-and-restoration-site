## Why Soso Properties See Mold Issues

Jones County's climate sits in a humid subtropical band that rarely gives wood-framed structures a true dry-out period. Summer relative humidity regularly exceeds 80 percent, and the sandy loam soils common around Soso drain unevenly — water from a heavy rain event can pool against a pier-and-beam foundation for days before it dissipates. Crawl spaces in these older homes are especially vulnerable: ground moisture wicks upward, floor joists stay damp, and without an intact vapor barrier the underside of a subfloor becomes a reliable mold habitat. Homeowners sometimes notice a musty smell in the living area long before they see visible growth, because the colony is already established below the floor deck.

HVAC systems also play a role here. Many homes in this part of Jones County run their air handlers year-round to manage heat, and a slow condensate-pan leak or a cracked duct joint can drip onto insulation for weeks undetected. Fiberglass batt insulation saturated with condensate is one of the most common mold substrates the team encounters on jobs in this area — it holds moisture without showing obvious staining until the colony is well established.

## Our Mold Remediation Process in Soso

Every job starts with a thorough moisture mapping inspection. Technicians use thermal imaging cameras and calibrated moisture meters to locate elevated readings behind walls, under flooring, and in crawl spaces — not just the visible growth. That step matters because treating only what you can see leaves active moisture sources intact, and regrowth typically follows within weeks.

Once the scope is defined, containment goes up: heavy-gauge poly barriers isolate the work area and negative air pressure is established using HEPA-filtered air scrubbers so spores disturbed during removal cannot migrate to clean areas of the home. Affected porous materials — drywall, insulation, compromised wood sheathing — are removed, bagged, and disposed of per Mississippi Department of Environmental Quality guidelines. Remaining structural surfaces are HEPA-vacuumed, treated with an EPA-registered antimicrobial, and dried to verified moisture levels before any reconstruction begins. The process follows the IICRC S520 Standard for Professional Mold Remediation throughout.
