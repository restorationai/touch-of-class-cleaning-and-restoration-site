## Why Pearlington Properties Face Elevated Water Damage Risk

Pearlington is one of the lowest-elevation communities on the Mississippi Gulf Coast. Much of the area drains poorly because the Pearl River delta soil — a mix of silty clay and organic marsh substrate — holds water rather than shedding it. After a heavy rain event, that saturated ground pushes hydrostatic pressure against slabs and crawl spaces long after the rain stops. A small plumbing failure that might dry out on its own in a higher-elevation neighborhood can become a sustained moisture event here because the soil never fully releases.

The local housing stock adds another layer of complexity. Katrina wiped out the majority of pre-storm structures, so a significant share of Pearlington homes were built or rebuilt between 2006 and 2015 using FEMA elevation requirements and Mississippi's post-storm building codes. Elevated pier-and-beam construction is common, which means water intrusion often collects in the enclosed under-floor space before anyone notices it inside the home. By the time a homeowner smells mildew or feels soft spots in the flooring, secondary damage — warped subfloor sheathing, compromised insulation batts, early mold colonization — may already be underway. Mold can begin to establish within 24 to 48 hours of a moisture event in warm, humid conditions, and Pearlington's Gulf Coast climate provides exactly that environment for most of the year.

## Our Water Damage Restoration Process in Pearlington

Every job starts with a moisture mapping inspection. Technicians use thermal imaging cameras and calibrated moisture meters to trace water migration through walls, flooring assemblies, and sub-slab areas — because in Pearlington's pier-and-beam homes, water rarely stays where it first entered. Once the full scope is mapped, standing water is removed with truck-mounted extraction equipment capable of pulling hundreds of gallons from affected spaces quickly.

Structural drying follows the IICRC S500 standard, which means drying goals are set based on material type and ambient conditions rather than a fixed number of days. In Pearlington's high-humidity climate, ambient air is often at or above 80% relative humidity during warmer months, which means passive drying is not an option. Industrial desiccant or refrigerant dehumidifiers are placed to create a controlled drying environment, and readings are logged daily to verify the drying curve is progressing. Affected materials — insulation, drywall, subfloor panels — are assessed individually. Salvageable materials are dried in place when structurally sound; materials that have absorbed beyond recovery thresholds are removed to prevent hidden mold growth.

Because many Pearlington properties were rebuilt with elevated floor systems, technicians frequently need to access the under-floor cavity to extract pooled water and place drying equipment below the subfloor deck. This is a step that gets skipped in a hurried job and leads to callbacks months later when the homeowner notices the floor is moving.

## Pearlington Insurance Coordination

Water damage claims in Hancock County follow standard Mississippi Department of Insurance guidelines, but the specifics of what a carrier will cover often hinge on documentation quality. HomeLyft Restoration MS photographs affected materials before any extraction begins, documents moisture readings at each measurement point, and produces a written scope of loss that aligns with Xactimate line-item formatting — the format most adjusters work from. This documentation record matters especially in Pearlington, where some properties carry both standard homeowner's policies and separate flood policies through the National Flood Insurance Program. The line between "sudden and accidental" water damage and flood-origin damage affects which policy responds, and having a thorough, timestamped record of conditions at arrival helps adjusters make that determination accurately.

## Local Note

One pattern that comes up repeatedly in Pearlington's post-Katrina rebuilt homes: the engineered wood I-joists used in many elevated floor systems are significantly more vulnerable to water damage than the solid lumber they replaced. I-joists are strong under load but the oriented strand board web at the center delaminates quickly when wet, and a delaminated I-joist cannot be dried back to structural integrity — it has to be replaced. Homeowners sometimes push back on replacement recommendations because the joist looks intact from below, but if moisture readings on the web material are elevated, the structural integrity is already compromised. This is a conversation we have regularly on Pearlington jobs, and we document our moisture readings so the homeowner and their engineer or adjuster can make an informed decision.
