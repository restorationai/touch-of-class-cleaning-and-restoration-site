## Why Lyman Properties Are Vulnerable to Severe Fire Damage

Lyman sits in a part of Harrison County where the housing stock skews toward single-family wood-frame construction — many homes built in the 1970s through 1990s with open attic spaces and minimal fire blocking between wall cavities. When fire enters those voids, it travels quickly and quietly, scorching structural members that look intact from the finished side of the drywall. That hidden char is one of the most common surprises during post-fire structural assessment in this area.

The coastal climate compounds the problem. Lyman's average relative humidity regularly exceeds 75 percent in summer months, which means smoke odor compounds — particularly the aldehydes and phenols left by burning synthetic materials — bind to surfaces faster and deeper than they would in a drier region. Protein-based smoke from kitchen fires is especially stubborn here; without thermal fogging or hydroxyl treatment calibrated to the ambient conditions, odors resurface weeks after a surface cleaning.

Local utility infrastructure is another factor worth noting. Harrison County properties on well systems can lose water pressure during a fire event, affecting how thoroughly the fire department can suppress the blaze before structural damage deepens. That means fire damage in Lyman sometimes involves more extensive char than a comparable fire in a municipality with high-pressure hydrant systems.

## Our Fire Damage Restoration Process in Lyman

Every fire loss is different, but the sequence we follow is grounded in the IICRC S700 standard for fire and smoke restoration — a framework that accounts for the type of fire, the materials burned, and the environmental conditions at the property.

**Step 1 — Emergency stabilization.** We board windows, tarp compromised roof sections, and secure the structure against weather intrusion. In Lyman's climate, an open roof after a fire is an invitation for secondary water damage within hours.

**Step 2 — Scope and documentation.** Our technicians photograph and document every affected surface, structural member, and content item. This documentation package is formatted to meet the requirements of most major insurance carriers and becomes the foundation of your claim file.

**Step 3 — Debris removal and dry-out.** Fire suppression water is extracted and affected materials are dried before restoration begins. Skipping this step leads to mold colonization — which in Harrison County's humidity can begin in as little as 48 to 72 hours.

**Step 4 — Smoke and soot remediation.** We use dry chemical sponges for dry smoke, wet cleaning agents for wet smoke residue, and thermal fogging or hydroxyl generation for odor neutralization — matched to the specific smoke type present, not a one-size approach.

**Step 5 — Structural repair and reconstruction.** Where framing, sheathing, or finish materials are compromised, our licensed reconstruction team handles repairs under Mississippi contractor license RO 2728, keeping the project under one roof rather than requiring you to hire a separate rebuild contractor.
