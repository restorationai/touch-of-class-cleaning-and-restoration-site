Hattiesburg's humid subtropical climate — long summers that push relative humidity well above 80%, heavy Gulf-influenced rainfall, and soils that stay saturated for weeks after a storm system moves through — creates exactly the conditions that turn an unprotected crawl space into a slow-motion moisture problem. Vapor migrates upward through bare dirt, condenses on floor joists, and feeds mold colonies that can establish within 48 to 72 hours of a wet event. Crawl space encapsulation stops that cycle by sealing the ground and walls with a continuous heavy-mil barrier, conditioning the air below your living space, and giving your home's structure a fighting chance against the Pine Belt's relentless humidity.

## Why Hattiesburg Properties Are Particularly Vulnerable

Forrest County sits in a transition zone where Gulf moisture streams inland and stalls. Rainfall totals routinely exceed 55 inches per year, and the clay-heavy soils common throughout the Hattiesburg area drain slowly — meaning water lingers against foundation walls and under slabs long after the rain stops. Many homes built during the mid-20th century construction boom were designed with vented crawl spaces, a standard that made sense in drier climates but actively draws humid outside air underneath the house during Mississippi summers. That warm, moisture-laden air hits the cooler surfaces of floor joists and ductwork, and condensation follows.

Older neighborhoods with pier-and-beam construction are especially susceptible. The original builders used Southern yellow pine for floor framing — a dense, strong wood that also happens to be a preferred food source for wood-decaying fungi once moisture content climbs above 19 percent. By the time a homeowner notices a soft spot in the floor or a musty smell drifting up from below, the structural damage is often already underway.

## Our Encapsulation Process, Calibrated for Hattiesburg Conditions

Every encapsulation job starts with a thorough crawl space assessment — measuring moisture levels in the wood framing, checking for existing mold growth, and mapping any standing water or drainage deficiencies. Because Hattiesburg's soil can hold water long after a rain event, we address drainage first when needed: French drains or sump pump installations go in before any liner touches the ground.

Once drainage is confirmed, we install a 20-mil reinforced polyethylene vapor barrier across the entire crawl space floor and up the foundation walls, sealed at every seam and mechanically fastened at the perimeter. Vents are sealed and insulated — a counterintuitive step for homeowners used to older building codes, but one that dramatically reduces the volume of humid outside air entering the space. A properly sized dehumidifier, sized to the actual cubic footage of your crawl space rather than a generic unit, maintains relative humidity below 55 percent year-round. The team holds IICRC AMRT (Mold) and IICRC ASD (Structural Drying) certifications, so the drying science behind every installation is grounded in current industry standards rather than guesswork.

Crawl space encapsulation sits in a gray zone for homeowners' insurance: if the moisture problem stems from a sudden, covered event — a burst pipe, a storm-driven water intrusion — portions of the remediation and encapsulation may be claimable. If it's the result of long-term humidity accumulation, coverage is less likely.
