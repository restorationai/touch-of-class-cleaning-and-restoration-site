## Why Hide-A-Way Lake Properties See Mold Issues

The lake itself is the first factor. Properties on or near the water carry persistently elevated relative humidity — often 70 percent or higher indoors during summer months without aggressive dehumidification. When air conditioning cycles off at night, that moisture condenses on cooler surfaces: the underside of subfloors, the back of exterior wall sheathing, inside closets on exterior walls. Many of the homes here were built in the 1970s and 1980s, before modern vapor barriers and building-science standards were routine in Mississippi residential construction. Crawl spaces in that era were often left open or vented in ways that actually pull ground moisture up into the structure rather than expelling it.

Rankin County's clay-heavy soil compounds the problem. After a heavy rain — and the area averages over 55 inches annually — that clay holds water against pier foundations and crawl space walls for days. Combine that with the shade from the tall pines that give the community its character, and you have exterior surfaces that stay wet far longer than they would in an open subdivision. Gutters that overflow or downspouts that discharge too close to the foundation are among the most common triggers we trace mold back to on these properties.

## Our Mold Remediation Process in Hide-A-Way Lake

Every job starts with a thorough inspection — not just the room where you spotted discoloration, but the crawl space, attic, and any wall cavities adjacent to plumbing or exterior exposure. Mold follows moisture, and moisture in a Hide-A-Way Lake home rarely stays in one place.

Once the scope is mapped, we establish containment using negative-air pressure and heavy-gauge poly barriers to prevent spores from migrating to clean areas of the house during work. HEPA air scrubbers run continuously throughout the remediation. Affected porous materials — drywall, insulation, wood framing where colonization is deep — are removed and bagged per EPA guidelines. Structural surfaces are then treated and encapsulated using EPA-registered antimicrobials. Because the underlying moisture problem is what allowed mold to establish in the first place, we address source conditions — crawl space encapsulation, drainage corrections, or ventilation improvements — before closing out the job. Leaving the moisture pathway open just restarts the clock.
