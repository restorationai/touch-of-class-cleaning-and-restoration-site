## Why Gulf Park Estates Homes Present Unique Renovation Challenges

The housing stock in Gulf Park Estates skews toward mid-century ranch and cottage construction — slab-on-grade or pier-and-beam homes built in the 1960s through 1980s, many of which predate modern wind-load and moisture-management codes. That era of construction often means original wiring that can't support a modern kitchen appliance load, galvanized supply lines that have been quietly corroding for decades, and insulation values that were adequate for 1972 but leave today's utility bills painful during a Mississippi August.

Then there's the soil. The low-lying terrain near the Back Bay means expansive clay soils in some pockets and sandy fill in others — both of which can shift under slab foundations over time. Before framing a room addition or repositioning a load-bearing wall, a proper renovation scope here includes a foundation assessment, not just a tape measure. Skipping that step is how a remodel turns into a structural repair mid-project.

Humidity is the other constant. Interior relative humidity in Gulf Park Estates routinely climbs above 70% in summer, which means new drywall, hardwood flooring, and millwork need to acclimate on-site before installation — not just be delivered and hung the same day. Contractors who don't account for that step see gaps in hardwood floors and tape lines in drywall within a single season.
