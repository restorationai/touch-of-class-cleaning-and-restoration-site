State Line sits right on the Mississippi-Alabama border, and the humidity that rolls through Greene County for most of the year doesn't pause when a pipe bursts or a kitchen fire fills a home with smoke. When that happens, your furniture, clothing, documents, and keepsakes are at risk within hours — not days. Contents restoration and storage is the part of disaster recovery most homeowners don't think about until they're standing in a damaged room wondering what can actually be saved. The answer, more often than not, is more than you'd expect — if the work starts quickly and is done right.

## Why State Line Properties Face Particular Contents Risks

The Greene County climate is humid for a long stretch of the year, with summer dew points that make even modest water intrusion a fast-moving problem. Upholstered furniture, wood cabinetry, and fabric window treatments can begin absorbing ambient moisture within the first 24 hours after a loss event, compounding the original damage. Fire losses in this area carry an additional wrinkle: homes heated with wood stoves or older HVAC systems — common in the rural stretches of State Line — tend to circulate smoke residue through ductwork and into closets and drawers that look untouched from the doorway. That residue sets into fabrics and finishes if it isn't addressed within the first few days.

Older homes in the State Line area also tend to have wood floors and solid-wood furniture that respond differently to moisture than composite or engineered materials. Solid wood swells, then can warp or crack as it dries unevenly — which means the drying environment matters as much as the drying speed. Moving contents to a controlled storage facility while the structure is being dried and repaired is often the single best decision a homeowner can make to protect heirloom pieces.

## Our Contents Pack-Out and Restoration Process

Every contents restoration job begins with a detailed inventory. Before anything leaves your home, our team photographs, catalogs, and condition-notes each item — furniture, electronics, artwork, clothing, documents, and anything else affected. That inventory becomes the backbone of your insurance claim and gives you a clear record of what was removed and where it went.

From there, affected items are packed out and transported to a climate-controlled storage facility where temperature and humidity are held steady — a significant advantage over leaving belongings in a damaged home where conditions can swing dramatically. Cleaning methods are matched to the material: ultrasonic cleaning for hard surfaces and small valuables, ozone or thermal fogging for smoke-saturated textiles, dry-cleaning coordination for garments, and document drying for papers and photos. Items that can be restored are cleaned, deodorized, and held until your home is ready to receive them. Items that cannot be restored are documented for your insurance adjuster.

Our team holds IICRC certifications in fire and smoke restoration (FSRT) as well as water restoration (WRT), which means the people handling your belongings understand how different damage types interact — smoke residue on water-damaged wood, for example, requires a different sequence than either problem alone.

## Working with Your Insurance Carrier on a State Line Loss

Most homeowner policies in Mississippi cover contents losses caused by sudden and accidental events — a burst pipe, a kitchen fire, a washing machine overflow. The documentation we produce during pack-out (photos, condition notes, item-by-item inventory) is formatted to support your adjuster's review and is typically what separates a smooth claim from a disputed one. We work directly with carriers and can communicate with your adjuster on scope and line items, which reduces the back-and-forth you'd otherwise have to manage yourself while also dealing with a displaced household.

If you're renting in the State Line area, your renters policy may cover personal property even when the building damage is the landlord's responsibility — worth a call to your agent before assuming coverage doesn't apply.

## Local Note

One thing that comes up specifically in the State Line corridor: homes close to the state line sometimes have contents stored in outbuildings or detached garages that were affected by the same loss event as the main structure. Alabama and Mississippi have slightly different rules around what's considered a "covered structure" under a homeowner policy, and contents in a detached building may fall under a separate sublimit. When we do the initial walk-through, we include outbuildings in the inventory assessment and flag any items there so your adjuster has the full picture — not just what was inside the four walls of the house.
