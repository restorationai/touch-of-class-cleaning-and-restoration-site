## Why Big Point Properties Are at Higher Risk for Asbestos-Containing Materials

Jackson County's building boom of the 1950s through the 1970s left a dense footprint of homes that were built during the peak years of asbestos use in American construction. In coastal Mississippi, builders favored materials that could stand up to heat, moisture, and the occasional hurricane — and asbestos fit that profile. Pipe wrap on older HVAC systems, vinyl floor tiles in kitchens and bathrooms, and the adhesive mastics beneath them were almost universally asbestos-containing during that era.

The Gulf Coast climate adds another layer of urgency. High humidity and the thermal cycling between hot summers and mild winters cause building materials to expand, contract, and degrade over time. When asbestos-containing materials become brittle or friable — meaning they crumble when touched — fibers can become airborne. A slow roof leak, a flooded crawl space, or even routine ductwork cleaning in an older Big Point home can disturb materials that were otherwise stable. That's when exposure risk jumps.

## Our Asbestos Abatement Process in Big Point

Every project starts with asbestos testing, not assumptions. A certified inspector collects bulk samples from suspect materials — floor tiles, ceiling texture, pipe insulation, roofing underlayment — and sends them to an accredited laboratory. Results typically come back within a few business days, and they determine exactly what we're dealing with before any removal work begins.

Once testing confirms the presence of asbestos-containing materials, the abatement scope is built around Mississippi Department of Environmental Quality (MDEQ) regulations and EPA guidelines. Work areas are isolated with negative-air containment barriers to prevent fiber migration into unaffected parts of the structure. Our team works in full personal protective equipment, and all removed materials are wetted, double-bagged in approved containers, and transported to a licensed disposal facility — no shortcuts, no exceptions.

For popcorn ceiling asbestos removal specifically, we take extra care in older Big Point homes where the texture may have been painted over multiple times, which can actually help contain fibers during removal but requires a different wetting and scoring technique than unpainted surfaces. After abatement is complete, air clearance testing confirms fiber levels are within safe limits before containment is removed and the space is released for re-occupancy or renovation.
