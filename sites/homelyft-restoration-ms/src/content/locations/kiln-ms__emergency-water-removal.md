## Why Kiln Properties See Water Damage

Much of Kiln is unincorporated Hancock County, built on heavy clay soil that drains slowly compared to the sandier ground closer to the coast. That soil holds water against foundations longer after a heavy rain, which is part of why so many homes out here were built up on piers or raised slabs rather than full basements. Add in well water and septic systems on most lots instead of municipal hookups, and a water event here behaves differently than one in a subdivision with curb drains and a sewer main. Hurricane season brings the bigger risk: slow-moving tropical systems can sit over the Jourdan River basin and dump rain for days, pushing water into crawlspaces and low-lying additions long after the wind has died down.

## Our Water Extraction Process in Kiln

We start with truck-mounted extraction to pull standing water fast, then move to moisture mapping under subfloors and around pier footings, which is where water likes to hide in this kind of construction. Crawlspace homes get extra attention because trapped moisture under the floor joists doesn't show on a surface walk-through the way it does in a slab home with wet carpet. Once extraction is done, we set dehumidifiers sized for Gulf Coast humidity levels, not just the water volume, because ambient moisture here fights the drying process even on a clear day. Materials are dried per IICRC S500 guidelines, and every reading gets logged so you have documentation if a claim follows.

## Kiln Insurance & HOA Coordination

Most of Kiln falls outside the kind of HOA-governed subdivisions you'd find closer to Gulfport or Diamondhead, so the paperwork side of a water loss here is usually a straight homeowner's policy or an NFIP flood policy if the property sits in a mapped floodplain near the Jourdan River. We document moisture readings, photograph affected materials, and bill most major carriers directly, and if flood coverage is involved we make sure the claim distinguishes between wind-driven rain intrusion and rising water, since carriers treat those very differently.

## Local note

On properties with a septic system, a water emergency inside the house can mean the drain field is already saturated from the same rain event that caused the problem, which changes how we manage extracted water on site rather than just running it out the nearest low point in the yard. It's a detail that doesn't come up on a municipal sewer system, but it matters here, and it's the kind of thing you only catch by working Hancock County properties regularly.
