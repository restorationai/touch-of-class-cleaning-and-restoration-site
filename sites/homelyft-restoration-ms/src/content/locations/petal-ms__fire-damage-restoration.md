## Why Petal Properties Are Particularly Vulnerable After a Fire

Petal sits in a climate zone where summer humidity regularly climbs above 80 percent, and that ambient moisture doesn't pause for a fire loss. Once a fire is extinguished, water from suppression efforts — hoses, sprinklers, even fire extinguishers — combines with that background humidity to accelerate secondary damage. Soot and smoke residue are hygroscopic: they pull moisture from the air and bond it to surfaces, making odor removal and surface cleaning significantly harder the longer materials sit untreated.

The residential housing stock in Petal skews toward ranch-style and split-level homes built in the 1970s through 1990s, many of which used wood-paneled interiors, textured ceilings, and older HVAC ductwork that acts like a highway for smoke particles. Duct systems in these homes can distribute fine soot throughout rooms that never saw direct flame — meaning a kitchen fire can leave detectable odor in bedrooms on the opposite end of the house. That's not a worst-case scenario; it's a common one here.

## Our Fire Damage Restoration Process in Petal

Every fire loss is different, but the sequence of work follows a disciplined framework. After an initial walkthrough to assess structural safety and scope, the team secures the structure — boarding windows, tarping roof damage, and stabilizing any compromised framing. Petal's warm, wet climate makes this step especially time-sensitive; an open roof in Forrest County during a summer rainstorm compounds a fire loss into a simultaneous water loss.

From there, contents are inventoried and moved to a staging area for cleaning or disposal. Soot removal from hard surfaces uses dry chemical sponges and HEPA-filtered vacuums before any wet cleaning begins — applying water to dry soot smears it deeper into porous materials. Thermal fogging and hydroxyl generation address odor at the molecular level rather than masking it. Structural materials that cannot be cleaned to pre-loss condition are removed, documented, and replaced. Our team holds the IICRC FSRT (Fire and Smoke Restoration Technician) certification, and all work is performed to IICRC S700 standards.

For homes with older ductwork — common in Petal's 1980s-era subdivisions — duct cleaning is performed after the main structure is cleared, not before, to prevent re-contamination of cleaned surfaces.
