## Why Gulf Hills Properties Are Particularly Vulnerable After a Fire

The residential character of Gulf Hills is defined largely by mid-century construction — ranch-style homes and pier-and-beam builds that were popular throughout coastal Mississippi in the 1950s through 1970s. These homes often feature original wood-paneled interiors, tongue-and-groove ceilings, and older insulation materials that absorb smoke and combustion byproducts far more aggressively than modern drywall and spray foam. Once soot works into those surfaces, standard surface cleaning is not enough — it requires controlled chemical sponging, dry-ice blasting in some cases, and careful attention to what can be restored versus what must be removed.

The Gulf Coast climate compounds this. Average relative humidity in this part of Jackson County regularly exceeds 75%, even in cooler months. After a fire, suppression water does not evaporate quickly on its own. Without active structural drying — using industrial dehumidifiers and air movers calibrated to the ambient conditions — wet framing and insulation will begin supporting mold growth within 24 to 48 hours of the fire event. That secondary damage often costs as much to address as the fire damage itself.

## Our Fire Damage Restoration Process in Gulf Hills

Every fire loss is different, but the sequence of work follows a disciplined path. The first step on-site is a thorough assessment — identifying the burn origin, mapping the spread of smoke and soot through the structure, and documenting all affected materials with photographs and moisture readings. This documentation is critical both for the restoration scope and for your insurance claim.

Once the structure is secured and utilities are confirmed safe, the team begins debris removal and controlled demolition of unsalvageable materials. In Gulf Hills homes with older construction, this phase requires particular care: original hardwood floors, cypress framing, and decorative millwork are often worth restoring rather than replacing, and experienced technicians know the difference. Salvageable materials are cleaned using dry chemical sponges, HEPA vacuuming, and where appropriate, thermal fogging or hydroxyl generation to neutralize embedded smoke odors at the molecular level.

Structural drying runs concurrently with smoke remediation, because suppression water left in wall cavities or under flooring will undermine every other step. HomeLyft's team holds both IICRC FSRT (fire and smoke) and IICRC WRT and ASD credentials, which means the same crew handles both the fire cleanup and the drying — no handoff delays, no coordination gaps.
