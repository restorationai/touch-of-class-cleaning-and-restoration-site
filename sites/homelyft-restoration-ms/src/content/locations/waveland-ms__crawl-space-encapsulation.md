## Why Waveland Properties Are Especially Vulnerable Below the Floor

The Gulf Coast climate is the primary driver, but Waveland has a few compounding factors worth understanding. A significant portion of the housing stock here was rebuilt or heavily repaired after Hurricane Katrina in 2005, which means many crawl spaces were constructed quickly under post-storm demand — sometimes with vapor barriers that were undersized, improperly lapped, or never installed at all. Homes along the beachside corridors and those closer to Bay St. Louis to the west also tend to sit on pier-and-beam or block foundations rather than slabs, leaving a direct pathway for ground moisture to migrate upward into living spaces.

Sandy coastal soils drain quickly after rain but stay damp at depth, and that capillary moisture rises steadily through an unprotected dirt crawl space floor. Combine that with Waveland's average summer dew points — regularly in the low 70s °F — and you have outside air that is nearly saturated before it even enters the crawl space vents. Traditional "passive ventilation" strategies that work in drier climates actively make things worse here by pulling that humid Gulf air directly under the floor system.

## Our Encapsulation Process, Calibrated for the Gulf Coast

Encapsulation in a coastal Mississippi climate is not the same job it is in a drier inland market. The process starts with a thorough inspection of the existing crawl space — checking for standing water, active wood decay, existing vapor barrier remnants, and any signs of mold growth on the joists or subfloor. If mold is present, remediation happens first; encapsulating over active mold growth only seals the problem in.

Once the space is clean and dry, the work proceeds in a specific sequence:

- **Ground cover installation** — a reinforced polyethylene liner (typically 20-mil) is laid across the entire dirt floor, overlapped at seams by at least 12 inches, and sealed with moisture-resistant tape rated for the application.
- **Wall and pier wrapping** — the liner runs up foundation walls and is mechanically fastened and sealed at the top, closing off the soil-to-air moisture pathway entirely.
- **Vent sealing** — existing foundation vents are closed and sealed. In a high-humidity coastal environment, open vents are a liability, not a benefit.
- **Dehumidification** — a crawl-space-rated dehumidifier is installed and sized to the square footage of the space, maintaining relative humidity below 60% year-round without manual intervention.
- **Drainage provisions** — if the inspection reveals any grading or drainage issues, a perimeter drain channel or sump system is incorporated before the liner goes down.

The team holds IICRC certification in Water Damage Restoration and Applied Structural Drying, which informs how we assess moisture conditions and verify dryness before closing out a job — not just a visual check, but actual moisture readings in the wood framing.
