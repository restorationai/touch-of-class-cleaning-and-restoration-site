## Why Waveland Properties See Water Removal Issues

Much of Waveland's housing stock was rebuilt or elevated after Hurricane Katrina, so you'll find a mix of newer homes raised on pilings alongside older slab-on-grade houses that predate the storm. The elevated homes handle storm surge better, but their crawlspaces and under-structure plumbing runs are still vulnerable to leaks that go unnoticed until insulation is saturated. The slab homes, meanwhile, trap water against concrete with nowhere to drain, so a washing machine supply line failure or water heater rupture can sit against baseboards and drywall for hours before anyone notices the smell. Add in the area's sandy coastal soil, which holds groundwater close to the surface after heavy rain, and basements or low crawlspaces can seep even without a direct storm hit.

## Our Water Removal Process in Waveland

We start with extraction using truck-mounted and portable pumps rated for the volume we're dealing with, whether that's a few inches in a utility room or several inches across an open-concept slab home. Moisture meters tell us how far water has traveled into drywall, baseboards, and subflooring before we set air movers and dehumidifiers, because what looks dry on the surface in a coastal Mississippi home often isn't dry underneath. Humidity here runs high most of the year, so drying times get calibrated accordingly rather than assuming the equipment will clear the air as fast as it might in a drier inland climate. We document moisture readings throughout the job, which matters both for drying confirmation and for insurance records.
