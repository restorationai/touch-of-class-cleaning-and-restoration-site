## Why Pascagoula Properties Face Repeated Board-Up and Tarping Needs

The Gulf Coast's exposure to named storms is well documented, but Pascagoula's specific geography adds another layer of risk. The city's position along the Singing River — as locals call the Pascagoula River — means that even a tropical system making landfall well to the west can push storm surge and sustained wind into neighborhoods that sit only a few feet above sea level. That wind-driven rain doesn't wait for a roofer's schedule.

The local housing stock compounds the challenge. A significant portion of Pascagoula's residential properties were built in the postwar decades of the 1950s through 1970s, when roof decking was often thinner and fastener patterns less robust than what Mississippi's current wind-load codes require. Older wood-framed homes with original rooflines are more susceptible to partial decking loss during high-wind events — meaning a tarp installed correctly and anchored with weighted perimeter strapping isn't just a temporary fix, it's the difference between a manageable repair and a total interior loss. Properties in lower-lying sections of the city also face the compounding risk of wind damage followed almost immediately by water intrusion, which can trigger mold colonization within 24 to 48 hours if the opening isn't sealed.

Fire damage board-up is a separate but equally pressing need in Pascagoula. Older balloon-frame construction — common in homes built before the 1960s — can allow fire to travel through wall cavities quickly, leaving charred openings that expose the interior to weather and unauthorized entry before the fire marshal's scene release is even complete.

## Our Board-Up and Tarping Process in Pascagoula

Every job starts with a rapid visual assessment of the full envelope — roof, windows, doors, and any fire-compromised wall sections — before a single sheet of plywood goes up. That sequence matters because boarding a window while leaving an unseen soffit breach open defeats the purpose entirely.

For roof tarping, we use heavy-duty polyethylene tarps rated for Gulf Coast conditions, secured with 2×4 cap boards screwed through the tarp and into structural decking rather than simply weighted at the edges. On steeper-pitched roofs common in older Pascagoula neighborhoods, proper anchor points are critical — a tarp that peels back in the next wind event has accomplished nothing. For broken window board-up and fire damage board-up, we cut OSB panels to fit the rough opening, use construction adhesive at the perimeter where framing is intact, and fasten with screws rather than nails so the panels can be removed cleanly when permanent repairs begin.

We document every opening with photographs before and after installation. That documentation goes directly into your insurance claim file — carriers want to see the scope of the opening, the materials used, and the installation method, and having it organized from day one shortens the adjustment process.

As an IICRC Certified Firm holding IICRC FSRT (Fire & Smoke) credentials and FORTIFIED Building Roofing MS Windstorm Certification, our crews understand how wind-driven water behaves once it's inside a structure — which informs how we prioritize and sequence each opening we close.
