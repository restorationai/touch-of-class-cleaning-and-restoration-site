## Why Moselle Homes Present Unique Renovation Challenges

Jones County's combination of clay-heavy soil and high annual rainfall creates a foundation environment that shifts seasonally. Homes built in the 1960s and 1970s — a common vintage in this part of the county — often have pier-and-beam foundations that have settled unevenly over the decades. Before a single cabinet is hung or a tile is set, a thorough renovation here starts with understanding what the structure beneath is actually doing. Crawl spaces in this region regularly show moisture intrusion, wood rot at sill plates, and occasional mold colonization that has to be addressed before cosmetic work begins.

The Gulf South humidity also means that materials acclimate differently here than they would in a drier climate. Hardwood flooring, for instance, needs extra time to acclimate to interior conditions before installation — skip that step and you will see cupping and gapping within a season. Our team accounts for these material behaviors at the planning stage, not after the flooring is already down.

For post-damage rebuilds specifically — homes that took on water from a plumbing failure or fire damage from an electrical fault — Mississippi's building codes require permitted work for structural repairs, electrical upgrades, and plumbing modifications. We handle the permitting process through Jones County's building department so homeowners are not navigating that paperwork alone.

## Our Renovation and General Contracting Process in Moselle

## Local Note

One thing that catches out-of-area contractors working in Jones County: the local soil's clay content means that exterior concrete flatwork — patios, walkways, garage aprons — tends to heave and crack more aggressively here than in sandier coastal soils. If your renovation includes any exterior concrete as part of a landscaping or addition project, the base preparation needs to account for that movement. Proper compaction, appropriate aggregate depth, and control joint placement are not optional here — they are the difference between flatwork that lasts a decade and flatwork that needs replacing in three years.
