## Why Gulfport Properties Face Elevated Board-Up and Tarping Risks

Gulfport sits squarely in the Gulf Coast's most active hurricane corridor, but tropical storms and named hurricanes aren't the only threat. Pop-up severe thunderstorms from April through October routinely push 60-to-80 mph wind gusts across Harrison County, enough to strip ridge caps, crack soffit panels, and send debris through single-pane windows common in the area's older ranch-style homes. The city's housing stock includes a substantial number of wood-frame structures built before modern wind-load codes were adopted — many pre-1980 homes lack hurricane straps connecting roof trusses to wall framing, which means the roof assembly is more vulnerable to uplift even in a moderate storm.

Beyond wind, Gulfport's high water table and frequent heavy rainfall mean that any breach in the building envelope — a missing section of roofing felt, an unboarded window — can allow interior flooding within a single storm event. Combine that with the region's average relative humidity hovering above 75% for most of the year, and an unprotected opening becomes an open invitation for mold.

## Gulfport Insurance and Coordination After a Storm or Fire

Most homeowners' policies in Mississippi cover emergency protective measures, including board-up and tarping, as part of the covered loss — but the insurer typically requires documentation that the work was necessary and performed promptly. We provide a written scope of work, timestamped photos of each opening, and a material and labor summary formatted to align with Xactimate line items, which is the estimating platform most carriers use. That paperwork reduces back-and-forth with adjusters and helps prevent disputes over whether the tarping was an "emergency" measure or an elective upgrade.

If your property is in a coastal flood zone — which applies to a significant portion of Gulfport addresses given the city's proximity to the Sound — there may be separate NFIP (National Flood Insurance Program) documentation requirements for any structural work. We flag those situations and can coordinate directly with your adjuster.

## Local Note: What Gulf Coast Humidity Does to an Unprotected Opening

Here's something that matters specifically in Gulfport and the broader Harrison County coastal zone: standard 6-mil poly tarps sold at big-box stores degrade measurably faster in direct Gulf Coast sun than in drier inland climates. UV index readings along the Mississippi coast regularly exceed 10 in summer months, and the combination of UV radiation and heat causes thin poly to become brittle and tear at seam points within days — sometimes within a single week of a storm. We use heavier-duty tarps with UV-stabilized coatings and reinforce all ridge attachments with mechanical fasteners rather than relying on tarp grommets alone. It's a small material difference that can mean the roof stays protected through a second rain event rather than failing before your adjuster even schedules a visit.
