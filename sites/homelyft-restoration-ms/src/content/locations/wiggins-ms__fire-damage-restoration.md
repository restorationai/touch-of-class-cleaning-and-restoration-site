## Why Wiggins Properties Face Particular Challenges After a Fire

Wiggins sits in the piney woods of Stone County, and a meaningful portion of the housing stock here consists of wood-frame construction — pier-and-beam foundations, wood-plank subfloors, and in older homes, tongue-and-groove pine walls that were standard across rural Mississippi through the mid-twentieth century. That building style is durable under normal conditions, but after a fire it creates layered problems. Char penetrates deeply into old-growth pine. Smoke odor embeds in the grain. And because many of these homes have minimal vapor barriers, post-fire moisture from firefighting efforts can migrate into the subfloor cavity and sit there for days before anyone notices.

Wiggins also sits roughly 60 miles north of the Gulf, far enough inland that it doesn't get the constant salt-air exposure of coastal communities, but close enough that summer humidity regularly pushes into the upper 80s and 90s percent relative humidity. After a fire, that ambient moisture accelerates the bonding of smoke residue to porous surfaces. Soot that might wipe off a wall in a drier climate becomes a chemically active film here within 24 to 48 hours if not treated promptly.

## Our Fire and Smoke Restoration Process in Wiggins

We follow the IICRC FSRT framework on every fire and smoke restoration job, which means the work is sequenced — not improvised. Here's how that plays out in practice on a Wiggins property:

**Stabilization and safety assessment** comes first. We identify structural compromises, confirm utilities are safely isolated, and establish a containment perimeter so unaffected areas of the home stay clean during work.

**Smoke and soot removal** follows a surface-by-surface protocol. Dry soot gets dry-sponged before any wet cleaning is introduced — reversing that order drives residue deeper into porous materials. For the wood-frame walls and pine ceilings common in Stone County homes, we use low-alkaline cleaning agents that lift residue without raising the grain or causing secondary staining.

**Odor neutralization** in Wiggins's climate requires more than ozone or thermal fogging alone. Because humidity allows odor molecules to re-absorb into surfaces, we pair hydroxyl generation with targeted sealing of affected framing to lock in any residual odor before reconstruction begins.

**Documentation for insurance** runs parallel to the physical work. We photograph affected materials, record moisture readings, and produce a scope of loss that aligns with the format most carriers expect — reducing back-and-forth during the claims process.

## Local Note

One thing we've learned working in the Wiggins area: older wood-frame homes with metal roofing — a common combination in rural Stone County — can trap heat in the attic space during a fire in ways that cause smoke to stratify differently than in homes with standard asphalt shingles and attic ventilation.
