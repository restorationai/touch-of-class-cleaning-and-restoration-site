## Why Lyman Properties Experience Sewage Backups

Harrison County's coastal plain soil is dense with clay and poorly draining fill in many subdivisions, which means heavy rainfall — and the Gulf Coast gets plenty of it — can saturate the ground around sewer laterals and septic drain fields faster than the system can handle. When the soil is already saturated from a tropical weather event or a prolonged wet season, hydrostatic pressure can push sewage backward through the lowest fixtures in a home: floor drains, washing machine standpipes, and ground-level toilets.

Lyman also has a mix of housing stock that matters for how a backup behaves. Older ranch-style homes common in the area often have cast-iron drain lines that have corroded or shifted over decades, narrowing the pipe diameter and creating a natural catch point for grease and debris. When a partial blockage meets a surge of rainwater infiltrating the municipal sewer, a backup isn't a question of if — it's when. Homes on private septic systems face a related but distinct problem: a drain field that has reached capacity or been damaged by root intrusion can cause effluent to surface in the yard or reverse into the home during a heavy rain event.

## Our Sewage Cleanup and Sanitization Process in Lyman

Sewage is classified as Category 3 water — the most contaminated class — because it carries bacteria, viruses, and parasites that persist on porous surfaces long after the visible water is gone. The process we follow is calibrated to that reality, not to a generic water-damage checklist.

**Step 1 — Containment and safety assessment.** Before extraction begins, technicians assess which materials are salvageable and which have absorbed enough contamination that removal is the safer call. Porous materials like carpet, pad, and compromised drywall below the flood line are typically removed rather than dried in place.

**Step 2 — Extraction and solid waste removal.** Truck-mounted extractors pull standing sewage and contaminated water from the structure. Solid waste is bagged and disposed of per Mississippi Department of Environmental Quality guidelines.

**Step 3 — Structural cleaning and disinfection.** Subfloor decking, wall framing, and concrete slab surfaces are cleaned with EPA-registered disinfectants. This step is not optional — pathogens adhere to wood grain and concrete pores and will not leave on their own.

**Step 4 — Drying and monitoring.** Because Lyman's ambient humidity regularly runs above 70% in summer months, drying sewage-affected structures here requires more aggressive dehumidification than the same job in a drier climate. We deploy refrigerant and desiccant dehumidifiers alongside high-velocity air movers, and we monitor moisture readings daily until the structure reaches acceptable levels per the IICRC S500 standard.

**Step 5 — Post-remediation verification.** Before any reconstruction begins, a final moisture and surface inspection confirms the affected area is dry and disinfected.

## Insurance Coordination for Sewage Backup Claims in Lyman

Sewage backup coverage is one of the more contested areas of homeowner insurance in Mississippi. Standard HO-3 policies often exclude sewer backup unless the homeowner has added a specific endorsement — and many Lyman residents don't realize the gap until they're standing in contaminated water. We document the loss thoroughly from the moment we arrive: photos of affected materials, moisture readings, contamination extent, and a written scope of work.
