St. Martin sits in a low-lying pocket of Jackson County where Gulf Coast humidity rarely drops below uncomfortable, and the ground stays saturated for days after a heavy rain. That combination — dense moisture in the air, slow-draining soil, and homes built close to the water table — means mold doesn't just appear after a plumbing failure. It can colonize behind a bathroom wall or under a crawl space vapor barrier within 48 to 72 hours of any moisture intrusion, often before a homeowner notices anything more than a faint musty odor.

## Why St. Martin Properties See Mold Issues

Jackson County's coastal plain climate is genuinely one of the more challenging environments for keeping a home dry. Summer dew points routinely sit in the upper 70s, which means even a brief AC outage can push interior relative humidity past the 60% threshold where mold spores begin to germinate on organic materials — drywall paper, wood framing, insulation facing. St. Martin's housing stock includes a significant number of slab-on-grade homes built in the 1970s through the 1990s, many of which have original fiberglass batt insulation in exterior walls. That insulation traps moisture against the framing when exterior vapor drives inward during summer, creating hidden wet pockets that don't dry on their own.

Crawl space homes in the area face a compounding problem: the sandy loam soil common to this part of the county wicks groundwater upward, and undersized or deteriorated vapor barriers allow that moisture to evaporate directly into the subfloor assembly. By the time a homeowner smells something off in the living room, the floor joists may already have visible mold colonization.

## Our Mold Remediation Process in St. Martin

Every job starts with a thorough moisture mapping inspection — not just a visual scan, but thermal imaging and pin-type moisture meter readings across suspect surfaces. In St. Martin's climate, visible mold is rarely the full picture; the wetter material is usually behind it. Once the scope is established, the affected area is sealed under negative air pressure using poly sheeting and HEPA-filtered air scrubbers. This containment step is critical in occupied homes because it prevents disturbed spores from migrating into clean living spaces.

Physical removal follows: porous materials like drywall, insulation, and saturated wood that exceed remediation thresholds are removed and double-bagged per industry protocol. Structural surfaces that can be cleaned — framing, concrete, metal — are HEPA-vacuumed, wire-brushed where needed, and treated with an EPA-registered antimicrobial. The team holds the IICRC AMRT (Applied Microbial Remediation Technician) credential, which means the process follows a documented standard rather than a spray-and-paint approach.

Drying the underlying structure before any reconstruction is non-negotiable. In Jackson County's ambient humidity, skipping that step means new drywall goes up over wood that's still above the 19% moisture content threshold — and the cycle repeats within a season.

Mold claims in Mississippi can be complicated by the distinction between sudden-and-accidental water damage (typically covered) and long-term seepage or maintenance-related moisture (often excluded). The documentation HomeLyft produces — moisture readings, thermal images, written scope of work, and photo logs — is structured to support the adjuster's review regardless of which direction the claim goes.
