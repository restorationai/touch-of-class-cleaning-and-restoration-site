## Why Lumberton Properties See Contents Damage Differently

Lamar County's climate swings between hot, saturated summers and mild but damp winters, and that humidity cycle is hard on household belongings even before a loss event. After a water or fire incident, items left inside a damaged home absorb ambient moisture rapidly — mold can begin colonizing porous materials like upholstery, books, and wood cabinetry within 24 to 48 hours of exposure. Lumberton's older housing stock, including many homes built in the 1960s and 1970s with solid-wood furniture and natural-fiber textiles, tends to hold moisture longer than modern synthetic materials, which means the window for successful restoration is shorter than homeowners expect. Acting quickly — and moving contents out of the affected environment — is often the difference between restoring a piece and replacing it.

The area also sees a meaningful number of losses tied to severe weather. Gulf-origin storms track through Lamar County regularly, and wind-driven rain through compromised rooflines can saturate attic storage, closets, and entire rooms before a homeowner realizes the extent of the intrusion. Contents that have been wet and then dried improperly in the home's ambient air often develop odor problems that are far harder to address than the original water damage.

## Our Contents Pack-Out and Restoration Process in Lumberton

The first step on any job is a room-by-room inventory. Every item is photographed, catalogued, and assessed — salvageable, restorable, or beyond recovery — before anything leaves the property. That documentation matters for your insurance claim and gives you a clear record of what was present at the time of loss.

Salvageable belongings are carefully packed and transported to a climate-controlled storage facility while structural work proceeds on the home. Cleaning methods are matched to the item: ultrasonic cleaning for hard goods and small valuables, ozone or hydroxyl treatment for odor-bearing textiles and soft furnishings, and dry-cleaning or foam methods for upholstered pieces. Smoke and soot residue from fire losses requires specific chemistry — alkaline cleaners for protein-based smoke, dry sponging for loose ash — and the IICRC FSRT standard guides how our team sequences that work to avoid driving residue deeper into fibers.

Once the home is cleared and rebuilt, contents are returned, unwrapped, and placed. The goal is a complete handoff, not a pile of boxes left in the living room.
