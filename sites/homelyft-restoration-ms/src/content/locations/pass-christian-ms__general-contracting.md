## Why Pass Christian Properties Present Unique Renovation Challenges

The Gulf Coast's climate doesn't just wear on finishes — it works its way into structure. Homes within a few blocks of the beachfront have often absorbed years of salt-laden air that corrodes fasteners, degrades caulk lines, and accelerates wood rot in ways that don't show up until walls come open. Older homes along the historic downtown corridor frequently reveal original balloon-frame construction, which requires careful evaluation before any load-bearing wall work begins.

Post-Katrina rebuilds — a significant portion of Pass Christian's current housing stock — were often built quickly under emergency permitting conditions. That means some of those homes are now approaching their first major renovation cycle with questions about whether the original work met long-term code standards. Mississippi's coastal building codes, shaped by decades of hurricane experience, also impose specific requirements around wind-resistant fastening, impact-rated windows, and elevated foundation work that don't apply to projects just a few counties north. Any general contractor working here needs to be fluent in those requirements before the first permit application goes in.
