## Why Long Beach Properties See Mold Issues

The Gulf Coast's humidity rarely dips low enough to give homes a true dry-out period. In Long Beach specifically, the combination of sandy, poorly-draining soil and the shallow water table means that slab foundations and crawl spaces stay damp well into the summer months. Homes built in the post-Katrina reconstruction era — many of them elevated on piers — can trap humid air beneath the structure, creating ideal conditions for mold to colonize floor joists and vapor barriers. Older homes closer to the shoreline, where building practices predate modern moisture-control standards, are particularly vulnerable: single-pane windows, minimal wall insulation, and original wood framing all absorb and hold moisture that modern construction sheds more readily.

Harrison County's extended storm season compounds the problem. A roof breach or a window seal failure during a tropical event doesn't just wet the interior once — it introduces moisture that wicks into framing, insulation, and drywall over days, often while the homeowner is still displaced. By the time residents return, mold is already established in areas that look dry on the surface.

## Our Mold Remediation Process in Long Beach

Every remediation starts with a thorough inspection — not a visual sweep, but moisture mapping with thermal imaging and calibrated meters that detect elevated readings behind finished surfaces. In Long Beach's coastal homes, we pay particular attention to exterior wall cavities on the south and east elevations, which take the most direct wind-driven rain exposure during Gulf storms.

Once the scope is confirmed, the affected area is isolated with negative-air containment barriers. HEPA-filtered air scrubbers run continuously to capture airborne spores before they migrate to unaffected rooms. Contaminated materials — drywall, insulation, damaged framing — are removed, bagged, and disposed of per EPA and Mississippi DEQ guidelines. Structural surfaces are treated and dried to confirmed moisture levels per the IICRC S520 standard before any reconstruction begins. We document every step with photographs and moisture readings, which matters both for your peace of mind and for the insurance claim process.
