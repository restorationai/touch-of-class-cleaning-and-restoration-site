## Why Moselle Properties Are Vulnerable After a Fire

Moselle sits in a part of Jones County where a significant share of the housing stock was built during the postwar decades through the 1980s — an era when homes were framed with old-growth pine lumber, insulated with materials that absorb smoke deeply, and wired with systems that may not have been updated since original construction. Older wiring and panel configurations are a known ignition risk, and when a fire does start, those same pine-heavy structures can sustain char damage faster and deeper than engineered lumber used in newer builds.

The regional climate compounds the problem. Mississippi's high relative humidity — routinely above 70% in summer — accelerates the way smoke odor compounds migrate into porous surfaces after the fire is out. Soot that looks dry on the surface is often still chemically active, and without proper neutralization it will continue to off-gas and stain. Homes with crawl spaces, which are extremely common in this part of the state, can trap smoke residue underneath the structure for months if the subfloor and vapor barrier aren't addressed as part of the restoration scope.

## Our Fire Damage Restoration Process in Moselle

When HomeLyft Restoration MS arrives at a fire-damaged property in Moselle, the first step is a systematic damage assessment — not just the rooms with visible char, but the entire structure. - **Reconstruction** of affected areas to pre-loss condition, coordinated with your adjuster's scope.
