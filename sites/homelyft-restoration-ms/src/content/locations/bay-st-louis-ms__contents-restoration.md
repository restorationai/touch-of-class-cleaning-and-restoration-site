## Why Bay St. Louis Homes See Contents Damage Differently

The Gulf Coast climate is unforgiving to personal belongings. Relative humidity in Hancock County regularly climbs above 80 percent during summer months, meaning that after any water intrusion event — a roof breach from a tropical band, a failed sump in a low-lying bungalow, or even a slow refrigerator line leak — the contents of a home begin absorbing ambient moisture almost immediately. Mold can colonize porous materials like upholstered furniture, books, and clothing within 24 to 48 hours under these conditions.

Bay St. Louis also has a significant inventory of older homes, many built before modern vapor barriers and synthetic materials became standard. Wood furniture, plaster-backed artwork, and antique textiles common in these properties respond differently to water and smoke damage than mass-produced modern goods. A mid-century chest of drawers or a family heirloom quilt requires hand-cleaning techniques and controlled drying environments — not industrial washing machines or aggressive chemical treatments.

Fire and smoke damage adds another layer of complexity. Smoke residue from Gulf Coast storms often involves a mix of materials — vegetation, older building components, and synthetic contents — producing a range of soot types that require different cleaning chemistry. An IICRC FSRT-trained technician knows the difference between dry soot that brushes off and wet, oily smoke residue that smears if treated incorrectly.

## Our Contents Pack-Out and Restoration Process

When we arrive at a Bay St. Louis property, the first step is a room-by-room inventory. Every item is photographed, catalogued, and assessed before anything is moved. This documentation matters both for insurance claims and for ensuring that nothing is misplaced during transport.

Salvageable contents are carefully packed and transported to our climate-controlled storage facility, where they are held securely while structural work proceeds on the property. Items are stored in a controlled environment — critical in a coastal climate where fluctuating outdoor humidity can re-damage belongings that were just dried and cleaned.

Cleaning methods are matched to the material. Upholstered furniture may go through ultrasonic cleaning or dry-cleaning processes. Hard surfaces — wood, metal, glass — are cleaned with chemistry appropriate to the damage type and the finish. Electronics are assessed for corrosion. Documents and photographs, which are especially vulnerable to Gulf Coast humidity, are stabilized and, where possible, restored. Deodorization using hydroxyl or ozone technology addresses smoke and mildew odors that standard cleaning cannot fully remove.

Once the structure is cleared and dried, contents are returned, re-inventoried, and placed back in the home. The full chain of custody is documented throughout.
