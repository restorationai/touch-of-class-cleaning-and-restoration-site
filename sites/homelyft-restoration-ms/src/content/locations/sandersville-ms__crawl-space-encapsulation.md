## Why Sandersville Properties Are Especially Vulnerable to Crawl Space Moisture

The geology beneath Sandersville is not kind to open crawl spaces. The region's clay soils expand when wet and contract when dry, a cycle that creates shifting ground conditions and channels moisture toward foundation perimeters. Homes built before the 1980s — a significant portion of the residential stock in this area — were typically constructed with vented crawl spaces, a design philosophy that has since been largely abandoned in humid climates because it allows warm, moisture-laden outdoor air to enter the crawl space and condense on cooler structural surfaces.

Wood floor joists and subfloor sheathing sitting in that environment don't last. Fungal growth can begin colonizing damp wood within 24 to 48 hours of sustained moisture exposure. In Sandersville's summers, where overnight lows rarely drop enough to dry things out, that window closes quickly. Fiberglass batt insulation stapled between joists — another common feature in older local homes — absorbs and holds moisture, adding weight that accelerates sag and rot.

## Our Crawl Space Encapsulation Process in Sandersville

Encapsulation is not simply rolling out a sheet of plastic. The process starts with a thorough inspection of the crawl space — measuring moisture levels in the wood framing, checking for existing mold growth, and assessing whether any standing water or drainage issues need to be resolved before sealing begins. Our team holds IICRC AMRT (mold) and IICRC WRT (water) certifications, so the inspection is informed by the same standards used in full remediation work, not just a sales walk.

Once the space is prepped, we install a heavy-duty vapor barrier — typically a reinforced polyethylene liner rated at 12 to 20 mils — across the entire crawl space floor and up the foundation walls, sealed at seams and penetrations with moisture-resistant tape. Vents are sealed or converted, and a properly sized dehumidifier is installed and set to maintain relative humidity below 55 percent, the threshold above which mold growth becomes likely. The goal is a conditioned, controlled environment that behaves more like a sealed basement than an open dirt crawl.

In Sandersville homes with older masonry piers or block foundation walls, we pay particular attention to wall penetrations — utility lines, plumbing runs, and HVAC ducts that pass through the foundation are common moisture entry points that get overlooked in less thorough encapsulation jobs.

## Local Note: What We See in Sandersville Crawl Spaces

One pattern that comes up consistently in Sandersville and the broader Jones County area is HVAC ductwork routed through the crawl space — a common configuration in single-story homes built from the 1960s through the 1990s. When those ducts develop small leaks or condensation issues, they introduce a steady supply of conditioned air into the crawl space, which sounds counterintuitive but actually worsens humidity problems by creating temperature differentials that promote condensation on the duct exterior and surrounding wood. During every encapsulation job in this area, we inspect duct condition and flag any issues before sealing the space, because encapsulating around a leaking duct system traps the problem rather than solving it.
