## Why Big Point Properties See Repeated Storm Damage

Jackson County's geography puts Big Point directly in the path of Gulf-fed weather systems that intensify quickly over warm water before making landfall. The region averages more named tropical events per decade than most of Mississippi, and even non-tropical severe weather — fast-moving thunderstorm complexes, straight-line wind events, and late-season tornado outbreaks — can produce localized damage that rivals a Category 1 landfall. Homes in this area tend to be a mix of older wood-frame construction and post-Katrina rebuilds, and each type presents its own vulnerability. Older structures may have roof sheathing fastened with smooth-shank nails that lose grip under sustained uplift pressure, while even newer builds can suffer when large hardwoods — the mature oaks and pines common throughout this part of Jackson County — fall across rooflines or punch through exterior walls. Saturated coastal soils also mean that standing water from heavy rain has nowhere to drain quickly, extending the window during which moisture migrates into subfloor assemblies and wall cavities.

## Our Storm Damage Restoration Process in Big Point

When we arrive at a storm-damaged property in Big Point, the first priority is a full exterior and interior assessment before any materials are moved or covered. That means documenting roof penetrations, wall breaches, and water intrusion paths with photos and moisture readings — the same documentation your insurance adjuster will need to process the claim. From there, the work moves in a deliberate sequence:

**Emergency stabilization** comes first: tarping open roof sections, boarding compromised windows and doors, and cutting away any tree material that's still loading the structure. We coordinate with local utility contacts if downed lines are involved — never assume a line is de-energized.

**Water extraction and structural drying** follow immediately if rain has entered the building. We deploy truck-mounted extractors, desiccant and refrigerant dehumidifiers, and high-velocity air movers calibrated to the affected material types. Drying is monitored daily against the IICRC S500 standard until readings confirm the structure has returned to baseline moisture levels.

**Debris removal and reconstruction scoping** close out the field phase. We document every material removed — roofing, insulation, drywall, flooring — so the scope of repairs is clear before reconstruction begins.
