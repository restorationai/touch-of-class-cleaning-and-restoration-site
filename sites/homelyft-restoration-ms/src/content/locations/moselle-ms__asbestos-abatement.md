## Why Moselle Properties See Asbestos Concerns

Jones County's building boom tracked closely with national trends: asbestos was the go-to additive for fireproofing, insulation, and durability from roughly 1940 through the late 1970s, and its use tapered off only after federal restrictions took hold in the 1980s. That means a large share of Moselle's single-family homes and small commercial buildings were constructed during the window when asbestos-containing materials were standard — not exceptional. Common hiding spots include attic insulation (particularly loose-fill vermiculite), vinyl floor tiles and their adhesive mastic, pipe wrap on older HVAC systems, and the popcorn or "cottage cheese" ceiling texture that was nearly universal in homes built before 1980.

Moselle's climate adds another layer of urgency. The region averages over 55 inches of rain per year, and the combination of heat, humidity, and seasonal flooding can degrade older building materials faster than in drier climates. When asbestos-containing materials become water-damaged or physically disturbed — by a roof leak, a plumbing failure, or a contractor's demo work — fibers can become airborne. That's the point at which a stable material becomes a health hazard.

## Our Asbestos Abatement Process in Moselle

Every project begins with a professional inspection and bulk material sampling. Suspect materials are collected following EPA sampling protocols and sent to an accredited laboratory. Results typically return within a few business days, and we walk you through the findings before any abatement work is scheduled.

Once abatement is confirmed, the work area is isolated using poly sheeting and negative air pressure containment — a critical step that prevents fibers from migrating into adjacent living spaces. Our EPA Lead-Safe Certified team removes the asbestos-containing materials using wet methods to suppress fiber release, double-bags all waste in labeled, sealed containers, and transports it to a licensed disposal facility. Throughout the job, air monitoring tracks fiber levels inside and outside the containment zone.

Final clearance involves a visual inspection and, where required or requested, independent air sampling to confirm the space is safe before containment is removed and the area is released for reconstruction or reoccupancy. We document every phase — sampling results, waste manifests, air monitoring data — so you have a complete paper trail for insurance, real estate disclosure, or permit purposes.

Asbestos abatement is sometimes a covered loss when the material has been disturbed by a covered peril — a storm, a pipe burst, or fire damage — rather than discovered during routine renovation. ## Local Note

One pattern that comes up repeatedly in Jones County homes built between the late 1950s and early 1970s: the original floor tile — often a 9×9-inch vinyl asphalt tile in kitchens and utility rooms — was almost universally manufactured with asbestos binders during that period. Homeowners frequently assume the tile itself is the only concern, but the black mastic adhesive beneath it is just as likely to contain asbestos and is often more friable once the tile is lifted. In Moselle properties of that era, we always sample both the tile and the adhesive separately before any flooring demo begins, because a negative result on the tile alone doesn't clear the substrate.
