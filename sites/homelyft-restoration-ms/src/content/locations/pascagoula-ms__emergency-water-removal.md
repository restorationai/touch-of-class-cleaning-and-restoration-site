## Why Pascagoula Properties See Water Damage

Pascagoula sits low and flat along the river and the Gulf, which means a lot of the city's housing stock, especially older homes near downtown and the residential streets off Highway 90, was built on pier-and-beam or shallow slab foundations designed for a region that floods. When a pipe bursts or a roof fails during a tropical system, water doesn't have far to travel before it's under the house or soaking into baseboards. High humidity through most of the year also means materials that would air-dry in a drier climate stay wet far longer here, which is exactly the condition mold needs to start colonizing drywall and framing within a day or two of a leak going unnoticed.

## Our Water Extraction Process in Pascagoula

We start with moisture mapping, not guesswork. Our technicians use penetrating moisture meters and thermal imaging to find water that's already migrated into wall cavities or under flooring, which is common in homes along the river where humidity keeps materials damp even after the visible puddle is gone. Extraction comes next with truck-mounted and portable units pulling standing water fast, followed by structural drying set up and monitored to the IICRC S500 standard. Because Jackson County's ambient humidity works against drying equipment, we run dehumidification longer and check moisture readings more frequently here than we would in a drier inland market, adjusting air movers and humidity targets daily until materials read dry, not just damp to the touch.

## Pascagoula Insurance Coordination

Water damage claims on the Mississippi coast often get complicated by the line between a standard homeowner's policy and flood insurance, since many carriers treat storm surge and rising water differently than a burst pipe or roof leak. We document the water line, source, and affected materials with photos and moisture logs so your adjuster has a clear record of what happened and when, and we bill most major carriers directly so the claims conversation isn't something you're managing alone while also trying to dry out your house.
