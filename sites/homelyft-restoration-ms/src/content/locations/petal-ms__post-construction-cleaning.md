## Why Petal Properties Present Unique Post-Construction Challenges

Petal sits in a climate band where relative humidity rarely drops below 60% even on clear days. That matters for post-construction cleanup because construction dust doesn't behave like household dust. Drywall compound particles are hygroscopic — they absorb ambient moisture and harden into a thin, chalky film on glass, cabinetry, and tile grout lines. In Petal's climate, that film sets faster than it would in, say, the Tennessee hill country two states north. Crews who show up with a mop and a shop vac will smear it rather than remove it.

The local housing stock adds another layer. Petal has seen significant infill construction alongside older ranch-style homes dating to the 1960s and 1970s that are being renovated and flipped. Those older homes often have original oak hardwood under carpet that contractors uncover mid-project — and that wood requires a different cleaning protocol than new engineered flooring. Lead-safe practices also apply to pre-1978 homes during renovation; HomeLyft carries EPA Lead-Safe Certification, which matters when a remodel has disturbed painted surfaces and the post-construction clean involves sweeping or HEPA-vacuuming in those same areas.

## Our Post-Construction Cleaning Process in Petal

Every post-construction clean starts with a walk-through — not a checklist, an actual room-by-room assessment of what trades left behind and in what sequence they need to be addressed. Rough cleaning happens first: debris removal, bulk dust extraction with HEPA-filtered vacuums rated for fine particulate, and wipe-down of framing dust from window wells and door jambs before any finish surfaces are touched.

The detail phase is where the work separates from a standard janitorial visit. Window tracks, outlet boxes, cabinet interiors, grout joints, and the inside of light fixture housings all get individual attention. In Petal homes where central air was running during construction — common in summer builds because crews need it — duct registers are pulled, vacuumed, and wiped before the HVAC system is cleared to run at full capacity. Leaving construction particulate in the duct system defeats the purpose of a final clean; it just redistributes the dust the moment the thermostat kicks on.

For specialty cleaning needs — post-fire odor neutralization on a renovation that uncovered smoke damage in wall cavities, or biohazard-adjacent cleanup on a property that sat vacant during a long remodel — the same IICRC-certified team handles the work under one project rather than requiring a homeowner to coordinate multiple contractors.
