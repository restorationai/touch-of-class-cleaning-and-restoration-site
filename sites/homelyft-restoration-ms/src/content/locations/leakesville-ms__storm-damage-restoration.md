## Why Leakesville Properties See Serious Storm Damage

The Piney Woods landscape that makes Greene County beautiful is also what makes storm damage here particularly destructive. Longleaf and loblolly pines grow tall and shallow-rooted in the sandy loam soils common throughout this part of Mississippi. When sustained winds from a Gulf hurricane or a fast-moving tornado outbreak push through the area, those trees fail at the root ball rather than snapping mid-trunk — meaning the entire tree, root mass included, can land on a structure with enormous force.

Leakesville's housing stock skews older, with a significant number of homes built in the 1960s through 1980s using construction methods that predate modern wind-resistance standards. Roof decks nailed with smooth-shank nails, older felt underlayment, and single-layer shingles offer far less protection against uplift than homes built to post-Katrina Mississippi building codes. When those roofs are breached, water enters fast — and in the humid Greene County climate, mold colonization can begin within 24 to 48 hours of a moisture intrusion event.

## Our Storm Damage Restoration Process in Leakesville

Every job starts with a full exterior and interior assessment before any debris is moved. From there, the process follows a deliberate sequence:

**Emergency stabilization** comes first: tarping breached roofs, boarding compromised windows and doors, and isolating any sections of the structure where water is still actively entering.
