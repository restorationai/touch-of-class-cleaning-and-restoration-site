## Why Sharon Properties See Mold Issues

Madison County's clay-heavy soil doesn't drain quickly, which means moisture lingers under and around foundations long after a storm passes. Homes built in the Sharon area — many of them pier-and-beam construction common to rural central Mississippi — often have crawl spaces with limited ventilation. When warm, humid outdoor air meets the cooler underside of a conditioned floor, condensation forms on wood joists and subfloor sheathing. That damp wood is exactly what mold spores need to colonize.

Sharon also sits in a region that sees periodic heavy rainfall events tied to Gulf moisture systems tracking north from the coast. A slow roof leak, a backed-up gutter, or a sweating HVAC duct can introduce enough moisture to start a mold problem that stays hidden for weeks inside wall cavities. By the time a visible stain appears on drywall or a smell becomes noticeable in a bedroom or closet, the underlying colony is often already well-established.

## Our Mold Remediation Process in Sharon

Every remediation job starts with a thorough moisture and mold assessment. Using thermal imaging cameras and calibrated moisture meters, the crew maps exactly where elevated moisture readings exist — not just where mold is visually apparent. In pier-and-beam homes common to the Sharon area, that means getting into the crawl space and checking joist moisture content, not just inspecting the living areas above.

Once the scope is confirmed, containment goes up before any disturbing work begins. Negative air pressure is established using HEPA-filtered air scrubbers so that spores disturbed during removal don't migrate to unaffected rooms. Affected porous materials — drywall, insulation, compromised wood — are removed and bagged according to EPA guidelines. Remaining structural surfaces are treated with an EPA-registered antimicrobial agent. The team holds IICRC AMRT (Applied Microbial Remediation Technician) certification, so the process follows the IICRC S520 standard from containment through final clearance.

Drying doesn't end when the visible mold is gone. Industrial-grade dehumidifiers and air movers run until moisture readings in structural materials return to acceptable baselines — typically below 16% in wood framing. Skipping that step is one of the most common reasons mold returns within a season.

## Local Note

One thing worth knowing about remediation work in rural Madison County: homes in the Sharon area that were built before modern vapor barrier requirements often have bare-earth crawl spaces or thin polyethylene sheeting that has degraded over the years. That ground moisture evaporates upward directly into the crawl space cavity. When the team finds this condition, encapsulation of the crawl space — sealing the ground and walls with a heavy-duty liner — is typically recommended alongside the mold remediation itself. Treating the mold without addressing that moisture source is a short-term fix; the problem tends to return within a year or two without the encapsulation.
