## Why Lyman Homes See Elevated Contents Damage

Harrison County's proximity to the Gulf means that even a minor roof leak or a burst supply line can escalate quickly. Relative humidity in this part of Mississippi regularly climbs above 80 percent from May through September, which shortens the window between a water event and the onset of mold colonization on porous materials to as little as 24 to 48 hours. Many homes in the Lyman area were built in the post-Katrina reconstruction era, when rapid development sometimes meant that attic insulation and vapor barriers were installed in ways that trap moisture against interior finishes. That trapped humidity makes upholstered pieces and wood furniture especially vulnerable after any water intrusion — the stuffing in a sofa or the veneer on a dresser can absorb ambient moisture even if the item was never directly wet.

Fire losses add another layer of complexity. Smoke from a structure fire in a tightly built home travels through HVAC ductwork and settles on contents in rooms that never saw flames. Protein smoke — the kind produced by kitchen fires — leaves an almost invisible film that smells intensely and bonds to glass, metal, and fabric surfaces. Without professional ultrasonic cleaning or dry-soot sponging, that residue becomes permanent.

## Our Contents Pack-Out and Restoration Process

Every contents job begins with a room-by-room inventory before a single item leaves the property. Each piece is photographed, logged by category and condition, and assigned a barcode that follows it through cleaning and storage. This documentation matters enormously for insurance claims — adjusters want line-item proof of what was affected and what was salvageable.

From there, items are packed and transported to our climate-controlled facility. Soft goods — curtains, bedding, clothing — go through ozone treatment and thermal fogging to neutralize odor molecules rather than mask them. Hard surfaces and furniture receive ultrasonic cleaning, which uses high-frequency sound waves in a water bath to pull smoke particles and soot out of crevices that a cloth can't reach. Electronics are evaluated by a certified technician before any cleaning attempt, because corrosive smoke residue can damage circuit boards if handled incorrectly.

Storage is held in a temperature- and humidity-controlled environment, which is not a small detail in coastal Mississippi. Storing a cedar chest or a leather sofa in an unregulated warehouse in this climate for six weeks while a home is being rebuilt is a reliable way to ruin it. Our facility maintains conditions that prevent secondary mold growth on items that arrived clean.

## Coordinating with Insurance on Lyman Contents Claims

Contents losses are frequently the most contested part of a homeowner's claim. Adjusters may dispute whether an item is restorable versus a total loss, and the difference in payout can be significant. Our team documents the pre-cleaning condition of every item, records the cleaning method applied, and provides a written outcome — restored or non-restorable — that supports your adjuster's review. We work directly with most major carriers and can provide the documentation in the format your insurer requires, which reduces back-and-forth and speeds up settlement.

For Lyman homeowners whose policies include additional living expense coverage, the timeline for contents return often ties directly to when you can move back in. We coordinate delivery scheduling with your general contractor so that restored items arrive after flooring and paint are complete — not before, when they could be damaged again.

## Local Note

One pattern that comes up repeatedly in Harrison County contents jobs is the presence of antique or heirloom pine furniture — the kind of longleaf yellow pine pieces that were built locally generations ago and have been passed down through families. This wood is extraordinarily dense and holds smoke and water differently than modern softwood furniture. It tends to resist surface staining but can check and crack if dried too aggressively. When we encounter these pieces on a Lyman job, we slow the drying process and use a conservator-grade wood conditioner before any refinishing is attempted. Rushing that step on a piece of irreplaceable furniture is not a trade-off worth making.
