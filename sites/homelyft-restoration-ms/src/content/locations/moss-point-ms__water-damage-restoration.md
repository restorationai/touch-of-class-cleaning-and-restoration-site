## Why Moss Point Properties Face Elevated Water Damage Risk

Jackson County's subtropical humidity — routinely above 80 percent through the summer months — is the silent accelerant in any water damage event. Moisture that would dry on its own in a drier climate lingers here, and mold colonization can begin in as little as 24 to 48 hours after a leak. Moss Point's housing stock adds another layer of complexity: many homes along the older residential corridors near the waterfront were built in the 1950s through 1970s and feature pier-and-beam construction over crawl spaces. Those crawl spaces trap standing water after heavy rain or plumbing failures, and the wood framing above them absorbs moisture from below even when the interior floors look dry.

The area's proximity to tidal waterways also means that storm surge and backflow events — not just interior plumbing failures — are a realistic cause of water intrusion. Flood events that push brackish water into a structure introduce contaminants that change the cleanup category entirely, requiring more aggressive extraction protocols and material removal than a clean-water pipe burst would.

## Our Water Damage Restoration Process in Moss Point

Every job starts with a thorough moisture mapping inspection. Technicians use thermal imaging cameras and calibrated moisture meters to trace water migration behind walls, under flooring, and into ceiling assemblies — because what you can see is almost never the full picture. Once the scope is established, we move into extraction using truck-mounted and portable extraction units capable of pulling hundreds of gallons from carpet, pad, and subfloor assemblies.

After bulk water is removed, the structural drying phase begins. We place industrial-grade desiccant or refrigerant dehumidifiers and high-velocity air movers in a calculated configuration based on the affected square footage and material types — concrete slab, wood subfloor, plaster, or modern drywall all dry at different rates. Moisture readings are logged daily so drying progress is documented, not guessed. In Moss Point's climate, we typically run equipment longer than the national average drying timeline because ambient humidity slows evaporation even with equipment running.

For homes with pier-and-beam construction, crawl space drying is a separate but parallel process. We deploy crawl space dehumidifiers and inspect vapor barriers for damage that would allow ground moisture to re-wet the structure after we leave.

## Local Note

In Moss Point's older pier-and-beam homes, we've found that original heart pine subfloor boards — common in houses built before 1970 — behave very differently from modern OSB or plywood during drying. Heart pine is dense and slow to release moisture, which means standard drying timelines can underestimate how long the structure actually needs equipment running. Pulling equipment too early because surface readings look acceptable is one of the most common reasons secondary mold problems develop in these homes weeks after a water event. Our technicians check both surface and deep-probe readings before signing off on a dry structure in any home with original wood framing.
