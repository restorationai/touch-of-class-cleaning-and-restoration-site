## Why St. Martin Properties Face Elevated Board-Up and Tarping Risks

Jackson County's position along the Mississippi Gulf Coast puts St. Martin directly in the path of tropical weather systems that can escalate from a tropical depression to a damaging storm in less than 24 hours. The housing stock here reflects decades of coastal construction — many homes feature wide roof overhangs and large window openings designed for cross-ventilation, both of which become vulnerabilities when wind speeds climb. Older wood-frame homes common to the area can lose roof decking in sections, leaving large gaps that a single tarp must bridge cleanly to prevent water intrusion into the attic and ceiling assemblies below.

Beyond storm events, St. Martin sees house fires that require immediate board-up of burned-out windows and compromised door frames. Fire damage weakens structural members faster in humid Gulf Coast air — charred wood absorbs ambient moisture rapidly, and an unsecured opening invites rain before the fire marshal has even cleared the scene. Getting plywood and heavy-duty tarps in place the same day a fire is extinguished is not a formality; it is the difference between a restoration project and a rebuild.

## Our Emergency Board-Up and Tarping Process in St. Martin

When a call comes in from a St. Martin address, the first step is a rapid damage assessment — photographing every breach, measuring opening dimensions, and identifying any structural instability that would affect how materials are anchored. For roof tarping, we use minimum 6-mil polyethylene tarps weighted and secured with lumber battens screwed into undamaged decking, not simply draped and weighted with sandbags. That distinction matters in coastal Mississippi, where afternoon thunderstorms can drive wind-blown rain horizontally under a loosely secured tarp.

Window and door board-ups use 3/4-inch plywood cut to fit and secured with structural screws into the surrounding framing, not just the window casing. On fire-damaged properties, we assess whether the surrounding wall framing can hold fasteners before committing to a board-up pattern — a wall that looks intact from the outside may have compromised studs behind the drywall. Our team holds IICRC FSRT (Fire and Smoke Restoration) certification, which informs how we approach fire-damaged openings differently than storm-only breaches.

Every secured opening is documented with timestamped photographs and a written scope — both of which your insurance adjuster will need when filing the claim.
