## Why Wade Properties Face Particular Challenges After a Fire

Hancock County's climate is the first complication. Wade sits in a zone where post-fire humidity regularly climbs above 80%, and that moisture doesn't just sit in the air — it bonds with soot particles and drives them deeper into porous materials like brick mortar, wood framing, and insulation. Smoke residue that might stay on the surface in a drier climate becomes embedded within 24 to 48 hours here, making professional cleaning both more urgent and more technically demanding.

The local housing stock adds another layer. Many homes in and around Wade were built during eras when balloon-frame construction was common — a style where wall cavities run floor-to-roof without fire blocking. In a fire, smoke travels those open cavities and deposits residue in places that look untouched from the outside. Missing that hidden contamination during cleanup means odor returns weeks after the job appears finished.

Utility considerations matter too. Properties in this part of Hancock County often rely on propane rather than natural gas, and after a fire, the propane system — tank, regulator, and lines — needs inspection before any restoration crew works near it. We coordinate that step before staging equipment.

## Our Fire Damage Restoration Process in Wade

Every fire job starts with a structured assessment, not a quick walk-through. Technicians document the burn origin, map smoke migration through the structure, and photograph every affected surface before touching anything — that documentation is what your insurance adjuster needs to process the claim accurately.

From there, the work moves in a defined sequence:

1. **Board-up and tarping** to secure the structure against weather and unauthorized entry.
2. **Content inventory and pack-out** — salvageable belongings are catalogued, packed, and transported to a climate-controlled facility for cleaning.
3. **Structural dry-out** if firefighting water is present (our IICRC WRT and ASD credentials cover this phase).
4. **Soot and smoke residue removal** using dry chemical sponges, HEPA vacuuming, and wet cleaning agents matched to the surface type — wood, drywall, masonry, and textiles each require a different chemistry.
5. **Thermal fogging and hydroxyl treatment** to neutralize smoke odor molecules embedded in the structure.
6. **Clearance inspection** before reconstruction begins.

Because we hold EPA Lead-Safe certification, homes built before 1978 — a common vintage in Hancock County — can be handled without a separate contractor for lead-paint disturbance protocols.
