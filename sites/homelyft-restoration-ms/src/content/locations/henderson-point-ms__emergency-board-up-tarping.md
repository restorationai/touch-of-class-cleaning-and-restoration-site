## Why Henderson Point Properties Face Elevated Board-Up and Tarping Risk

The geography that makes Henderson Point a desirable place to live — waterfront exposure on three sides, prevailing Gulf breezes, and a tight peninsula footprint — is the same geography that concentrates wind and storm-surge risk. Homes here sit in a high-velocity wind zone, and the Mississippi Gulf Coast's hurricane season runs a full six months, from June through November. But it isn't only named storms that cause the damage requiring emergency board-up: pop-up squalls, waterspout activity near the Bay of St. Louis shoreline, and even severe thunderstorm lines tracking east along I-10 can produce localized gusts strong enough to blow out windows or lift roofing sections.

The housing stock along the Point adds another layer of complexity. Many of the older cottages and camp-style homes were built before modern wind-load codes were adopted for coastal Mississippi, meaning roof-to-wall connections and window framing may not meet current FORTIFIED standards. When a window fails on a pre-code home, the opening is often irregular — not a standard rough-opening dimension — and board-up materials have to be cut and fitted on-site rather than pulled from a template. Salt air also accelerates corrosion on fasteners and flashing, which can cause roof decking to separate at the edges faster than it would on an inland property.

## Our Emergency Board-Up and Tarping Process in Henderson Point

When you call, the first thing we establish is the nature and location of the opening — roof breach, blown window, compromised door frame, or structural wall damage — because the sequencing of work matters. A roof tarp goes on before we board a window if rain is still falling; a structural wall opening gets sheathed before a secondary glazing breach if the interior is at risk of water intrusion at the subfloor level.

For roof tarping, we use heavy-duty polyethylene tarps rated for high-wind coastal conditions, secured with batten boards and screwed into the decking — not just weighted at the edges, which can fail in the Gulf's gusty post-storm conditions. For board-up, we use 5/8-inch plywood cut to fit each opening, fastened with structural screws into the surrounding framing rather than nailed into trim, which preserves the ability to remove panels cleanly once permanent repairs begin. Every opening is photographed before and after installation, which matters when your insurance adjuster is documenting the loss.

Our team holds IICRC FSRT (Fire & Smoke) certification alongside our general restoration credentials, so when board-up follows a fire — a common scenario after a kitchen fire leaves a wall or window compromised — we understand the interaction between smoke residue, open-air exposure, and the humid Gulf Coast environment, and we communicate that to your carrier.
