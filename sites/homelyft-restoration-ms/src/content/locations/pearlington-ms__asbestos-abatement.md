Pearlington sits at the western edge of Hancock County, tucked along the Pearl River where Gulf Coast humidity rarely relents and storm-driven flooding has shaped the town's building history for generations. That combination — persistent moisture, a housing stock that includes many structures built before asbestos was phased out of residential materials in the late 1970s, and repeated cycles of damage and repair — means asbestos-containing materials turn up in Pearlington homes with unsettling regularity. When a renovation uncovers suspicious floor tile, textured ceiling material, or pipe insulation, the right call is to stop work and get a professional assessment before the air in your home becomes the problem.

## Why Pearlington Properties Carry a Higher Asbestos Risk

The Pearl River corridor experienced significant rebuilding after Hurricane Katrina in 2005, but many structures that predated the storm were patched rather than replaced — and some of those patches layered new materials over old ones that still contain asbestos. Homes built between the 1940s and 1980s in this part of Hancock County frequently incorporated asbestos in floor tiles (especially 9×9 vinyl tiles, a reliable indicator), roof shingles, attic insulation, duct wrap, and the popcorn texture sprayed on ceilings through the 1970s. When those materials are intact and undisturbed, risk is low. When they crack, crumble, or get cut during a DIY renovation, microscopic fibers become airborne — and that's where the health concern begins.

The coastal climate adds another layer of complexity. High relative humidity accelerates the deterioration of older building materials, and Pearlington's position near the river mouth means homes here see more moisture intrusion than properties further inland. Deteriorating asbestos-containing materials in crawl spaces and attics are a particular concern: what started as a slow roof leak or a crawl space drainage issue can soften and fray insulation that would otherwise have stayed stable for years.

## Our Asbestos Abatement Process in Pearlington

Every project begins with a thorough inspection and bulk sampling of suspect materials. Samples are sent to an accredited laboratory — results typically return within a few business days — and the scope of work is built around what the lab confirms, not assumptions. This matters in Pearlington because multi-layer flooring is common: a 1960s tile floor may have been covered with a 1980s layer, then laminate on top of that. Each layer needs to be evaluated independently.

Once the scope is confirmed, the work area is isolated with negative air pressure containment and heavy-gauge poly sheeting. Our EPA Lead-Safe Certified team wets materials before removal to suppress fiber release, double-bags all waste in labeled, sealed containers, and disposes of everything at a licensed facility in compliance with Mississippi Department of Environmental Quality regulations. Air clearance testing — conducted after removal and before containment is broken — confirms fiber levels have returned to acceptable thresholds before the space is handed back to you.

For popcorn ceiling asbestos removal specifically, we test a small sample before any scraping begins. Many Pearlington homeowners assume their textured ceilings are safe because the house "wasn't that old," but application of asbestos-containing texture compounds continued into the early 1980s, and some contractors used up existing stock even later.
