## Why Ovett Homes Present Unique Renovation Challenges

Jones County's climate is not gentle to buildings. Summers push heat and moisture into wall cavities, attic spaces, and crawl spaces with relentless consistency, and the region averages enough annual rainfall to keep wood framing perpetually cycling through wet and dry phases. In older Ovett homes — many built between the 1950s and 1980s — that cycle shows up as cupped hardwood floors, failed caulk lines around tubs and windows, and framing members that have drifted out of plumb over decades of soil movement.

Clay-rich soils in this part of Mississippi expand when wet and contract in dry spells, which means pier-and-beam foundations common to the area can settle unevenly. Before a kitchen remodel or bathroom renovation moves forward, it's worth assessing whether the subfloor is truly level — a crooked cabinet run or a tile floor that cracks within a year usually traces back to foundation movement that wasn't addressed during the planning phase.

Post-damage rebuilds add another layer. When a roof leak or burst pipe has been sitting undetected, the framing and sheathing behind the finished surfaces often need replacement, not just cosmetic repair. The IICRC AMRT and FSRT certifications the HomeLyft team holds mean we can assess mold colonization or smoke residue before the rebuild begins — not discover it mid-project.

## Our Renovation and General Contracting Process in Ovett

Every project starts with a detailed on-site assessment. For post-damage work, that includes moisture mapping and documentation that supports your insurance claim. For elective remodels, it means measuring actual conditions — not assuming the existing walls are square or that the plumbing is where the original blueprints say it is.

From there, the process moves through:

- **Scope and permitting** — Jones County requires permits for structural work, electrical upgrades, and plumbing changes. We pull the permits, coordinate inspections, and keep the project on the right side of the county building department.
- **Demolition and discovery** — Older homes in this area frequently reveal surprises behind walls: knob-and-tube wiring, galvanized supply lines, or insulation that's been compromised by moisture. We document what we find and adjust the scope before it becomes a cost overrun.
- **Framing, mechanical rough-ins, and insulation** — In a climate where attic temperatures regularly exceed 140°F in summer, proper insulation and ventilation aren't optional upgrades; they're the difference between an energy bill that's manageable and one that isn't.
- **Finish work and final inspection** — Tile, cabinetry, trim, paint, and fixtures are installed to a defined punch list, and the project doesn't close until the county inspection is signed off.

## Local Note: What Jones County Contractors Know About Crawl Spaces

In Ovett and the surrounding Jones County communities, a large share of the older housing stock sits on pier-and-beam foundations with open or vented crawl spaces. What contractors who work here regularly understand — and out-of-area crews sometimes miss — is that Mississippi's humidity means an "encapsulated" crawl space isn't just a nice upgrade; it's often the reason a kitchen or bathroom remodel holds up long-term. Vapor intrusion from below can buckle new hardwood floors and promote mold behind fresh cabinetry within a few seasons if the crawl space isn't addressed as part of the renovation scope. We factor that into project planning from the start, not as an add-on after the floors are already installed.
