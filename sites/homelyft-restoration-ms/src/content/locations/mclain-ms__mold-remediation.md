## Why McLain Properties See Mold Issues

Greene County's climate does most of the work for mold. The area averages more than 60 inches of rainfall annually, and the sandy-loam soils common throughout the county drain unevenly — meaning water from a heavy storm or a slow plumbing leak can pool beneath a crawl space for days before anyone notices. Many homes in the McLain area were built in the mid-20th century, when vapor barriers under crawl spaces were either thin or absent entirely. Without an effective barrier, ground moisture wicks upward into floor joists and subfloor sheathing, and mold colonies can establish themselves long before visible signs appear on interior surfaces.

Wood-framed construction with pier-and-beam foundations — common throughout this part of Greene County — is particularly susceptible. The open crawl space allows air circulation, which sounds helpful, but in south Mississippi's climate that circulating air is often saturated. When warm, humid outdoor air meets the cooler wood surfaces above, condensation forms, and mold follows. Central air conditioning systems can compound the problem: if ductwork runs through an unconditioned crawl space and develops even a small leak, the combination of cool duct surfaces and humid air creates a reliable condensation point.

## Our Mold Remediation Process in McLain

Every remediation starts with a thorough inspection — not just the room where you spotted the problem, but adjacent spaces, the crawl space if accessible, and the HVAC system. Mold rarely stays where you first find it. Our IICRC AMRT-certified technicians use moisture meters and thermal imaging to map hidden moisture pockets before any demolition begins, because removing visible mold without addressing the underlying moisture source guarantees a return visit.

Once we understand the full scope, we establish containment using negative air pressure and 6-mil poly barriers to prevent spores from migrating to unaffected areas of the home during the removal process. Affected porous materials — drywall, insulation, sections of subfloor — are removed, bagged, and disposed of properly. Structural wood surfaces that can be saved are HEPA-vacuumed, treated with an EPA-registered antimicrobial, and dried to confirmed moisture content before any rebuild begins. We document every step with photographs and moisture readings, which becomes important when you're working through a homeowner's insurance claim.

Mold claims in Mississippi can be complicated. Many standard homeowner's policies cover mold remediation only when it results directly from a covered peril — a burst pipe, storm-related intrusion, or appliance failure — and adjusters will ask for documentation showing the source. We photograph the moisture source, the affected materials, and the remediation progress at every stage, and we provide a written scope of work that aligns with how carriers typically want a claim structured.
