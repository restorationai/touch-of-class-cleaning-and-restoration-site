## Our Emergency Board-Up and Tarping Process in Waveland

When a call comes in from Waveland, the first priority is an honest assessment of what's exposed. Fire damage board-up, broken window board-up, and roof tarping each follow a different sequence, and conflating them wastes time.

For **broken window and door board-up**, the crew measures each opening, cuts 3/4-inch OSB to fit with a minimum 4-inch overlap on all sides, and secures panels with structural screws into framing — not just into the siding or brick veneer. On Waveland's coastal homes, we also apply a bead of construction sealant along the panel perimeter to resist wind-driven rain infiltration, which can push water horizontally at speeds that defeat a loose-fit panel.

For **roof tarping**, we use 6-mil polyethylene tarps rated for high-wind exposure, secured with 2×4 lumber battens screwed through the tarp and into the roof deck rather than relying on sandbags, which shift in wind. On steeper-pitch roofs common in post-Katrina rebuilds, battens are spaced no more than 4 feet apart to prevent billowing. Every tarp installation is photographed before and after for insurance documentation.

For **fire damage board-up**, the process adds a structural walk-around first — fire can compromise rafters and floor joists that aren't visually obvious, and we won't put crew on a roof or near an opening until that assessment is done.
