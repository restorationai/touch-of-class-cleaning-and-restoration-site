## Why Ovett Properties See Mold Issues

Jones County's climate is the primary driver. Average relative humidity in this part of Mississippi regularly exceeds 70 percent through the summer months, and the dense tree canopy around rural Ovett properties limits sunlight and airflow that would otherwise help structures dry out after rain. Crawl spaces are especially vulnerable: many homes in this area were built on pier-and-beam foundations with vented crawl spaces, and when vapor barriers are absent, torn, or improperly lapped, ground moisture migrates upward into floor joists and subfloor sheathing year-round.

Older wood-framed construction common to this part of Jones County also presents a specific challenge. Heart pine and older-growth lumber hold moisture differently than modern engineered wood — they can feel dry to the touch while still harboring enough moisture content in their core to sustain active mold growth. A moisture meter reading alone isn't enough; thermal imaging helps identify pockets that standard probes miss.

HVAC systems in Ovett homes also contribute. Ductwork that runs through unconditioned attic or crawl space zones sweats during the transition between seasons, and condensation on poorly insulated supply lines can drip onto insulation batts or wood framing for months before anyone notices a musty odor.

## Our Mold Remediation Process in Ovett

Every job begins with a thorough inspection — visual assessment, moisture mapping with a calibrated meter, and thermal imaging where conditions warrant. We identify the moisture source first, because removing mold without correcting the water intrusion is a temporary fix at best.

Once the scope is defined, we establish containment using 6-mil poly barriers and negative air pressure with HEPA-filtered air scrubbers. This prevents cross-contamination to unaffected rooms during the removal phase. Affected porous materials — drywall, insulation, compromised wood framing — are removed, bagged, and disposed of per Mississippi solid waste guidelines. Non-porous surfaces are HEPA-vacuumed and treated with an EPA-registered antimicrobial agent.

Structural drying follows remediation. Our IICRC ASD-certified technicians place desiccant or refrigerant dehumidifiers and high-velocity air movers calibrated to the specific material types and square footage of the affected area. Moisture readings are logged daily until the structure reaches target equilibrium moisture content. We do not close out a job until the numbers confirm the space is dry — not just dry enough.

For properties where an independent post-remediation verification (PRV) test is requested or required, we coordinate with a third-party industrial hygienist for air sampling and clearance testing before reconstruction begins.
