Out here in Greene County, the humidity sits heavier for longer stretches of the year, homes tend to be older frame houses or manufactured homes without the tight HVAC systems you find closer to the coast, and furniture takes on a different kind of wear from farm work, hunting season, and pets that live both indoors and out. Getting a couch or sectional truly clean, not just surface-fresh, means accounting for all of that before a single tool touches the fabric.

## Why State Line Furniture Wears Differently

South Mississippi's humid subtropical climate doesn't let up much between April and October, and in a rural county like Greene, air conditioning in older homes often runs on window units or aging central systems rather than whole-house humidity control. That moisture in the air settles into upholstery fibers, holding onto odors, dust, and the fine red clay soil common to this part of the state longer than it would in a drier climate. Add in a household with hunting dogs, farm chores, or kids tracking in from outside, and fabric sofas and recliners pick up ground-in soil that a home vacuum simply pushes deeper into the cushion fill. Leather furniture fares differently but not better, since humidity swings can dry and crack leather that isn't conditioned regularly.

## Our Upholstery Cleaning Process in State Line

We start with a fabric or leather identification step, because a hot-water extraction method that's safe on a synthetic blend sectional can permanently water-stain a natural fiber or aniline leather piece. For fabric upholstery, we pre-test in a hidden area, apply a pH-balanced pre-conditioner to break down oils and soil, then use low-moisture steam extraction so the piece isn't left saturated in a house where humidity already slows drying. Leather upholstery gets a separate cleaning and conditioning process that restores oils lost to sun exposure and dry indoor heat during Mississippi winters. On every job we set fans or use air movers to speed drying, since a damp cushion sitting in a humid Greene County living room for two days is exactly the kind of thing that invites mildew odor to come back.
