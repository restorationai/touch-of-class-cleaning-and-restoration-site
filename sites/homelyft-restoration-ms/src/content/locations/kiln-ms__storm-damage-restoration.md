## Why Kiln Properties See Distinctive Storm Damage

Hancock County's geography puts Kiln in a difficult position during Gulf storm seasons. The county sits close enough to the coast to catch tropical-force winds yet far enough inland that surge warnings don't always prompt the same urgency as they do in Bay St. Louis or Waveland — meaning residents sometimes stay put and deal with the aftermath themselves before calling a contractor. That delay matters. Once roof decking is exposed to Mississippi's summer humidity, interior framing and insulation can begin absorbing moisture within hours, and secondary damage compounds quickly.

The local housing stock adds another layer of complexity. A significant share of homes in this part of Hancock County were built in the 1970s through 1990s, when roofing standards were less stringent than today's Mississippi Fortified Home requirements. Older three-tab shingles and minimal roof-to-wall connections are common, which means even a Category 1 storm can strip sections of roofing that a newer Fortified-rated structure would retain. Our team holds Fortified Building Roofing MS Windstorm Certification, so when repairs reach the roof system, we understand what the upgraded standard actually requires — not just what looks acceptable on the surface.

## Our Storm Damage Restoration Process in Kiln

Every job starts with a full exterior and interior assessment. We photograph and document structural damage, roof penetrations, broken windows, and any water intrusion before a single piece of debris is moved — that documentation is essential for insurance claims and protects you if a dispute arises later.

Once the scope is clear, we move through a defined sequence:

1. **Emergency stabilization** — tarping exposed roof sections, boarding compromised windows and doors, and removing hanging limbs or debris that pose an immediate safety risk.
2. **Water intrusion mitigation** — storm damage almost always brings water inside. We use moisture meters and thermal imaging to map where water has traveled, then deploy drying equipment calibrated to the ambient conditions. Hancock County's high summer humidity means drying targets must account for elevated outdoor dew points; we adjust equipment settings accordingly rather than applying a standard protocol.
3. **Structural assessment and debris removal** — tree impact, wind uplift, and fallen materials can stress framing in ways that aren't visible from the surface. We assess load-bearing elements before reconstruction begins.
4. **Reconstruction and finishing** — from replacing roof decking and sheathing to repairing interior walls and ceilings, we carry the work through to a finished, insurable state.

All drying work follows IICRC S500 and S520 standards. Our team is IICRC certified in Water Damage Restoration and Structural Drying, and we hold EPA Lead-Safe Certification — relevant in older Kiln homes where disturbing painted surfaces during repairs requires proper handling protocols.

## Local Note

One pattern we've observed in this part of Hancock County: homes built on pier-and-beam foundations — common in rural stretches outside the Kiln town center — are more vulnerable to wind-driven rain infiltrating the subfloor cavity than slab-on-grade construction.
