## Why Sandersville Homes Present Unique Renovation Challenges

Jones County's housing stock leans older. A significant portion of homes in and around Sandersville were built between the 1940s and 1970s, which means renovation crews routinely encounter knob-and-tube wiring tucked behind walls, asbestos-containing floor tiles under vinyl, and lead-based paint on trim and window sashes. Because HomeLyft holds EPA Lead-Safe Certification, we follow federally mandated containment and disposal protocols when disturbing painted surfaces in pre-1978 homes — something a handyman or unlicensed contractor legally cannot do.

The clay soil common throughout this part of Jones County also plays a direct role in foundation behavior. Seasonal moisture swings cause the ground to heave and settle, which over time creates uneven subfloors, sticking doors, and cracked drywall — problems that look cosmetic but often point to something structural underneath. A remodel that skips a foundation assessment in this region is a remodel that may need to be redone.

Finally, Mississippi's Gulf Coast influence pushes humidity inland. Even in Sandersville, attic moisture levels and crawl space condensation can quietly rot floor joists and degrade insulation before a homeowner notices. Any renovation plan that doesn't account for vapor management is leaving the next problem already in the walls.
