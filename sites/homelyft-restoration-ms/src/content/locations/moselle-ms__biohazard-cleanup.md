## Why Moselle Properties Present Unique Biohazard Challenges

Moselle sits in a part of Mississippi where summer humidity regularly climbs above 80 percent, and that moisture doesn't stay outside. Older wood-framed homes common throughout the area — many built on pier-and-beam foundations — have subfloor assemblies and wall cavities that can absorb biological contamination quickly. Unlike poured-slab construction, pier-and-beam floors allow fluids to migrate downward into crawl spaces, where heat and humidity create conditions that accelerate microbial activity. That means the visible affected area is rarely the full extent of the problem.

Moselle's rural and semi-rural character also means properties sometimes include outbuildings, detached garages, or storage structures where incidents can occur away from the main residence. These spaces often lack climate control, which compounds the urgency of prompt remediation before heat and humidity do additional damage to structural materials.

## Our Biohazard Remediation Process in Moselle

Every job begins with a discreet arrival — unmarked vehicles when requested, no unnecessary foot traffic, and a walkthrough conducted only by certified technicians. The scope of contamination is assessed before any materials are disturbed.

From there, the process follows regulated protocols for the safe removal and disposal of biohazardous waste, including sharps, infectious material, and contaminated porous surfaces. As an IICRC Certified Firm with OSHA-trained technicians and EPA Lead-Safe Certification, the team handles materials according to Mississippi State Department of Health guidelines for biohazardous waste — not a generalized "clean and disinfect" approach, but a documented, compliant process.

Affected hard surfaces are treated with hospital-grade disinfectants. Porous materials — flooring, drywall, insulation — are evaluated for removal when contamination has penetrated beyond the surface layer. Given Moselle's humidity, any structural materials that remain are dried and monitored to prevent secondary microbial growth from taking hold in the weeks after remediation.

All waste is containerized, labeled, and transported to a licensed disposal facility. Documentation is provided for insurance and for your own records.
