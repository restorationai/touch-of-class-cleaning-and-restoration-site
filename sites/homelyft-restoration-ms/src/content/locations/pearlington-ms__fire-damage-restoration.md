## Why Pearlington Properties Face Particular Fire Damage Challenges

The housing stock in and around Pearlington skews older, with many homes built in the decades before modern fire-rated drywall and updated electrical codes became standard. Knob-and-tube wiring remnants, aging panel boxes, and wood-frame construction with minimal fire blocking mean that once a fire starts, it moves quickly through wall cavities and attic spaces before it's ever visible from outside. That hidden travel is one reason post-fire inspections here often uncover structural damage that goes well beyond what the scorch marks suggest.

The climate compounds the problem. Hancock County averages over 60 inches of rainfall annually, and relative humidity along the Pearl River corridor stays elevated for most of the year. After firefighters soak a structure, that moisture has nowhere to go quickly — especially in homes with pier-and-beam foundations where airflow under the floor is restricted. Without aggressive structural drying alongside fire cleanup, mold colonization can begin in as little as 24 to 48 hours in conditions like these.

## Our Fire and Smoke Restoration Process in Pearlington

Every fire loss is different, but the sequence matters. The first step on any Pearlington job is a thorough scope of the structure — not just the rooms with visible fire damage, but adjacent spaces where smoke and heat have migrated. Soot particles are acidic; left on surfaces, they etch glass, corrode metal fixtures, and permanently stain porous materials. Speed in the early phase protects salvageable contents and finishes.

From there, the process moves through:

- **Board-up and tarping** to secure the structure and prevent additional weather intrusion — critical in a region where an afternoon thunderstorm can turn a manageable loss into a catastrophic one.
- **Structural drying** using industrial dehumidifiers and air movers, run to IICRC S500 standards, to address water left by suppression efforts.
- **Dry and wet soot removal** using HEPA-filtered vacuums, chemical sponges, and appropriate cleaning agents matched to each surface type — painted wood, brick, insulation, and cabinetry all require different approaches.
- **Deodorization** using thermal fogging and hydroxyl generation to neutralize smoke odor at the molecular level, not just mask it.
- **Structural repairs and reconstruction** coordinated through the same team, so there's no handoff gap between remediation and rebuild.

## Local Note

One thing that catches people off guard on Pearlington fire jobs is the behavior of older heart pine flooring, which appears in a meaningful number of homes in this part of Hancock County. Heart pine is extraordinarily dense, and while that density makes it resistant to surface charring, it also means it absorbs firefighting water very slowly and releases it even more slowly during drying. Standard drying timelines don't apply. On jobs where heart pine subfloor or finish flooring is present, the team extends monitoring periods and adjusts dehumidifier placement accordingly — otherwise, moisture readings look fine at the surface while the wood core remains saturated weeks later.
