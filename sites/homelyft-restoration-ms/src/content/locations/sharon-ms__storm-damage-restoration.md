## Why Sharon Properties Face Distinctive Storm Damage Risks

Madison County's position in central Mississippi puts Sharon directly in the path of two overlapping weather threats: the Gulf moisture that fuels severe thunderstorm lines from spring through early fall, and the occasional tornado outbreak that tracks northeast across the state. What makes Sharon's situation specific is the housing stock. Many homes in the area were built in earlier decades with roof systems that predate modern wind-load standards. Older ridge-and-valley roof geometries, common on the ranch-style and split-level homes throughout the county, create uplift points that modern hip-roof designs handle more efficiently.

The clay-heavy soils in this part of Madison County also matter after a storm. When heavy rain saturates the ground faster than it can drain, hydrostatic pressure builds against foundation walls and crawl space vents. Tree canopy is another Sharon-specific factor. The tall hardwoods and pines that shade properties throughout the area are assets in summer heat, but a storm with 60-mph gusts turns them into hazards. ## Our Storm Damage Restoration Process in Sharon

Every storm job starts with a full exterior and interior assessment before any debris moves. That sequence matters: disturbing a partially collapsed section before it's documented can complicate an insurance claim and, more importantly, create a secondary collapse risk. We photograph and measure every affected area, note pre-existing conditions, and flag any safety hazards — downed lines near the structure, compromised load-bearing elements, standing water near electrical panels.

Once the site is documented, emergency stabilization begins. That typically means temporary roof tarping to stop active water intrusion, board-up of breached windows or doors, and if water has entered the structure, immediate extraction and drying equipment deployment. We use commercial-grade desiccant and refrigerant dehumidifiers alongside high-velocity air movers calibrated to the affected material types — drying a plaster ceiling over wood lathe, which appears on older Sharon-area homes, requires a different airflow strategy than drying modern OSB sheathing.

From stabilization, the process moves to structural assessment, debris removal, and repair scoping.
