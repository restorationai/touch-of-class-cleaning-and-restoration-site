## Why Laurel Properties See Mold Issues

Laurel's climate is the primary driver. The city averages over 55 inches of rainfall annually, and the region's clay-heavy soils drain slowly, meaning moisture lingers against foundations and crawl space vapor barriers long after a storm passes. Many homes in Laurel were built between the 1940s and 1970s — an era when vapor barriers were thin or absent entirely, crawl space ventilation was minimal by today's standards, and wall cavities were often insulated with materials that hold moisture rather than repel it.

HVAC systems in older Laurel homes also contribute. Ductwork routed through unconditioned crawl spaces sweats during the transition between cool nights and hot, humid days — a cycle that repeats dozens of times each spring and fall. That condensation drips onto wood joists and subfloor panels, and once those materials stay damp for more than a couple of days, mold spore counts in the air below the living space begin to climb. Homeowners often notice a musty odor in the main living area long before they see visible growth.

## Our Mold Remediation Process in Laurel

Every job starts with a thorough visual inspection and moisture mapping. We use thermal imaging cameras and calibrated moisture meters to trace the moisture source — because removing visible mold without addressing the underlying moisture pathway guarantees regrowth within weeks. Once the source is identified and controlled, we establish negative-air containment using 6-mil poly barriers and HEPA-filtered air scrubbers to prevent cross-contamination to unaffected rooms.

Affected porous materials — drywall, insulation, wood sheathing — are removed and bagged in sealed poly before being transported off-site. Structural wood that can be retained is HEPA-vacuumed, wire-brushed, and treated with an EPA-registered antimicrobial agent. Drying equipment remains in place until moisture readings confirm the substrate has returned to an acceptable baseline, following IICRC S520 standard protocol. We document every step with photographs and moisture logs, which your insurance adjuster will want.

For Laurel homes with crawl spaces — which is most of them — we pay particular attention to vapor barrier integrity and cross-ventilation. A remediation that stops at the floor decking without addressing the crawl space environment below is an incomplete job.
