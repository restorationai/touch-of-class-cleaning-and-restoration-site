## Why Gautier Properties See Elevated Contents Damage

Gautier's position between the Pascagoula River to the east and St. Martin Creek to the west means the local water table is rarely far below grade. When a plumbing failure or roof breach happens — especially during the heavy convective storms that roll in off the Gulf from June through October — water doesn't just pool on floors. It wicks upward into furniture legs, saturates area rugs, and migrates into cardboard storage boxes within the first 24 to 48 hours. Mold colonization can begin on porous materials in as little as 48 to 72 hours in Gautier's warm, humid climate, which means the window for saving fabric, paper, and wood items is genuinely short.

Fire losses add another layer. Upholstered pieces and clothing that might be salvageable in a drier region can reach a point of no return quickly here if they aren't removed, catalogued, and treated promptly.

## Local Note: Coastal Antiques and Older Wood Furniture

Gautier has a meaningful number of homes that have been in families for generations, and those households often contain furniture built from old-growth cypress and heart pine — wood species that were common in Gulf Coast construction and cabinetry through much of the 20th century. These materials are denser and more dimensionally stable than modern lumber, but they respond poorly to aggressive drying. Pushing airflow and dehumidification too hard on a cypress armoire or a heart pine sideboard can cause checking and joint separation that wasn't there before the loss. Our IICRC-certified technicians adjust drying parameters for these pieces specifically, slowing the process down when the material calls for it rather than optimizing for speed alone.
