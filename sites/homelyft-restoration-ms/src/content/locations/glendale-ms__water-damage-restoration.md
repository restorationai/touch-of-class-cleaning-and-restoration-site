Mississippi's Gulf Coast climate doesn't spare Glendale. The combination of high annual humidity, heavy summer thunderstorms, and the occasional named storm pushing moisture inland means water damage here isn't a rare emergency — it's a recurring reality. When a supply line fails at 2 a.m. or a storm pushes water under your back door, the clock starts immediately: mold can begin colonizing wet building materials in as little as 24 to 48 hours, and in Glendale's warm, humid air that window closes fast.

## Why Glendale Properties See Water Damage Issues

Glendale sits in Harrison County, where the soil profile shifts between sandy coastal sediment and clay-heavy subsoil depending on elevation. Clay soils expand when saturated and drain slowly, which means groundwater can press against slab edges and crawl-space foundations long after a rain event ends. Homes built on pier-and-beam foundations — common throughout this part of Mississippi — are especially vulnerable to prolonged moisture intrusion underneath the structure, where standing water can go undetected for days.

The region's aging housing stock adds another layer of complexity. Many homes in and around Glendale were built in the 1960s through 1980s using materials and construction methods that predate modern moisture-resistant standards. Older fiber-backed flooring, original hardwood subfloors, and plaster-over-lath wall systems absorb and hold water differently than contemporary drywall and cement board. That means the drying timeline is longer and the monitoring more intensive than a newer build would require.

Heavy rain events — including the remnants of Gulf tropical systems — can overwhelm residential drainage in a matter of hours, sending water through garage doors, window wells, and any low-lying entry point. Storm-driven water is also frequently contaminated, which changes how the affected area must be handled from a safety standpoint.

## Our Water Damage Restoration Process in Glendale

Every job starts with a thorough inspection using moisture meters and thermal imaging to map exactly where water has traveled — not just where it's visible. Water follows the path of least resistance, and in a slab-on-grade home it can wick horizontally under flooring for 10 to 15 feet from the source before showing any surface sign.

Once the scope is documented, water extraction begins using truck-mounted and portable extraction units capable of pulling hundreds of gallons from flooring, carpet, and wall cavities. After bulk water is removed, the structural drying phase begins. Our team deploys commercial-grade desiccant and refrigerant dehumidifiers alongside high-velocity air movers, positioned according to the IICRC S500 standard for psychrometric drying. Daily moisture readings track progress and determine when materials have reached acceptable dryness levels — we don't guess, and we don't pull equipment early.

As an IICRC Certified Firm with technicians holding Water Restoration Technician (WRT) and Applied Structural Drying (ASD) credentials, the drying plan is built on industry science, not intuition. If building materials contain lead paint — a real possibility in pre-1978 homes throughout this area — our EPA Lead-Safe Certified protocols govern how we handle demolition and debris.

## Glendale Insurance Coordination

Most standard homeowners policies in Mississippi cover sudden and accidental water damage — a burst pipe, a washing machine supply line failure, an ice maker hose that let go. What they typically don't cover is gradual leakage that went unaddressed, or flooding from rising surface water (which requires a separate NFIP or private flood policy).

We document every loss thoroughly: written scope, photographs of affected materials before and after extraction, moisture readings at baseline and throughout drying, and a final drying report. That documentation is what your adjuster needs to process the claim efficiently.
