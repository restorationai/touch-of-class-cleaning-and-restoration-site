## Why Gautier Properties Experience Sewage Backups

The Gulf Coast's subtropical climate delivers intense rainfall events — sometimes several inches in a matter of hours — that overwhelm both municipal sewer systems and private septic tanks throughout Jackson County. When the ground is already saturated from a prior storm, there is nowhere for the excess to go except back up through floor drains, toilets, and sink lines. Gautier's proximity to tidal waterways compounds the problem: during high-water events, the natural drainage gradient that normally carries waste away from your property can temporarily reverse.

Older neighborhoods in the city also tend to have cast-iron or clay sewer laterals that have been in the ground for decades. Root intrusion, joint separation, and corrosion narrow these pipes over time, and a heavy rain event is often what finally pushes a slow-draining line into a full backup. Septic systems on larger lots — common on the north side of town toward the Pascagoula River corridor — face similar pressure when drain fields become waterlogged and can no longer absorb effluent.

## Our Sewage Cleanup and Sanitization Process in Gautier

Raw sewage is classified as Category 3 water — the most contaminated classification in the IICRC S500 standard — which means the response protocol is more involved than a standard water loss. Here is what the process looks like on a Gautier property:

**Containment first.** Technicians establish a containment barrier around the affected area before any extraction begins. This prevents cross-contamination from spreading bacteria and pathogens into unaffected rooms, which is especially important in the open floor plans common in newer coastal construction.

**Extraction and removal.** Standing sewage is extracted using truck-mounted or portable units. Porous materials — carpet, pad, drywall below the flood line, insulation — that have contacted Category 3 water are bagged, documented, and removed. There is no safe way to dry and restore these materials in place.

**Disinfection and deodorization.** After removal, all affected hard surfaces are treated with EPA-registered disinfectants rated for sewage contamination. This step addresses the bacteria, viruses, and potential parasites present in raw sewage. Odor treatment follows, targeting the volatile organic compounds that linger in subfloor cavities and wall cavities even after visible waste is gone.

**Structural drying.** Once the space is sanitized, drying equipment — air movers, dehumidifiers, and where needed, desiccant units — is deployed to bring structural moisture levels back to baseline. In Gautier's humid climate, ambient moisture levels are already elevated, which means equipment runs longer than it would in a drier region. Technicians monitor readings daily until clearance is reached.

**Documentation.** Every step is photographed and logged. Moisture readings, material removal, and disinfection applications are recorded in a format that supports insurance claims.
