## Why Wade Properties See Water Damage Issues

The soil composition around Wade creates a particular challenge that homeowners often don't anticipate. The region's expansive clay contracts and swells with rainfall cycles, which gradually stresses foundation walls, shifts crawl space piers, and opens small gaps where groundwater can intrude during heavy rain events. Mississippi averages over 55 inches of rainfall per year, and a significant portion of that arrives in concentrated bursts — the kind that overwhelm gutters, saturate perimeter soil, and push water toward the lowest point of any structure.

Older homes in the Wade area — many built in the mid-20th century with pier-and-beam foundations — are especially vulnerable. Crawl spaces in these homes often lack adequate vapor barriers, and once moisture gets under the floor system, it doesn't leave without mechanical intervention. Wood floor joists in that environment can begin showing elevated moisture readings within 24 to 48 hours, and mold colonization on organic materials can start in as little as 48 to 72 hours under the right humidity conditions.

Plumbing failures are another consistent driver. Galvanized supply lines common in homes built before the 1970s corrode from the inside out, and by the time a pinhole leak becomes visible, the surrounding framing may have been absorbing moisture for weeks.

## Our Water Damage Restoration Process in Wade

Every water damage job starts with a thorough moisture assessment — not just the visible wet area, but the walls, ceilings, and flooring on the other side of the damage. HomeLyft technicians use thermal imaging cameras and calibrated moisture meters to map exactly where water has traveled before any drying equipment is placed. This step matters more in older Wade-area homes where plaster walls and tongue-and-groove wood subfloors can hold moisture in ways that standard drywall does not.

Once the scope is documented, standing water is extracted using truck-mounted and portable extraction units. Structural drying follows — a process governed by the IICRC S500 standard — using industrial-grade desiccant or refrigerant dehumidifiers and high-velocity air movers positioned to create a controlled drying chamber around affected materials. Moisture readings are logged daily so drying progress is documented, not guessed. This documentation matters directly for insurance claims.

If building materials cannot be dried in place — saturated drywall, swollen cabinetry, compromised insulation — controlled demolition removes only what needs to go, preserving as much of the original structure as possible before reconstruction begins.
