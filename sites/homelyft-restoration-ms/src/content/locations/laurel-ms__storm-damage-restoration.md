## Why Laurel Properties See Elevated Storm Damage Risk

Jones County averages more than 60 thunderstorm days per year, and Laurel's position inland doesn't insulate it from tropical systems that weaken but retain damaging wind and rain as they push north from the Gulf. What makes Laurel distinct is the housing stock: a significant portion of the city's residential neighborhoods feature homes built between the 1920s and 1960s, with wood-framed construction, older roofing systems, and mature pecan and water oak trees that have root systems extending beneath foundations and drip lines over rooflines. When those trees fail in high winds — and they do, regularly — the structural exposure is immediate. Roof decking from that era is often thinner and less resistant to impact than modern OSB, meaning a single large branch can open a large section of roof to rainfall within minutes.

Tornado activity is also a genuine local risk. Jones County has recorded multiple confirmed tornadoes in recent decades, and the terrain through Laurel offers little natural wind break. ## Our Storm Damage Restoration Process in Laurel

Every job starts with a full exterior and interior assessment — roof, attic, walls, and subfloor — because water from storm intrusion rarely stays where it enters. A breach at the roofline can travel along rafters, pool in wall cavities, and saturate insulation before it shows up as a ceiling stain. We document everything with photos and moisture readings before any material is moved, which matters both for your insurance adjuster and for making sure no wet pocket is missed.

From there, the sequence is: emergency weatherproofing (tarps, plywood board-up, or temporary roof panels depending on the breach), controlled water extraction where standing water is present, and then drying using commercial-grade desiccant and refrigerant dehumidifiers calibrated for Mississippi's ambient humidity levels — not the drier conditions equipment is often factory-set for. Structural drying follows the IICRC S500 standard, with daily moisture mapping to confirm drying progress rather than guessing. Reconstruction — framing, sheathing, roofing, drywall, and finish work — is handled in-house under license #RO 2728, so there's no handoff gap between the mitigation phase and getting your home closed up.

One pattern that shows up repeatedly on Laurel storm jobs involves homes with older shake or three-tab shingles over skip sheathing — a common construction method in Jones County homes built before the 1960s.
