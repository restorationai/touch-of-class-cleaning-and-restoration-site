## Why Ocean Springs Properties See Asbestos Risks

Mississippi's Gulf Coast saw a significant construction boom in the mid-20th century, and Ocean Springs was no exception. The city's historic residential neighborhoods contain a dense concentration of homes built between the 1940s and 1970s — precisely the window when asbestos was used most heavily in residential construction. Popcorn ceilings, vinyl floor tiles, duct insulation, and exterior siding from that era routinely tested positive for chrysotile asbestos in our work across Jackson County.

The coastal climate adds a layer of urgency. High relative humidity, combined with the thermal cycling that comes from hot Gulf summers and mild-but-damp winters, causes building materials to expand, contract, and degrade over time. Asbestos-containing materials that have been repeatedly wet from storm surge, roof leaks, or plumbing failures are at elevated risk of becoming friable — meaning fibers can be released with minimal disturbance. After major storm events, this is not a hypothetical concern; it's a documented pattern on the Mississippi Gulf Coast.

## Our Asbestos Abatement Process in Ocean Springs

Every project starts with a thorough inspection and bulk sampling — no assumptions, no guesswork. Suspect materials are collected by trained personnel and sent to an accredited laboratory for polarized light microscopy (PLM) analysis. Results typically return within 24–48 hours, giving you a clear picture before any work begins.

If asbestos is confirmed, the abatement scope is scoped to Mississippi Department of Environmental Quality (MDEQ) requirements and EPA NESHAP regulations, both of which govern notification timelines, containment standards, and disposal procedures. Our team establishes negative air pressure containment using HEPA-filtered air scrubbers, seals off affected areas from the rest of the structure, and removes materials using wet methods to suppress fiber release. All waste is double-bagged, labeled, and transported to a licensed disposal facility — nothing is left on-site or in a standard dumpster.

After removal, a visual inspection and air clearance sampling confirm fiber levels have returned to acceptable thresholds before containment is broken. This step is not optional — it's required for regulatory compliance and protects your family or tenants from re-entry into an unsafe space.
