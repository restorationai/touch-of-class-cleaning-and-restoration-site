## Why Lyman Properties Face Elevated Board-Up and Tarping Risks

Harrison County's position along the Mississippi Gulf Coast puts Lyman squarely in the path of named storms, tropical systems, and the fast-moving squall lines that spin off Gulf low-pressure systems between June and November. What makes Lyman distinct from coastal communities a few miles south is the tree canopy. Mature loblolly pines and water oaks — common throughout the unincorporated residential areas west of U.S. 49 — grow tall and root in sandy, poorly draining soil. When saturated ground meets 60-mph gusts, root failure is common, and a falling pine doesn't just damage a roof; it can shear off fascia, collapse an attic section, or drive through an exterior wall entirely.

Older ranch-style and pier-and-beam homes built in the 1960s and 1970s — a significant portion of Lyman's housing stock — were constructed before Mississippi adopted its current wind-load standards. Roof sheathing on those structures is often 3/8-inch plywood or skip-sheathing, which delaminates quickly once water infiltrates. That means the window for effective tarping before structural sheathing is compromised is shorter than on newer builds.

## Our Emergency Board-Up and Tarping Process in Lyman

When a call comes in from Lyman, the first thing our crew assesses remotely is the damage type — storm breach, fire, or vandalism — because each dictates different materials and sequencing on arrival.

For **roof tarping**, we use 6-mil reinforced polyethylene tarps secured with 2×4 lumber battens screwed through the tarp and into intact decking — not just weighted down or stapled. On older homes where sheathing integrity is uncertain, we probe before anchoring to avoid driving fasteners into compromised wood. Tarps are lapped a minimum of four feet over the ridge and secured on all four sides to prevent wind uplift, which matters enormously in a county that regularly sees post-storm gusts even after a system passes.

For **board-up after fire damage or broken windows**, we cut OSB panels to fit each opening, frame them with interior ledger boards where possible so the panel bears against the wall rather than relying solely on exterior fasteners, and seal edges with construction adhesive to limit smoke and moisture migration. On fire-damaged structures, we coordinate with the fire marshal's office if the scene has not yet been released, and we photograph every opening before and after boarding for your insurance documentation.

All work is performed by an IICRC Certified Firm with FSRT (Fire and Smoke Restoration) credentialing, which means the crew reading your structure has formal training in how fire and smoke behave — not just how to swing a hammer.
