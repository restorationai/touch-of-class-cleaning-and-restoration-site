**Need a new roof in Pass Christian or a roof replacement after storm damage?** Call anytime, our crews answer 24/7. Salt air off the Mississippi Sound and the wind-driven rain that rolls in off the Gulf put a different kind of wear on a roof here than you'd see thirty miles inland, and that changes how we approach installation and replacement work in this city.

Pass Christian's roofs don't fail the way roofs fail in drier climates. Years of humidity and salt spray corrode exposed fasteners and flashing long before the shingles themselves wear out, and UV exposure off the water bleaches and brittles asphalt faster than manufacturer warranties usually account for. A lot of the housing stock here was rebuilt or re-roofed in the years following Hurricane Katrina, which means a wave of roofs installed in the same stretch of years are now aging out together. If your roof went on in that post-storm rebuilding period, it's worth having it inspected even if there's no visible leak yet, because granule loss and underlayment breakdown often show up before a ceiling stain does.

## Why Pass Christian Properties See Roofing Issues Sooner

Beyond salt and sun, wind is the other big variable. Homes near the beachfront along Highway 90 take direct gusts off open water with nothing to break them up, so uplift at the eaves and ridge tends to be the first failure point, not the field of the roof. Older homes with original decking sometimes have fastener patterns that don't meet current wind-rated nailing schedules, which is something we flag during tear-off rather than after a storm finds the weak spot for us.

## Our Roofing Installation and Replacement Process in Pass Christian

We start with a full roof and attic inspection, including deck condition, flashing at every penetration, and ventilation, because trapped attic moisture is as common a cause of early failure here as storm damage. Tear-off and deck repair come next, and on coastal properties we pay close attention to any soft decking near chimneys or plumbing stacks where years of humidity have done slow damage. From there we install underlayment and a wind-rated shingle or metal system matched to your home's exposure, with flashing and fastening done to the standard required for coastal wind zones. Every job is cleaned up and photo-documented, which matters when the paperwork goes to your insurer.

## FORTIFIED Standards and Insurance on the Coast

Mississippi coastal insurers increasingly look for FORTIFIED roofing standards when they're pricing windstorm coverage, and we build to that standard as a FORTIFIED-certified roofing contractor rather than treating it as an upsell. That distinction can matter directly for your premium, and we document the installation in a way that supports a FORTIFIED designation request to your carrier if you want to pursue one. We're a licensed Mississippi roofing contractor (MS #RO 2728), and we handle the estimate and claim documentation side of a storm-damage replacement so you're not negotiating scope with an adjuster alone.

## Local note

One thing we watch for on Pass Christian jobs that doesn't come up as often inland: homes close to the water often have HVAC condensate lines, satellite mounts, or solar conduit routed through the roof deck in ways that weren't part of the original plan, usually added after a rebuild. We trace those penetrations during tear-off so nothing gets re-roofed over and sealed in by accident, since a buried, unflashed penetration is one of the more common slow leaks we find in homes along this stretch of coast.
