## Why Eastabuchie Properties See Sewage Backup Issues

Jones County's red-clay soil drains poorly. During wet seasons — and this part of Mississippi sees significant rainfall from late winter through early summer — the ground around septic systems becomes so saturated that drain fields lose their ability to absorb effluent. The result is a backup that pushes sewage up through floor drains, toilets, and shower pans inside the home. Older rural properties in the Eastabuchie area often rely on aging septic infrastructure that hasn't been pumped or inspected in years, which compounds the risk. Cast-iron sewer lines common in homes built before the 1980s are also prone to root intrusion and corrosion, creating partial blockages that turn a slow drain into a full backup the next time the system is stressed. Understanding these local patterns shapes how we approach every job — we're not just extracting water; we're dealing with Category 3 contamination in structures that often have pier-and-beam foundations with open crawl spaces, which means contamination can spread laterally under the floor before it's even visible inside the living area.

## Our Sewage Cleanup and Sanitization Process in Eastabuchie

The first thing our crew does on arrival is contain the affected zone to prevent cross-contamination into clean areas of the home. Category 3 water — which is what raw sewage constitutes — requires full personal protective equipment and a methodical approach that goes well beyond mopping up visible liquid.

Here's how the process unfolds on a typical Eastabuchie job:

- **Extraction**: Truck-mounted and portable extraction units remove standing sewage and contaminated water from floors, subfloor cavities, and crawl spaces.
- **Controlled demolition**: Porous materials that have absorbed sewage — drywall, insulation, carpet, and in some cases subfloor sheathing — are removed and bagged for proper disposal. There is no safe way to sanitize saturated porous material in place.
- **Disinfection and antimicrobial treatment**: Hard surfaces, framing lumber, and concrete are treated with EPA-registered disinfectants rated for Category 3 contamination. We apply, dwell, and verify — not a single pass with a mop.
- **Structural drying**: After sanitization, drying equipment (desiccant dehumidifiers and air movers) runs until moisture readings in framing and subfloor materials return to pre-loss baselines. Our IICRC ASD-certified technicians monitor readings daily and adjust equipment placement accordingly.
- **Documentation**: Every affected area is photographed and logged before, during, and after — a complete file your insurance carrier can work from directly.
