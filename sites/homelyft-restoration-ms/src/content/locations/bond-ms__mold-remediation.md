## Why Bond Properties See Mold Issues

Mississippi's climate is the primary driver. Bond and the surrounding areas of Lawrence County experience long stretches of high relative humidity — often above 70% for weeks at a time during spring and summer — which is exactly the threshold at which common mold species like Cladosporium, Aspergillus, and Stachybotrys (the genus behind what people call "black mold") establish and spread. Unlike coastal cities where salt air is the complicating factor, inland communities like Bond deal with a combination of warm temperatures, clay-heavy soils that retain ground moisture, and older housing stock that wasn't built with today's vapor barriers or modern HVAC dehumidification in mind.

Many homes in Bond and the broader Lawrence County area were constructed in eras when crawl space ventilation standards were minimal. Unencapsulated crawl spaces draw ground moisture upward, and once that moisture contacts wood floor joists or subfloor sheathing, mold has everything it needs. A slow drip from a supply line or a single season of inadequate crawl space airflow can silently colonize structural wood before a homeowner ever sees visible growth.

## Our Mold Remediation Process in Bond

Every job starts with a thorough visual inspection and moisture mapping — we use thermal imaging cameras and calibrated moisture meters to locate hidden moisture pockets behind drywall and under flooring before any demolition begins. Guessing at the boundary of mold growth is how remediation jobs fail; measuring it is how they succeed.

Once the affected area is defined, we establish negative-air containment using 6-mil poly barriers and HEPA-filtered air scrubbers. This prevents mold spores disturbed during removal from migrating to unaffected rooms — a step that matters especially in homes with open floor plans or central HVAC systems that could redistribute spores. Contaminated materials — drywall, insulation, compromised wood — are removed, bagged, and disposed of per Mississippi DEQ guidelines.

Structural wood that can be preserved is treated with EPA-registered antimicrobial agents and dried to documented moisture levels before any encapsulation or rebuild begins. Our team holds the IICRC AMRT (Applied Microbial Remediation Technician) certification, which means the process follows a defined industry standard — not a crew's best guess. We also carry EPA Lead-Safe Certification, which matters in Bond's older homes where disturbing painted surfaces during mold removal can create a secondary lead exposure risk.
