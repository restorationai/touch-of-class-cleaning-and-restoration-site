## Why Moss Point Properties Face Distinct Fire Damage Challenges

The housing stock in Moss Point skews older, with a significant portion of single-family homes built during the postwar timber-industry boom of the 1950s and 1960s. Those homes were framed with old-growth pine — dense, resinous wood that burns hot and slow and holds smoke odor deep in the grain. When fire moves through a structure like that, the char isn't just surface-level. Soot particles migrate into wall cavities, subflooring, and attic insulation in ways that modern OSB construction simply doesn't replicate.

The Gulf Coast climate adds another layer. Jackson County averages over 60 inches of rainfall annually, and the ambient humidity rarely drops below 60 percent even in winter. After a fire, that moisture accelerates the bonding of acidic smoke residue to painted surfaces, metal fixtures, and glass — a process that can cause permanent etching and corrosion within 72 hours if surfaces aren't treated. Homes near the waterways also tend to have crawl spaces rather than slab foundations, and smoke-laden air settles into those spaces and saturates insulation and floor joists in ways that aren't immediately visible.

## Our Fire and Smoke Restoration Process in Moss Point

The first step on any fire damage job is a structured assessment — not a visual walkthrough, but a systematic inspection that documents char depth, smoke migration paths, and moisture readings throughout the structure. We use thermal imaging to identify heat-affected areas inside walls before any demolition begins, which prevents unnecessary tear-out and gives your insurance adjuster a defensible scope of loss.

Once the scope is established, controlled demolition removes unsalvageable materials — charred framing, melted insulation, smoke-saturated drywall. What remains gets treated with dry-ice blasting or chemical sponge cleaning depending on the substrate. Porous surfaces like brick and unfinished wood require a different protocol than painted drywall or cabinetry. HEPA air scrubbers run continuously to capture fine particulate, and hydroxyl generators or thermal fogging address odor molecules that have penetrated deep into structural materials — a step that can't be skipped in a high-humidity environment like Moss Point's.

Our team holds IICRC FSRT (Fire and Smoke Restoration) certification, and the work follows documented industry standards for scope, sequencing, and clearance. That documentation matters when your adjuster reviews the claim.
