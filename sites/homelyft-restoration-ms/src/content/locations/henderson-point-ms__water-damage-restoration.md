## Why Henderson Point Properties See Water Damage Issues

The geography here is the first factor. Henderson Point is a low-lying coastal community on the western edge of Harrison County, and its elevation means that even moderate rainfall events can overwhelm drainage. The soil profile along the peninsula is predominantly sandy and saturated, so groundwater sits close to the surface year-round. When a slab foundation develops a plumbing leak or a heavy rain event pushes water under a door threshold, that water has an easy path to spread laterally across a floor system before it becomes visible.

The housing stock adds another layer of complexity. Many homes in the Henderson Point area were built in the post-Katrina reconstruction era, but a meaningful number of older cottages and camp-style homes predate modern moisture barriers and vapor retarders. Older crawl space construction in this area often lacks the cross-ventilation or encapsulation that would slow moisture migration into floor joists and subfloor sheathing. When water gets into those systems, drying times are longer and monitoring has to be more frequent.

Coastal salt air also accelerates corrosion on supply lines, valves, and washing machine hoses — components that fail quietly until they don't. A slow drip behind a cabinet can saturate particleboard and framing for weeks before the smell gives it away.

## Our Water Damage Restoration Process in Henderson Point

The first step on any job is moisture mapping. Technicians use thermal imaging cameras and calibrated moisture meters to trace water migration beyond what is visible — under tile, inside wall cavities, and beneath baseboards. In Henderson Point homes with slab-on-grade construction, this step is especially important because water wicks laterally through concrete and into adjacent rooms without leaving an obvious trail.

Once the scope is confirmed, water extraction begins using truck-mounted and portable extraction units capable of pulling standing water and saturated material quickly. Structural drying follows using commercial-grade desiccant and refrigerant dehumidifiers, along with high-velocity air movers positioned to create a directed airflow pattern through affected cavities. Drying targets are set per the IICRC S500 standard and verified with daily moisture readings documented in a drying log — that log matters later if an insurance adjuster requests evidence of proper protocol.

Materials that cannot be dried in place — saturated insulation, compromised drywall below the flood line, swollen cabinetry — are removed and documented before disposal. The goal is to hand back a structure that reads dry on instruments, not just dry to the eye.
