## Why DeLisle Properties Are Especially Vulnerable After a Storm or Fire

The Gulf Coast's combination of high annual rainfall, hurricane-season wind events, and persistent humidity creates a short window between structural damage and secondary loss. In DeLisle specifically, a significant portion of the housing stock consists of wood-frame construction — pier-and-beam homes and slab-on-grade builds from the 1970s through the 1990s — that can absorb moisture rapidly once the building envelope is breached. Roof decking that was already softened by years of heat cycling can fail faster than newer OSB panels, meaning a partial tarp that doesn't extend far enough past the damage zone will still allow water infiltration at the edges.

Harrison County also sees a pattern of fast-moving convective storms that cause localized damage — a single street loses trees and roofing while the next block over is untouched. That kind of isolated event often means a homeowner is dealing with the damage alone, without neighbors who experienced the same loss and can share contractor contacts. Getting a licensed, insured crew on-site quickly matters more in those situations.

## Our Emergency Board-Up and Tarping Process in DeLisle

When we arrive at a DeLisle property, the first step is a rapid exterior assessment — we walk the perimeter before touching anything, looking for compromised structural members, downed utility lines, and secondary hazards like broken glass or unstable fascia. Only after that safety check do we begin measuring and cutting materials.

For roof tarping, we use 6-mil reinforced poly tarps secured with 2×4 cap strips screwed into solid decking — not just draped over the ridge and weighted with sandbags, which fail in the first wind gust. The tarp is sized to overlap the undamaged roofing by at least three feet on every side, and we run the downslope edge past the gutter line so water sheds clear of the fascia. On homes with steep pitches or second-story damage, we use fall-protection equipment and never rush the anchor points.

For fire damage board-up and broken window board-up, we cut plywood to fit each opening precisely, using exterior-grade ¾-inch panels secured with structural screws rather than nails, which back out under thermal expansion. Door openings get a framed panel with a hasp lock so the property owner or adjuster can still access the interior. Every opening is photographed before and after — that documentation becomes part of your insurance claim file.

We hold an IICRC FSRT (Fire & Smoke) certification and are a FORTIFIED Building / Roofing / MS Windstorm Certified contractor, so our methods align with the standards insurers and adjusters expect to see on a documented loss.

## Local Note: Pier-and-Beam Homes and Tarp Anchor Points

One thing that catches out-of-area crews in DeLisle is the prevalence of older pier-and-beam homes where the roof overhangs extend further than typical modern construction. That extra overhang is great for shade but it means a standard tarp run from ridge to eave doesn't reach the drip edge — water still gets behind the fascia and into the soffit cavity. We account for that by measuring overhang depth before cutting tarp length, and we add a secondary seal strip along the fascia using self-adhering flashing tape on any home where the soffit shows prior moisture staining. It's a small detail that prevents a "tarped" roof from still leaking at the edges.
