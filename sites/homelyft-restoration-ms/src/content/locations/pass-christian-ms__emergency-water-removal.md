## Why Pass Christian Properties See Water Removal Emergencies

Pass Christian sits directly on the Mississippi Gulf Coast in Harrison County, and that location shapes almost every water loss we see here. Heavy Gulf-driven rain events and tropical storm bands can dump several inches in an afternoon, overwhelming older drainage patterns in low-lying yards and sending water up through slab seams or crawlspace vents. Homes built or rebuilt since Hurricane Katrina are often elevated on piers, which protects the main living level from storm surge but means plumbing runs, water heaters, and HVAC lines sometimes sit in the elevated crawlspace or ground-floor utility area, exactly where a slow pipe leak can go unnoticed until it's saturated insulation and subfloor framing. Pre-Katrina and historic district homes with original wood siding and heart pine flooring behave differently once wet: the flooring swells and cups, and moisture can wick along baseboards for days if extraction isn't thorough the first time.

## Our Water Removal Process in Pass Christian

We start with moisture mapping to find out how far water has traveled, not just where it's visible, because Gulf Coast humidity means surface-dry doesn't mean structure-dry. Truck-mounted extraction pulls standing water fast, then we move to targeted extraction on carpet pad, cushion, and any flooring that can be saved. Air movers and dehumidifiers get positioned based on the home's construction, elevated pier-and-beam homes need airflow directed into the crawlspace and subfloor, while slab homes need drying concentrated at the wall base and baseboard line. We monitor moisture content daily with meters and document readings for the insurance file, drying structural materials to the IICRC S500 standard rather than guessing by feel or smell.
