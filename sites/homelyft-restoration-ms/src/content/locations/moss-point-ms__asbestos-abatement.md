## Why Moss Point Properties Face Elevated Asbestos Risk

The Gulf Coast's humidity is relentless, and in Moss Point that means building materials age faster than they might in a drier climate. When moisture infiltrates a home — through roof damage after a tropical system, through plumbing failures, or through the slow creep of a crawl space flood — it accelerates the deterioration of asbestos-containing materials. Friable asbestos, meaning material that crumbles easily under hand pressure, releases fibers into the air. A water-damaged ceiling tile or a swollen floor section in a home built before 1980 is not just a cosmetic problem; it may be an active exposure risk.

Moss Point's residential building stock skews older. Many homes were constructed between the 1940s and the 1970s, the precise window when asbestos use in residential construction peaked. Roofing shingles, HVAC duct insulation, drywall joint compound, and vinyl floor tiles from that period routinely tested positive for asbestos content. Before any renovation — even a cosmetic refresh like scraping a popcorn ceiling or replacing original flooring — a professional inspection and bulk sampling is the responsible first step.

## Our Asbestos Abatement Process in Moss Point

Every project begins with an asbestos inspection and bulk material sampling. Suspect materials are collected according to EPA protocol and sent to an accredited laboratory. Results typically return within a few business days, and the findings determine the scope of work.

If asbestos-containing material is confirmed, the abatement area is isolated using polyethylene sheeting and negative air pressure equipment — HEPA-filtered air scrubbers that pull contaminated air through the containment and exhaust it safely, preventing fiber migration to adjacent rooms. Workers wear full respiratory protection and disposable coveralls throughout the process.

Removal follows the Mississippi DEQ's asbestos NESHAP (National Emission Standards for Hazardous Air Pollutants) requirements, which govern everything from wetting procedures during removal to the double-bagging and labeling of waste. All asbestos-containing debris is transported to a licensed disposal facility — none of it ends up in a standard construction dumpster. After removal, air clearance testing confirms fiber counts are within acceptable limits before containment is dismantled. That final clearance is the checkpoint that protects both the occupants and the contractor.
