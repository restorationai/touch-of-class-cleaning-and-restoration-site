## Why Hattiesburg Properties See Water Damage Issues

The local housing stock tells a big part of the story. Hattiesburg grew rapidly in the mid-20th century, and a large share of its single-family homes were built between the 1940s and 1980s — an era when vapor barriers were minimal, crawl space ventilation was inconsistent, and galvanized steel supply lines were standard. Those galvanized pipes corrode from the inside out, narrowing over decades until a pressure spike or a hard freeze splits them without warning. The region doesn't get many hard freezes, but when overnight temperatures drop into the mid-20s — as they do a handful of times each winter — pipes in unconditioned crawl spaces and exterior walls are genuinely vulnerable.

The clay-heavy soils common across Forrest County also shift with moisture changes. After a wet spring, that soil expands and can push against foundation walls; after a dry summer, it contracts and creates gaps that redirect surface water toward the foundation during the next heavy rain. Homes with aging or improperly graded drainage around the perimeter are especially susceptible to water intrusion at the slab edge or through crawl space vents.

## Our Water Damage Restoration Process in Hattiesburg

Every job starts with a moisture survey — thermal imaging and calibrated moisture meters across walls, ceilings, and subfloor assemblies — before any equipment is placed. That step matters more in older Hattiesburg homes than it might in newer construction, because water in a pier-and-beam crawl space travels laterally along floor joists in ways that aren't obvious from the room above. Skipping the survey means missing wet framing that will grow mold behind finished walls.

Once the scope is mapped, standing water is removed with truck-mounted extraction units, followed by targeted drying with commercial-grade desiccant and refrigerant dehumidifiers and high-velocity air movers positioned to create directional airflow through the structure. Drying is monitored daily with psychrometric readings — temperature, relative humidity, and grain depression — and documented against the IICRC S500 standard. The process typically runs three to five days for a contained loss; larger events or homes with significant crawl space involvement often run longer.

## Local Note

One pattern that comes up repeatedly in Hattiesburg's older neighborhoods is the combination of hardwood floors over pier-and-beam construction. When water enters from below — a crawl space flood, a plumbing leak under the subfloor — the hardwood absorbs moisture from the underside first, which causes cupping (edges rising, center dropping) before the surface shows any visible damage. Homeowners sometimes assume the floor is fine because it looks dry from above. By the time the cupping is visible, the subfloor sheathing may already be at elevated moisture content. Catching this early with underfloor moisture readings is the difference between saving the floor and replacing it.
