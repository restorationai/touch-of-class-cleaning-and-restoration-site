## Why Ellisville Properties See Sewage Backup Problems

Ellisville sits in a part of Jones County where a significant share of the housing stock relies on private septic systems rather than a centralized sewer connection. Septic tanks that haven't been pumped on schedule — or drain fields that have reached the end of their service life — are among the most common sources of sewage overflow calls in the area. When a drain field fails in waterlogged soil, effluent has nowhere to go except back toward the structure.

Older homes in and around Ellisville also tend to have cast-iron or clay sewer laterals that corrode, crack, or shift over time. Tree root intrusion into those aging lines is a recurring problem, particularly on lots with mature hardwoods. A slow-developing root blockage can go unnoticed until a single heavy-use event — a large family gathering, a washing machine running back-to-back loads — pushes the system past its limit and waste backs up into living spaces.

Mississippi's climate adds another layer of risk. Extended dry spells cause soil to contract and shift, stressing pipe joints. When the rains return hard and fast, the sudden ground movement and surge in groundwater can crack compromised laterals or overwhelm a septic system that was already borderline.

## Our Sewage Cleanup and Sanitization Process in Ellisville

Raw sewage is classified as Category 3 water — the most contaminated type — because it contains bacteria, viruses, and parasites that pose real health risks. The process we follow is structured around that reality, not just surface cleaning.

**Containment and removal first.** We establish containment to prevent cross-contamination to unaffected rooms, then extract standing sewage and remove porous materials — flooring, drywall, insulation — that cannot be adequately disinfected once saturated with Category 3 water. We photograph and document everything before demolition for your insurance adjuster.

**Disinfection and deodorization.** After extraction, affected structural surfaces are treated with EPA-registered disinfectants rated for sewage contamination. This isn't a single wipe-down — it's a multi-step application process that addresses both the odor compounds and the microbial load embedded in subfloor materials, wall cavities, and concrete.

**Drying and verification.** Once surfaces are disinfected, we deploy commercial dehumidifiers and air movers calibrated to Mississippi's ambient humidity levels, which run higher than national averages for much of the year. Moisture readings are logged daily until structural materials reach acceptable drying targets per the IICRC S500 standard.
