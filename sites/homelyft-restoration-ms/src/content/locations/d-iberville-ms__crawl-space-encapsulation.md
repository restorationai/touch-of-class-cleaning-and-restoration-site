D'Iberville sits in a bowl of coastal Mississippi humidity where summer air routinely holds more moisture than the Gulf itself — and that moisture finds its way into every unprotected crawl space in Harrison County. Homes built on the sandy, poorly draining soils common to this part of the coast can see standing water under the floor joists after a single heavy rain, and once wood stays damp for more than 48 hours, mold colonization begins. Crawl space encapsulation seals that cycle off at the source, protecting the structure and the air quality of everything above it.

## Why D'Iberville Properties Face Persistent Crawl Space Problems

The geography here works against homeowners in a few compounding ways. D'Iberville's position between Back Bay of Biloxi and the I-10 corridor means the water table is shallow — in some areas only a foot or two below grade during wet seasons. Vented crawl spaces, which were the standard construction method for decades, were designed for drier climates. In coastal Mississippi's subtropical humidity, those vents pull warm, saturated outside air directly into a cool crawl space, where it condenses on every wood surface and pier block it touches.

That condensation feeds wood rot, attracts termites, and pushes musty air up through floor gaps and HVAC returns into the living space above. Homeowners often notice soft spots in flooring or a persistent earthy smell long before they realize the crawl space is the source. By the time those symptoms appear, the subfloor framing may already need remediation before encapsulation can begin.

## Our Crawl Space Encapsulation Process in D'Iberville

Every project starts with a thorough inspection of the crawl space — moisture readings at multiple points across the floor, visual assessment of existing vapor barriers (if any), and a check for standing water, mold growth, or pest damage. In D'Iberville's climate, we almost always find that thin poly sheeting laid loosely on the ground has failed: it tears, shifts, and leaves gaps where soil moisture escapes freely.

Once the space is cleared and any remediation is complete, we install a reinforced, 20-mil polyethylene encapsulation liner across the entire floor and up the foundation walls, sealed at every seam and penetration. Vents are closed and sealed — a step that surprises some homeowners but is consistent with modern building science for humid coastal climates. A properly sized dehumidifier is then installed and connected to a condensate drain, keeping relative humidity in the crawl space below 60% year-round. We document pre- and post-installation moisture readings so you have a baseline for future reference.

Because our team holds IICRC certification in Applied Structural Drying and Mold Remediation, we can address any active mold or moisture damage discovered during the inspection before the liner goes down — rather than sealing a problem inside.
