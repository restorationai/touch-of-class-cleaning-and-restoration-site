## Why Sharon Properties See Water Damage Issues

Madison County's climate delivers humid summers and periodic heavy rainfall events that can overwhelm gutters, crawl space drainage, and aging supply lines in a matter of hours. Sharon's housing stock includes a meaningful share of older homes where galvanized or early copper plumbing has reached the end of its service life — small pinhole leaks that go unnoticed for weeks before a cabinet floor gives way or a musty odor signals something worse behind the drywall. The expansive clay soils common throughout this part of central Mississippi also shift seasonally, stressing foundation penetrations and the joints where supply lines enter the slab or crawl space. That combination — older plumbing, clay soil movement, and high ambient humidity — means water that enters a structure here doesn't evaporate on its own; it migrates into subfloor assemblies and wall bays where mold can begin colonizing within 24 to 48 hours of initial saturation.

## Our Water Damage Restoration Process in Sharon

When our crew arrives at a Sharon property, the first step is a full moisture mapping of the affected area using thermal imaging cameras and calibrated moisture meters. Standing water is extracted with truck-mounted and portable extraction units before any drying equipment is placed — skipping extraction and going straight to air movers is a shortcut that leaves moisture trapped in subfloor cavities. Once extraction is complete, we establish a drying system using industrial-grade desiccant or refrigerant dehumidifiers paired with high-velocity air movers positioned to create directed airflow across wet assemblies. Drying targets are set to the IICRC S500 standard, and we return daily to log moisture readings and adjust equipment placement until structural materials reach documented dry standard. Every reading is recorded and shared with your adjuster in the format most carriers require for Mississippi residential claims.

One pattern we've observed in older Sharon-area homes is that crawl space vapor barriers — when present at all — are often undersized or have pulled away from the piers over years of soil movement.
