## Why Escatawpa Properties See Crawl Space Problems

The Gulf Coast climate doesn't stop at the water's edge. Escatawpa, tucked along the Alabama border in Jackson County, experiences the same oppressive summer humidity as coastal communities but without the coastal breeze that occasionally dries things out. Warm, moisture-laden air migrates into open or vented crawl spaces and condenses on cooler wood framing and floor joists — a process that repeats itself daily from April through October. Over time, that cycling moisture softens subflooring, encourages mold growth (mold can begin colonizing damp wood within 24 to 48 hours of sustained moisture exposure), and attracts termites and other wood-destroying insects that are already active throughout Jackson County.

Many homes in this part of Mississippi were built before modern building science caught up with the region's climate realities. Older construction often relied on passive venting — the idea that outside air would dry out a crawl space — but in a climate where outdoor air is itself saturated for months at a time, venting can make things worse, not better. Fiberglass batt insulation stapled between floor joists, a common sight in older Escatawpa homes, holds moisture against wood rather than protecting it.

## Our Crawl Space Encapsulation Process in Escatawpa

Encapsulation in this climate isn't a one-size product install — it's a sequenced process that starts with an honest assessment of what's already happening under the home.

**Step 1 — Inspection and moisture mapping.** Before any material goes down, the crew documents existing moisture readings in the wood framing, checks for standing water or drainage issues, and identifies any active mold or pest evidence. If mold is present, remediation happens first; our team holds IICRC AMRT (Applied Microbial Remediation Technician) certification, so that work stays in-house rather than requiring a separate contractor.

**Step 2 — Prep and drainage.** Low spots that pond water are addressed. Any existing fiberglass batt insulation that has absorbed moisture is removed and disposed of properly — leaving saturated insulation in place defeats the encapsulation entirely.

**Step 3 — Liner installation.** A reinforced polyethylene vapor barrier — typically 20-mil in this climate, heavier than the minimum code allows — is run across the crawl space floor and up the foundation walls, sealed at seams and penetrations with purpose-made tape. In Escatawpa's clay-heavy soil, a thicker liner resists puncture from ground movement and root intrusion better than lighter alternatives.

**Step 4 — Conditioning or dehumidification.** A sealed crawl space still needs active moisture control. Depending on the home's HVAC configuration, the crawl space is either conditioned (connected to the home's air system) or fitted with a dedicated crawl space dehumidifier. Given the sustained humidity levels typical of Jackson County summers, a dehumidifier with a condensate drain to daylight is often the more reliable long-term solution.

**Step 5 — Final readings and documentation.** Before the job closes, moisture readings are taken again and photographed. If the homeowner is filing an insurance claim related to water intrusion or mold damage, that documentation supports the claim file.
