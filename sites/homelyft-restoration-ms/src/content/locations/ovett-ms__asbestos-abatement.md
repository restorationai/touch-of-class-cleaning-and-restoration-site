## Why Ovett Properties Face a Higher Asbestos Risk

Much of the residential building stock in and around Ovett was constructed between the 1940s and early 1980s — the exact window when asbestos use in construction materials peaked in the United States. Rural Jones County properties often weren't renovated on the same cycle as suburban homes closer to Hattiesburg or Laurel, which means original materials are frequently still in place. Popcorn ceiling texture applied before 1978, vinyl floor tiles laid over concrete slabs, pipe insulation in crawl spaces, and roofing felt on older outbuildings are all common sources.

The humid, subtropical climate here compounds the risk. Mississippi summers push moisture into crawl spaces and attics repeatedly, and that thermal cycling — hot days, cool nights, wet seasons — degrades older insulation materials over time. Friable asbestos (the kind that crumbles and releases fibers into the air) becomes more likely when materials have been subjected to years of moisture intrusion and temperature swings. A home that's been sitting vacant through a few Jones County summers may have asbestos-containing materials in worse shape than you'd expect from the outside.

## Our Asbestos Abatement Process in Ovett

Every project starts with a thorough asbestos inspection before any material is disturbed. Suspect materials are sampled and sent to an accredited laboratory — results typically come back within a few business days. If asbestos is confirmed, we build a containment plan specific to the structure and the scope of the affected area.

For a typical Ovett property, that might mean negative-air containment in a crawl space where pipe insulation is deteriorating, or full-room plastic sheeting and HEPA-filtered air scrubbers for a kitchen where old floor tiles are being lifted. Our team follows Mississippi Department of Environmental Quality (MDEQ) regulations and EPA guidelines throughout removal, bagging materials in labeled, sealed containers for transport to an approved disposal facility. Once abatement is complete, air clearance testing confirms fiber levels are within acceptable limits before containment is dismantled.

Because we hold EPA Lead-Safe Certification and are an IICRC Certified Firm, the documentation we produce — chain-of-custody records, lab reports, clearance results — is the kind that holds up whether you're refinancing, selling, or simply keeping records for your own peace of mind.

## Local Note

One pattern we've noticed on older Jones County properties — particularly the wood-frame farmhouses common throughout the Ovett area — is that asbestos-containing materials were sometimes layered during renovations rather than replaced. A 1970s kitchen remodel might have added new vinyl flooring directly over original 9×9 asbestos floor tiles from the 1950s. When a homeowner pulls up the top layer expecting one problem, they find two. It's worth having any layered flooring in a pre-1980 home tested before demo begins, not after, because disturbing the upper layer can release fibers from the one beneath it.
