## Why Poplarville Properties Are Prone to Sewage Backup

Pearl River County sits atop a mix of sandy loam and clay soils that can shift seasonally, especially after the heavy rainfall events that roll through the region from spring through fall. That ground movement puts stress on aging sewer laterals and septic drain fields alike. A significant share of Poplarville's residential housing stock was built in the 1950s through 1970s, when cast-iron and clay-tile pipes were standard — materials that corrode, crack, and root-intrude over decades. When those pipes fail, the backup doesn't just affect a single drain; it can push raw sewage up through floor drains, bathtubs, and washing machine standpipes simultaneously.

Poplarville also has a higher-than-average proportion of homes on private septic systems compared to more urbanized Mississippi cities. Heavy rain events can saturate drain fields, forcing sewage back toward the structure before the ground can absorb the excess. That combination — older pipe materials, clay-heavy soil zones, and widespread septic reliance — makes sewage backup a recurring issue here rather than a rare one.

## Our Sewage Cleanup and Sanitization Process in Poplarville

The first step on any sewage job is containment. Before any extraction begins, the affected area is isolated to prevent cross-contamination from Category 3 (black water) to clean areas of the home. Personal protective equipment goes on, the source is identified and stopped if possible, and standing waste is extracted using truck-mounted vacuum systems capable of handling solid-laden water.

Once the bulk waste is removed, affected porous materials — drywall, insulation, carpet, and in many Poplarville homes, tongue-and-groove pine subfloor — are assessed. Material that has absorbed sewage cannot be dried and retained; it has to come out. After demolition of unsalvageable material, all surfaces are treated with EPA-registered disinfectants and allowed appropriate dwell time. Structural drying then begins using commercial dehumidifiers and air movers, calibrated to the elevated ambient humidity typical of Pearl River County summers. Moisture readings are logged throughout the drying phase per the IICRC S500 standard, and clearance is confirmed before any reconstruction begins.
