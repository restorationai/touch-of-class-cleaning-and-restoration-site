## Why Agricola Properties See Water Damage Issues

George County's geology plays a quiet but persistent role in how water behaves around homes. The sandy-loam soils common to this part of Mississippi drain reasonably well after light rain, but during heavy Gulf moisture events the ground saturates quickly, and hydrostatic pressure against crawl space walls and pier-and-beam foundations rises fast. Many homes in the Agricola area were built in the mid-twentieth century on pier-and-beam foundations — a practical choice for the terrain, but one that leaves floor systems exposed to ground moisture year-round. When a plumbing failure or roof leak adds standing water to an already-humid crawl space, wood framing and subfloor sheathing can begin absorbing moisture within hours.

The region's proximity to the Gulf also means tropical systems and named storms are a real part of the calendar, not a once-a-decade event. Wind-driven rain enters through compromised roof decking, soffit gaps, and window seals in ways that don't always produce obvious ceiling stains right away — the water migrates inside wall cavities and sits there, invisible, until a musty odor or soft drywall gives it away.

## Our Water Damage Restoration Process in Agricola

Every job starts with a moisture mapping inspection. Technicians use thermal imaging cameras and calibrated moisture meters to locate water that has migrated beyond the visible wet area — inside wall assemblies, under flooring, and in subfloor cavities. This step matters especially in older Agricola homes where tongue-and-groove pine subflooring is common; that material holds moisture differently than OSB and requires adjusted drying targets.

Once the extent of the damage is documented, water extraction begins using truck-mounted and portable extraction units. Standing water is removed first, followed by surface moisture from carpet, padding, and hard flooring. Structural drying equipment — commercial-grade desiccant and refrigerant dehumidifiers, axial and centrifugal air movers — is then positioned to create a controlled drying environment. Drying is monitored daily with moisture readings logged against the IICRC S500 standard until affected materials reach acceptable equilibrium moisture content. Nothing is closed up until the numbers confirm it.

Where building materials contain lead paint — a realistic consideration in homes built before 1978, which represent a meaningful share of the housing stock in rural George County — our EPA Lead-Safe Certified protocols govern how we handle demolition and debris removal.
