## Why State Line Properties See Asbestos Risks

Greene County's housing stock skews older. Many of the single-family homes and small commercial structures in and around State Line were built during the 1950s through 1970s — the exact window when asbestos use in building materials peaked across the American South. Common culprits in homes of that era include vinyl floor tiles (especially 9×9-inch squares, a near-certain indicator), joint compound, attic insulation, duct wrap on HVAC systems, and popcorn or "cottage cheese" ceilings that were sprayed on as a low-cost finish.

The regional climate adds another layer of concern. The high humidity and heat cycles that define summers in this corner of Mississippi accelerate the deterioration of older insulation and ceiling materials. When asbestos-containing materials become friable — meaning they crumble easily — fibers become airborne far more readily. A ceiling tile that was stable for 40 years can shift to a hazardous condition after a roof leak, a plumbing failure, or even repeated cycles of expansion and contraction from seasonal temperature swings.

## Our Asbestos Abatement Process in State Line

Every project begins with a professional asbestos inspection before any material is disturbed. A certified inspector collects bulk samples from suspect materials — floor tiles, ceiling texture, pipe wrap, drywall compound — and sends them to an accredited laboratory. Results typically return within a few business days, and we walk you through what the findings mean in plain language before any abatement work is scheduled.

Once a positive identification is confirmed, the abatement process follows a strict protocol:

- **Containment**: The work area is sealed with heavy-gauge polyethylene sheeting and negative air pressure is established using HEPA-filtered air scrubbers to prevent fiber migration into unaffected spaces.
- **Wet removal**: Asbestos-containing materials are wetted before removal to suppress fiber release — a step required under EPA NESHAP guidelines and Mississippi DEQ rules.
- **Waste handling**: All removed material is double-bagged in labeled, 6-mil poly bags, sealed, and transported to a licensed disposal facility. No asbestos waste is left on-site.
- **Air clearance**: Post-abatement air sampling is conducted to confirm fiber levels are below clearance thresholds before containment is removed and the space is returned to normal use.

Because HomeLyft Restoration MS holds EPA Lead-Safe Certification and operates under OSHA-trained protocols, the same careful containment practices that apply to lead work are applied here — relevant in State Line homes where lead paint and asbestos often coexist in structures of the same vintage.

## State Line Insurance Coordination

Asbestos abatement is sometimes covered under homeowner's insurance policies when the presence of asbestos is tied to a covered loss — a roof failure that soaks and damages asbestos ceiling tiles, for example, or a plumbing leak that disturbs pipe insulation. Coverage is less common for routine pre-renovation abatement, but it's always worth filing a claim and letting the adjuster make the determination. We document affected materials thoroughly with photographs and written scope, which gives your carrier what it needs to evaluate the claim accurately.

## Local Note

Contractors working in older homes along the Greene County side of the State Line area have noted that 9×9-inch vinyl floor tiles — often found under carpet or a second layer of flooring installed in the 1980s — are among the most frequently encountered asbestos-containing materials in this region. Homeowners planning to pull up carpet before a sale or renovation sometimes discover these tiles underneath and assume they can be removed as normal flooring waste. They cannot. Even tiles that appear intact should be tested before disturbance; the adhesive mastic beneath them often contains asbestos as well, and scraping it dry releases fibers. If you lift a corner of carpet and see a dark, 9-inch square tile pattern underneath, stop and call before going further.
