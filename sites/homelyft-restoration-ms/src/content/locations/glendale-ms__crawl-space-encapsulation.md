## Why Glendale Properties See Crawl Space Moisture Problems

Glendale sits in a stretch of north Mississippi where the combination of high annual rainfall, warm temperatures, and expansive clay soil creates near-constant crawl space stress. Unlike sandy coastal soils that drain quickly, the tighter clay profiles here hold standing water after storms and wick ground moisture upward long after the rain stops. Vented crawl spaces — still the default in most homes built before the 2000s in this area — pull in warm, humid outdoor air during summer, which then condenses on cooler joists and insulation. That condensation cycle, repeated across hundreds of humid days per year, is exactly what mold needs to establish itself. IICRC AMRT standards classify this as a Class 3 moisture condition, and it's common enough in Glendale that it's not a matter of *if* encapsulation is needed but *when*.

## Our Crawl Space Encapsulation Process in Glendale

Every encapsulation job starts with a thorough crawl space inspection — not a quick visual from the access hatch, but a full under-floor assessment with moisture meters and thermal imaging. In Glendale homes, we pay particular attention to the perimeter walls where clay soil presses against block or poured concrete, and to the band joists at the foundation rim where air infiltration is heaviest.

Once we understand what we're dealing with, the process moves in a defined sequence:

1. **Debris removal and prep** — existing damaged insulation, old vapor barriers, and debris come out first. Mold-affected wood is treated per IICRC AMRT protocols before any sealing begins.
2. **Drainage and sump installation** (where needed) — if standing water is present or the grade drains toward the foundation, we address that before sealing anything in.
3. **Heavy-duty liner installation** — we install a reinforced polyethylene barrier (typically 20-mil) across the entire crawl space floor and up the foundation walls, sealed at all seams and penetrations with moisture-resistant tape and mastic.
4. **Conditioned air or dehumidification** — sealing the crawl space changes its air dynamics. We size and install a crawl space dehumidifier or connect the space to the home's conditioned air supply so humidity stays controlled year-round.
5. **Final moisture readings and documentation** — before we close out, we record baseline humidity and moisture readings so you have a benchmark for future reference.

The entire process on a typical Glendale single-story home runs two to three days depending on crawl space depth and pre-existing conditions.

## Equipment and Methods We Use for Crawl Space Encapsulation

The liner thickness matters in this climate. Thinner 6-mil sheeting sold at hardware stores is not rated for the ground contact pressures and foot traffic that a properly maintained crawl space sees. We use reinforced 20-mil liners with a cord-grid interior that resists puncture from rocks, roots, and HVAC service visits. Seams are overlapped a minimum of 12 inches and double-sealed. Foundation wall coverage extends at least 6 inches above grade on the interior face, terminating at a treated ledger to prevent moisture from wicking behind the liner.

For humidity control, we size dehumidifiers to the cubic footage of the sealed space — not to the square footage of the house above it. An undersized unit running continuously will fail early and leave the space at 65–70% relative humidity, which is still within mold's comfort zone. We target a sustained 50–55% RH in the sealed crawl space.

## Local Note

One pattern we see repeatedly in Glendale is homes where a previous owner installed fiberglass batt insulation between the floor joists as a DIY moisture fix. In this climate, that approach backfires: the batts absorb and hold ground moisture against the wood rather than blocking it, and they create a dark, humid habitat that accelerates both mold growth and wood rot. By the time a homeowner notices the problem, the insulation is often black with mold and the joists above it are soft. Removing that old insulation — and treating the wood before encapsulating — adds a day to the project but is non-negotiable for a durable result.
