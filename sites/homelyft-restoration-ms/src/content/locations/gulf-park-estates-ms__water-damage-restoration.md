## Why Gulf Park Estates Properties See Water Damage Issues

The geography here works against you in ways that aren't obvious until you're standing in a wet living room. Gulf Park Estates is a low-lying coastal community bordered by tidal water on multiple sides, and the sandy, poorly draining soil common to this stretch of the Mississippi Gulf Coast means groundwater sits close to the surface year-round. During heavy rain events — and Harrison County averages over 60 inches of rainfall annually — that water has nowhere to go quickly. Crawl spaces flood before the rain even stops. ## Our Water Damage Restoration Process in Gulf Park Estates

Every job starts with a thorough moisture assessment using thermal imaging cameras and calibrated moisture meters — not a visual walkthrough. In coastal homes, water migrates into wall cavities, under flooring, and into insulation in ways that aren't visible to the eye. We map the full extent of saturation before any equipment is placed.

From there, water removal comes first. Truck-mounted and portable extraction units pull standing water from floors, carpeting, and subfloor assemblies. In slab-on-grade homes common to Gulf Park Estates, we pay particular attention to the gap between the slab and hardwood or LVP flooring, where water pools and accelerates cupping and delamination.

Structural drying follows, calibrated to the ambient conditions here. Gulf Coast humidity means drying targets must account for high outdoor dew points — we use industrial desiccant and refrigerant dehumidifiers in combination, adjusting daily based on psychrometric readings rather than a fixed schedule. Drying logs are documented throughout, which matters for your insurance claim and for confirming the structure is genuinely dry before any reconstruction begins. Our team holds IICRC Water Restoration Technician (WRT) and Applied Structural Drying (ASD) certifications, and all work is performed to the IICRC S500 standard.
