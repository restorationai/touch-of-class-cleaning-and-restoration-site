## Why Agricola Properties Present Unique Post-Construction Cleaning Challenges

Stone County's climate sits at the intersection of heat, humidity, and coastal air movement. That combination accelerates how construction residues behave after a project closes out. Silica dust from cut concrete block, joint compound overspray, and insulation fibers don't dry out and blow away the way they might in an arid climate — they settle, absorb ambient moisture, and create a film that standard cleaning equipment smears rather than removes.

The region's building stock adds another layer. Agricola and the surrounding Stone County area include a meaningful share of older wood-frame homes alongside newer builds, and older structures often have uneven subfloor gaps, exposed rough-sawn framing in crawl spaces, and window casings that trap debris in ways that newer construction doesn't. A renovation on an older home here can push dust into wall cavities and beneath original hardwood in ways that take systematic, room-by-room extraction to address properly.

Contractors working in this part of Mississippi also frequently encounter homes with pier-and-beam foundations, where construction dust and debris fall through gaps and accumulate underneath — an area that gets skipped in a standard final clean but that can become a moisture-trapping problem once the house is occupied.

## Our Post-Construction Cleaning Process in Agricola

Every post-construction clean starts with a walk-through to assess what trades were on-site and what they left behind. Framing and drywall crews leave different residues than tile setters or painters, and the sequence of cleaning matters — working top to bottom, rough to fine, before any detail or glass work begins.

The process typically runs in three passes:

**Rough clean** — debris removal, construction material disposal, sweeping and vacuuming of all surfaces including inside cabinets, closets, and mechanical chases. HEPA-filtered vacuums capture fine particulate rather than recirculating it.

**Detail clean** — all surfaces wiped down with appropriate agents for the material: non-streaking solutions on new windows, pH-neutral cleaners on stone countertops, solvent-based removers for adhesive residue on tile or LVP. Fixtures, outlets, and hardware are cleaned individually.

**Final inspection clean** — the pass that prepares a space for photography, occupancy, or HOA inspection. Windows, mirrors, and stainless surfaces are polished; floors are cleaned in the direction of the grain or plank; all light fixtures are checked and wiped.

For renovation projects in older Agricola homes where lead-based paint may have been disturbed, our team holds EPA Lead-Safe certification — work is handled in compliance with RRP protocols, and we document the process.

## Equipment and Methods We Use for Post-Construction Cleaning

The tools that matter most in a post-construction environment are the ones most general cleaning services don't carry. Commercial HEPA air scrubbers run continuously during the detail clean phase, capturing airborne particulate down to 0.3 microns — the size range that includes drywall dust and insulation fiber. This matters especially in Stone County's humid conditions, where airborne dust can settle and re-adhere quickly if the air isn't being actively filtered.

For hard-surface floors — common in both new construction and renovated older homes in this area — we use low-moisture flat-mop systems rather than wet mops, which can raise grain on new hardwood or push debris into LVP seams. Specialty adhesive removers are matched to the substrate to avoid finish damage on new installations.

On commercial or multi-unit projects, we coordinate staging and access around active contractor schedules when punch-list work is still ongoing, so the clean doesn't have to be redone after a final trade visit.
