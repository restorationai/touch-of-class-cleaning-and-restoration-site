## Why Eastabuchie Properties See Asbestos Concerns

Jones County's building stock tells the story clearly. The decades between the 1940s and the late 1970s were a period of heavy construction across rural Mississippi — schools, churches, homes, and outbuildings — and asbestos-containing materials (ACMs) were cheap, fire-resistant, and widely available. In Eastabuchie and the surrounding unincorporated areas of Jones County, that era left behind a specific pattern: single-story wood-frame homes with asbestos-backed vinyl floor tiles, vermiculite insulation in attics, and corrugated asbestos cement on outbuilding roofs.

The region's climate compounds the risk. Hot, wet summers followed by temperature swings in winter cause building materials to expand and contract repeatedly. Over decades, that cycling can crack and friabilize asbestos-containing materials that were once stable and sealed — turning a low-risk encapsulated product into an airborne hazard. A roof repair, a bathroom remodel, or even aggressive pressure washing can disturb those materials without the homeowner ever realizing it.

## Our Asbestos Abatement Process in Eastabuchie

Every project begins with sampling and laboratory confirmation — we do not assume, and we do not skip this step. Suspect materials are collected following established protocols, sent to an accredited lab, and results are reviewed before any physical work begins. If ACMs are confirmed, the scope is defined: encapsulation where materials are intact and accessible, full removal where they are damaged, friable, or in the path of planned renovation.

Containment is established using negative air pressure with HEPA filtration units, polyethylene sheeting, and regulated entry/exit decontamination chambers. Workers wear full personal protective equipment throughout. Removed materials are wetted to suppress fiber release, double-bagged in labeled, sealed poly bags, and transported to a licensed disposal facility that accepts regulated asbestos waste — Mississippi DEQ requirements govern the disposal chain, and our documentation follows that chain from your property to the final disposal site.

Air clearance testing after abatement confirms that fiber counts have returned to background levels before containment is removed. You receive a written clearance report — useful for real estate transactions, insurance documentation, and permit closeouts.
