## Why Gautier Homes See More Upholstery Wear

Gautier sits along the Pascagoula River and the Mississippi Sound, which means relative humidity stays high most of the year, not just during summer storm season. Fabric upholstery in this climate absorbs ambient moisture even when nothing has been spilled, and that moisture gives mold and mildew spores a head start, particularly in family rooms with window units or homes that go weeks between AC filter changes. Leather furniture has its own problem here: salt-laden air and UV exposure through south-facing windows dry out natural oils faster than in drier inland climates, which is why leather pieces in coastal Jackson County homes often crack or stiffen years before they would elsewhere. Add in the number of Gautier households that store outdoor or patio furniture indoors ahead of tropical storms, and upholstery ends up carrying salt residue and grit that regular vacuuming never fully lifts.

## Our Upholstery Cleaning Process

We start every job with a fiber and construction check, since fabric blends, natural fibers, and leather all need different chemistry and heat settings. For fabric sofas and sectionals, we pre-treat soiled areas, agitate with the appropriate brush attachment, then hot water extract so the cleaning solution and lifted soil come back out rather than sitting in the cushion foam. Given how much moisture Gulf Coast air already puts into upholstery, we're deliberate about extraction dwell time and drying: pulling out too little water on a humid Gautier afternoon can leave cushions damp for days and invite the mildew smell right back. Leather pieces get a conditioner-based clean instead of water extraction, since saturating leather in this climate accelerates the same drying and cracking problem we're trying to fix. We finish with fans or air movers positioned to speed drying, especially useful during the stickier months when indoor air alone won't dry a cushion overnight.
