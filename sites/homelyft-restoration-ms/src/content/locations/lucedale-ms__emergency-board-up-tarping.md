## Our Emergency Board-Up and Tarping Process in Lucedale

When we receive a call from a Lucedale property owner, the first step is a rapid damage assessment — either by phone with photos or on-site — to determine whether the priority is roof tarping, window and door board-up, or both. That distinction matters because the sequencing affects how well the structure stays protected if another rain event moves through before permanent repairs begin.

For roof tarping, we use heavy-duty polyethylene tarps rated for wind exposure, secured with weighted battens and screwed-down lumber runs rather than simple rope ties, which pull free in the kind of gusts George County sees during afternoon thunderstorm season. Tarps are lapped over the ridge when possible so water sheds away from both sides rather than pooling at the peak.

For window and door board-up, plywood panels are cut to fit the rough opening, and fasteners go into structural framing — not just the window casing. On fire-damaged openings, we assess the surrounding framing for heat damage before anchoring, because charred or heat-weakened wood won't hold a fastener under wind load. Our team holds IICRC FSRT (Fire and Smoke Restoration Technician) certification, which means we're reading the structure, not just covering the hole.
