## Why Waveland Properties Face Particular Challenges After a Fire

Waveland's housing stock tells the story of a community that rebuilt after Katrina, which means you'll find two distinct eras of construction side by side: post-2005 elevated homes built to updated FEMA flood and wind codes, and older slab-on-grade structures that survived or were repaired after the storm. Both present specific complications after a fire.

Older rebuilt homes often used materials sourced quickly during the post-Katrina construction surge — some of that lumber and drywall absorbed moisture during construction and never fully dried, meaning smoke and heat can penetrate deeper into wall assemblies than you'd expect. Elevated post-Katrina homes, meanwhile, have large crawl-space volumes beneath the living area where smoke and soot can settle and linger, making odor elimination a multi-step process rather than a single treatment.

The Gulf Coast's high relative humidity — Waveland averages above 75% humidity for much of the year — accelerates the bonding of smoke residue to porous surfaces like drywall, insulation, and wood framing. Soot that might wipe off a wall in a drier climate becomes chemically set here within 48 to 72 hours. That timeline matters when you're deciding whether to call a restoration company or wait for the insurance adjuster.

## Our Fire Damage Restoration Process in Waveland

Every fire loss is different, but the sequence of work follows a disciplined order that prevents secondary damage from compounding the original loss.

**Structural assessment and board-up.** Before any cleaning begins, the structure is evaluated for safety. Compromised windows, doors, and roof sections are secured to prevent weather intrusion — critical on the Gulf Coast, where an afternoon thunderstorm can turn a manageable fire loss into a combined fire-and-water loss overnight.

**Soot and smoke mapping.** Smoke travels through HVAC systems, wall penetrations, and attic spaces far beyond the burn area. Technicians trace the full migration path before any surface cleaning begins, so no hidden soot pocket is left to re-contaminate the space after restoration.

**Dry and wet cleaning.** Different residues require different chemistry. Dry sponge methods lift loose soot from ceilings and walls without smearing. Wet chemical cleaning follows for set residues, using products calibrated to the surface material — important in Waveland homes where you may encounter original cypress wood trim, a material common in older Gulf Coast construction that responds differently than pine or MDF.

**Odor elimination.** Thermal fogging and hydroxyl generation are used in sequence to neutralize smoke odor molecules rather than masking them. In Waveland's humid environment, a single treatment is rarely sufficient — odor protocols are extended until air quality readings confirm the space is clear.

**Structural drying and reconstruction.** Fire suppression water from sprinklers or hose lines creates a secondary moisture problem. Structural drying runs concurrently with restoration, using calibrated drying systems monitored daily against psychrometric targets.
