## Why Rawls Springs Properties See Sewage Backup Issues

Rawls Springs sits in a transitional zone where municipal sewer service gives way to private septic systems on larger residential lots. That mix matters: homes on septic are vulnerable to drain-field saturation during the extended rain events that roll through Forrest County in spring and late summer. When the ground is already at capacity, a single heavy rain can push effluent back through floor drains, toilets, and tub drains simultaneously. Homes on the municipal system aren't immune either — aging lateral lines, root intrusion from the area's abundant pine and hardwood trees, and occasional lift-station failures can send raw sewage back into finished living spaces with almost no warning.

The pier-and-beam construction style still found on many Rawls Springs properties creates a particular challenge: sewage that reaches a crawl space can pool against the ground vapor barrier, saturate wood joists, and begin breaking down structural members before a homeowner even realizes the backup happened. By the time the smell is obvious, contamination may have spread well beyond the original point of entry.

## Our Sewage Cleanup and Sanitization Process in Rawls Springs

The work starts with containment, not extraction. Before anything is moved or pumped, the affected area is isolated to prevent cross-contamination to clean spaces — a step that matters especially in open-plan homes where a kitchen or utility room backup can be adjacent to living areas. From there, the process follows a defined sequence:

1. **Category 3 water removal** — truck-mounted extractors pull standing sewage and contaminated water from floors, crawl spaces, and any affected cavities.
2. **Contaminated material removal** — porous materials that cannot be fully sanitized (saturated drywall, insulation, carpet padding) are bagged and disposed of per Mississippi Department of Environmental Quality guidelines.
3. **Surface disinfection** — EPA-registered antimicrobials are applied to all affected hard surfaces, subfloor sheathing, and framing. Because sewage carries pathogens including bacteria and viruses, surface cleaning alone is not sufficient — the disinfection step is non-negotiable.
4. **Structural drying** — industrial dehumidifiers and air movers are positioned to dry framing and subfloor to documented moisture content targets, calibrated for Rawls Springs's humidity baseline, which runs higher than coastal averages in the summer months.
5. **Post-remediation verification** — moisture readings are logged before equipment is pulled, and a written scope of completed work is provided for insurance documentation.
