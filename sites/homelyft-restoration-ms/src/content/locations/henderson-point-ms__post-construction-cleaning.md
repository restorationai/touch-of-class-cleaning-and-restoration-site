Henderson Point sits at the narrow tip of a peninsula between the Bay of St. Louis and the open Gulf, and that geography shapes every construction project here — and every cleanup that follows. Salt-laden air accelerates oxidation on exposed hardware and window tracks, coastal humidity keeps construction dust airborne longer than it would inland, and the sandy, fine-grained soil that blows in off the shoreline works its way into every surface a crew leaves unprotected. When a renovation or new build wraps up in Henderson Point, the residue left behind is a different animal than what you'd find after a project in Jackson or Hattiesburg — and cleaning it properly requires knowing that going in.

## Why Henderson Point Properties Face Unique Post-Construction Challenges

The coastal Mississippi climate is the defining variable. Relative humidity along the Henderson Point peninsula regularly climbs above 80 percent during summer months, which means construction dust doesn't simply settle — it clumps, clings to surfaces, and bonds to fresh paint or newly installed cabinetry if it isn't removed quickly. Drywall compound dust, one of the finest and most pervasive byproducts of interior renovation, becomes almost paste-like in humid conditions, making it harder to vacuum and easier to smear if the wrong technique is used.

The building stock here adds another layer. Many homes along the peninsula were built in the decades following Hurricane Camille and then again after Katrina, meaning you'll find a mix of elevated slab construction, pier-and-beam foundations, and newer FORTIFIED-standard builds — each with different surface types, ventilation configurations, and crawl-space conditions that collect debris differently. Elevated homes with open underbellies trap sawdust and insulation fragments in ways that ground-level slabs don't, and those areas need deliberate attention during a final clean.

## Our Post-Construction and Specialty Cleaning Process in Henderson Point

A thorough post-construction cleanup moves through the property in a deliberate sequence — rough clean first, then detail clean, then final polish — because working out of order means redistributing dust you've already captured.

**Rough clean** begins at the highest points: ceiling fans, light fixtures, tops of cabinets, and HVAC vents. Construction debris — scrap lumber, packaging, adhesive tubes, wire offcuts — is removed before any surface cleaning starts. In coastal properties, we pay particular attention to window tracks and sliding door channels, where fine sand and sawdust pack together and can score new glass if dragged across it during cleaning.

**Detail clean** addresses every horizontal and vertical surface with HEPA-filtered vacuums before any wet wiping begins. This step matters especially in Henderson Point homes where the humidity has already started bonding dust to surfaces. We wipe down walls, baseboards, door frames, and interior window sills with appropriate solutions matched to the finish — a matte painted wall needs a different touch than a semi-gloss or a natural wood surface.

**Final clean and inspection** covers floors last: tile grout lines cleared of grout haze, hardwood surfaces inspected for adhesive overspray, and LVP or laminate checked for installation debris trapped in the click joints. We do a walk-through against the contractor's punch list before signing off.

For specialty cleaning requests — appliance deep cleans after installation, post-renovation HVAC interior wipe-downs, or pressure washing of exterior concrete and pavers — those are scoped and scheduled alongside the interior work so the property is move-in ready in a single mobilization.

## Local Note

One thing that catches out-of-area cleaning crews working Henderson Point for the first time: the fine quartz sand that blows off the beach and Gulf-side lots is abrasive enough to scratch unprotected hardwood and LVP floors if it's swept dry rather than vacuumed. We've seen brand-new flooring installation marred during the final clean because a crew used a push broom instead of a HEPA vacuum on the first pass. On peninsula properties, we always vacuum before any mop or dry cloth touches a finished floor — it's a small protocol change that protects a significant investment.

## Equipment and Methods We Use for Post-Construction Cleaning

HEPA-rated vacuums with fine-dust bags are non-negotiable for drywall dust — standard shop vacs recirculate particles smaller than 1 micron back into the air, which defeats the purpose of cleaning and creates an air quality problem in a freshly finished space. We use commercial-grade HEPA units alongside microfiber flat mops, extension dusters for high ceilings common in newer elevated coastal builds, and pH-neutral cleaners that won't dull new grout sealers or strip factory finishes from cabinetry. For exterior work — concrete washing, deck cleaning, window exterior wipe-down — we bring pressure washing equipment calibrated for the surface type, since the salt air deposits a film on exterior glass and masonry that standard rinsing won't remove.
