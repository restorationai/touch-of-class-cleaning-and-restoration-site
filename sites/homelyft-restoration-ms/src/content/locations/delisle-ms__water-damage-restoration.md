## Why DeLisle Properties See Water Damage Issues

Harrison County's combination of high annual rainfall, a shallow water table, and clay-heavy soil creates conditions that work against homeowners year-round. During heavy rain events — which the Mississippi Gulf Coast sees repeatedly from late spring through hurricane season — the ground becomes so saturated that water migrates through foundation cracks and slab seams rather than percolating away. DeLisle's largely rural and semi-rural character means many properties rely on older plumbing infrastructure, some of it installed decades ago when polybutylene pipe was still common. That material is prone to sudden failure, and when it goes, it can release hundreds of gallons before the main shutoff is located.

Homes built on pier-and-beam foundations, which appear throughout this part of Harrison County, present a particular challenge: water pools in the crawl space below the subfloor, where it's invisible from inside the house but actively wicking upward into floor joists and insulation. Relative humidity in this region routinely runs above 70 percent even on dry days, which means wet structural materials don't air-dry on their own — they stay wet long enough for mold colonies to begin forming within 24 to 48 hours of initial saturation.

## Our Water Damage Restoration Process in DeLisle

The first step on any job is a thorough moisture assessment using thermal imaging cameras and calibrated moisture meters. This tells us where water has traveled beyond the visible wet area — under tile, inside wall cavities, beneath baseboards — so extraction targets the actual extent of the loss, not just what's visible. Standing water is removed with truck-mounted extraction units capable of pulling large volumes quickly, which matters when flooring adhesives or subfloor panels are at risk of delaminating.

Once bulk water is out, the drying phase begins. Because DeLisle's ambient humidity is so high, standard air movement alone is rarely sufficient. The team deploys industrial-grade desiccant or refrigerant dehumidifiers sized to the affected square footage, paired with high-velocity air movers positioned to create directed airflow across wet surfaces. Drying is monitored daily with moisture readings logged against target values established by the IICRC S500 standard, so there's a documented record of progress — not a guess about when materials are dry.

For homes with pier-and-beam construction, crawl space drying requires its own equipment setup: ground-contact vapor barriers, dedicated dehumidification, and sometimes temporary encapsulation to prevent ambient outdoor humidity from re-wetting materials that are already drying. The process is slower than above-grade drying, but skipping it leads to joist rot and persistent odor problems months later.
