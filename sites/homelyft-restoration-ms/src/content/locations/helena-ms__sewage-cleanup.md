## Why Helena Properties See Sewage Backup Issues

Phillips County's low-lying terrain along the Mississippi Delta means storm events and river-stage fluctuations can overwhelm municipal sewer capacity faster than in higher-elevation parts of the state. When the main line backs up, the path of least resistance is often a floor drain, a basement toilet, or a laundry standpipe — and the material that comes up is Category 3 water, the most hazardous classification in the restoration industry.

Helena's housing stock adds another layer of complexity. Many homes in the city were built before modern PVC sewer lines became standard, meaning cast-iron or clay-tile laterals that have had decades to crack, root-intrude, or offset at the joints. A single tree root working into a clay-tile joint can cause a slow blockage that goes unnoticed until a heavy rain event tips the system over. Septic overflow cleanup is also common on properties at the city's edges where municipal sewer service gives way to private systems — and those tanks can fail quietly until the drain field is fully saturated.

## Our Sewage Cleanup and Sanitization Process in Helena

Sewage cleanup is not a mop-and-bleach job. Category 3 contamination carries bacteria, viruses, and parasites that survive on porous surfaces long after visible waste is removed. Our process follows the IICRC S500 standard and is calibrated to the conditions we actually find in Helena homes.

**Step 1 — Containment and safety:** We establish containment barriers before extraction begins to prevent cross-contamination to unaffected rooms. Technicians work in full PPE throughout.

**Step 2 — Extraction and removal:** Truck-mounted extraction units pull standing sewage and contaminated water from flooring, subfloor cavities, and crawl spaces. Saturated materials — drywall, insulation, flooring — that cannot be adequately sanitized are removed and bagged per Mississippi Department of Environmental Quality disposal guidelines.

**Step 3 — EPA-registered disinfection:** After extraction, all affected surfaces are treated with hospital-grade, EPA-registered antimicrobial agents. We pay particular attention to subfloor framing and concrete block foundations common in older Helena construction, where sewage can wick into porous material quickly.

**Step 4 — Structural drying:** Moisture meters and thermal imaging confirm residual moisture levels before drying equipment is placed. We use industrial air movers and desiccant or refrigerant dehumidifiers sized to the affected area — not consumer-grade units.

**Step 5 — Documentation:** Every step is photo-documented for your insurance carrier. We provide a detailed scope of work that most major carriers recognize for direct billing purposes.
