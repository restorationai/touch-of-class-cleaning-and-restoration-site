## Why Long Beach Carpets Take a Harder Hit Than Inland Homes

The Harrison County coastline gets an average of more than 60 inches of rain per year, and Long Beach absorbs its share. That moisture doesn't just stay outside. It rides in on shoes, seeps under doors, and raises the indoor relative humidity in ways that accelerate mold spore activity in carpet backing and padding. Salt particulate from Gulf breezes also settles into pile fibers, acting like fine sandpaper that breaks down carpet strands over time and makes surface soiling cling more stubbornly.

Homes closer to the waterfront tend to have older slab-on-grade construction, which means there's no crawl space buffer between the concrete and your carpet — ground moisture migrates upward, and carpet padding absorbs it silently. That's a very different profile from a home in a drier inland county, and it's why the cleaning process here has to account for what's happening beneath the surface, not just what's visible on top.

## Our Carpet Cleaning Process in Long Beach

Every job starts with a walk-through to assess fiber type, soiling level, and — critically — moisture conditions in the subfloor area. In Long Beach's coastal environment, we check ambient humidity before we introduce any additional moisture into the space. Hot water extraction (truck-mounted, not portable) is our primary method: it drives cleaning solution deep into the pile and pulls it back out along with loosened soil, allergens, and microbial material.
