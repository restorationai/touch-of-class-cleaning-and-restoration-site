## What Renovations, Remodels and General Contracting actually involves

You had a pipe burst in the kitchen, or a tree came through the roof, or you've simply lived with a bathroom that hasn't been touched since 1987 — and now you're staring at a space that needs more than a coat of paint. Post-damage rebuilds and planned remodels share the same core challenge: coordinating trades, materials, permits, and timelines so that the finished space actually matches what was scoped. That's where a licensed general contractor earns its keep. HomeLyft Restoration MS holds Mississippi General Contractor License #RO 2728 and handles both insurance-driven reconstruction and elective renovation under one roof, which means your kitchen remodel and your post-flood rebuild go through the same accountable chain of command.

The work spans everything from demolition and framing to finish carpentry, tile, cabinetry, plumbing rough-ins, and final punch-list walkthroughs. A bathroom remodel might run four to six weeks from demo to grout cure. A full post-damage rebuild of a water-damaged first floor can stretch eight to fourteen weeks depending on structural scope, permit timelines, and material lead times. Gulfport's coastal humidity also affects finish scheduling — paint, caulk, and grout all cure differently in high-dew-point conditions, and experienced crews account for that rather than rushing a finish that will bubble or crack by the following summer.

## Our process

1. **Scope of work and pre-construction assessment.** Before a single wall comes down, we walk the space with you and document existing conditions — structural, mechanical, and cosmetic. For post-damage projects, this step integrates with the insurance adjuster's estimate so nothing is missed or double-billed. For elective remodels, this is where selections (tile, fixtures, cabinetry) are locked in before demolition begins, preventing the costly change orders that derail most residential projects.

2. **Permitting and trade coordination.** We pull the required permits through the City of Gulfport or the relevant municipality, schedule inspections, and coordinate licensed subcontractors — electricians, plumbers, HVAC — so trades aren't waiting on each other. Permit timelines in Harrison County typically run one to three weeks for residential work; we build that window into the project schedule upfront.

3. **Demolition and structural work.** Selective demo removes only what needs to go. Because our team is EPA Lead-Safe Certified, pre-1978 homes in older Gulfport neighborhoods — think the built stock along Hardy Avenue or in the North Gulfport area — get proper lead-paint containment and disposal before any cutting or sanding begins. Framing, subfloor repair, and sheathing happen in this phase, with inspections signed off before walls close.

4. **Mechanical rough-ins and inspections.** Electrical, plumbing, and HVAC rough-ins are completed and inspected before insulation or drywall goes up. Skipping or rushing inspections at this stage is the single most common reason a remodel fails a final inspection — or worse, creates a latent defect that surfaces years later.

5. **Finish work and final walkthrough.** Drywall, paint, flooring, tile, cabinetry, fixtures, and trim are installed in sequence. We conduct a formal punch-list walkthrough with you before we consider the project closed. For post-damage insurance claims, we provide a final completion package — photos, receipts, and signed documentation — that satisfies carrier requirements for claim closure.

## What separates a good renovation response from a bad one

The most common failure point in home renovation services isn't craftsmanship — it's scope creep and hidden conditions. Contractors who skip a thorough pre-construction assessment routinely discover mid-project that the subfloor is rotted, the electrical panel is undersized, or the existing plumbing doesn't meet current code. Each surprise becomes a change order, and change orders inflate both cost and timeline.

For post-damage rebuilds specifically, insurance adjusters look for line-item documentation that matches the actual scope of damage. Vague estimates get challenged or underpaid. A general contractor who has worked the restoration side understands how to write a Xactimate-compatible scope that holds up under adjuster review — and knows which line items are routinely overlooked, like code-upgrade allowances, temporary housing costs, and debris disposal.

In coastal Mississippi, wind and moisture resistance matter at every finish decision. HomeLyft Restoration MS is FORTIFIED Building / Roofing / Windstorm Certified, which means roofing and exterior work we perform can qualify for the Alabama and Mississippi FORTIFIED designation — a meaningful factor for homeowners seeking insurance premium reductions under Mississippi's FORTIFIED Home incentive program.

## Seasonal and regional considerations

Gulfport's Gulf Coast climate creates a narrow window for exterior work that involves moisture-sensitive materials. Late spring through early fall brings sustained humidity above 70% and afternoon thunderstorms that can delay roofing, siding, and exterior paint. Planning exterior phases for October through March reduces weather-related delays. Interior remodels run year-round, but high-humidity summers require dehumidification during drywall finishing to prevent tape failure and paint adhesion problems — a detail that separates crews familiar with coastal Mississippi from those who aren't.

Hurricane season (June through November) also affects material lead times and subcontractor availability across the Gulf Coast. Projects scoped before June tend to secure better trade scheduling and avoid the post-storm surge in demand that follows any named storm making landfall near Harrison County.
