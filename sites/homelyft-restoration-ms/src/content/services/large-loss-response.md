**Large loss event at a commercial property or multi-unit building in Gulfport?** When a fire, flood, or storm surge affects an entire structure or multiple buildings at once, the response has to scale immediately, not grow one phone call at a time. We answer 24/7 and mobilize crews, equipment, and large loss adjusters' documentation from the first hour on site.

A large loss isn't a bigger version of a residential water call. It's a different operational category: multiple affected floors or units, structural systems (HVAC, electrical, elevators) that have to be assessed before drying even starts, and often a general contractor, property manager, and multiple insurance stakeholders all needing the same information at the same time. Catastrophic loss response covers hurricane storm surge, major fire loss in commercial or multifamily buildings, large-scale pipe failures in high-rise or garden-style apartment complexes, and any event where the damage footprint exceeds what a standard 2 to 4 person crew can safely contain.

## What large loss and catastrophic response actually involves

Large loss work means running parallel drying zones instead of one, often with dozens of air movers and multiple commercial dehumidifiers tracked against a single moisture map so nothing gets missed between units. It means coordinating with building engineers on power and water shutoffs, staging contents pack-out for entire floors, and keeping a documentation trail detailed enough to support a claim that may run into six or seven figures. On the Mississippi Gulf Coast, that often means commercial slab-on-grade construction, garden apartments with shared attic space that lets water or smoke travel between units, and buildings close enough to the water that storm surge and wind damage hit the same structure simultaneously.

## Our process

1. **Initial scoping and stakeholder call.** Before equipment moves, we get the property manager, ownership group, and any assigned large loss adjuster on the same page about scope, access, and safety hazards like compromised electrical or structural elements.
2. **Zone mapping and crew mobilization.** The affected area is divided into drying or remediation zones so multiple crews can work simultaneously without duplicating effort or missing a section.
3. **Structural and moisture assessment across the full footprint.** Moisture mapping, thermal imaging, and air quality checks run building-wide, not room by room, so the scope of work reflects the actual loss.
4. **Mitigation and drying at scale.** Extraction, containment, and structural drying run per IICRC S500 standards, with daily moisture logs across every affected unit or floor, not just a sample.
5. **Documentation and handoff to reconstruction.** Photo and moisture-log documentation is compiled for the adjuster and ownership before any rebuild work begins, so the paper trail matches what actually happened on site.

## What separates a good large loss response from a bad one

The most common failure in catastrophic response is undersizing the initial crew and equipment, which lets drying fall behind the clock and lets secondary damage (mold, delamination, corrosion) start in units that were only marginally wet. Large loss adjusters are looking for consistent daily documentation across the whole site, not a single inspection report. They also watch for scope creep that isn't backed by moisture readings or photos, since that's what slows claim approval. A properly run large loss job keeps a single point of contact coordinating between crews, the adjuster, and the property manager so decisions don't get made in three different places at once, and keeps detailed logs on every affected unit so partial-loss and total-loss determinations hold up under review.

## Seasonal & regional considerations

Hurricane season brings the Gulf Coast's highest concentration of catastrophic claims, with storm surge, wind-driven rain, and roof failures often hitting the same building at once. Gulfport's proximity to the water and the coast's older multifamily and commercial stock, much of it built before current FORTIFIED and windstorm codes, means a single storm event can generate multiple large loss claims in the same complex or business park. Humidity levels along the coast also mean mold growth can start within 24 to 48 hours in large wet structures that aren't dried fast enough, which is part of why crew scale matters more here than in drier climates.
