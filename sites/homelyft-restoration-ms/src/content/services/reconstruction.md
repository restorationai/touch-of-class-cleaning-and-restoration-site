**Reconstruction follows a specific finding: water, fire, or storm damage has already been mitigated, and now someone has to put the structure back together.** That's a different skill set than extraction or cleanup. It means reading engineering reports, pulling permits, matching existing finishes, and sequencing framing, electrical, and drywall so a Gulfport home or commercial space passes final inspection and looks like nothing happened. Homeowners searching for reconstruction services in Gulfport are usually past the emergency phase and need a contractor who can carry the project from demolition to move-in without losing momentum between trades.

## What reconstruction services actually involves

Post-damage reconstruction covers everything required to rebuild a structure after water, fire, or storm damage has been remediated: framing repairs, subfloor and flooring replacement, drywall and insulation, electrical and plumbing rough-in where walls were opened, roofing and exterior envelope work, cabinetry, trim, and paint. On a coastal property this often includes matching older lath-and-plaster or board sheathing common in Gulfport's pre-1970s housing stock, or coordinating FORTIFIED roofing upgrades on homes rebuilt after storm damage. Timelines range from a few weeks for a single-room rebuild to several months for whole-structure reconstruction after a fire or major flood, depending on permitting, material lead times, and the scope the insurance carrier approves.

## Our process

1. **Scope of work.** We document the extent of damage, take moisture and structural readings where relevant, and write a detailed scope that breaks out materials, labor, and code-required upgrades.
2. **Permits and engineering.** Reconstruction that touches structural framing, electrical, or load-bearing walls requires permits through the local building department; we pull those and coordinate any engineering review before work starts.
3. **Framing and rough-in.** Damaged studs, joists, and sheathing are replaced to current code, and electrical, plumbing, or HVAC rough-in is restored before walls close back up.
4. **Insulation, drywall, and finishing.** Once rough-in passes inspection, we insulate, hang and finish drywall, and move into flooring, trim, cabinetry, and paint to match the original finish level or an agreed upgrade.
5. **Final walkthrough.** We walk the completed space against the written scope and the adjuster's estimate before calling the job closed.

## What separates a good reconstruction response from a bad one

The most common failure point is sequencing: closing up walls before an inspector signs off on rough-in, or finishing flooring before the subfloor has had time to fully dry and stabilize. That leads to callbacks, cracked tile, or failed inspections months later. A second common gap is documentation. Adjusters want photos of the damage before demolition, itemized material and labor costs that match the approved scope, and change orders in writing when hidden damage is discovered once walls are opened, which happens often in older Gulfport homes with outdated wiring or undersized framing. A good reconstruction contractor flags those surprises immediately instead of absorbing them silently or stalling the job while waiting on a supplemental approval.

## Seasonal & regional considerations

Gulfport's hurricane season runs roughly June through November, which is when most storm-driven reconstruction work gets queued up behind roofing and board-up crews. Humidity year-round means framing and subfloor need to read dry on a moisture meter before drywall goes back up, not just look dry. Homes near the coast or in flood zones often need reconstruction paired with FORTIFIED roofing or elevated electrical rough-in to meet current wind and flood codes, which can extend permitting timelines compared to a straightforward interior rebuild.
