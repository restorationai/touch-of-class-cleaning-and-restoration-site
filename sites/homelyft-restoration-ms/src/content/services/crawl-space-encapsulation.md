The first sign is usually subtle — a faint musty smell that you can't quite place, hardwood floors that feel soft underfoot, or energy bills that keep climbing despite nothing obvious changing. What's happening beneath your feet is a slow, invisible process: humid Gulf Coast air is migrating up through your open crawl space, soaking into floor joists, feeding mold colonies, and rotting the structural wood that holds your home up. Crawl space encapsulation stops that cycle by sealing the space from the ground up, converting it from an uncontrolled humidity zone into a dry, conditioned envelope.

## What crawl space encapsulation actually involves

Encapsulation is not simply laying a sheet of plastic on the dirt. A properly encapsulated crawl space gets a heavy-duty vapor barrier — typically 12 to 20 mil reinforced polyethylene — sealed to the foundation walls, piers, and any penetrations with foil tape and mechanical fasteners. Vents that once let in outside air are sealed or conditioned. A dehumidifier sized for the cubic footage of the space is installed and wired to a drain or condensate pump. If standing water, existing mold, or damaged insulation is present, those issues are addressed before any barrier goes down.

In Gulfport and coastal Mississippi, crawl spaces face some of the most aggressive humidity conditions in the country — average outdoor relative humidity regularly exceeds 70–80% in summer, and that air finds every gap in a vented crawl space. The work typically takes one to three days depending on square footage, access, and the condition of the space going in.

## Our process

1. **Crawl space inspection and moisture mapping.** Before any material goes in, the space is inspected for standing water, active leaks, existing mold growth, damaged insulation, and pest activity. Moisture readings are taken at the subfloor, joists, and soil to establish a baseline and identify any issues that must be resolved first.

2. **Pre-encapsulation remediation (if needed).** Wet or moldy insulation is removed and bagged. Any mold on wood framing is addressed — HomeLyft is an IICRC AMRT-certified firm, so mold remediation is handled in-house rather than handed off. Structural concerns are documented and flagged before the barrier goes down.

3. **Vapor barrier installation.** A reinforced polyethylene barrier is cut to fit and laid across the entire crawl space floor, lapped up the foundation walls at least 6–12 inches, and sealed at all seams, piers, and penetrations. Seams are overlapped a minimum of 12 inches and taped; corners and pipe penetrations get extra attention because those are where failures start.

4. **Vent sealing and air sealing.** Existing foundation vents are sealed with rigid foam and spray foam or replaced with conditioned-air vents depending on the design. Rim joists are air-sealed and insulated to prevent the warm, humid outside air from bypassing the barrier entirely.

5. **Dehumidifier sizing and installation.** A crawl space-rated dehumidifier — not a portable box-store unit — is installed, set to maintain relative humidity below 55%, and plumbed to drain continuously. A hygrometer or monitoring sensor is placed so the system's performance can be verified over time.

## What separates a good encapsulation from a bad one

The most common mistake is skipping the pre-work. Installers who are focused on selling a vapor barrier sometimes lay it directly over damp soil, existing mold, or compromised insulation. The barrier traps the problem rather than solving it, and within a season the mold is back — now hidden under a sealed layer of plastic.

The second failure point is barrier thickness. Six-mil poly is inexpensive and tears easily during installation and normal crawl space access. Twelve-mil minimum, and 20-mil in high-traffic or rocky crawl spaces, is the industry standard for a durable installation.

Third: dehumidification is not optional in coastal Mississippi. An encapsulated crawl space without active dehumidification will still accumulate moisture from concrete foundation walls and any remaining vapor drive. Homeowners who skip the dehumidifier often call back within a year wondering why their floors still feel soft.

Insurance adjusters reviewing a claim that involves crawl space damage — from a plumbing leak, storm flooding, or HVAC condensate failure — will look for documentation of pre-existing conditions versus damage caused by the covered loss. Thorough photo documentation and moisture readings taken before and after work are what separate a clean claim from a disputed one.

## Seasonal and regional considerations

The Gulf Coast does not get a dry season the way inland climates do. Relative humidity in the Gulfport area stays elevated from March through October, and even winter months rarely drop low enough to give a crawl space a natural drying window. That means a damaged or unencapsulated crawl space is under constant moisture pressure year-round — there is no "wait until summer is over" option here. Mold can begin colonizing damp wood within 24–48 hours under the right conditions, and Gulf Coast crawl spaces regularly provide exactly those conditions.

Post-storm periods are particularly high-risk. After a hurricane or tropical system moves through, crawl spaces that took on water from flooding or wind-driven rain need to be dried and assessed quickly — sitting water under a home creates the same Category 3 contamination concerns as any other groundwater intrusion.
