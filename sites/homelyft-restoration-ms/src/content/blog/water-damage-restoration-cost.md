**TL;DR:** Water damage restoration in Mississippi typically costs $1,500 to $5,000 for mitigation (drying and extraction) on a small to mid-size loss, and $8,000 to $25,000 or more when reconstruction is added. The single biggest cost driver is water category: clean water from a burst pipe is far cheaper to remediate than sewage or floodwater. Most Gulf Coast homeowners never write a check directly to the restoration company, the bill goes to the insurer via Xactimate line-item pricing. Your out-of-pocket is usually just your deductible.

If you just discovered water damage in your Gulfport home and you're trying to figure out what this is going to cost before the adjuster calls back, this is the breakdown you need. Costs vary a lot, a Category 1 (clean water) pipe burst in a single bathroom is a very different job from a Category 3 (sewage or floodwater) loss that soaked two floors. The numbers below reflect real-world industry pricing for the Mississippi Gulf Coast market in 2026.

## How much does water damage restoration cost on average?

Most water damage restoration jobs in Mississippi fall between $1,500 and $8,000 for mitigation alone, with a national average around $3,000 to $4,500. Add reconstruction and the total climbs fast. The range is wide because the job scope varies enormously: a washing machine overflow caught in two hours is a half-day extraction job; a slow slab leak discovered after six weeks is a full gut-and-rebuild.

Here is a realistic price sheet by common scenario:

| Scenario | Typical Range (Mitigation Only) |
|---|---|
| Category 1, single room, caught quickly | $1,200 - $3,500 |
| Category 1, multiple rooms or finished basement | $3,500 - $7,500 |
| Category 2 (gray water: appliance, toilet overflow) | $4,000 - $9,000 |
| Category 3 (black water: sewage, floodwater) | $7,000 - $20,000+ |
| Storm surge or major flood, whole-home | $15,000 - $40,000+ |

These are mitigation figures only. Reconstruction (drywall, flooring, cabinetry, insulation) is billed separately and can equal or exceed the mitigation cost on larger losses.

## What is the cost per square foot for water damage restoration?

Water damage restoration typically runs $3.75 to $7.50 per square foot for mitigation, depending on the water category and materials affected. That range reflects extraction, drying equipment deployment, and antimicrobial treatment, not reconstruction.

Here is how the per-square-foot math plays out in practice:

- **Category 1 (clean water):** $3.75 - $5.00 per sq ft. Drywall and flooring can often be dried in place if caught within 24-48 hours.
- **Category 2 (gray water):** $5.00 - $7.00 per sq ft. Porous materials (carpet, drywall below the flood cut) usually require removal.
- **Category 3 (black water):** $7.00 - $12.00+ per sq ft. All porous materials in the affected zone are typically removed per [IICRC S500](https://www.iicrc.org/page/IICRCS500) guidelines. Full PPE, containment, and antimicrobial treatment are required.

On the Gulf Coast, the humidity factor matters. Gulfport and Biloxi average 75-80% relative humidity in summer months, which slows structural drying and can add equipment days to the job, meaning the per-square-foot cost can run toward the top of these ranges in August and September compared to a drier climate.

## What factors move the price up or down?

Several variables drive water damage restoration costs more than square footage alone.

**Water category and class.** Category (1, 2, or 3) describes contamination level. Class (1 through 4) describes how deeply water has penetrated materials. A Class 4 loss involves specialty drying of dense materials like hardwood subfloor or concrete, it requires desiccant dehumidifiers and longer drying cycles, which adds equipment cost.

**Response time.** The [EPA notes](https://www.epa.gov/mold) that mold can begin colonizing wet materials within 24 to 48 hours. A job called in at hour two is a drying job. The same job called in at day four may now also require [mold remediation](/services/mold-remediation/), which adds cost. On the Gulf Coast, where ambient humidity is already high, that 24-hour window is not a guideline, it is a hard deadline.

**Materials affected.** Hardwood floors, tile-over-concrete slabs, and spray-foam insulated walls each require different drying protocols. Engineered hardwood often cannot be dried in place and must be replaced. Tile over concrete can trap moisture underneath for weeks without proper drying equipment.

**Reconstruction scope.** Mitigation ends when the structure reads dry on a moisture meter. Everything after that, hanging new drywall, installing flooring, repainting, is reconstruction billed at general contracting rates. A typical flood-cut drywall replacement runs $2.50 to $5.00 per square foot of wall area before paint.

**Access and containment.** Crawl space losses in older Gulf Coast homes add cost: limited access, tight working conditions, and the need for encapsulation after drying. If your home has a pier-and-beam foundation common in older Gulfport neighborhoods, budget for crawl space work as a separate line item.

## Mitigation vs. reconstruction: what is the difference in cost?

Mitigation (also called drying or remediation) and reconstruction are two separate scopes of work, often handled by the same company but billed as distinct phases.

**Mitigation** covers everything needed to stop the damage and dry the structure: water extraction, demolition of unsalvageable materials (flood cuts, carpet removal), deployment of air movers and LGR dehumidifiers, antimicrobial application, and daily moisture monitoring until the structure reaches dry standard per the IICRC S500. This phase typically takes 3 to 5 days for a standard loss.

**Reconstruction** covers putting the home back together: framing, drywall, insulation, flooring, paint, and cabinetry. This phase can take days to weeks depending on scope and material lead times.

A typical mid-size Gulf Coast loss might look like this:

- Mitigation: $4,500
- Reconstruction: $6,200
- Total: $10,700

Your insurance adjuster will review both phases separately. Make sure the restoration company you hire provides a written scope for each phase before work begins.

## How does insurance actually pay for water damage restoration?

Most water damage restoration invoices are never paid by the homeowner directly. The restoration company bills the insurance carrier using Xactimate, an industry-standard line-item pricing software that adjusters and contractors both use. Xactimate prices are updated regionally and reflect local labor and material rates for the Gulf Coast market.

Here is how the process typically works:

1. You file a claim and the carrier assigns an adjuster.
2. The restoration company documents the loss (photos, moisture readings, scope of damage) and submits a line-item estimate via Xactimate.
3. The carrier reviews, may send their own adjuster or desk adjuster, and issues payment minus your deductible.
4. You pay the restoration company your deductible. The carrier pays the rest directly or via a two-party check with your mortgage lender.

What is NOT covered varies by policy. Gradual leaks (a slow drip behind a wall over months) are typically excluded. Flood damage from storm surge requires a separate NFIP (National Flood Insurance Program) policy, standard homeowners policies do not cover rising water. For a full breakdown of what policies typically cover, see our guide on [does homeowners insurance cover water damage](/blog/does-homeowners-insurance-cover-water-damage/).

Proper documentation is the single biggest factor in a smooth claim. Moisture logs, psychrometric readings, and daily equipment records from a certified firm give the adjuster what they need to approve the full scope.

## What should you do right now if you have active water damage?

Stop the water source first if it is safe to do so. Then document everything with photos and video before any cleanup begins, your adjuster needs the before-state. Call your insurance carrier to open a claim, then call a certified restoration company to begin mitigation. Do not wait for the adjuster to arrive before starting drying; most policies require you to mitigate further damage promptly.
