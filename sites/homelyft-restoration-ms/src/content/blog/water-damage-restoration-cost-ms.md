## How Much Does Water Damage Restoration Cost in Mississippi?

In Mississippi, water damage restoration typically costs $1,500 to $15,000, with the average residential job landing between $3,000 and $7,500. A small clean-water leak caught within 24 hours sits at the low end. A Category 3 sewage backup or a multi-room flood that sat over a weekend pushes toward the high end or beyond.

The Gulf Coast's climate makes timing especially critical. Gulfport's average humidity runs above 75% for much of the year, and the [EPA notes](https://www.epa.gov/mold) that mold can begin colonizing wet building materials within 24 to 48 hours. A job that costs $4,000 on day one can become a $12,000 job by day three if mold remediation gets added to the scope.

## What Factors Drive the Cost Up or Down?

Five things move the price more than anything else.

**Water category.** The [IICRC S500 standard](https://www.iicrc.org) classifies water losses into three categories. Category 1 is clean water from a supply line or appliance. Category 2 (gray water) carries contaminants from a dishwasher or washing machine. Category 3 (black water) is sewage, floodwater, or any water that has sat long enough to become grossly contaminated. Each step up the scale adds cost because it adds required PPE, antimicrobial treatment, and disposal protocols.

**Class of loss (how deep the water went).** Class 1 means a small area with minimal absorption. Class 4 means water saturated hardwood floors, concrete, or plaster, which requires specialty drying equipment and much longer dry times. Gulf Coast homes with pier-and-beam construction often see Class 3 and 4 losses because water migrates into floor systems quickly.

**Square footage affected.** Restoration crews price by the area dried, not the area flooded. A 200-square-foot bathroom loss is a different job than a 1,200-square-foot ground floor.

**Response time.** The longer water sits, the more it wicks into walls, subfloors, and insulation. A loss called in at two hours costs less to dry than the same loss called in at 48 hours. This is the single biggest cost lever a homeowner controls.

**Materials affected.** Drywall, insulation, and carpet are relatively cheap to remove and replace. Hardwood floors, tile, custom cabinetry, and plaster are not. Homes near the water in areas like Long Beach and Pass Christian often have older materials that require more careful handling.

## What Does the Restoration Process Actually Involve?

Knowing the steps helps you understand the invoice when it arrives.

1. **Emergency extraction.** Truck-mounted or portable extractors remove standing water. This happens first, within hours of the call.
2. **Moisture mapping.** Technicians use thermal imaging cameras and calibrated moisture meters to find water that has migrated behind walls, under floors, and into cavities that look dry to the eye.
3. **Structural drying.** LGR (low-grain refrigerant) dehumidifiers and high-velocity air movers are placed in a calculated pattern per the IICRC S500 drying standard. Equipment runs continuously, typically for three to five days.
4. **Demolition of unsalvageable materials.** Wet drywall, insulation, and flooring that cannot be dried in place are removed. This is called a "flood cut" when it involves cutting drywall to the affected height.
5. **Antimicrobial treatment.** Applied to structural surfaces after demolition to prevent mold growth during the drying phase.
6. **Daily monitoring.** Moisture readings are logged each day. Drying is complete when materials reach target moisture content, not on a fixed schedule.
7. **Reconstruction.** Once the structure is dry and cleared, rebuild begins: drywall, insulation, flooring, paint. HomeLyft Restoration MS handles [general contracting](/services/general-contracting/) so homeowners deal with one company from loss to livable.

## Will Homeowners Insurance Cover Water Damage Restoration in Mississippi?

Most standard homeowners policies cover sudden, accidental water damage but exclude gradual leaks and flooding from outside the home. Here is how it typically breaks down.

**Usually covered:** Burst pipe, failed appliance supply line, sudden roof leak from a storm, accidental overflow from a bathtub or sink.

**Usually not covered:** Slow drip behind a wall that went undetected for months, sewer backup (unless you have a rider), and rising floodwater from a storm (that requires a separate NFIP or private flood policy through [FEMA's National Flood Insurance Program](https://www.fema.gov/flood-insurance)).

Gulf Coast homeowners, especially those in Gulfport, Biloxi, and Waveland, are in a high-risk flood zone. If you do not have a separate flood policy, storm surge losses will not be covered by your standard homeowners policy, period.

**The documentation that protects your claim.** Adjusters look for proof of the source, the timeline, and the scope. A restoration company that photographs the loss on arrival, logs moisture readings daily, and provides a written scope of work gives your adjuster exactly what they need. Gaps in documentation are the most common reason partial claims get disputed.

For a plain-English breakdown of what policies actually pay, see [Does Homeowners Insurance Cover Water Damage?](/blog/does-homeowners-insurance-cover-water-damage/)

**Deductible reality.** Mississippi homeowners' deductibles commonly run $1,000 to $2,500 for standard losses. Wind and hail deductibles on Gulf Coast policies are often percentage-based (1% to 5% of dwelling value), which can mean $3,000 to $10,000 out of pocket before insurance pays a dollar on a storm-related loss.

## What Should You Do Right Now?

If water is still entering the structure, stop the source first. Shut off the main water supply if it is a plumbing failure. If it is storm-related and you cannot stop it, move to the next step immediately.

Call a restoration company before you call your insurance company. This is counterintuitive but correct. Restoration crews document the loss in real time, which builds your claim. Calling insurance first does not speed anything up, and waiting for an adjuster before extraction starts costs you money in additional damage.

Remove standing water from electronics, documents, and irreplaceable items yourself if it is safe to do so. Do not run fans and open windows as a substitute for professional drying equipment. Residential fans move surface moisture into the air; they do not remove it from wall cavities or subfloors, and they can actually spread mold spores if mold is already present.
