**TL;DR:** To maximize a water damage insurance claim, photograph everything before touching anything, log the exact date and time of the loss, keep all damaged materials until the adjuster visits, and get a written mitigation invoice from your restoration company. On the Mississippi Gulf Coast, know this critical distinction: standard homeowners policies cover sudden, accidental water damage from inside the home, but storm surge and rising floodwater require a separate NFIP flood policy. Document first, then mitigate.

You just found water where it shouldn't be. Maybe a supply line burst under the kitchen sink, or last night's storm pushed water through a compromised roof. Your first instinct is to start cleaning up. Stop. The next 30 minutes of documentation will determine how much your insurance company pays, and skipping it is the most expensive mistake homeowners make. This guide covers the mechanics of filing a water damage claim correctly, from the first photo to the final invoice.

For a plain-English breakdown of what your policy actually covers, see our related post: [Does Homeowners Insurance Cover Water Damage? A Plain-English Guide](/blog/does-homeowners-insurance-cover-water-damage/).

## What should you photograph before touching anything?

Photograph every affected area before moving furniture, pulling up rugs, or starting any cleanup. Adjusters are trained to spot post-loss staging, and a claim without pre-mitigation photos is a claim that invites disputes. Use your phone's timestamp feature or take a quick video with the date visible on a newspaper or your phone screen in frame.

Here is what to capture:

- **The source of the water** (the burst pipe, the failed appliance hose, the roof penetration). Get close enough that the failure point is clear.
- **Standing water levels** against a fixed reference point, like a baseboard or door frame, so the adjuster can gauge depth.
- **Every affected room**, shot wide (to show scope) and close (to show material damage: soaked drywall, buckled flooring, saturated insulation).
- **Personal property losses**: furniture, electronics, clothing, appliances. Serial numbers in frame when possible.
- **Exterior damage** if the loss is storm-related: missing shingles, damaged soffits, compromised flashing.

Back up everything to cloud storage immediately. Phones get lost. Claims take months.

## How do you log the loss date and notify your insurer?

Call your insurance company the same day you discover the damage. Most homeowners policies require "prompt notice," and some carriers define that as 24 to 72 hours. Waiting too long gives the adjuster grounds to question whether secondary damage (mold, structural deterioration) was caused by the original loss or by your delay.

When you call, have ready:

1. Your policy number
2. The date and approximate time you discovered the damage
3. A one-sentence description of the source ("supply line to the refrigerator failed" or "roof damage from wind let rain into the attic")
4. An estimate of affected square footage if you have it

Write down the claim number, the adjuster's name, and their direct phone number. Every conversation after this should be followed by a brief email confirming what was discussed. Paper trails protect you.

Most carriers allow you to begin emergency mitigation before the adjuster arrives, and you should. Mold can begin colonizing wet building materials in as little as 24 to 48 hours under [IICRC S500 Standard](https://www.iicrc.org/page/IICRCS500) conditions. Waiting for an adjuster appointment before extracting standing water is not required and is not recommended.

## Why should you keep damaged materials instead of throwing them away?

Keep every piece of damaged drywall, flooring, insulation, and personal property until the adjuster has either seen it in person or confirmed in writing that photos are sufficient. Disposing of materials before documentation gives the carrier a reason to undervalue the claim.

This matters most for:

- **Flooring**: A 12-inch sample of buckled hardwood or delaminated LVP shows the adjuster the product quality and finish, which affects replacement cost.
- **Drywall sections**: Wet drywall that has been cut out and stacked in the garage proves the scope of demolition. A photo of an empty wall cavity does not.
- **Appliances and electronics**: Keep the damaged unit. Adjusters often require a repair estimate before authorizing replacement.
- **Insulation**: Saturated batt insulation is a mold risk and should be removed quickly, but bag and label it rather than sending it to the curb.

Once the adjuster confirms the scope in writing, you can dispose of materials. Not before.

## What is the difference between a homeowners policy and a flood policy in Mississippi?

On the Mississippi Gulf Coast, this distinction can be the difference between a full payout and a denied claim. Standard homeowners insurance (HO-3 and similar forms) covers sudden, accidental water damage that originates inside the home: burst pipes, appliance failures, roof leaks from wind damage. It does NOT cover flooding.

Flooding, as defined by the [National Flood Insurance Program (NFIP)](https://www.floodsmart.gov/), means water that overflows from a body of water, accumulates on the ground from rainfall, or enters the home as storm surge. In Harrison, Hancock, and Jackson counties, storm surge is the primary flood risk during hurricane season. If water came in from outside during a named storm or tropical system, your homeowners carrier will almost certainly deny the claim, and your NFIP flood policy is the correct vehicle.

Key differences:

| Coverage Type | What It Covers | What It Excludes |
|---|---|---|
| Homeowners (HO-3) | Burst pipes, appliance leaks, wind-driven rain through a damaged roof | Storm surge, rising floodwater, sewer backup (unless endorsed) |
| NFIP Flood Policy | Floodwater from any external source, storm surge, overflow from drainage systems | Finished basement contents, currency, most vehicles |
| Sewer Backup Rider | Sewage or drain backup into the home | Ground-surface flooding |

If you experienced both wind damage and flooding in the same event, you may have claims under two separate policies. Document the source of each type of damage separately. Your [storm damage restoration](/services/storm-damage-restoration/) scope and your flood mitigation scope should be invoiced separately to match each policy's coverage.

## How do you get the mitigation invoice right?

The mitigation invoice is the document your adjuster uses to authorize payment for the emergency work. A vague invoice creates delays. A detailed, line-item invoice in Xactimate format (the industry-standard estimating platform most carriers use) moves claims faster.

When you hire a restoration company, ask for:

- A written scope of work before work begins, not after
- Line-item billing that separates extraction, drying equipment, antimicrobial treatment, and demolition
- Daily moisture readings logged by room and material, showing the drying curve from wet to dry
- Equipment placement logs (which air movers and dehumidifiers were placed where, and for how many days)
- A final moisture report confirming materials were dried to [IICRC S500](https://www.iicrc.org/page/IICRCS500) standard before reconstruction begins

This documentation is what protects you if the carrier tries to reduce the mitigation payment. It also protects you from a future mold claim being denied on the grounds that drying was incomplete.

## How long do you have to file a water damage claim in Mississippi?

Mississippi law does not set a universal deadline for filing an insurance claim, but your individual policy will. Most homeowners policies require prompt notice (typically within a few days of discovery) and set a statute of limitations for legal action at one year from the date of loss, though some policies extend this to two years. Read your declarations page.

For NFIP flood policies, the deadline is stricter: you must file a Proof of Loss within 60 days of the flood event. Missing that deadline can void your right to payment entirely. If you are filing under both a homeowners policy and an NFIP policy after a storm event, track the deadlines separately.

If you are unsure whether your loss is within the filing window, call your agent today. Do not wait.

## What should you do right now?

If you are standing in a wet room reading this, here is the sequence:

1. Stop the source of water if you can do so safely (shut off the main supply valve or call a plumber).
2. Photograph everything before moving anything.
3. Call your insurance company and open a claim.
4. Call a certified restoration company to begin emergency extraction and drying.
5. Keep all damaged materials until the adjuster confirms scope.
6. Request a written, line-item mitigation invoice.

If the loss involves storm surge or rising water, pull out your NFIP flood policy alongside your homeowners policy before you call.
