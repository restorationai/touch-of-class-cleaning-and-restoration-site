## How to Compare Water Damage Companies in Gulfport

Not all restoration companies are equal, and the differences matter most when you're filing an insurance claim. Here is what to look for before you commit:

**IICRC Certification.** The [IICRC](https://www.iicrc.org) (Institute of Inspection, Cleaning and Restoration Certification) sets the industry standard for water damage mitigation (S500) and structural drying. Ask specifically whether the company holds WRT and ASD credentials, not just a generic "certified" label. These are the credentials that govern how drying is planned, measured, and documented.

**Structural drying capability.** Water damage restoration is not just extraction. Walls, subfloors, and framing absorb water and must be dried to measured moisture content targets. This requires LGR (low-grain refrigerant) dehumidifiers, calibrated air movers, and moisture mapping with a pin or pinless meter. A company that only extracts and fans is not doing full mitigation.

**Insurance documentation.** Your claim depends on a written scope of work, moisture readings at baseline and throughout drying, and daily drying logs. A company that does not produce these documents is leaving your claim exposed. Ask upfront whether they use Xactimate or a comparable estimating platform and whether they will communicate directly with your adjuster.

**License and insurance.** Mississippi requires a contractor license for restoration work. HomeLyft Restoration MS holds license RO 2728. Verify any company you hire is licensed and insured before work begins.

**24/7 availability.** Water damage gets worse by the hour. Mold can begin colonizing within 24-48 hours on wet organic materials, according to [EPA guidance on mold and moisture](https://www.epa.gov/mold). A company that can respond at 2 a.m. when a pipe bursts is worth more than one that calls back the next morning.

## What Happens When You Call a Water Damage Company in Gulfport

Knowing what to expect helps you move faster and make better decisions in a stressful situation.

**Step 1: The intake call.** A good restoration company will ask about the source of water (still active or stopped?), the affected rooms, whether there is standing water, and your insurance carrier. Have that information ready. If the source is still active, a plumber needs to stop it first. The restoration crew handles what comes after.

**Step 2: Arrival and assessment.** The crew will map moisture with a meter, photograph all affected areas, and classify the water (Category 1 is clean water from a supply line; Category 2 is gray water from appliances or toilets; Category 3, also called black water, includes sewage or floodwater). The category determines the PPE, the cleaning protocol, and what materials can be dried versus what must be removed.

**Step 3: Extraction and drying setup.** Standing water is extracted first. Then air movers and LGR dehumidifiers are placed according to a drying plan. In Gulf Coast homes, ambient humidity is already high, so equipment sizing matters. Underpowering a drying job in Gulfport's August humidity is a common mistake that leads to mold.

**Step 4: Monitoring.** Daily moisture readings track progress against drying targets. A company following the IICRC S500 standard will document readings at each check. This documentation is your insurance record.

**Step 5: Clearance and rebuild.** When materials reach target moisture content, the drying phase closes. Reconstruction, whether drywall, flooring, or cabinetry, follows. HomeLyft Restoration MS handles the general contracting phase in-house, which means no handoff gap between mitigation and rebuild.
