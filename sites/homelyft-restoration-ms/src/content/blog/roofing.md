## What Counts as Storm Damage to a Roof?

Storm damage to a roof means any wind, hail, or debris impact that compromises the roof's ability to keep water out. That includes missing or lifted shingles, cracked or bruised shingles from hail, damaged flashing around chimneys and vents, punctures from fallen branches, and granule loss that shortens the shingle's remaining life.

D'Iberville sits in Harrison County, directly in the path of Gulf Coast storm systems. The area sees regular tropical weather from June through November, with wind gusts that can strip shingles rated for 90 mph if they're already aged or improperly fastened. After any named storm or severe thunderstorm warning, it's worth a ground-level inspection even if you don't see obvious damage from inside.

Hail damage is often invisible from the ground. Look for:
- Dented or cracked shingles with a dark, bruised center
- Granules collecting in gutters or at downspout exits
- Dented metal flashing, ridge caps, or gutters
- Soft spots when you press on a shingle (requires safe roof access)

If you're not comfortable on a ladder or the roof pitch is steep, don't go up. A contractor or restoration professional can do the inspection safely.

## How Do You Protect Your Roof Before Repairs Are Made?

Emergency tarping is the single most important step between a storm event and a permanent repair. A properly installed tarp stops water intrusion, which prevents the water damage, mold growth, and structural deterioration that follow a compromised roof.

For insurance purposes, tarping also matters legally. Mississippi homeowners policies include a "duty to mitigate" clause: you're required to take reasonable steps to prevent further damage after a loss. A roof left open for days while you wait for an adjuster can result in a partial claim denial for the interior damage that follows.

Emergency board-up and tarping services can deploy quickly after a storm. A tarp should:
- Cover the full damaged area plus at least 3 feet of overlap on all sides
- Be secured to the decking with furring strips or battens, not just weighted down
- Be rated for the wind speeds typical to this region (minimum 6 mil poly)

If the storm also broke windows or doors, board-up services handle that at the same time. Getting the structure sealed is the priority before any interior cleanup begins.

## Will Homeowners Insurance Cover Roof Storm Damage in Mississippi?

Most standard homeowners policies in Mississippi cover sudden, accidental damage from wind and hail, including roof damage. What they typically exclude: damage from wear and tear, improper installation, or pre-existing deterioration that a storm simply worsened.

The practical reality in D'Iberville and coastal Harrison County is that many older roofs have some pre-existing wear. Adjusters are trained to look for it, and they will note it in their report. This is why your documentation matters as much as the damage itself.

Before the adjuster arrives, do this:

1. **Photograph everything from ground level** immediately after the storm. Time-stamp your photos.
2. **Save any fallen shingles or debris** in a bag. Physical evidence of wind impact helps.
3. **Document interior damage** (ceiling stains, wet insulation, water in the attic) with photos and video.
4. **Get a written scope from a licensed contractor** before the adjuster visit if possible. A written scope gives the adjuster something concrete to respond to rather than setting the number themselves.
5. **File your claim promptly.** Mississippi has no specific deadline for storm claims written into statute, but most policies require "timely" notice. File within days, not weeks.

For roofing work specifically, Mississippi requires a Residential Roofing Contractor license (RCL) for jobs over $10,000. Verify any contractor's license through the [Mississippi State Board of Contractors](https://www.msboc.us) before signing anything.

For guidance on what homeowners policies typically cover more broadly, see [Does Homeowners Insurance Cover Water Damage?](/blog/does-homeowners-insurance-cover-water-damage/) on this site.

## What Is the FORTIFIED Roofing Standard and Why Does It Matter in Mississippi?

The FORTIFIED Home program, developed by the Insurance Institute for Business and Home Safety (IBHS), is a construction standard that makes roofs significantly more resistant to wind and hail. Mississippi adopted FORTIFIED as a major incentive program because Gulf Coast homes are disproportionately exposed to hurricane-force wind events.

In Mississippi, a FORTIFIED Roof designation can qualify homeowners for meaningful insurance premium discounts, and some insurers require it for new policies in coastal counties. The standard requires sealed roof decks, enhanced edge fastening, and impact-rated shingles or metal roofing.

When a storm damages a roof in D'Iberville, the repair or replacement is an opportunity to upgrade to FORTIFIED standards at the same time. A contractor who holds FORTIFIED certification can document the work to IBHS specifications, which the homeowner then submits to receive the official designation.

For more information on the program and eligibility, visit [ibhs.org/fortified](https://ibhs.org/fortified).

## What Should You Do Right Now If Your Roof Is Damaged?

If you're reading this after a storm and your roof is actively leaking or visibly damaged, the sequence is straightforward.

First, stop the water. If it's safe, place buckets under active drips and move valuables away from wet areas. Do not attempt to go on the roof during or immediately after a storm.

Second, call for emergency tarping. This is a same-day service for active storm damage. A crew can assess the roof, install a proper tarp, and give you a written scope of the visible damage, all of which supports your insurance claim.

Third, call your insurance company to open the claim. Have your policy number ready. Note the claim number and the adjuster's name and contact information.

Fourth, do not sign a roofing contract that includes an Assignment of Benefits (AOB) clause. Mississippi law has restricted AOB in insurance contexts, and contractors who push AOB agreements are a red flag. You retain the right to negotiate your own claim.

Fifth, get the permanent repair scoped and permitted. Most roofing replacements in Harrison County require a building permit. A licensed contractor pulls the permit; you should confirm they do before work starts.
