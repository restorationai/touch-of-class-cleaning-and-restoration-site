## Why Baytown Sees Storm Damage That's Different From Inland Houston

Baytown sits at the convergence of Galveston Bay, the Houston Ship Channel, and Cedar Bayou, which means tropical systems bring surge and bayou backflow on top of the usual wind and hail. Neighborhoods like Goose Creek and parts of Pelly sit close enough to the water that a Category 1 or even a strong tropical storm can push brackish water into yards and garages well before the eye makes landfall. Add in Baytown's mature tree canopy, especially in older sections of Wooster and around the Texas Avenue historic district, and you get a predictable pattern after severe weather: downed limbs on roofs, fence lines flattened, and shingles peeled back from sustained coastal wind. The proximity to the ExxonMobil Baytown Refinery and the industrial corridor along the ship channel also means some properties deal with debris and runoff that's different from a typical residential storm loss, which affects how we approach cleanup and disposal.

## Our Storm Damage Restoration Process in Baytown

The first visit is about stopping further loss, not full repair. That means emergency tarping over compromised roof sections, board-up on blown-out windows or doors, and extraction of any standing water before it sits long enough to wick into drywall and insulation. From there we follow IICRC S500 drying protocols, using our WRT and ASD training to set moisture goals for framing, subfloor, and any salvageable finish materials rather than guessing at a timeline. Homes near the bay that took on brackish surge water get extra attention during drying, since salt residue left behind in wall cavities can continue attracting moisture long after the structure reads dry on the surface. Once materials are stabilized, we document everything with photos and moisture readings your adjuster will need.

## Baytown Insurance & HOA Coordination

Storm claims in Baytown often split across two policies: wind and hail damage typically runs through a standard homeowner's policy, while surge or bayou flooding usually falls under NFIP flood coverage or a separate flood rider. We've found that documenting the source of water, wind-driven rain versus rising surge, matters a great deal to how a claim gets paid out here, so we note entry points and water lines on walls as part of our initial assessment. For properties inside an HOA, particularly newer developments further from the historic core, we coordinate tarping and debris staging with property management to keep emergency work compliant with community standards while the claim is still open.

In Pelly and Wooster, a number of homes were built before Baytown's current elevation and drainage standards were in place, which means they tend to sit closer to original grade than newer construction near Lee College.
