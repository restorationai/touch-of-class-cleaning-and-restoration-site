## Why Spring Properties See Water Damage

Spring sits low relative to its two namesake waterways, and neighborhoods built along Cypress Creek and Spring Creek flood in a pattern locals know well: heavy rain upstream in the Klein area or around Gleannloch Farms can raise creek levels hours after the rain stops here. Add in clay-heavy soil that swells and shifts foundations, aging cast-iron and galvanized plumbing in homes built through the 1980s and 90s, and slab foundations that hide leaks until flooring starts to cup or carpet smells musty, and you get a city where water damage rarely announces itself all at once. Old Town Spring's older wood-frame buildings and the brick-and-slab tract homes common in Champion Forest and Northgate Forest each hold water differently, which changes how fast we need to move once we're on site.

## Our Water Extraction Process in Spring

We start with a moisture map, not a guess. Submersible pumps and truck-mount extractors pull standing water first, then we check subfloor, baseboards, and wall cavities with a moisture meter because surface-dry doesn't mean structure-dry. In homes with engineered or laminate flooring, common throughout newer Spring subdivisions, we often need to pull sections to extract water trapped underneath before it delaminates. Air movers and dehumidifiers go in next, sized to the room and the material, and we monitor daily until readings hit dry standard under IICRC S500 guidelines rather than pulling equipment early because the carpet feels dry to the touch.

## Spring Insurance & HOA Coordination

Most Spring homeowners' policies cover sudden water damage from a burst pipe or appliance failure but draw a hard line at flood water from rising creeks, which falls under separate flood coverage. We document the source of water, photograph affected materials, and write up moisture readings in a format most carriers accept, which matters because adjusters often ask whether water came from a covered peril or storm surface flooding. Several Spring HOA communities, particularly newer developments near Gleannloch Farms, also require written notice before dumpsters or drying equipment sit in a driveway longer than a day or two, and we can provide documentation homeowners need for that request.

## Local note

Homes within a few blocks of Pundt Park and the Spring Creek floodplain often have crawlspace or pier-and-beam foundations instead of slab, a detail that changes the whole extraction plan: water pools under the house instead of just in it, so we bring submersible pumps and inspect the crawlspace vapor barrier even when the interior floors look fine, because trapped moisture down there feeds mold growth that never shows upstairs until it's established.
