## Why The Woodlands Sees Water Damage Calls

Most homes from Grogan's Mill through Creekside Park sit on slab foundations poured over expansive clay that swells and contracts with Montgomery County's wet-dry cycles. That movement stresses plumbing joints under the slab over time, and a pinhole leak can run for days before a homeowner notices a warm spot on the floor or a water bill that doesn't match usage. Mature pine root systems add another wrinkle: roots seeking moisture during dry spells will migrate toward sewer laterals and irrigation lines, and a root intrusion that finally gives way often floods a utility room or garage with no warning at all.

The community's wooded lots also mean slower surface drainage after heavy rain. Water that would run off an open yard elsewhere in the metro can pool against a foundation or back up through a French drain in low-lying sections near Lake Woodlands, and that standing water finds its way inside through brick weep holes or door thresholds faster than most homeowners expect.

## Our Water Extraction Process

We start with extraction, pulling standing water with truck-mounted or portable units depending on how much volume is involved and whether the affected area is a slab-on-grade living space or a finished garage. From there we map moisture in the walls, subfloor, and any cabinetry with penetrating meters, because a floor that reads dry at the surface in a humid Gulf Coast climate can still be saturated two inches down. Drying follows the IICRC S500 standard: air movers and dehumidifiers sized to the room, with daily monitoring until materials hit a stable, dry benchmark rather than a guess. Our crews hold IICRC WRT and ASD credentials, which matters most on jobs where wood flooring or built-ins need to be dried in place instead of torn out.
