## Why Spring Properties See Water Damage Issues

Spring sits low relative to its two namesake creeks, and a lot of the housing stock, especially the ranch-style slab homes built through the 70s and 80s around Klein and Northgate Forest, was never elevated with flood events in mind. Add Houston-area clay soil that swells and shrinks with moisture, and you get slab movement that opens hairline gaps under baseboards and tile, gaps water finds fast during a heavy rain event or a failed supply line. Older PVC and polybutylene plumbing runs in these neighborhoods also fail more often than copper, so a slow leak inside a wall cavity can go unnoticed for weeks before it surfaces as warped flooring or a musty smell near the baseboards.

## Our Water Damage Restoration Process in Spring

We start with extraction, pulling standing water with truck-mount units before it has a chance to wick further into drywall, baseboards, or subfloor. From there we moisture-map the affected rooms with meters and thermal imaging, because in homes with foam or batt insulation under the slab edge, surface readings can look dry while the material underneath is still saturated. Air movers and dehumidifiers go in next, set up and monitored daily against the IICRC S500 drying standard rather than pulled on a fixed schedule, since humidity swings here mean two houses on the same street can dry at different rates depending on how tight the HVAC system is. We document moisture readings at each visit so the drying record holds up if your adjuster asks for it later.
