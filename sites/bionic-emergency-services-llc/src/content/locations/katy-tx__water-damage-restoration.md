## Why Katy Properties See Water Damage Issues

Most homes west of the Grand Parkway, from Seven Meadows to Grand Lakes, sit on concrete slab foundations poured into Katy's gumbo clay. That clay shrinks hard during Gulf Coast summer droughts and swells just as fast once the rain returns, and the movement stresses the plumbing lines running through and under the slab. A hairline crack in a supply line can leak for weeks before a homeowner notices a soft spot in the flooring or a musty smell near a wall. Add Katy's humidity, which rarely drops low enough to help a wet structure dry on its own, and a slow leak turns into a saturated subfloor fast. Older sections of Old Katy still have original cast iron or galvanized plumbing that corrodes from the inside, while newer construction in Firethorne and Cinco Ranch relies on PEX that's more leak-resistant but not immune to slab shift or a bad fitting.

## Our Water Damage Restoration Process in Katy

We start with extraction, pulling standing water with truck-mount and portable units before it migrates further into wall cavities or under cabinetry toe-kicks. From there we map moisture with meters and thermal imaging to find out how far the water traveled, which matters on a slab home where water can wick several feet from the original source. Drying is set up and monitored against the IICRC S500 standard, with daily readings to confirm the structure is actually losing moisture, not just feeling dry on the surface. In Katy's climate, dehumidification runs longer than it would in a drier region, and we adjust equipment placement accordingly rather than pulling it early because a moisture meter reads close enough.
