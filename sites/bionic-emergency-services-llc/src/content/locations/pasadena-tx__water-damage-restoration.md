Pasadena's housing stock runs heavy on slab-on-grade construction built from the 1950s through the 1980s, especially in the neighborhoods closer to Downtown Pasadena and along Fairmont Parkway. These slabs were poured before modern vapor barrier standards were common, which means ground moisture can wick upward through concrete even without a visible leak. Add in the region's high water table near the Houston Ship Channel and the clay-heavy Gulf Coast soil that expands and contracts with rainfall, and you get foundations prone to hairline cracks that let water intrude during heavy storms. ## Our Water Damage Restoration Process in Pasadena

The first step is always extraction, pulling standing water out with truck-mounted or portable extraction units depending on how deep the water sits and how far it's traveled under flooring. In slab homes, we pay close attention to what's happening beneath tile and laminate, because trapped moisture can read dry at the surface while the slab itself stays saturated for days. We set structural drying equipment, air movers and dehumidifiers sized to the affected square footage and monitor moisture content daily with meters until readings return to normal for the material type. Homes near the Spencer Highway corridor with carpet over pad often need extra attention here, since pad holds water long after the carpet face feels dry to the touch.
