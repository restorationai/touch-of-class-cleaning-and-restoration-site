Raw sewage is classified as Category 3 water, the most contaminated category under industry standards, and it doesn't wait for a convenient time to show up. Whether it's a sewer line backing up through a floor drain, a septic overflow in the yard, or wastewater pushing up through a toilet or shower during heavy rain, the longer it sits, the deeper it travels into subfloors, drywall, and insulation.

## What Sewage Cleanup and Sanitization actually involves

Raw sewage carries bacteria, viruses, parasites, and other pathogens that standard cleaning products aren't built to handle. This isn't a mop-and-bucket job. It requires full PPE, EPA-registered antimicrobial and enzymatic products, and in most cases the removal of any porous material the water touched, carpet, carpet pad, baseboards, and sometimes drywall or subflooring, because those materials can't be reliably sanitized once contaminated.

The work typically starts with sewage extraction using truck-mounted or portable pumps rated for solids, followed by removal of anything that can't be disinfected. Hard surfaces get cleaned, disinfected, and in many cases tested for microbial activity before the space is considered safe to rebuild. Depending on how far the water traveled and what it touched, a sewage backup cleanup can run anywhere from a single afternoon for a contained bathroom overflow to several days when a sewer line backup has spread water under flooring across multiple rooms.

## Our process

1. **Contamination assessment and containment.** We identify the source, whether it's a sewer line backup, septic overflow, or supply-side failure, and seal off the affected area to stop cross-contamination into clean parts of the structure.
2. **Raw sewage removal and extraction.** Standing wastewater and solids are pumped and extracted first, since leaving contaminated water in place even a few extra hours increases how far bacteria migrate into building materials.
3. **Removal of non-salvageable materials.** Carpet, pad, and other porous materials that absorbed sewage are removed and bagged for disposal, since Category 3 water contamination generally can't be cleaned out of soft materials.
4. **Antimicrobial and enzymatic treatment.** Remaining hard surfaces, framing, and subfloor are treated with EPA-registered disinfectants and enzymatic cleaners formulated to break down organic waste and odor-causing bacteria, not just mask the smell.
5. **Drying, monitoring, and sanitization verification.** Structural drying equipment is set per IICRC S500 guidelines, and moisture readings are logged until the affected materials reach a safe, dry standard before any rebuild work begins.

## What separates a good sewage cleanup response from a bad one

The most common mistake in sewage cleanup is treating it like a standard water loss: extracting the water, running some fans, and calling it done. That approach leaves bacteria embedded in subflooring and wall cavities, which shows up later as persistent odor or a mold problem that traces straight back to the original backup. A thorough response documents the contamination category, photographs affected materials before removal, and keeps a disposal record for anything discarded, which matters both for health reasons and for insurance documentation.

Adjusters reviewing a sewage claim look for clear evidence that contaminated porous materials were removed rather than just cleaned, that antimicrobial treatment was applied and logged, and that drying was verified with moisture readings rather than assumed. Skipping any of those steps is where claims get questioned or where a homeowner ends up with a lingering odor months later.

## Seasonal & regional considerations

Houston's flat terrain and heavy clay soil make drainage sluggish, and the city's older neighborhoods often sit on sewer lines that have shifted or cracked over decades, which is a common source of recurring backups. Heavy rainfall during hurricane season adds another pressure point, since saturated ground and overwhelmed municipal lines can push wastewater back through household plumbing, especially in homes with older cast iron or clay sewer laterals. Homes on septic systems in outlying areas around Houston also see more overflow calls after extended wet stretches when the drain field can't absorb water fast enough.
