**Burst pipe in your Houston home right now?** Shut off the main water valve, kill power to any outlets near standing water, and call. We answer 24/7 for burst pipe cleanup and repair across the Houston area.

A pipe doesn't usually fail without warning. Galvanized supply lines corrode from the inside until a pinhole becomes a split. Copper work-hardens at a joint and cracks during a hard freeze. PEX fittings let go under water hammer. Whatever the cause, a full pipe break can push out dozens of gallons a minute, and in a two-story house that water finds its way through ceiling cavities, down wall chases, and under flooring before anyone notices the bill, the sound, or the stain. The longer a break runs, the more it behaves like a flood instead of a leak.

## What burst pipe cleanup and repair actually involves

This service covers two distinct jobs that have to happen in the right order. First, stopping and containing the water: locating the break, isolating the line, and extracting standing water with truck-mount or portable pumps before it migrates further into subfloor, drywall, and insulation. Second, drying the structure: cavity drying under flooring, wall chase ventilation where supply lines run inside framing, and moisture mapping with penetrating meters and thermal imaging so drying equipment targets the materials that are actually wet, not just the ones that look wet. Pipe break water is almost always clean (Category 1) at the moment of failure, but that classification degrades fast if it sits against drywall paper or wood framing for more than 24 to 48 hours. Timeline matters more here than on most other water losses because a pipe break is frequently discovered hours after it started, often from a wet ceiling spot or a spike on the water meter.

## Our process

1. **Source confirmation and isolation.** We find the actual break point, not just the wet spot, and confirm the line is fully shut down before any demolition or drying begins.
2. **Extraction.** Standing water comes out first, using pumps and extraction wands matched to the volume, whether that's a half inch on tile or water trapped under engineered flooring.
3. **Controlled access to the break.** Where the pipe runs inside a wall or under a slab, we open only what's needed to expose the damaged section and the wet materials around it, documenting the opening with photos for the claim file.
4. **Structural drying with monitoring.** Air movers, dehumidifiers, and sometimes injection drying systems for wall cavities go in, and moisture content gets logged daily per IICRC S500 guidelines until materials read dry, not just dry to the touch.
5. **Repair coordination.** Pipe repair itself (soldering, PEX replacement, coupling) is scheduled with a licensed plumber as needed; our scope covers the water damage response and drying, and we coordinate timing so drying isn't undone by a second release.

## What separates a good burst pipe response from a bad one

The most common mistake is drying the surface and closing the wall back up before the framing and subfloor actually read dry on a moisture meter. Drywall can feel dry within a day while the stud behind it holds water for a week, and that's exactly how hidden mold gets a head start. A second mistake is skipping wall cavity drying entirely and just running a few fans in the room, which does almost nothing for water that tracked down inside a stud bay or under a baseboard.

Adjusters look for documented moisture readings over time, not a single reading at the start. They also look for clear photos of the break point and the extent of water travel, since pipe breaks often cause damage far from where the break actually occurred, a second-floor break showing up as a first-floor ceiling stain is typical. A scope that only covers the visibly stained drywall without accounting for subfloor or insulation saturation tends to get kicked back for supplemental review later.

## Seasonal & regional considerations

Houston doesn't see the hard freezes that burst pipes in northern climates, but the region does get occasional cold snaps, and homes here are often built with supply lines run through uninsulated exterior walls or attic space never designed for freezing temperatures. A single overnight freeze can crack a line that then fails hours later once things thaw. Outside of freeze events, the more common cause locally is aging galvanized or polybutylene supply lines in homes built before the 1990s, along with high water pressure from municipal lines that stresses fittings over time.
