**Musty smell that won't go away, a water stain that's been there for months, or a home inspection report that flagged "possible moisture intrusion"?** Those are the three most common reasons Houston homeowners search for mold testing, and all three share the same problem: you can't see what's actually growing behind the drywall or under the subfloor without sampling the air and surfaces directly. A visual walk-through tells you where moisture has been. Testing tells you what's actually colonizing there and at what concentration.

## What mold inspection and testing actually involves

Mold inspection is a diagnostic process, not a cleanup process. A technician walks the property with a moisture meter and thermal imaging camera to find elevated readings in walls, flooring, and ceilings, especially around plumbing penetrations, exterior walls, and any area with a known or suspected water history. From there, air sampling and surface sampling determine what's actually in the air and on the material, not just where it's wet. Samples go to a third-party lab, and results typically come back within a few business days. The final report compares indoor spore counts to an outdoor baseline, which is the standard way labs determine whether indoor levels are elevated relative to what's naturally in the Houston air.

## Our process

1. **Visual inspection and moisture mapping.** We check attics, crawlspaces, cabinet interiors, and exterior wall cavities where humidity and past leaks tend to concentrate, using a moisture meter to flag readings that don't match the surrounding material.
2. **Outdoor baseline sample.** Before testing inside, we pull an outdoor air sample. This gives the lab something to compare indoor readings against, since Houston's humid climate means outdoor spore counts are rarely zero.
3. **Indoor air and surface sampling.** Spore trap air samples capture what's circulating in the HVAC system and living spaces. Where visible growth or suspect staining exists, we also take tape or swab samples directly off the material.
4. **Lab analysis.** Samples are sent to an accredited third-party lab for spore identification and count, not evaluated on-site, which keeps the results independent of whoever performed the testing.
5. **Written report and recommendation.** You receive a document that spells out sample locations, lab results, and whether remediation is warranted, along with a scope if it is.

## What separates a good mold inspection from a bad one

The most common shortcut is skipping the outdoor baseline sample. Without it, an indoor spore count is just a number; there's nothing to compare it to, and in Houston's climate that number alone can be misleading. Another frequent miss is testing only the room where the smell is strongest and ignoring return air ducts, which pull spores from throughout the house and often tell a more complete story. Timing matters too: testing right after running the AC on a dry setting for hours can artificially lower a count that would read differently on a humid afternoon. Insurance adjusters reviewing a mold claim typically want to see the moisture source identified, not just a spore count, since a positive test without a documented water intrusion event is harder to tie back to a covered loss.

## What does mold inspection and testing cost?

Typical costs depend on the size of the property, how many samples are collected, and whether lab rush processing is needed. Homeowners insurance will sometimes cover testing when it's tied to a covered water event, such as a burst pipe, but it typically does not cover testing performed for general peace of mind or a real estate transaction.

## Seasonal and regional considerations

Houston's humidity rarely drops below the range where mold can establish itself, and spore counts in the air outdoors run higher for much of the year than in drier climates. That makes the outdoor baseline comparison especially important here, since what's "normal" in Houston isn't the same as what's normal elsewhere. Homes built before widespread vapor barrier use, along with slab foundations common across the area, also create specific moisture pathways worth checking during an inspection, particularly where plumbing runs through the slab.
