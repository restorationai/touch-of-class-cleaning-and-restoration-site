## What Actually Matters When You're Choosing

The fastest way to separate a solid water damage company from a shop that just owns a few fans is to ask one question before anything else: are your technicians IICRC certified, and in which disciplines? The [IICRC](https://www.iicrc.org) sets the S500 standard for water damage restoration, covering everything from category classification (clean water, gray water, and Category 3 black water) to drying verification. A technician without that training may dry a space to the eye but leave moisture trapped behind baseboards or inside wall cavities, which is how a water claim turns into a mold claim weeks later.

Second, ask how the company documents the job for your insurance claim. Houston homeowners file a lot of water damage claims, from slab leaks in older Fort Bend-area homes to storm-driven water intrusion after a Gulf system. A company that provides moisture logs, drying equipment placement records, and daily readings gives your adjuster something concrete to work from, rather than a verbal summary. If you haven't started a claim yet, our [burst pipe emergency checklist](/blog/burst-pipe-emergency-checklist/) and guide to [what homeowners insurance actually covers](/blog/does-homeowners-insurance-cover-water-damage/) walk through what to document before the adjuster arrives.

Third, confirm the company's actual Houston-area coverage. A company based in one part of the metro may take longer reaching Baytown or The Woodlands than a company with established routes in that direction. For a property near Buffalo Bayou or in a known flood-prone stretch like Meyerland, local familiarity with which streets back up first isn't a nice-to-have, it changes how fast a crew gets ahead of standing water before it wicks into framing.

If you're still deciding which type of company you even need, our guide on [how to choose a restoration company](/blog/choosing-a-restoration-company/) covers the broader vetting checklist, and [what to do in the first 24 hours after water damage](/blog/what-to-do-first-24-hours-water-damage/) walks through the steps that matter most before any crew arrives.

Whoever you call, get a written scope of work before anything gets torn out, and ask how they verify a structure is fully dry rather than just "dry to the touch." Those two questions alone separate the companies on this list that treat drying as a measured process from ones treating it as a guess.
