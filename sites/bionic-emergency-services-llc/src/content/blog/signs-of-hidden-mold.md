If your house smells musty in one room, a stain keeps coming back after you clean it, or someone in the house has started coughing more at home than anywhere else, you likely have mold growing somewhere you can't see it. Hidden mold usually hides behind walls, under flooring, inside HVAC ducts, or in crawlspaces, anywhere moisture has been sitting quietly after a leak, a flood, or just Houston's humidity working on a poorly ventilated space. The fix starts with finding the moisture source, not just the mold. Here's how to recognize the signs, what to do first, and when the problem is bigger than a bottle of cleaner can handle.

## The 7 Signs Worth Paying Attention To

1. **A musty smell that doesn't go away.** Mold produces microbial volatile organic compounds, the source of that damp, earthy odor. If it's strongest in one closet, bathroom, or corner of a room, that's usually where the moisture source is.
2. **Discoloration on walls or ceilings.** Yellow, brown, or dark gray staining, especially in a ring pattern, often means water has been traveling through drywall or framing for a while.
3. **Peeling paint or bubbling drywall.** Moisture trapped behind the surface breaks the bond between paint and wall, or softens drywall paper. Mold frequently follows close behind.
4. **Warped or buckling flooring.** Laminate that's lifting at the seams, hardwood that's cupping, or tile that sounds hollow when tapped can mean water sitting under the surface, exactly the environment mold needs.
5. **Condensation on windows or AC vents.** In Houston's humidity, persistent condensation points to a ventilation or humidity problem that, left alone, becomes a mold problem inside wall cavities or ductwork.
6. **Allergy-like symptoms that improve when you leave the house.** Sneezing, itchy eyes, or a scratchy throat that shows up at home and eases elsewhere is worth paying attention to, though it's not a diagnosis, just a pattern worth noting.
7. **A recent leak, flood, or roof repair you didn't fully dry out.** Any past water event, even a minor one, is a reason to check behind the affected materials. Mold can begin colonizing drywall and wood within roughly 24 to 48 hours of sustained moisture.

## What To Do First

Start by finding and stopping the moisture source. Check under sinks, around toilet bases, behind the washing machine, at AC drip pans, and along the roofline for active leaks. If you find standing water or a slow drip, shut off the water supply to that fixture if possible, and get a fan or dehumidifier moving air through the space.

Next, document what you're seeing. Take photos of staining, discoloration, or visible growth before you touch or clean anything. If you end up filing an insurance claim, this record matters.

If the affected area is small (less than roughly 10 square feet) and on a non-porous surface like tile or glass, the EPA notes homeowners can often clean it themselves with water and detergent. Porous materials like drywall, carpet, or insulation that have been wet for more than a day or two usually need to be removed, not cleaned, because mold grows into the material itself.

## What Not To Do

- **Don't paint or caulk over a stain and call it handled.** Covering discoloration hides the visual sign but does nothing about the mold or moisture behind it.
- **Don't run a fan blowing directly on visible mold.** Moving air across active growth can send spores into rooms that were previously clean.
- **Don't rely on bleach for porous materials.** Bleach can lighten surface staining on drywall or wood without killing mold growing below the surface, giving a false sense that the problem is solved.
- **Don't ignore a small patch because it's "just in the closet."** Hidden mold behind a wall spreads along moisture, not along what's visible, and a small patch today can be a much larger remediation job in six months.
- **Don't skip ventilation while you investigate.** Keeping a space sealed and humid while you decide what to do only gives mold more time.

## When To Call a Professional

A few situations are worth a call rather than a DIY attempt:

- The affected area is larger than about 10 square feet.
- The growth is inside HVAC ductwork, since disturbing it can spread spores through the whole system.
- The source was a flood, sewage backup, or a leak that went undetected for more than a couple of days.
- You've cleaned a visible patch before and it came back in the same spot, which usually means the moisture source was never addressed.
- Someone in the household has a chronic respiratory condition and you want the area assessed before anyone spends more time in it.

## What the Remediation Process Actually Looks Like

Once a professional is involved, the general sequence is similar across most jobs: the affected area gets contained (often with plastic sheeting and negative air pressure) to keep spores from traveling, affected porous materials are removed and bagged for disposal, remaining surfaces are cleaned and treated, and the space is dried down with air movers and dehumidifiers until moisture readings confirm it's dry. Only after the moisture source itself is fixed, whether that's a pipe repair, a grading change, or a roof patch, does rebuilding the drywall or flooring make sense. Skipping that last step is the most common reason mold comes back.
