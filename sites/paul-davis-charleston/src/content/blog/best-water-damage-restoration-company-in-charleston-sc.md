## What Should You Ask a Water Damage Company Before Hiring?

The right questions separate a restoration company from a drying-only crew. Before you sign anything, ask these:

**Are your technicians IICRC-certified in water damage restoration (WRT)?** The [IICRC](https://www.iicrc.org) is the governing body for restoration standards. Certification means the technician has been trained to the S500 standard, not just handed a moisture meter.

**Do you provide a written scope of work before demolition begins?** Any legitimate company will document what needs to come out and why before touching a wall. This protects your insurance claim and your home.

**Do you bill the insurance company directly?** Many restoration companies handle assignment of benefits and direct billing. This reduces your out-of-pocket exposure and keeps the documentation chain clean.

**What equipment will you use, and how long will it stay?** A proper residential drying setup includes low-grain refrigerant (LGR) dehumidifiers, high-velocity air movers, and a moisture-monitoring log updated at every visit. If a company can't name their equipment or won't commit to daily monitoring, that's a red flag.

**Do you handle mold remediation if mold is found during drying?** In Charleston's humidity, mold growth after water damage is common. A company that handles both services under one roof avoids the coordination delays that let mold spread further. See our post on [how quickly mold grows after water damage](/blog/mold-after-water-damage/) for context on the timeline.

## How Does Water Damage Restoration Work?

Water damage restoration follows a defined process, regardless of which company you hire. Understanding the phases helps you evaluate whether a company is cutting corners.

**1. Emergency contact and inspection.** A technician arrives, assesses the source of water, and classifies the loss. Category 1 (clean water from a supply line), Category 2 (gray water from appliances or overflow), and Category 3 (black water from sewage or floodwater) each require different PPE and disposal protocols. Class of loss (1 through 4) describes how deeply the water has penetrated the materials.

**2. Water extraction.** Truck-mounted or portable extractors remove standing water. The faster this happens, the lower the class of loss and the less demolition is typically required.

**3. Demolition of unsalvageable materials.** Wet drywall, insulation, and flooring that cannot be dried in place come out. This is where a written scope matters most.

**4. Structural drying.** Air movers and LGR dehumidifiers run continuously, typically for 3 to 5 days on a Class 2 loss. Technicians take psychrometric readings at every visit and log them. Drying is complete when materials reach the IICRC S500 target moisture content for that material type.

**5. Mold prevention treatment.** An EPA-registered antimicrobial is applied to affected framing and cavities before any reconstruction begins.

**6. Reconstruction.** New drywall, insulation, flooring, and finishes go back in. Some companies handle this in-house; others subcontract it.

## Does Insurance Cover Water Damage Restoration in Charleston?

Most standard homeowners policies in South Carolina cover sudden and accidental water damage, such as a burst pipe or appliance failure, but exclude gradual leaks and flooding from storm surge or rising water (which requires a separate NFIP or private flood policy). The key documentation your insurer will want: the cause of loss, the moisture readings at discovery, the drying logs, and the written scope of work.

Filing a claim without that documentation is the most common reason adjusters reduce or deny water damage payouts. A restoration company that provides daily moisture logs and a detailed scope protects your claim from the start.
