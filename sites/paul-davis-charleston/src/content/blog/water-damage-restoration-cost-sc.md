## How Much Does Water Damage Restoration Cost in SC?

Most South Carolina homeowners pay between $1,500 and $8,000 for water damage restoration, with the national average hovering around $3,500 for a mid-sized residential loss. Coastal and low-country properties face additional risk from storm surge and high groundwater, which can push Category 2 and Category 3 losses that would otherwise be straightforward into significantly higher territory.

## What Factors Move the Price Up or Down?

Four variables account for most of the difference between a $2,000 job and a $15,000 job.

**Water category.** The [IICRC S500 standard](https://www.iicrc.org) classifies water losses on a three-category scale. Category 1 is clean water from a supply line or rain intrusion. Category 2 (gray water) comes from appliances, sump pump failures, or toilet tank overflows and carries biological contaminants. Category 3 (black water) is sewage, floodwater from outside, or any water that has sat long enough to become grossly contaminated. Cat 1 jobs are the least expensive. Cat 3 jobs require full PPE, antimicrobial treatment, and disposal of porous materials, which drives costs significantly higher.

**Class of loss (how deeply water penetrated).** IICRC also classifies losses by moisture penetration: Class 1 (minimal absorption, hard surfaces only) through Class 4 (deep saturation in dense materials like hardwood, concrete, or plaster). A Class 4 loss in a Charleston historic home with heart-pine floors and plaster walls costs more to dry than a Class 1 loss in a modern home with vinyl plank.

**Square footage and materials affected.** Carpet, drywall, and insulation are relatively inexpensive to replace. Hardwood floors, tile, cabinetry, and structural framing are not. A 200-square-foot bathroom loss and a 1,200-square-foot open-plan ground floor are categorically different jobs even if the water source is the same.

**Time elapsed before extraction.** Water that sits for 12 hours causes more damage than water extracted in the first two hours. Wet drywall that has begun to swell, delaminate, or grow mold must be removed rather than dried in place. Every hour of delay can shift a job from one cost tier to the next.

## What Does the Restoration Process Actually Involve?

Understanding the process helps you evaluate a contractor's scope and protects you from being oversold or undersold on what your loss actually requires.

1. **Emergency extraction.** Truck-mounted or portable extractors remove standing water. This is the first priority and the step where speed matters most.
2. **Moisture mapping.** Technicians use thermal imaging cameras and pin-type or pinless moisture meters to document how far water has migrated into walls, floors, and ceilings. This defines the drying zone and protects you during the insurance claim.
3. **Structural drying.** High-capacity LGR (low-grain refrigerant) dehumidifiers and axial or centrifugal air movers are deployed in a calculated pattern based on the affected square footage and material type. Drying typically takes 3 to 5 days for a standard residential loss, though dense materials can take longer. See our post on [how long water damage restoration actually takes](/blog/how-long-does-water-damage-restoration-take/) for a detailed breakdown.
4. **Demolition of unsalvageable materials.** Drywall, insulation, flooring, and cabinetry that cannot be dried to IICRC standard are removed. This is called "flood cut" drying when it involves cutting drywall above the waterline.
5. **Antimicrobial treatment.** Applied to structural cavities and surfaces after demolition to prevent secondary mold growth. This is standard on any Cat 2 or Cat 3 loss and on any loss where materials were wet for more than 24 hours.
6. **Final moisture verification.** Readings are documented before equipment is removed. A reputable contractor will not demobilize until moisture readings are at or below the regional equilibrium moisture content for the materials involved.
7. **Reconstruction.** Replacing drywall, flooring, cabinetry, and finishes. This phase is often billed separately from mitigation and is where costs can vary most based on material choices.

## Will Homeowners Insurance Cover This?

Most standard homeowners policies in South Carolina cover sudden and accidental water damage but exclude gradual leaks, flooding from outside, and maintenance-related failures. The distinction matters enormously for your out-of-pocket cost.

**Typically covered:** burst pipes, sudden appliance failures (washing machine hose, water heater), accidental overflow, and roof leaks caused by a covered peril (wind, hail).

**Typically not covered:** slow leaks that caused damage over time, sewer backup (unless you have a rider), and any flooding that originates outside the structure. Flood damage in South Carolina requires a separate NFIP (National Flood Insurance Program) policy or a private flood policy. FEMA's [National Flood Insurance Program](https://www.fema.gov/flood-insurance) covers the structure and contents separately, with different limits.

Documentation is what protects your claim. Moisture readings, photos taken before extraction begins, a written scope of work, and daily drying logs all support the adjuster's evaluation. A contractor who skips documentation is a liability to your claim, not an asset.

## What Should You Do Right Now?

If the water source is still active, stop it first: shut the main water supply valve, call your utility if it is a municipal line, or call a plumber if you cannot locate the shutoff. Once the source is controlled, the clock starts on mitigation.

Call a certified restoration contractor before you call your insurance company.
