## Restoration emergencies common in Kennewick

Kennewick sits in the high desert of the Columbia Basin, which means long, hot, dry summers followed by winters that can drop well below freezing for days at a time. That swing is hard on plumbing: pipes in exterior walls, crawlspaces, and unheated garages are vulnerable to freeze-and-burst events every winter, and a frozen supply line that lets go overnight can put standing water into a slab or crawlspace before anyone notices. Summer brings its own risk profile, with irrigation and sprinkler system failures, roof damage from windstorms that blow through the Yakima and Columbia valleys, and the occasional wildfire smoke event that settles into HVAC systems and soft materials across the region. Low annual rainfall also means many homes were never built with aggressive grading or drainage in mind, so a single hard rain or an appliance leak can sit against a foundation longer than it would in a wetter climate, which matters once mold or wood rot enters the picture.

## Services we provide in Kennewick

Water damage restoration is our most frequent call in Kennewick, often tied to a frozen or failed supply line, a water heater that lets go in a garage or utility closet, or an irrigation line that backs up against a foundation wall. We extract standing water, pull moisture readings from flooring and framing, and set drying equipment to bring materials back to a dry standard rather than guessing at when a space feels dry to the touch.

Fire and smoke damage restoration covers everything from a contained kitchen fire to whole-structure smoke and soot penetration, including the odor that settles into drywall, insulation, and HVAC ductwork long after the flames are out.

Mold remediation addresses the colonies that take hold after a slow leak goes unnoticed in a crawlspace or behind a shower wall, which happens more often in Kennewick's older housing stock where ventilation wasn't built to today's standard.

Storm damage restoration responds to wind, hail, and water intrusion from the windstorms that move through the Tri-Cities, including tarping and drying out a structure once the roof or siding has been compromised.

Biohazard cleanup handles trauma, sewage backup, and other contamination events that require trained handling and proper disposal, separate from standard water or fire cleanup.

## Building stock, site conditions, and permits in Kennewick

Much of Kennewick's housing dates from the postwar growth years through the 1970s and 80s, with a mix of slab-on-grade and crawlspace construction and a good number of mid-century homes still running original galvanized or early copper plumbing. Those systems are past their expected service life in many cases, and galvanized pipe in particular is prone to internal corrosion that leads to pinhole leaks rather than a single dramatic failure. Homes built before the late 1970s may also carry asbestos-containing materials in flooring, siding, or duct insulation, which changes how a restoration scope has to be handled once walls or flooring are opened up. The regional soil is largely sandy and well-draining over basalt bedrock, which generally limits standing groundwater problems but does little to slow surface water from reaching a foundation during a sprinkler or plumbing failure. Permits for structural repair or rebuild work are issued through the City of Kennewick's building department, and most jobs involving framing, drywall replacement beyond a minor patch, or electrical work in a affected area will trigger a permit review.
