**If a pipe has burst, turn off your main water shutoff valve first, then cut power to any affected outlets or breakers before you do anything else.** The shutoff valve is usually near the water meter, in a basement, crawlspace access, or utility closet. Once the water is off, open a faucet downstream to relieve pressure in the line, then start moving anything you can lift off the floor. Everything after that, drying, insurance, repair, matters less than those first two minutes.

## Why This Happens in Pasco

Most burst pipes around Pasco trace back to one of two things: a hard freeze hitting a line that runs through an exterior wall or unheated crawlspace, or a supply line failure behind a washing machine, dishwasher, or water heater. The Columbia Basin doesn't freeze every week, but when a cold snap does roll through, pipes in older homes with galvanized or thin-walled copper plumbing are the ones that split. Newer slab-foundation homes aren't immune either; a cracked PEX fitting under a slab can leak for days before anyone notices a damp spot on the carpet.

Appliance supply lines deserve their own mention because they fail differently. A rubber or braided line on a washing machine or ice maker can blow out with no warning at all, dumping gallons per minute directly onto flooring and cabinetry. If that's your situation, the shutoff step below is identical, but the appliance's own valve may let you stop the leak without touching the home's main line.

## Immediate Steps: The First 30 Minutes

1. **Shut off the main water valve.** If you don't know where it is, find it today, before you need it in a panic. Label it.
2. **Kill power to the area if water is near outlets, switches, or a breaker panel.** Water and electricity sharing a room is a hazard, not a cleanup task.
3. **Contain what you can.** Towels, a shop vac, a trash can under a drip, anything that slows the spread while you figure out next steps.
4. **Move furniture, rugs, and boxes off wet flooring.** Wood furniture legs bleed stain into wet carpet within an hour; paper and cardboard wick water and start softening almost immediately.
5. **Photograph everything before you move or discard anything.** Wide shots of the room, close-ups of the source, and the water line on walls or baseboards. This is the documentation an insurance adjuster will ask for later.
6. **Open closet doors and cabinet doors** under sinks or near the leak so trapped air can start moving and surfaces aren't sealed against damp wood.

## What Not to Do

- **Don't run a space heater or heat gun on a frozen pipe that hasn't burst yet.** Thawing a line unevenly builds pressure right at the weak point and can turn a slow drip into a full rupture. Warm the room gradually instead.
- **Don't flip breakers back on** in a room with standing water, even if the power was already off when the leak started.
- **Don't wait to see if it dries on its own.** Water behind drywall or under flooring can look fine at the surface while the material underneath stays saturated. Drywall, insulation, and subfloor hold moisture long after the visible puddle is gone, and that's exactly the condition mold needs to take hold.
- **Don't throw out wet materials before photographing them.** Once drywall, baseboard, or flooring goes in the dumpster, that's one less piece of evidence for the claim.
- **Don't assume a shutoff fixes the underlying problem.** A pipe that burst once from freezing or age is a signal the line (or the insulation around it) needs a real look, not just a patch.

## When to Call a Professional

A plumber stops the leak at the source. A water damage restoration team handles everything the leak left behind, and those are two different jobs that often need to happen back to back. Call a restoration professional if any of the following apply:

- Water reached flooring, baseboards, or drywall and sat for more than an hour
- You can hear or feel water under laminate, hardwood, or tile when you walk on it
- The leak happened in a crawlspace, behind a wall, or under a slab where you can't see the full extent
- There's any sign of water near electrical panels, outlets, or recessed lighting
- It's been more than 24 to 48 hours since the leak and the area still feels damp

Past that 24 to 48 hour mark, you're no longer just drying out water, you're racing mold colonization, which can start in wall cavities and under flooring faster than most homeowners expect. Equipment matters here too: moisture meters and infrared cameras find saturation inside walls and subfloor that a hand on the carpet never will, and materials get dried per the IICRC S500 standard rather than guessed at by feel.

If the source was a washing machine, dishwasher, ice maker, or water heater line rather than the home's main plumbing, that falls under appliance leak cleanup rather than a whole-house pipe failure, but the drying and documentation process is largely the same.

## The Recovery Process After the Water Is Off

Once the active leak is stopped, recovery usually runs through a few predictable stages. First, extraction: pulling standing water out of carpet, padding, and hard flooring with pumps and extractors rather than towels and fans, which only move moisture around. Second, assessment: moisture mapping the affected rooms to find out how far water traveled inside walls and under flooring, since visible damage is rarely the full footprint. Third, structural drying: placing air movers and dehumidifiers to pull moisture out of framing, subfloor, and drywall over several days, with moisture readings checked along the way rather than guessed at. Fourth, repair: replacing drywall, baseboard, flooring, or cabinetry that didn't survive, and in some cases coordinating that rebuild work with your insurance adjuster directly.

Throughout that process, keep your photos, a written timeline of when the leak started and when water was shut off, and any receipts for emergency supplies like a shop vac rental or fans. Most carriers want that documentation before they'll move on a claim, and having it ready speeds up the part of this you actually want to be over quickly.
