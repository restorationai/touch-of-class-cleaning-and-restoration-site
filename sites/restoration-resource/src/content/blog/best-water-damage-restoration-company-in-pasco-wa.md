## What Should You Actually Check Before Hiring Anyone?

Verify three things before signing anything: an active IICRC Certified Firm status, a written scope of work before mitigation starts, and a clear answer on who's handling your insurance claim paperwork. A company that won't put the drying plan in writing is a company you'll be arguing with later about what was actually done.

Ask directly whether the technicians on-site hold IICRC Water Damage Restoration Technician (WRT) certification, not just the company as a whole. Ask how they classify the water loss (Category 1, 2, or 3) and what that classification means for what gets removed versus dried in place. If the answer is vague, keep calling. For a deeper walkthrough of this vetting process, see [How To Choose a Restoration Company](/blog/choosing-a-restoration-company/), and if the water is still active right now, start with [What To Do in the First 24 Hours After Water Damage](/blog/what-to-do-first-24-hours-water-damage/).

If you're not sure your homeowners policy even covers the type of loss you have, [Does Homeowners Insurance Cover Water Damage?](/blog/does-homeowners-insurance-cover-water-damage/) walks through what's typically covered versus excluded before you call anyone.

Whoever you choose, get the scope of work in writing, confirm their IICRC credentials directly, and document the damage with photos before cleanup starts. That paper trail is what protects your claim later, regardless of which company's van shows up in your driveway.
