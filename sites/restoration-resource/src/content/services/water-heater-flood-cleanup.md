A leaking or burst water heater behaves differently than a slow plumbing leak. Tanks fail in two ways: a slow weep from a corroded seam that soaks a closet or garage floor for days before anyone notices, or a sudden rupture at the bottom seam that empties the full tank almost instantly. Either way, the water is typically Category 1 (clean) at the moment of failure, but that classification doesn't last. If it's been sitting against drywall or under vinyl flooring for more than 24 to 48 hours, or if it picked up anything from a garage floor, it can shift toward Category 2 and change how we handle disposal and material removal.

## What water heater flood cleanup actually involves

The work starts with stopping the source, usually by confirming the shutoff valve is closed, then moving fast on extraction before water wicks further into flooring and framing. Tank failures most often show up in utility closets, garages, and basements, locations where the water heater sits near drywall, baseboards, and sometimes finished flooring that sits flush against the unit. We use submersible pumps and truck-mounted extraction for standing water, then switch to air movers and dehumidifiers once the bulk volume is gone. Moisture meters and thermal imaging tell us what's actually wet behind walls and under flooring, which matters because surface-dry materials can still be saturated a few inches in. Most residential water heater losses dry out in three to five days, though it depends on how far the water traveled and what it's sitting against.

## Our process

1. **Source confirmation and safety check.** We verify the water heater is shut off and isolated before extraction starts, and check for electrical hazards if the unit sits near outlets or a breaker panel.
2. **Water extraction.** Pumps and extraction wands pull standing and absorbed water from flooring, baseboards, and any adjacent carpet or pad.
3. **Moisture mapping.** Meters and thermal imaging identify how far water traveled, including under cabinetry, behind drywall, and into subfloor near the unit.
4. **Structural drying.** Air movers and dehumidifiers are placed per a drying plan built around the affected materials, with daily moisture readings logged until materials hit dry standard.
5. **Material removal, if needed.** Saturated drywall, insulation, or flooring that won't dry in place gets removed and documented for the claim before any rebuild begins.

## What separates a good water heater flood response from a bad one

The most common mistake is treating a water heater leak like a simple mop-up job. Operators who skip moisture mapping often miss water that tracked under a subfloor seam or into an adjoining wall cavity, which shows up weeks later as a musty smell or soft flooring. Another frequent miss: not checking the water heater's drain pan and overflow line, which can mean water traveled further than the visible puddle suggests. Insurance adjusters look for documented moisture readings at the start and end of drying, not just a before-and-after photo, so a thin paper trail can slow down or shrink a claim. Our IICRC-trained technicians (WRT and ASD certified) document moisture content at each affected material before, during, and after drying, which is the record adjusters actually want to see.
