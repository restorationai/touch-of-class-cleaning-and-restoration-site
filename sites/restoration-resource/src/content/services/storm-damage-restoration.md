## What Storm Damage Restoration actually involves

Storm damage restoration covers the stretch of work between "the storm just passed" and "the structure is dry, stable, and documented for your insurer." That usually means emergency roof tarping or board-up to stop ongoing water intrusion, extraction of any water that made it inside, moisture mapping of walls and subfloor with thermal imaging and penetrating meters, and structural drying equipment set to pull hidden moisture out of framing cavities before mold has a chance to establish. Where a tree limb or debris has compromised siding, a window, or a section of roofline, we also handle safe debris clearing and temporary weatherproofing so the building isn't exposed to the next weather system while repairs are scheduled. Timelines vary with the scope of water intrusion, but structural drying typically runs several days and is monitored daily, not guessed at.

## Our process

1. **Emergency stabilization.** Tarping exposed roof sections, boarding broken windows or doors, and bracing or removing debris that's actively threatening the structure.
2. **Water intrusion assessment.** Checking attic insulation, top-plate framing, and wall cavities for moisture that wind-driven rain pushed past the exterior envelope, not just the obvious puddle on the floor.
3. **Extraction and structural drying.** Removing standing water, then setting air movers and dehumidifiers to dry framing and subfloor per IICRC S500 drying standards, with moisture readings logged until materials hit dry targets.
4. **Debris removal and damage documentation.** Clearing downed branches or storm debris from the roof and yard while photographing and measuring every affected area for the claim file.
5. **Handoff for permanent repair.** Once the structure is dry and stable, we provide a written scope so roofing, siding, or other permanent repairs can be scheduled with your insurer.

## What separates a good storm damage response from a bad one

The mistake we see most often isn't the obvious damage, it's what gets missed behind it. A tarp that stops visible leaking can still leave saturated insulation drying slowly inside a closed wall cavity for weeks, which is exactly the environment mold needs. A rushed debris removal crew that doesn't check for cracked rafters or a compromised ridge beam can clear the yard and leave a structural problem standing. Adjusters are looking for moisture logs, drying equipment placement records, and dated photos that tie the damage directly to the storm event, not just a invoice for labor. A thorough response documents the water path from entry point to drying completion, because that record is what gets a claim paid without a second inspection fight.

Costs depend heavily on how much water got in, how long it sat, and whether structural materials like framing or subfloor were affected. Pasco sits in the Columbia Basin, where open farmland and few windbreaks let spring and fall windstorms build speed before they hit rooftops in town.
