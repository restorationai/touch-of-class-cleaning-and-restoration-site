**Flooding in your home right now?** Call now for emergency service. Standing water that enters from outside the structure, whether from a swollen irrigation canal, river overflow, or a storm drain backing up, behaves differently than a burst supply line. It carries silt, bacteria, and sometimes chemical runoff into your walls and flooring within minutes, and the clock on mold colonization starts the moment water stops moving.

## What flood damage restoration actually involves

Flood damage restoration deals with water that originated outside the home's plumbing system, which almost always means it needs to be treated as Category 2 (gray) or Category 3 (black) water rather than the clean-water Category 1 losses from a supply line failure. That distinction drives everything downstream: what materials can be dried in place versus removed, what PPE a crew wears on site, and what antimicrobial treatment is required before any rebuild work starts. A typical flood response involves truck-mounted or portable extraction equipment to pull standing water first, moisture mapping with penetrating and non-penetrating meters to find where water has traveled inside wall cavities and under flooring, and a drying plan that's monitored daily rather than assumed. Depending on how much water intruded and how long it sat before the crew arrived, the drying phase can run several days to over a week.

## Our process

1. **Safety check and water categorization.** Before extraction starts, the crew confirms electrical safety and classifies the water source, since contaminated flood water changes every step that follows.
2. **Extraction of standing water.** Pumps and extractors remove bulk water from floors, crawlspaces, and basements, prioritizing areas where water depth or sitting time is highest.
3. **Removal of compromised porous materials.** Carpet padding, baseboards, and drywall that absorbed contaminated water typically can't be salvaged and are removed to stop the material from becoming a longer-term mold or odor source.
4. **Antimicrobial treatment.** Affected framing, subfloor, and remaining structure are treated to address bacterial and mold risk before drying equipment goes in.
5. **Monitored structural drying.** Air movers and dehumidifiers are placed and tracked against daily moisture readings until the structure reads dry per the IICRC S500 standard, not just by feel or guesswork.

## What separates a good flood damage response from a bad one

The most common mistake is treating flood water like a clean-water loss because the water looks clear on the surface. Gray and black water carry contamination that isn't visible, and skipping the categorization step means porous materials get dried and reinstalled when they should have been removed, which shows up later as odor or microbial growth behind a wall that was supposedly dried. A second common miss is drying to a surface-dry condition and pulling equipment early; subfloor and framing can still hold moisture well after the top layer feels dry. Insurance adjusters reviewing a flood claim typically want to see documented moisture readings over time, photos of material removal decisions, and a clear record of the water's classification, not just a final invoice. A written scope that ties each line item back to a documented condition holds up far better during claim review than a generic cleanup description.

## What does flood damage restoration cost?

Costs vary widely based on how much water intruded, how contaminated it was, and how long it sat before extraction started. Every loss is different, and Restoration Resource provides a written scope of work before any restoration begins so there are no surprises once the drying equipment is already running. Homeowners insurance typically covers flood damage restoration when the water source is internal (like a failed appliance or pipe), but water that enters from outside the home, such as river or canal overflow, is often only covered under a separate flood insurance policy, so it's worth checking your policy type before assuming coverage.
