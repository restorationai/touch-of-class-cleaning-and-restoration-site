A water bill that's climbed for no reason, a warm spot on the slab, a musty smell behind the baseboard that won't go away no matter how many times you mop. These are the quiet signals of a hidden water leak, and by the time they're noticeable, the leak has usually been running for weeks. Water leak detection finds the source before it turns into a drywall tear-out, a mold problem, or a slab that needs to be cored open.

## What water leak detection actually involves

Leak detection is diagnostic work, not demolition. The goal is to pinpoint the exact location of a hidden water leak, whether it's in a wall cavity, under a slab, behind a shower pan, or inside a crawlspace, without opening up more of the structure than necessary. Technicians use a combination of thermal imaging cameras (which read temperature differentials in flooring and drywall), acoustic listening equipment (which picks up the sound of pressurized water moving through pipe under a slab), and moisture meters that confirm what the other tools suggest. For suspected slab leak detection, this often includes isolating sections of the plumbing system to narrow down which line is pressurized and losing water. A straightforward interior wall leak can be located in under an hour; a slab leak in an older Pasco home with cast iron or galvanized supply lines sometimes takes longer because the pipe routing isn't always where the original plans say it is.

## Our process

1. **Intake and symptom review.** We ask about water bill history, visible staining, sounds of running water, and which fixtures or areas prompted the call. This narrows the search area before any equipment comes out.
2. **Non-invasive scanning.** Thermal imaging and acoustic sensors scan walls, floors, and slab areas to identify temperature and sound anomalies consistent with a hidden water leak in wall cavities or under flooring.
3. **Moisture meter confirmation.** Once a probable zone is flagged, moisture meters and, where needed, a borescope confirm the finding before any cutting or coring happens.
4. **Point-of-access recommendation.** We mark the smallest reasonable access point, typically a single drywall section or a localized slab core, rather than opening an entire wall or room.
5. **Written findings.** You get a report documenting the leak location, likely cause, and what repair work (plumbing, drywall, flooring) will follow, which also becomes part of your insurance documentation if you file a claim.

## What separates a good leak detection response from a bad one

The most common mistake is guessing instead of confirming. A contractor who cuts open three sections of drywall "to be sure" costs you more in repair work than the detection should ever have required. A good leak detection service narrows the search electronically first and treats destructive access as a last step, not a first one.

The second mistake is stopping at the leak itself without checking how long it's been running. Water behind plaster or under flooring can read dry at the surface while the framing or subfloor underneath stays saturated, and moisture that's been sitting for more than 24 to 48 hours starts creating conditions where mold can take hold. A thorough leak detection visit checks surrounding materials, not just the source point, and flags anything that needs drying or further evaluation.

Insurance adjusters reviewing a leak-related claim generally want to see when the leak was found, how it was located, and whether water intrusion was ongoing or a single event. Documentation that includes thermal images, moisture readings, and a dated report carries more weight than a verbal description of "we found a leak and fixed it."

## What does water leak detection cost?

Costs vary with how accessible the suspected leak is and whether it's a wall leak versus a slab leak, since slab work generally requires more specialized acoustic equipment and time. These are typical industry ranges, not a quoted price; every property and plumbing layout is different, and Restoration Resource provides a written scope before any work begins. Homeowners insurance often covers the cost of locating a leak and the resulting damage if the leak was sudden and accidental, though gradual leaks from long-term wear are sometimes excluded, so it's worth checking your policy language.

## Seasonal & regional considerations

Pasco's older neighborhoods still have homes running on galvanized and early copper supply lines, both of which corrode from the inside out over decades and are common sources of slab leaks. Freeze-thaw cycles during Columbia Basin winters can also stress exposed or poorly insulated piping, particularly in crawlspaces and exterior walls, leading to small cracks that leak long before they burst outright. Irrigation season brings a second wave of calls, as buried irrigation lines and backflow assemblies develop slow leaks that show up as soggy lawn patches or unexplained water bill spikes.
