## Why Paterson Properties See Water Damage Issues

Most of the water losses we see around Paterson trace back to infrastructure built for irrigation, not plumbing failure in the traditional sense. Center-pivot lines, drip systems, and buried agricultural feeder pipes run close to homes and outbuildings, and a cracked fitting can push water into a foundation for days before it's caught, especially on properties where nobody's checking the back forty daily. Winters here bring hard freezes that catch exposed well lines and hose bibs off guard, and because humidity is normally low, homeowners sometimes underestimate how much water has gotten into wall cavities or subfloor until they see staining or smell something musty. The same arid climate that usually works in a property's favor, fast evaporation, low ambient moisture, can also mean surface materials dry quickly on top while the insulation or subfloor underneath stays wet for weeks if it isn't professionally checked.

## Our Water Damage Restoration Process in Paterson

We start by finding the actual boundary of the water, not just where it's visible. On crawl space homes that's often a mix of moisture mapping and probing insulation, since batt insulation and dirt-floor crawl spaces common in older rural construction here hold moisture long after the surface reads dry. Extraction comes first, pulling standing water with truck-mount or portable extractors depending on access, followed by structural drying set up to the IICRC S500 standard: axial air movers positioned to move air across wet materials, not just blow air into a room, and low-grain-refrigerant dehumidifiers sized to the actual cubic footage of the affected space. We monitor daily with moisture meters rather than guessing based on how a wall feels, because in this climate a wall can feel dry to the touch while the stud behind it is still saturated.
