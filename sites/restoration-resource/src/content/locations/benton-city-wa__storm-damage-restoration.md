## Why Benton City Properties See Storm Damage

Benton City sits in a high-desert wind corridor where gusts funnel down through the Horse Heaven Hills and across the Yakima River valley with little to slow them down. Annual rainfall is low, but when a thunderstorm cell does move through, it tends to arrive fast and hard, dropping hail or a short, intense downpour on ground that isn't built to shed water quickly. Add in the area's irrigation canals and ditches that run through or near residential lots, and a heavy storm can push standing water toward foundations that were never designed for it. Many homes in and around Benton City are older ranch-style or manufactured construction with lighter roof framing than newer Tri-Cities builds, which means wind uplift and shingle loss happen at lower wind speeds than you'd expect in a denser urban area.

## Our Storm Damage Restoration Process in Benton City

The first call is always about stopping active damage: a tarp over an open roof section, board-up on a blown-out window, or extraction if wind-driven rain has already made it inside. From there, we document everything with photos and moisture readings before any material comes out, because that documentation is what your adjuster will ask for. Water intrusion from wind-driven rain behaves differently than a plumbing leak; it tends to travel along framing and insulation rather than pooling in one spot, so our drying plan accounts for hidden pockets in attic spaces and wall cavities, not just the visible wet drywall. Structural drying follows IICRC S500 guidelines, and any downed limbs or tree debris against the structure get cleared before we assess what's underneath, since a tree resting on a roofline can be masking additional damage that isn't obvious from the ground.
