## Why West Richland Properties See Water Damage Issues

The high desert climate around the Tri-Cities swings hard between summer heat and winter cold snaps, and that swing is one of the most common reasons we get called out here. A hard freeze followed by a quick thaw is a classic setup for a burst supply line or a split hose bib, especially in homes where pipes run through an exterior wall or an unheated garage. Combine that with West Richland's irrigation-heavy landscaping, much of it fed by pressurized irrigation separate from domestic water, and you get a second common failure point: irrigation lines and backflow assemblies that freeze, crack, and leak into foundations during the shoulder seasons. We also see water damage tied to washing machine supply lines and water heater tanks in newer construction, where builders often use flexible braided connectors that degrade faster than rigid copper.

## Our Water Damage Restoration Process in West Richland

The first thing our crew does on arrival is identify the water category and the extent of migration, not just the visible wet spot. On slab homes, that means moisture mapping along the base of walls in rooms adjacent to the source, since slab water travels under flooring far beyond where it first appeared. We extract standing water with truck-mounted or portable extraction equipment, then set containment and drying plans based on what's actually wet rather than what's visibly damaged. Structural drying on a slab-on-grade home typically runs longer than on a home with a vented crawlspace, because the concrete itself holds and slowly releases moisture back into the air for days after the surface reads dry. We monitor with moisture meters and thermal imaging throughout the dry-out and document readings for the insurance file.

## Local note

One detail we watch for specifically in West Richland: homes built during the 2000s-2010s expansion often used PEX plumbing run through tight wall cavities with minimal insulation at exterior-facing runs. In a hard freeze, those runs are more likely to split than older rigid copper, and because PEX failures tend to be pinhole leaks rather than clean breaks, the leak can run for hours before anyone notices the pressure drop or the stain on drywall.
