**Storm damage in Prosser?** We answer 24/7, and when wind off the Horse Heaven Hills peels back a roof or hail shreds siding on a Yakima Valley property, the clock on secondary damage starts immediately. Restoration Resource handles storm damage restoration for homes and ag-adjacent properties throughout Prosser, from emergency tarping through structural drying and rebuild coordination.

## Why Prosser Properties See Storm Damage Issues

Prosser sits in a wind corridor. Air funnels down through the Horse Heaven Hills and across the open vineyard and orchard land that surrounds the city, and that terrain produces gusts strong enough to lift composition shingles, rack metal roofing on outbuildings, and snap limbs off the older shade trees found along the residential streets near downtown. Summer thunderstorms rolling through the Columbia Basin can bring fast hail with very little warning, and because much of Prosser's housing stock includes manufactured homes and older stick-built houses from the mid-20th century, roof coverings and flashing details are often past their design life before a storm ever hits. Add in irrigation canals and drainage ditches that serve the surrounding farmland, and a hard rain event can push standing water toward foundations faster than the ground can absorb it, especially on properties near the Yakima River floodplain.

## Our Storm Damage Restoration Process in Prosser

The first call is about stopping further loss. If a roof is breached or a window is blown out, our crew tarps or boards the opening to keep wind-driven rain from soaking insulation and framing. From there we extract standing water, pull wet insulation and drywall that won't dry in place, and set up drying equipment calibrated to the structure, not a one-size-fits-all setup. Structural drying follows the IICRC S500 standard, with moisture readings tracked daily so we know when framing and subfloor are actually dry rather than just surface-dry. For properties with detached shops, barns, or equipment buildings, which are common on Prosser's larger lots, we inspect those structures separately since metal roofing and pole-building framing respond to wind uplift differently than a conventional house roof.

## Prosser Insurance & HOA Coordination

Most Prosser properties we see are standalone parcels rather than HOA-governed developments, which simplifies the approval side of storm work considerably. What takes more coordination is documenting damage across multiple structures on one claim, a house, a detached garage, a hobby vineyard trellis, since carriers often want separate photo documentation and scope for each. We photograph and log damage by structure and material before any tarping or debris removal happens, which keeps the claim file clean and reduces back-and-forth with the adjuster later. As an IICRC Certified Firm, we document drying and structural work to the standard most carriers expect, which helps keep the claims process moving.

## Local note

Outbuildings on Prosser's vineyard and orchard properties are frequently finished with screw-down metal panel roofing rather than standing-seam, and after a wind event those panels can look intact from the ground while several fasteners have actually backed out or sheared. We check panel attachment point by point on these structures rather than relying on a visual pass, because a panel that looks fine today can lift in the next gust if the fastening wasn't caught the first time.
