**Mold in a house that barely gets rain?** It happens more in Highland than most homeowners expect. The high desert climate around the Mid-Columbia Basin keeps outdoor humidity low most of the year, but irrigated lawns, evaporative cooling systems, and slab or crawlspace moisture can create pockets of trapped humidity indoors that outdoor weather never hints at. By the time a musty smell reaches the living room, the colony behind the drywall or under the subfloor has usually had weeks to establish itself.

## Why Highland Properties See Mold Issues

Eastern Washington's arid climate is deceptive. Low ambient humidity doesn't mean low risk, it means the moisture problems that do occur are often hidden rather than obvious. Homes built with crawlspace foundations, common throughout the agricultural communities of the Columbia Basin, can trap ground moisture from irrigation runoff or a slow plumbing leak for months without a visible sign at the surface. Evaporative coolers, popular in this region because of the dry summer heat, push humid air into attic spaces and wall cavities if ductwork isn't sealed correctly, and that moisture condenses in places that rarely see direct airflow. Add an older water heater, an undetected roof flashing failure, or a basement that floods seasonally from snowmelt runoff, and the growth conditions are there even in a climate that looks bone dry on paper.

## Our Mold Remediation Process in Highland

We start with a moisture map, not a guess. Using moisture meters and, where warranted, a borescope to check wall cavities, we find out whether the growth is isolated to a bathroom exhaust failure or tied to a structural moisture source like a crawlspace vapor barrier that's failed. Containment goes up before any disturbance begins, negative air pressure keeps spores from traveling into HVAC returns or adjoining rooms, which matters more in homes with forced-air systems tied to evaporative cooling add-ons. Affected materials that can't be cleaned, usually porous drywall or insulation, are removed and bagged for disposal. Remaining surfaces are treated and HEPA-vacuumed, and we dry the area to a target moisture content before closing anything back up. The goal is a verified dry structure, not just a surface that looks clean.

## Equipment & Methods We Use for Mold

Containment barriers, HEPA air scrubbers, and negative air machines are standard on every job regardless of size, because cross-contamination is the most common way a small mold problem becomes a whole-house problem. For crawlspace jobs, which are common in this region's older farmhouse-style construction, we often pair remediation with a vapor barrier repair or replacement, since treating the mold without addressing the ground moisture underneath it just resets the clock. Our technicians hold IICRC AMRT mold remediation certification, and drying is tracked against the IICRC S500 standard rather than a visual check, so the structure's moisture content, not just its appearance, determines when a job is finished.

## Local note

One thing we watch for specifically in this part of Washington: homes retrofitted with swamp coolers often have attic ductwork that was never fully sealed when the system was installed, and that gap becomes a slow moisture source that shows up as mold on the north-facing attic sheathing long before it's visible from inside the house. If your attic access hasn't been opened in a while and you've got an evaporative cooling unit, it's worth a look even without a visible leak anywhere else.
