Kahlotus sits in Franklin County's dry wheat country, where low humidity, summer heat, and wind off the Snake River breaks combine to push grass and field fires toward homes and barns faster than most insurance adjusters expect. Many properties here rely on propane tanks, wood stoves, or older electrical panels original to the structure, and rural fire districts covering wide territory mean a working structure fire can burn longer before suppression starts. That extra burn time changes the restoration job: soot penetrates deeper into framing, and heat can warp metal roofing and siding on outbuildings as badly as the main house.

## Our Fire Damage Restoration Process in Kahlotus

We start with a structural safety check before anyone goes past the front door, since rural fires often damage load-bearing elements that aren't obvious from outside. From there, our crew separates salvageable contents from total loss, runs HEPA air scrubbers to pull soot particulate before it resettles, and treats smoke odor at the source rather than masking it, because open country air movement tends to carry smoke smell back into a house through gaps around older windows and doors. Structural drying and deodorizing follow IICRC S700 fire restoration guidelines, with special attention to crawlspaces and outbuildings common on Kahlotus properties. We document every step with photos for the insurance file, since rural claims often involve detached structures, fencing, or equipment loss alongside the home itself.
