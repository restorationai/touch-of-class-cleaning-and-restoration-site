## Why Kennewick Properties See Water Damage Issues

Much of Kennewick sits on basalt bedrock and caliche soil, which is part of why slab-on-grade construction is so common here compared to basement-heavy markets. Slab homes hide plumbing failures differently than a basement would: a cracked supply line under the slab can push water sideways through the foundation and up into baseboards and flooring before anyone notices a drop in water pressure. Add in the region's hard freeze-thaw cycles each winter, when overnight lows drop hard after mild days, and you get a steady pattern of split copper lines and frozen hose bibs that don't show themselves until a thaw sends water across the floor. Irrigation season brings its own version of the problem: older sprinkler systems and aging backflow preventers tied into Kennewick Irrigation District lines can leak slowly against a foundation for weeks before a wall or slab finally shows staining.

## Our Water Damage Restoration Process in Kennewick

The first call is about stopping the source and assessing what the slab and wall cavities are holding, not just what's visible on the surface. We extract standing water with truck-mounted or portable units depending on access, then use moisture meters to map how far water has traveled through drywall, baseboards, and subfloor, since slab construction often pushes moisture laterally rather than down. Structural drying follows the IICRC S500 standard, with air movers and dehumidifiers placed based on those moisture readings rather than a flat room count. In Kennewick's drier climate, ambient humidity is usually on our side for faster drying days, but hot summer afternoons can still slow equipment performance inside a closed-up house, so we monitor and adjust rather than set it and walk away.
