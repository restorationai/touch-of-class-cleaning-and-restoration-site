A burst irrigation line or a failed water heater behaves differently in Highland than it does on the wet side of the state. ## Why Highland Properties See Water Damage Issues

Most of the water damage calls we get from this part of the state trace back to a handful of regional patterns rather than storm flooding. Irrigation lines feeding rural and semi-rural properties fail or freeze and thaw with the season, sending water into crawl spaces before anyone notices a drop in pressure. Older well pump setups and pressure tanks can also fail quietly in a utility room or basement, soaking subfloor for days before the smell or the sagging floor gives it away. Because the regional soil tends to be clay-dense, water that gets into a crawl space doesn't drain the way it would in sandier ground elsewhere in the state. It sits, it wicks up into floor joists, and it sets up the right conditions for secondary damage and microbial growth faster than most homeowners expect.

## Our Water Damage Restoration Process in Highland

We start every job with moisture mapping, not guesswork. Our techs use moisture meters and thermal imaging to find out how far water has traveled behind walls and under flooring before we commit to a drying plan, since what's visible at the baseboard is rarely the whole story. Extraction comes first, pulling standing water out of crawl spaces, carpet, and pad with truck-mounted or portable extraction equipment depending on access. From there we set air movers and dehumidifiers calibrated to the square footage and the material types involved, because a slab-on-grade home dries differently than one with a vented crawl space. Our drying protocols follow the IICRC S500 standard, and our techs carry WRT and ASD training, so the plan adjusts daily based on moisture readings rather than running on a fixed timeline. We document everything with photos and moisture logs as we go, which matters later if a claim needs to be substantiated.

## Local note

One thing that catches Highland homeowners off guard: because the regional climate is dry most of the year, people assume a wet crawl space will just evaporate on its own. In practice, clay soil underneath the vapor barrier holds moisture long after the surface looks dry, so a slow leak that seemed minor can still be feeding mold growth in the joists weeks later. We always pull back the vapor barrier and check the soil moisture directly rather than trusting what the surface looks like.
