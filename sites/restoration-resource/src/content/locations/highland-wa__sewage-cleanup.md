## Why Highland Properties See Sewage Backups

Much of the land around Highland sits in a transitional zone between older agricultural lots and newer residential subdivisions, which means sewer infrastructure varies block to block. Properties on the outskirts often still rely on septic systems, and those tanks and drain fields are vulnerable to root intrusion from mature trees, soil compaction from vehicle traffic, and the freeze-thaw cycles that hit eastern Washington hard in January and February. When the ground freezes and then thaws quickly, septic lines can shift or crack, and that's when homeowners start noticing slow drains, gurgling toilets, or standing water near the drain field, all warning signs that precede a full backup. Homes on municipal sewer lines face a different problem: older clay or cast-iron lateral lines that have settled over decades are prone to blockages and collapse, especially after heavy rain events saturate the surrounding soil.

## Our Sewage Cleanup and Sanitization Process in Highland

Every job starts with containment. We isolate the affected area to stop contaminated water from migrating into clean parts of the structure, then begin extraction using truck-mounted and portable pumps rated for solids-laden wastewater. Porous materials that have absorbed sewage, carpet pad, baseboards, sometimes subflooring, typically can't be sanitized and have to come out; we document everything with photos before removal so your adjuster has a clear record. After extraction and demolition, we apply EPA-registered antimicrobial treatments and run air scrubbers to manage airborne pathogens, then dry the structure per IICRC S500 drying standards, checking moisture content in framing and subfloor before we call a space clear. For septic-related overflows, we also coordinate timing with your septic service provider so cleanup doesn't start until the source of the backup is actually resolved, otherwise you're just cleaning the same area twice.
