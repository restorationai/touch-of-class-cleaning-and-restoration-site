## Why Kahlotus Properties See Water Damage

Most of the housing stock around Kahlotus is older farmhouse construction, built for a dry wheat-country climate with hot summers and cold, often snowy winters. That combination means two recurring failure points: frozen and burst supply lines in unheated crawlspaces during hard freezes, and well or pressure tank failures that run unchecked because nobody's home to hear the pump cycling nonstop. Basalt and scabland soils in this part of the Palouse don't drain the way sandy or loamy soil does, so when water does get loose, either from a plumbing failure or spring runoff pooling against a foundation, it tends to sit rather than disperse. On properties with septic systems, a sewer line backup adds a contamination layer that straight freshwater intrusion doesn't carry, which changes both the drying plan and the safety precautions on site.

## Our Water Damage Restoration Process in Kahlotus

We start every call with an assessment of the water category and the structure itself, since a 1940s farmhouse with plank subfloor behaves very differently under moisture than newer stick-built construction. Extraction comes first, pulling standing water from flooring and crawlspaces before it migrates further into framing. From there we set up structural drying equipment calibrated to the building: older homes with minimal insulation and exposed joists often dry faster at the surface but can hide moisture in wall cavities and subfloor seams longer than modern materials would. We track moisture content daily with meters rather than guessing by feel, and we don't call a job finished until readings match dry standard for that specific structure, not a generic number.
