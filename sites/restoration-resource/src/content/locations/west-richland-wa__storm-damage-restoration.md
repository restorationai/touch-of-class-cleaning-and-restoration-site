## Why West Richland Properties See Storm Damage

West Richland sits in the Columbia Basin's high desert climate zone, which means long dry stretches punctuated by sudden, intense weather: gusty spring windstorms, occasional summer thunderstorms with hail, and the kind of flash wind events that can snap brittle tree limbs weakened by drought stress. Much of the city's housing stock was built from the 1980s onward, so homes generally meet more current wind and roofing codes than older Tri-Cities neighborhoods, but that doesn't make them immune. Asphalt shingle roofs exposed to repeated UV and heat cycling in this region can lose granule adhesion over time, which makes them more vulnerable to wind lift during a storm than a roof of the same age in a milder climate. Fence lines and detached structures, common on the larger residential lots found throughout the city, also take a disproportionate share of wind damage because they act as the first line of resistance before wind reaches the house.

## Our Storm Damage Restoration Process in West Richland

We start with a structural safety check, because storm calls often involve compromised rooflines, broken glass, or trees leaning against a structure that aren't safe to approach until they're secured. From there, we move to emergency board-up or tarping to stop active water intrusion, followed by water extraction if wind-driven rain has already reached interior drywall, insulation, or flooring. Basalt bedrock sits close to the surface across much of West Richland, which limits how quickly storm water drains away from foundations in low-lying yards, so we pay close attention to crawlspace and foundation moisture even after the visible damage is cleared. Structural drying follows IICRC S500 guidelines, with moisture readings logged daily until materials return to normal levels, not just until they look dry.
