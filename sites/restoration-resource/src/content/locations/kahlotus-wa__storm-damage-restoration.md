## Why Kahlotus Properties See Storm Damage

Kahlotus and the surrounding Channeled Scablands funnel wind through open basalt coulees with almost nothing to slow it down. There are few mature windbreaks left on most properties, so gusts that would lose strength moving through a tree-lined neighborhood hit roofs, pole barns, and grain storage at close to full speed. The isolated trees that do remain, often planted decades ago as a single windbreak row near an old farmhouse, take the brunt of it and come down across driveways, power lines, or roof lines instead of being protected by a stand of neighbors. Metal outbuildings and older wood-frame homes built before modern wind-load codes are especially exposed, and a lifted section of roofing or a blown-out window during a thunderstorm can let in more water in twenty minutes than a slow leak does in a week.

## Our Storm Damage Restoration Process in Kahlotus

We start with emergency tarping or board-up to stop active water and wind intrusion, then move into debris removal, which in this part of Franklin County often means downed limbs, loose shake or metal roofing, and blown insulation scattered across a yard or field edge. Once the structure is secured, we moisture-map affected walls, ceilings, and flooring and begin structural drying per IICRC S500 guidelines, since wind-driven rain can push water behind siding and into wall cavities even when the exterior damage looks minor. For properties with detached shops, grain bins, or equipment sheds, we assess those structures separately from the main house, since storm damage to a pole barn roof often needs different materials and timelines than repairs to a residence.
