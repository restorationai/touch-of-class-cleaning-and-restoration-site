## Why Plymouth Properties See Water Damage Issues

Plymouth sits in the high desert stretch of the Columbia Basin, where homes and outbuildings are built for heat and wind, not sustained moisture. That means a lot of properties here run on well water or private irrigation lines feeding orchards and small farm plots, and a cracked supply line or a failed pressure valve can dump hundreds of gallons before anyone notices, especially on acreage where the leak isn't right next to a living space. Slab-on-grade foundations are common in the newer construction around the area, and when water gets under that slab through a plumbing failure or heavy spring runoff, it tends to travel sideways through the fill material before it ever shows as a wet spot on the floor. Older farmhouses and outbuildings, meanwhile, often have crawlspaces that were never built with vapor barriers, which gives water nowhere to go but into the joists.

## Our Water Damage Restoration Process in Plymouth

We start with extraction, pulling standing water with truck-mounted or portable pumps depending on how far we are from the nearest access point, since many Plymouth properties sit on longer driveways or gravel access roads rather than city streets. From there we moisture-map the affected rooms with meters, checking not just the visible flooring but the subfloor and, where relevant, the slab edge, since trapped moisture under a slab can hide longer here than it would in a basement-heavy market. Structural drying follows, set up per IICRC S500 guidelines, with air movers and dehumidifiers sized to the actual cubic footage of the space rather than a one-size-fits-all count. Because the regional climate runs dry for most of the year, we can sometimes shorten drying time by venting to the exterior once humidity levels allow it, something that would be a bad idea in a coastal market but works in Plymouth's arid air for much of the season.
