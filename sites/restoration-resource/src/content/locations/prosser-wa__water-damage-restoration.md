## Why Prosser Properties See Water Damage Issues

Much of Prosser's older housing stock near the downtown core dates back to the early 1900s, with crawlspace foundations and galvanized or early copper plumbing that was never built for today's water pressure or for decades of hard well water scaling the pipe walls from the inside. When those lines finally let go, the water often tracks along floor joists and sill plates before anyone notices a stain. Newer construction on the edges of town sits on slab or shallow crawlspace over the area's basalt-influenced soils, which drain slowly and can hold moisture against a foundation far longer than sandier ground would. Add in Yakima County's hot, dry summers followed by hard freezes, and you get a cycle of pipe stress that produces a steady run of winter burst-pipe calls and summer irrigation or supply-line failures tied to agricultural water use near residential lots.

## Our Water Damage Restoration Process in Prosser

The first visit is about stopping the loss and reading the structure, not just running a shop vac. We shut off or isolate the water source, extract standing water with truck-mount or portable extraction equipment depending on how far a crew has to reach into a crawlspace or outbuilding, and then map moisture behind walls and under flooring with meters rather than guessing from surface appearance. In Prosser's older homes this matters: a subfloor can read dry on top while the joist bay underneath is still saturated, especially where original wood flooring sits over an unvented crawlspace. Drying is set up according to the IICRC S500 structural drying standard, with air movers and dehumidifiers sized to the actual cubic footage and material type rather than a flat per-room count, and we monitor and log moisture daily until materials are back to a dry standard instead of just a drier one.
