## Why Richland Properties See Storm Damage

Richland sits in the high desert of the Columbia Basin, and that geography drives most of the storm damage we see here. Summer thunderstorms can build fast over the basin and drop large hail or straight-line winds with almost no warning, while shoulder-season windstorms funneling down the Columbia and Yakima river corridors regularly exceed what asphalt shingles and older fascia boards were built to handle. Flat terrain means there's little to slow wind gusts before they hit a roofline, and the region's dry summers followed by wind events can leave trees with shallow, stressed root systems more likely to come down on a garage or fence line during a fall storm. Add in the occasional dust storm rolling off the basin, and you get a mix of wind-driven debris, roof damage, and downed limbs that's a little different from the rain-saturated storm damage you'd see on the coast.

## Our Storm Damage Restoration Process in Richland

The first call usually covers a damaged roof, a fallen tree limb, or windows broken by flying debris, so our crews arrive prepared to tarp and board before anything else. Because Richland's hot, dry air pulls moisture out of water-damaged materials faster than a humid climate would, we move quickly to extract standing water and set containment before the sun and wind finish the job in an uneven, damaging way, pulling surface layers dry while leaves subfloor and framing saturated. Once the structure is secured, our IICRC-trained technicians document every affected material, remove debris, and begin structural drying using the same S500-standard protocols regardless of whether the loss started with hail, wind, or a tree through the roof. We coordinate tree removal, tarping, and reconstruction scheduling so a homeowner isn't juggling three separate contractors during an already stressful week.
