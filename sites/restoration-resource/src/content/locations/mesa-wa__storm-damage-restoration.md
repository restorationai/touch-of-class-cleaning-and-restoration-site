Mesa sits in open Franklin County farm country, surrounded by center-pivot fields and grain operations with little tree cover or terrain to break up wind. That means storm cells moving through eastern Washington often arrive at full strength, with straight-line gusts and hail that hit homes, pole barns, and metal-roofed outbuildings directly. ## Our Storm Damage Restoration Process in Mesa

The first priority on any Mesa storm call is stopping further intrusion: tarping a compromised roof section, boarding broken windows, and extracting standing water before it works into subfloor and insulation. From there our crews assess moisture penetration room by room, since wind-driven rain in an open-country storm often pushes water sideways under eaves and around window flashing rather than straight down, which means damage can show up well away from the visible breach. Structural drying follows IICRC S500 guidelines, with moisture mapping to confirm framing and subfloor have actually released trapped water, not just dried at the surface. Debris removal, including downed limbs, damaged siding, and torn roofing material, is handled alongside drying so the property isn't left half-finished between trades.
