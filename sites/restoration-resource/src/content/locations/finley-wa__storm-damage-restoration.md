## Why Finley Properties See Storm Damage Issues

Finley sits in open agricultural country east of Pasco, with far less tree cover and fewer windbreaks than the denser Tri-Cities neighborhoods closer to the river. That exposure means wind gusts hit roofs and outbuildings at full force, and summer thunderstorms that roll off the Horse Heather Hills often arrive with hail and dry lightning before much rain ever falls. Pole barns, equipment sheds, and older farmhouse roofing common out here were often built for a drier, calmer climate than what a severe downburst actually delivers, so flashing, shingles, and metal roofing panels tend to fail at the edges first. When a storm does bring heavy rain, the clay and loess soils in this part of Benton County drain slowly, which means standing water around foundations and crawlspaces can linger for days after the sky clears.

## Our Storm Damage Restoration Process in Finley

We start with emergency tarping or board-up to stop the active intrusion, then move to water extraction if rain or hail has gotten into the structure. Framing and subfloor moisture gets mapped with meters and dried to the IICRC S500 standard rather than by guesswork, which matters here because older rural framing and exposed outbuildings hold moisture differently than a typical in-town structure. If trees or large limbs have come down on a roof or fence line, we handle the structural tarping and debris clearing directly tied to the building, and we'll point you to a tree removal contractor for anything beyond that scope rather than overstepping into work we don't do. Photo documentation happens at every stage so your insurance adjuster has what they need without a second site visit.

## Local note

Most properties out here run on private wells and septic systems rather than city utilities, which changes how we handle storm flooding. A downed power line or flooded well pit isn't just a water problem, it can be a safety issue, so we check well heads and septic risers as part of the initial walkthrough rather than assuming municipal service like you'd find in town. Underinsured outbuildings are also common; a lot of pole barns and ag storage sheds were added to a policy years after the house and never got reassessed for replacement cost, which is worth flagging to your adjuster early.
