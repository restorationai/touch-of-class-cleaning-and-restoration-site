## Why Basin City Properties See Fire Damage

Basin City sits in the high desert of the Columbia Basin Irrigation Project, where low humidity and seasonal wind push grass and field fires toward homes, barns, and equipment sheds faster than in wetter parts of the state. A lot of the housing stock here is manufactured or modular, built during the area's mid-century irrigation boom, and heated with propane rather than natural gas. Propane tanks, older electrical panels, and wood stoves used for supplemental heat in the colder months are common fire sources we see across rural Franklin County properties, along with agricultural equipment fires that start in outbuildings and spread to attached storage or living space.

## Our Fire Damage Restoration Process in Basin City

We start with a walk-through to document the loss for your insurance file, board up any openings, and tarp roof damage to stop weather and wildlife intrusion while the property sits vacant. From there we remove charred debris and heavily soot-damaged materials, clean salvageable surfaces, and treat for smoke odor using thermal fogging or hydroxyl generators depending on how deep the smoke traveled into wall cavities and ductwork. If firefighters used water to suppress the blaze, structural drying follows the IICRC S500 standard before we rebuild or hand the space off for reconstruction. On manufactured and modular homes common in this area, particleboard subflooring and vinyl interior surfaces hold smoke odor and moisture differently than solid lumber framing, so we pull baseboards and check subfloor moisture earlier in the process than we would on a site-built house.

## Basin City Insurance Coordination

A lot of properties out here carry farm or rural homeowner policies that cover the house, detached structures, and sometimes equipment separately, which changes how a claim gets scoped. We photograph and document damage to each structure individually, whether that's the main residence, a shop, or a grain or equipment shed, so your adjuster has what's needed to process coverage on each piece without extra back-and-forth.

## Local note

Many Basin City properties rely on private wells rather than municipal water, which matters after a fire because suppression water and runoff can pool near a wellhead or septic field if drainage grading wasn't part of the original build. We check for that during the initial assessment so we're not creating a secondary water problem while cleaning up the fire damage.
