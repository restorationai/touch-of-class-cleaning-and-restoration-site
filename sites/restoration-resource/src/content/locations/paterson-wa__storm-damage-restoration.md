## Why Paterson Properties See Storm Damage Issues

Paterson sits inside the Columbia River Gorge wind funnel, where gusts accelerate as they move through the gorge and hit this stretch of Klickitat County with little to break them up. Properties out here tend to be spread across open agricultural land with fewer windbreaks than a typical subdivision, so roofs, outbuildings, and fencing take the brunt of straight-line wind events that a city lot might never see. Older farmhouses and metal-roofed outbuildings common to this area handle wind differently than modern residential construction: metal panels can peel back at the fasteners in a way that opens a roof deck to rain within minutes, and older wood-frame structures can shift enough on their foundations to crack interior plaster or drywall even when the roof itself holds. When a storm tears through, the follow-up water damage often does more harm than the wind that caused it.

## Our Storm Damage Restoration Process in Paterson

We start with a structural walkthrough to separate immediate safety hazards (compromised roof sections, downed power lines, leaning trees) from damage that can wait a few hours. If the roof or a window has been breached, we tarp or board it the same visit to stop active water intrusion before it spreads into insulation and framing. From there we extract standing water, pull saturated materials that won't dry in place, and set up drying equipment calibrated to the structure, following the IICRC S500 standard for monitoring moisture rather than guessing when a wall or subfloor is actually dry. For properties with ag outbuildings or detached structures, we scope those separately since they often dry on a different timeline than the main house and sometimes fall under a different line on the insurance policy.
