A house fire never behaves the way homeowners expect. The flames might stay contained to a kitchen or a garage, but soot and smoke travel through HVAC ducts, wall cavities, and attic space long after the fire department leaves. In Highland, where many homes rely on forced-air heating and older duct runs, that migration pattern means a contained fire can still leave acrid residue two rooms away. Add in the dry, high-desert air common across this part of Eastern Washington, and smoke odor tends to bond more aggressively to drywall, insulation, and fabric than it would in a humid climate, which is part of why DIY odor fixes (ozone machines bought online, scrubbing walls with vinegar) rarely solve the problem on their own.

## Why Highland Properties See Fire Damage Issues

Wildfire smoke infiltration is a real concern for homes in this region during late summer, when haze from regional fires settles into attics and crawlspaces even without a structure fire on the property. Combine that with the area's mix of older ranch-style homes and newer stucco or fiber-cement construction, and you get two very different restoration problems: older homes often have wood-frame construction and permeable interior finishes that absorb smoke deeper, while newer builds trap soot behind tighter vapor barriers where it off-gasses slowly. Either way, surface cleaning alone rarely resolves the odor, because the source is usually inside the material, not on top of it.

## Our Fire Damage Restoration Process in Highland

Every fire loss starts with a walk-through to separate what's salvageable from what isn't, documented with photos for your claim file. From there we board up and tarp any exposed openings to secure the structure, then move into content pack-out for anything that can be cleaned off-site. Structural cleaning follows IICRC FSRT protocols: soda-blasting or dry-sponge cleaning on char and soot depending on the surface, HEPA vacuuming to pull particulate out of porous materials, and thermal fogging or hydroxyl generators to neutralize odor at the molecular level rather than masking it. Drywall, insulation, and carpet padding that absorbed heavy smoke typically get removed rather than cleaned, since the cost of deep cleaning often exceeds replacement. We coordinate demolition and rebuild scope with your adjuster before any wall comes down.

## Highland Insurance Coordination

Fire claims move differently than water claims. If your policy includes additional living expense coverage, we can usually turn around an initial damage assessment quickly enough that you're not waiting weeks to find out whether the home is habitable.

## Local note

One detail that catches Highland homeowners off guard: a fire that starts in an attached garage can pull smoke into the living space through the garage-to-house door and shared attic venting, even when the door was closed during the fire. We always check attic insulation and ductwork on garage fires, because that's usually where lingering odor complaints come from weeks after the visible damage looks cleaned up.
