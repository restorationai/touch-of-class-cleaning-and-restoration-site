FireDEX Butler has been in the restoration business since 1981 — long enough to have worked through every kind of water loss, fire, and mold situation western Pennsylvania's housing stock can produce. The company is based in Cranberry Township and registered with the Pennsylvania Attorney General as a licensed Home Improvement Contractor (#PA001913). That's not a credential most homeowners think to ask about, but it matters: it means the company carries the bonding and accountability the state requires before anyone is allowed to work inside your home.

## What we do

The day-to-day reality of restoration work is not glamorous. It's arriving at a house at 2 a.m. because a pipe burst behind the kitchen wall. It's spending three hours walking an adjuster through moisture readings so a homeowner's claim doesn't get underpaid. It's explaining to a family why the flooring they want to save has to come out, and what happens if it doesn't. FireDEX Butler operates around the clock — when a loss happens on a Sunday evening or a holiday weekend, the response is the same as a Tuesday morning. Most of the jobs we handle involve an insurance claim, and we work directly with carriers to document damage, submit scopes, and keep the process moving so property owners aren't left managing paperwork on top of an already stressful situation.

## Our certifications and licensure

The restoration industry has a credentialing body — the IICRC, publisher of the S500 (water), S520 (mold) and S700 (fire) standards most reputable contractors follow. Its standards (S500 for water damage, S520 for mold, S700 for fire and smoke) define how drying equipment gets placed, how air quality gets tested, and when a structure is actually safe to close back up. Working with a contractor who follows those protocols isn't just a quality preference; it affects whether your insurance carrier accepts the scope of work. FireDEX Butler holds Pennsylvania Home Improvement Contractor Registration (#PA001913), which is a state-level requirement — not optional paperwork — for any contractor performing residential work in the commonwealth.

## Where we work

The company's primary service area runs through Butler County and Allegheny County, with Cranberry Township sitting roughly at the center. That covers communities like Mars, Zelienople, Wexford, Allison Park, and the northern Pittsburgh suburbs. Western Pennsylvania's climate — cold winters that stress older plumbing, humid summers that accelerate mold growth in basements and crawlspaces — creates a predictable pattern of losses across the region. The housing stock here skews older in many neighborhoods, which means lead-paint considerations and aging infrastructure are part of the job, not exceptions to it.
