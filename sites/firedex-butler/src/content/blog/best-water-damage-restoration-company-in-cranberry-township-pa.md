## How Water Damage Restoration Actually Works

Understanding the process helps you evaluate any company you call. A properly run water damage job follows these phases:

1. **Emergency extraction.** Truck-mounted or portable extractors remove standing water. In a flooded Cranberry Township basement, this can mean hundreds of gallons before drying equipment is even staged.
2. **Moisture mapping.** Technicians use penetrating and non-penetrating moisture meters to map how far water has traveled into walls, subfloor, and framing. This determines what gets removed and what can be dried in place.
3. **Structural drying.** Commercial-grade LGR dehumidifiers and high-velocity air movers run continuously, typically for three to five days for a standard Category 1 loss. Readings are logged daily against drying goals.
4. **Antimicrobial treatment.** Affected surfaces receive EPA-registered antimicrobial treatment to inhibit mold growth during the drying window.
5. **Clearance testing.** Moisture readings return to acceptable levels before any reconstruction begins. Skipping this step is how mold problems start inside rebuilt walls.
6. **Reconstruction.** Drywall, insulation, flooring, and finishes are restored to pre-loss condition. A company that handles this in-house, like FireDEX Butler, eliminates the gap between mitigation and rebuild.

## What To Do Right Now If You Have Water Damage

If you are dealing with an active loss in Cranberry Township, do these things in order:

1. **Stop the source** if you can safely do so. Shut the main water valve, call the utility, or get the plumber on the line. The restoration company handles the water that is already there; the plumber handles the source.
2. **Call a restoration company.** Do not wait to call your insurance company first. Mitigation needs to start immediately. You are allowed to hire a restoration company before the adjuster arrives.
3. **Document everything.** Take photos and video of every affected area before anything is moved or dried. This protects your claim.
4. **Move contents if safe.** Get rugs, furniture, and valuables off wet floors if you can do so without injury.
5. **Do not run HVAC fans over wet areas.** Circulating air through a wet space without proper dehumidification can spread moisture and increase mold risk.
