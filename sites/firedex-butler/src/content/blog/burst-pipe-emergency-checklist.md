When a pipe bursts, the first five minutes matter more than the next five hours. Shut off your home's main water supply valve immediately — it's usually near the water meter, in the basement, or in a utility closet. Then cut power to any rooms where water is pooling near outlets or panels. Once the water stops flowing and electricity is no longer a hazard, you can start assessing the damage. Everything after that follows a clear sequence, and that's what this checklist covers.

---

## Step-by-Step: What to Do Right Now

Work through these steps in order. Don't skip ahead to cleanup before the source is controlled.

1. **Shut off the main water valve.** Turn it clockwise until it stops. If you've never located it, do that today — before an emergency happens. In Cranberry Township, many homes built in the 1990s and 2000s have the shutoff inside the garage near the front foundation wall.

2. **Turn off the water heater.** Once the main supply is off, your water heater can overheat or crack if it keeps running dry. Switch it to the "pilot" setting on gas units, or flip the breaker on electric models.

3. **Kill the electricity in affected rooms.** If water is near outlets, light fixtures, or your electrical panel, flip those breakers at the main box. If the panel itself is in a wet area, don't touch it — call an electrician first.

4. **Open faucets to drain remaining pressure.** Turning on cold-water taps at the lowest point in the house (a basement utility sink, a hose bib) releases residual pressure and pulls standing water away from the break faster.

5. **Locate and document the break.** Take photos and video before you move anything. Your insurance adjuster will need this. Note the time, what you see, and where the water traveled.

6. **Start moving water out.** Use a wet/dry vacuum, mop, or towels to pull standing water off hard floors. The longer water sits, the deeper it wicks into subfloor, drywall, and insulation. Mold can begin colonizing porous materials in as little as 24 to 48 hours under warm, humid conditions.

7. **Ventilate the space.** Open windows if outdoor humidity is lower than indoor humidity. Run box fans across wet surfaces. Do not run a regular household fan directly into a wall cavity — you'll push moisture deeper.

8. **Call your insurance company.** Most homeowner policies cover sudden and accidental pipe bursts. Report the claim before any major repairs begin so you don't accidentally void coverage by altering the loss scene.

## What NOT to Do After a Pipe Bursts

Some instincts make things worse. Avoid these common mistakes:

- **Don't use a regular household vacuum on standing water.** It will destroy the motor and won't extract water from carpet padding or subfloor. Use a wet/dry vac rated for liquid pickup.
- **Don't crank the heat to dry things out.** High heat without airflow traps moisture inside wall cavities and accelerates mold growth. Controlled airflow — dehumidifiers and air movers — is what actually dries a structure.
- **Don't assume the damage stops at the visible wet spot.** Water travels along joists, inside wall cavities, and under flooring. What looks like a two-foot stain on the ceiling might mean four feet of wet insulation above it. Moisture meters tell the real story.
- **Don't start cutting drywall unless you know what's behind it.** Electrical wiring, plumbing runs, and HVAC ducts are all common in interior walls. If you're not sure, wait for a professional.
- **Don't throw away damaged materials before your adjuster sees them.** Flooring, drywall, and insulation are part of the documented loss. Move them out of the way if you must, but keep them accessible.

## Hidden Damage: What You Might Be Missing

The water you can see is rarely the whole problem.

If the burst pipe is behind drywall, you may notice a soft or bubbling section of wall, a musty smell developing over 24–48 hours, or paint that starts to peel without an obvious source. In finished basements — common in the newer construction throughout Butler County — water can travel under luxury vinyl plank or laminate flooring for several feet before showing at the surface.

Appliance supply lines are another frequent source of hidden damage. A refrigerator ice-maker line or a washing machine hose that fails slowly can saturate the subfloor under a finished laundry room for weeks before anyone notices. If you're dealing with that kind of slow leak rather than a sudden burst, the extraction and drying process is similar, but the affected area is often larger than expected. [Appliance leak cleanup](/appliance-leak-cleanup) follows the same structural drying protocol as a pipe burst — the source just requires a different repair trade.

Thermal imaging cameras and calibrated moisture meters are the only reliable way to map where water actually traveled. This is one of the main reasons professional assessment adds value even when the visible damage looks manageable.

## When to Call a Water Damage Professional

You can handle the immediate steps above on your own. But call a restoration contractor when:

- Standing water covered more than a few square feet, especially on carpet or over a wood subfloor
- Water reached drywall, insulation, or wall cavities
- The affected area includes a bathroom, kitchen, or laundry room (higher contamination risk from drain backflow)
- The smell of mildew appears within 24–48 hours of drying out
- Your moisture readings (if you have a meter) are still elevated after 48 hours of fan and dehumidifier use
- You're filing an insurance claim and need documented scope of loss

Professional [water damage restoration](/water-damage-restoration) uses commercial-grade drying equipment — high-CFM air movers, desiccant or refrigerant dehumidifiers — that pulls moisture out of structural materials at a rate household fans simply can't match. The goal is to dry the structure to pre-loss moisture levels, typically within three to five days, before any repairs begin.

## The Recovery Timeline: What Comes After Drying

Once the structure is verified dry, the repair sequence generally looks like this:

1. **Plumber repairs or replaces the failed pipe.** This happens before any walls are closed.
2. **Insulation is replaced** if it was saturated — wet insulation loses R-value and holds moisture against framing.
3. **Drywall is patched or replaced.** Restoration contractors typically handle this as part of the scope, or coordinate with a separate drywall crew.
4. **Flooring is reinstalled.** Hardwood may need acclimation time. Tile requires the subfloor to be fully dry and structurally sound first.
5. **Final moisture check.** Before painting or finishing, a final moisture reading confirms the structure is back within normal range.

The full timeline from burst pipe to finished repair runs anywhere from one to three weeks depending on the extent of saturation and material lead times. Structural drying alone typically takes three to five days.
