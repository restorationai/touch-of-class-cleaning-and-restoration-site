## What Are the Most Common Water Damage Calls in Cranberry Township?

Cranberry Township's housing stock creates a predictable set of losses. Knowing which one you're dealing with helps the crew arrive prepared.

**Sump pump failures** are the most common call, especially during spring thaw and summer thunderstorm season. Cranberry's heavy clay soil doesn't drain well, and Brush Creek drainage backs up in heavy storms. Nearly every home in the township has a basement, many of them finished, so a failed sump means water in a space with drywall, flooring, and contents.

**Burst and frozen pipes** spike in January and February during polar cold snaps. Older Fernway homes with end-of-life galvanized plumbing are especially vulnerable. So are bonus rooms and garage-adjacent spaces in newer two-story plans where insulation is thin and pipes run through exterior walls.

**Appliance and supply-line failures**, washing machines, water heaters, refrigerator ice-maker lines, dishwashers, happen year-round and often go unnoticed for hours. A second-floor laundry failure in a Glen Eden or Ehrman Farms two-story can soak through the subfloor into the ceiling below before anyone notices.

**Foundation seepage** shows up in wet springs when the water table rises and hydrostatic pressure pushes through block or poured walls. This is typically Category 1 water but can become Category 2 quickly if it contacts stored materials or sits for more than 24 hours.

## Will Insurance Cover My Water Damage Claim?

Most standard homeowners policies cover sudden, accidental water damage, a burst pipe, a failed appliance hose, a sump pump overflow (if you carry the sump pump rider). They typically do not cover gradual leaks or maintenance-related failures.
