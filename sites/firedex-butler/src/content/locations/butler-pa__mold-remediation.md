## Our Mold Remediation Process in Butler

Every job starts with a thorough inspection — moisture meters, thermal imaging where warranted, and a room-by-room assessment of visible and suspected growth. Before any physical work begins, the affected area is isolated with negative air pressure containment to prevent spores from migrating to clean areas of the home. This step matters especially in Butler's older row homes and foursquares, where open floor plans and shared wall cavities can carry contamination further than expected.

Once containment is established, affected porous materials — drywall, insulation, wood framing where necessary — are carefully removed and bagged for disposal. Structural surfaces are then treated and HEPA-vacuumed. Air scrubbers run throughout the process to filter particulates. After remediation, the area is dried to target moisture levels before any reconstruction begins. We document the entire process with photos and moisture readings, which your insurance adjuster will need for the claim file.

## Local Note

One thing that comes up repeatedly in Butler's pre-1950 homes: original horsehair plaster behaves very differently from modern drywall when it gets wet. It doesn't wick moisture to the surface the way drywall does, so it can read as dry on a surface probe while holding significant moisture in the substrate. We use penetrating moisture meters on plaster walls rather than relying on surface readings alone — otherwise you can sign off on a job that still has active moisture driving mold growth behind the finish. It's a small calibration difference, but it's the kind of thing that prevents a callback six weeks later.
