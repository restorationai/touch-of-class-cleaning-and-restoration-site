## Why Economy Properties See Water Damage Issues

Economy's housing stock reflects its long industrial history along the Ohio River corridor, a mix of mid-20th-century ranches and older frame homes with block or fieldstone foundations. Those foundation types were common across Beaver County before modern waterproofing membranes became standard, and they tend to develop hairline cracks that let groundwater seep in during spring thaw or after a heavy rain event. Add in the clay-heavy soil typical of this part of southwestern Pennsylvania, which drains slowly and holds water against foundation walls, and you get basements that flood even when the storm itself wasn’t severe. Freeze-thaw cycles through the winter months also stress older cast iron and galvanized supply lines, which is a common source of the slow leaks that go unnoticed until a ceiling stain or warped floorboard gives it away.

## Our Water Damage Restoration Process in Economy

The first move on any call is stopping the source and getting standing water out, whether that means a submersible pump for a flooded basement or truck-mount extraction for saturated carpet and pad. From there we check subfloor and framing moisture with meters rather than guessing from the surface, because a hardwood floor can look dry on top while the joists underneath stay wet for days. Structural drying in Economy's older homes often takes longer than in newer construction because plaster and full-dimension lumber hold moisture differently than drywall and engineered lumber, so we set drying goals based on material readings and track them daily instead of pulling equipment on a fixed schedule. We document every step with photos and moisture logs, which matters when a claim goes through a carrier and an adjuster wants to see the drying was done to a defensible standard rather than guessed at.
