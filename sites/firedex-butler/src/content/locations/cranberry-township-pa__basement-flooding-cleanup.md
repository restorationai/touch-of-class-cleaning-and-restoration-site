## Our Basement Flooding Cleanup Process in Cranberry Township

Every job starts with moisture mapping, not guessing. Technicians use thermal imaging and calibrated moisture meters to trace exactly how far water has migrated into wall cavities, under flooring, and into structural framing — critical in finished basements where water travels silently behind drywall before it becomes visible. From there the process moves through four stages:

**1. Water extraction.** Truck-mounted and portable extractors pull standing water from carpet, pad, and hard flooring. In basements with luxury vinyl plank or glued-down tile — common in Fox Run and Park Place builds from the 2000s — we assess whether the floor can be saved before deciding on removal versus aggressive drying in place.

**2. Controlled demolition when needed.** Wet drywall below the flood line, saturated insulation, and soaked carpet padding are removed. Saving these materials is not worth the mold risk; in Butler County's humid summers, colonization can begin within 24 to 48 hours of a moisture event.

**3. Structural drying.** Industrial air movers and desiccant or refrigerant dehumidifiers run in a calculated configuration based on the cubic footage and material types in the space. Readings are logged at each visit so drying progress is documented — useful for insurance adjusters and for confirming the space is genuinely dry, not just surface-dry.

**4. Clearance and restoration scope.** Once moisture readings reach target levels, we provide a written drying report and coordinate with your adjuster on the rebuild scope — framing, insulation, drywall, flooring, and paint.
