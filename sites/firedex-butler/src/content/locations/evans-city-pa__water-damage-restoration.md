## Our Water Damage Restoration Process in Evans City

Every job starts with a thorough moisture assessment before any equipment is staged. In a 1920s frame home with plaster-over-lath walls, water travels differently than in modern drywall construction — it wicks laterally behind baseboards and into subfloor cavities before it becomes visible on the surface. We use thermal imaging and calibrated moisture meters to map the full extent of saturation, not just the wet spots you can see.

From there, the process follows a structured sequence:

- **Water extraction** — truck-mounted and portable extraction units pull standing water from floors, carpets, and crawl spaces. In basement flooding situations common near Breakneck Creek, we address any active seepage source before extraction begins.
- **Structural drying** — commercial-grade desiccant and refrigerant dehumidifiers combined with high-velocity air movers are positioned based on the moisture map, not a generic layout. Older plaster walls in Evans City homes release moisture slowly; drying plans account for extended dry times compared to newer construction.
- **Monitoring and documentation** — moisture readings are logged at each visit so you and your insurance adjuster have a clear record of progress. We do not close out a job until materials reach target moisture levels.
- **Controlled demolition if needed** — saturated insulation, buckled subfloor sections, or compromised framing that cannot dry in place is removed cleanly, with photo documentation at every stage.
