## Why Wexford Properties Face Distinct Fire Damage Challenges

The housing stock in the 15090 ZIP code skews toward large homes built between the early 1990s and the mid-2010s — a construction era that used engineered lumber, open floor plans, and synthetic materials that burn faster and produce denser, oilier smoke than older dimensional-lumber framing. That smoke travels farther before a fire is suppressed, meaning rooms two floors away from the origin point can carry measurable soot and odor. Finished walk-out basements — common in the hillside lots near the Treesdale fringe — add another full story of affected square footage, often containing high-value contents like wine storage, home theaters, and custom wet bars.

Bonus rooms built over garages are a specific vulnerability in this area. They're thermally isolated from the main house, which means they run colder in January and February — a condition that has contributed to frozen and burst supply lines that, when they fail, can feed a secondary water damage event on top of an existing fire loss. Wooded lots on private well systems add another variable: pressure-tank failures during or after a fire can complicate suppression water management on-site.

## Our Fire Damage Restoration Process in Wexford

When we arrive at a Wexford property, the first priority is a room-by-room scope that documents every affected surface before anything is moved or cleaned. Homeowners here typically carry strong insurance policies, and carriers expect detailed photo documentation, moisture mapping, and itemized content inventories — not a rough estimate written on a clipboard. We produce that documentation from the first hour on-site.

Structural assessment comes next. Engineered I-joists and LVL beams — standard in 1990s and 2000s construction — behave differently under fire exposure than solid lumber; they can lose structural integrity faster, and replacement requires matching the original load specifications. We coordinate with a structural engineer when beam or joist replacement is in scope, which is more common in these larger homes than in older, smaller housing stock elsewhere in Butler County.

Smoke and odor mitigation in a home with a three-zone HVAC system requires cleaning or replacing ductwork throughout — not just in the rooms adjacent to the fire. We use thermal fogging and hydroxyl generation for odor neutralization, and we seal affected cavities before any reconstruction begins so that odor doesn't resurface after the rebuild is complete.
