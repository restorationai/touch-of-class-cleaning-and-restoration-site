## Why Cranberry Township Properties Hold Odors Longer

The township's housing stock spans nearly six decades of construction, and each era has its own odor-retention profile. Older Fernway plats from the 1960s were built with plaster-and-lath interiors and galvanized supply lines — both of which are reaching end of life. When a galvanized pipe weeps or fails, the slow seepage saturates wall cavities before anyone notices, and the resulting mildew odor embeds in the plaster matrix in a way that surface sprays cannot reach. Newer two-story plans in developments along the Route 19 / Route 228 corridor — built from the 1990s through the 2020s — use engineered wood subfloors and open-cell spray foam insulation that trap smoke and biological odors differently than solid lumber does.

Cranberry Township also sits at an elevation where winter temperature swings are sharp. A January polar snap that freezes a supply line in an uninsulated garage wall can also push combustion odors from a cracked heat exchanger into the living space at the same time. By spring, that same home may be dealing with hydrostatic seepage through the foundation as Brush Creek drainage backs up during cloudbursts, layering a second odor source on top of the first.

## Our Odor Removal and Deodorization Process

Effective odor elimination is not a single-step treatment — it follows the odor molecule to wherever it has migrated. The process begins with a room-by-room assessment that identifies source materials: charred framing, smoke-coated HVAC registers, water-damaged subfloor, or biologicals left behind by a sewage backup. We document everything with photographs and moisture readings before any equipment is placed.

For smoke odor removal, thermal fogging is often the right tool — a petroleum-based or water-based deodorizer is heated into a fine fog that penetrates the same porous surfaces the smoke particles reached, neutralizing odor at the molecular level rather than masking it. For post-water-damage mold and mildew odors, hydroxyl deodorization is frequently the better choice: hydroxyl generators produce reactive hydroxyl radicals that break down odor compounds continuously over time, and unlike ozone treatment, they can run safely while occupants are present in adjacent spaces. Ozone treatment remains effective for severe smoke odor in unoccupied structures — we use it in sealed, evacuated rooms where the concentration can be held long enough to work.

After active treatment, we verify results with air sampling and surface checks. A job is not complete until the odor is gone at the source, not just reduced at the doorway.

## Equipment Calibrated to Cranberry Township Conditions

Humidity is the variable that changes everything about deodorization timelines here. Cranberry Township's wet springs — when relative humidity inside unventilated basements can exceed 80% — slow the thermal fogging process and reduce the effective radius of hydroxyl generators. We account for this by staging commercial-grade desiccant dehumidifiers alongside odor-treatment equipment, bringing indoor humidity down to a range where deodorization chemistry actually works. Running a hydroxyl generator in a 78% RH basement without dehumidification first is a common shortcut that produces mediocre results; we do not take it.

For homes near the I-79 / Pennsylvania Turnpike interchange where traffic and commercial activity are heavier, diesel exhaust infiltration through foundation cracks is an occasional secondary odor complaint we have seen layered onto fire or water losses. Identifying that source early prevents a second service call.
