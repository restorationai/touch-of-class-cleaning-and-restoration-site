## Our Soot Removal Process in Gibsonia

Soot residue is not uniform. Protein-based soot from a kitchen fire behaves differently than the wet, smeared residue from a slow-smoldering chimney event or the dry, powdery ash from a fast-moving structure fire. Before any cleaning begins, we identify the soot type and the substrate — painted drywall, raw wood framing, stone veneer, fabric, HVAC ductwork — because the wrong cleaning method drives soot deeper into the material rather than lifting it.

Our process follows a consistent sequence:

1. **Containment** — sealing HVAC registers and doorways to prevent cross-contamination into unaffected rooms.
2. **Dry soot removal** — HEPA-filtered vacuuming of loose particulate from all surfaces before any wet cleaning agent is introduced.
3. **Chemical sponge and dry-cleaning methods** — used on porous or delicate surfaces like acoustic ceiling tile, unfinished wood, and textured walls common in Gibsonia's 1990s-era builds.
4. **Alkaline or solvent-based wet cleaning** — applied to hard surfaces, cabinetry, and sealed stone, calibrated to the soot type identified in step one.
5. **Odor treatment** — thermal fogging or hydroxyl generation to neutralize smoke odor molecules embedded in structural cavities, not just surface deodorization.
6. **Post-cleaning verification** — visual inspection and odor check before any reconstruction or repainting is approved.
