## Why Aliquippa Properties See Water Damage Issues

Much of Aliquippa's housing was built quickly in the early-to-mid 1900s to house Jones & Laughlin Steel workers, which means a large share of the borough's homes and duplexes are now past the 70-year mark on their original plumbing. Galvanized supply lines corrode from the inside out and often fail without warning, and cast iron drain stacks crack at the joints long before a homeowner sees a visible leak. Add in Beaver County's freeze-thaw winters, which stress pipes running through uninsulated crawlspaces and hillside foundations, and you get a steady pattern of pinhole leaks, burst lines, and sewer backups that show up every winter and early spring. The borough's hillside terrain also means a lot of basements were cut into clay-heavy soil, which drains slowly and holds groundwater against foundation walls after heavy rain.

## Our Water Damage Restoration Process in Aliquippa

Extraction starts the same way everywhere: pull standing water, get air moving, and protect what's still dry. In Aliquippa's older homes, the next steps change. Plaster-and-lath walls common in the borough's pre-1950 housing stock hold moisture longer than drywall and release it slowly, so we run drying equipment longer and recheck moisture content more often before we call a wall section dry. Narrow lots and tight alley access in the older sections of town also mean we sometimes stage equipment differently than we would at a newer build with a wide driveway, and we plan for that during the initial walkthrough instead of discovering it mid-job. Every job is documented and dried to the IICRC S500 standard regardless of the home's age, but the path to "dry" looks different in a 1920s duplex than it does in a house built after 2000.
