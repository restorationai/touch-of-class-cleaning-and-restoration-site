## Why Seven Fields Properties See Water Damage Differently

The housing stock along the Route 228 corridor and deeper into the Brandywine and Castlebrook neighborhoods follows a consistent pattern: attached and semi-attached townhomes and patio homes with shared walls, finished lower levels, and appliance-heavy kitchens and laundry areas on upper floors. That combination creates two compounding risks that older housing stock rarely presents.

First, second-floor laundry rooms — common in the floor plans popular when Seven Fields was platted — sit directly above finished kitchens or living areas. A failed supply hose or a clogged drain line means water travels through the subfloor and appears as a bubbling ceiling or soaked drywall one story below before anyone realizes there's a problem. Second, shared-wall construction in the townhome clusters near the Cranberry Township border means a loss that starts in one unit can migrate laterally through wall cavities into a neighbor's home within hours. Refrigerator ice-maker lines and dishwasher supply connections are also frequent culprits here. These are small-diameter lines under constant pressure, often routed through finished cabinetry where a slow leak goes unnoticed for weeks until flooring or cabinet bases show visible damage.

## Our Water Damage Restoration Process in Seven Fields

When we arrive, the first priority is stopping ongoing water intrusion if the source hasn't been shut off, then a full moisture mapping of the affected area using thermal imaging and calibrated meters. In Seven Fields homes, that mapping almost always extends beyond the obvious wet zone — engineered wood flooring, LVP, and the OSB subfloor beneath them hold moisture in ways that standard visual inspection misses.

Extraction comes next. Truck-mounted and portable extraction units pull standing water from carpet, hard flooring, and low-lying areas. For finished basements — a near-universal feature in Seven Fields — we assess whether the slab is holding moisture beneath flooring assemblies before setting drying equipment.

Structural drying follows a calculated placement of air movers and industrial dehumidifiers calibrated to the square footage, material types, and ambient conditions in the structure. We monitor drying progress with daily moisture readings and adjust equipment placement until affected materials reach their target moisture content. Documentation of every reading is kept throughout — useful both for your records and for insurance claims.

If building materials are saturated beyond recovery — drywall that has lost structural integrity, insulation that won't dry in place, flooring that has buckled — we handle controlled demolition and removal as part of the mitigation scope, not as a separate engagement.
