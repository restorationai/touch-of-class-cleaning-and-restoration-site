## Why Butler Properties Hold Odors Longer

Most of Butler's residential housing stock predates 1950. That matters for odor removal because older building materials are far more porous than modern alternatives. Original horsehair plaster, tongue-and-groove subfloors, and uninsulated balloon-frame cavities act like sponges — absorbing smoke particles, soot gases, and microbial off-gassing deep into surfaces that a simple wipe-down or spray deodorizer will never reach.

The cast-iron flue stacks common in Lyndora and East Butler homes also contribute. When a chimney fire or kitchen fire sends smoke through a cracked or open flue, the gases migrate into wall cavities and attic spaces along paths that weren't sealed when the house was built. By the time the fire department leaves, the odor source is already distributed through the structure — not just concentrated in one room.

Sewage odors follow a similar pattern. Low-lying streets near Connoquenessing Creek flood during heavy rain events, and when that water carries sewer surcharge into a basement, the biological compounds absorb into concrete block, wood framing, and stored belongings within hours. Waiting even a day or two before calling for professional deodorization makes the job measurably harder.

## Our Odor Removal and Deodorization Process in Butler

Effective deodorization isn't a single product — it's a sequence of methods matched to the odor type, the affected materials, and the structure itself. The process typically moves through these stages:

**Source removal first.** No deodorization technology eliminates odor from charred wood or saturated insulation that's still in place. Contaminated materials that can't be cleaned are removed before treatment begins.

**Thermal fogging** penetrates porous surfaces by converting a deodorizing agent into a fine fog that follows the same pathways smoke traveled — into wall cavities, under flooring, and into ceiling voids. It's particularly effective in the balloon-frame construction common in Butler's pre-war housing stock, where those open cavities give smoke (and fog) a clear path.

**Hydroxyl generation** runs continuously in occupied or semi-occupied spaces where chemical fogging isn't practical. Hydroxyl generators produce oxidizing radicals that neutralize odor molecules in the air and on surfaces without requiring the space to be vacated for extended periods.

**Ozone treatment** is reserved for unoccupied spaces with persistent smoke or biological odors. At elevated concentrations, ozone is highly effective at oxidizing odor compounds embedded in materials — but the space must remain unoccupied during treatment and ventilated thoroughly before re-entry.

**Encapsulants and sealers** close out the process on surfaces like concrete, drywall, and wood framing where residual odor compounds can't be fully removed. This is a finishing step, not a shortcut.
