## Our Smoke Damage Restoration Process in Gibsonia

Smoke damage restoration is not a single task — it is a sequenced set of interventions, and skipping steps is how odor returns six months later. Here is how we approach it in Gibsonia homes:

**Scope and air quality assessment.** Before any cleaning begins, we identify all affected zones, including areas that show no visible soot. Smoke follows air pressure differentials, which means finished basements, attic spaces, and interior closets often carry heavier contamination than the room of origin.

**Dry soot removal.** Dry chemical sponges lift loose soot from walls, ceilings, and trim before any wet cleaning. Applying water or cleaning solution to dry soot first drives it deeper into porous surfaces — a mistake that is especially costly in the plaster walls common in older Bakerstown homes.

**HVAC and ductwork decontamination.** Smoke residue in ductwork will re-circulate odor every time the system runs. We clean and treat duct interiors and replace filters before the system is returned to service.

**Surface cleaning and deodorization.** We use hydroxyl generators and thermal fogging to neutralize odor molecules at a chemical level rather than masking them. For the open-concept layouts typical in Treesdale and the Deer Creek area, hydroxyl treatment allows us to deodorize occupied adjacent spaces without the evacuation requirements that ozone treatment demands.

**Structural and content documentation.** Every affected surface and item is photographed and catalogued for your insurance carrier before it is cleaned, moved, or discarded.
