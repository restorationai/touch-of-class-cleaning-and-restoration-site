## Our Soot Removal Process in Zelienople

The first step on any soot job is mapping the contamination — not just what's visibly black, but where the invisible dry-smoke film has traveled. We use controlled lighting and chemical sponge test wipes to trace the residue path through the structure before any cleaning begins. In Zelienople's older homes, that mapping phase often reveals soot in rooms that looked untouched at first glance.

From there, the process follows a logical sequence:

- **Dry soot removal first.** Dry chemical sponges lift loose carbon particles from plaster, wood trim, and masonry without smearing. Wiping wet before dry removal is one of the most common mistakes in post-fire cleanup, and it permanently drives residue into porous surfaces.
- **HEPA vacuuming of cavities and surfaces.** Ductwork, cabinet interiors, and the spaces behind removable trim all get vacuumed before any wet cleaning begins.
- **Wet cleaning with pH-appropriate solutions.** Different surfaces require different chemistry. The original woodwork common in Main Street district homes responds well to alkaline cleaners; the same solution on galvanized metal surfaces can accelerate corrosion.
- **Odor treatment.** Soot smell is not just a surface issue — smoke odor compounds absorb into porous materials and off-gas for weeks if not addressed at the source. Thermal fogging or hydroxyl treatment is used based on the structure's ventilation profile.
- **Documentation throughout.** Before-and-after photos and written scope notes support your insurance claim and create a clear record of what was cleaned and how.
