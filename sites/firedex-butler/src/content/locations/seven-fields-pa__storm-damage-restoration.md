## Why Seven Fields Properties See Concentrated Storm Damage

The borough's layout amplifies storm losses in ways that aren't obvious until you've worked here. Townhome and patio-home clusters along the Brandywine and Wakefield Estates sections share party walls, which means a single tree strike or wind-driven roof breach can push water into two or three units before anyone realizes the scope. Finished basements — nearly universal in this housing stock — are the first casualty when gutters fail or downspout extensions wash away: water sheets off saturated ground and finds every crack in the foundation wall. The two-story floor plans with second-floor laundry rooms create a secondary risk that has nothing to do with wind: a storm power surge or a hose failure during a weather event sends water straight through the subfloor and into the kitchen ceiling below, a pattern FireDEX crews see repeatedly in this zip code.

The tree canopy along Brandywine Park and the interior green spaces throughout the borough adds another layer of exposure. Mature oaks and maples planted when the development was new are now large enough to drop significant limbs — or fall entirely — onto roofs, fences, and parked vehicles. Structural punctures from tree strikes require emergency tarping and board-up before any drying work can begin, and the clock matters: Pennsylvania summers mean mold colonization can begin within 24 to 48 hours of a moisture intrusion.
