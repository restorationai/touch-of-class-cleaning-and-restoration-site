## Why Butler Homes See Above-Average Fire Risk

Butler's housing stock tells the story. The majority of single-family homes in the 16001 and 16002 ZIP codes were built before 1950, when residential construction codes looked very different. Balloon framing — where exterior wall studs run continuously from foundation to roof without fire stops — was the norm, and it creates a chimney effect inside the wall when a fire starts. Combine that with original knob-and-tube wiring that has been spliced and extended over decades, unlined masonry chimneys on the older foursquares near the Butler County Courthouse corridor, and cast-iron supply lines that corrode and fail — and you have a pattern that fire investigators and restoration crews see repeatedly in this part of Western PA.

## Our Fire Damage Restoration Process in Butler

After the fire department clears the scene, the clock starts on a different kind of damage. Smoke residue continues to etch metal fixtures and discolor porous surfaces for days after the flames are out. Soot from synthetic materials — insulation, wiring jackets, vinyl flooring — is oily and acidic, and it behaves differently than the dry soot from a wood fire. Our process accounts for that distinction.

**Structural assessment and board-up.** We secure the structure first — windows, doors, roof penetrations — to prevent weather intrusion and unauthorized entry. In Butler's climate, an unsecured fire-damaged home can take on water from rain or snow within hours, adding a water damage layer to an already complex loss.

**Controlled demolition of unsalvageable material.** In pre-1950 homes, this often means removing plaster and lath rather than drywall. Plaster absorbs smoke odor deeply and is rarely worth attempting to clean; removing it also exposes the wall cavity so we can confirm fire travel and treat the framing.

**Smoke and soot cleaning.** We use dry chemical sponges, alkaline cleaners calibrated to soot type, and thermal fogging or hydroxyl generation for odor neutralization. Exposed brick — common in older Butler homes — requires specific cleaning chemistry to avoid driving soot deeper into the mortar joints.

**Structural drying and dehumidification.** Firefighting water must come out before reconstruction begins. We use desiccant and refrigerant dehumidifiers with monitoring sensors to document moisture levels in framing and subfloor.

**Reconstruction coordination.** Our team holds a Pennsylvania Home Improvement Contractor Registration (#PA001913), which means we can carry the job from emergency mitigation through finished rebuild under one contract — one point of contact for your insurance adjuster.
