The Breakneck Creek valley geography that makes Evans City picturesque also shapes how fires behave and how smoke spreads after them. ## Our Smoke Damage Restoration Process in Evans City

When we arrive at a property in the 16033 ZIP code, the first step is a systematic scope — not a quick visual walkthrough. We use thermal imaging and air sampling to map where smoke has traveled, including inside wall cavities, HVAC ductwork, and crawl spaces. In the older homes near Evans City Cemetery and along the lower blocks of the borough, that often means pulling duct registers and inspecting systems that haven't been serviced in decades, because smoke residue in a forced-air system will re-contaminate a cleaned room every time the heat kicks on.

Once the scope is documented, the process moves through four concrete phases:

- **Containment and ventilation** — negative air pressure units isolate affected zones and begin pulling smoke-laden air out of the structure.
- **Dry chemical and wet cleaning** — different soot types (protein residue from kitchen fires, petroleum-based residue from synthetic materials, smoke residue from wood-frame fires) require different cleaning agents and agitation methods. We match the chemistry to what burned.
- **Deodorization** — hydroxyl generators and thermal fogging reach into the same porous cavities that absorbed the odor compounds. This is not an air freshener step; it's a chemical neutralization step.
- **Content and structural restoration** — salvageable materials are cleaned in place or packed out; surfaces that can't be restored are documented for insurance replacement.
