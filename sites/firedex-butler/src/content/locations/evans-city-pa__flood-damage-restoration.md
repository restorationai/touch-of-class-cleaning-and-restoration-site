## Our Flood Damage Restoration Process in Evans City

Every job starts with a thorough moisture assessment before a single piece of equipment is placed. In Evans City's older housing stock, that means probing stone foundation walls, checking the underside of original hardwood subfloors, and identifying whether water has wicked into plaster lath — a material that holds moisture long after the surface feels dry to the touch.

Once the scope is mapped, we extract standing water using truck-mounted and portable extraction units, then establish a drying system calibrated to the structure. Stone and block foundations release moisture slowly and unevenly, so we position desiccant or refrigerant dehumidifiers and air movers to pull vapor out of the assembly rather than just the air. Moisture readings are logged at each visit so you — and your insurance adjuster — have a documented drying curve, not just a verbal assurance. Affected materials that cannot be dried in place (saturated insulation, compromised subfloor sections, wicking drywall) are removed and documented before reconstruction begins.
