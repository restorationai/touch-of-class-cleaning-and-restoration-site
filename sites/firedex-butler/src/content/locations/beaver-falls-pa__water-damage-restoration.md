## Our Water Damage Restoration Process in Beaver Falls

When we arrive at a Beaver Falls property, the first priority is stopping ongoing intrusion — shutting the supply, isolating the affected drain line, or boarding a roof breach — before any drying equipment goes in. From there, the process follows a structured sequence:

**Inspection and moisture mapping.** We use thermal imaging and calibrated moisture meters to trace water migration through walls, subfloors, and ceiling assemblies. In plaster-and-lath construction, common throughout College Hill, water travels laterally along lath strips before showing any visible staining — so the wet zone is often wider than it looks.

**Water extraction.** Truck-mounted and portable extraction units pull standing water from finished floors, subfloor cavities, and crawl spaces. In basement situations common to Beaver Falls stone-foundation homes, we address both the pooled water and the saturated block or fieldstone itself.

**Structural drying and dehumidification.** Commercial-grade desiccant dehumidifiers and high-velocity air movers are positioned to create a controlled drying environment. We monitor moisture readings daily and adjust equipment placement until structural readings return to pre-loss baselines.

**Content and material assessment.** We document damaged materials for your insurance claim, separate salvageable contents from unsalvageable ones, and coordinate any necessary demolition of compromised drywall, insulation, or flooring before reconstruction begins.
