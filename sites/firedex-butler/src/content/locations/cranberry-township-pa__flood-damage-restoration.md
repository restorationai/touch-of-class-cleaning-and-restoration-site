## Our Flood Damage Restoration Process in Cranberry Township

Every flood job starts with a moisture map, not a guess. Before any equipment is placed, a technician walks the affected area with a thermal imaging camera and calibrated moisture meters to trace exactly how far water has migrated — under floating floors, behind baseboards, inside wall cavities. In Cranberry Township's finished basements, that migration path often extends further than it looks because water follows the path of least resistance along concrete slab edges.

Once the scope is documented, standing water comes out first using truck-mounted extraction units. Structural drying follows with industrial-grade desiccant dehumidifiers and high-velocity air movers positioned to create a controlled drying chamber. Readings are logged daily so the drying curve is documented — that documentation matters for insurance adjusters and for confirming the structure is genuinely dry, not just surface-dry.

If materials can't be saved — saturated drywall below the flood line, buckled laminate, compromised insulation — they're removed cleanly and the framing is treated before any reconstruction begins. Skipping that step is how mold establishes itself in walls that look fine from the outside.
