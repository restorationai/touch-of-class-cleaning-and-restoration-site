## Why Mars Properties See Distinctive Fire Damage Challenges

Mars Borough's housing stock spans more than a century. The older homes closest to the borough center often feature original horsehair plaster walls, balloon-frame construction, and cast-iron drain systems — materials that behave very differently under fire and suppression water than modern OSB and PVC. Balloon framing, common in pre-1950 builds, allows fire to travel vertically inside wall cavities with almost no resistance, meaning smoke and heat can reach the attic in minutes even when the visible burn area looks contained.

Out along Three Degree Road and into Adams Ridge, the story shifts. Newer tract and semi-custom homes built over finished basements present a different hazard: fire that starts near a laundry room or utility closet can push smoke through HVAC returns and deposit soot throughout the entire living space before the fire itself spreads far. Finished lower levels also trap suppression water, which begins promoting secondary mold growth within 48 to 72 hours if not extracted and dried immediately.

## Our Fire Damage Restoration Process in Mars

The first priority after the fire department clears the scene is stabilization. That means emergency board-up and roof tarping if the structure is compromised, followed by a systematic walk-through to document every affected surface before anything is disturbed — a step that matters as much for your insurance adjuster as it does for the restoration plan.

Once documentation is complete, the crew moves into controlled demolition of unsalvageable materials, HEPA vacuuming of dry soot from surfaces and cavities, and thermal fogging or hydroxyl treatment to begin neutralizing smoke odor at the molecular level. In older Mars Borough homes with plaster walls, soot removal requires slower, more deliberate hand-cleaning — aggressive mechanical methods can damage original surfaces that are worth preserving. Structural drying runs concurrently using commercial dehumidifiers and air movers calibrated to the actual square footage and material types on-site, not a one-size estimate.

Final steps include odor verification, rebuilt or repaired structural elements, and a walkthrough with the homeowner before the job is closed.
