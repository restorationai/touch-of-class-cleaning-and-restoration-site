## Our Smoke Damage Restoration Process in Cranberry Township

Smoke damage restoration follows a defined sequence because skipping steps creates callbacks. Here's how the work unfolds on a Cranberry Township loss:

**Scope and air quality assessment.** Before any cleaning begins, the affected areas are mapped — including the HVAC system, attic space if smoke migrated upward, and any finished basement level. Air quality readings establish a baseline.

**Dry soot removal.** Loose soot is removed with HEPA-filtered vacuums before any wet cleaning. Applying liquid to dry soot drives it deeper into porous materials.

**Chemical sponge and surface cleaning.** Walls, ceilings, cabinetry, and structural surfaces are cleaned with chemistry matched to the soot type — protein residue from kitchen fires behaves differently than petroleum-based smoke from an electrical or structural fire.

**Thermal fogging and hydroxyl treatment.** Odor-bearing molecules that have penetrated porous materials — insulation, wood framing, fabric — are neutralized using thermal fogging or hydroxyl generator technology. This step is particularly important in Cranberry Township's finished basements, where carpet padding and drywall cavities hold odor long after surfaces look clean.

**Duct cleaning and HVAC decontamination.** Because smoke travels through forced-air systems, ductwork is inspected and cleaned to prevent recirculation of contaminated air after restoration.

**Sealing and reconstruction coordination.** Affected drywall, trim, and surfaces are sealed before repainting. Where structural materials require replacement, FireDEX Butler coordinates the reconstruction phase under our Pennsylvania Home Improvement Contractor Registration (#PA001913).
