## Why Wexford Properties See Distinct Reconstruction Challenges

The housing stock in the 15090 ZIP code is newer than much of Allegheny County, but newer doesn't mean simpler. Finished walk-out basements in Pine Township and Franklin Park borders are common — and when a water heater fails or a sump pump loses power during a hard freeze, those lower levels can sustain losses that run well into six figures: engineered hardwood, custom cabinetry, dedicated home theater wiring, and high-end HVAC zoning all need to be matched on the rebuild.

Bonus rooms over garages are a signature feature of Wexford's larger homes, and they're also a freeze-pipe vulnerability. The space below those rooms is often unconditioned or poorly insulated, and in a cold snap the supply lines feeding those upper bathrooms can rupture without warning. Wooded lots on well water — common on the quieter roads that wind away from the Route 910 / I-79 interchange — add another layer of complexity: pressure-tank failures and well-line breaks can go undetected longer, meaning water migration spreads further before anyone calls.

Fire damage in this neighborhood profile tends to involve complex roof structures — hip roofs, dormers, and attached three-car garages — where structural reconstruction requires precise framing to satisfy both the original architectural intent and current Allegheny County building codes.

## Our Reconstruction Process in Wexford

Every rebuild starts with a scope that insurance adjusters can actually use. We photograph and document affected materials before anything is touched, itemizing finishes by grade so the claim reflects real replacement cost — not a generic line item for "flooring" when what burned was hand-scraped hickory.

From there, the process follows a logical sequence:

1. **Structural assessment and permitting** — We pull the required Allegheny County building permits and, where applicable, coordinate with Pine Township or Franklin Park Township inspectors before framing begins.
2. **Structural framing and sheathing** — Whether it's a fire-damaged roof deck, a water-compromised floor system, or a collapsed interior wall, we rebuild to current code with materials specified to match the original construction class.
3. **Mechanical rough-in coordination** — We schedule HVAC, plumbing, and electrical subcontractors in the correct sequence so inspections don't create delays.
4. **Insulation and drywall** — Finished basements and bonus rooms get closed-cell spray foam where appropriate to address the thermal bridging that caused the problem in the first place.
5. **Finish carpentry and surface restoration** — Custom millwork, cabinetry, and flooring are sourced to match existing finishes. We work with local suppliers who stock the grades common in Wexford builds.
6. **Final inspection and punch-list** — We walk the property with you before closing out the job.

Because FireDEX Butler handles both the mitigation and the reconstruction under one license (PA001913, Pennsylvania Attorney General — Home Improvement Contractor Registration), there is no handoff gap where work stalls between phases.
