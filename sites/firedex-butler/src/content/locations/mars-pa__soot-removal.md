## Our Soot Removal Process in Mars

Soot removal is not a single step. The chemistry of smoke residue changes depending on what burned — synthetic materials produce a wet, smearing residue; natural wood fires leave a dry, powdery char — and the cleaning method has to match. Getting that wrong means pushing soot deeper into the substrate rather than lifting it.

The process FireDEX Butler uses in Mars follows a consistent sequence:

1. **Damage mapping** — Every affected surface is documented before anything is touched. Photos, moisture readings, and surface-type notes guide the cleaning protocol and support your insurance claim.
2. **Dry soot removal first** — Dry-chemistry sponges and HEPA-filtered vacuums pull loose particulate off walls, ceilings, and structural members before any wet cleaning begins. Skipping this step turns dry soot into permanent staining.
3. **Surface-specific wet cleaning** — Alkaline or neutral cleaners are matched to the substrate. Plaster in Mars Borough's older homes requires a gentler approach than the painted drywall in newer Adams Township builds.
4. **Deodorization** — Thermal fogging or hydroxyl generation neutralizes odor molecules embedded in porous materials, not just masking surface smells.
5. **Final documentation** — Cleaned surfaces are re-photographed and readings are logged for the insurance file.

Because FireDEX Butler operates 24/7, the call can happen the same night as the fire — which matters, because soot begins etching surfaces and off-gassing acidic compounds within hours.
