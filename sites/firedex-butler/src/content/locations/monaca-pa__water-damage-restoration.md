## Why Monaca Properties See Water Damage Issues

A lot of Monaca's housing stock dates back to the borough's mill-town boom in the early-to-mid 1900s, which means cast iron and galvanized supply lines that are well past their service life in plenty of homes. Those pipes corrode from the inside out, and a slow leak behind a wall can go unnoticed for weeks before it shows up as a stain or a soft spot in the floor. Add in basements built into riverbank soil that holds moisture longer after heavy rain, and you get a town where groundwater seepage and plumbing failures both show up more than they would in newer, drier-sited developments. Winter freeze-thaw cycles along the river valley also stress older pipe joints, which is why we see a steady run of burst-pipe calls once temperatures swing hard in January and February.

## Our Water Damage Restoration Process in Monaca

We start with extraction, pulling standing water with truck-mount and portable units depending on how deep into the structure the water has traveled. From there we move to assessment: moisture meters and thermal imaging tell us whether water has tracked under flooring, up into plaster, or into the wall cavity, which matters a great deal in Monaca's older homes where lath-and-plaster walls hold water differently than drywall. Structural drying follows, calibrated to the specific materials in the home rather than a generic timeline, because plaster and old-growth lumber release moisture more slowly than modern building materials. We document every stage with photos and moisture readings, which becomes the paper trail your insurance adjuster will want to see.
