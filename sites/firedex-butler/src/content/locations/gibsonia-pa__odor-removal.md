## Our Deodorization Process, Calibrated to Gibsonia Conditions

Odor removal isn't a single step — it's a sequence, and skipping steps is why odors return after a few weeks. The process starts with a source assessment: locating every material that has absorbed the odor, including hidden cavities behind knee walls, HVAC ductwork, and subflooring. In Gibsonia's finished-basement homes, that often means pulling up carpet tack strips and inspecting the concrete slab edge where odor-laden moisture collects.

Once affected materials are identified, the appropriate delivery method is selected for the specific odor type and structure:

- **Thermal fogging** — a solvent-based fog that follows the same pathways smoke traveled, reaching inside wall cavities and ductwork. Effective for fire and smoke odor in homes where the HVAC distributed particles throughout the structure.
- **Hydroxyl deodorization** — UV-generated hydroxyl radicals that oxidize odor molecules in occupied or sensitive spaces. Useful in homes where re-occupancy timelines are tight or where ozone-sensitive materials like leather, rubber, or electronics are present.
- **Ozone treatment** — high-concentration ozone in a fully evacuated structure, appropriate for severe smoke or biological odors when the home can be vacated for the required treatment period.
- **Counteractant application** — direct surface treatment of porous materials (drywall, wood framing, concrete) with encapsulant or neutralizing agents before any structural repairs close off access.

The method — or combination of methods — depends on the odor source, the building's construction, and how long the odor has had to penetrate. A chimney fire caught the same day requires a different protocol than smoke that sat in a closed house for two weeks while a family stayed in a hotel.
