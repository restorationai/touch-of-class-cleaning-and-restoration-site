The housing stock in and around Gibsonia spans several decades and several risk profiles. ## Our Water Damage Restoration Process in Gibsonia

The first thing we do on arrival is locate the water source and stop the flow — obvious in concept, but it requires knowing where shutoffs are in homes that may have been built by a dozen different builders over thirty years. Once the source is controlled, we use thermal imaging cameras and moisture meters to map how far water has traveled. In a finished Treesdale basement, that often means checking behind entertainment-center walls, under luxury vinyl plank flooring, and inside the mechanicals closet.

Extraction comes next. Truck-mounted and portable extraction units pull standing water from carpet, padding, and hard flooring before structural drying begins. We then set a calculated drying system — commercial-grade dehumidifiers and air movers positioned according to the room's cubic footage and the materials involved — and monitor moisture readings on a daily basis until every affected assembly reaches its dry standard. Nothing gets closed up until the numbers confirm it's dry.

For losses that have touched framing, insulation, or drywall, we handle the controlled demolition needed to expose wet cavities and prevent secondary damage. That work is documented with photographs and moisture logs, which feeds directly into the insurance claim.

## Gibsonia Insurance Coordination

Most standard homeowners policies cover sudden and accidental water losses — a burst pipe, a failed water heater, an appliance supply line. What they typically don't cover is gradual seepage or maintenance-deferred failures, which is why documenting the cause of loss accurately from the first hour matters. We photograph source, path of travel, and affected materials before anything is moved or removed. That documentation package goes to your adjuster and is formatted to match what carriers expect, which tends to keep the claim moving rather than stalling on missing information.

If you carry a sewer or water backup rider — worth having in any home with an ejector pump or older lateral lines — we can help you understand what that endorsement covers before work begins.
