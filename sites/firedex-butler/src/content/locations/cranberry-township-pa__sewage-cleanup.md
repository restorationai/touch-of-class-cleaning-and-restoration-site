## Our Sewage Cleanup and Sanitization Process in Cranberry Township

Sewage is classified as Category 3 water — the most contaminated class — and the process for handling it is more involved than a standard water loss. Here is what happens from the moment we arrive:

**Containment and safety assessment.** We establish a contamination perimeter before any extraction begins. In finished Cranberry Township basements, that means identifying whether sewage has wicked into adjacent rooms through shared flooring or wall cavities.

**Extraction and removal.** Truck-mounted extraction equipment pulls standing sewage and saturated material. Porous materials — carpet, pad, drywall below the contamination line, insulation — are bagged and removed. There is no safe way to dry and restore Category 3-saturated porous material in place.

**Sanitization.** Every hard surface that contacted sewage or sewage-laden water is treated with EPA-registered antimicrobial agents. This step is not optional and cannot be abbreviated. We apply, dwell, and document.

**Structural drying.** After sanitization, industrial air movers and dehumidifiers run until moisture readings in framing, subfloor, and concrete return to baseline. In Cranberry Township's humid spring and summer conditions, this typically takes three to five days with equipment running continuously.

**Post-remediation verification.** Before any reconstruction begins, we verify that surfaces are dry and that no residual contamination remains.
