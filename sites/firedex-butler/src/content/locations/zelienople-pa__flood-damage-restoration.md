## Our Flood Damage Restoration Process in Zelienople

The first priority on any flood call is stopping active water intrusion and documenting the full scope before anything is moved or discarded — insurance carriers require it, and in older homes the hidden damage is often worse than what's visible. Here's how the work typically unfoys:

**Water extraction and moisture mapping.** Truck-mounted extractors pull standing water from floors and cavities. Thermal imaging and moisture meters map saturation behind plaster walls and under original hardwood floors — materials that look dry on the surface but can read 30–40% moisture content an inch in.

**Controlled demolition and drying.** In Zelienople's pre-war homes, plaster releases moisture significantly more slowly than modern drywall. We set commercial-grade desiccant dehumidifiers and high-velocity air movers calibrated to the actual cubic footage and material type, not a generic formula. Drying logs are kept daily and provided to your adjuster.

**Structural assessment and repair.** Stone and brick foundations that have absorbed floodwater need time to dry before any repointing or waterproofing work begins. Rushing that step traps moisture and leads to efflorescence, spalling, and recurring seepage. We sequence the work to match the material, not the calendar.

**Sanitization and odor control.** Floodwater from the creek or a backed-up drain line is Category 3 water — it carries bacteria and sediment that require EPA-registered antimicrobial treatment on every affected surface.
