When a summer cloudburst pushes the creek over its banks and storm runoff funnels down toward the low blocks off Main Street, moisture finds its way into crawl spaces and block foundations that were already aging when Eisenhower was president. ## Why Evans City Borough Homes See Persistent Mold Problems

The housing stock along and around Evans City's Route 68 corridor tells the story plainly: most of these homes were built between 1900 and 1950, with stone or block foundations that were never designed to handle the hydrostatic pressure that creek-valley geography generates. Original drain tile — where it exists at all — is clay or cast iron, prone to root intrusion and collapse. Sump pits in many of these basements were retrofitted decades after construction and are often undersized for the volume of water a serious storm event delivers.

Winter adds another layer. Freeze-thaw cycles crack aging supply lines inside walls and under slabs. A slow drip behind a plaster wall in January may go unnoticed until spring, by which point the framing cavity has been wet long enough to support a well-established mold colony. The combination of creek-valley flooding risk and cold-weather pipe failures means mold remediation in Evans City isn't a rare event — it's a recurring consequence of where and how these homes were built.

Forward Township properties on the borough's outskirts face a related but distinct pattern: outbuildings, well houses, and agricultural structures with minimal vapor barriers and high ambient humidity create ideal conditions for mold growth that can eventually migrate into attached living spaces.

## Our Mold Remediation Process in Evans City

Every job starts with a thorough moisture mapping inspection — not just the visible stain, but the surrounding materials, the wall cavities behind it, and the subfloor below it. In Evans City's older frame homes, mold rarely stays contained to one surface. It follows the moisture path, which in a stone-foundation home can mean traveling along a sill plate for 20 feet before becoming visible.

Once the scope is defined, we establish negative-air containment using 6-mil poly barriers and HEPA-filtered air scrubbers to prevent spores from migrating to unaffected areas of the home during removal. Affected porous materials — drywall, insulation, compromised wood framing — are removed, bagged, and disposed of per Pennsylvania DEP guidelines. Non-porous surfaces are treated with EPA-registered antimicrobial agents. Structural wood that is salvageable is HEPA-vacuumed, treated, and dried to verified moisture content before any encapsulation or rebuild begins.

Because so many Evans City homes have plaster-and-lath interior walls rather than modern drywall, we adjust our removal boundaries carefully. Plaster is dense and slow to release moisture, which means the framing cavity behind it can stay wet — and actively growing mold — long after the plaster surface appears dry to the touch.
