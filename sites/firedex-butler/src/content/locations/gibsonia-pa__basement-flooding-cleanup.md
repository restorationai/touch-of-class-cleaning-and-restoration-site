## Our Basement Flooding Cleanup Process in Gibsonia

The first step on every flooded basement call is moisture mapping — not just mopping up what's visible. Standing water gets extracted with truck-mounted or portable units depending on access, then the crew uses thermal imaging and moisture meters to trace how far water has wicked into framing, insulation cavities, and concrete block walls. In Gibsonia's finished basements, that often means pulling baseboard trim and drilling weep holes in drywall to let trapped moisture escape before it feeds mold.

Drying equipment — industrial desiccant or refrigerant dehumidifiers and high-velocity air movers — gets positioned based on the room layout and the materials involved. Concrete slabs under floating floors hold moisture longer than they appear to, and we monitor readings daily until every affected assembly reaches its dry standard. Contaminated-water losses (ejector pump, sewage backup, or any Category 3 event) require antimicrobial treatment and, in many cases, controlled demolition of porous materials that can't be reliably sanitized.

Throughout the process, we document with photographs and moisture logs — the kind of organized file that makes an insurance adjuster's job straightforward.
