## Why Mars Properties See Basement Flooding Issues

Mars sits at an interesting intersection of housing eras, and each era brings its own water vulnerability. The century-old homes in Mars Borough proper were built on rubble-stone and mortar foundations that were never designed to be fully waterproof — they were built to "breathe." When the water table rises after a heavy storm along the Route 228 corridor, those foundations seep. Original cast-iron drain lines, common in homes built before the 1950s, corrode from the inside out; a partial blockage during a high-flow event backs water up into the basement floor drain before any pump can keep pace.

Move a mile or two out toward Adams Ridge and Three Degree Road, and the problem shifts entirely. Tract and semi-custom homes built in the last fifteen years often have fully finished lower levels — drywall, LVP flooring, home-theater setups — sitting directly over a slab. Builder-grade supply lines on dishwashers, refrigerators, and washing machines have a well-documented failure window around the 7–10 year mark. When one of those lines lets go over a finished basement, the water is hidden behind walls and under flooring long before it pools visibly.

## Our Basement Flooding Cleanup Process in Mars

The first thing we do on arrival is map the moisture — not just the standing water you can see, but the water that has already migrated into wall cavities, under subfloors, and behind baseboards. Thermal imaging and calibrated moisture meters tell us exactly how far the event traveled. That mapping drives every decision that follows.

Standing water comes out first with truck-mounted extraction equipment. For stone-foundation homes in Mars Borough, we pay close attention to the floor drain and any below-grade window wells, which are common secondary entry points. Once bulk water is removed, we set industrial air movers and desiccant or refrigerant dehumidifiers in a calculated pattern — the number of units, their placement, and the drying targets are all set to the specific square footage and material types in your basement, not a generic formula.

Structural materials that cannot be dried in place — saturated drywall, soaked insulation, buckled flooring — are removed and documented before disposal. We photograph everything and produce a written scope that your insurance adjuster can work from directly. Drying progress is monitored on a daily basis until all readings return to pre-loss moisture levels.

## Local Note

One thing that catches homeowners off guard in the older sections of Mars Borough: the original clay-tile floor drains in pre-1960 basements are often connected to the sanitary sewer rather than a storm line. During a combined-sewer surcharge event — which can happen when Breakneck Creek watershed is saturated — those drains can backflow rather than drain. If your basement water smells like sewage rather than clean groundwater, that changes the cleanup protocol significantly. Category 3 (sewage-contaminated) water requires additional protective equipment, antimicrobial treatment, and more aggressive material removal than a clean-water flood. We assess contamination level on arrival and adjust the scope accordingly — it is not something to guess at.
