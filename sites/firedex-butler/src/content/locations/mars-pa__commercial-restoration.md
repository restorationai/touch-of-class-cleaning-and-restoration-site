## Our Commercial Restoration Process, Calibrated to Mars Conditions

Commercial jobs require a different sequencing than residential work — tenants, employees, and customers may need partial access while restoration is underway, and every day of full closure has a dollar figure attached to it.

We start with a documented scope assessment: moisture mapping with thermal imaging, air quality readings where smoke or mold is suspected, and a written inventory of affected materials. For water losses, extraction begins the same visit. Drying equipment — desiccant dehumidifiers and high-velocity air movers — is staged to protect the portions of the building still in use wherever the floor plan allows a containment boundary.

For fire and smoke losses, the odor work is often what takes longest. Smoke residue from a commercial kitchen fire behaves differently than a residential blaze: grease-laden smoke penetrates HVAC ductwork, coats hard surfaces with a protein film, and embeds in acoustic ceiling tiles in ways that standard cleaning misses. We use thermal fogging and hydroxyl generation as appropriate to the occupancy type, and we document every step for the insurance adjuster.

Reconstruction is handled in-house — drywall, flooring, painting, millwork — so the property owner is not coordinating a separate GC while the space sits idle.

One thing that catches property owners off guard in the older Mars Borough commercial blocks: the stone and brick foundation walls common in buildings constructed before the 1950s do not dry on the same schedule as poured concrete.
