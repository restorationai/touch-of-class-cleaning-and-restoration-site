## Why Wexford Properties See Burst Pipe Issues

The housing stock in Wexford is dominated by large two-story and estate homes built between the early 1990s and the late 2000s — a generation of construction that relied heavily on polybutylene and early CPVC supply lines that are now reaching the end of their service life. Those materials become brittle under repeated freeze-thaw stress, and Wexford gets that stress reliably: the wooded lots and slightly elevated terrain around the Treesdale fringe and Village of Pine funnel cold air down slopes during January and February cold snaps, and bonus rooms cantilevered over three-car garages are among the first spaces to lose heat when a furnace cycles off overnight.

## Our Burst Pipe Cleanup and Repair Process in Wexford

The first thing we do on arrival is locate and confirm the source — not always the obvious one. A supply line that bursts inside a wall cavity can travel along a top plate and surface in a room two doors away. We use moisture mapping and thermal imaging to trace the migration path before any demolition begins, which matters on high-finish homes where opening the wrong wall panel means matching expensive millwork.

Once the source is confirmed and the water is shut down, extraction comes first. Standing water under flooring — particularly the glue-down LVP and nail-down hardwood common in Wexford homes built after 2000 — has to come out fast. We deploy truck-mounted extraction alongside portable units for lower-level spaces, then set drying systems calibrated to the actual cubic footage and material density of the affected area. Drying is documented with daily moisture readings so your insurance adjuster has a clear record of progression.

Structural repairs — framing, drywall, insulation, subfloor — are handled in-house. We hold Pennsylvania Home Improvement Contractor Registration (#PA001913), so the mitigation and the rebuild are coordinated under one project rather than handed off between separate contractors.
