## Why Cranberry Township Homes Present Specific Soot Challenges

Cranberry Township's housing stock spans roughly six decades, and that range matters when soot gets into a structure. The 1960s-era Fernway plats were built with plaster-over-lath walls in many rooms — a surface that absorbs smoke residue differently than the paper-faced drywall in homes built after 1990. Plaster is denser, so soot doesn't penetrate as deeply, but it's also more fragile under aggressive cleaning. Use the wrong chemical or too much mechanical pressure and you're refinishing a wall instead of cleaning one.

On the newer end, the 1990s-through-2020s two-story plans common throughout the 16066 ZIP code typically have open floor plans with high ceilings and shared HVAC returns. In a fire event, that layout distributes soot-laden air efficiently through the entire house before the system shuts down. Upstairs bedrooms that were nowhere near the fire source can end up with a film of oily residue on every horizontal surface — shelving, ceiling fan blades, the tops of door casings. Identifying every affected zone before cleanup begins is the step that separates a complete job from a callback.

## Our Soot Removal Process in Cranberry Township

Soot cleanup is not a single step. It's a sequence, and skipping any stage leaves odor and residue behind even when surfaces look clean.

We start with a room-by-room assessment to map the soot type — dry smoke from fast-burning materials behaves differently than the wet, sticky residue left by slow-smoldering synthetic materials like upholstery foam or PVC wiring insulation. Both are common in residential fires here, and they require different chemical agents and application methods.

Once the type is confirmed, we pre-treat surfaces with dry chemical sponges on loose soot before introducing any liquid cleaner. Applying a wet cleaner to unsponged soot drives the particles deeper into porous surfaces — a mistake that creates permanent staining. After dry removal, we apply pH-balanced cleaning solutions appropriate to the surface: different formulas for painted drywall, wood trim, brick, and fabric. HVAC registers and ductwork are sealed or cleaned depending on how far the smoke migrated.

Odor control runs parallel to surface cleaning. Thermal fogging and hydroxyl generation neutralize smoke molecules that have bonded to materials rather than just sitting on top of them. In homes with finished basements — which describes the majority of properties we work in throughout Cranberry Township — we pay particular attention to stairwells and lower-level HVAC equipment, where smoke stratifies and settles.
