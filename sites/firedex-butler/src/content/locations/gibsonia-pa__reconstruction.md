The housing stock across Gibsonia is unusually varied for a single postal area. ## Our Reconstruction Process in Gibsonia

Every project starts with a scope document, not an estimate. We walk the loss with the property owner, photograph structural members, identify load-bearing elements, and note anything that will require a Richland Township building permit before work begins. Pennsylvania's Home Improvement Contractor Registration (license #PA001913) covers our reconstruction work, and we pull permits under that registration so the inspection record stays with the property.

Demolition of unsalvageable material comes next — wet insulation, charred framing, smoke-saturated drywall. On Treesdale-area homes with finished lower levels, that often means removing a complete living space down to the concrete before rebuilding. We sequence the work so mechanical rough-ins (electrical, plumbing, HVAC) are inspected before walls close, which is a step some contractors skip and homeowners later regret when they need to sell.

Framing, sheathing, insulation, and drywall follow in order. Final finishes — paint, flooring, trim, cabinetry — are matched to pre-loss condition as closely as materials allow. We document the match process in writing for the insurance file.
