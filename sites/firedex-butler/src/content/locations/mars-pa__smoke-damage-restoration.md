## Why Mars Properties See Smoke Damage Differently

Mars Borough sits at an interesting crossroads of housing stock. The century-old homes clustered near the center of the Borough — many with original plaster walls, cast-iron drain stacks, and uninsulated balloon-frame cavities — absorb smoke residue in ways that newer construction simply doesn't. Plaster is porous in a different way than modern drywall: it pulls soot deeper into its substrate, and the air gaps inside balloon framing act as chimneys themselves, pulling smoke vertically from a first-floor fire event up through wall cavities into attic spaces that homeowners never think to check.

Out along the Route 228 growth corridor in Adams Ridge and the newer Adams Township sections, the housing stock shifts to builder-era tract and semi-custom homes — finished basements, open floor plans, and HVAC systems with more square footage of ductwork to contaminate. A fire in the kitchen of one of these homes can push smoke residue through a forced-air system into every room within minutes. The cleanup scope is often larger than it looks from the doorway.

## Our Smoke Damage Restoration Process in Mars

The first thing we do on-site is scope the full migration path of the smoke — not just the room of origin. That means checking HVAC returns, attic access points, crawl spaces, and in older Borough homes, the spaces behind original plaster and lathe. Smoke follows air movement, and in a house that's been sealed up while you waited for help, it has had time to settle into every low-pressure cavity it could find.

From there, the process moves through four concrete phases:

1. **Containment and ventilation** — negative air pressure is established in the affected zone to stop cross-contamination to clean areas.
2. **Dry-smoke and wet-smoke separation** — the type of material that burned determines the cleaning chemistry. Protein residues from kitchen fires require enzyme-based agents; structural wood and drywall fires leave a different soot profile entirely.
3. **Surface cleaning and deodorization** — walls, ceilings, contents, and ductwork are cleaned using methods matched to the substrate. Thermal fogging or hydroxyl generation is used for embedded odor in porous materials.
4. **Verification and documentation** — every affected surface is documented with photos and moisture readings before we close out, which matters directly for your insurance claim.
