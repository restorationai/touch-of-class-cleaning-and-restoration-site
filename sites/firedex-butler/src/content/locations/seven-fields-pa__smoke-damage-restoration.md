The housing stock in 16046 is relatively uniform in age — most of it constructed between the late 1980s and early 2000s — which creates a predictable set of vulnerabilities. Synthetic materials dominate: engineered hardwood, laminate cabinetry, vinyl flooring, and spray-foam insulation. When these materials burn, even partially, they release oily, acidic smoke residues that bond aggressively to surfaces. ## Our Smoke Damage Restoration Process in Seven Fields

The first thing our crew does on arrival is map the smoke migration — not just the room of origin, but every space the smoke touched. In Seven Fields homes, that typically means tracing the HVAC return path, checking finished basement ceilings below the kitchen, and inspecting second-floor closets and attic bypasses. Smoke follows air, and air follows pressure differentials; a house that "looks fine" upstairs often has heavy residue in the air handler.

From there, the process moves in a defined sequence:

- **Containment and ventilation** — negative air pressure keeps cross-contamination from spreading during cleaning.
- **Dry and wet residue removal** — dry soot from fast-burning fires is vacuumed with HEPA equipment before any liquid contact; wet, oily residues from slow smoldering fires require chemical sponges and alkaline cleaners matched to the surface type.
- **Structural surface cleaning** — walls, ceilings, framing, and subflooring are cleaned before any reconstruction begins. Painting over soot without cleaning first guarantees bleed-through within months.
- **Thermal fogging and hydroxyl treatment** — deodorization penetrates the same pathways smoke used to spread, neutralizing odor molecules rather than masking them.
- **HVAC cleaning and filter replacement** — smoke residue in ductwork re-circulates indefinitely if left untreated.
