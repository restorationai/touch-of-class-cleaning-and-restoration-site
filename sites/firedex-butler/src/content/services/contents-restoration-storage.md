**Soot residue doesn't stop working once the fire trucks leave.** Furniture, upholstery, electronics, and keepsakes left inside a fire- or water-damaged structure keep absorbing odor, moisture, and acidic ash for as long as they sit there. A dresser that looked fine the day after a kitchen fire can show etched finish and embedded smoke smell a week later. Contents Restoration & Storage exists to get your belongings out of that environment fast, inventoried, cleaned by material type, and held in a controlled space until your home is ready for them.

## What Contents Restoration & Storage actually involves

This is the part of a restoration job most homeowners don't think about until they're standing in a house full of smoke-stained furniture or water-logged boxes: what happens to everything you own while the structure itself gets dried, demoed, or rebuilt. It covers the pack-out (removing contents from the affected rooms), cleaning (method depends entirely on material and damage type), climate-controlled storage for the duration of repairs, and pack-back once the space passes final inspection. Soft goods, hard goods, electronics, and documents each need different handling, and mixing them during transport or storage is one of the fastest ways to cause cross-contamination or additional damage.

## Our process

1. **Room-by-room inventory and photo documentation.** Every item gets logged and tagged before it leaves the property, so there's a clear record tied to the insurance claim.
2. **Pack-out and transport.** Contents are boxed or crated by category, loaded, and moved to a climate-controlled storage facility rather than left on-site where humidity and temperature swings continue the damage.
3. **Sorting and triage by material.** Upholstery, electronics, documents, artwork, and hard goods each get separated, since a wet-wipe method that works on a wood table can ruin a circuit board or a photograph.
4. **Specialized cleaning treatment.** Ultrasonic cleaning for hard, nonporous items; ozone or thermal fogging for odor in textiles and porous materials; dry cleaning or wet cleaning for soft goods depending on the contamination category.
5. **Storage and scheduled pack-back.** Items stay logged and climate-controlled until the structure is cleared for move-back, then they're returned, unpacked, and checked off against the original inventory.

## What separates a good contents response from a bad one

The biggest failure point is a missing or sloppy inventory. Without room-by-room photos and itemized tags before pack-out, there's no way to prove to an adjuster what existed, what condition it was in, or where it ended up. The second failure is treating all contents the same way: running smoke-damaged electronics through the same cleaning process as upholstery, or storing porous and non-porous items together, can cause odor transfer or corrosion that wasn't there to begin with. A capable operator separates by material and damage category before cleaning even starts, tests electronics before attempting to power them on, and keeps documents and photographs in a stabilized environment rather than general storage. Adjusters pay close attention to whether items were reasonably salvageable or should have been scheduled for replacement, so detailed before-and-after documentation matters as much as the cleaning itself.

## Seasonal & regional considerations

Butler County winters bring hard freezes that make unconditioned storage risky for anything with moisture already in it, water-damaged upholstery or boxed documents can develop mold if they sit in a cold garage instead of a climate-controlled unit. Summer humidity cuts the other way: textiles and paper goods pulled from a water loss need to be dried and stabilized quickly before mildew sets in, which is part of why pack-out timing matters as much as the cleaning method itself.
