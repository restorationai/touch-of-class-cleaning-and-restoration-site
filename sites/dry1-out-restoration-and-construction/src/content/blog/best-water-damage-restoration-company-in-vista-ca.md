## What Should You Actually Look for in a Water Damage Company?

The right water damage company for your situation carries IICRC certification, answers around the clock, and gives you a written scope before work begins, not just a verbal estimate.

Beyond the rating on a review page, a few things separate a company that dries your home correctly from one that just runs fans and hopes.

**Certification.** Ask directly whether the technician on-site is IICRC certified in water restoration (WRT) and structural drying (ASD). These credentials mean they're trained to identify the water category (Category 1 clean water, Category 2 gray water, or Category 3 black water, which involves sewage or contaminated flood water) and set drying targets accordingly, not just point an air mover at the wet spot.

**Documentation.** A company that photographs and moisture-maps every affected room, and logs daily readings until the structure hits dry-standard, gives you a paper trail your insurance adjuster can actually use. Our related [burst pipe emergency checklist](/blog/burst-pipe-emergency-checklist/) walks through what to photograph before anyone arrives.

**Equipment on the truck, not on order.** Ask if extraction equipment, LGR dehumidifiers, and air movers are dispatched with the first truck. A company that has to "schedule delivery" of dehumidifiers for tomorrow is losing you a day of drying time, which matters because mold can begin colonizing damp material within [24 to 48 hours per the EPA](https://www.epa.gov/mold).

**Insurance fluency.** A restoration company that's handled hundreds of claims in San Diego County will know how to write an estimate in the format your carrier's software expects (usually Xactimate), which speeds up approval. If you're unsure what your policy actually covers before you call anyone, our guide on [whether homeowners insurance covers water damage](/blog/does-homeowners-insurance-cover-water-damage/) breaks down the common exclusions.

## Who Should You Call First for Water Damage in Vista?

Call a restoration company, not your insurance company, the moment you find standing water, because most policies require you to mitigate damage promptly and a delay can complicate the claim.

Stop the water source first if it's safe to do so (shut the supply valve at a burst pipe or fixture). Then call a local crew that can be on-site quickly and start extraction before the drywall, subfloor, and baseboards absorb more than they can shed. Dry1, based in Vista, is one option built for exactly this: an IICRC Certified Firm with 24/7 emergency service and a licensed, insured crew that documents the loss as they work.

Once mitigation is underway, that's when you call your insurer to open the claim, ideally with photos and a preliminary scope already in hand. Our [24-hour water damage checklist](/blog/what-to-do-first-24-hours-water-damage/) covers the order of operations in more detail, and if you're weighing multiple bids, [how to choose a restoration company](/blog/choosing-a-restoration-company/) has the questions worth asking before you sign anything.

Whichever company you call, get a written scope of work before demolition starts, ask what dry-standard they're targeting, and confirm in writing whether they bill your insurer directly or you pay and get reimbursed. Those three questions tell you more about a company than any star rating.
