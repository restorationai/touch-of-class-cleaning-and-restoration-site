**Mold remediation in Berkeley** has to account for a climate that almost never dries out. The marine layer that rolls off the Bay sits over North Berkeley and the flats most mornings, and combined with a housing stock where a good share of homes predate 1950, that moisture finds its way into crawlspaces, cripple walls, and old plaster long before anyone notices a smell. By the time a homeowner spots staining on a closet wall or catches that musty note near a baseboard, the colony behind it has usually had weeks to establish.

## Why Berkeley Properties See Mold Issues

Berkeley's fog belt keeps relative humidity elevated for much of the year, which is a slow, steady feed for mold even without an obvious leak. Add in raised foundations common to Elmwood and Claremont bungalows, where cripple-wall crawlspaces trap ground moisture against untreated joists, and you get conditions that modern slab construction in other parts of the Bay Area simply doesn't have. West Berkeley's older industrial-adjacent buildings, many converted to residential or live-work space, often carry original single-pane windows and poor ventilation, so condensation collects on interior wall surfaces and window sills in ways that read as a plumbing problem but are really a building-envelope problem.

## Our Mold Remediation Process in Berkeley

We start with a visual inspection and moisture mapping to find where the colony is actually feeding, not just where it's visible, because surface mold on drywall is frequently the smallest part of the problem. Containment goes up next, using negative air pressure and plastic sheeting sized to the room and HVAC layout, since older Berkeley homes rarely have the sealed duct systems that make containment simple in newer construction. Affected porous materials get removed per IICRC S520 guidelines, non-porous surfaces are HEPA-vacuumed and treated, and we run air scrubbers through the containment zone until clearance conditions are met. Our team holds the IICRC AMRT (Mold) credential, and we work carefully around original lath, plaster, and trim, since those materials are harder to source and more labor-intensive to replace than drywall.

## Berkeley Permits and Older-Home Considerations

Homes in the 94708 and 94705 zip codes, much of the Berkeley Hills and Claremont area, were frequently built directly into graded hillside lots, and retaining walls or uphill drainage can push moisture toward crawlspace vents in ways that have nothing to do with the plumbing inside the house. Flatland neighborhoods like Southside and parts of West Berkeley near 94702 and 94710 tend to have older cast iron or galvanized plumbing, which corrodes from the inside and can produce slow, hidden leaks behind walls for months before a stain appears. We document moisture readings and photos at every stage, which matters if the work needs to support an insurance claim or a disclosure requirement on a future sale.

## Local Note

In the Berkeley Hills, cut-and-fill lots often have crawlspace vents oriented toward the uphill drainage path rather than away from it, so even a house with sound plumbing and a recent roof can run damp underfloor air for months after a wet winter. We check vent orientation and grading before assuming a leak is the only source, because treating the mold without addressing the airflow just invites it back within a season.
