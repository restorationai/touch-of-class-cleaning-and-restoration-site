San Francisco's fog doesn't just roll over Twin Peaks and burn off by noon in every neighborhood. In the Sunset and Richmond Districts, that marine layer can sit against a building's west and north walls for most of the day, and inside older homes with limited cross-ventilation, that moisture has nowhere to go. Mold remediation here isn't just about removing what you can see; it's about understanding why a closet, a bathroom, or a bay window keeps coming back damp.

## Why San Francisco Properties See Mold Issues

Much of the city's housing stock predates modern vapor barriers and mechanical ventilation codes. Victorians and Edwardians in Noe Valley and Pacific Heights were built with lath and plaster, single-pane windows, and crawlspaces that were never sealed against ground moisture. Add the fog belt's year-round humidity in the Sunset and Richmond, and you get walls that read dry on the surface while moisture sits behind the plaster for weeks. Flat-roofed buildings common in SoMa and parts of the Mission District also tend to develop slow roof leaks that go unnoticed until a ceiling stain or musty smell shows up in a closet two floors down.

## Our Mold Remediation Process in San Francisco

We start with containment calibrated to the building, not a generic checklist. In a pre-1950 home with lath and plaster, we treat the wall cavity differently than we would in a 1980s SoMa condo with drywall and batt insulation; plaster holds and releases moisture much more slowly, so containment stays up longer and drying is monitored more closely before we close anything up. Work follows IICRC AMRT guidance for assessment, containment, and remediation, with negative air machines and HEPA filtration isolating the work area so spores don't migrate into occupied rooms. Once visible growth and affected materials are addressed, we document moisture readings so the homeowner, and the insurance carrier if one's involved, has a clear record of what was found and what was done.

## San Francisco Insurance & HOA Coordination

Most homeowner's policies treat mold as excluded unless it's tied to a covered, sudden event like a burst pipe or a failed water heater, so the paperwork trail matters as much as the cleanup. We photograph and document the originating moisture source before remediation begins, which gives homeowners in Pacific Heights or Bernal Heights something concrete to hand their adjuster rather than just a mold report. Condo and co-op buildings in SoMa and parts of the Mission often have HOA rules requiring advance notice before containment barriers go up in shared hallways or stairwells; we build that lead time into scheduling so the job doesn't stall waiting on board sign-off.
