## Why Chula Vista Properties See Mold Growth

Western Chula Vista, including the 91910 area around Third Avenue Village, still has a good number of homes built in the 1950s and 60s with original cast iron or galvanized plumbing. Those lines corrode and weep slowly, often behind plaster or lath that hides the leak for years. Out toward Eastlake and Otay Ranch (91913, 91914), construction is newer but built fast during the 1990s and 2000s boom, with stucco exteriors over wood framing that can trap condensation if weep screeds were installed too tight to grade. Add in the coastal humidity that settles into Chula Vista overnight even in dry months, and both housing stocks end up with the same end result: moisture sitting somewhere it shouldn't, and mold colonizing within 24 to 48 hours of that moisture finding organic material to feed on.

Hillside properties in Rancho del Rey and Rolling Hills Ranch add another wrinkle. Homes built into graded canyon slopes sometimes see grading or drainage shift over time, pushing water toward foundations and crawlspaces that were never designed to handle it. We see a disproportionate number of crawlspace mold calls from that part of the city for exactly this reason.

## Our Mold Remediation Process in Chula Vista

We start with a visual and moisture-meter inspection to find where water is actually entering, not just where mold is visible. From there, our IICRC AMRT-trained technicians set up physical containment, typically negative-air chambers with HEPA filtration, to keep spores from migrating into unaffected rooms while we work. Affected porous material, drywall, insulation, sometimes subfloor, gets removed and bagged for disposal rather than "cleaned," because porous materials with established colonization don't come back to a safe state with surface treatment alone. Non-porous surfaces get HEPA-vacuumed and treated with an EPA-registered antimicrobial. Once remediation is complete, we run air scrubbers and dehumidifiers to bring the space back to a normal moisture reading before we close anything up.

For homes in Eastlake and Otay Ranch with stucco-related moisture intrusion, we often coordinate with a moisture-mapping pass on the exterior wall assembly before we ever open drywall, since cutting into the wrong section means repeated patch work and repeated cost.

## Chula Vista HOA & Insurance Coordination

Many of the planned communities in Eastlake and Otay Ranch operate under active HOAs, and some of those associations require a third-party clearance test before a remediation job is considered closed, particularly for shared-wall townhomes or condos where mold in one unit raises concerns for the neighbor.
