In a city where Victorian and Edwardian rowhouses share party walls from the Mission District to Noe Valley, a fire that starts in one unit rarely stays contained to it, and the fog-belt humidity rolling in off the Sunset and Richmond makes every hour before drying begins count more than it would in a drier climate.

## Why San Francisco Properties See Fire Damage Issues

A large share of San Francisco's housing stock predates 1940, which means lath-and-plaster walls, original redwood framing, and in many homes knob-and-tube wiring still tucked behind later renovations. Plaster and old-growth redwood both absorb smoke odor more aggressively than modern drywall and dimensional lumber, and they hold onto it longer. Add the marine layer that settles over the Sunset and Richmond most mornings, and you get wall cavities that stay damp from firefighting water long after the visible scorching looks dry. Rowhouse construction near the Mission and Bernal Heights, where party walls are often shared between adjacent buildings, also means a single-unit fire can push smoke and heat into a neighbor's attic or crawlspace, turning a one-address job into a two- or three-address scope before the fire department even clears the scene.

## Our Fire Damage Restoration Process in San Francisco

We start with board-up and a structural walk-through to flag compromised framing, unsafe wiring, and any area where firefighting water has pooled behind baseboards or under subfloor. From there we separate the work into soot and residue removal, structural drying calibrated to plaster's slower release rate, and odor sealing before any cosmetic repair begins. Reconstruction work that touches load-bearing elements or exterior walls typically needs a permit through San Francisco's Department of Building Inspection, and our crew coordinates that paperwork alongside the drying and cleaning so the rebuild phase isn't waiting on approvals it should have had from day one. Our FSRT-trained technicians document moisture readings and soot patterns as they go, which becomes the backbone of both the insurance claim and any permit inspection.

## San Francisco Insurance & HOA Coordination

Condo buildings in SoMa and Pacific Heights frequently carry HOA bylaws that require board sign-off before any unit opens a wall cavity shared with a neighboring unit, even for smoke remediation. We've learned to request that paperwork early rather than after drying equipment is already staged, since a stalled HOA approval is one of the more common reasons a straightforward fire job runs long. On the insurance side, adjusters covering San Francisco claims often want separate scope documentation for each affected unit in a multi-family building, particularly in older Mission and Noe Valley properties where party-wall damage blurs the line between one owner's policy and another's.

## Local note

Homes within a few blocks of Golden Gate Park and through the Richmond and Sunset corridors sit squarely in the fog belt, and that persistent moisture does something specific to fire-damaged lath and plaster: it slows drying enough that mold risk becomes a real second problem layered on top of the original fire, usually inside wall cavities that look fine from the room side. We factor that into how long we run drying equipment on these jobs rather than pulling it the moment surface moisture readings look acceptable.
