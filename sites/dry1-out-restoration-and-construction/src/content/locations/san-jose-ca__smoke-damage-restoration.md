**Smoke damage emergency in San Jose?** We answer 24/7, whether the loss is a kitchen fire, an electrical fire in the attic, or a wildfire smoke event that rolled through the South Bay and settled into your HVAC system overnight. Smoke residue is acidic and keeps etching glass, metal, and painted surfaces the longer it sits, so the clock matters as much as the source of the smoke.

## Why San Jose Properties See Smoke Damage Issues

San Jose's fire risk isn't limited to structure fires inside the city. Hillside neighborhoods near Almaden Valley and the open space abutting Evergreen sit close to the wildland-urban interface, and when fires burn in the Santa Cruz Mountains or further north in wine country, Diablo wind events can push smoke across the Valley for days. That smoke finds its way into homes through attic vents, window gaps, and especially through HVAC return ducts that keep cycling particulate-laden air long after the smell outside has faded. Older Willow Glen bungalows, many built in the 1920s and 1930s with original plaster and wood trim, absorb smoke odor into porous surfaces far more readily than the drywall and engineered flooring common in newer Evergreen tract homes. That difference changes how aggressively we have to treat walls, cabinetry, and trim before a space reads clean again.

## Our Smoke Damage Restoration Process in San Jose

We start with a walkthrough to identify the type of smoke involved, protein, synthetic, or wildfire particulate, because each leaves a different residue and calls for a different cleaning agent. Protein residue from a stovetop fire is nearly invisible but carries a strong odor; synthetic smoke from burning plastics or electronics leaves a sticky, smeary film; wildfire smoke tends to coat surfaces in a fine ash layer that settles into carpet fibers and HVAC coils. Once we know what we're dealing with, we HEPA-vacuum loose soot before any wet cleaning, seal off unaffected rooms to stop cross-contamination, and work room by room with the appropriate cleaning solution on walls, ceilings, hard surfaces, and contents. Odor control comes last, after the source residue is physically removed, not as a substitute for it.

## Equipment & Methods We Use for Smoke Residue Cleanup

Thermal fogging and hydroxyl generators both have a place in post-fire work, but we choose based on the materials involved and whether the home is occupied during treatment. Hydroxyl generators are slower but safe to run around people and pets, which matters in a Rose Garden duplex or a Japantown apartment where tenants may need to stay on site. Thermal fogging penetrates porous materials more aggressively and works well in vacant structures or isolated rooms. HVAC duct cleaning is almost always part of the scope here, since San Jose's central-air systems are a primary path for smoke particulate to recirculate into living spaces long after visible soot is gone.

## San Jose Insurance & HOA Coordination

Most San Jose homeowners' policies cover smoke and soot damage from a covered fire event, including wildfire smoke infiltration in many cases, but documentation matters. We photograph affected materials room by room, note odor intensity and residue type, and provide the itemized scope adjusters expect before approving contents cleaning or HVAC remediation. Condo and townhome associations in complexes around Cambrian Park and Berryessa sometimes require their own vendor sign-off on shared-wall or shared-duct work, so we coordinate directly with property management when a unit sits inside an HOA building rather than leaving that step to the homeowner.
