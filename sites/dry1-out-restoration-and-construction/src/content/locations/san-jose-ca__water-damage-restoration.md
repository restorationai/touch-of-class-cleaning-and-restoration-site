## Why San Jose Properties See Water Damage Issues

Santa Clara Valley's clay soil goes through a real shrink-swell cycle each year. During the dry season the ground pulls away from foundations, then winter atmospheric river storms saturate it again, and slabs shift just enough to crack supply lines or separate joints that were never meant to flex. In Rose Garden and Japantown, where a lot of the housing stock predates 1960, that plumbing is often original galvanized or early copper, already thinning from decades of mineral buildup. In Almaden Valley, hillside lots add a second issue: graded slopes can push runoff toward a home's foundation instead of away from it during a hard rain, which shows up as water intrusion along the lowest level or garage slab. Newer construction in Evergreen isn't immune either; PEX manifold systems fail less often but tract-home irrigation lines and slab penetrations can leak for weeks behind drywall before anyone notices the smell.

## Our Water Damage Restoration Process in San Jose

We start with moisture mapping, not guesswork, using meters to find out how far water traveled past what's visible. Extraction comes next, pulling standing water from flooring and, where needed, from wall cavities. Then we set up structural drying calibrated to the materials in the house: older plaster and lath common near the Rose Garden holds moisture longer and dries at a different rate than the drywall and engineered flooring typical of Berryessa or Cambrian Park homes built in the last twenty years. We monitor daily, adjusting airflow and dehumidification until moisture readings confirm the structure is dry, following IICRC S500 drying standards rather than a fixed number of days.
