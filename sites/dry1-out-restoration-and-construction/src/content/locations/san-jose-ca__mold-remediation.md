San Jose's dry summers can fool homeowners into thinking mold is someone else's problem. But a slow roof leak above a Willow Glen bungalow or a condensation line that's been sweating behind drywall in an Almaden Valley kitchen doesn't care what the forecast says. Mold needs moisture and a food source, not rain, and plenty of San Jose properties quietly supply both for months before anyone notices the smell or the staining.

## Why San Jose Properties See Mold Issues

Housing stock in San Jose spans several very different eras, and each one fails in its own way. Pre-1960 bungalows around Willow Glen and the Rose Garden district were built with crawlspace foundations and galvanized plumbing that corrodes from the inside out, often leaking for weeks before a stain shows on the ceiling below. Newer tract homes in Evergreen and parts of Berryessa use slab foundations and stucco exteriors, which trap moisture against sheathing when a window flashing or stucco crack lets water in, especially during the heavier atmospheric river storms that have become more common in recent winters. Townhomes and condos in Cambrian Park add another layer: shared walls and HVAC chases mean a mold source in one unit can travel further than it would in a detached house, and water heaters tucked into closets are a common slow-leak culprit we see across all these housing types.

## Our Mold Remediation Process in San Jose

We start every job with a moisture investigation, not a visual guess. Thermal imaging and moisture meters help us trace the source, whether that's a roof penetration near the chimney on an older Rose Garden home or a failed pan under a dishwasher in a Cambrian Park condo. Once we've confirmed the extent of the colonization, we set up physical containment with negative air pressure to keep spores from migrating into unaffected rooms, a step that matters more in homes with central HVAC systems that can pull contaminated air through the whole duct network. Affected porous materials, drywall, insulation, and sometimes subflooring, are removed and bagged for disposal, and remaining structural surfaces are HEPA vacuumed and treated with an EPA-registered antimicrobial. We dry the space per the IICRC S520 mold remediation standard before any rebuild begins, confirming moisture content with meters rather than guesswork or a quick sniff test.

## San Jose Insurance & HOA Coordination

Most homeowner policies treat mold as a secondary damage item tied to a covered peril, like a burst supply line or a roof leak, rather than as a standalone claim, so documentation of the original water event matters as much as the mold itself. We photograph and log moisture readings throughout the job so your adjuster has a clear record connecting the source to the damage. For condo and townhome owners in developments around Cambrian Park or near the Rose Garden, HOA governing documents often specify who's responsible for mold inside shared walls versus inside your unit, and some associations require a licensed contractor's report before approving interior work. We can provide documentation formatted for both your carrier and your HOA board when that's needed.

## Equipment & Methods We Use for Mold

Containment barriers, HEPA-filtered air scrubbers, and negative air machines form the core setup on most jobs, sized to the square footage and number of rooms involved. For attic and crawlspace mold, which we see often in older homes near Japantown and Willow Glen with original under-floor ventilation, we use borescopes to inspect joists and subflooring without tearing out more material than necessary. Post-remediation verification, visual inspection and moisture confirmation, happens before we clear containment, and we can coordinate third-party air clearance testing through an independent hygienist when a policy, HOA, or buyer's inspection calls for it.
