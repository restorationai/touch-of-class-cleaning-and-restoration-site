## Why San Francisco Properties See Sewage Backup Issues

Much of San Francisco runs on a combined sewer system, meaning stormwater and sanitary sewage share the same pipe network under normal conditions. During the city's heavier winter rain events, that combined flow can surcharge, and homes on lower-lying blocks near the Mission District or along older sections of the Sunset District sometimes see sewage pushed back up through basement drains and laundry tubs instead of flowing out. Add to that the age of the sewer laterals themselves, many Edwardian and Victorian properties in Noe Valley and Bernal Heights still run on clay or cast-iron lateral pipe installed decades before current plumbing code, and root intrusion or pipe collapse becomes a second common trigger. In flats and multi-unit buildings common to the Mission and SoMa, a single failed lateral or shared stack can affect more than one unit at once.

## Our Sewage Cleanup and Sanitization Process in San Francisco

Every job starts with containment: we isolate the affected area, identify the contamination category under IICRC S500 guidance, and extract standing sewage before anyone walks it further into the home. From there, our crews remove and discard porous materials that can't be sanitized, carpet pad, baseboards, drywall below the flood line, and anything else that held contact with the water. Hard surfaces and structural framing are cleaned, disinfected with EPA-registered antimicrobials, and monitored through drying using moisture meters rather than guesswork. In pre-1950 homes typical of the Richmond and Sunset Districts, plaster walls and solid wood subfloor hold moisture longer than modern drywall and OSB, so we extend drying time and recheck readings before closing a job rather than working off a fixed calendar.

## San Francisco Insurance & HOA Coordination

Sewage losses in San Francisco often involve a mix of homeowner's insurance, a sewer line endorsement, or in condo buildings around SoMa and Pacific Heights, an HOA master policy that covers common-area plumbing while the unit owner's policy covers interior finishes. We document the loss with photos and moisture readings before any demolition, which gives adjusters and HOA boards the evidence they need to determine whose coverage applies. In multi-unit buildings, we coordinate scheduling with property managers so shared stacks or laterals serving more than one unit get addressed without repeat visits.
