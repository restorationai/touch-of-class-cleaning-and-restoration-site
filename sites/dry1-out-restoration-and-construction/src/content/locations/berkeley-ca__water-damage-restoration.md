## Why Berkeley Properties See Water Damage Issues

Much of Berkeley was built before 1950, and that pre-war construction behaves differently once water gets into it. Original fir subfloors, horsehair plaster, and galvanized or cast-iron supply lines are common from North Berkeley down through the Elmwood and Claremont districts, and aging pipe joints are a frequent source of slow leaks that go unnoticed inside walls. In the Berkeley Hills, steep lots and clay-heavy soil push storm runoff toward foundations and crawlspaces, so hillside homes see water intrusion at grade even when the roof and plumbing are fine. Flatter West Berkeley properties, many with low-slope roofs and interior roof drains, are prone to backups during heavy winter storms when debris clogs the drain line. Southside apartment buildings near campus add another variable: shared walls and stacked units mean a leak on one floor often shows up as a ceiling stain two units below.

## Our Water Damage Restoration Process in Berkeley

We start with moisture mapping, using thermal imaging and penetrating meters to find out how far water has traveled behind plaster, baseboards, and subflooring, which matters more in a pre-war Berkeley home than in newer construction because moisture can wick sideways through lath for several feet. Extraction comes next, removing standing water with truck-mounted or portable units depending on access. Structural drying follows IICRC S500 guidelines, but we adjust equipment placement and drying time for original materials: plaster releases moisture more slowly than drywall, so a wall that reads dry at the surface may still need several more days of controlled airflow before we close it up. Throughout the job we photograph and log moisture readings, which becomes the documentation your insurance adjuster or HOA board will want to see.
