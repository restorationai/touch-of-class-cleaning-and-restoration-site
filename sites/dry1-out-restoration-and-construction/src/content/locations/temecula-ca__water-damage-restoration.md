A lot of Temecula's housing stock dates to the big build-out of the 1990s and early 2000s: slab-on-grade construction, stucco exteriors, and PEX or copper plumbing systems that were standard for that era. That construction style handles the region's dry heat well, but it leaves little room for soil movement. In neighborhoods like Temeku Hills and Harveston, we regularly see slab cracks that show up as a hairline stain on tile or carpet long before anyone suspects a plumbing problem underneath. Wine Country estates around 92591 add another variable: large irrigation systems and private wells that, when a line fails, can push far more water into a crawlspace or foundation perimeter than a typical residential supply line would.

## Our Water Damage Restoration Process in Temecula

We start by finding the actual source, not just the visible wet spot, using moisture meters and thermal imaging to track water migration under slabs and through wall cavities. Extraction comes next, pulling standing water out of flooring, carpet pad, and cabinetry before it sits long enough to wick further. From there we set up structural drying equipment calibrated to the material: tile over slab dries differently than the engineered hardwood common in Paloma del Sol's newer builds, and we adjust airflow and dehumidification accordingly. Temecula's low summer humidity actually works in our favor during drying, though winter storms that roll through can slow things down if a home's HVAC system is also affected.
