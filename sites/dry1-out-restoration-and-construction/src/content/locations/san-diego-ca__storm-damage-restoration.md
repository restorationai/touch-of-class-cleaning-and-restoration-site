## Why San Diego Properties See Storm Damage Issues

San Diego doesn't get hurricanes or tornadoes in any regular sense, but the atmospheric river systems that roll through December through March can dump more rain in a 48-hour window than the city sees most of the year, and local drainage infrastructure, especially in canyon-adjacent neighborhoods like Mira Mesa and Clairemont, wasn't built for that volume. Water pools against hillside retaining walls, backs up through yard drains, and finds its way under slab foundations that were never meant to handle standing water. Along the coast in Pacific Beach and La Jolla, the same storm systems bring sustained onshore wind that drives rain sideways into stucco walls and window flashing that's been slowly degrading from salt air for decades. Inland, Santa Ana wind events before and after a storm can snap tree limbs weakened by saturated soil, and North Park's older Craftsman lots, many with mature trees planted close to the house, see a disproportionate share of roof punctures and fence damage as a result.

## Our Storm Damage Restoration Process in San Diego

The first call is almost always about stopping active intrusion: emergency tarping on a compromised roof, board-up on a window blown in by debris, or extraction from a flooded garage or ADU. From there we moisture-map the structure, because storm water rarely stays where it entered, it tracks along slab edges and up into wall cavities. Structural drying follows the IICRC S500 standard, with air movers and dehumidifiers placed based on actual moisture readings rather than guesswork. If trees or large debris are involved, we coordinate timing with removal crews so our drying equipment isn't fighting against an open roof section. Every step gets photographed and logged for the insurance file before we close anything up.

## San Diego Insurance & HOA Coordination

Many of the coastal and inland communities we work in, including gated developments around Rancho Bernardo, require HOA sign-off before a tarp, debris bin, or exterior repair goes up, even on an emergency basis. We've learned to get that conversation started early rather than after the fact, since delays here can stall a repair timeline more than the actual drying work does. On the insurance side, San Diego carriers generally distinguish between wind damage, which is typically covered, and flood intrusion from rising water, which often is not without separate flood coverage, so we document the mechanism of loss carefully from the first visit.

## Local note

Homes on the canyon-rim lots common to Clairemont and parts of Mira Mesa sit on hillside soil that can shift noticeably after a heavy storm season, even without a visible slide. We check foundation and slab areas on these properties for new cracking or settling after major storms, not just water intrusion, because the two problems often show up together and get missed if the inspection only looks for wet drywall.
