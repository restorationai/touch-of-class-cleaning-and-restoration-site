## Why San Francisco Properties See Storm Damage Issues

San Francisco's weather doesn't bring the hail or straight-line winds you'd see inland, but it brings something just as damaging to older housing stock: sustained wind-driven rain combined with persistent fog-belt humidity. In the Sunset and Richmond Districts, where many homes date to the mid-20th century, flat or low-slope roofs and stucco exteriors were never built to shed the kind of sideways rain that comes off a Pacific storm system. Water doesn't just pool, it gets pushed up under roofing material and behind siding, then sits there because the ambient moisture never fully evaporates. Mature street trees in Pacific Heights and Bernal Heights also take a beating in high wind events, and a limb or whole tree coming down on a roof or fence is one of the more common storm calls we see across the city. Steep terrain in neighborhoods like Bernal Heights adds another wrinkle: runoff that would simply soak into flat ground elsewhere instead channels toward foundations and garage-level entries.

## Our Storm Damage Restoration Process in San Francisco

The first step is always stopping active intrusion, tarping a damaged roof section, boarding a broken window, or extracting standing water before it migrates into wall cavities. From there we assess what's actually wet versus what only looks wet. A plaster wall in a pre-war Mission District flat can feel dry to the touch while the lath behind it is still saturated, which is why we use moisture meters and thermal imaging rather than guesswork. Drying follows the IICRC S500 standard, with air movers and dehumidifiers sized to the space, not a one-size-fits-all setup. In homes near the fog belt we typically run equipment longer than we would in a drier inland city, because ambient humidity slows evaporation even after the active leak is sealed. Once materials read dry, we move into repairs: drywall, flooring, roofing, and any structural framing affected by the storm.

## San Francisco Insurance & HOA Coordination

A lot of San Francisco's housing stock is multi-unit, think Edwardian and Victorian buildings converted into condos in SoMa and the Mission, which means storm damage often isn't a single-owner decision. If a shared roof or exterior wall is involved, we document the loss with photos and moisture readings that your HOA board and property manager can hand straight to the association's insurer, alongside your own carrier if your unit sustained interior damage. We bill most major carriers directly and can speak with adjusters about scope, which matters when a claim touches both an individual unit and common elements. For single-family homes, the process is simpler, but the documentation standard is the same: a clear record of what was wet, what we did, and why, written the way a carrier expects to see it.

## Local note

Fog-belt moisture in the Sunset and Richmond Districts means a storm-damaged home there can still read "damp" on a moisture meter three or four days after the rain has stopped, longer than you'd expect in a sunnier part of the city like Noe Valley.
