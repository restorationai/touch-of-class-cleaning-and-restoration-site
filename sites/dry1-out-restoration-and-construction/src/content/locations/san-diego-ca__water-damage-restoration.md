## Why San Diego Properties See Water Damage Issues

San Diego's housing stock splits into two problem categories, and both show up on our calls. Older neighborhoods like North Park and Hillcrest were largely built before 1950, with galvanized steel supply lines that corrode from the inside out and plaster walls that wick and hold moisture longer than modern drywall. Closer to the water, Pacific Beach and La Jolla properties deal with salt air that accelerates pipe and fitting corrosion, plus stucco exteriors that can trap intrusion behind the weather barrier for weeks before a stain finally shows indoors. Further inland, Mira Mesa and Rancho Bernardo sit on expansive clay soils that shift with seasonal moisture swings, which is a common contributor to slab leaks under concrete foundation homes built from the 1970s onward. None of these are exotic failures. They're just specific to how and when this city was built.

## Our Water Damage Restoration Process in San Diego

We start every call with extraction, pulling standing water with truck-mounted or portable units depending on how far the vehicle can stage. From there we map moisture with meters and thermal imaging to find out how far water traveled under flooring and behind walls, which matters more here than in newer-construction markets because older lath-and-plaster assemblies and original hardwood don't always show surface damage that matches what's happening underneath. Structural drying follows the IICRC S500 standard: air movers and dehumidifiers are placed based on the moisture map, not guesswork, and we monitor daily until materials read dry, not just damp-to-the-touch. For coastal properties near Mission Bay Park or Pacific Beach, we also check for salt residue left behind as water evaporates, since it can keep drawing moisture back into wood and drywall if it's not addressed during cleanup.

## Local note

In North Park's original Craftsman homes, we regularly find that plaster walls read dry on a surface moisture meter within a day or two, but the lath behind them stays wet for considerably longer because plaster releases moisture slowly compared to drywall. Pulling drying equipment too early on these homes is a common mistake that leads to hidden mold growth months later, so we run extended monitoring on pre-1950 construction even when the surface numbers look good early.
