In New Jersey, water damage restoration typically costs $1,500 to $15,000 for a residential loss, with the average homeowner paying somewhere between $3,000 and $8,000. Small, contained losses caught quickly come in on the low end. Large-area flooding, sewage backups, or losses that sat undetected for days push toward the high end or beyond.

## What Factors Move the Price Up or Down?

Four variables explain most of the cost spread in water damage restoration.

**Water category.** The [IICRC S500 standard](https://www.iicrc.org) classifies water losses into three categories. Category 1 is clean water from a supply line or appliance. Category 2 (gray water) comes from dishwashers, washing machines, or toilet overflow without solids. Category 3 (black water) includes sewage, floodwater, or any water that has sat long enough to become contaminated. Cat 3 requires full PPE, antimicrobial treatment, and disposal of porous materials that absorbed the contaminated water. That drives cost up significantly compared to a clean-water loss of the same size.

**Square footage and class of loss.** The IICRC also classifies losses by how deeply water has penetrated. Class 1 means minimal absorption in a small area. Class 4 means water has wicked into dense materials like hardwood subfloor, concrete, or plaster. A Class 4 loss in a 400-square-foot Kenilworth basement takes more drying time, more equipment, and more labor than a Class 1 loss in the same space.

**Materials affected.** Drywall, insulation, hardwood flooring, and carpet all absorb water and usually need to be removed if they cannot be dried within the IICRC's 3-to-5-day window. Removing and replacing these materials adds cost, but leaving wet materials in place leads to mold growth within 24 to 72 hours. Tile and concrete can often be dried in place. Older homes in Kenilworth and surrounding Union County towns often have plaster walls, which behave differently than modern drywall and sometimes require specialized drying approaches.

**Response time.** The faster extraction and drying equipment arrives, the less material typically needs to come out. A loss that gets addressed within 4 hours may need only drying. The same loss at 48 hours may need full drywall removal and mold prevention treatment. This is why The Restoration Group maintains 24/7 emergency response and a 60-minute response commitment.

## What Does the Restoration Process Actually Look Like?

Knowing the steps helps you understand what you are paying for and what to expect on your property.

1. **Emergency extraction.** The crew arrives and removes standing water using truck-mounted extractors or portable units. This is the single most time-sensitive step.
2. **Moisture mapping.** Technicians use thermal imaging cameras and calibrated moisture meters to find exactly where water has traveled, including inside walls and under flooring. You cannot dry what you cannot find.
3. **Demo and removal.** Wet drywall, insulation, and flooring that cannot be dried in place are removed and disposed of. This is called "flood cuts" when it involves cutting drywall at a measured height above the waterline.
4. **Structural drying.** Commercial-grade LGR (low-grain refrigerant) dehumidifiers and high-velocity air movers run continuously, typically for 3 to 5 days. Moisture readings are logged daily to document drying progress.
5. **Antimicrobial treatment.** Affected surfaces receive EPA-registered antimicrobial application to inhibit mold growth.
6. **Final documentation.** The crew produces a drying log with before-and-after moisture readings. This documentation is what your insurance adjuster needs to process the claim.
7. **Reconstruction.** Once the structure is dry and cleared, rebuilding begins: new drywall, insulation, flooring, and paint. The Restoration Group handles reconstruction directly, so you are not managing two separate contractors.

## Will Homeowners Insurance Cover Water Damage Restoration in NJ?

Most standard homeowners policies in New Jersey cover sudden, accidental water damage but exclude gradual leaks and flooding from outside the home.

Here is how coverage typically breaks down:

- **Covered:** Burst pipe, failed water heater, appliance supply line failure, ice dam intrusion, accidental overflow. These are sudden and accidental losses.
- **Not covered under standard HO policy:** Gradual leaks (a slow drip behind a wall for months), sewer backup (unless you have a rider), and surface flooding from storms or rivers. Flood damage from outside the structure requires a separate [NFIP flood insurance policy](https://www.fema.gov/flood-insurance) or private flood coverage.
- **Sewer backup rider:** Many NJ homeowners add a sewer backup endorsement for $50 to $150 per year. Given how often Black Brook and the clay laterals in Kenilworth's prewar housing stock cause backups, this rider is worth reviewing with your agent.

Your deductible applies before coverage kicks in. Most NJ homeowners carry a $1,000 to $2,500 deductible, which means smaller losses may fall entirely out of pocket.

Documentation is what protects your claim. The Restoration Group generates a full drying log, moisture readings, and photo documentation from day one. This is the same package your adjuster needs to approve the scope.
