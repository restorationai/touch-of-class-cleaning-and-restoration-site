## Restoration emergencies common in Cranford

Cranford earned the nickname "the Venice of New Jersey" honestly. The Rahway River loops directly through the center of town, and that geography has produced some of the most serious repeat-flood history in Union County. Tropical Storm Floyd in 1999, Hurricane Irene in 2011, and Ida in 2021 each put whole blocks underwater — many of the same blocks, owned by many of the same families. The Riverside Drive area floods from the river itself overtopping its banks; neighborhoods farther from the water, including parts of Downtown Cranford and Cranford West, flood when the storm sewer system simply cannot keep pace with heavy rainfall.

The housing stock makes the damage worse than it looks on the surface. The colonials and cape cods built between roughly 1900 and 1940 that line streets near Nomahegan Park and the Rahway River Parkway almost all sit on full, unfinished or finished basements. When water enters, it saturates wood framing, insulation, and drywall that has already been through previous flood cycles. Mold can begin colonizing porous materials within 24 to 48 hours of saturation — and in a basement that has flooded before, spore counts in the ambient air are often already elevated before the next event begins.
