## Restoration emergencies common in Newark

Newark's geography and housing stock create a specific pattern of losses that anyone who owns property here recognizes. The Ironbound sits in a low-lying bend along the Passaic River — when Tropical Storm Ida pushed the river over its banks, basements throughout the neighborhood filled with a mix of floodwater and raw sewage within hours. That combination isn't unusual: Newark's aging combined sewer system backs up into ground-floor units and basement apartments during any sustained heavy rain, which makes sewage remediation one of the most frequent calls we receive from ZIP codes 07105 and 07106.

Above grade, the city's dense pre-war housing stock creates its own risks. Brick multifamily buildings and frame two- and three-families stand close together throughout Vailsburg, Weequahic, and University Heights — close enough that a fire on one property can scorch siding and melt windows on the adjacent structure before the engines from the nearest firehouse arrive. Inside, a single riser failure in a three-story building soaks every unit stacked below it, and older cast-iron plumbing doesn't always give warning before it goes. Forest Hill's larger colonials, many built in the 1920s and 1930s, carry their own concerns: balloon-frame construction allows fire to travel inside wall cavities with alarming speed, and original plaster over wood lath holds moisture long after an apparent leak is repaired, creating conditions where mold can colonize within 48 to 72 hours if drying isn't done properly.

Institutions near Prudential Center and Newark Penn Station — arena-district hotels, university housing, medical facilities — face a different pressure: they need board-up and mitigation completed fast, with photo documentation and moisture logs ready for adjusters, because vacancy and liability costs mount by the day.

## Services we provide in Newark

**Water damage and flood cleanup** — Standing water under flooring sounds like a low creak when you walk across a room; by the time you hear it, the subfloor may already be saturated. We extract standing water, remove wet materials that can't be saved, and dry structural assemblies to IICRC S500 standards using industrial dehumidifiers and air movers. For Passaic River flooding events or sewer backups in the Ironbound and Downtown Newark (07102, 07103), we also handle contaminated water categorization and the additional disinfection those losses require.

**Fire and smoke damage restoration** — Smoke residue from even a contained kitchen fire migrates through HVAC systems and settles into porous surfaces throughout a building. We assess the full spread of odor and soot — not just the room of origin — and use thermal fogging and hydroxyl treatment where needed. For close-set multifamily buildings in Weequahic or Vailsburg, we can coordinate simultaneous assessment of adjacent units to document what crossed property lines.

**Mold remediation** — Newark's combination of aging building envelopes, basement moisture intrusion, and humid Mid-Atlantic summers creates persistent mold pressure. **Sewage cleanup** — Sewer backups are a Category 3 loss — the most contaminated classification — and require full protective protocols. We handle extraction, disposal of non-salvageable contents, and disinfection of affected surfaces.

**Board-up and emergency stabilization** — After a fire or break-in, an unsecured property in a dense urban neighborhood invites secondary damage fast. We provide plywood board-up and temporary weatherproofing to stabilize the structure while the longer restoration work is planned.
