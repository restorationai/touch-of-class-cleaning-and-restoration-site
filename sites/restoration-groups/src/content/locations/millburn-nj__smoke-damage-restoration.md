## Our Smoke Damage Restoration Process in Millburn

When we arrive on-site, the first step is a structured assessment — identifying smoke type (wet, dry, protein, or fuel-oil), mapping the spread path through the structure, and cataloging affected contents before anything is moved. Smoke from a grease fire in a Millburn Center kitchen behaves very differently from smoke produced by an electrical fire in a finished basement: protein smoke is nearly invisible but leaves a powerful odor and a greasy film that standard cleaning agents smear rather than remove.

From there, the process follows a defined sequence:

- **Dry soot removal** using HEPA-filtered vacuums before any wet cleaning — wiping soot without vacuuming first drives particles deeper into porous materials.
- **Chemical sponge and dry-cleaning methods** on plaster ceilings and walls, which are common in pre-1950 Millburn homes and react poorly to excess moisture.
- **Thermal fogging and hydroxyl generation** to neutralize odor molecules embedded in structural cavities, not just surface odors.
- **HVAC duct inspection and cleaning** — smoke travels through duct systems quickly, and recontamination through the air handler is one of the most common reasons odor returns weeks after a cleanup.
- **Content pack-out and ozone treatment** for salvageable furnishings, clothing, and high-value items.

All drying and deodorization work follows IICRC standards, and The Restoration Group holds IICRC Certified Firm status (#210213).
