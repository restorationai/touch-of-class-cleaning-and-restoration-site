## Our Water Cleanup Process in Hillside

Every job starts with moisture mapping — thermal imaging and calibrated moisture meters applied to walls, subfloors, and ceiling assemblies before a single fan is placed. In Hillside's older homes, this step matters more than in newer construction. Plaster-and-lath walls, common in pre-1950 colonials throughout the Saybrook and Hillside Center sections, absorb water into the lath cavity and can read nearly dry at the surface while the wood framing behind stays saturated. Skipping the mapping step and relying on surface readings alone leads to mold growth inside wall cavities weeks after the visible water is gone.

Once the scope is confirmed, we extract standing water with truck-mounted and portable units, then establish a drying system calibrated to the specific materials and square footage involved — not a generic number of air movers dropped and left. Drying logs are recorded daily and shared with your insurance adjuster. Affected materials that cannot be dried in place (saturated insulation, compromised subfloor panels, buckled base cabinets) are documented photographically before removal so the scope is defensible at the claims stage.

For commercial losses along the Route 22 corridor or in the Conant Street industrial zone, we coordinate after-hours access and work around tenant operations where possible, since a closed retail bay or warehouse aisle carries its own business-interruption cost.

## Local Note: What Hillside's Basement Finishes Change About Drying

In Hillside's semi-finished basements — the kind with drop-ceiling tiles, paneled walls over concrete block, and carpet over a slab — water cleanup is more complicated than it looks on the surface. Carpet and pad over concrete trap moisture against a non-permeable surface, and the concrete itself wicks water laterally for several feet beyond the visible wet zone. Drop-ceiling tiles hide the condition of the joists and any insulation above. We pull ceiling tiles and probe the joist bays before finalizing the drying plan, because what's happening above the ceiling line in a flooded Hillside basement is almost always worse than what's visible from the floor. Skipping that step is how a "dried" basement ends up with a mold problem sixty days later.
