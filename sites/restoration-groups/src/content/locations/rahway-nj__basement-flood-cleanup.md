## Why Rahway Basements Flood — and Why It's Complicated

Rahway's geography puts it in a genuinely difficult position. The Rahway River bisects the city, and the floodplain extends into residential neighborhoods that were built long before modern stormwater infrastructure was a design priority. Victorian-era and 1920s homes near Downtown Rahway frequently have rubble-stone or poured-concrete foundations with minimal waterproofing — materials that were never intended to hold back a storm surge, only to support the structure above. When water pressure builds in saturated soil, it finds every crack.

Postwar capes and colonials in neighborhoods like Leesville and Hazelwood present a different problem. Many were finished in the 1970s and 1980s with paper-faced drywall directly against the foundation wall — no vapor barrier, no drainage mat. That assembly soaks up water like a wick, and the damage often extends several feet higher than the actual flood line. Original galvanized or cast-iron supply and drain lines in these homes are also prone to backflow during combined sewer surges, which means a basement can flood from below even when no water is visibly entering from outside.

## Our Basement Flood Cleanup Process in Rahway

Every job starts with moisture mapping before any equipment is placed. In Rahway's older homes, surface readings can be deceptive — a plaster wall or a concrete block foundation can feel dry to the touch while holding significant moisture several inches in. We use thermal imaging and penetrating moisture meters to establish where the water actually is, not just where it's visible.

Once the scope is documented, extraction comes first: truck-mounted and portable units pull standing water from the floor, and if the drain has backflowed, we address that before assuming the space will drain on its own. Structural drying follows using refrigerant dehumidifiers and high-velocity air movers positioned to create a drying chamber around affected walls and floor assemblies. In finished basements, this often means removing a controlled section of drywall at the base of the wall — a process called flood cuts — to allow airflow behind the surface where moisture is actually trapped. We document drying readings daily and do not close out a job until materials reach acceptable moisture content per the IICRC S500 standard.

If sewage backflow is involved, the cleanup protocol changes. Contaminated water (Category 3 in industry classification) requires disposal of porous materials that absorbed it — carpet, padding, drywall — and disinfection of the structural surfaces that remain. We communicate clearly about what can be saved and what cannot before any demolition begins.
