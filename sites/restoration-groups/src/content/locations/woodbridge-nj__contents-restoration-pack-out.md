When the Rahway River backs up into finished basements across Colonia and Fords, or a sprinkler line lets go overnight at a Route 1 & 9 corridor retail space, the structure is only half the story. The furniture, documents, clothing, electronics, and irreplaceable personal items soaking inside are the other half — and they have a much narrower window before smoke odor sets permanently, mold colonizes fabric, or water warps wood beyond recovery. Contents restoration and pack-out is the work that saves what the building held, and in a township like Woodbridge, where postwar split-levels and colonials are packed with decades of accumulated belongings, that work demands both speed and precision.

## Why Woodbridge Properties Face Elevated Contents Risk

Woodbridge Township's housing stock tells a familiar mid-century story: capes, split-levels, and colonials built largely in the 1950s through 1970s, many with finished lower levels that function as living rooms, home offices, and storage. Those finished basements sit directly in the flood path when the Woodbridge River or Rahway River rises. Tropical Storm Ida in 2021 left a visible mark on low-lying sections near Sewaren and Port Reading, where tidal creek flooding combined with storm surge pushed water into homes that had never flooded before. In those events, contents losses are often more costly than structural repairs — a soaked sectional sofa, a ruined home theater setup, water-logged filing cabinets full of tax records.

Beyond flooding, the aging supply lines and cast-iron drain stacks common in Woodbridge's older homes fail without warning. A slow leak behind a kitchen wall or a burst washing-machine hose on the second floor can saturate flooring, migrate into walls, and reach finished spaces below before a homeowner notices. Smoke from kitchen fires, electrical faults, and heating equipment also travels through the open floor plans and HVAC systems typical of split-level construction, coating contents two or three rooms away from the origin.

## Our Contents Pack-Out and Restoration Process

The first step is a detailed inventory — every item photographed, described, and logged before anything leaves the property. That documentation matters for your insurance claim and gives you a clear chain of custody for every box that moves. Items are sorted on-site: what can be cleaned in place, what needs to travel to a controlled facility, and what is too far gone to restore (that determination is made transparently, with you present or informed before disposal).

Packed contents travel to a climate-controlled facility where each category gets the treatment it requires. Textiles go through ozone or thermal deodorization chambers to remove smoke and mildew odor at the molecular level rather than masking it. Hard surfaces — furniture, cabinetry, electronics housings — are cleaned with appropriate solvents and dried under controlled conditions. Documents and photographs that have been wet are stabilized through freeze-drying or air-drying protocols depending on the degree of saturation. Electronics are assessed by technicians before any power is applied. Throughout the process, the inventory log tracks every item so nothing is misplaced during storage or return.

Once the structure is dried, repaired, and cleared, contents are returned in the same documented order they left — unpacked, placed, and confirmed against the original inventory.

## Coordinating with Insurance on Woodbridge Claims

Contents losses in Woodbridge frequently run through homeowner policies and, for river-adjacent properties in ZIP code 07095 and surrounding areas, through separate NFIP flood policies. Those two coverages handle contents differently, and the documentation requirements don't always align. We photograph and inventory to the standard that satisfies both, and we communicate directly with adjusters so you are not translating between the restoration process and the claims process simultaneously.

For commercial properties along the Route 1 & 9 corridor — restaurants, retail, and light industrial spaces — business interruption implications often make the contents timeline as important as the structural timeline. We coordinate pack-out schedules to minimize operational downtime where possible and provide the documentation commercial carriers require for equipment and inventory claims.

## Local Note

In the postwar split-levels common through Woodbridge Proper and Colonia, the mid-level entry and lower-level finished spaces often share a single open stairwell with no fire door. When smoke or soot from a first-floor event rises through that stairwell, it deposits on contents throughout the upper levels — sometimes more heavily than in the room of origin. Homeowners are often surprised to find that clothing in second-floor closets needs odor treatment after a kitchen fire that never reached the stairs. We account for that vertical migration during the initial scope walk rather than discovering it after pack-out has already been staged.
