## Why Paramus Properties See Flood Damage So Often

Paramus was built out almost entirely between 1950 and 1975, which means the majority of its residential housing stock sits on either slab foundations or finished basements with aging drain tile and cast-iron waste lines. ## Our Flood Damage Restoration Process in Paramus

Every job starts with a moisture survey before a single piece of equipment is placed. In Paramus's split-levels and ranches, water migrates in non-obvious directions — down through subfloor assemblies into crawl spaces, laterally through concrete block walls, and up through vinyl flooring that traps moisture underneath. We use thermal imaging and pin-type meters to map the full extent of saturation before scoping the work.

From there, the process follows a clear sequence:

- **Water extraction** — truck-mounted and portable extractors pull standing water and surface moisture from carpet, pad, and hard flooring.
- **Controlled demolition** — saturated drywall, insulation, and flooring that cannot be dried in place are removed to the flood cut line, typically 12–24 inches above the waterline, to expose the structural cavity for drying.
- **Structural drying** — commercial-grade desiccant or refrigerant dehumidifiers paired with high-velocity air movers create a drying system calibrated to the room's cubic footage and the ambient humidity typical of a Bergen County summer or shoulder season.
- **Antimicrobial treatment** — applied to structural framing and concrete surfaces after drying, not before, so it isn't diluted by residual moisture.
- **Documentation and clearance** — final moisture readings are logged and provided to the homeowner and insurer before any reconstruction begins.

As an IICRC Certified Firm (#210213) and NJ Licensed Home Improvement Contractor, we can carry the project from emergency mitigation through finished reconstruction under one contract, which simplifies the insurance claim and eliminates the gap between mitigation and rebuild.
