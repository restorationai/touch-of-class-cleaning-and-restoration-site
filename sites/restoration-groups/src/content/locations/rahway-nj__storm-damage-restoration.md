## Why Rahway Properties Are Especially Vulnerable to Storm Damage

Rahway's geography sets it up for a particular kind of storm punishment. The Rahway River bisects the city, and low-lying neighborhoods near the riverfront — including the streets just west of Rahway River Park and the older blocks of Leesville — sit in a natural flood corridor. When a nor'easter or a remnant tropical system stalls over northern New Jersey, rainfall that would drain harmlessly in a drier landscape has nowhere fast to go.

The housing stock compounds the problem. Much of the city between Downtown Rahway and the Clark border was built between the 1880s and the 1950s: Victorian-era two-families, 1920s colonials, and postwar capes, most with finished basements over original cast-iron drain lines that are decades past their design life. Those drains back up under surge conditions, turning a roof or siding loss into a simultaneous basement flood. Older wood-framed roofs with original sheathing are also more susceptible to sheathing delamination when wind-driven rain gets under lifted shingles — a failure mode we see far less often in newer construction.

The transit-oriented development around Rahway Train Station and the Main Street business district adds a commercial layer: multi-unit residential buildings and mixed-use storefronts with sprinkler systems and flat or low-slope roofs that pond water and fail at seams during heavy events.

## Our Storm Damage Restoration Process in Rahway

Every storm loss is a sequence of overlapping problems, and the order you address them matters. When we arrive at a property in the 07065 ZIP code, the first priority is always safety and stabilization: boarding or tarping breached roof sections, covering broken windows, and shutting down power or gas to compromised areas in coordination with the homeowner.

Once the structure is stabilized, we move into documentation — detailed photos and moisture mapping before a single piece of material is moved. That documentation is the foundation of your insurance claim, and it protects you if the carrier disputes scope later.

From there, the process branches based on what the storm actually did:

- **Wind and tree damage**: Structural assessment, debris removal, temporary weatherproofing, and coordination with a licensed contractor for permanent repairs.
- **Flood and water intrusion**: Industrial extraction, desiccant and refrigerant dehumidification, and thermal imaging to find moisture hidden inside wall cavities — critical in Rahway's older plaster-and-lath construction.
- **Combined losses**: Many Rahway storms produce both. We sequence the work so drying begins while structural repairs are being scoped, rather than waiting for one phase to close before starting the next.

All drying is performed to IICRC S500 standard psychrometric targets, with daily moisture readings logged until the structure reaches equilibrium.
