## Why Hillside Properties See Sewage Backup More Than Most

Hillside's housing stock tells the story. The colonials, two-families, and postwar capes that line streets through neighborhoods like Saybrook and Winans were built between roughly 1925 and 1955, which means their original cast-iron drain stacks and clay sewer laterals are now 70 to 100 years old. Cast iron corrodes from the inside out; clay laterals crack under root intrusion and soil shift. When a heavy summer downpour overwhelms the municipal combined sewer system — and Union County's older combined systems do surcharge during intense rain — that pressure has nowhere to go except back up through floor drains and basement toilets.

The Elizabeth River's eastern corridor through town amplifies the problem. Properties on low ground near Evergreen Cemetery and the Conant Street area sit at elevations where even a modest river rise can push groundwater into foundation walls faster than a sump pump can keep up. Sump-pump failures during summer downpours are among the most common calls we receive from this part of the township, and when the pump fails during a surcharging sewer event, the backup is rarely clean water.

## Our Sewage Cleanup and Sanitization Process in Hillside

Sewage cleanup in an older Hillside home requires a different approach than a newer build. The finished and semi-finished basements common here often have wood-framed walls, drop ceilings, and carpet or laminate over concrete — materials that wick contaminated water quickly and cannot be dried in place once Category 3 exposure is confirmed.

Our process moves in a deliberate sequence:

1. **Containment and PPE staging** — We establish a contamination boundary before any equipment enters the affected area to prevent cross-contamination to upper floors.
2. **Extraction** — Truck-mounted and portable extraction units remove standing sewage. In tight basement stairwells common to 1940s capes, portable units often have to do the heavy lifting.
3. **Controlled demolition** — Saturated drywall, insulation, and flooring below the contamination line come out. In homes with original plaster-and-lath walls — still present in some Hillside Center properties — we assess carefully, because plaster can hold contamination in the lath cavity even when the surface looks dry.
4. **Antimicrobial treatment** — EPA-registered disinfectants are applied to all affected structural surfaces, not just the floor.
5. **Drying and monitoring** — Industrial dehumidifiers and air movers run until moisture readings return to pre-loss baselines, verified with thermal imaging and pin meters.
6. **Post-remediation documentation** — We photograph and document every step for your insurance carrier.
