## Our Water Damage Restoration Process in Hoboken

When we arrive, the first priority is stopping active water intrusion and protecting unaffected areas. In Hoboken's dense rowhouse blocks, that often means working in tight stairwells, through shared utility chases, and around original hardwood floors and plaster walls that require careful handling.

**Inspection and moisture mapping** — We use thermal imaging cameras and calibrated moisture meters to trace water migration through walls, subfloors, and ceiling assemblies. In older buildings, water travels further than it appears on the surface.

**Water extraction** — Truck-mounted and portable extractors pull standing water from flooring, carpet, and subfloor cavities. In below-grade units with limited access, portable high-capacity units go where trucks cannot.

**Structural drying** — Industrial desiccant dehumidifiers and axial air movers are positioned according to a drying plan calibrated to the specific materials in the space. Drying timelines are documented daily and adjusted based on psychrometric readings — not guesswork.

**Monitoring and clearance** — We return each day to log moisture readings until affected materials reach dry standard per the IICRC S500. Documentation is provided to you and, when applicable, directly to your insurance carrier.

## Local Note

In Hoboken's pre-war brownstones, original plaster walls behave very differently from modern drywall during a water loss. Plaster is dense and slow to absorb moisture, but once saturated it holds water for an extended period and releases it slowly — meaning drying timelines run longer than they would in a comparable newer building, and moisture readings can plateau before the wall assembly is actually dry. We account for this in our drying plans rather than pulling equipment prematurely, which is one of the most common causes of secondary mold growth in older Hoboken units.
