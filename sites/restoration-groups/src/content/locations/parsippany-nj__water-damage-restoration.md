## Our Water Damage Restoration Process in Parsippany

Every job starts with a moisture survey — thermal imaging and calibrated moisture meters across floors, walls, and ceilings — before a single piece of equipment is placed. In Parsippany's older homes, that step matters more than it does in newer construction. Plaster-over-lath walls, common in the Mount Tabor Historic District's Victorian-era cottages, absorb water more slowly than drywall but release it more slowly too. Drying times in those structures routinely run longer than the industry average, and our drying plans account for that rather than assuming a standard three-day cycle.

Once the scope is mapped, we move through extraction, structural drying, and monitoring in sequence:

- **Water removal and extraction** — truck-mounted and portable extractors pull standing water from carpet, hardwood, and subfloor assemblies. In slab-on-grade sections common in Parsippany split-levels, we use weighted extraction tools to pull water from carpet padding before it wicks laterally.
- **Structural drying** — commercial-grade desiccant and refrigerant dehumidifiers, paired with high-velocity air movers, are positioned based on the psychrometric readings taken at intake, not placed by habit.
- **Daily monitoring** — moisture readings are logged each visit so the drying curve is documented. That documentation matters when your adjuster asks why equipment ran for five days instead of three.
- **Controlled demolition if needed** — when wet insulation or saturated wall cavities can't be dried in place, we remove only what's necessary, photograph everything, and prepare a scope for reconstruction.

All drying is performed to IICRC S500 standard. The Restoration Group holds IICRC Certified Firm status (#210213) and is licensed through the NJ Division of Consumer Affairs as a Home Improvement Contractor.

## Parsippany Insurance Coordination

Most standard homeowners policies cover sudden and accidental water losses — a burst pipe, an appliance failure, an ice-dam overflow — but exclude gradual leaks and flood events. In Parsippany, that distinction is especially relevant: losses tied to the Whippany River or Troy Brook flooding are typically NFIP (National Flood Insurance Program) claims, not standard HO claims, and the documentation requirements differ. We photograph and catalog all affected materials before anything is moved, generate moisture logs that match the timeline adjusters need, and communicate directly with your carrier's field adjuster.
