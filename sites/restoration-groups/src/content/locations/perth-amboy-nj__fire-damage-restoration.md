## Why Perth Amboy's Housing Stock Makes Fire Damage More Complex

The majority of residential properties in Perth Amboy's neighborhoods — from the rowhouses near Downtown Smith Street to the multifamily conversions in Budapest and Dunham Heights — were built before 1940. That era of construction creates several compounding factors after a fire:

**Balloon framing.** Unlike modern platform framing, balloon-frame walls run continuously from foundation sill to roof plate. Smoke, soot, and heat travel vertically through those cavities without stopping at each floor level. A kitchen fire on the first floor of a Chase Avenue three-family can deposit soot inside wall cavities two stories up — damage that isn't visible until walls are opened.

**Aging galvanized and cast-iron plumbing.** Firefighting water pressure can fracture already-corroded galvanized supply lines or shift cast-iron drain joints that were barely holding. Post-fire water intrusion from broken supply lines is common in these older buildings and must be addressed alongside the fire damage itself.

**Dense multifamily occupancy.** A fire in one unit of a converted multifamily in the Kearny Heights area creates smoke and odor damage across shared walls, common hallways, and HVAC systems serving multiple tenants — each of whom may have a separate insurance claim.

## Our Fire Damage Restoration Process in Perth Amboy

Every fire loss is different, but the sequence we follow is consistent and designed to stop secondary damage from overtaking the primary loss.

**1. Emergency board-up and tarping.** Before any cleaning begins, we secure open windows, compromised roof sections, and breached walls. Perth Amboy's coastal position means a nor'easter or even a hard overnight rain can soak an already-damaged structure within hours of a fire — waterproofing the shell is not optional.

**2. Structural assessment and debris removal.** We document all affected materials photographically before anything is moved — critical for insurance purposes — then remove charred structural members, burned contents, and contaminated insulation. In older Perth Amboy homes, this often includes asbestos-containing materials in floor tiles, pipe insulation, or plaster texture coats. We coordinate licensed abatement when sampling confirms hazardous materials, keeping the project on a single timeline.

**3. Smoke and soot cleaning.** Soot chemistry varies by what burned. Protein fires (kitchen grease) leave an almost invisible film that smells intensely and bonds to surfaces differently than the dry soot from a wood-frame fire. We use HEPA vacuuming, dry chemical sponges, and wet cleaning agents matched to the surface and soot type — not a single-product approach.

**4. Odor elimination.** Thermal fogging and hydroxyl generation reach into wall cavities, ductwork, and porous materials that surface wiping cannot address. In a balloon-frame home, this step is non-negotiable.

**5. Structural drying and reconstruction.** Firefighting water must be fully extracted and dried before reconstruction begins — wet framing behind new drywall becomes a mold problem within days. We dry per the IICRC S500 standard and monitor moisture readings until materials reach acceptable levels before any rebuild work starts.

## Local Note

In Perth Amboy's older multifamily buildings, particularly the converted three-families common in the Budapest and Dunham Heights neighborhoods, original horsehair plaster walls are still found behind later drywall layers. After a fire, these plaster assemblies absorb smoke odor deeply — more so than modern drywall — and they also trap soot between the plaster and the lath. Surface cleaning alone will not eliminate the odor. When we encounter this construction, we plan for either full plaster removal to the lath or aggressive cavity treatment with thermal fogging before any encapsulation, because painting over smoke-saturated plaster reliably fails within months.
