**Water damage emergency in Short Hills?** We answer 24/7, and our crews travel in from Kenilworth with extraction equipment ready to go. Short Hills' mix of older stone-foundation homes and hillside lots means water from a failed sump pump or a burst supply line rarely stays where it started. It finds the lowest point in the basement, soaks into plaster and subfloor, and keeps moving until someone with the right equipment pulls it out.

Short Hills sits within Millburn Township in Essex County, on rolling terrain that rises toward the South Mountain Reservation. That topography is part of why water damage here behaves differently than it does on flatter ground nearby. Homes built into a slope often have finished basements set partially below grade, and when groundwater rises after heavy rain or rapid snowmelt, those lower levels take on water through foundation cracks and window wells rather than through an obvious pipe failure. Add in the area's stock of older homes, many built from the 1920s through the 1950s with plaster walls, lath, and original cast iron or galvanized plumbing, and you get a slower, more deceptive drying problem. The surface can feel dry within a day while the wall cavity behind it stays saturated for a week or more.

## Our Water Damage Restoration Process in Short Hills

We start with extraction, pulling standing water with truck-mounted and portable units so it is not sitting against plaster, baseboards, or hardwood for any longer than necessary. From there we moisture-map the affected areas with meters that read behind walls and under flooring, not just what is visible on the surface. In the older Millburn Township homes common throughout Short Hills, this step matters more than usual. Plaster and lath release moisture far slower than drywall, so a wall that reads dry to the touch can still be feeding mold growth behind it. We set air movers and dehumidifiers calibrated to that slower release rate and monitor readings daily, drying to the IICRC S500 standard rather than to a fixed number of days. For finished basements built into hillside lots, we also check for ongoing groundwater intrusion before we close out drying, since a one-time extraction does little good if water is still entering through a foundation crack.

## Short Hills Insurance Coordination

Many Short Hills properties carry higher coverage limits and more detailed contents, which means claims documentation needs to be thorough from the first visit. We photograph and log affected materials, room by room, before any demolition or drying equipment goes in, and we bill most major carriers directly so homeowners are not covering the full cost upfront while a claim is processed. If a property has custom millwork, hardwood flooring, or finished basement built-ins, we document those separately so they are not lumped into a generic line item that undervalues the loss.

## Local note

A good number of homes near the South Mountain Reservation side of Short Hills sit on lots with a noticeable grade, and their sump pumps often tie into a French drain system that was added decades after the house was built. When that pump fails during a storm, water doesn't just pool in one spot, it tracks along the original drain path and shows up in a different corner of the basement than where the pump sits. We always check the drain layout before we start demolition, because tearing out drywall in the wrong spot can miss where the water actually traveled.
