## Our Sewage Cleanup and Sanitization Process in New Brunswick

Sewage is classified as Category 3 water — the most contaminated classification under the IICRC S500 standard — meaning every porous material it contacts is presumed contaminated until proven otherwise. The process here is methodical, not rushed.

First, we contain the affected area to prevent cross-contamination into clean living spaces. In multifamily buildings, that often means establishing negative air pressure in stairwells and common hallways before any extraction begins. We then remove standing sewage using truck-mounted extraction units, followed by the removal of any unsalvageable porous materials — flooring, drywall, insulation — that absorbed Category 3 water.

After structural drying begins, we apply EPA-registered antimicrobial agents to all affected surfaces, including subfloor framing, concrete, and masonry. In New Brunswick's older rowhouses, that masonry step matters: brick and mortar are porous and hold moisture and bacteria longer than poured concrete. Drying equipment runs until moisture readings return to pre-loss baselines, and we document every reading for your insurance carrier.
