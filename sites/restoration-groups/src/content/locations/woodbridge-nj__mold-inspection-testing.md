## Our Mold Inspection and Testing Process in Woodbridge

A mold inspection is not a visual scan with a flashlight. The process starts with a structured walkthrough — checking mechanical rooms, crawl spaces, attic decking, bathroom exhaust paths, and any area with a history of water intrusion. Moisture mapping with calibrated meters identifies elevated readings inside walls and under flooring without destructive opening, giving us a map of where mold is most likely colonizing even when it isn't visible.

From there, air sampling captures what's actually circulating through the living space. Spore trap cassettes are collected from multiple rooms and from outside the structure to establish a baseline. Those samples go to an accredited third-party laboratory, and the resulting report identifies genus, concentration, and whether indoor counts are elevated relative to outdoor control samples. Surface swab or tape-lift samples are added wherever visible growth is present to confirm species and guide any remediation scope. The full written report — not a verbal summary — is what you receive, and it's the document your insurance adjuster and any future buyer's inspector will want to see.

## Local Note: What Ida Left Behind in Woodbridge Basements

One pattern that comes up repeatedly in Woodbridge inspections — particularly in homes near the Rahway River flood plain — is mold that established itself in the wall cavity just above the flood line, not at floor level where most homeowners look first. When floodwater recedes, capillary wicking pulls moisture upward into framing and insulation for several inches above the visible water mark. In postwar split-levels with fiberglass batt insulation in the lower-level walls, that batt holds moisture like a sponge and creates ideal conditions for Cladosporium and Penicillium growth inside a cavity that looks perfectly dry from the finished side. Inspectors who only check the floor and the obvious wet spots miss this zone entirely.
