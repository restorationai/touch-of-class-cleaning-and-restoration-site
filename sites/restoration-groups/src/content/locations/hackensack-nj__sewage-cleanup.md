## Why Hackensack Properties See Sewage Backup So Often

Hackensack's plumbing problems are largely a product of its building history. The Fairmount neighborhood and the Southside are dense with two- and three-family homes built before 1950, most of which still run their original cast-iron drain lines. Cast iron corrodes from the inside out; root intrusion, grease accumulation, and simple age eventually cause partial or complete blockages that send sewage back up through the lowest fixture in the house — usually a basement floor drain or a first-floor toilet.

The Prospect Avenue corridor adds a different wrinkle. High-rise apartment stacks mean a single clogged or broken lateral can force sewage upward through multiple units simultaneously. A backup on the fourth floor isn't just a fourth-floor problem — it can cascade material down through shared walls and ceiling cavities to units below before a building manager even knows there's an issue.

The Hackensack River's tidal influence on the city's eastern lowlands compounds the risk during heavy rain events. Properties within a few blocks of the river are especially exposed to this pattern.

## Our Sewage Cleanup and Sanitization Process in Hackensack

Arriving on site, the first priority is containment. Sewage loss is classified as Category 3 water intrusion under the IICRC S500 standard, which means all affected porous materials — drywall, insulation, carpet, subflooring — are treated as contaminated and typically require removal rather than drying in place. In Hackensack's older homes, that often means dealing with original hardwood floors over board subfloor, which trap sewage in the gap between layers and are far harder to dry than modern OSB construction.

Once the area is contained and personal protective equipment is in place, the process follows these steps:

- **Extraction and removal** — standing sewage is extracted with truck-mounted or portable units; contaminated materials are bagged, documented, and disposed of per New Jersey Department of Environmental Protection guidelines.
- **Structural cleaning** — all remaining hard surfaces are cleaned with EPA-registered disinfectants rated for Category 3 contamination, including floor joists, concrete slabs, and masonry block walls common in Hackensack basement construction.
- **Drying and dehumidification** — commercial desiccant dehumidifiers and air movers are deployed; in tightly built prewar structures, drying times run longer because vapor has fewer escape paths.
- **Post-sanitization verification** — surfaces are tested before equipment is pulled and documentation is prepared for your insurance carrier.
