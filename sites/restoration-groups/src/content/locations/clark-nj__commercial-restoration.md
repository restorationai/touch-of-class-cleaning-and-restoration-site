## Our Commercial Restoration Process in Clark

Commercial jobs require a different sequencing than residential work. The first priority is stabilizing the building to allow partial operations — isolating the affected zone with poly containment, establishing a clean path of egress, and protecting undamaged inventory or equipment before extraction begins.

For water losses, we deploy truck-mounted extraction units capable of pulling standing water from large floor plates quickly, followed by desiccant or refrigerant dehumidifiers and high-velocity air movers calibrated to the actual square footage and material density of the space. Drying is documented daily with psychrometric readings so your adjuster and property manager have a clear record of progress — important when a commercial lease has a business-interruption clause that requires proof of mitigation timeline.

For fire and smoke losses, the chemistry of smoke residue in commercial kitchens and retail spaces differs from residential. Protein smoke from cooking fires bonds to surfaces at a molecular level and requires enzymatic cleaning rather than simple wiping. Synthetic smoke from electrical or HVAC fires leaves an oily, acidic residue that etches metal fixtures and discolors grout if not neutralized quickly. We assess residue type before selecting cleaning agents — the wrong product on the wrong surface extends the job and can void a warranty on commercial flooring.

Throughout the process, we coordinate directly with your insurance carrier. We document the loss with photographs, moisture mapping, and a scope of work formatted to carrier standards, which reduces the back-and-forth that delays commercial claim approvals.
