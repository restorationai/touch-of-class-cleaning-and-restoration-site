## Why Bayonne Properties Face Compounding Storm Damage

The housing stock in Bayonne is a significant part of the story. Two- and three-family frame homes built before 1950 are common throughout Bergen Point and the blocks running north toward Uptown, and those structures carry decades of deferred maintenance into every storm season. Aging roof sheathing fails under wind-driven rain before the shingles even lift. Original cast-iron drains in finished basements back up when storm volume overwhelms the combined sewer system — a pattern that repeats in nearly every significant rain event across the 07002 ZIP code. Once water enters a basement with original supply plumbing and a finished ceiling, you are dealing with hidden saturation inside wall cavities that a surface inspection will miss entirely.

The peninsula's low elevation also means that properties near Stephen R. Gregg Park and along the waterfront corridors can experience both wind damage from above and surge pressure from below in the same event. That combination — roof breach plus groundwater intrusion — is what turns a manageable loss into a structural problem if the drying response is delayed.

## Our Storm Damage Restoration Process in Bayonne

Every storm loss is different, but the sequence matters: stop ongoing intrusion first, document everything second, then dry aggressively before secondary damage sets in. When our crews arrive, the first priority is emergency tarping or board-up for any roof or wall breach, followed by a full moisture mapping of the structure using thermal imaging and pin-type meters. In Bayonne's older frame homes, that mapping often reveals water migration through balloon-frame wall cavities that would not show up on a standard visual inspection.

Extraction and structural drying follow the IICRC S500 standard — the industry benchmark for water damage response — using commercial-grade desiccant dehumidifiers and high-velocity air movers calibrated to the affected material class. Plaster walls common in pre-war Bayonne homes release moisture more slowly than modern drywall, so drying timelines in these structures typically run longer than a newer build of comparable square footage. We monitor daily with calibrated readings and do not demobilize equipment until the structure reaches documented dry standard. Debris removal, tree limb extraction from roof penetrations, and temporary weatherproofing are handled in parallel so the property is secure throughout the process.
