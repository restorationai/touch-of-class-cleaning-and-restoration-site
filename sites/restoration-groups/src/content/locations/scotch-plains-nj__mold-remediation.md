## Why Scotch Plains Properties See Mold Issues

The housing stock here tells most of the story. The bulk of Scotch Plains was built out in the 1950s and 1960s — a period when vapor barriers were minimal, basement wall assemblies relied on block and mortar rather than waterproof membranes, and mechanical systems were sized for a different era. Those original cast-iron drain lines corrode and seep; the supply lines crack at fittings behind finished walls. By the time a homeowner smells something musty in the rec room, mold may have been growing on the paper face of drywall or the back of wood paneling for weeks.

The geography compounds the problem. The Green Brook and Cedar Brook trace the southern edge of town, and the low spots near the Fanwood border flood during heavy rain events — Ida being the most dramatic recent example, but not an isolated one. Homes in the Willow Grove and Ashbrook areas that absorbed groundwater intrusion in 2021 and weren't fully dried within the critical window are still turning up secondary mold losses today. Crawl spaces and slab-on-grade additions are especially vulnerable when the water table rises.

On the commercial side, the Route 22 corridor adds a different category of mold risk: roof-leak and sprinkler-discharge losses in retail and warehouse spaces where HVAC systems can distribute spores through ductwork before anyone notices a stain on the ceiling tile.

## Our Mold Remediation Process in Scotch Plains

Every job starts with a thorough inspection — not just a visual scan, but moisture mapping with thermal imaging and pin meters to locate hidden saturation inside wall cavities and under flooring. In Scotch Plains's older homes, that step is critical because original plaster walls and tongue-and-groove subfloors hold moisture differently than modern assemblies and can mask active growth.

Once the scope is defined, the affected area is isolated with negative air pressure containment using 6-mil poly barriers and HEPA-filtered air scrubbers running continuously. This prevents cross-contamination to unaffected living spaces — important in a split-level where the HVAC return is often located near the basement stairwell. Mold-affected materials are removed, bagged, and disposed of per New Jersey Department of Environmental Protection guidelines. Structural surfaces are HEPA-vacuumed, treated with an EPA-registered antimicrobial, and dried to verified moisture levels before any reconstruction begins.

The Restoration Group is an IICRC Certified Firm (#210213), and remediation work follows the IICRC S520 Standard for Professional Mold Remediation — the same protocol that most NJ insurance adjusters reference when reviewing a mold claim.
