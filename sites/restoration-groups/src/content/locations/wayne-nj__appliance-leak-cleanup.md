## Why Wayne Homes See More Appliance Leak Damage Than You'd Expect

The housing stock tells most of the story. The colonials and capes built through the 1950s–70s in neighborhoods like Mountain View and Preakness were designed around appliances that sat on vinyl or linoleum — materials that show water fast. Today those same laundry rooms and kitchens have been updated with engineered hardwood, laminate plank, or tile over cement board, and those surfaces hide slow leaks beautifully until the subfloor is already saturated.

Refrigerator ice maker lines are a particular culprit in Wayne's older homes. The supply lines run behind cabinetry and through wall cavities that were never intended to be inspected, and a pinhole drip at a compression fitting can go undetected for weeks. By the time the homeowner notices a soft spot in the floor or a musty smell near the kitchen, mold can begin colonizing porous materials in as little as 24–48 hours after initial saturation — and Wayne's humid summers accelerate that window considerably.

Water heater failures follow a similar pattern. Many Wayne homes have water heaters tucked into finished basement utility corners, and a failing pressure relief valve or corroded tank bottom can release 40–80 gallons before anyone notices. In a sump-dependent basement — common throughout the lake communities — that water has nowhere to go until it's extracted.

## Our Appliance Leak Cleanup Process in Wayne

When we arrive, the first priority is stopping ongoing water introduction: shutting the supply valve to the affected appliance, confirming the leak source, and doing a rapid moisture scan of the surrounding structure with thermal imaging and calibrated moisture meters. Appliance leaks are deceptive because the visible wet area is almost always smaller than the actual wet area — water tracks along joists, wicks into wall bottom plates, and pools inside cabinet toe-kicks.

Extraction comes next. We use truck-mounted and portable extraction units to pull standing water from hard surfaces and carpet, then transition to drying equipment: commercial-grade low-grain refrigerant dehumidifiers and high-velocity air movers positioned to create a drying chamber around the affected zone. Drying timelines in Wayne homes vary by construction — a 1960s ranch with original hardwood subfloor over a vented crawl dries differently than a 1980s colonial with OSB subfloor over a conditioned basement — so we set equipment based on daily moisture readings, not a fixed schedule.

If affected materials need to come out — saturated drywall, swollen cabinet bases, buckled flooring — we document everything photographically before any demolition, which your insurance adjuster will need.
