## Why Fort Lee Properties See Basement Flood Cleanup Issues

Fort Lee's geography is the root cause of most storm-related basement losses. The borough sits atop the Palisades escarpment, and when rainfall exceeds what the municipal storm system can carry, runoff accelerates downhill through residential streets rather than dispersing gradually across flat terrain. Tropical Storm Ida in 2021 illustrated this pattern across all of Bergen County — communities on elevated, rocky ground saw rapid surface flooding that overwhelmed basement waterproofing systems that had held for decades.

Beyond storm events, the building stock itself creates vulnerability. These foundations wick groundwater laterally during prolonged wet periods, producing slow seepage that is easy to dismiss until finished materials are already saturated. ## Our Basement Flood Cleanup Process in Fort Lee

The first priority on arrival is source control — if water is still entering, we identify whether it is groundwater intrusion, a failed sump pump, a broken supply line, or storm-driven backflow. Each source requires a different containment step before extraction begins.

Once the inflow is stopped, truck-mounted and portable extractors remove standing water. In Fort Lee's older homes, this phase often reveals a secondary problem: water has wicked into rubble-stone foundation walls or migrated beneath slab-on-grade floors where standard surface extraction cannot reach it. We use thermal imaging cameras and calibrated moisture meters to map the full extent of saturation — including areas that appear dry to the eye but are holding moisture inside wall cavities or under flooring.

Structural drying follows, using high-capacity air movers and refrigerant dehumidifiers placed according to the IICRC S500 standard for water damage restoration. Drying logs are recorded daily, which matters when an insurance adjuster or a co-op board requests documentation of the drying timeline. We do not close out a job until readings confirm materials have returned to acceptable moisture levels — not just until the floor looks dry.
