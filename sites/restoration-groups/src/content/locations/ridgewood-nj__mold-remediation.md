## Our Mold Remediation Process, Calibrated for Ridgewood Homes

Mold remediation in a 1920s Tudor with horsehair-plaster walls and a stone foundation requires a different approach than work in a 1990s colonial. The process begins with a thorough visual inspection and moisture mapping — thermal imaging helps locate moisture migration behind plaster and lathe assemblies that would be invisible on standard drywall. Air sampling establishes a baseline spore count before any physical work begins, giving you and your insurance adjuster a documented picture of the pre-remediation condition.

Containment comes next. Negative air pressure chambers isolate the affected area so that disturbing mold-colonized material does not spread spores to clean rooms. HEPA-filtered air scrubbers run continuously throughout the project. Affected porous materials — drywall, insulation, wood framing where necessary — are removed and bagged for disposal per New Jersey Department of Environmental Protection guidelines. Structural surfaces are treated with EPA-registered antimicrobial agents, and the area is dried to IICRC S520 standard before any reconstruction begins. A post-remediation air clearance test, conducted by an independent industrial hygienist, confirms spore counts have returned to normal background levels before the project is closed out.

## Local Note: Plaster Walls Change the Drying Timeline

Ridgewood's older homes — particularly the Willard and Somerville neighborhoods where pre-war construction is concentrated — frequently have original horsehair or gypsum plaster walls rather than modern drywall. Plaster absorbs moisture more slowly than drywall but releases it more slowly too. In practice, this means that even after visible mold is remediated and affected material removed, adjacent plaster assemblies can retain elevated moisture readings for significantly longer than a comparable drywall surface would. Rushing the drying phase in these homes risks trapping residual moisture behind a newly painted or reconstructed surface — exactly the condition that leads to a second mold event within a year. The team uses extended drying monitoring and documents moisture readings at every check-in so there is a clear record that the substrate was genuinely dry before closeout.
