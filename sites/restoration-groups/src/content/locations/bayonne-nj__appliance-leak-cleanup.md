## Why Bayonne Homes See So Many Appliance Leaks

The housing stock tells the story. Most of Bayonne's residential blocks — particularly the rowhomes and two-families running through Midtown and the older sections near Downtown Bayonne — were constructed between 1900 and 1950. Supply plumbing in those buildings is frequently galvanized steel that has long since exceeded its service life. Galvanized lines corrode from the inside out, narrowing flow and raising water pressure at appliance connections just enough to stress braided supply hoses over time. A washing machine hose that looks fine from the outside can be working against 90 PSI of irregular pressure from a corroded main.

Basements are another factor. Postwar capes and brick rowhomes throughout the city commonly have finished or semi-finished basements with cast-iron floor drains. When a water heater leaks slowly over days, that drain may capture just enough to mask the problem — until the water heater pan overflows or the subfloor above it starts to buckle. By the time the smell reaches the first floor, the framing has been wet for longer than the 24–48 hour window before mold colonization begins.

Tight rental density compounds the risk. A single appliance failure in a multi-unit building can affect two or three households simultaneously, and coordinating access across tenants and landlords slows the response window if a contractor isn't experienced working in that environment.

## Our Appliance Leak Cleanup Process in Bayonne

When we arrive on-site, the first priority is stopping the water source — shutting the supply valve at the appliance, or at the building shutoff if the valve is corroded or inaccessible. We then use thermal imaging cameras and calibrated moisture meters to map how far water has traveled beyond the visible wet area. In Bayonne's older frame homes, water follows the path of least resistance through subfloor gaps, wall cavities, and along joists — the wet zone is almost always larger than it looks.

Extraction comes next: truck-mounted or portable wet-vac equipment pulls standing water from hard surfaces and saturated carpet. We then set drying systems — axial and desiccant air movers, low-grain refrigerant dehumidifiers — calibrated to the affected square footage and material types. Drying timelines are monitored against psychrometric readings, not guesswork, and documented daily in a moisture log. If affected materials (subfloor sheathing, drywall, insulation) cannot reach drying targets, we remove and replace them rather than seal moisture in.

Throughout the process we photograph affected areas, document equipment placement, and generate the scope-of-work records your insurance carrier will need.
