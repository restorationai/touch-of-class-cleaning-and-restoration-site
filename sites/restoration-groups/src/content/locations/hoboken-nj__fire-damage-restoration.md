## Why Hoboken's Housing Stock Complicates Fire Recovery

Most of Hoboken's residential buildings predate World War II. That means balloon-frame construction in some older rowhouses — a framing style where wall cavities run continuously from basement to attic, giving fire and smoke a hidden highway through the building. Even after the flames are out, smoke residue migrates upward through those open stud bays and settles into ceiling joists, attic insulation, and the underside of roof decking floors above the fire's origin point.

Brick and brownstone exteriors hold up well structurally, but the interior finishes tell a different story. Original horsehair plaster walls and tongue-and-groove wood floors absorb smoke odor deeply — far more than modern drywall — and standard deodorization cycles that work in newer construction often require extended thermal fogging or ozone treatment to fully neutralize the char smell in these older materials. If the building has a below-grade garden unit, smoke can also settle into that lower space through stairwells and utility chases, requiring separate scope and treatment.

The Washington Street business district and the blocks surrounding Stevens Institute of Technology also see commercial and mixed-use fire losses, where sprinkler activation during a fire event adds a significant water-damage component on top of the smoke and char work.

## Our Fire Damage Restoration Process in Hoboken

The first priority on any Hoboken fire loss is stabilization. We secure open wall and roof sections with plywood and tarps to prevent weather intrusion — critical in a city where a brownstone with an exposed roof deck can take on water within hours of a nor'easter. We then run industrial air scrubbers with HEPA filtration to begin pulling airborne soot particles out of the living space before they migrate further into porous surfaces.

Once the structure is stable, the process moves through a documented sequence:

- **Soot and char removal** — dry-cleaning sponges and chemical sponges on walls and ceilings before any wet cleaning, which prevents soot from smearing deeper into plaster or masonry
- **Content pack-out** — furniture, clothing, and salvageable belongings are inventoried, packed, and transported to a climate-controlled facility for cleaning and deodorization off-site
- **Structural drying** — if sprinkler or suppression water is present, desiccant dehumidifiers and air movers run until moisture readings return to pre-loss baselines
- **Odor neutralization** — thermal fogging and hydroxyl generators for occupied multi-family buildings where ozone treatment is not safe to run continuously
- **Reconstruction scoping** — a written scope of repairs is prepared for your insurance carrier, covering everything from replacement plaster to structural framing where fire compromised load-bearing elements

All work is performed under our IICRC Certified Firm (#210213) standards and in compliance with NJ Division of Consumer Affairs licensing requirements.
