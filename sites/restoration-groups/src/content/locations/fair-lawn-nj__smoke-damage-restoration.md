## Our Smoke Damage Restoration Process in Fair Lawn

When a crew arrives at a Fair Lawn property, the first priority is scope documentation — not just the rooms with visible char, but every space smoke could have reached. That means pulling HVAC registers, checking attic cavities, and probing wall assemblies with moisture and particle meters. Insurance carriers want this documentation, and it protects homeowners from discovering hidden damage months later.

From there, the process moves through four concrete phases:

**1. Containment and air scrubbing.** HEPA air scrubbers and negative-pressure units are staged to capture fine soot particles still suspended in the air. This step matters especially in Fair Lawn's older homes, where gaps in the building envelope mean smoke particles can migrate to unaffected rooms if airflow isn't controlled.

**2. Dry and wet residue removal.** Dry chemical sponges lift loose soot from ceilings and walls before any liquid cleaner is applied — wetting loose soot first drives it deeper into porous surfaces. Technicians then follow with alkaline or acidic cleaners matched to the residue type: protein smoke from a kitchen fire requires a different chemistry than the heavy carbon soot left by a structural fire.

**3. Odor neutralization.** Thermal fogging and hydroxyl generators are used on the structure itself. For Radburn-era homes with original hardwood floors and plaster walls, hydroxyl treatment is often preferred over ozone because it is safe to run while contents remain in the space and does not degrade rubber seals or aged finishes.

**4. Content and HVAC cleaning.** Ductwork is cleaned and sealed or lined where necessary. Salvageable contents — furniture, clothing, documents — are inventoried and either cleaned on-site or packed out to a controlled facility.

All drying and deodorization work is performed in accordance with IICRC standards, and The Restoration Group holds IICRC Certified Firm status (#210213).
