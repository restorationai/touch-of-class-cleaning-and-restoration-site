## Our Water Damage Restoration Process in Hackensack

Every job starts with a moisture survey — thermal imaging and calibrated moisture meters mapped against the floor plan — so we know what is wet, not just what looks wet. In older Hackensack homes, water routinely migrates inside balloon-frame wall cavities and under original hardwood floors well beyond the visible damage boundary. We find it before we dry it.

Extraction comes next. Truck-mounted and portable extraction units pull standing water and saturated material from flooring systems. For hardwood floors common in prewar homes, we deploy drying mats that draw moisture from the subfloor upward rather than forcing air across the surface, which reduces cupping and warping.

Structural drying follows the IICRC S500 standard — desiccant or refrigerant dehumidifiers, high-velocity air movers, and daily moisture readings until every affected assembly reaches its dry standard. We do not pull equipment early to close a job faster. Documentation of daily readings goes directly into the claim file.

Where demolition is necessary — saturated insulation, Category 3 intrusion from a sewer backup or floodwater — we scope the removal, photograph it, and provide line-item documentation before reconstruction begins.

## Local Note

In Hackensack's prewar two- and three-family homes — particularly the ones in Fairmount built before 1940 — original plaster walls behave very differently from modern drywall during a water loss. Plaster absorbs moisture slowly but releases it even more slowly, and it holds water in a way that standard moisture meters can underread if the technician is not calibrated for the material.
