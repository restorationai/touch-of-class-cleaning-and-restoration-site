## Why Millburn Properties Face Elevated Board-Up and Tarping Needs

Millburn's housing stock tells the story. The large Tudors, colonials, and estate homes concentrated in Short Hills and Old Short Hills were built primarily between the 1910s and 1940s. Their original wood-framed windows — many still single-pane or featuring divided lights — are far more vulnerable to storm impact and fire pressure waves than modern construction. When a window fails on a home with a finished basement, a wine cellar, or irreplaceable built-ins, the secondary losses from rain intrusion or temperature exposure can quickly exceed the cost of the original event.

The commercial corridor adds another layer. Sprinkler discharges at The Mall at Short Hills and water main breaks along Millburn Avenue retail properties are a recurring pattern — and when a sprinkler head activates overnight or a pipe fails over a weekend, the resulting breach in the building envelope needs to be secured before the next business day. Aging cast-iron and copper supply lines in the older commercial buildings along that corridor fail without warning, and the resulting pressure can blow out window glazing or compromise door frames.

The Rahway River's flood history is the other constant. Properties near Taylor Park and the downtown train station area sit in a floodplain that has been remapped multiple times. After a flood event, exterior doors swell and warp, basement egress windows fail, and roof penetrations that were marginal before the storm become active leak points. Board-up and tarping in this context isn't cosmetic — it's structural triage.

## Our Emergency Board-Up and Tarping Process in Millburn

When a call comes in from a 07041 or 07078 address, the first step is a rapid site assessment to identify every breach point — not just the obvious ones. On the older estate homes in the Wyoming and Glenwood sections, that often means checking dormer flashings and slate or clay-tile roof fields for secondary damage that isn't visible from street level. A tarp placed over the wrong ridge line on a steeply pitched Tudor roof can channel water into the attic rather than away from it.

For window and door openings, we use exterior-grade plywood cut and fastened to structural framing, not just tacked to trim. On historic or architecturally detailed homes — common throughout Short Hills — we take care to fasten into framing members rather than ornamental woodwork, and we document the condition of every opening before and after boarding so the insurance adjuster has a clear photographic record.

Roof tarping follows a similar logic. Ballasted tarps held down with sandbags are a temporary measure at best; on a steep-pitched roof in a neighborhood that sees nor'easter winds funneling through the Reservation ridge, they become a liability. We secure tarps with batten strips fastened to the deck and extend them past the ridge when possible to prevent wind uplift. We also flag any pre-existing roof conditions — missing flashing, deteriorated underlayment — that the adjuster should document separately from the storm damage.

As an IICRC Certified Firm (#210213) and NJ Licensed Home Improvement Contractor, the work is performed to a documented standard, which matters when your insurance carrier is reviewing the claim.

## Millburn Insurance and HOA Coordination

Millburn and Short Hills have a high concentration of properties governed by homeowners associations, particularly in the planned sections of Old Short Hills. Some of those HOAs have specific requirements about the appearance of exterior boarding — color, material, or maximum duration before a more permanent repair must begin. We've worked in these neighborhoods enough to know that a call to the property manager before work starts prevents a dispute after the fact.

On the insurance side, emergency board-up and tarping is typically covered as a mitigation expense under a homeowner's or commercial property policy, separate from the primary loss. We photograph every affected area before boarding, document materials used, and provide a detailed scope that aligns with how adjusters from the major carriers write their estimates. That documentation is yours regardless of how the claim resolves.
