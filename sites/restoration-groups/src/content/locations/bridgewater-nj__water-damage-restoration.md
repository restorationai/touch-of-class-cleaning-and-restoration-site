## Why Bridgewater Properties Are Especially Vulnerable to Water Damage

Bridgewater is a township of layers — geographically and architecturally. The elevated sections around Chimney Rock and Martinsville drain reasonably well, but the Finderne neighborhood and the broader floodplain near the Raritan sit in a bowl that collects water from upstream Somerset County before it ever reaches the river gauge. Homes in these areas have flooded in major storms going back to Hurricane Floyd in 1999.

The housing stock adds its own complications. The ranches and split-levels built across Bradley Gardens and Green Knoll in the 1950s through 1970s frequently have galvanized or early copper supply lines that are now at or past their service life. Cast-iron drain stacks in finished basements corrode from the inside out — they look intact until they fail, and when they do, the water goes straight into the slab or the subfloor cavity. Newer townhome developments near the Route 22/287 interchange have their own exposure: shared wall assemblies and HOA-managed common areas mean a neighbor's supply-line failure can migrate through a party wall before anyone notices.

Commercial properties along the Route 22 corridor and at Bridgewater Commons face a different set of risks — sprinkler-system discharges, roof membrane failures, and HVAC condensate overflows that can saturate thousands of square feet of commercial flooring overnight.

## Our Water Damage Restoration Process in Bridgewater

When a call comes in from a Bridgewater address, the process starts before the crew arrives. We ask the right questions on the phone — is the source stopped, is there any odor suggesting sewage involvement, how many finished levels are affected — so the truck is loaded with the right equipment for the job, not a generic kit.

On arrival, the first priority is moisture mapping. We use thermal imaging cameras and calibrated moisture meters to trace water migration through wall cavities, under hardwood floors, and into subfloor assemblies. In the older split-levels common to Bradley Gardens, water that enters a lower-level utility room can wick horizontally into adjacent finished spaces that look completely dry on the surface.

Extraction comes next — truck-mounted and portable units pull standing water and then transition to deep extraction from carpet and pad. Once the visible water is out, we set a drying system calibrated to the specific materials: LGR dehumidifiers, desiccant units when ambient humidity is high (a real factor in Bridgewater's humid summers), and high-velocity air movers positioned to create directed airflow through wall cavities where needed. Drying is monitored daily against IICRC S500 drying goals, and we don't close out a job until the readings confirm structural moisture is back within normal range.

All affected materials — drywall, insulation, flooring — are documented photographically and in a written scope before any demolition begins. That documentation matters for insurance claims and, in some cases, for township permit compliance when structural repairs follow.

## Local Note: What Bridgewater's Older Basements Hide

One pattern we see repeatedly in the ranches and split-levels of Bradley Gardens and Finderne: the finished basement was added or upgraded years after original construction, often without a vapor barrier between the slab and the new subfloor. When water intrudes — whether from a failed sump, a backed-up floor drain, or lateral seepage after a heavy rain — it pools in that unprotected cavity and begins degrading the subfloor from below while the surface still looks intact. Homeowners sometimes assume the damage is limited because the carpet feels only slightly damp. Lifting a corner of that carpet to check the pad and subfloor condition before calling is worth doing — but regardless of what you find, the hidden cavity beneath almost always holds more moisture than the surface suggests. We've pulled subfloor assemblies in these homes that were structurally compromised while the finished flooring above showed only minor staining.
