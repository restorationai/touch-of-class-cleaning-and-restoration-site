When a burst radiator line or a clay sewer backup soaks through the finished basement of a century-old Summit colonial, the damage rarely stops at the walls. Antique furniture, Persian rugs, built-in millwork, and family heirlooms absorb water and smoke the same way the plaster does — slowly and deeply. ## Why Summit Properties See Elevated Contents Losses

Summit sits high enough to avoid the river flooding that hits communities downhill, but that elevation creates its own problems. Steep lots funnel stormwater directly into lower levels, and the deep finished basements common throughout the Franklin School and Brayton School areas become collection points when drains are overwhelmed. Inside the walls, original cast-iron supply lines and uninsulated radiator branches in older wings are vulnerable to hard freeze events — the kind that arrive fast in a hilltop microclimate with little thermal buffer from surrounding development.

The housing stock compounds the risk. Homes built between the 1890s and 1930s were constructed with plaster over wood lath, not drywall. Plaster is dense and slow to release moisture, which means contents sitting against a wet plaster wall can absorb humidity for days after the surface reads dry on a pin meter. That sustained exposure is what turns a salvageable upholstered chair into a mold substrate, or causes the finish on a mahogany sideboard to lift and blister long after the obvious water is gone.

## Our Contents Restoration & Pack-Out Process in Summit

The process starts before anything leaves the house. Every item in the affected area is photographed, catalogued, and assessed on-site — condition before pack-out, material type, and any pre-existing damage that needs to be documented for the insurance file. High-value pieces get individual condition reports.

From there, contents move to a climate-controlled facility where the actual restoration work happens: ultrasonic cleaning for hard goods and decorative items, ozone and hydroxyl treatment for smoke and odor in textiles, specialized drying chambers for documents and artwork, and structural drying for furniture frames. Items that can be safely treated on-site — large case pieces, built-ins, architectural millwork — are handled in place using containment and controlled drying rather than risking transport damage.

Repack and return is coordinated around the reconstruction schedule. Nothing comes back until the structure is dry, repaired, and the environment is stable. For homes near the Springfield Avenue downtown corridor or closer to the Summit train station neighborhood, where street parking and narrow driveways can complicate staging, we plan the logistics in advance rather than improvising on the day.

## Local Note

Something specific to Summit's older homes: the deep basements common in the Franklin School and Brayton School neighborhoods are often finished with original hardwood floors laid directly over a concrete subfloor with minimal vapor barrier — a construction method that predates modern moisture standards. When water intrudes, the hardwood acts as a wick, pulling moisture laterally across the room even as the standing water is extracted. Contents sitting on that floor — rugs, upholstered furniture bases, stored items in fabric bins — can be wetter than they appear and need to be assessed with that substrate behavior in mind, not just surface-checked and cleared.
