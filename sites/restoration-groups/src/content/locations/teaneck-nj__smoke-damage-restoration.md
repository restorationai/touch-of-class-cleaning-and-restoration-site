## Why Teaneck's Housing Stock Complicates Smoke Damage

Teaneck's residential neighborhoods — from the center-hall colonials near the Bryant School area to the brick Tudors scattered through West Englewood — were built in an era when interior walls were plaster over wood lath, not gypsum drywall. Plaster is porous and absorbs smoke residue at a different rate than modern materials. Soot migrates deeper into the substrate, and the wall cavities behind that lath act like chimneys, pulling smoke up and distributing it far from the room of origin. A fire in a first-floor kitchen can leave detectable smoke odor in second-floor closets and attic framing within hours.

The density of the Cedar Lane commercial district also creates a distinct loss pattern. Those combined losses require a sequenced approach: water extraction and drying must begin before full smoke remediation can proceed, or the moisture drives soot deeper into porous finishes.

## Our Smoke Damage Restoration Process in Teaneck

Every job starts with a room-by-room scope assessment before any cleaning begins. Smoke residue is not uniform — dry smoke from a fast-burning fire behaves differently than the protein residue left by a grease fire, and wet smoke from a slow, smoldering burn is the most difficult to remove. Identifying the smoke type determines which cleaning agents and mechanical methods apply.

For Teaneck's older homes, that typically means:

- **Structural pre-cleaning** of plaster surfaces with dry chemical sponges before any wet cleaning agent is applied — wet-cleaning plaster that hasn't been dry-sponged first drives soot into the substrate permanently.
- **HVAC and ductwork inspection**, because cast-iron forced-air systems common in pre-war homes have joints and seams that trap particulate; contaminated ducts re-distribute odor every time the system runs.
- **Thermal fogging or hydroxyl generation** for odor neutralization in finished basements and enclosed spaces where surface cleaning alone won't reach embedded odor molecules.
- **Controlled demolition** of materials that cannot be cleaned to pre-loss condition — documented for insurance purposes before removal.

All drying and dehumidification work follows IICRC S500 and S520 standards. The Restoration Group holds IICRC Certified Firm status (#210213) and is licensed through the NJ Division of Consumer Affairs as a Home Improvement Contractor.
