## Our Commercial Restoration Process in Teaneck

Every commercial loss is different, but the sequence is consistent. When we arrive on-site, the first priority is moisture mapping — we use thermal imaging cameras and calibrated moisture meters to trace water migration through walls, under flooring, and into ceiling cavities before any material is removed. In older masonry buildings common to the Cedar Lane district, water can wick laterally through brick and mortar for surprising distances, and missing a wet cavity means a mold problem two weeks later.

Once the scope is documented, we extract standing water with truck-mounted and portable extraction units, then deploy commercial-grade desiccant and refrigerant dehumidifiers alongside high-velocity air movers. Drying targets are set to IICRC S500 standard psychrometric goals, not guesswork. We monitor daily and adjust equipment placement as readings change — a process that typically runs three to five days for a contained commercial water loss, longer for fire or smoke events where structural materials need sequential cleaning and deodorization.

For fire and smoke damage, our scope includes content pack-out, structural cleaning of HVAC ductwork (smoke travels fast through commercial air-handling systems), and odor neutralization using hydroxyl generators rather than masking agents. We coordinate temporary board-up and tarping when exterior openings are involved, and we can manage subcontractors for reconstruction so the business owner has a single point of contact from first call through final inspection.
