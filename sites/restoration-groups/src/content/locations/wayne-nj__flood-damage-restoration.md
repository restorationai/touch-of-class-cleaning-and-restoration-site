## Why Wayne Properties Flood the Way They Do

Wayne's flood exposure isn't random. The town sits at the confluence of two major river systems — the Passaic and the Pompton — and large sections of the 07470 ZIP code lie within FEMA-designated Special Flood Hazard Areas. The Hoffman Grove and Mountain View neighborhoods have been evacuated multiple times in living memory, and many of those homes were rebuilt or finished with the assumption that it "wouldn't happen again." It does.

The lake communities add a different layer of risk. Packanack Lake, Pines Lake, and Lionshead Lake were developed heavily in the 1950s and 1960s, and the housing stock reflects that era: midcentury ranches and split-levels with finished basements, block foundation walls that wick groundwater, and sump pump systems that were never designed to handle the volume of a 100-year storm. When the sump fails or the power goes out mid-storm, those finished lower levels — carpet, drywall, drop ceilings, stored belongings — absorb water fast.

Beyond the rivers and lakes, Wayne's broader housing stock of 1950s–1970s colonials and capes carries its own vulnerabilities: aging cast-iron drain lines prone to backflow, original clay sewer laterals that collapse under saturated soil, and crawl spaces that have never been properly encapsulated. Any of these can turn a heavy rain event into a significant interior flood loss.

## Our Flood Damage Restoration Process in Wayne

Flood restoration in Wayne requires more than extraction equipment — it requires a methodical sequence that accounts for contamination category, building materials, and the real possibility of a second flood event before drying is complete.

When we arrive, we assess the water source first. A Passaic River overflow carries Category 3 contamination (sewage, agricultural runoff, industrial residue from upstream), which changes everything: PPE protocols, disposal of porous materials, disinfection requirements, and documentation for your insurance adjuster. A failed sump pump with clean groundwater is a Category 2 loss — still serious, but handled differently.

From there, the process moves through extraction, controlled demolition of unsalvageable materials (wet drywall, saturated insulation, soaked carpet and pad), placement of commercial drying equipment, and daily moisture monitoring until the structure reaches target drywall moisture content per the IICRC S500 standard. We photograph and document every step — not as a formality, but because Wayne homeowners filing flood claims with their insurance carriers need a defensible, time-stamped record.
