## Our Water Cleanup Process in Roselle

Every water cleanup starts with a moisture mapping inspection — not just the visible wet area, but the surrounding materials that may have wicked water silently. In Roselle's older homes, that means checking behind plaster walls, inside wall cavities adjacent to cast-iron stacks, and beneath hardwood subfloors that may have been laid over original board sheathing. Thermal imaging helps locate moisture that a pin meter alone would miss.

Once we understand the full scope, extraction comes first: truck-mounted or portable units pull standing water from the structure before any drying equipment is placed. Drying is then calibrated to the specific materials — plaster releases moisture more slowly than drywall, and a reading that looks acceptable at the surface can mask a saturated lath layer behind it. Industrial air movers and desiccant or refrigerant dehumidifiers run in a closed system, with daily moisture readings logged against the IICRC S500 standard to confirm the structure is actually drying, not just surface-dry. Affected materials that cannot be dried in place — saturated insulation, wicking baseboards, compromised subfloor sections — are removed and documented for the insurance claim.
