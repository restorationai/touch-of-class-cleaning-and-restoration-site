## Why East Brunswick's Housing Stock Complicates Fire Recovery

East Brunswick grew fast in the postwar decades, and most of that growth produced one- and two-story ranches, bi-levels, and colonials built between roughly 1955 and 1980. Those homes share a few traits that matter after a fire. Finished basements are nearly universal, which means smoke and suppression water travel downward through floor penetrations and settle into a second finished living space. Original cast-iron supply and drain lines in older tracts corrode on schedule, so a fire department's suppression water — or a triggered sprinkler system — can overwhelm drains that were already marginal. Attic insulation in homes of that era is often fiberglass batt or early blown-in cellulose, both of which trap soot and require full removal after a significant fire rather than surface cleaning alone.

Newer townhome and condo clusters layered into East Brunswick over the past two decades introduce a different challenge: shared walls and common HVAC chases mean smoke from one unit can migrate into adjacent units before anyone realizes it. A fire in one end of a Farrington-area townhome row can deposit odor-causing particles in units two or three doors down, and those residents may not discover the problem until days later when the smell intensifies.

## Our Fire Damage Restoration Process in East Brunswick

Every fire loss is different, but the sequence of work follows a disciplined order. We begin with an emergency board-up and structural assessment — securing windows, doors, and any compromised roof sections before weather or unauthorized entry makes the loss worse. East Brunswick's humidity swings, particularly in summer months near the Lawrence Brook watershed, accelerate secondary damage: soot becomes acidic faster in humid air, etching chrome fixtures, pitting aluminum window frames, and discoloring grout within 48 to 72 hours of the fire.

Once the structure is secured, our IICRC Certified Firm (#210213) technicians move into controlled demolition of unsalvageable materials — removing char-damaged drywall, insulation, and cabinetry down to clean framing. Smoke residue on salvageable surfaces is treated with dry chemical sponges first, followed by wet cleaning agents appropriate to the surface type. Thermal fogging and hydroxyl generation address embedded odor in wall cavities, subfloor assemblies, and the HVAC system. We document every step with photographs and written scope notes, which feeds directly into the insurance claim file.
