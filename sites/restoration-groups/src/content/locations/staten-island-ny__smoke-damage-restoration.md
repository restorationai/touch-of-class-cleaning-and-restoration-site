## Why Staten Island's Housing Stock Complicates Smoke Damage

Statten Island's building inventory spans nearly a century of construction styles, and that variety matters when smoke gets into a structure. Early-1900s frame homes in St. George and Port Richmond were built with true-dimension lumber, horsehair plaster, and minimal vapor barriers — materials that absorb smoke particulates and odor compounds at a different rate than modern drywall. Plaster walls, in particular, hold soot in their pores long after a surface wipe-down looks clean, which means standard dry-sponge or chemical sponge protocols have to be followed by deeper encapsulation or controlled removal to achieve a genuine odor-neutral result.

## Our Smoke Damage Restoration Process in Staten Island

Every job starts with a structured assessment — not a visual walkthrough, but a room-by-room mapping of smoke migration using thermal imaging and air sampling. Smoke does not stop at the door of the room that burned; it follows pressure differentials through wall cavities, ductwork, and any gap in the building envelope. We document the full extent before touching a surface, because insurance carriers require that scope to authorize the right repair budget.

Once the scope is confirmed, the process moves in a defined sequence:

1. **HVAC isolation and duct cleaning** — forced-air systems are shut down immediately to stop recirculating soot. Ductwork is inspected and cleaned using negative-air equipment before the system is returned to service.
2. **Dry and wet soot removal** — protein-based smoke (common in kitchen fires) and synthetic smoke (electrical or structural fires) require different chemical agents. Mismatching the chemistry smears residue rather than lifting it.
3. **Structural deodorization** — thermal fogging and hydroxyl generation reach into wall cavities and subflooring where spray-and-wipe methods cannot penetrate.
4. **Content pack-out or on-site cleaning** — soft goods, furniture, and personal items are either cleaned on-site or transported to a controlled facility, with a detailed inventory maintained for the insurance claim.
5. **Reconstruction coordination** — when smoke damage requires drywall replacement, ceiling repair, or repainting, we coordinate those trades so the homeowner has a single point of contact through project close.

All drying and decontamination work follows IICRC standards (we hold Certified Firm #210213), and our technicians document each phase with timestamped photos for the claim file.
