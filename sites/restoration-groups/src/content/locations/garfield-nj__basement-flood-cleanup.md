## Our Basement Flood Cleanup Process in Garfield

The first priority on arrival is stopping any ongoing water entry and assessing what category of water you're dealing with. Stormwater that's backed up through a drain or entered through a foundation crack is not the same as a burst supply line, and the extraction and disinfection protocols differ accordingly.

Once the source is controlled, truck-mounted extractors pull standing water from the floor. In finished Garfield basements — the kind with carpet, drop ceilings, and framed interior walls — this step is followed by moisture mapping using thermal imaging and calibrated meters. Water behind plaster or inside a framed wall cavity can read dry at the surface while the substrate stays saturated. Missing that moisture is how mold colonizes within 24 to 48 hours of a flood event.

Drying equipment — industrial air movers and desiccant or refrigerant dehumidifiers — runs in a calculated configuration based on the room's cubic footage and measured humidity levels. We monitor readings daily and adjust placement until the structure reaches target moisture content. Nothing is closed up or rebuilt until the numbers confirm it's dry.

As an IICRC Certified Firm (#210213) and NJ Licensed Home Improvement Contractor, we document every phase with photos and moisture logs — the same documentation your insurance adjuster will need to process the claim.

## Garfield Insurance Coordination

Basement flood claims in Garfield often involve a question that trips up homeowners: standard homeowners policies typically exclude rising groundwater (flood) but may cover sudden water backup through a drain or a sudden pipe failure. The distinction matters because it changes which coverage applies and what documentation the carrier needs.

We photograph affected materials before anything is removed, write itemized scope reports, and communicate directly with adjusters.
