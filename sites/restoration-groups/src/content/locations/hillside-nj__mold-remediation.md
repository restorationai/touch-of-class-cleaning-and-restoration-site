Hillside's older housing stock and low-lying eastern terrain create a mold problem that's easy to underestimate. When Tropical Storm Ida pushed the Elizabeth River over its banks in 2021 and drove sewer water into basements across the township's 07205 zip code, many homeowners dried out the visible water — and discovered a mold colony three weeks later, hidden behind drywall or underneath the plywood subfloor of a finished basement. That's the pattern here: the moisture event ends, life resumes, and the biology keeps moving.

## Why Hillside Properties See Mold Issues

The township's residential core is built almost entirely on homes constructed between the 1920s and 1950s — colonials, two-families, and postwar capes where original galvanized supply lines and cast-iron drain stacks are well past their service life. When a galvanized pipe starts weeping at a joint inside a wall cavity, the leak can run for weeks before it shows on a ceiling or floor. By then, mold has already colonized the paper facing on fiberglass batt insulation or the back side of drywall.

The low ground along the eastern side of town compounds the risk. Hard summer rain events regularly overwhelm aging storm infrastructure, and sump pumps in the Saybrook and Winans neighborhoods — areas that sit closer to the drainage basin — see some of the highest failure rates in the township. A sump that quits at 2 a.m. during a July downpour can leave four inches of standing water in a finished basement. Carpet, pad, and drywall that stay wet for more than 48 hours are on a reliable schedule toward mold colonization.

The Conant Street industrial corridor adds a different category of loss. Warehouse and light-manufacturing buildings along that zone see sprinkler discharges, roof membrane failures, and overnight pipe breaks that can soak tens of thousands of square feet of concrete slab, racking, and interior partition wall before anyone arrives Monday morning. Commercial mold abatement in those structures requires containment at a different scale than a residential basement.

## Our Mold Remediation Process in Hillside

Every job starts with a thorough inspection — not just the visible surface growth but the moisture map behind it. We use thermal imaging and calibrated moisture meters to trace the water source, because treating mold without finding where the moisture is still coming from is a short-term fix. In Hillside's older homes, that often means checking inside wall cavities around the original cast-iron stack, under bathroom tile set on mortar beds, and along the rim joist where basement walls meet the sill plate.

Once the scope is defined, we establish negative air pressure containment using 6-mil poly barriers and HEPA-filtered air scrubbers. This is critical in two-family homes — common in the Hillside Center area — where an uncontained remediation on the first floor can push spores into the second-floor unit. Affected materials that can't be dried and cleaned (paper-faced drywall, saturated insulation, compromised OSB sheathing) are removed, bagged, and disposed of per New Jersey DEP guidelines. Structural wood surfaces are HEPA-vacuumed, treated with an EPA-registered antimicrobial, and dried to verified moisture content before any rebuild begins.

We are an IICRC Certified Firm (#210213) and a licensed NJ Home Improvement Contractor, which matters when your insurance adjuster or a future buyer's home inspector asks for documentation of the remediation scope and methodology.

## Hillside Insurance Coordination

Mold claims in New Jersey are fact-specific, and insurers look closely at whether the underlying moisture event — the burst pipe, the sewer backup, the roof leak — was a covered peril. We document the loss thoroughly from the start: moisture readings, thermal images, photographs of affected materials before removal, and a written scope of work. That documentation travels with your claim and gives your adjuster what they need to process it accurately. We bill most major carriers directly and work with your adjuster throughout the process so you are not managing two conversations at once.
