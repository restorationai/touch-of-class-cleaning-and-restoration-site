## Our Burst Pipe Cleanup and Repair Process in Maplewood

The first priority when we arrive is source control — locating the main shutoff and confirming the break is isolated. In Maplewood's older homes, shutoffs are sometimes buried in crawl spaces or behind finished walls, so we carry pipe-locating tools to avoid tearing out more material than necessary. Once water flow is stopped, we conduct a full moisture mapping of the affected area using thermal imaging cameras and calibrated moisture meters, tracing the migration path through walls, floors, and ceilings before any drying equipment is placed.

Extraction comes next. For slab-level water in finished basements we use truck-mounted and portable extraction units to pull standing water, followed by desiccant and refrigerant dehumidifiers sized to the cubic footage of the space. Air movers are positioned to create a drying vortex rather than simply blowing surface air — a distinction that matters in plaster-wall construction because the drying gradient has to pull moisture out of a dense substrate, not just evaporate surface film. We monitor moisture readings daily against IICRC S500 drying targets and adjust equipment until structural materials reach acceptable levels, typically over three to five days depending on saturation depth.

Pipe repair or coordination with a licensed plumber is handled in parallel so the home is not left without water service any longer than necessary. ## Local Note: Plaster Walls Change the Drying Timeline

One thing that consistently surprises Maplewood homeowners is how long it takes to dry out a plaster wall compared to modern drywall. Horsehair plaster — still intact in many of the Tudors and colonials throughout the Hilton and Jefferson neighborhoods — can hold three to four times as much moisture by weight as standard 5/8" drywall, and it releases that moisture slowly even under aggressive airflow. We flag this upfront with every customer in older Maplewood homes: the drying phase may run a day or two longer than a neighbor in a newer construction might have experienced, and that's normal. Rushing the timeline and closing walls prematurely is the single most common cause of secondary mold growth we see on re-inspections — so we don't do it.
