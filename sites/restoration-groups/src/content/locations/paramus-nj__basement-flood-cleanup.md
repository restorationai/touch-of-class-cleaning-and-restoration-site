## Why Paramus Basements Flood — and Why It Spreads Fast

Paramus sits in a bowl shaped by decades of development. The Route 17 and Route 4 corridors — home to Westfield Garden State Plaza, Paramus Park Mall, and miles of big-box retail — replaced natural absorption with impervious pavement. During heavy rain events, that runoff funnels into residential neighborhoods in Arcola, Farview, and East Paramus faster than municipal storm infrastructure was designed to handle.

Hurricane Ida in 2021 made that dynamic impossible to ignore. Across Bergen County, finished basements that had never flooded took on inches of water in hours. But storm surges aren't the only culprit. The supply plumbing in Paramus's postwar homes — much of it galvanized steel or early copper — has reached the end of its service life. Pinhole leaks and full-pipe failures happen without warning, often inside walls or beneath slabs where the damage is invisible until the floor is wet. Cast-iron drain lines corrode from the inside, collapse under root pressure, and back up during any sustained rain event.

Finished basements compound the problem. Carpeting, drop ceilings, and wood-framed partition walls trap moisture against the structure. What reads dry at the surface can stay saturated for weeks inside the wall cavity — long enough for mold to colonize if drying is incomplete.

## Our Basement Flood Cleanup Process in Paramus

Every job starts with a moisture map, not a visual inspection. Thermal imaging and calibrated moisture meters locate water that has wicked behind finished walls or migrated under slab-on-grade sections — common in the ranch-style homes throughout Spring Valley and West Paramus. We document conditions photographically before anything is moved, which matters when an insurance claim is involved.

Standing water comes out first with truck-mounted extraction. Then the drying system goes in: industrial desiccant or refrigerant dehumidifiers paired with high-velocity air movers, positioned to create airflow through wall cavities, not just across open floor space. We monitor moisture readings at each visit and adjust equipment placement as materials dry at different rates. Drying is complete when structural readings return to regional equilibrium — not when the floor feels dry underfoot.

When finished materials need to come out — saturated drywall, carpet, or insulation — we remove and dispose of them properly. Paramus is in Bergen County, and Bergen County has specific requirements for disposal of water-damaged materials that may contain regulated substances. We handle that paperwork so you don't have to.

## Paramus Insurance Coordination

Most standard homeowners policies in New Jersey cover sudden and accidental water discharge — a burst pipe, a failed water heater, an appliance supply line. Gradual leaks and groundwater intrusion are typically excluded, though coverage language varies by carrier and policy year. We document the loss thoroughly from the moment we arrive: moisture readings, photographs, affected material quantities, and a written scope of work. That documentation goes to your adjuster in the format carriers expect, which keeps the claim moving and reduces the back-and-forth that delays your project.

If your policy includes additional living expense coverage and the basement is a finished living space, we can help you understand what to ask your adjuster about temporary housing reimbursement.
