## Why Paramus Homes See More Appliance Leak Damage Than You'd Expect

Most of Paramus's residential neighborhoods — West Paramus, East Paramus, Farview, and the quieter streets around Arcola — were developed between the late 1940s and mid-1960s. That housing stock is now 60 to 75 years old, and the supply plumbing that feeds dishwashers, refrigerators, and washing machines has aged alongside it. Braided stainless supply lines degrade. Rubber washing machine hoses crack. Ice maker saddle valves corrode at the copper fitting and weep for months before anyone notices.

The construction patterns compound the risk. Slab-on-grade ranches have no crawl space to absorb a slow drip — water spreads laterally under the finished floor and has nowhere to go. Split-levels and colonials with finished basements turn a kitchen appliance leak into a two-story problem when water migrates through subfloor seams. The finished basement that was dry during Tropical Storm Ida in 2021 can still flood from a water heater that fails quietly in a utility corner.

Cast-iron drain lines in these older homes also matter. When a dishwasher drain backs up because the cast-iron stack has scaled down to half its original diameter, the overflow isn't a plumbing problem — it's a water damage event that needs extraction, not just a snake.

## Our Appliance Leak Cleanup Process in Paramus

When we arrive, the first priority is stopping the source — shutting the supply valve, isolating the appliance, and assessing how far water has traveled. In Paramus's slab-foundation ranches, that means lifting a section of flooring if necessary to check whether water has migrated beyond the visible wet zone. Thermal imaging cameras let us trace moisture under tile and hardwood without tearing up the entire room.

Once the scope is mapped, extraction begins. We use truck-mounted and portable extraction units to pull standing water, then deploy desiccant and refrigerant dehumidifiers alongside high-velocity air movers calibrated to the room's cubic footage. Drying is documented daily with moisture readings logged against the IICRC S500 standard — not just a visual check, but hard numbers that your insurance adjuster can review.

Affected materials are assessed individually. Wet drywall below the cabinet kick plate, saturated insulation behind a dishwasher, swollen laminate flooring — each gets a documented decision: dry in place or remove. We don't default to tear-out when drying is achievable, and we don't leave wet materials in place just to save a day of work.
