## Our Sewage Cleanup and Sanitization Process in Rahway

Sewage cleanup is not the same as water damage restoration, and the steps matter in a specific order.

**Containment first.** Raw sewage carries bacteria, viruses, and parasites. Before extraction equipment enters the space, the affected area is isolated with physical barriers and negative air pressure where the layout allows, so contaminated particulates don't migrate to clean areas of the home.

**Extraction and removal.** Standing sewage and saturated materials — drywall, insulation, carpet, subfloor — are removed and bagged for regulated disposal. In Rahway's older homes, this often means cutting out the lower 2–4 feet of drywall on exterior-facing basement walls where wicking has already begun, even if the visible contamination stops short of that line.

**Structural cleaning and disinfection.** Exposed framing, concrete, and block foundation walls are cleaned with EPA-registered disinfectants rated for Category 3 water loss. We follow IICRC S500 protocol — not because it's a marketing point, but because it defines the minimum contact time and concentration needed to actually kill what's living in sewage-contaminated materials.

**Drying and documentation.** Moisture readings are logged throughout the drying phase. In Rahway's below-grade spaces, which tend to run humid even in dry weather, drying timelines can run 3–5 days depending on the extent of saturation and ambient conditions. Every reading is documented for your insurance claim.

## Local Note: What Rahway's Basement Layouts Mean for Sanitization

One thing that comes up repeatedly in Rahway's pre-1950 homes near Downtown is the combination of a stone or poured-concrete block foundation, a low ceiling height, and a finished drop-ceiling that hides the floor joists above. When sewage backs up into that space, the contamination travels horizontally along the concrete slab and wicks into the base of any stud walls — but it also mists upward and settles on the underside of the subfloor above. Crews who don't pull the drop-ceiling tiles and inspect the joist bays miss that contamination entirely. It doesn't show up until months later as an odor problem or a mold issue that's much harder to remediate.
