## Why Paramus Homes Demand More From a General Contractor

The 1950s and 1960s construction that defines most of Paramus's residential neighborhoods — from the quieter streets of Arcola to the denser blocks of East Paramus — comes with a predictable set of complications. Supply plumbing in these homes is often original galvanized steel or early copper, and cast-iron drain lines that have been in the ground for sixty-plus years crack, root-infiltrate, and corrode. When a pipe finally fails, the damage rarely stays contained to one room. Finished basements — nearly universal in this housing stock — trap moisture behind paneling and under carpet for weeks before anyone notices.

Tropical Storm Ida in 2021 drove that point home across Bergen County. Basements that had never flooded took on two or three feet of water in hours. The rebuild work that followed wasn't just drywall and flooring — it meant re-evaluating sump systems, upgrading egress, and in many cases bringing electrical panels up to current NEC requirements before a certificate of occupancy could be issued. Paramus's building department enforces those upgrades consistently, and any contractor who doesn't account for them in the scope is setting the homeowner up for a failed inspection.

## Our Renovation and General Contracting Process in Paramus

Every project starts with a documented scope — not a ballpark estimate, but a line-item breakdown that accounts for what's behind the walls before demolition begins. In Paramus's older homes, that means probing for knob-and-tube wiring, checking for asbestos-containing floor tile or pipe insulation (common in pre-1978 construction), and confirming whether the existing framing can carry the loads a remodel requires.

Once scope is set, we pull the permits through the Paramus Building Department and schedule inspections at each required phase — rough framing, rough mechanical, insulation, and final. Kitchen and bathroom remodels in this housing stock almost always require plumbing and electrical sub-permits in addition to the general building permit. We manage that coordination directly so the homeowner isn't chasing down subcontractors or waiting on inspection queues.

For post-damage rebuilds — the work that follows a water loss, fire, or mold remediation — we work from the adjuster's scope and photograph every deviation. If concealed damage discovered during demolition changes the line items, we document it in real time and submit a supplemental claim before proceeding. That process protects the homeowner and keeps the project moving without out-of-pocket surprises.
