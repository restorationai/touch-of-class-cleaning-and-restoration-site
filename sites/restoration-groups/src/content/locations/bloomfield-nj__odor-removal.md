## Why Bloomfield Properties Hold Odors Differently

Bloomfield's housing stock is a significant variable. The colonials, two-families, and Tudor revivals that line streets from Brookdale to North Center were built between roughly 1910 and 1940, and they share a few traits that make odor penetration worse than in newer construction. Plaster-and-lath walls — still common in these homes — are more porous than modern drywall and absorb smoke, soot, and sewage gases deeper into the substrate. Original hardwood subfloors, often laid directly over uninsulated crawl spaces or on top of finished basements, wick odor-bearing moisture from below and hold it long after the surface appears dry.

The geography compounds the issue. The Third River (Yantacaw) and Second River run through the Watsessing and Brookdale neighborhoods, and the low-lying lots near those corridors are prone to sump-pump failures during summer downpours — not just major storms. When a sump fails at 2 a.m. and a finished basement takes on Category 3 water, the clock on odor colonization starts immediately. Sewage-contaminated water begins producing detectable hydrogen sulfide and microbial off-gassing within hours, and in a closed basement with limited airflow, that odor embeds into every porous surface before morning.

Fire-related odor calls have their own Bloomfield pattern. The dense lot spacing along the Broad Street and Bloomfield Avenue corridors means smoke from a kitchen fire in one unit of a two-family can migrate into the adjoining unit through shared wall cavities — a problem that doesn't resolve until both sides are treated.

## Our Odor Removal and Deodorization Process

Effective deodorization isn't a single step — it's a sequence, and the sequence matters. We begin with a thorough source assessment: locating and removing any saturated materials that are still off-gassing before any deodorizing agent is introduced. Treating odor on top of an active source is one of the most common reasons DIY attempts fail.

Once the source is controlled, we select the appropriate technology for the odor type and the structure:

- **Thermal fogging** disperses a deodorizing solvent as a fine fog that penetrates the same pathways smoke traveled — into wall cavities, behind baseboards, into upholstery fibers. It is particularly effective in the older Bloomfield homes where smoke has followed original plaster cracks and wood framing into spaces that can't be wiped down.
- **Hydroxyl deodorization** uses UV-generated hydroxyl radicals to break down odor molecules at the molecular level. Unlike ozone, hydroxyl generators can operate safely in occupied or semi-occupied spaces and are well-suited for the Broad Street commercial corridor where a restaurant or retail tenant may need to re-enter the space quickly.
- **Ozone treatment** is reserved for unoccupied spaces with severe, embedded odors — post-fire scenarios in vacant homes, for example — where high-concentration ozone can reach porous surfaces that other methods cannot. We follow established IICRC protocols on concentration levels and re-entry timing.
- **Hydroxyl and HEPA air scrubbing** run in tandem throughout the process to capture particulates and continuously reduce airborne odor load.

After treatment, we verify results before closing out the job. If a specific area — a basement stairwell, a shared wall cavity — still reads elevated on our equipment, we treat again before we leave.
