## Why Somerville Properties See Mold Issues

The combination of aging infrastructure and low-lying geography makes mold a recurring problem throughout the 08876 ZIP code. Galvanized supply lines common in pre-1950 homes corrode from the inside out, producing slow pinhole leaks inside wall cavities that go undetected for months. Cast-iron drain lines in finished basements develop cracks that allow ground moisture to seep in during wet seasons. Add the periodic flooding from Peters Brook and the Raritan River, and you have a borough where moisture intrusion is less a question of *if* than *when*.

Downtown Somerville's commercial corridor introduces a different set of risks. Overnight roof leaks, sprinkler discharges, and HVAC condensate failures in the storefronts and apartment conversions along the Main Street business district can saturate ceiling assemblies and interior partitions before anyone arrives in the morning. By the time the damage is noticed, mold spore counts inside the space may already be elevated.

Newer mid-rise residential developments near the downtown core face their own vulnerability: multi-unit supply-line failures can spread moisture horizontally across multiple units before a single resident reports it, and shared wall assemblies make containment more complex.

## Our Mold Remediation Process in Somerville

Every job starts with a thorough moisture mapping and visual inspection — not just the area with visible growth, but adjacent cavities, subfloor assemblies, and HVAC pathways that can carry spores through a structure. In Somerville's Victorian and colonial homes, that often means pulling back finished basement ceilings and checking the rim joist area, where condensation and flood intrusion tend to concentrate.

Once the scope is established, the affected area is isolated using physical containment barriers and negative air pressure. HEPA-filtered air scrubbers run continuously throughout the project to capture airborne spores before they can migrate to unaffected rooms. Contaminated porous materials — drywall, insulation, wood framing where warranted — are removed, bagged, and disposed of per New Jersey Department of Environmental Protection guidelines.

Surfaces are then treated with EPA-registered antimicrobial agents and dried to verified moisture levels using calibrated meters. The job isn't complete until readings confirm the structure has returned to normal moisture baselines, which in Somerville's humid summers can take longer than in drier climates. As an IICRC Certified Firm (#210213), the work follows the S520 Standard for Professional Mold Remediation from start to finish.
