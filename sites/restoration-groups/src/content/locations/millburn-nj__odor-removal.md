## Our Deodorization Process, Calibrated to Millburn Conditions

Every odor job starts with a source assessment — not an air freshener, not an ozone machine left running in an empty room. A technician walks the property to identify where the odor originates, what materials are affected, and which deodorization method is appropriate for the space and its contents.

For smoke odor in a large Short Hills colonial with high ceilings and original millwork, **thermal fogging** is often the most effective first step. A fogging agent is heated into a dry vapor that penetrates the same porous pathways smoke traveled, neutralizing odor at the molecular level rather than masking it. For occupied or semi-occupied spaces — or homes with sensitive electronics, artwork, or wine collections — **hydroxyl deodorization** is the preferred method. Hydroxyl generators produce reactive oxygen species that break down odor compounds without the off-gassing risks associated with ozone, making them safe to run in spaces where contents cannot be removed.

For sewage or floodwater odors, particularly in finished basements near the Rahway River flood plain, the process typically combines antimicrobial treatment of affected surfaces, HEPA air scrubbing to capture airborne particulates, and hydroxyl or ozone treatment after the space is cleared of occupants and sensitive materials. The IICRC Certified Firm standard (Firm #210213) guides our drying and deodorization protocols throughout.
