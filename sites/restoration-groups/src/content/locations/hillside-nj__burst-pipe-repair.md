## Why Hillside Properties See Burst Pipe Issues More Than Most

Hillside's housing stock is the core reason calls here follow a predictable pattern. The township is dense with 1920s–1950s colonials, two-family homes, and postwar capes — most of them carrying original galvanized steel supply lines that were already past their 50-year service life before the current owners moved in. Galvanized pipe corrodes from the inside out: scale builds up, flow slows, and the walls thin until a hard freeze or a sudden pressure spike finishes the job.

The Saybrook and Winans neighborhoods are particularly representative of this pattern. Homes there were built in tight rows on shallow lots, with basements that were finished or semi-finished over the decades — adding drywall, drop ceilings, and carpet directly over concrete that was never waterproofed for interior living space. When a pipe bursts behind a finished wall in one of those basements, the water has nowhere visible to go. It travels horizontally along the slab, wicks into wall cavities, and can saturate 200 square feet of material before the homeowner sees a single wet spot.

## Our Burst Pipe Cleanup and Repair Process in Hillside

Every job starts the same way: stop the water, assess the damage, document everything. When our crew arrives, the first priority is confirming the supply has been isolated — either at the fixture shutoff or the main — because water still under pressure will defeat any extraction effort. If the homeowner hasn't been able to locate the shutoff, we handle that before pulling a single piece of equipment off the truck.

From there, the process moves through four stages:

1. **Extraction** — Standing water is removed with truck-mounted or portable extractors. In finished Hillside basements, this often means moving furniture, pulling carpet and pad, and cutting drywall at the flood line to expose wet framing before moisture migrates further.
2. **Moisture mapping** — Thermal imaging cameras and calibrated moisture meters identify hidden saturation in walls, subfloors, and ceiling assemblies. In older plaster-and-lath construction, readings are taken at multiple depths because plaster holds water differently than modern drywall.
3. **Structural drying** — Industrial air movers and low-grain refrigerant dehumidifiers are placed to dry the structure to IICRC S500 standard. Drying logs are recorded daily and shared with the homeowner and their insurance adjuster.
4. **Repair coordination** — Once materials are confirmed dry, we coordinate or perform the physical pipe repair and handle reconstruction of affected drywall, flooring, and insulation under our NJ Licensed Home Improvement Contractor license.
