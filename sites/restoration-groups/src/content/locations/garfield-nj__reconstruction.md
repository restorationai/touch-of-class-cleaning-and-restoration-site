## Our Reconstruction Process in Garfield

Reconstruction begins where mitigation ends — once affected materials are dried, removed, and documented, the rebuild scope is set. For Garfield properties, that scope document matters: it's the basis for your insurance claim, and it needs to reflect the actual condition of the structure, not a generic line-item estimate.

The sequence typically runs: structural assessment and framing repair, rough mechanical coordination (electrical, plumbing, HVAC), insulation and vapor management, drywall and plaster matching, finish carpentry, and final inspections. In Garfield's older homes, plaster matching is not cosmetic — original plaster walls in pre-1950 houses have a texture and density that standard drywall compound doesn't replicate without additional skim-coat work. We account for that in scope rather than leaving homeowners with a visible patch line across a living room ceiling.

All reconstruction work is performed under our NJ Licensed Home Improvement Contractor license, and permits are pulled through Garfield's building department when the scope requires them. Inspections are coordinated so they don't stall your timeline unnecessarily.

## Insurance Coordination for Garfield Reconstruction

Most reconstruction projects in Garfield follow a covered loss — fire, water intrusion, storm damage. ## Local Note

One thing that catches out-of-area contractors in Garfield's older neighborhoods: the finished basements that are so common here were often built with furring strips directly against the foundation wall, no air gap, no vapor barrier. After a flood event, that assembly hides moisture against the concrete for weeks after the visible water is gone. Reconstruction that starts too soon — before the wall cavity and slab are genuinely dry — traps residual moisture and sets up a mold problem inside the new wall within months. We use thermal imaging and deep-probe moisture readings before any framing or insulation goes back, not just surface checks, because Garfield basements have taught that lesson more than once.
