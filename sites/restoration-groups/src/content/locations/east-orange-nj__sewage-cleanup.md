## Why East Orange Properties See Sewage Backup More Often Than Newer Suburbs

East Orange's housing stock tells the story. The city's residential fabric is largely early-1900s through 1930s construction — Victorians, two-families, and brick walk-ups built when plumbing codes looked very different. Cast-iron drain stacks corrode from the inside out over decades, and galvanized risers narrow with mineral buildup until a grease clog or root intrusion is enough to force sewage back up through the lowest fixture in the building. Basement apartments and finished cellars in the 07017 and 07018 ZIP codes sit at or below the municipal sewer grade in several low-lying blocks, which means there's no gravity working in their favor when the main line surcharges.

That surcharging happens more than residents expect. During heavy rain events — Tropical Storm Ida in 2021 flooded low ground near the Second River tributaries that border parts of East Orange — the combined sewer system can become overwhelmed, pushing wastewater backward into private laterals. Flat-roofed apartment buildings compound the problem: roof drains tie into the same drainage system, and a blocked roof drain during a storm adds volume the aging infrastructure wasn't designed to handle.

## Our Sewage Cleanup and Sanitization Process in East Orange

Sewage is classified as Category 3 water — the most contaminated category — and the work is sequenced accordingly. When we arrive at a property, whether it's a two-family near Brick Church station or a multi-unit building closer to the Central Avenue business district, the first priority is stopping ongoing contamination at the source if the backup hasn't already been cleared by a plumber. We work alongside plumbers when needed, but our scope begins the moment the flow stops.

Extraction comes first: truck-mounted and portable units pull standing sewage from flooring, subfloor cavities, and any carpet or padding that absorbed the backup. Porous materials — drywall below the flood line, saturated insulation, compromised flooring — are removed and bagged for disposal per New Jersey Department of Environmental Protection guidelines for Category 3 waste. We don't attempt to dry and save materials that have been in direct contact with raw sewage; the pathogen load makes that a health risk, not a cost-saving measure.

Once the structure is cleared down to cleanable surfaces, we apply EPA-registered disinfectants rated for sewage contamination, treating walls, concrete floors, framing, and any HVAC components that may have drawn in contaminated air. Dehumidification and air scrubbing run continuously until moisture readings return to pre-loss baselines. We document every step with photos and moisture logs — both because IICRC Certified Firm standards (Firm #210213) require it and because your insurance adjuster will need that documentation.
