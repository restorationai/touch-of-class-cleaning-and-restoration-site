## Our Reconstruction Process in Clifton

Reconstruction begins only after the source of loss has been fully remediated — no framing goes back up over wet substrate, and no insulation gets installed until moisture readings confirm the structure is dry. That sequencing matters especially in Clifton's postwar split-levels, where a flooded lower level can wick moisture upward into the main-floor framing if the drying phase is cut short.

Once the structure is confirmed dry and stable, the rebuild follows a defined sequence:

- **Structural assessment and permitting**: We pull the required permits through Clifton's Building Department before any structural work begins. Older homes often reveal surprises once demo is complete — undersized headers, non-standard joist spacing, or previous unpermitted work — and addressing those findings early keeps the project on schedule.
- **Framing and sheathing**: Replacement framing matches or exceeds original load specifications. Where original lumber dimensions don't match current lumber sizing (common in pre-1960 construction), we account for the difference so finished surfaces align correctly.
- **Mechanical rough-in**: Damaged electrical, plumbing, and HVAC runs are replaced to current code. In many Clifton homes, this is the first time these systems have been touched since original installation.
- **Insulation, drywall, and finishes**: Work proceeds in inspectable stages so nothing gets closed in before it's been verified.
- **Final inspection and punch-out**: We coordinate the final inspection with the city and walk the completed work with the property owner before closing out the job.
