## Why Garfield Properties See Elevated Contents Damage

Garfield's housing stock creates conditions that accelerate damage to personal property. Homes built before World War II — common throughout the Belmont and Plauderville sections — were constructed with wood-lath plaster walls, older cast-iron drain lines, and finished basements that were never designed to handle the water intrusion they routinely face today. When a cast-iron drain backs up or a water heater fails in a basement unit, water spreads quickly across finished floors and wicks into upholstered furniture, cardboard storage boxes, and area rugs within minutes. In multifamily buildings, a leak in one unit migrates through shared walls and subfloors into adjacent units, multiplying the affected contents before anyone realizes the extent of the problem.

The Saddle River tributaries that drain through low-lying parts of the city add another layer of risk after heavy storms — groundwater rises and enters basements even when surface flooding isn't obvious. Contents stored at floor level in those basements are the first casualties.

## Our Contents Restoration & Pack-Out Process in Garfield

The process starts with a room-by-room inventory before anything moves. Every item is photographed, logged, and assigned a condition code — this documentation is what your insurance adjuster needs and what protects you if a dispute arises later. Items that can be restored on-site stay in place when the structure allows it; items that need cleaning, deodorization, or drying in a controlled environment are carefully packed, labeled, and transported to our facility.

For Garfield homes, the pack-out step often involves navigating narrow staircases, tight hallways, and shared entryways common in two- and three-family frame construction. We work methodically to avoid secondary damage during the move — a smoke-damaged piece of furniture that survives the fire shouldn't get scratched on a stair rail during extraction. Soft contents — clothing, bedding, drapery — go through ozone or thermal fogging treatment to neutralize smoke and mold odors that penetrate fabric deeply. Hard goods are cleaned using methods matched to the material: ultrasonic cleaning for small valuables and electronics components, hand-wiping for finished wood, and appropriate solvents for plastics and metals.

Once the structure is dried and cleared, contents are returned, unwrapped, and placed according to the original room inventory. Nothing is guessed at.
