## Our Fire Damage Restoration Process in Montclair

When we arrive, the first priority is a safe-entry assessment coordinated with the Montclair Fire Department's clearance. We document structural integrity, identify smoke travel paths, and establish a drying and decontamination plan before any material is moved.

**Board-up and weatherization** comes first if the structure is open to the elements — a common situation when roof assemblies are compromised under Montclair's slate or clay tile. We use plywood sheathing rated for temporary weatherization and can tarp large slate roof sections without damaging surviving tile.

**Soot and smoke removal** in plaster-wall homes requires a different approach than standard drywall construction. Plaster is porous and alkaline; dry-chemical soot (the type left by fast-burning synthetic materials) bonds to it differently than it does to painted gypsum. We use dry sponge and chemical sponge methods on plaster surfaces before any wet cleaning, preventing the smearing that locks odor deeper into the substrate.

**Odor elimination** follows with hydroxyl generators and thermal fogging calibrated to the room volume — critical in the high-ceiling parlors and stair halls common in Montclair's Victorians and colonials, where air volume is substantially larger than in a typical 1980s ranch.

**Structural drying** addresses suppression water using commercial-grade desiccant and refrigerant dehumidifiers alongside high-velocity air movers, with moisture readings logged daily until the structure reaches target equilibrium.
