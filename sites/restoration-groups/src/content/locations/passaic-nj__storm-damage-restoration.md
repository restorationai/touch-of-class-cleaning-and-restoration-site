## Why Passaic Properties Take Storm Damage Harder Than Most

Passaic is one of New Jersey's most densely built small cities, and that density creates compounding problems after a major storm. Rooftops on the older rowhouses and mixed-use storefronts along the Main Avenue business district are often flat or low-slope with aging membrane systems — a single breach during a nor'easter or a fast-moving thunderstorm can push water into multiple units stacked below. Tree canopy in the Third Ward and along streets near Third Ward Park is mature and heavy; when wind gusts exceed 50 mph, limbs and full trees come down on rooflines that were already stressed.

The housing stock adds another layer of complexity. Many buildings in the Fourth Ward and Rutherford Heights still have original cast-iron drain lines that back up the moment storm surge overwhelms the municipal sewer. Knob-and-tube remnants in attic spaces become a serious concern the moment a roof opens up and water tracks toward old wiring. These aren't abstract risks — they're the specific conditions that turn a manageable roof leak into a multi-system loss.

## Our Storm Damage Restoration Process in Passaic

Every storm job starts with a systematic damage assessment before any material comes out. We photograph and document structural breaches, water intrusion paths, and any secondary hazards — including electrical panels in flooded basements, which are common in Passaic's older walk-up stock. That documentation matters for your insurance claim and for understanding the full scope before we commit to a repair sequence.

Once the structure is secured — emergency tarping on compromised roofs, board-up on blown windows or damaged facades — we move into water extraction and structural drying. We use truck-mounted and portable extraction units alongside commercial-grade desiccant and refrigerant dehumidifiers and high-velocity air movers calibrated to the material we're drying. Brick and plaster, common in Passaic's pre-war buildings, hold moisture differently than modern drywall: surface readings can look acceptable while the substrate stays wet, so we use thermal imaging and deep-wall moisture meters to confirm drying is complete before any reconstruction begins.

Reconstruction is handled under our NJ Licensed Home Improvement Contractor license, so there's no handoff gap between the mitigation crew and the rebuild — one point of contact, one timeline.
