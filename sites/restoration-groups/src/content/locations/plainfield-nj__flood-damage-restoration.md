When a storm event backs up the municipal system, those basements don't just flood — they flood fast, and the water is often Category 3 (sewage-contaminated), which changes the entire scope of cleanup and disposal.

## Our Flood Damage Restoration Process in Plainfield

Every flood loss is different, but the sequence matters and the science doesn't change. Here is how a typical residential flood job unfolds in Plainfield:

**1. Emergency extraction and safety assessment.** We arrive, assess structural safety, identify the water category (clean supply line vs. gray vs. black water), and deploy truck-mounted or portable extraction units to remove standing water. In finished basements with carpet over concrete, that extraction step alone can pull hundreds of gallons.

**2. Controlled demolition of unsalvageable materials.** Category 3 water — common in sewer-backup and brook-overflow events — requires removing saturated drywall, insulation, and flooring to defined flood cuts rather than attempting to dry materials in place. In Plainfield's older homes, this sometimes means cutting through horsehair plaster or removing original hardwood that has cupped beyond refinishing.

**3. Antimicrobial treatment and structural drying.** After demo, we apply EPA-registered antimicrobial agents to framing and concrete, then deploy commercial-grade LGR dehumidifiers and high-velocity air movers calibrated to the room volume and ambient conditions. We monitor moisture readings in framing, subfloor, and wall cavities daily — not just surface readings — and adjust equipment placement until we reach documented dry standard per the IICRC S500.

**4. Documentation and rebuild coordination.** We photograph and log every affected material for your insurance claim, then coordinate the rebuild phase — framing, drywall, flooring, and finishing — so you are not managing two separate contractors.

## Local Note

One thing that surprises homeowners in the Van Wyck Brooks Historic District and similar pre-war Plainfield neighborhoods: plaster walls dry on a fundamentally different timeline than modern drywall. Plaster is dense and holds moisture deep in its substrate long after the surface reads dry on a pin meter. We use both pin and pinless meters at multiple depths and extend drying cycles accordingly — sometimes by several days compared to a comparable drywall home. Skipping that step is how mold colonizes behind a wall that "passed" a surface check. If a previous contractor told you the job was done in 48 hours on a plaster-wall Victorian, it is worth a second opinion.
