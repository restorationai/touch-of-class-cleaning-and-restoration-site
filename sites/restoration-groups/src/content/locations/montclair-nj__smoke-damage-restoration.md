## Why Montclair Properties Are Especially Vulnerable to Smoke Damage

Montclair's housing stock is one of the most architecturally varied in Essex County — and that variety creates real complications after a fire. The Estate Section's grand center-hall colonials and Tudor revivals were built with old-growth lumber, horsehair plaster, and intricate millwork that absorbs smoke odor at a cellular level. Balloon-frame construction, standard in homes built before roughly 1940, means smoke can migrate vertically through wall cavities from the basement to the attic in a single event, depositing soot far from the room where the fire actually started.

## Our Smoke Damage Restoration Process in Montclair

Every smoke job starts with a structured assessment rather than a walk-through with a clipboard. We use thermal imaging to trace smoke migration through wall cavities — particularly important in Montclair's balloon-frame and plaster-wall homes where soot deposits can exist well beyond the visible burn area. That assessment drives the scope: there is no point cleaning surfaces that will be re-contaminated by hidden residue in an adjacent cavity.

Once the scope is set, we establish containment and negative air pressure to prevent cross-contamination to unaffected areas. Dry chemical sponging removes loose soot from plaster, trim, and structural members before any wet cleaning begins — reversing that order drives residue deeper into porous material. For the persistent smoke odor that settles into wood framing, subfloor decking, and fabric, we deploy hydroxyl generators and thermal fogging, both of which penetrate the same cavities the smoke originally traveled through.

Contents — furniture, textiles, artwork — are inventoried and, where appropriate, packed out to our facility for ultrasonic cleaning and deodorization. For homes near the Montclair Art Museum neighborhood or the Estate Section where fine art and antiques are common, we document condition before and after handling and can coordinate with a conservator for high-value pieces.

The process closes with air quality verification, not just a visual inspection. We confirm particulate levels are within acceptable ranges before we sign off.

## Local Note

Montclair's plaster walls — the real lime-and-lath variety found throughout Upper Montclair and the Estate Section — behave very differently from modern drywall when exposed to smoke. Plaster is highly alkaline, which partially neutralizes the acidic soot on the surface, but the same porosity that gives it that quality also allows smoke odor compounds to penetrate deeply into the substrate. In our experience, plaster walls that look clean after a dry-sponge pass can still off-gas odor for weeks if the underlying lath and framing haven't been treated. We account for this in our scope rather than discovering it after a homeowner calls back with a complaint.
