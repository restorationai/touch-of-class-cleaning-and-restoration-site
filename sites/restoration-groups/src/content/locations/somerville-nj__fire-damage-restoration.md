## Why Somerville's Housing Stock Amplifies Fire and Smoke Damage

Most of the residential neighborhoods ringing Downtown Somerville were built between 1900 and 1940, a period when balloon framing was standard. In that construction method, wall cavities run continuously from the foundation sill plate to the roof ridge — there are no fire stops at each floor level. A fire that starts in a first-floor kitchen can push superheated gases and smoke protein up those open channels into second-floor bedrooms and attic spaces within minutes, long before flames reach those areas. Restoration crews working in the 08876 ZIP code encounter this pattern regularly: visible char confined to one room, yet acrid smoke odor saturating insulation, subflooring, and plaster throughout the structure.

Older Somerville homes also tend to have original horsehair-plaster walls, which absorb smoke particulates differently than modern drywall. The porous lime matrix traps odor-causing compounds deeper into the substrate, which means surface wiping alone won't eliminate the smell. Effective fire and smoke restoration here requires HEPA vacuuming of surfaces, thermal fogging or hydroxyl treatment to neutralize odor molecules in cavities, and in some cases encapsulation of plaster before repainting.

## Our Fire Damage Restoration Process in Somerville

Every post-fire restoration starts with a structured assessment before any cleaning begins. Our IICRC Certified Firm (#210213) technicians document the extent of char, soot, and smoke migration room by room, photograph all affected materials for the insurance file, and identify structural concerns — compromised joists, weakened load-bearing walls, or roof penetrations from fire department ventilation cuts — that need to be addressed before interior work proceeds.

From there, the process moves through five concrete phases:

1. **Board-up and tarping** — securing window openings, door frames, and any roof penetrations to prevent weather intrusion and unauthorized entry while the property is uninhabitable.
2. **Debris removal and controlled demolition** — removing charred framing, flooring, and insulation that cannot be restored, with disposal handled per NJ DEP requirements.
3. **Smoke and soot cleaning** — dry-chemical sponging of ceilings and walls, HEPA vacuuming of HVAC registers, and cleaning of salvageable contents using appropriate methods for each material type.
4. **Odor neutralization** — thermal fogging or hydroxyl generation to reach smoke molecules trapped inside wall cavities, ductwork, and soft furnishings.
5. **Structural drying and reconstruction** — if fire suppression water is present, drying per the IICRC S500 standard before any rebuild work begins, followed by framing, drywall, finish carpentry, and painting to return the home to pre-loss condition.
