## Our Emergency Board-Up and Tarping Process in Teaneck

When a call comes in, the crew loads the van with pre-cut OSB panels, 6-mil polyethylene sheeting, commercial-grade tarps rated for 50 mph wind, and the fastening hardware appropriate to the substrate — masonry anchors for Teaneck's brick facades, structural screws for wood-frame openings. On arrival, the first priority is a quick walk-around to identify every breach: broken windows, kicked-in doors, fire-vented roof sections, and any soffit or fascia damage that isn't obvious from the street.

For window and door openings, panels are cut to overlap the frame by at least two inches on each side and fastened through the exterior trim into the structural framing — not just into the window casing, which can pull free under wind load. For roof damage, tarps are run from the ridge down past the eave and secured with weighted battens or screwed-down lumber nailers rather than simply draped and tied, which is the method that fails in the first gust. Every secured opening is photographed before and after for your insurance documentation, and a written scope of the work is left on-site or emailed same day.

## Local Note: Brick Facades and Masonry Anchoring in Teaneck

One thing that catches crews off guard on Teaneck's older brick homes is the mortar joint condition. On a 1920s or 1930s Tudor, mortar can be soft enough that a standard Tapcon anchor won't hold under repeated wind loading — the anchor spins in deteriorated joint material rather than biting into the brick. Experienced crews carry both masonry anchors and through-bolt hardware so that if the mortar won't hold, a panel can be secured with a through-bolt and backing plate on the interior side of the opening.
