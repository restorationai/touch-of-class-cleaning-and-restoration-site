## Our Appliance Leak Cleanup Process in New Brunswick

When we arrive, the first priority is stopping the water source if it hasn't been shut off. We then use thermal imaging cameras and calibrated moisture meters to map exactly how far saturation has traveled — under cabinets, behind toe kicks, beneath vinyl plank or hardwood, and into adjacent walls. In New Brunswick's multifamily buildings, that mapping step is critical because water follows the path of least resistance through shared floor assemblies, and a washing machine flood on the second floor can show up as a wet ceiling in a first-floor unit before it's visible anywhere in the origin unit.

Extraction comes next: truck-mounted or portable extractors pull standing water and surface saturation, followed by placement of commercial drying equipment — air movers and desiccant or refrigerant dehumidifiers sized to the affected area. We monitor moisture readings at each visit and adjust equipment placement until materials reach target drying goals per the IICRC S500 standard. If flooring or cabinetry is too saturated to dry in place, we document the damage thoroughly for your insurance carrier before any removal begins.
