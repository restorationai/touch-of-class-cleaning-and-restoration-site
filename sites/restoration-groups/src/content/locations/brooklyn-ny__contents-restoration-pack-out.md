When smoke, soot, or floodwater works through a Brooklyn brownstone, the damage rarely stops at the walls. It moves into furniture, clothing, artwork, heirlooms, and the irreplaceable accumulation of a household — and in a borough where a Park Slope parlor floor might hold antique plaster moldings, original hardwood, and a century's worth of family belongings, getting those contents out safely and restoring them properly is as important as drying the structure itself. That's the work of a professional pack-out and contents restoration team, and it demands a different approach in Brooklyn than almost anywhere else in the region.

## Why Brooklyn's Housing Stock Makes Contents Restoration Different

Brooklyn's brownstone belt — running through Park Slope, Brooklyn Heights, and the fringes of Bed-Stuy — is largely composed of masonry rowhouses built between the 1880s and 1920s. These buildings were designed before modern vapor barriers, and their garden-level and cellar apartments sit at or below grade. When a pipe bursts in a third-floor unit or a nor'easter pushes water through a failing areaway drain, the moisture migrates downward through plaster, brick, and original wood lath before it ever reaches a surface that reads wet on a standard moisture meter.

That means contents on lower floors — in finished basements, garden apartments in ZIP code 11215, or street-level commercial spaces in Williamsburg — can absorb significant contamination before a resident even notices a problem. Fabric, paper, and porous materials sitting on or near the floor are the first casualties. In coastal neighborhoods like Canarsie, the memory of Sandy's storm surge is not abstract; saltwater intrusion leaves a corrosive residue on metal, electronics, and finished wood that accelerates deterioration within days if items aren't moved, inventoried, and treated promptly.

## Our Pack-Out and Contents Restoration Process

A pack-out begins with a room-by-room inventory before anything is moved. Every item is photographed, logged, and assigned a condition rating — documentation that matters both for your insurance claim and for ensuring everything comes back to the right address. In a rowhouse with a shared party wall and a co-op board that requires written notice before work begins, that paper trail is essential.

Once items are catalogued, they move to a controlled facility environment where the actual restoration work happens: ultrasonic cleaning for hard goods and non-porous surfaces, ozone and hydroxyl treatment for odor-bearing textiles and soft furnishings, specialized drying chambers for documents and photographs, and hand-cleaning for artwork or items with delicate finishes. Electronics go to certified technicians for assessment before any power is applied.

When the structural work on the property is complete and the environment passes clearance, contents are returned, unpacked, and placed according to the original inventory — not left in boxes in the hallway.

## Insurance and Building Documentation in Brooklyn

Brooklyn's mix of co-op buildings, condo associations, and privately owned brownstones means the insurance and documentation picture varies significantly by address. Co-op shareholders typically deal with both a personal HO-6 policy and a master building policy held by the board — and boards often require their own documentation before approving remediation work on shared systems. Condo associations in newer Williamsburg and Brooklyn Heights conversions may have similar requirements.

The Restoration Group, as an IICRC Certified Firm (#210213), produces the scope-of-loss documentation, photo inventories, and drying logs that carriers and management companies expect. We communicate directly with adjusters and, where needed, with building managers to keep the approval process moving.

## Local Note

In Brooklyn's pre-war rowhouses, garden-level apartments often have interior stairs that are too narrow for standard furniture dollies — a detail that matters enormously during a pack-out when speed counts. Crews working in these buildings need to plan for manual carries, furniture disassembly at the threshold, and tight stairwell clearances before the first box is loaded. Building access in a shared brownstone also means coordinating with upstairs neighbors and, in some cases, the building's managing agent, to ensure the front vestibule and stoop are available during the move. Knowing this before arrival — not after — is the difference between a smooth pack-out and a four-hour delay on a bad day.
