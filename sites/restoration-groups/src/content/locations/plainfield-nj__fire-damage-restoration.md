## Why Plainfield's Housing Stock Shapes Every Fire Job

The density and age of Plainfield's residential neighborhoods creates fire restoration challenges that newer suburban markets simply don't see. Homes in Netherwood and the West End were largely built between 1890 and 1940, and many still have original balloon framing — vertical studs that run uninterrupted from foundation sill to roof rafter with no horizontal fire-blocking between floors. In a fire, that cavity acts like a chimney flue, pulling smoke and heat upward and depositing soot inside walls on floors that never showed visible flame damage. When we open those walls, the char pattern often surprises homeowners who assumed the fire "stayed in the kitchen."

Add to that the cast-iron plumbing, older electrical panels, and finished basements common across the Crescent Area and Sleepy Hollow, and you have a structural picture that requires careful scoping before any demo begins. Knob-and-tube wiring that survived the fire may still need to be evaluated and often replaced before reconstruction can close walls — a coordination step we flag with the homeowner and their electrician early in the process.

## Our Fire Damage Restoration Process in Plainfield

The first hour after we arrive is about containment and documentation, not demolition. We photograph and measure every affected room, note smoke migration paths, and identify materials — plaster, original hardwood, period millwork — that can be restored rather than replaced. That distinction matters in Plainfield's historic neighborhoods, where matching original trim profiles and plaster textures is both a preservation priority and, often, a condition of the homeowner's insurance settlement.

From there, the process moves in a defined sequence:

- **Board-up and structural stabilization** — securing openings left by fire department ventilation cuts or roof damage before weather can compound the loss.
- **Smoke and soot removal** — dry chemical sponging of surfaces, HEPA vacuuming of HVAC cavities, and thermal fogging or hydroxyl generation to neutralize odor molecules embedded in porous materials.
- **Selective demolition** — removing only what cannot be restored, with documentation for your adjuster at every step.
- **Deodorization and air quality control** — running air scrubbers with activated carbon filtration until ambient odor readings drop to pre-loss baselines.
- **Reconstruction** — framing, drywall, insulation, paint, and finish work performed under our NJ Licensed Home Improvement Contractor license, so you're not managing a second contractor.

All drying and cleaning protocols follow IICRC standards (Certified Firm #210213), which most major carriers require before approving a final scope.
