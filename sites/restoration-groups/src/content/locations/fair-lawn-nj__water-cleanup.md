## Our Water Cleanup Process in Fair Lawn

When a crew arrives, the first priority is stopping any water that is still moving — shutting the source, diverting flow, or sandbagging if the entry point is exterior. From there, the work follows a structured sequence:

**Assessment and moisture mapping.** Technicians use thermal imaging and calibrated moisture meters to trace water migration behind walls and under flooring. In Fair Lawn's older plaster-and-lath homes, water can read dry at the surface while the lath behind it stays saturated — thermal imaging catches what a surface reading misses.

**Standing water extraction.** Truck-mounted and portable extractors remove standing water from finished basements, crawl spaces, and slab floors. The goal is to get bulk water out before it wicks further into building materials.

**Structural drying.** Industrial air movers and low-grain refrigerant dehumidifiers are placed according to the IICRC S500 standard for water damage drying. Drying logs are recorded daily so there is a documented record for your insurance adjuster.

**Material evaluation.** Not everything can be dried in place. Saturated drywall, insulation, and flooring that has begun to delaminate is removed to prevent secondary mold growth. Decisions are made based on moisture readings, not guesswork.

**Antimicrobial treatment.** Affected cavities and structural surfaces are treated to inhibit microbial growth before reconstruction begins.
