## Why Montclair Properties See Water Damage Differently

Montclair's housing stock is part of what makes it one of Essex County's most distinctive communities — and one of the more technically demanding for water cleanup work. The grand Tudors and center-hall colonials in the Estate Section were built when plaster-and-lath was standard, and those walls behave nothing like modern drywall when wet. Plaster absorbs moisture slowly and releases it even more slowly, which means a surface reading can appear dry while the lath behind it stays saturated for days. Rushing the drying phase in these homes leads to hidden mold growth inside wall cavities.

The town's topography adds another layer of risk. Hillside drainage off the Watchung ridge concentrates stormwater runoff into low-lying streets, and aging cast-iron drain lines in older homes struggle to handle the surge. Hurricane Ida in 2021 made this visible in the worst way — flash flooding and sewer backups hit Frog Hollow and the South End hard, sending contaminated water into basements that had never flooded before. The Third River and its tributaries running through town mean that certain low-lying streets carry real flood risk during any significant rain event, not just named storms. Sump pumps in these areas work overtime, and when they fail — or when power goes out during the storm that caused the flooding — the basement fills fast.

## Our Water Cleanup Process in Montclair

Every job starts with moisture mapping. Before any extraction equipment runs, we use thermal imaging cameras and calibrated moisture meters to trace where water has traveled — including inside plaster walls, beneath hardwood floors, and into the framing around finished basement ceilings. In Montclair's older homes, water rarely stays where it entered.

Extraction comes next. Truck-mounted and portable extraction units pull standing water and surface saturation from flooring, carpet, and subfloor material. For sewage-involved losses — common after the kind of sewer backups Frog Hollow and South End residents saw during Ida — we follow Category 3 protocols: affected porous materials are removed rather than dried in place, and the space is treated with EPA-registered antimicrobials before drying equipment is set.

Structural drying uses commercial-grade desiccant and refrigerant dehumidifiers alongside high-velocity air movers, positioned based on the moisture map rather than placed generically around the room. Drying logs are recorded daily and provided to your insurance carrier as part of the claim documentation. As an IICRC Certified Firm (#210213), our drying protocols follow the S500 standard — the benchmark most carriers require before closing a water damage claim.
