## Our Emergency Board-Up and Tarping Process in Hoboken

Every board-up and tarping job starts with a rapid site assessment — we photograph and measure every opening before a single sheet goes up, both for structural accuracy and for your insurance documentation. In Hoboken's rowhouse blocks, that assessment includes checking shared party walls for breach points that aren't visible from the street.

For window and door openings, we use exterior-grade OSB cut to fit and secured with structural screws into the masonry surround — not just friction-fit panels that a gust off the Hudson can dislodge. For roof openings, we deploy heavy-duty polyethylene tarps (minimum 6-mil, typically 10-mil for larger spans) lapped and mechanically fastened to avoid the wind uplift that flat Hoboken rooftops are particularly prone to. On slate or clay-tile roofs common in the Uptown and Castle Point neighborhoods, we use non-penetrating ballast anchoring where possible to avoid adding fastener damage to an already compromised surface.

## Local Note: Hoboken's Brick Facades Require a Different Anchoring Approach

One thing that catches out-of-area crews in Hoboken: the exterior brick on most pre-war rowhouses is a single wythe — roughly 3.5 inches thick — with no structural backup behind it. Driving long lag screws through that brick to anchor board-up panels can fracture the masonry and create a new damage claim. Our crews use masonry anchors sized for single-wythe brick, or we anchor to the window frame itself when the frame is intact. On buildings where the facade brick is visibly spalled or cracked — common in blocks that took on water during Sandy in 2012 — we flag the condition in our documentation before touching the wall, so there's no dispute later about what was pre-existing.
