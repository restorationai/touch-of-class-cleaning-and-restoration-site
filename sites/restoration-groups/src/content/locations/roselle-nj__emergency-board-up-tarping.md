## Our Emergency Board-Up and Tarping Process in Roselle

Every board-up and tarping job starts with a rapid exterior assessment — we walk the full perimeter before touching anything, because the visible breach is rarely the only one. On Roselle's older homes, we check for secondary openings: rotted fascia that lifted with the storm, attic vents that blew free, or basement window frames that shifted when the foundation settled.

For roof tarping, we use minimum 6-mil polyethylene tarps anchored with 2×4 cap strips screwed into sound decking — not draped loosely and weighted with bricks, which is a temporary fix that fails in the next wind event. On steeper Victorian rooflines common in the Warinanco neighborhood, we use roof jacks and fall-arrest systems; speed never overrides safe rigging.

Window and door board-up uses ½-inch CDX plywood cut to fit the rough opening, secured with exterior-grade screws into the framing — not just the trim. On fire-damaged frames where the wood is compromised, we sister in temporary blocking before fastening so the board actually holds.
