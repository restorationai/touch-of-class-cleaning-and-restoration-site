## Our Smoke Damage Restoration Process in Livingston

When a call comes in from a 07039 address, the first priority is scoping the smoke migration — not just the room where the fire occurred. On arrival, the crew walks the structure with an air quality monitor and a thermal camera to identify cold spots where soot has penetrated wall cavities. In older plaster homes common to the Squiretown and Northland sections, that step is especially important because plaster is porous in ways that modern drywall is not; soot embeds into the substrate rather than sitting on the surface.

From there, the process follows a defined sequence:

- **Containment and HVAC isolation** — registers are sealed and the air handler is locked out to prevent recirculation of airborne particulates.
- **Dry residue removal** — dry chemical sponges lift loose soot from walls, ceilings, and structural surfaces before any wet cleaning begins. Introducing moisture too early smears soot and sets stains permanently.
- **Chemical cleaning** — alkaline cleaners neutralize acidic smoke residue on hard surfaces; protein-fire residue from kitchen fires requires an enzymatic treatment.
- **Duct cleaning and deodorization** — forced-air systems are cleaned mechanically and then treated with hydroxyl generators or thermal fogging to break down odor-causing compounds in the ductwork and wall cavities.
- **Content pack-out and cleaning** — soft goods, clothing, and furnishings are inventoried and transported to a controlled cleaning facility when on-site cleaning is not sufficient.
- **Structural repairs and repainting** — affected drywall or plaster is repaired, sealed with an odor-blocking primer, and refinished.

As an IICRC Certified Firm (#210213), the work is documented to the S700 standard for smoke and fire damage, which matters when the insurance adjuster reviews the scope.
