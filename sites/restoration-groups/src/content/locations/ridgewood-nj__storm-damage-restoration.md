## Our Storm Damage Restoration Process in Ridgewood

Every job starts with a full structural and moisture assessment before any materials are moved. In a historic Ridgewood home, that means probing plaster walls and original hardwood floors with non-invasive moisture meters to map the full extent of saturation — because water in a 1920s Tudor travels differently than it does in a 1990s colonial. Plaster is dense and slow to release moisture; if it isn't fully dried before reconstruction begins, mold colonization can follow within 24 to 48 hours.

Once the scope is documented — with photographs, moisture readings, and written notes formatted for insurance carriers — we move to emergency stabilization: tarping compromised roof sections, boarding openings left by fallen limbs, and extracting any standing water. Structural drying follows using commercial-grade desiccant dehumidifiers and high-velocity air movers calibrated to the volume and material composition of each space. Our process follows the IICRC S500 and S520 standards, and as an IICRC Certified Firm (#210213), our documentation holds up to carrier scrutiny.

Reconstruction — roofing, framing, drywall, trim, and finish work — is handled under our NJ Licensed Home Improvement Contractor credential, so homeowners deal with one point of contact from first call through final walkthrough.
