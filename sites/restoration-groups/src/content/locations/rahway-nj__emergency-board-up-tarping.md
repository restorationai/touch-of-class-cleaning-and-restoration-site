## Our Emergency Board-Up and Tarping Process in Rahway

When a call comes in from a Rahway address, the first thing we assess — before a crew leaves Kenilworth — is the nature and scale of the opening. A single broken window on a ground-floor unit calls for a different response than a fire that has vented through a second-story gable on a balloon-frame home near Hazelwood. That initial triage shapes what materials and crew size we dispatch.

On arrival, we do a rapid perimeter walk to identify every compromised point: broken glazing, damaged door frames, fire-vented soffits, missing ridge sections. Ground-level openings are measured and fitted with plywood panels cut to overlap the frame and fastened with structural screws — not staples — so the board-up holds through wind events rather than just keeping rain out overnight. Roof tarps are secured with weighted battens and anchor lines tied to structural members, not just draped and left; a tarp that lifts in a 40 mph gust causes more damage than no tarp at all. We photograph every opening before and after, which feeds directly into the insurance documentation package.

## Local Note: Balloon-Frame Homes Near Downtown Rahway

One thing worth knowing if you own or manage a pre-1940 home in the blocks around Downtown Rahway: balloon-frame construction means the wall studs run continuously from the sill plate to the roof rafters with no fire-stop blocking between floors. When a window is broken or a section of siding is compromised, wind-driven rain doesn't just wet the surface — it travels vertically inside the wall cavity and can emerge as moisture damage two floors away from the original opening. When we board up these homes, we also probe adjacent wall sections for moisture intrusion that may not be visible yet, because catching it at the board-up stage is far less expensive than finding it during a later mold remediation call.
