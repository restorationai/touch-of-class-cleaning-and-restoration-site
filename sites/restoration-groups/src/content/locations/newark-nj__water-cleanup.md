Newark's vulnerability to water intrusion is structural, not accidental. ## Our Water Cleanup Process in Newark

Effective water cleanup in Newark starts with understanding what's behind the walls, not just what's visible on the floor. The process follows the IICRC S500 standard for water damage restoration, adapted to the realities of Newark's building types.

**Assessment and containment** come first. Moisture meters and thermal imaging cameras identify saturation in plaster, brick cavities, and subfloor assemblies that look dry at the surface. In multifamily buildings, this step also determines whether adjacent units are affected — a critical question for landlords and property managers who need to know their exposure before calling their carrier.

**Standing water removal** uses truck-mounted and portable extraction equipment sized to the space. Basement flooding from sewer backup requires both water extraction and antimicrobial treatment, since combined sewer overflow is classified as Category 3 (grossly contaminated) water under industry standards.

**Structural drying** in Newark's older buildings takes longer than in newer construction. Plaster releases moisture slowly and can hold saturation even when the surface reads dry. Drying chambers, desiccant dehumidifiers, and directed airflow are calibrated to actual moisture readings — not to a standard number of days — and documented throughout for insurance purposes.

**Debris removal and documentation** close out the mitigation phase. Every affected material is photographed, measured, and logged in a format that insurance adjusters can work with directly.
