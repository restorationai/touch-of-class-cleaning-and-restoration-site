## Our Smoke Damage Restoration Process in Passaic

The first step on any smoke job is containment and assessment — mapping exactly where smoke traveled, not just where the fire burned. In a multi-unit building, that means walking every floor, checking shared mechanical spaces, and testing HVAC returns before anything gets cleaned. Smoke residue falls into categories (dry, wet, protein-based, fuel-oil) and each requires a different cleaning agent and technique. Protein smoke from a kitchen fire — the kind that produces almost no visible soot but leaves a greasy, nearly invisible film — is among the hardest to detect and the most persistent if treated with the wrong chemistry.

Once the scope is mapped, the process moves through structural cleaning (walls, ceilings, framing), content cleaning or pack-out, HVAC decontamination, and odor neutralization using thermal fogging and hydroxyl generation. In Passaic's older plaster-wall homes, we allow additional dwell time for cleaning agents because the substrate absorbs at a different rate than modern drywall. Final air quality checks confirm that particulate levels are within acceptable ranges before we close out the job.
