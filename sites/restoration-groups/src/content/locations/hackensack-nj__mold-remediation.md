## Why Hackensack Properties See Mold Issues

The housing stock tells most of the story. Older two- and three-family homes throughout the Southside and Fairmount neighborhoods were built when vapor barriers weren't standard, crawl spaces were common, and galvanized supply lines were the norm. Those galvanized pipes corrode from the inside out — a slow pinhole leak inside a wall cavity can feed mold growth for months before water stains appear on a ceiling. In the high-rise apartment buildings along the Prospect Avenue corridor, a single burst pipe on an upper floor can cascade water through stacked units, leaving multiple tenants dealing with wet walls and ceilings simultaneously.

The geography adds pressure. The eastern edge of the city borders tidal-influenced lowlands where heavy rain events push groundwater up through foundation cracks. Basements in blocks near Johnson Park and lower-elevation streets flood not just from above but from below, leaving concrete block foundations saturated. Block foundations wick moisture into framing and insulation long after the visible water is gone — and that sustained dampness is exactly what mold needs.

## Our Mold Remediation Process in Hackensack

Every job starts with a thorough inspection, including moisture mapping with thermal imaging to find wet pockets that aren't visible to the eye. In Hackensack's older homes, that often means checking inside plaster walls, beneath original hardwood floors, and around cast-iron drain stacks where condensation accumulates.

Once affected areas are identified, we establish negative-air containment — plastic sheeting, sealed doorways, and HEPA-filtered air scrubbers running under negative pressure so mold spores can't migrate to clean rooms during removal. Compromised drywall, insulation, and wood framing are removed and bagged on-site per EPA guidelines. Surfaces are treated with an EPA-registered antimicrobial agent, and we run HEPA air scrubbers through a full air-exchange cycle before clearance testing. As an IICRC Certified Firm (#210213), our process follows the IICRC S520 standard for mold remediation — the same standard that most insurance carriers and industrial hygienists reference when evaluating a completed job.

## Local Note

In Hackensack's prewar two- and three-family homes — particularly the wood-frame buildings common through the Southside — original horsehair plaster over wood lath is still intact in many units. Plaster holds moisture differently than modern drywall: it doesn't crumble and discolor as quickly, so mold can colonize the back side of a plaster wall for an extended period before any visible sign appears on the painted surface. When we're working in these buildings, we probe carefully before assuming a wall is dry, and we account for longer drying times after water intrusion events. Cutting into original plaster also triggers lead paint protocols under NJ regulations, so our crews are prepared to work under those requirements when the building age warrants it.
