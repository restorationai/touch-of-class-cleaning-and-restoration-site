## Our Commercial Restoration Process in Hillside

Every commercial job starts with a documented scope — not a rough estimate, but a line-item moisture map and damage assessment that your insurance adjuster can work from directly. For water losses, that means thermal imaging and calibrated moisture readings across flooring assemblies, wall cavities, and ceiling systems before a single piece of equipment is placed. ## Local Note

One pattern worth knowing if you manage commercial property in Hillside: buildings along the lower sections of Conant Street that connect to the municipal combined sewer system are particularly exposed during hard summer rain events. When the system surcharges, sewage-contaminated water can enter floor drains even in buildings with no visible pipe failure — the pressure simply reverses flow. That's a Category 3 contamination event under IICRC classification, which requires a different extraction and disinfection protocol than a clean-water pipe burst. Calling it a "water damage" job and treating it like one leads to incomplete remediation and potential liability. If your building sits in that drainage zone, it's worth confirming with your plumber whether a backflow preventer is installed on your main drain lateral.
