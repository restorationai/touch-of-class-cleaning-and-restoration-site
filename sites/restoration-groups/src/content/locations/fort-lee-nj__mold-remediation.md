## Our Mold Remediation Process in Fort Lee

Every job starts with a thorough visual inspection and moisture mapping using thermal imaging and calibrated moisture meters — not a visual guess. In Fort Lee's high-rises, that means checking not just the reported unit but adjacent walls and the unit directly below, because water in a concrete-framed building travels laterally in ways that surprise owners who assume damage is contained.

Once the affected area is scoped, we establish negative-air containment using 6-mil poly barriers and HEPA-filtered air scrubbers to prevent cross-contamination to neighboring units — a step that matters especially in shared-wall condo buildings. Mold-affected drywall, insulation, and porous materials are removed, bagged, and disposed of per New Jersey DEP guidelines. Structural surfaces are then treated and dried to IICRC S520 protocol standards before any reconstruction begins. We document every phase with photographs and moisture readings, which supports insurance claims and satisfies HOA management requests for remediation records.

## Local Note

Something worth knowing if you're in one of Fort Lee's older Coytesville or The Hill buildings: pre-1960 plaster-and-lath wall assemblies hold moisture differently than modern drywall. The plaster itself is relatively mold-resistant, but the wood lath behind it is not, and it dries slowly — often taking 30 to 40 percent longer to reach acceptable moisture content than standard half-inch drywall would. Rushing to reconstruction before those readings normalize is one of the most common reasons mold returns in these older buildings. We extend the drying phase on lath-and-plaster assemblies specifically because of this, and we re-test before any new drywall goes up.
