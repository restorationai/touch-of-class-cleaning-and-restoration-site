## Why Rahway's Housing Stock Shapes Fire Damage Recovery

Rahway's residential neighborhoods span nearly a century of construction. The blocks closest to Downtown Rahway and the Main Street business district hold Victorian-era and 1920s homes with original plaster walls, balloon-frame construction, and wood lath that has dried out over generations. Balloon framing — where wall cavities run uninterrupted from the foundation to the roof — acts like a chimney flue during a fire, pulling smoke and heat vertically through the entire structure even when the visible burn stays in one room. That means a stove fire on the first floor can deposit soot in second-floor closets and attic spaces that look untouched.

Postwar capes and colonials in Leesville and Hazelwood add a different wrinkle: many were built with asbestos-containing materials in floor tiles, pipe insulation, and textured ceilings. Fire disturbs those materials. Before any demo or cleaning begins in homes of that era, we assess for regulated materials so the remediation doesn't create a secondary hazard. ## Our Fire Damage Restoration Process in Rahway

We start with a structured assessment, not a clipboard walk-through. The first hour on-site is spent mapping the fire's travel path: where heat peaked, where smoke migrated, and where water from suppression efforts is now sitting. Standing water and saturated materials from fire hoses are a secondary damage source that accelerates mold colonization within 24 to 48 hours — a timeline that matters in Rahway's humid summers.

From there, the process follows a defined sequence:

- **Board-up and stabilization** — securing windows, doors, and any structurally compromised areas so the property is safe and weather-tight before detailed work begins.
- **Soot and smoke mapping** — identifying dry soot (easier to vacuum), wet soot (sticky, acidic, found near lower-oxygen burn zones), and protein residue (nearly invisible but intensely odorous, common after kitchen fires).
- **Controlled demolition of unsalvageable material** — charred framing, saturated insulation, compromised drywall or plaster — documented photographically for your insurance adjuster.
- **Structural drying** — fire suppression water treated per the IICRC S500 standard, using commercial desiccants and air movers calibrated to the specific materials in the home.
- **Odor neutralization** — thermal fogging and hydroxyl generation to reach smoke compounds inside wall cavities and HVAC ductwork, not just surface-level deodorizing.
- **Reconstruction** — framing, drywall, finish carpentry, and painting scoped to match existing materials, which in older Rahway homes often means sourcing period-appropriate trim profiles.

As an IICRC Certified Firm (#210213) and NJ Licensed Home Improvement Contractor, we handle both the remediation and the rebuild under one contract, which simplifies the insurance claim and eliminates the gap period where a remediated-but-unrepaired home sits exposed.
