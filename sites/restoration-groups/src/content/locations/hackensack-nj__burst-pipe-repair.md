## Why Hackensack Properties See Burst Pipe Problems

Hackensack's housing stock tells the story. Much of the city was built between the 1920s and 1960s, which means a significant share of properties still carry galvanized steel supply lines and cast-iron drain stacks that have been corroding quietly for decades. Galvanized pipe doesn't fail with much warning — internal rust narrows the bore, water pressure spikes, and a seam gives way, often inside a wall cavity or under a slab where the leak runs undetected for hours.

The Prospect Avenue corridor adds another layer of complexity. Property managers dealing with stacked-unit losses face damage claims across multiple tenants simultaneously, and drying timelines extend because moisture migrates through concrete decking in ways it doesn't through wood-frame floors.

The commercial and institutional core of Hackensack — the Bergen County Courthouse district, Main Street retail, and the facilities surrounding Hackensack University Medical Center — adds overnight and weekend losses to the mix. ## Our Burst Pipe Cleanup and Repair Process in Hackensack

When a call comes in, a crew mobilizes from our Kenilworth location and heads into Hackensack via Route 9W or the Garden State Parkway to Route 4, depending on traffic and the exact address. Once on-site, the first priority is source control — shutting the affected valve or coordinating with the building's main shutoff so water stops moving.

From there, the process follows a structured sequence:

- **Moisture mapping** using thermal imaging cameras and pin-type meters to trace where water traveled inside wall cavities, subfloor assemblies, and ceiling systems — not just where it pooled visibly.
- **Extraction** of standing water with truck-mounted and portable extraction units. In older Hackensack homes with original hardwood floors, early extraction matters: hardwood begins to cup and buckle within 24 to 48 hours of saturation.
- **Controlled demolition** of non-salvageable materials — wet insulation, saturated drywall — documented with photographs for the insurance file before anything is removed.
- **Structural drying** using industrial air movers and refrigerant dehumidifiers, calibrated to the specific material assemblies present. Plaster walls common in prewar homes release moisture more slowly than modern drywall and require extended dry times and more frequent moisture readings.
- **Repair coordination** for the pipe itself, working with licensed plumbers to restore the line before reconstruction begins.

Every job is documented to the IICRC S500 standard, which matters when an insurance adjuster reviews the claim.

## Hackensack Insurance Coordination

Most standard homeowners and commercial property policies cover sudden and accidental pipe bursts — the key phrase insurers look for. What they scrutinize is whether the damage was documented promptly and whether drying was performed to a verifiable standard. We photograph affected areas before, during, and after work, produce moisture logs with daily readings, and communicate directly with adjusters to keep the claim moving.

For multi-unit buildings on the Prospect Avenue corridor or in the Heights, losses often involve both the building owner's policy and individual tenant renters' insurance. We can provide separate scopes of work for each affected unit, which simplifies the claims process when multiple carriers are involved.

As an NJ Licensed Home Improvement Contractor registered with the NJ Division of Consumer Affairs, we can pull required permits for any structural repair work that follows cleanup — something adjusters and property managers sometimes need confirmed before authorizing reconstruction.

## Local Note

In Hackensack's older two- and three-family homes — particularly the wood-frame colonials and cape cods common in the Southside and near Johnson Park — the original plaster-on-lath wall construction absorbs water differently than the paper-faced drywall most drying protocols are written for. Plaster is denser and holds moisture longer, so drying equipment runs longer and moisture readings can plateau for days before the wall assembly actually reaches equilibrium. We flag this at setup so homeowners aren't surprised when the dehumidifiers are still running on day four or five. Pulling equipment too early in plaster construction is one of the more common reasons mold problems appear weeks after a "completed" water job.
