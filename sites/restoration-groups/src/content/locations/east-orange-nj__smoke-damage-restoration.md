## Why East Orange Properties See Smoke Damage Spread Differently

The building stock between Ampere and Elmwood tells the story. Pre-war brick construction with original horsehair plaster, pocket doors, and uninsulated interior walls creates a network of hidden pathways smoke exploits almost immediately. Unlike modern stick-frame homes where smoke damage is more contained, a fire in one unit of a three-story walk-up on a side street off Central Avenue can deposit soot and odor-causing particles two floors away within minutes.

Flat roofs — common on the postwar infill throughout the 07017 and 07018 ZIP codes — trap heat and recirculate smoke back into attic crawl spaces and rooftop mechanical rooms before ventilation can clear it. Galvanized risers and older HVAC systems without modern filtration pull smoke particulate through shared ductwork, meaning the unit above the fire often smells as bad as the unit where it started. That cross-unit contamination pattern shapes every decision we make on-site.

## Our Smoke Damage Restoration Process in East Orange

We start with a full scope walk before any cleaning begins. That means tracing smoke migration through every connected space — stairwells, utility chases, basement mechanical rooms, and adjacent units if the building owner grants access. Skipping this step is how crews miss contaminated ductwork and end up with callbacks six weeks later when odor returns.

Once the scope is documented, the process moves through four stages:

1. **Dry soot removal** — HEPA-filtered vacuuming of loose carbon deposits from surfaces before any wet cleaning, which prevents soot from being ground deeper into plaster or masonry.
2. **Chemical sponge and dry cleaning** — for delicate historic trim, original hardwood, and unpainted brick that can't tolerate aggressive wet methods.
3. **Wet cleaning and deodorization** — pH-balanced detergents followed by hydroxyl or thermal fogging to neutralize odor molecules embedded in porous surfaces. In units with original plaster walls, we extend dwell times because plaster is more absorbent than drywall and holds odor compounds longer.
4. **Air scrubbing** — HEPA air scrubbers run continuously during the project to capture airborne particulate, especially important in occupied multi-family buildings where neighboring units are still inhabited.

All work follows IICRC S700 standards for smoke and soot remediation. The Restoration Group holds IICRC Certified Firm status (#210213), and our technicians are trained to document scope and progress for insurance carriers.
