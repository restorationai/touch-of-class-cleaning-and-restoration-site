## Why Clifton Basements Flood — and Why It's Complicated Here

Clifton's geography puts a significant share of its housing stock in direct tension with two waterways that have a documented history of overrunning their banks. The Passaic River flooding during Tropical Storm Irene and again during Ida pushed water into finished basements across the 07011 and 07014 ZIP codes — not just surface water, but sewage-laden backflow that traveled through aging municipal storm systems and into floor drains. That's a different cleanup problem than a burst supply line.

The housing stock adds another layer. The majority of Clifton's residential neighborhoods — Athenia, Richfield, Montclair Heights — were built out in the 1940s through 1960s. Those capes, colonials, and split-levels were constructed with finished basements as usable living space, and their original cast-iron drain lines are reaching or past their service life. A drain that held for 60 years can fail quietly, letting water seep under tile or behind paneling for days before it's noticed. Original galvanized supply lines in the same era homes are similarly prone to pinhole leaks that go undetected until a wall cavity is already saturated.

In Botany Village, the older brick multi-family buildings present a different challenge: shared drainage infrastructure and masonry foundations that wick groundwater laterally. A flood event in one unit can migrate into adjacent units through the foundation itself, not just through shared plumbing.

## Our Basement Flood Cleanup Process in Clifton

The first step on any flooded basement is an honest assessment of what kind of water you're dealing with. Groundwater intrusion after a Passaic overflow is categorized differently than a broken supply line, and the extraction and disposal protocols differ accordingly. We use moisture meters and thermal imaging to map saturation behind finished walls and under subfloors — surfaces that read dry to the touch can conceal lath or subfloor material that is still holding significant moisture.

Once the water category is confirmed, we extract standing water with truck-mounted and portable units, then set drying equipment calibrated to the actual square footage and material types in the space. Concrete block foundations common in postwar Clifton construction absorb and release moisture slowly; drying timelines account for that, not a generic industry average. Affected drywall, insulation, and flooring that cannot be dried in place are removed to the flood cut line, and affected cavities are treated before any reconstruction begins.

Throughout the process, we document everything — photographs, moisture readings, equipment logs — in a format that supports your insurance claim directly.

## Clifton Insurance Coordination

Flood losses in Clifton frequently involve two separate policies: a standard homeowner's policy (which may cover sudden and accidental discharge but typically excludes rising groundwater) and a separate NFIP or private flood policy.
