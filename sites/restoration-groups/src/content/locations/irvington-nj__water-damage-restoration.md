## Our Water Damage Restoration Process in Irvington

When we arrive, the first priority is stopping the source — whether that means shutting a main, isolating a supply line, or coordinating with a plumber if the break is inside a shared wall. From there, the process follows IICRC S500 standards for structural drying, calibrated to what we actually find in Irvington buildings:

**1. Water extraction.** Truck-mounted and portable extractors pull standing water from floors, carpet, and subfloor assemblies. In finished basements, we move furniture and pull saturated carpet and pad immediately — leaving them in place accelerates mold colonization, which can begin within 24–48 hours in a closed basement.

**2. Moisture mapping.** Thermal imaging cameras and calibrated moisture meters map how far water has migrated inside wall cavities, under flooring, and into framing. In older plaster-and-lath construction — common in Irvington's prewar homes — moisture readings behave differently than in modern drywall assemblies, and we account for that in our drying targets.

**3. Structural drying.** Industrial desiccant and refrigerant dehumidifiers, along with high-velocity air movers, are positioned based on the moisture map. We monitor readings daily and adjust equipment placement until materials reach acceptable dryness levels per IICRC guidelines.

**4. Documentation.** Every moisture reading, equipment placement, and daily log is recorded and formatted for your insurance carrier. As an IICRC Certified Firm (#210213) and NJ Licensed Home Improvement Contractor, our documentation meets the standards adjusters expect.

## Local Note: Plaster Walls and Drying Time

In Irvington's prewar rowhouses and frame homes, original plaster-and-lath walls are still common — especially on upper floors that haven't been renovated. Plaster absorbs water more slowly than drywall but releases it more slowly too. A wall that reads elevated moisture after 48 hours of drying isn't necessarily failing to respond — it's releasing moisture at the rate the material allows. We factor this into our drying timelines and communicate it clearly to homeowners and adjusters so no one pulls equipment prematurely and leaves wet framing behind a "dry" surface reading.
