## Why Millburn's Older Homes Complicate Fire and Smoke Restoration

The homes that make Short Hills and Old Short Hills so desirable are the same ones that make post-fire restoration more demanding. Plaster-and-lath construction — standard in colonials and Tudors built before 1950 — absorbs smoke particulate and odor compounds differently than modern drywall. The calcium-based plaster acts almost like a sponge for acidic soot, and if cleaning is delayed even 24 to 48 hours, that acidity begins etching the surface and permanently discoloring paint finishes.

Old-growth Douglas fir and chestnut framing timbers, common in homes built along the Wyoming and Glenwood corridors, char deeply but often retain structural integrity — which is actually good news. It means more of the original structure can be cleaned, treated, and preserved rather than demolished, but it requires careful structural assessment before any restoration work begins. Knocking out sound framing because it looks charred is a costly mistake that experienced fire restoration crews know to avoid.

Millburn's proximity to the South Mountain Reservation also affects humidity levels year-round. Higher ambient moisture in the air slows the off-gassing of smoke odor from porous materials and can complicate the thermal fogging and hydroxyl treatments used to neutralize odor at the molecular level.

## Our Fire Damage Restoration Process in Millburn

Every fire loss is different, but the sequence matters. When a call comes in from a 07078 or 07041 address, the first priority is a same-day assessment — not a phone estimate, but eyes on the property. Structural fire damage requires a hands-on evaluation of load-bearing elements, the condition of the HVAC system (which spreads smoke residue through every duct run in the house), and the extent of water damage left behind by fire suppression.

From there, the process moves through board-up and temporary weatherproofing if the structure is compromised, followed by controlled demolition of materials that cannot be restored. Contents that can be saved — furniture, textiles, artwork, documents — are inventoried, packed out, and cleaned off-site using ultrasonic and ozone treatment where appropriate. Structural surfaces are cleaned using dry chemical sponges and wet cleaning agents matched to the soot type: protein-based smoke from a kitchen fire behaves very differently from the thick, oily residue left by a synthetic-material fire in a finished basement.

Odor neutralization comes last, not first. Masking smoke smell before the source material is cleaned is a temporary fix that fails within weeks. The Restoration Group's IICRC Certified Firm (#210213) protocols require that odor treatment follow confirmed surface cleaning — not replace it.
