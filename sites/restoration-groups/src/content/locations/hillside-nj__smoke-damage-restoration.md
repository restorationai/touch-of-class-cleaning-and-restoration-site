## Why Hillside Homes Are Especially Vulnerable to Smoke Damage

Hillside's housing stock is one of the oldest in Union County. The tight grid of colonials, capes, and postwar two-families that runs from Hillside Center out toward Coe's Corner was built largely between the 1920s and 1950s, and most of those structures share a few characteristics that make smoke damage harder to contain and clean.

First, balloon framing — common in homes built before the 1950s — lacks the fire-blocking between floors that platform-frame construction provides. When smoke enters a wall cavity, it can travel vertically from the basement to the attic without interruption, depositing fine particulate soot along every stud bay. Second, original plaster walls are far more porous than modern drywall. Smoke penetrates the surface layer and embeds in the brown coat beneath, which means surface wiping alone won't eliminate the odor. Third, many Hillside basements are finished or semi-finished, adding carpeting, drop ceilings, and drywall that trap smoke particles and hold odor long after the source fire is extinguished.

The Route 22 corridor and the Conant Street industrial area also generate a different category of loss — commercial warehouse fires, sprinkler-discharge events, and overnight structure fires in mixed-use buildings where smoke spreads across large open floor plates before anyone notices.

## Our Smoke Damage Restoration Process in Hillside

Smoke damage restoration isn't a single step — it's a sequenced process, and cutting corners on any phase means the odor comes back. As an IICRC Certified Firm (#210213), the work follows established industry protocols from the first walk-through to final clearance.

**Step 1 — Scope and documentation.** We photograph and document every affected surface, cavity, and material before anything is touched. This protects the insurance claim and establishes a baseline for clearance.

**Step 2 — Controlled demolition of unsalvageable materials.** Plaster that has absorbed heavy soot beyond the surface layer, saturated insulation, and charred structural members are removed before any cleaning begins. Attempting to clean over deeply contaminated substrate is one of the most common reasons smoke odor returns months later.

**Step 3 — Dry and wet chemical sponge cleaning.** Soot chemistry varies by fire type — protein fires from kitchen grease behave differently than synthetic fires from furniture or electrical components. We match the cleaning agent to the residue type rather than applying a single product to every surface.

**Step 4 — Thermal fogging and hydroxyl treatment.** For odor that has penetrated porous materials — plaster, wood framing, subflooring — thermal fogging or hydroxyl generation neutralizes odor molecules rather than masking them. In Hillside's older homes, this step is rarely optional.

**Step 5 — HEPA air scrubbing and final inspection.** HEPA-filtered air scrubbers run continuously through the restoration to capture fine particulate before the space is re-occupied.
