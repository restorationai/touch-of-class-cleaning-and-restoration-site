## Our Biohazard Cleanup Process in New Brunswick

Every job begins with a contained assessment. Before any materials are disturbed, the affected area is evaluated to determine the true extent of contamination — including surfaces that may not be visibly affected. In older New Brunswick buildings, this step is especially important because biological material can wick into porous substrates quickly.

Once the scope is established, the crew works in appropriate personal protective equipment, following OSHA bloodborne pathogen protocols and EPA guidelines for infectious material handling. Affected porous materials — flooring, drywall, insulation — are removed, bagged, and transported as regulated waste to a licensed disposal facility. Hard surfaces are cleaned with hospital-grade disinfectants, and the area is treated for odor at the molecular level, not masked.

Documentation is produced throughout: photographs, a written scope of work, and a completion report. That paperwork matters when an insurance carrier, a landlord, or a future tenant needs confirmation that the space was properly remediated.
