## Why Staten Island Properties See More Board-Up and Tarping Losses

The borough's housing stock is unusually varied, and that variety drives different failure modes. The early-1900s wood-frame homes clustered near St. George and Port Richmond were built before modern sheathing standards; their roof decks are often skip-sheeted boards rather than continuous plywood, which means a single missing shingle can open a gap large enough to admit wind-driven rain within one storm cycle. Move south toward Great Kills and Tottenville and you find dense postwar Cape Cods and split-levels — smaller footprints, but shallow roof pitches that pool water aggressively when gutters ice over in a hard freeze.

Then there's the commercial strip exposure. The Hylan Boulevard corridor in the 10306 zip code runs through retail centers with aging flat membrane roofs; a puncture from storm debris or a failed seam can mean a flooded stockroom by morning. Sprinkler-discharge events — a frozen head, a contractor nick — also generate board-up needs when the water pressure blows out a storefront window or warps a fire-rated door frame past the point of closure.

Aging cast-iron supply lines, common in pre-1960 homes throughout the borough, are a quieter driver. A pipe-break in a finished basement can go unnoticed for hours; by the time the homeowner calls, the burst has sometimes taken out a basement egress window or caused enough structural swelling that an exterior door no longer latches. Boarding that opening is step one before any drying work begins.

## Our Emergency Board-Up and Tarping Process in Staten Island

Every job starts with a rapid site assessment — we photograph and document conditions before touching anything, which matters for insurance purposes and for understanding what's structurally sound enough to anchor into. For roof tarping, we use reinforced polyethylene tarps rated for sustained wind loads, secured with weighted lumber battens rather than staples alone; on the older skip-sheeted decks common in St. George and Port Richmond, staples pull out under load, and a tarp that fails mid-storm is worse than no tarp at all.

For window and door board-up, panel sizing is cut on-site to fit the actual opening — not a standard sheet dropped in front of it. We use exterior-grade OSB or plywood, typically ¾-inch for ground-floor openings in commercial properties and ½-inch for residential, secured with structural screws into the surrounding framing rather than the window casing. On homes with aluminum or vinyl-clad frames, we add foam backer at the perimeter to prevent water infiltration around the panel edge.

When fire damage is involved, we coordinate with the fire marshal's release before boarding — FDNY and NYPD protocols require that the scene be cleared before any contractor secures an opening, and we're familiar with that sequence from working losses across all five boroughs.

One thing that catches crews off guard on East Shore properties — particularly the post-Sandy rebuilds and elevated homes in South Beach and Oakwood Beach — is the roof geometry.
