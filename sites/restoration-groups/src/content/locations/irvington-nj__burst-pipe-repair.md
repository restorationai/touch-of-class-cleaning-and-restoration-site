## Why Irvington Properties See Burst Pipe Issues

Most of the residential blocks radiating out from Irvington Center and down toward the Chancellor Avenue corridor were developed between roughly 1910 and 1940. That means galvanized steel supply lines are still common — and galvanized pipe doesn't fail gradually. It corrodes from the inside out over decades, then ruptures without warning, often during the first hard freeze of the season when water expands inside a line that's already paper-thin from rust buildup.

Cast-iron drain stacks in these same buildings present a different problem: they crack rather than burst, but a cracked drain inside a multifamily rowhouse can flood a lower unit just as thoroughly as a supply-line failure. Finished and semi-finished basements — nearly universal in the Grove Park and Union Avenue neighborhoods — turn what might be a crawl-space nuisance elsewhere into a full-content loss. Flooring, drywall, stored belongings, and mechanicals all sit in the direct path of water that has nowhere else to go.

The Springfield Avenue commercial corridor adds another layer: storefront buildings there often have aging rooftop mechanical rooms and exposed supply runs that freeze when HVAC systems cycle down overnight in January or February. An overnight pipe burst in a retail or mixed-use space on that corridor can mean standing water across the entire ground floor by the time staff arrives in the morning.

## Our Burst Pipe Cleanup and Repair Process in Irvington

The first priority when we arrive is stopping the source. We locate the shutoff — sometimes in a shared utility space that requires coordination with upstairs or downstairs tenants — and confirm the flow is stopped before any extraction begins. In multifamily buildings, that step alone can take longer than it would in a single-family home, and skipping it means pumping water out of a space that's still filling.

Once the source is controlled, we extract standing water with truck-mounted and portable units, then begin the moisture mapping process using thermal imaging cameras and calibrated moisture meters. In Irvington's older plaster walls, moisture readings at the surface often understate how far water has traveled — plaster wicks laterally through the lath, and we probe deeper than the obvious wet zone before setting drying equipment.

Structural drying follows the IICRC S500 standard: commercial-grade desiccant or refrigerant dehumidifiers, high-velocity air movers positioned to create a drying vortex around affected cavities, and daily monitoring logs until materials reach documented dry standard. We don't pull equipment early because a reading looks close — we pull it when the numbers confirm it.

For the repair side, we coordinate licensed plumbing work and handle the water-damage reconstruction — drywall, subfloor, insulation, finish materials — so you're not managing two separate contractors through an already stressful claim.
