## Why East Orange Properties See Persistent Odor Problems

The building stock here tells most of the story. Pre-war construction throughout Brick Church, Elmwood, and Doddtown means thick plaster walls, original hardwood subfloors, and uninsulated pipe chases that act as highways for smoke, mold, and sewer gases to migrate between floors. Flat roofs on postwar infill buildings pool water and fail quietly — by the time a tenant notices a musty smell in a top-floor unit near the Central Avenue corridor, the roof deck has been wet for weeks and microbial odor has set into the ceiling assembly.

Sewer backups are a recurring issue in low-lying sections of the city, particularly after heavy rain events. The flooding East Orange experienced during Hurricane Ida in 2021 — with the Second River tributaries overwhelming storm infrastructure — left dozens of finished basements and ground-floor units with standing sewage. That category of loss produces hydrogen sulfide and organic decay odors that bond chemically to porous surfaces; standard cleaning doesn't neutralize them.

Multi-unit occupancy adds another layer. A cooking fire or cigarette-smoke-saturated apartment in a 12-unit building doesn't stay contained to one unit for long. Shared HVAC returns, gaps around cast-iron stacks, and open stairwells in older walk-ups distribute odor throughout the structure within hours.

## Our Odor Removal and Deodorization Process in East Orange

Every job starts with a source assessment, not a treatment. We identify where the odor originates — whether that's charred framing inside a wall cavity, a contaminated HVAC air handler, saturated subfloor in a basement unit, or a combination — before selecting a method. Treating symptoms without finding the source is why odors return after a few weeks.

For smoke odor removal following fire damage, we typically use **thermal fogging** to replicate how smoke penetrated surfaces, driving a deodorizing agent into the same pores and voids the smoke reached. In occupied multi-family buildings, we coordinate unit access carefully and seal adjacent spaces before fogging to prevent cross-contamination.

For biological odors — sewer backup, decomposition, or mold-related mustiness — **hydroxyl deodorization** is often the right tool. Hydroxyl generators are safe to run in occupied or semi-occupied spaces and work continuously over 24–72 hours, breaking down odor-causing molecules at the molecular level rather than masking them. We also deploy **ozone treatment** in unoccupied spaces where rapid, deep penetration is needed, following strict re-entry protocols.

In East Orange's older buildings, we pay particular attention to plaster and lathe assemblies. Plaster is far more porous than drywall and can hold odor compounds deep in its substrate. We use injection-based deodorization on wall cavities when surface treatment alone won't reach the affected material, and we test air quality before and after to document that odor levels have actually dropped — not just that the space smells like product.
