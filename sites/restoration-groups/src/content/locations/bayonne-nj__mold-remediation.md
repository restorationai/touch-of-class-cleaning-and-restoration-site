## Why Bayonne Properties See Mold Problems

The housing stock here tells most of the story. Bayonne's residential blocks — particularly the dense rows of two- and three-family frame homes and brick rowhouses that stretch from Bergen Point up through Midtown — were built largely in the early 20th century. Original cast-iron drain lines and aging supply plumbing fail quietly, often inside walls or under finished basement slabs, feeding slow leaks that go unnoticed for weeks. By the time a tenant reports a stain or a landlord smells something off, mold colonies are already established behind the drywall or under the flooring.

The peninsula's geography compounds the problem. Newark Bay sits at the foot of many western-facing backyards, and the water table in low-lying areas is high enough that even a modest rain event can push moisture through foundation walls. Postwar capes with finished basements — common throughout Bayonne's residential neighborhoods — trap that humidity with little ventilation, creating the warm, damp, dark conditions mold thrives in. Tight rental turnover means water losses sometimes go unreported between tenancies, giving mold weeks or months of undisturbed growth.

## Our Mold Remediation Process in Bayonne

Every job starts with a visual inspection and moisture mapping using thermal imaging and calibrated moisture meters — not a guess based on what's visible. In Bayonne's older frame homes, that matters: mold frequently hides inside plaster-and-lath assemblies or beneath original hardwood floors where surface appearance gives no indication of what's underneath.

Once we've mapped the affected area, we establish negative-air containment to prevent spores from migrating to unaffected rooms. This step is especially important in multi-family homes, where an outbreak in one unit can spread through shared wall cavities to a neighboring apartment. Affected materials — drywall, insulation, subfloor sections — are removed, bagged, and disposed of per EPA guidelines. We then treat structural surfaces with EPA-registered antimicrobial agents and run HEPA-filtered air scrubbers until air quality readings confirm the space is clear. The process follows the IICRC S520 standard for mold remediation; The Restoration Group holds IICRC Certified Firm status (#210213).

## Local Note: What Bayonne's Plaster Walls Mean for Containment

In Bayonne's pre-1950 housing stock, original plaster-and-lath walls behave differently from modern drywall when they've been exposed to moisture. Plaster is denser and releases absorbed water more slowly, which means the drying phase after mold removal takes longer and requires more aggressive dehumidification than a comparable job in newer construction. It also means mold sometimes penetrates the plaster face without visibly discoloring it — a thermal camera pass during the initial inspection catches what the eye misses. We factor this into scope estimates upfront so there are no surprises mid-job when additional material removal becomes necessary.
