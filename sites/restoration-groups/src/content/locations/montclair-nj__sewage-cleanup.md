## Why Montclair Properties See Sewage Backup Issues

Montclair's housing stock is part of the story. The town is dense with pre-1950 construction — Tudors, Victorians, and center-hall colonials whose original cast-iron drain lines are now 70 to 100 years old. Cast iron corrodes from the inside out; root intrusion from the mature trees that line streets in neighborhoods like Frog Hollow and the Estate Section accelerates the process. When a root mass finally blocks a main line, sewage has nowhere to go but back up through floor drains and toilets on the lowest level.

The geography compounds the risk. Montclair sits on the eastern slope of the Watchung ridge, and heavy rain pushes enormous volumes of runoff downhill fast. When the municipal combined sewer system surcharges — as it did repeatedly during Ida in 2021 — homes in low-lying areas near the South End and along Third River (Yantacaw) tributaries experience sewer backups even when their own plumbing is in perfect condition. That's a municipal surcharge event, not a private-line failure, and it changes how the loss is documented for insurance purposes.

## Our Sewage Cleanup and Sanitization Process in Montclair

Sewage is classified as Category 3 water — the most contaminated classification under the IICRC S500 standard — which means every affected material is treated as a potential biohazard until proven otherwise. The process moves in deliberate stages:

**Containment and safety assessment.** The crew isolates the affected area, establishes negative air pressure where needed, and identifies all materials in contact with sewage — flooring, wall cavities, insulation, and HVAC components if the system pulled contaminated air.

**Extraction and removal.** Standing sewage is extracted with truck-mounted or portable units. Porous materials that cannot be adequately disinfected — saturated drywall, carpet, insulation — are removed and bagged for disposal per NJ Department of Environmental Protection guidelines. In Montclair's older homes, this often means removing original hardwood flooring sections or plaster-and-lath wall sections that absorbed sewage.

**Disinfection and antimicrobial treatment.** All hard surfaces, framing, and subflooring are treated with EPA-registered disinfectants. Sewage carries bacteria, viruses, and parasites; surface cleaning alone is not sufficient. We document each treatment step with photos for the insurance claim file.

**Drying and dehumidification.** Structural drying follows IICRC protocols using calibrated desiccant dehumidifiers and air movers. Montclair's plaster walls — common in pre-war homes — hold moisture longer than modern drywall, so drying timelines are extended and monitored with daily moisture mapping.

**Post-remediation verification.** Before reconstruction begins, moisture readings are confirmed at target levels across all structural assemblies. We provide a written clearance report.
