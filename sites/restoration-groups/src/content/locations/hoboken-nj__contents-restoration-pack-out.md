## Why Hoboken Properties See Disproportionate Contents Losses

Hoboken's geography explains a lot. The city sits just above sea level on land that was marshland before it was urbanized, and its combined stormwater and sanitary sewer system — largely laid in the 19th and early 20th centuries — struggles under heavy rainfall. Superstorm Sandy in 2012 submerged much of the city. Hurricane Ida in 2021 overwhelmed the same system again, pushing sewage-laden water into basements and garden apartments from Southwest Hoboken up through Uptown. That pattern repeats on a smaller scale every time a significant storm stalls over the Hudson Valley.

The housing stock compounds the problem. The dense rows of 19th-century brick brownstones that define neighborhoods from Downtown Hoboken to Castle Point were built with plaster walls, original hardwood floors, and shared party-wall plumbing stacks. A burst pipe in one unit can migrate through shared walls and floor assemblies before anyone notices. Waterfront high-rises near Pier A Park and Hoboken Terminal introduce a different set of risks — commercial-grade sprinkler activations, roof-drain failures, and the kind of large-floor-plate water events that can displace contents from dozens of units simultaneously. In all of these scenarios, the contents inside the property are often the most financially and emotionally significant part of the loss.

## Our Contents Restoration & Pack-Out Process

The first step is a thorough inventory. Before anything leaves the property, every item is photographed, catalogued by condition, and assigned a unique identifier. That documentation matters for your insurance claim and for ensuring that everything that leaves comes back.

From there, contents are sorted into three categories: items that can be cleaned and restored on-site, items that need to be transported to a controlled facility for specialized treatment, and items that are beyond recovery. Porous materials — upholstered furniture, mattresses, certain textiles — that have been exposed to Category 3 sewage water (the kind that backs up through Hoboken's combined sewer system) are evaluated carefully; restoration is sometimes possible with ozone treatment and antimicrobial processing, but we document the assessment honestly rather than overpromising.

Electronics, documents, artwork, and hard goods travel to a climate-controlled facility where they receive targeted treatment: ultrasonic cleaning for hard surfaces, freeze-drying for water-damaged documents and photographs, and controlled drying chambers for items that cannot tolerate heat. Once the structure is cleared, dried, and cleared for re-occupancy, contents are returned, inventoried again on delivery, and placed back in their original positions where possible.

## Coordinating with Insurance in Hoboken's Multi-Unit Context

Contents claims in Hoboken frequently involve more than one policy. A pipe burst originating in a third-floor unit that damages contents two floors below may involve the upstairs owner's liability coverage, the downstairs owner's or renter's contents coverage, and the building's master policy — all at once. The Washington Street corridor's mixed commercial and residential buildings add another layer, sometimes pulling in a commercial property policy alongside residential contents coverage.

The Restoration Group documents losses in the format most carriers request: line-item inventories with photographs, pre- and post-treatment condition notes, and itemized restoration versus replacement cost breakdowns. We work directly with adjusters and can communicate with multiple carriers on the same loss, which matters when you are dealing with a multi-unit brownstone and three separate policies moving at different speeds.

## Local Note

In Hoboken's older brownstones — the kind that line block after block from Elysian Park down through Midtown — original plaster walls and horsehair-reinforced ceilings absorb and hold moisture in ways that modern drywall does not. During a pack-out, that matters because contents stored against those walls may have absorbed more moisture than they appear to on the surface. We check ambient humidity readings inside closets, against exterior walls, and in below-grade storage areas before finalizing the contents inventory, because an item that looks dry in a plaster-walled closet may still be actively taking on moisture from the surrounding structure.
