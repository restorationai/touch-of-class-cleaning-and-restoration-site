## Why Jersey City Properties Present Unique Biohazard Challenges

Jersey City is not one housing market — it's several layered on top of each other. At Newport and along the Exchange Place waterfront, glass-and-steel residential towers share HVAC systems, elevator banks, and common corridors. A situation on one floor can affect air quality and shared surfaces on adjacent floors within hours, and building management typically needs unit-by-unit documentation before the association's insurer will close a claim.

A few miles inland, the Heights and Bergen-Lafayette are full of attached brownstones and wood-frame rowhouses built between the 1890s and 1920s. These buildings were constructed before modern vapor barriers and subfloor membranes, which means biological contamination can wick into original hardwood subfloors, plaster lath, and unfinished masonry cellars faster than it would in newer construction. Combined-sewer backups — a chronic problem in older sections of the city — can introduce infectious material into basement units without warning.

The result is that no two biohazard jobs in Jersey City look alike, and the approach has to match the building.

## Our Biohazard Remediation Process in Jersey City

Every call begins the same way: a conversation, not a form. We ask enough to understand the situation, the access constraints, and who else needs to be notified — building super, property manager, HOA contact — before a crew arrives. Discretion is built into how we schedule and how we park.

On-site, the process follows a contained, methodical sequence:

- **Containment** — affected areas are isolated to prevent cross-contamination of shared corridors or adjacent units, which matters especially in multifamily buildings.
- **Removal and packaging** — all biohazardous materials, including sharps and needles if present, are collected and packaged for compliant disposal under New Jersey Department of Environmental Protection guidelines for regulated medical waste.
- **Surface treatment** — affected surfaces are treated with EPA-registered disinfectants. In older buildings with porous materials, this step takes longer and may require multiple applications.
- **Verification** — we document conditions before, during, and after remediation with photographs and written logs. That paperwork is what landlords, condo boards, and carriers need to close a claim cleanly.

As an IICRC Certified Firm (#210213) and NJ Licensed Home Improvement Contractor, the work meets the documentation standards most carriers and property managers require.

## Local Note

In Jersey City's older attached rowhouses — particularly in the Heights and parts of Journal Square — original tin ceilings and plaster-over-lath construction are still common. When a situation occurs on an upper floor of one of these buildings, biological material can migrate through gaps in the original subfloor into the ceiling cavity of the unit below in ways that aren't visible from either side. We check below-grade and below-floor spaces as a standard part of assessment in pre-war construction, not as an add-on, because skipping that step in these buildings leads to odor and contamination complaints weeks after a job is otherwise finished.
