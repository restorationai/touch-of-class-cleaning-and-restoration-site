## Our Basement Flooding Cleanup Process in East Brunswick

Every flooded basement job starts with the same first question: where is the water still coming from? We identify and confirm the source before extraction begins — there's no point pulling water out of a space that's still taking it in. Once the source is controlled or confirmed stopped, we deploy truck-mounted and portable extraction units to remove standing water, then transition to the drying phase.

In East Brunswick's finished basements, that drying phase is the most labor-intensive part of the job. Drywall along the lower wall cavity traps moisture behind it; we use calibrated moisture meters and thermal imaging to map exactly how far water has traveled up the wall before deciding how much material needs to come out. Industrial desiccant dehumidifiers and high-velocity air movers run in a calculated pattern — equipment placement matters as much as equipment quantity. We monitor moisture readings daily until structural materials reach the target dry standard per the IICRC S500 framework, then document final readings for your insurance file.

For jobs in multi-unit buildings — particularly the denser condo developments in East Brunswick — we coordinate directly with property managers to assess whether adjacent units need evaluation, which is a step that often gets skipped and leads to mold complaints weeks later.

## East Brunswick Insurance Coordination

Most standard homeowner policies cover sudden and accidental water losses — a burst pipe, a failed sump pump during a storm — but exclude gradual seepage or maintenance-related failures.
