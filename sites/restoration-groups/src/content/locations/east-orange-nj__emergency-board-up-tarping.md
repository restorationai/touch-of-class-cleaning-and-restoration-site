## Our Emergency Board-Up and Tarping Process in East Orange

When a call comes in from a 07017 or 07018 address, the first thing we assess is the type of structure and the nature of the breach. A fire-damaged Victorian in the Presidential Estates area with a compromised front bay window calls for a different approach than a flat-roofed commercial building near East Orange General Hospital with a section of membrane pulled back by wind.

For window and door board-up, we use exterior-grade plywood secured with structural screws — not staples, not interior-grade panels — sized and cut on-site to fit the opening flush. On older brick facades common throughout East Orange, we anchor into the masonry frame rather than the surrounding plaster or lath, which can be brittle in buildings from the 1910s through 1930s. For roof tarping, we use heavy-duty polyethylene tarps ballasted and secured with weighted battens or screwed lumber frames, depending on roof surface and pitch. The goal is a seal that holds through a nor'easter, not just a clear afternoon.

We photograph every opening before and after securing, document materials used, and provide a written scope summary — all formatted to support an insurance claim from the first contact.

## Local Note

One thing that catches property owners off guard in East Orange's older brick buildings: the parapet walls on flat-roofed structures from the 1920s and 1930s are often unreinforced and can be partially dislodged during a fire or severe wind event without being visibly obvious from street level.
