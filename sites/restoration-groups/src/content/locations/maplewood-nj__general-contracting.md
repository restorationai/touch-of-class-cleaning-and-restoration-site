Maplewood's housing stock is a renovator's dream and a general contractor's puzzle at the same time. The 1910s–1930s Tudors and colonials that line the streets off Maplewood Village and into the Hilton and Jefferson neighborhoods were built with genuine craftsmanship — original plaster walls, stone foundations, old-growth hardwood floors — but decades of deferred maintenance, post-storm repairs, and the occasional Rahway River flood have left many of them carrying layers of patchwork that need untangling before real renovation work can begin. The Restoration Group brings licensed general contracting to 07040 with the specific experience those homes demand.

## Why Maplewood's Older Homes Require a Different Renovation Approach

A kitchen remodel in a 1925 colonial near the Maplewood Train Station is not the same project as one in a 1990s subdivision. Behind those plaster walls you often find knob-and-tube wiring that has to be addressed before any permitted work closes up. Stone foundations in the Tuscan and Fields areas frequently show efflorescence and minor seepage — cosmetic finishing over an unresolved moisture problem is a mistake that compounds over time. Cast-iron drain stacks, original galvanized supply lines, and undersized electrical panels are routine discoveries once demolition starts.

Then there's the flood history. Tropical Storm Ida in 2021 pushed the East Branch of the Rahway River over its banks and into Maplewood Village and the low-lying streets around Memorial Park, leaving finished basements and first-floor framing saturated. Many of those homes were dried out and patched — but not fully rebuilt to current code. A bathroom remodel or basement renovation in those areas often uncovers framing that was never properly remediated, subflooring that needs replacement, and insulation that lost its R-value years ago.

Understanding that history before the first demo swing is part of what separates a competent general contractor from one who will call you with surprises on day three.

## Our Renovation and General Contracting Process in Maplewood

Every project starts with a thorough walkthrough — not a sales visit, a technical one. We document existing conditions, flag code-compliance items (Essex County and Maplewood Township both require permits for structural, electrical, plumbing, and HVAC work), and build a scope that accounts for what's likely behind the walls rather than just what's visible.

From there, the process moves through four phases:

1. **Pre-construction planning** — permits pulled, subcontractors scheduled, materials specified. We coordinate directly with the Township's building department so inspections don't stall your timeline.
2. **Selective demolition** — careful in older homes where original millwork, tile, and plaster details are worth preserving wherever possible.
3. **Rough-in and systems upgrades** — framing corrections, electrical panel upgrades, plumbing reroutes, and insulation updates happen before anything gets closed up.
4. **Finish work and final inspection** — cabinetry, tile, flooring, trim, and punch-list items completed before the certificate of occupancy is issued.

For post-damage rebuilds — the category that overlaps with our restoration background — we also handle direct insurance billing documentation, providing the line-item estimates and photo packages that carriers require to process structural repair claims.

## Coordinating with Maplewood's Permitting and Insurance Requirements

Maplewood is an Essex County municipality with its own building department, and permit timelines here can run two to four weeks for standard residential work. We submit complete permit packages the first time — incomplete applications are the single biggest cause of project delays in older-home renovations — and we schedule inspections proactively so rough-in work isn't sitting idle waiting for a sign-off.

For homeowners whose renovation follows a water, fire, or storm loss, we work directly with adjusters to scope the rebuild accurately. Insurance estimates written by desk adjusters often undercount the labor involved in matching original plaster profiles, sourcing period-appropriate trim, or sistering compromised floor joists in a century-old frame. We provide the supplemental documentation that closes that gap.

## Local Note

One pattern that comes up repeatedly in the blocks between Maplewood Village and the South Mountain Reservation: original plaster on wood-lath ceilings in these Tudors and colonials is significantly heavier than modern drywall, and when a roof leak or upstairs plumbing failure saturates it, the ceiling can fail suddenly rather than gradually. Homeowners sometimes assume a small water stain means a small repair. In practice, we often find that two or three ceiling bays need full replacement once we probe the extent of the damage — and the lath behind the plaster may need to be replaced as well. Budgeting for that possibility upfront, rather than treating it as a change order, is the honest way to scope a renovation in this housing stock.
