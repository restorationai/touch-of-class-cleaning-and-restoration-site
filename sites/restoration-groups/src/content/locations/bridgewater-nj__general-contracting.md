## Why Bridgewater's Building Stock Shapes Every Renovation

The township spans roughly 32 square miles and three distinct eras of construction. The older Finderne and Bradley Gardens tracts — many built between the late 1940s and early 1960s — carry the typical challenges of that period: knob-and-tube wiring that hasn't been fully replaced, galvanized supply pipes that restrict flow and corrode from the inside, and plaster-over-lath walls that behave very differently from modern drywall when you're cutting in a new window or adding a bathroom.

The newer colonials and townhome developments closer to the Route 22/287 interchange were built to later codes, but they come with their own complications: HOA architectural guidelines, shared-wall considerations, and in some cases restrictive covenants that govern exterior finishes and window styles. A general contractor who hasn't worked in Somerset County before will miss those details until they're already a problem.

Then there's the flood exposure. The low-lying sections along the Raritan River corridor — particularly the Finderne flats — saw catastrophic inundation during Hurricane Floyd and again during Ida. Homes in those areas that weren't rebuilt to current flood-resilient standards are overdue for work that goes beyond cosmetic: subfloor replacement, moisture-resistant framing, updated electrical panels relocated above base flood elevation.

## Our Renovation and General Contracting Process in Bridgewater

Every project starts with a detailed scope walkthrough — not a ballpark estimate over the phone. For older homes in the 08807 ZIP code, that walkthrough includes a look behind walls where possible, because what's visible on the surface rarely tells the full story. We document existing conditions before any work begins, which matters both for insurance-funded rebuilds and for homeowners who want a clear record of what was done.

From there, we pull the required Somerset County and Bridgewater Township permits. Permit timelines here run roughly two to four weeks for standard residential work; we build that into the project schedule rather than treating it as a surprise. Inspections are coordinated with the township's building department, and we don't skip rough-in inspections to save a day — that shortcut creates title problems when the home sells.

For kitchen and bathroom remodels, we handle the full scope: demo, rough plumbing and electrical, tile, cabinetry, fixtures, and final finishes. For post-damage rebuilds — the category of work we do most frequently in flood-affected neighborhoods — we coordinate directly with the homeowner's insurance adjuster, document every line item, and rebuild to current code even when the original structure was grandfathered under older standards.

## Local Note: What Bridgewater Contractors Often Miss in Older Split-Levels

The split-level homes common in Green Knoll and Martinsville were designed with a structural quirk that catches contractors off guard: the intermediate floor framing is often load-bearing in ways that aren't obvious from a visual inspection. Homeowners in these neighborhoods frequently want to open up the kitchen or create a more connected living space, which requires removing or modifying walls. In a standard ranch, that's a straightforward beam-and-post calculation. In a Bridgewater split-level, it often means engaging a structural engineer before a single stud comes down — and the engineer's letter needs to be in the permit file. We include that step in our scope for any open-concept work in these homes rather than treating it as an add-on.
