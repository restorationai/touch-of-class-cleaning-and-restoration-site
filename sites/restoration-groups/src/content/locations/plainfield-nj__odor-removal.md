## Why Plainfield Homes Hold Odors Longer Than Most

Building age is the single biggest factor. The majority of residential properties in ZIP codes 07060 and 07062 were built before 1960, and a significant share predate World War II. That means original plaster and lath, hardwood subfloors with decades of finish layers, and cast-iron drain stacks that corrode from the inside out. Each of those materials is highly porous and traps odor compounds — volatile organic compounds from smoke, hydrogen sulfide from sewer gas, microbial VOCs from mold — deep in the substrate rather than just on the surface.

The Green Brook and Cedar Brook corridors compound the problem. Brook-adjacent streets in the Sleepy Hollow and Crescent Area neighborhoods flooded severely during Hurricane Ida in 2021, leaving standing water in finished basements for days. Water that sits that long doesn't just damage structure — it seeds mold colonies within 24 to 48 hours, and the musty odor from that mold can persist for months if the source isn't fully addressed before deodorization begins. Treating the smell without treating the biology underneath is a temporary fix that fails.

Commercial properties along the Front Street and Park Avenue corridors face a different pattern: sprinkler activations and roof leaks that soak drop-ceiling tiles and carpet, leaving a damp, stale odor that drives customers away. The density of mixed-use buildings in that corridor means odors migrate between units through shared HVAC chases and open ceiling plenums.

## Our Deodorization Process, Calibrated for Plainfield

Odor removal is not a single-step service. The process we follow is structured around what the specific odor source is, what materials it has penetrated, and how long it has been present.

**Source removal first.** No deodorization technology works on an active odor source. Before any equipment is deployed, we identify and remove or remediate the source — charred material, wet insulation, contaminated soil, mold-colonized drywall. In older Plainfield homes, that often means pulling up original fir subfloor or opening plaster walls that have absorbed years of moisture.

**Thermal fogging for deep penetration.** Smoke and fire odors in Victorian-era millwork and plaster respond well to thermal fogging, which converts a deodorizing solvent into a dry fog that penetrates the same pores smoke did. This is particularly effective in the ornate woodwork common to homes near the Drake House Museum corridor, where stripping and replacing trim is not always practical or historically appropriate.

**Hydroxyl generation for occupied or sensitive spaces.** Hydroxyl generators produce hydroxyl radicals that break down odor molecules at the chemical level without requiring occupants to vacate. For multi-unit buildings in the Netherwood area or commercial spaces on East Front Street that cannot close for days, hydroxyl treatment allows work to continue while the space remains accessible.

**Ozone treatment for severe cases.** Ozone is the most aggressive option for heavily contaminated spaces — a basement that held sewage backup for 72 hours, or a room with significant smoke char. Because ozone at effective concentrations requires the space to be vacated, we coordinate timing carefully and verify dissipation before re-entry.

**HEPA air scrubbing and post-treatment verification.** We run HEPA-filtered air scrubbers throughout the process and conduct a final walkthrough to confirm odor elimination before we close out the job. We document conditions before and after for insurance purposes.
