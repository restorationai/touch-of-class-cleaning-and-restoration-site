## Our Flood Damage Restoration Process in Garfield

Flood restoration in Garfield's older homes requires a different sequence than a straightforward pipe-break in newer construction. Here's how a typical job moves:

**Rapid assessment and water extraction.** On arrival, the crew identifies all water intrusion points — floor drains, sump pits, wall penetrations — and deploys truck-mounted and portable extractors to pull standing water before it migrates further into wall cavities or under flooring.

**Moisture mapping.** Thermal imaging and calibrated moisture meters map exactly how far water has traveled inside walls and under subfloors. In Garfield's plaster-and-lath homes, this step is critical: plaster holds surface moisture longer than drywall, but the wood lath and framing behind it can be saturated even when the plaster face feels dry.

**Controlled demolition and drying.** Flood-category water (IICRC Category 2 or 3, which is typical for Passaic River backflow events) requires removing affected materials that cannot be dried in place — baseboards, lower wall sections, saturated insulation. Industrial air movers and desiccant or refrigerant dehumidifiers are positioned per the IICRC S500 standard drying protocol and monitored daily until all readings reach dry standard.

**Antimicrobial treatment and documentation.** Flood water from river backflow carries contaminants. Affected structural surfaces receive EPA-registered antimicrobial treatment before any reconstruction begins. Every step is photo-documented for your insurance claim.
