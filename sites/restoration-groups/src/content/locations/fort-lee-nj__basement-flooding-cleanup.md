## Why Fort Lee Properties See Basement Flooding Issues

Fort Lee sits atop the Palisades cliffs, and that geography shapes how water behaves here in ways that aren't obvious until you've worked dozens of jobs in the borough. The steep grade running from the George Washington Bridge approach down toward the older residential streets means stormwater moves fast and concentrates at low points — basements in The Hill neighborhood and along the older blocks of Palisade Avenue collect runoff that flat-grade towns simply don't see. Beyond storm events, Fort Lee's building stock creates its own vulnerabilities. ## Our Basement Flooding Cleanup Process in Fort Lee

The first step on any flooded basement job is stopping the source — something that sounds obvious but gets skipped when crews rush straight to extraction. Once the water is no longer flowing, we use truck-mounted and portable extractors to pull standing water from the floor. In Fort Lee's older homes, where basement floors are often unfinished concrete with a porous surface, that extraction phase takes longer than it does on sealed slabs because water wicks into the substrate.

After bulk water removal, we deploy industrial desiccant and refrigerant dehumidifiers alongside high-velocity air movers, calibrated to the actual cubic footage of the space. Moisture readings are taken at the concrete, at the base of any framed walls, and at the sill plate — the sill plate in particular is a failure point in older Fort Lee homes where the wood has been cycling wet and dry for generations. Affected drywall, insulation, and framing that cannot be dried in place are documented, removed, and inventoried for your insurance claim. As an IICRC Certified Firm (#210213), our drying protocols follow the S500 standard, and we monitor daily until target moisture levels are reached before any reconstruction begins.
