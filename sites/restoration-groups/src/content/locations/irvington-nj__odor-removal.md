## Why Irvington Properties Face Persistent Odor Problems

The building stock along the Springfield Avenue corridor and through neighborhoods like Grove Park and Chancellor Avenue was constructed when ventilation was an afterthought. Plaster walls, horsehair insulation, and tongue-and-groove subfloors absorb odor molecules the way a sponge absorbs water — and they release them slowly, sometimes for years. Smoke from a kitchen fire doesn't just coat surfaces; it penetrates into the plaster substrate and the framing behind it. Sewer gas from a deteriorating cast-iron stack seeps into finished basement ceilings. Mold colonies established after a slow pipe leak produce microbial volatile organic compounds (MVOCs) that no consumer air freshener can neutralize.

Multifamily ownership adds another layer of complexity. In a three-family rowhouse near Irvington Center, an odor source in one unit can migrate through shared wall cavities and floor assemblies into adjacent units, meaning a single-unit treatment often leaves the problem half-solved.

## Our Odor Removal and Deodorization Process in Irvington

Every job starts with a source assessment — because deodorization without source removal is temporary at best. A technician walks the property, identifies the odor category (smoke, microbial, sewage, protein, chemical), and traces it to its origin before any equipment is staged.

From there, the treatment method is matched to the odor type and the building's construction:

- **Thermal fogging** — a heated solvent-based fog that follows the same pathways smoke traveled, bonding with odor molecules embedded in porous materials. Particularly effective in the plaster-and-lathe construction common in Irvington's prewar homes.
- **Hydroxyl deodorization** — UV-generated hydroxyl radicals that oxidize odor compounds in the air and on surfaces. Safe to run in occupied or partially occupied buildings, which matters in multifamily properties where displacing all tenants isn't practical.
- **Ozone treatment** — high-concentration ozone applied in unoccupied spaces for severe smoke or mold odors. Requires full evacuation and post-treatment airing, but reaches into wall cavities and HVAC ductwork that other methods can't.
- **Encapsulants and sealers** — applied to framing or subfloor after cleaning when the substrate itself has absorbed odor beyond what gas-phase treatment alone can address.

All drying and deodorization work follows IICRC standards. The Restoration Group holds IICRC Certified Firm status (#210213), which means the process documentation your insurance adjuster receives reflects recognized industry protocols.
