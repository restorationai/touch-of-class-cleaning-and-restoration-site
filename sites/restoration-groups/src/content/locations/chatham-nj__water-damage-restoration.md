## Why Chatham Properties See Water Damage Issues

Chatham sits in the Passaic River watershed, and Morris County properties near that corridor have a long, well-documented history of seasonal flooding and high water tables, especially after heavy spring rain or a fast snowmelt. Basements in Chatham's older colonial and farmhouse-style homes were often finished or semi-finished decades after the original build, which means wiring, insulation, and subfloor are sitting closer to grade than current code would allow. Add a failed sump pump or a clogged perimeter drain, and water finds the lowest point in the house fast. Homes on higher ground aren't exempt either: ice-damming on steep roof pitches and aging cast-iron or galvanized supply lines in pre-1960s construction are common sources of interior water intrusion that have nothing to do with the river at all.

## Our Water Damage Restoration Process in Chatham

We start with a moisture map, not a guess. Pinless and pin-type meters read moisture content in flooring, drywall, and framing, and in older Chatham homes we pay close attention to plaster-and-lath walls, which hold water longer than modern drywall and can look dry at the surface while the lath behind them is still saturated. Extraction comes first, using truck-mounted or portable extraction equipment depending on how far we have to run hose from the truck to the affected rooms. Once standing water is gone, we set air movers and dehumidifiers calibrated to the room's cubic footage and material type, and we track drying progress daily against the IICRC S500 standard rather than pulling equipment on a fixed schedule. Basements with finished ceilings or built-in cabinetry sometimes require selective demolition to dry the cavity behind them properly, and we walk homeowners through that decision before any cutting happens.

## Chatham Insurance & HOA Coordination

Most water damage claims in this part of Morris County involve either a homeowner's policy (for a burst pipe or appliance failure) or, less commonly, flood coverage through a separate policy if the loss is tied to river or surface water intrusion, since standard homeowner's policies typically exclude flood. We document the source, the affected materials, and the drying process with photos and moisture logs so the paperwork matches what the adjuster needs, and we bill most major carriers directly. For properties under a homeowners association, we coordinate exterior access and any required notice to the association before equipment or dumpsters go curbside.

## Local note

A lot of Chatham's older housing stock has partially finished basements with a mix of slab-on-grade sections and crawlspace additions tacked on over the years. That transition point is where we most often find hidden moisture: water travels along the top of the slab, hits the crawlspace framing, and wicks upward into sill plates that never get checked unless someone is specifically looking for it. We check that seam on every basement loss in this area regardless of where the visible water stopped.
