## Why Staten Island Properties See Sewage Backup More Often Than You'd Expect

The borough's housing stock tells part of the story. A large share of homes in ZIP codes like 10304 and 10306 were built between the 1940s and 1970s — Cape Cods and split-levels with cast-iron drain stacks that are now 50 to 80 years old. Cast iron corrodes from the inside out, and the first sign of trouble is often a slow drain that suddenly becomes a full backup. In older sections of Port Richmond and New Dorp, clay tile sewer laterals are still common; tree root intrusion into those lines is one of the most frequent causes of sewage overflow we encounter on the North and Mid-Island.

The East Shore adds a separate layer of risk. The flooding that Hurricane Sandy brought to Oakwood Beach and Midland Beach in 2012 — and the inland flash flooding Ida pushed through low-lying South Shore streets in 2021 — overloaded the municipal combined sewer system repeatedly. When storm surge or heavy rain overwhelms capacity, sewage can back-flow through floor drains and basement toilets even in homes with no plumbing defect at all. That's a Category 3 contamination event regardless of how it started, and it requires the same full sanitization protocol as a direct pipe failure.

## Our Sewage Cleanup and Sanitization Process in Staten Island

Every sewage job starts with containment. Technicians establish a work perimeter, don PPE, and assess the source before anything else moves. If the line is still active, we coordinate with the property owner and, when necessary, with NYC DEP to confirm the lateral is cleared before extraction begins.

From there, the process follows a defined sequence:

- **Extraction and removal**: Standing sewage is removed with truck-mounted extraction units. Porous materials — drywall, insulation, carpet, subfloor sections — that absorbed Category 3 water are bagged and removed per NYC sanitation guidelines. There is no drying porous material that has been saturated with raw sewage; it comes out.
- **Antimicrobial treatment**: All affected structural surfaces are treated with EPA-registered disinfectants. In basements with concrete block or poured concrete walls, we apply multiple passes because those materials are more porous than they appear.
- **Drying and dehumidification**: After contaminated materials are removed and surfaces are treated, drying equipment is staged to bring structural moisture levels down to pre-loss readings. In Staten Island's humid summers, ambient moisture slows drying times — we account for that in equipment placement and monitoring schedules.
- **Post-treatment verification**: We document moisture readings and surface conditions before closing out, providing records that support your insurance claim.
