## Why Hillside's Building Stock Makes Reconstruction More Involved

Hillside's housing inventory skews heavily toward 1920s–1950s construction: two-family colonials, postwar capes, and row-style commercial blocks, many of which were built before modern framing standards and current NJ Uniform Construction Code requirements. When fire or water damage exposes the bones of one of these structures, what looks like a straightforward drywall replacement often reveals knob-and-tube wiring, original galvanized supply lines corroding at the joints, or cast-iron drain stacks that have cracked behind the walls. Reconstruction in these properties isn't a simple swap — it's a coordinated sequence of trades working through surprises that newer construction simply doesn't present.

The low-lying ground along the eastern side of town compounds the challenge. After Tropical Storm Ida in 2021, river overflow and sewer backups pushed water into finished basements across the township. Homes that took on two or three feet of water often needed not just new drywall and flooring but full subfloor replacement, treated framing, and in some cases foundation-wall repairs before any finish work could begin. Reconstruction in that context requires a scope-of-work that accounts for what the water touched structurally, not just cosmetically.

## Our Reconstruction Process in Hillside

Every rebuild starts with a documented scope written against the actual damage — not a template estimate. For residential properties, that means a room-by-room structural assessment before a single sheet of drywall is ordered. For commercial losses on the Route 22 corridor or in the Conant Street industrial zone, it means coordinating with property managers, reviewing existing as-built drawings when available, and sequencing trades so a warehouse or retail space loses the least possible operating time.

The typical sequence runs: structural framing and sheathing repair → MEP rough-in (mechanical, electrical, plumbing) → insulation and vapor barrier → drywall hang and finish → flooring and millwork → final inspections and certificate of occupancy. Because The Restoration Group is an NJ Licensed Home Improvement Contractor, we pull the required permits directly through the Township of Hillside's building department and schedule inspections — property owners are not left managing that paperwork on their own.

For fire-damaged properties, reconstruction also involves odor-sealing exposed framing with an encapsulant before closing walls, a step that matters especially in older homes where smoke has penetrated plaster lath and original wood studs more deeply than it would in modern OSB construction.
