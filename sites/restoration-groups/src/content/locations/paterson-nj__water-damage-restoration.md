## Why Paterson Properties Face Elevated Water Damage Risk

Paterson's housing stock is among the oldest in New Jersey. The dense mill-worker rowhomes in Sandy Hill and the brick multi-family walkups throughout Eastside were built for a different era of plumbing — cast-iron drain stacks that have been patching themselves with rust for decades, and supply lines that run through uninsulated exterior walls where winter temperatures routinely push them to the breaking point. Many two- and three-family frame houses still carry remnants of knob-and-tube wiring routed alongside original plumbing, which complicates drying because you cannot simply open wall cavities without an electrical assessment first.

The city's converted silk-era industrial buildings — now apartment complexes scattered through the ZIP codes 07501 and 07522 — introduce a different problem: aging sprinkler and standpipe systems that discharge dozens of gallons per minute when a head activates. A single sprinkler event on an upper floor can saturate multiple units below before the building's shutoff is located. Overcrowded rental stock across South Paterson compounds the issue — unventilated bathrooms and chronically damp basements create the baseline moisture conditions that turn a minor pipe drip into a mold problem before a landlord is even notified.

## Our Water Damage Restoration Process in Paterson

Every job starts with a thorough moisture mapping inspection using thermal imaging and calibrated moisture meters — not a visual walk-through. In Paterson's older construction, water migrates through plaster lath, travels along cast-iron pipe chases, and pools inside rubble-stone foundation walls in ways that a flashlight inspection will miss entirely.

Once we know where the water is, extraction comes first. We deploy truck-mounted and portable extraction units to pull standing water from floors, subfloor assemblies, and crawl spaces. In multi-family buildings — common throughout Eastside and the Totowa Section — we coordinate with building management to access affected units on multiple floors simultaneously, because water does not stop at unit boundaries.

Structural drying follows extraction. We calculate the drying system using IICRC S500 psychrometric principles: the number and placement of commercial desiccant or refrigerant dehumidifiers, air movers, and any necessary negative-air containment are determined by the actual cubic footage and material classes present — not a one-size number. In Paterson's plaster-walled homes, drying timelines run longer than in modern drywall construction; plaster releases absorbed moisture slowly, and rushing the process by pulling equipment too early is how callbacks happen. We monitor daily with moisture readings documented in writing.

Demolition of unsalvageable material — wet insulation, saturated subfloor sections, compromised drywall — is performed only after drying confirms what can and cannot be saved. We photograph and document every removed material for your insurance claim.
