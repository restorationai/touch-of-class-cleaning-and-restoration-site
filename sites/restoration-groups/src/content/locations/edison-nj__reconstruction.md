## Why Edison Properties Face Distinct Reconstruction Challenges

Edison's housing stock is largely postwar — capes, split-levels, and colonials built between the late 1940s and the 1980s, concentrated in neighborhoods like Stelton, North Edison, and Nixon. Many of these homes were finished in stages by original owners: basements converted to living space, garages enclosed, additions tacked on without always pulling permits. When fire or water damage exposes those layers, reconstruction crews often find original cast-iron drain lines, knob-and-tube wiring buried in walls, and framing that predates modern lumber sizing. None of that is a dealbreaker, but it changes the scope estimate and the permit pathway.

On the commercial side, the Route 1 retail corridor and the warehouse and logistics clusters throughout the township generate a different category of loss. Sprinkler discharges in big-box retail spaces, overnight roof failures in office parks near Middlesex County College, and kitchen suppression activations in restaurants along Route 27 all produce large-loss reconstruction needs with tight reopening timelines. The Menlo Park Mall footprint alone represents the kind of multi-tenant commercial exposure where a single water event can cascade across shared structural assemblies.

## Our Reconstruction Process in Edison

Reconstruction begins where mitigation ends — once water has been extracted and dried, or fire-damaged materials have been removed, the rebuild scope gets documented in detail before a single piece of lumber goes up. For Edison jobs, that documentation phase includes a code compliance review against current Middlesex County and Township of Edison building department requirements, because permits are required for structural, electrical, and plumbing work on any insured loss of meaningful size.

The process typically moves through these phases:

**Structural assessment and framing.** Load-bearing elements are inspected and, where compromised, replaced to current span and load requirements. In older split-levels this sometimes means sistering original 2x6 rafters or replacing undersized headers above door and window openings that were never upgraded.

**Mechanical rough-in coordination.** Electrical, plumbing, and HVAC subcontractors are scheduled in sequence so inspections don't bottleneck the drywall phase. On commercial jobs along the Route 1 corridor, we coordinate directly with the tenant's facilities team and the building's mechanical engineer of record when drawings are required.

**Insulation and envelope.** Edison's climate — hot, humid summers and freeze-thaw winters — means vapor management matters. Older homes being rebuilt after water damage are a good opportunity to upgrade to closed-cell spray foam in rim joist cavities and crawlspaces, which reduces the reoccurrence risk from future condensation.

**Finish and close-out.** Drywall, paint, flooring, trim, cabinetry, and fixtures are matched to pre-loss condition or upgraded per the owner's direction. Final inspections are pulled and certificates of occupancy or completion are obtained before the job is closed.

## Insurance and Permit Coordination in Edison

Most reconstruction jobs in Edison are insurance-driven, and the documentation requirements for a Middlesex County building permit and an insurance carrier's scope approval overlap more than most homeowners expect. Both want the same thing: a line-item breakdown of what was damaged, what is being replaced, and what it costs. We prepare that documentation in a format carriers recognize — Xactimate-compatible line items with photo support — which reduces the back-and-forth that delays project start.

For commercial properties, particularly multi-tenant buildings or properties with HOA or condominium association governance, there is often a second approval layer. Association bylaws in some of Edison's larger multifamily complexes require board sign-off on exterior work or shared-wall repairs. We flag those requirements during scoping so they don't surface as surprises at permit submission.

As an NJ Licensed Home Improvement Contractor registered with the NJ Division of Consumer Affairs, all residential reconstruction contracts are structured to meet New Jersey's consumer protection requirements — written contracts, deposit limits, and cancellation rights included.

## Local Note

One pattern that comes up repeatedly in Edison's older split-levels and raised ranches: when fire or water damage exposes the original subfloor, crews often find two or even three generations of flooring stacked on top of each other — vinyl tile over hardwood over the original subfloor decking.
