## Our Water Cleanup Process in East Orange

The first priority on arrival is category and class assessment. Water from a burst supply line behaves differently than a sewer backup or a roof leak that has been seeping for days — the contamination level determines what personal protective equipment the crew wears, what materials can be dried in place, and what has to be removed. In East Orange's older plaster-and-lath construction, this matters especially: plaster can read dry at the surface while the wood lath behind it stays saturated, and a moisture meter that only reads the face of the wall will miss it.

Once the category is confirmed, standing water is extracted using truck-mounted and portable extraction units — portable units are essential in walk-ups where elevator access is limited or nonexistent. Structural drying follows with calibrated drywall dehumidifiers and axial air movers positioned to create directed airflow through wall cavities and floor assemblies. Drying logs are documented daily, both for the building owner's records and for insurance carriers who require proof of drying progression before closing a claim.

In multi-unit buildings, we coordinate with property managers to sequence unit access so drying equipment can run continuously without interruption — a detail that matters when a single water loss has affected three floors and six tenants.
