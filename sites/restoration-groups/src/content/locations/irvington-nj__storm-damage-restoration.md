## Why Irvington Properties Take Storm Damage Differently

Irvington's housing stock was largely built between 1905 and 1940, and that age shows up immediately in a post-storm assessment. Roof decks are often original tongue-and-groove board sheathing rather than plywood — when shingles lift or flashing fails, water wicks laterally across the boards before it ever drips into the living space below, meaning the visible ceiling stain is rarely the full picture. The same era of construction means interior plaster walls and ceilings, which absorb and hold moisture more stubbornly than modern drywall; drying timelines routinely run longer than the industry average for comparable square footage.

Below grade, the situation compounds. Finished and semi-finished basements are common throughout the 07111 ZIP code, and the Elizabeth River watershed that drains through the area turns low-lying blocks into collection points during intense rainfall. Tropical Storm Ida in 2021 drove widespread basement flooding across Essex County, and Irvington was among the hardest-hit communities — finished rec rooms, laundry areas, and in-law suites took on two to four feet of water in a matter of hours. Storm surge and surface flooding in those events is categorically different from a simple pipe burst: it carries sediment, sewage backflow, and exterior contaminants that require a more aggressive remediation protocol before any rebuild work can begin.

## Our Storm Damage Restoration Process in Irvington

The first priority on any storm loss is stopping ongoing intrusion. If a tree has breached the roof or a wall, the structure needs emergency tarping and board-up before any interior work begins — otherwise drying equipment is fighting a live water source. Once the building envelope is stabilized, the process moves to water extraction and moisture mapping. We use thermal imaging and calibrated moisture meters to trace where water has traveled inside wall cavities, under flooring, and into the framing — especially important in Irvington's older rowhouses where water can migrate across an entire floor before showing itself.

Extraction is followed by a structured drying phase using commercial-grade desiccant and refrigerant dehumidifiers and high-velocity air movers, staged to the IICRC S500 standard for water damage drying. Readings are logged daily. Once materials reach target moisture levels, we assess what can be dried in place and what requires removal — storm-contaminated materials, saturated insulation, and compromised structural members are documented photographically before anything is touched, which matters for the insurance claim.

The rebuild phase covers everything from roof deck repair and shingle replacement to interior framing, drywall, plaster patching, and finished flooring. We carry an NJ Licensed Home Improvement Contractor license and coordinate all required township permits through Irvington's building department.

One pattern our crews have seen repeatedly in Irvington's attached rowhouse blocks — particularly near Irvington Park and along the Union Avenue corridor — is that a roof breach on one unit quietly feeds water into the party-wall cavity shared with the adjacent unit.
