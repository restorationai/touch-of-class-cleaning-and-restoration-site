## Our Basement Flooding Cleanup Process in Edison

The work starts with water removal, not paperwork. When we arrive, we deploy truck-mounted and portable extraction units to pull standing water from the basement floor, including from beneath flooring assemblies where water migrates under vinyl plank and tile. We use thermal imaging cameras to locate moisture that has wicked into wall cavities and concrete block cores — water that looks dry on the surface is often still saturating the structure behind it.

Once extraction is complete, we establish a drying system calibrated to the actual conditions in the space: industrial-grade desiccant or refrigerant dehumidifiers, high-velocity air movers positioned to create the airflow patterns the IICRC S500 standard calls for, and daily moisture readings documented with calibrated meters. In Edison's humid summers, ambient outdoor air can actually work against drying if a basement is opened up too early — we keep the space controlled and closed until readings confirm the structure has reached target moisture levels. Affected materials that cannot be dried in place — wet insulation, saturated OSB, compromised drywall — are removed and properly disposed of before reconstruction begins.
