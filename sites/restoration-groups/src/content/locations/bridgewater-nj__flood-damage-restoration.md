## Why Bridgewater Properties See Flood Damage So Often

Bridgewater's geography works against it in a few specific ways. The Raritan River and Middle Brook drain a wide watershed, and the flat sections around Finderne and Bradley Gardens sit at elevations that leave them vulnerable whenever upstream volumes spike. Soil in these older subdivisions is heavily compacted from decades of development, which slows absorption and pushes surface water toward foundations rather than away from them.

The housing stock adds another layer of risk. Many of the postwar ranches and split-levels built between the 1950s and 1970s were finished with cast-iron drain lines and galvanized supply plumbing that has long since corroded. A single failed joint can dump hundreds of gallons into a finished basement before anyone notices. Newer townhome developments and the commercial corridor stretching along Route 22 and Route 202/206 — including office parks near the Route 22/287 interchange — face their own exposure: aging fire-suppression sprinkler heads, flat commercial rooflines that pond water, and overnight losses that go undetected until morning.

## Our Flood Damage Restoration Process in Bridgewater

Every flood loss starts with a scope assessment before any equipment is staged. We document standing water depth, identify the moisture boundary in walls and subfloor assemblies, and photograph everything for your insurance carrier before extraction begins. That documentation step matters especially in Bridgewater's older split-levels, where water migrates from a flooded lower level into wall cavities that connect to upper living spaces — a pattern that's easy to miss if you start pulling equipment in without mapping the loss first.

Extraction comes next: truck-mounted units for standing water, then desiccant and refrigerant dehumidifiers calibrated to the cubic footage of the affected space. Drying is monitored with daily moisture readings logged against the IICRC S500 standard until structural materials reach acceptable levels. We don't close a job based on how the space looks — we close it based on what the meters say.

For finished basements with drywall, we perform flood cuts at the appropriate height to expose wet insulation and framing rather than trapping moisture behind intact walls. For homes with original plaster — still common in some of the older Martinsville and Chimney Rock-area tracts — drying timelines run longer because plaster releases absorbed moisture more slowly than drywall. We adjust equipment placement and dwell time accordingly.

## Bridgewater Insurance Coordination

Flood losses in Bridgewater frequently involve two separate policies: a standard homeowner's policy (which may cover sudden and accidental discharge from a burst pipe) and a separate NFIP or private flood insurance policy (which covers rising water from the Raritan or Middle Brook). We document the loss in a format that satisfies both claim types — moisture mapping, photo logs, drying records, and a scope of work that separates the categories of damage. That distinction matters because adjusters handle the two policy types differently, and a combined documentation package speeds up both claims simultaneously.
