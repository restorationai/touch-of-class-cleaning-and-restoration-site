## Why East Orange Properties See Contents Losses Differently

East Orange's housing inventory is dominated by brick apartment buildings constructed between roughly 1910 and 1940, interspersed with Victorian two-families and postwar infill. That era of construction means galvanized steel supply lines and cast-iron drain stacks that have been corroding quietly for decades. ## Our Contents Restoration & Pack-Out Process in East Orange

The process starts with a room-by-room contents inventory before anything is moved. Every item is photographed, catalogued, and assigned a preliminary salvageability rating. In East Orange's older apartments — where closets are small, hallways are narrow, and stairwells in three-story walk-ups can be tight — the logistics of moving contents out safely without adding to the damage require planning before the first box is lifted.

Once the inventory is complete, salvageable items are packed, labeled, and transported to a climate-controlled facility for cleaning, drying, and deodorization. Smoke-affected textiles go through thermal fogging and ozone treatment. Water-damaged documents and photographs are stabilized and, where possible, restored. Electronics are evaluated by a technician before any power is applied.

Items that can be safely treated on-site — larger furniture, built-ins, area rugs — are handled in place when the structure allows. In a dense 07017 or 07018 apartment building where elevator access may be limited, that on-site option often saves significant time and reduces the risk of transit damage to fragile pieces.

When the structure is cleared and dried, contents are returned, unwrapped, and placed back in their documented positions. The goal is a home that looks and feels like itself again, not a staged approximation.

## Local Note

In East Orange's pre-war brick apartment buildings, particularly in the Ampere and Doddtown sections, interior walls are often plaster over wood lath rather than drywall. When water migrates behind those walls, the plaster face can appear dry within a day or two while the lath and framing behind it stay saturated for much longer. That matters for contents decisions: items stored against those walls — wardrobes, bookshelves, upholstered pieces — may show surface drying while continuing to absorb moisture from the wall behind them. Checking contents in context, not just in isolation, is part of how we avoid returning items to an environment that will re-damage them.
