## Why Piscataway Properties Face Distinct Reconstruction Challenges

Piscataway's building stock spans roughly seven decades, and that range matters when you're rebuilding. The postwar capes and split-levels clustered in neighborhoods like Arbor and Possumtown were constructed before modern vapor barriers, engineered lumber, and energy codes became standard. Many have finished basements framed with dimensional lumber sitting directly on concrete slabs — a configuration that wicks moisture and accelerates rot once a flood event saturates the framing. Older supply lines in these homes are frequently galvanized steel, and cast-iron drain stacks are common; a reconstruction scope that ignores corroded adjacent piping is one that generates a callback six months later.

On the other end of the spectrum, the Society Hill townhome and condo developments operate under HOA governing documents that specify approved materials, exterior finishes, and sometimes require association sign-off before work begins. That layer of coordination — pulling the CC&Rs, confirming approved siding profiles or roofing materials, and scheduling inspections with the property manager — has to happen before the first sheet of drywall goes up, not after.

The Raritan River corridor adds a third variable: repeat flood exposure. Properties along Johnson Park and the River Road flatlands that absorbed water during Ida in 2021 had often already been affected by Floyd and Irene. Repeated saturation cycles degrade OSB sheathing, compromise sill plates, and can cause microbial colonization inside wall cavities that isn't visible from the surface. A reconstruction estimate that doesn't account for hidden structural degradation in a repeatedly flooded home is almost certainly incomplete.

## Our Reconstruction Process in Piscataway

Every rebuild starts with a documented scope — line-item estimates formatted for insurance carriers, with photos, moisture readings, and material specifications attached. ## Insurance and HOA Coordination for Piscataway Rebuilds

Middlesex County flood losses frequently involve NFIP (National Flood Insurance Program) policies layered on top of standard homeowners coverage, and the two carriers don't always agree on scope. We document losses in Xactimate-compatible line items, respond to desk adjuster questions directly, and flag coverage gaps early so property owners aren't surprised by out-of-pocket exposure at the end of the job.

For Society Hill and similar HOA-governed communities, we request the association's approved vendor list and material specifications before finalizing the scope.
