## Why Garfield Properties Hold Odors Longer

Building age is the single biggest factor. Most of the housing stock in Garfield's Belmont and Plauderville neighborhoods was framed before 1950, which means wood subfloors, plaster walls, and minimal vapor barriers throughout. Porous materials like these absorb odor compounds at a molecular level — cigarette smoke, cooking grease, pet dander, mold metabolites, and flood-borne sewage all penetrate deeper than surface cleaning can reach.

Garfield's geography adds another layer. The city sits in a low basin where Saddle River tributaries drain toward the Passaic. Hard summer storms regularly push water into below-grade spaces on the west side of the city, and the aging cast-iron drain systems common in this housing stock can back up under heavy flow. A basement that floods twice in five years — even shallowly — accumulates a biological odor load that simple mopping and fans cannot neutralize. The same applies to the commercial corridor along Passaic Street, where overnight pipe failures in older retail buildings can go undetected for hours, letting water and the odors it carries soak into concrete block and wood framing.

## Our Deodorization Process, Calibrated for Older Garfield Homes

Effective odor removal in a 1920s Garfield two-family is not the same job as deodorizing a 2010 townhouse. Our process starts with a source identification walkthrough — because masking an odor without finding its origin just delays the problem. We use thermal imaging and moisture meters to locate residual wet material inside walls and under floors before any treatment begins.

Once the source is confirmed dry and clean, we select the deodorization method based on the specific odor type and the building's construction:

- **Thermal fogging** disperses a deodorizing solvent as a fine, penetrating mist that follows the same pathways smoke and odor molecules traveled into porous materials — effective for fire and smoke losses in plaster-wall homes.
- **Hydroxyl generation** uses UV-light-driven chemistry to break odor molecules apart at the molecular level without requiring occupants to vacate; useful in multifamily buildings where neighboring units are occupied.
- **Ozone treatment** delivers high-concentration oxidation for severe biological or smoke odors in unoccupied spaces; we seal and time treatments carefully because ozone requires full evacuation and post-treatment airing.
- **Direct application of IICRC-standard encapsulants** on subfloor and framing where flood-borne odors have penetrated wood grain.

All drying and deodorization work follows IICRC S500 and S520 standards. The Restoration Group holds IICRC Certified Firm status (#210213) and is licensed through the NJ Division of Consumer Affairs as a Home Improvement Contractor.
