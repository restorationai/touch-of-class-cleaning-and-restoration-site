## Our Smoke Damage Restoration Process in West Orange

When we arrive, the first priority is a structured scope assessment — not a quick visual walk-through, but a systematic inspection that maps smoke migration through the HVAC system, wall cavities, and every finished space. Smoke follows air pressure, so rooms far from the fire origin often carry heavy odor loads that get missed without thermal imaging and air sampling.

From there, the work moves through a defined sequence:

- **Dry smoke and wet smoke separation.** High-heat fires produce dry, powdery soot that brushes off hard surfaces. Low-oxygen smoldering fires — common in upholstered furniture or electrical fires — leave a wet, oily residue that smears if treated the same way. We identify the smoke type before touching a surface.
- **HEPA containment and negative air pressure.** Containment barriers prevent cross-contamination to unaffected rooms while industrial HEPA air scrubbers run continuously.
- **Surface decontamination.** Walls, ceilings, and structural members are cleaned with chemistry matched to the soot type. For the plaster surfaces common in older West Orange homes, we use alkaline cleaners that lift embedded residue without saturating the substrate.
- **Thermal fogging and hydroxyl treatment.** Odor molecules bond to surfaces at a microscopic level. Thermal fogging replicates the penetration pattern of the original smoke, neutralizing odor at the source. Hydroxyl generators run afterward to address residual odor in porous materials.
- **Duct cleaning and HVAC decontamination.** Smoke that entered the air handler during the fire will recirculate indefinitely if the duct system isn't addressed. We clean and treat the full duct run, not just the registers.

All drying and decontamination work is documented to IICRC Certified Firm standards (#210213), which matters when your insurance adjuster reviews the scope.
