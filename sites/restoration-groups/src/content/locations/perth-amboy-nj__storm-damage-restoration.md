## Why Perth Amboy Properties See Serious Storm Damage

Perth Amboy's geography makes it one of the more storm-exposed communities in Middlesex County. The city sits at the confluence of the Raritan River and Raritan Bay, and that open-water exposure means coastal storms arrive with tidal surge potential that inland towns simply don't face. Sandy's 2012 surge put waterfront blocks underwater and left a generation of homeowners with a healthy fear of what a strong northeast wind can do. That fear is well-founded — the low-lying streets near the Raritan Bay Waterfront and the older rowhouse blocks in Kearny Heights sit in areas where even a moderate nor'easter can push water into basements and crawlspaces.

Beyond the water, the housing stock itself creates vulnerability. Late-1800s and early-1900s frame construction means balloon-framing in many two- and three-family homes — a style where a roof breach can channel water down interior wall cavities all the way to the foundation before anyone sees a stain on the ceiling. Brick rowhomes along the Downtown corridor have parapet walls and built-up roofing systems that degrade over decades; a single storm can open a seam that wasn't visible the week before. The Smith Street commercial corridor adds storefront glass and flat-roof losses to the mix after any significant wind event.

## Our Storm Damage Restoration Process in Perth Amboy

When we arrive on-site, the first priority is stopping active intrusion — tarping compromised roof sections, boarding broken windows, and identifying any structural elements that pose a safety risk before anyone walks the interior. In Perth Amboy's older multifamily buildings, that inspection step matters more than it might in a newer single-family home, because water traveling through a balloon-framed wall cavity can affect units on multiple floors before it becomes visible.

Once the structure is stabilized, we document everything photographically and in writing for your insurance claim — scope of damage, affected materials, moisture readings. We use thermal imaging cameras and calibrated moisture meters to map water migration behind plaster and lath walls, which are common in pre-1950 homes throughout Dunham Heights and the Budapest neighborhood. Plaster releases moisture more slowly than drywall, so drying timelines in these buildings run longer, and we set equipment accordingly rather than pulling it prematurely.

Debris removal, structural drying, and controlled demolition of unsalvageable materials follow in sequence. If tree impact has compromised a roof rafter or a load-bearing wall, we coordinate with structural contractors before closing anything in. Reconstruction — framing, roofing, siding, interior finishes — is handled under our NJ Licensed Home Improvement Contractor license so you're not managing a separate rebuild contractor.
