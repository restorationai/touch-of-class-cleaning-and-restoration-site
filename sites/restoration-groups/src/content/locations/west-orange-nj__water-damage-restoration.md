## Our Water Damage Restoration Process in West Orange

The first priority on every call is stopping the source and getting eyes on the full scope — because water in a finished basement travels further than it looks. We use thermal imaging and moisture meters to map saturation behind walls and under flooring before any equipment is placed, which prevents the common mistake of drying only what's visible.

Once the scope is documented, water extraction begins with truck-mounted and portable units capable of pulling hundreds of gallons from carpet, pad, and subfloor. In homes with hardwood or engineered flooring — frequent in older West Orange colonials and Tudors — we assess whether targeted drying with floor mat systems can save the floor or whether controlled demolition is the faster path to a dry structure. Drying proceeds according to the IICRC S500 standard, with daily moisture readings logged and shared with your adjuster.

For sewage-involved losses, which are common after the kind of sewer backup events West Orange experienced during Ida, affected materials are handled as Category 3 water — contaminated content is removed, surfaces are treated with EPA-registered antimicrobials, and the area is not closed up until clearance readings confirm safe conditions.
