## Why Montclair Commercial Properties Are Especially Vulnerable

Montclair's commercial corridors run through some of the oldest developed blocks in Essex County. The Bloomfield Avenue and Walnut Street strips include buildings that date to the early 1900s — structures with cast-iron drain stacks, galvanized supply lines, and flat or low-slope roofs that were never designed for the rainfall intensities New Jersey now sees regularly. When those systems fail, they fail inside finished spaces: dining rooms, retail floors, server closets, and storage areas with irreplaceable inventory.

The town's topography compounds the risk. Properties at the base of the Watchung ridge — including parts of the South End and the Frog Hollow area — sit at the receiving end of hillside runoff. Hurricane Ida in 2021 made this visible in the worst way, with flash flooding and sewer backups affecting dozens of commercial and residential properties in those lower elevations. Sump systems that handled ordinary storms were overwhelmed in hours. For commercial tenants and property owners, that translated directly into business interruption, damaged equipment, and contaminated inventory.

The Estate Section and Upper Montclair add a different set of challenges. Grand homes converted to professional offices or multi-tenant use often retain original plaster walls, slate roofing, and unlined chimneys — all of which create unusual pathways for water intrusion and smoke migration that standard commercial drywall construction does not.

## Our Commercial Restoration Process in Montclair

Every commercial job starts with a documented scope — not a verbal estimate. We photograph and measure affected areas, identify moisture migration paths using thermal imaging and moisture mapping, and produce a written loss report formatted for your insurance carrier and property manager from day one.

For water losses, extraction and structural drying follow the IICRC S500 standard. Commercial spaces typically require more drying equipment per square foot than residential because of concrete subfloors, drop ceilings, and HVAC systems that can spread moisture laterally. We deploy commercial-grade desiccant or refrigerant dehumidifiers sized to the actual cubic footage of the affected space — not a one-size unit — and monitor readings daily until target moisture levels are reached and documented.

Fire and smoke losses in Montclair's older commercial buildings require attention to materials that residential crews sometimes underestimate: exposed brick, original wood millwork, and plaster surfaces absorb smoke odor differently than modern construction. We use hydroxyl and thermal fogging in combination, not as a substitute for physical cleaning of affected surfaces.

For mold remediation in commercial settings, we follow EPA guidelines and contain affected areas to protect occupied portions of the building. We coordinate with your operations team to schedule work around business hours where the scope allows.
