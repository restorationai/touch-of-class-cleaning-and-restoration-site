## Our Flood Damage Restoration Process in Perth Amboy

Every job starts with a moisture mapping inspection — thermal imaging and pin-type meters across walls, floors, and ceilings — so we're not guessing where the water traveled. In Perth Amboy's older buildings, water routinely migrates through plaster lath, travels along floor joists, and pools inside wall cavities two rooms away from the visible damage. We document everything photographically before any material is moved, which matters when an insurance adjuster is reviewing the scope.

Extraction comes next: truck-mounted and portable units pull standing water from finished and unfinished spaces. After extraction, we set a calibrated drying system — industrial desiccant or refrigerant dehumidifiers paired with directional air movers — sized to the actual cubic footage and material types in the structure. In buildings with plaster walls, drying timelines run longer than in modern drywall construction; we monitor daily with psychrometric readings and don't close out a job until materials reach the IICRC S500 standard for structural drying. For flood losses involving sewage backup or tidal water, we follow EPA-aligned protocols for Category 3 contamination: affected porous materials are removed rather than dried in place, and all contacted surfaces are treated with EPA-registered antimicrobials before reconstruction begins.
