## Why Maplewood Properties Hold Odors Longer

Maplewood's housing stock — predominantly Tudors, colonials, and craftsman bungalows built between roughly 1910 and 1940 — creates odor challenges that newer construction simply doesn't. Original plaster walls are porous in a way that modern drywall is not. Smoke particles, mold volatile organic compounds (VOCs), and sewer gases penetrate deeply into the substrate and bind there. Surface cleaning or a few open windows won't reach them.

Flood losses compound the problem. The East Branch of the Rahway River runs through Memorial Park and directly through Maplewood Village, and it has a documented history of overtopping during major storms — the flooding from Ida in 2021 left standing water in finished basements and street-level commercial spaces across the village and surrounding low-lying streets for days. Water that sits in a finished basement for 48 to 72 hours begins supporting microbial growth, and that biological activity produces the musty, earthy odor that persists long after the water is extracted. Older cast-iron drain lines in these homes are also prone to partial blockages and slow seepage that introduce sewage odors into crawl spaces and utility areas without an obvious visible source.

## Our Odor Removal and Deodorization Process in Maplewood

Effective deodorization isn't a single step — it's a sequence calibrated to the source, the affected materials, and the structure itself.

**Source removal first.** No deodorization technology eliminates an odor that is still actively generating. We locate and remove or treat the source material — charred framing, saturated insulation, contaminated subfloor — before any chemical or equipment treatment begins.

**Thermal fogging** penetrates the same pathways smoke traveled. A petroleum-based or water-based deodorizing fog is heated and introduced into the space; the fine particles follow smoke residue into wall cavities, HVAC ducts, and behind original trim work — the exact places a spray-and-wipe approach misses in these older homes.

**Hydroxyl deodorization** is our preferred ongoing treatment for occupied or partially occupied spaces. Hydroxyl generators produce hydroxyl radicals that break down odor-causing molecules at a chemical level without requiring occupants to vacate. For severe smoke odor removal or post-flood biological odors, we may use **ozone treatment** in unoccupied spaces, which is highly effective but requires a complete evacuation of people, pets, and plants during the treatment window.

**HVAC and ductwork treatment** is a step that gets skipped in a lot of odor jobs — and it's the reason odors return. In Maplewood's older homes, original duct runs often pass through unconditioned spaces and collect years of particulate. We treat accessible duct surfaces and supply registers as part of the deodorization scope.

## Equipment & Methods We Use for Odor Removal

The right tool depends on the odor source and the affected materials:

- **Ozone generators** — highly effective for smoke odor removal in vacant structures; breaks down VOCs at the molecular level
- **Hydroxyl generators** — safe for occupied spaces; slower than ozone but appropriate when full evacuation isn't practical
- **Thermal foggers** — penetrate porous surfaces including original plaster and wood framing
- **Air scrubbers with activated carbon filtration** — capture airborne odor molecules and particulates during and after treatment
- **Moisture meters and thermal imaging** — identify hidden wet material that is the actual odor source before treatment begins

## Local Note

One pattern we see repeatedly in Maplewood's pre-war homes: finished basements with original wood-panel or tongue-and-groove walls. After a flood event — the kind the Rahway River corridor sees during a nor'easter or a tropical remnant — those panels absorb water along the bottom two feet and begin off-gassing a musty odor within days. Homeowners often treat the visible surface and assume the job is done. The actual odor source is usually the backside of the panel and the framing cavity behind it, which never fully dried. Thermal imaging and moisture readings on both faces of the wall are the only way to confirm whether the material needs to come out or can be dried in place — and skipping that step is why odor jobs in these houses sometimes have to be redone.
