## Our Basement Flood Cleanup Process in Englewood

The first thing our technicians do on arrival is identify the water source and category — clean supply-line water behaves very differently from groundwater that has picked up soil contaminants on its way through a foundation wall. That distinction drives every decision that follows.

Once the source is controlled or contained, we extract standing water using truck-mounted and portable extraction units. In Englewood's older finished basements, that often means pulling up saturated carpet and pad immediately, because leaving them in place traps moisture against the subfloor and accelerates structural damage. Drywall that has wicked water above the flood line gets cut and removed to the appropriate height — leaving wet material inside a wall cavity is one of the most common reasons mold colonizes within days of a flood.

After extraction, we place commercial-grade air movers and refrigerant or desiccant dehumidifiers calibrated to the square footage and construction materials in the space. Readings from moisture meters and thermal imaging cameras guide the drying schedule. We document every reading so the file is ready for your insurance adjuster without gaps.

## Englewood Insurance Coordination

Most standard homeowners policies cover sudden and accidental water discharge — a burst pipe, a failed water heater, a washing machine supply line. Groundwater intrusion from a storm event is a different category and typically falls under flood insurance, which is written separately. We photograph and document the loss origin carefully because that distinction matters when your carrier reviews the claim.

We bill most major carriers directly and provide the scope, moisture logs, and photo documentation adjusters need to process a claim. If your policy requires a preferred vendor list, ask your adjuster — we can often work alongside or in coordination with those programs.

## Local Note

Englewood's East Hill homes frequently have plaster-over-lath interior walls that extend into finished basement spaces — a construction method common in Bergen County homes built before the 1950s. Plaster absorbs water more slowly than drywall, which can make a wall surface feel firm and dry to the touch while the lath and framing behind it remain saturated. Standard moisture meters sometimes read the plaster face rather than the substrate. Our technicians use penetrating probes and thermal imaging to check behind the surface before closing out the drying phase — skipping that step is how a basement that "passed" a visual inspection ends up with mold growth six weeks later.
