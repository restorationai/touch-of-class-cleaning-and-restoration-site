## Why North Bergen Properties See Flood Damage Differently

North Bergen's geography sets up a specific flood risk that most suburban towns don't face. The Palisades ridge runs through the eastern half of town, but the western flats — the stretch running from the Transfer Station area down toward the Tonnelle Avenue corridor — sit at low elevation with limited natural drainage. Heavy rain has nowhere easy to go. Aging municipal storm infrastructure, much of it sized for a smaller population, backs up quickly, pushing water through floor drains and sump pits into finished and unfinished basements alike.

The housing stock makes the problem worse. A large share of North Bergen's residential buildings date to the early-to-mid 20th century: brick-and-frame two-families, attached row houses, and walkup apartment buildings with cast-iron drain lines that corrode and crack over decades. When a drain fails during a storm surge, the water doesn't just wet the floor — it wicks into the mortar joints of masonry foundation walls, travels up into wood framing, and saturates the plaster-and-lath assemblies still common in pre-1960 construction. That kind of moisture moves slowly and hides well.

## Our Flood Damage Restoration Process in North Bergen

Flood restoration in an older, densely occupied building requires a different pace than a new-construction suburban house. Our IICRC Certified Firm (#210213) technicians start with moisture mapping — thermal imaging and pin-type meters across walls, floors, and ceilings — before any equipment is placed. In North Bergen's older multi-family stock, that step matters: water travels through shared wall cavities and can affect a neighboring unit without visible surface signs.

Once the scope is documented, we extract standing water using truck-mounted and portable extraction units, then set drying systems calibrated to the actual material assemblies present. Plaster walls, common in homes near the Woodcliff section and throughout the older residential blocks off Bergenline, release moisture more slowly than modern drywall — drying timelines typically run longer, and we adjust equipment placement and monitoring schedules accordingly. We do not pull the equipment early to hit an arbitrary deadline.

Throughout the drying phase, we monitor daily and log psychrometric readings. When materials reach established dry standards, we document final readings and walk the property with the owner or property manager before closing out the job.
