## Our Contents Restoration & Pack-Out Process in Edison

The process begins on-site with a room-by-room inventory. Every affected item is photographed, logged by category and condition, and assigned a disposition — restore, clean-and-return, or document-for-claim. Nothing leaves the property without a signed chain-of-custody record, which matters when you're working with a carrier adjuster later.

From there, contents move to a climate-controlled facility where they're sorted by material type. Textiles go through ozone or thermal treatment to address smoke odor at the molecular level rather than masking it. Hard goods — furniture frames, lamps, artwork, electronics — are cleaned using material-appropriate methods: ultrasonic cleaning for small parts and electronics, dry-ice blasting for intricate woodwork, hand-cleaning for fragile items. Documents and photographs get freeze-drying or air-drying depending on the extent of saturation.

Pack-back is scheduled around the structural restoration timeline so contents return to a dry, remediated space — not a half-finished one. Every item is re-inventoried against the original log before delivery.

## Local Note

Edison split-levels and raised ranches from the 1960s often have a partial lower level that's neither a true basement nor a slab — a half-story with a concrete floor, wood-framed walls, and minimal vapor barrier. When water enters that space, it wicks into wall framing and subfloor material that isn't visible from above, and the contents stored there — often the densest accumulation in the house — sit in contact with wet concrete for hours before the loss is discovered. In those homes, the pack-out has to happen before any meaningful drying can begin, because furniture and boxes sitting on wet concrete are actively pulling moisture upward. We account for that sequencing when we scope the job, rather than treating pack-out as an afterthought to the structural work.
