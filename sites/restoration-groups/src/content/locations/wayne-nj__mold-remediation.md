## Why Wayne Properties See Elevated Mold Risk

The town's geography stacks the deck. It seeps under laminate, behind drywall, and into the fiberglass batts that were standard insulation in 1960s construction.

Beyond the flood zones, Wayne's broader housing stock of 1950s–70s colonials and capes carries its own vulnerabilities. Aging galvanized supply lines and cast-iron drain stacks develop slow leaks inside wall cavities — the kind that go undetected for months. By the time a homeowner in the 07470 ZIP code notices a soft spot in drywall or a dark stain on a ceiling tile, a significant mold colony may already be established behind it. The region's humid summers — humidity regularly climbs above 70% from June through September — accelerate growth on any surface that stayed damp after a leak.

## Our Mold Remediation Process in Wayne

Every job begins with a thorough inspection before a single piece of material is removed. Technicians use moisture meters and thermal imaging cameras to map hidden wet zones — critical in Wayne homes where water from a river event may have traveled laterally through slab cracks or along framing members before pooling somewhere unexpected.

Once the scope is confirmed, the affected area is isolated with negative-air containment barriers. HEPA-filtered air scrubbers run continuously to capture airborne spores and prevent cross-contamination to unaffected rooms. Mold-damaged porous materials — drywall, insulation, subfloor sections — are removed, bagged, and disposed of per New Jersey DEP guidelines. Structural surfaces that can be preserved are treated with EPA-registered antimicrobial agents and dried to verified moisture content before any reconstruction begins.

As an IICRC Certified Firm (#210213), the remediation follows the S520 Standard for Professional Mold Remediation, which governs containment protocols, clearance criteria, and documentation — the same documentation your insurance adjuster will want to see.
