Cast-iron drains crack at joints, allowing slow seepage into the surrounding soil and slab. Neither failure is dramatic until it suddenly is.

## Our Water Damage Restoration Process in Hillside

Every job starts with moisture mapping — thermal imaging and calibrated moisture meters across walls, floors, and ceilings to define the actual wet zone, not just the visible one. In Hillside's older homes, water travels farther than it looks. A burst supply line in a second-floor bathroom can wick through original hardwood subfloor, into the joist bays, and reach the finished basement ceiling before the homeowner realizes the scope.

Once the boundary is established, water extraction comes first: truck-mounted and portable extraction units pull standing water from carpets, hardfloor assemblies, and crawl spaces. For sewer-backup losses — common after heavy rain events in the lower-lying parts of town — the affected materials are handled under Category 3 protocols, meaning contaminated porous materials like carpet and drywall are removed rather than dried in place.

Structural drying follows IICRC S500 standards, using refrigerant and desiccant dehumidifiers paired with high-velocity air movers positioned to create calculated drying chambers. We set equipment, document daily moisture readings, and adjust placement until affected assemblies reach dry standard — typically three to five days for a contained loss in a single-story home, longer for multi-story two-families where moisture has migrated through multiple floor systems. Every reading is logged and provided to your insurance carrier.

One thing that catches out-of-area crews in Hillside's older two-family homes: the original plaster-and-lath wall construction common in pre-1950 buildings along streets near Evergreen Cemetery and through the Winans area holds moisture very differently than modern drywall.
