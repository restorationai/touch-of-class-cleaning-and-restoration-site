## Our Flood Damage Restoration Process in Fair Lawn

Every flood loss is different, but the sequence is consistent and follows IICRC S500 standards for water damage restoration.

**Step 1 — Emergency extraction.** Truck-mounted and portable extractors pull standing water from finished floors, carpet, and subfloor assemblies. In older Radburn homes with original hardwood over plank subfloors, we slow down here — aggressive extraction without controlled drying can cause cupping and buckle the floor before it has a chance to stabilize.

**Step 2 — Moisture mapping.** Thermal imaging cameras and calibrated moisture meters locate water that has migrated behind baseboards, under tile, and into wall cavities. In block-foundation basements common throughout Fair Lawn, moisture often travels laterally through the block cores and appears 8 to 10 feet from the original entry point.

**Step 3 — Controlled demolition (when necessary).** Saturated drywall, insulation, and flooring that cannot be dried in place are removed to the flood cut line. We document everything photographically before demolition — a step that directly supports your insurance claim.

**Step 4 — Structural drying.** Commercial-grade desiccant and refrigerant dehumidifiers combined with high-velocity air movers create a drying chamber. Psychrometric readings are logged daily so we can show the adjuster a documented drying log, not just a verbal estimate.

**Step 5 — Antimicrobial treatment and clearance.** Once materials reach target moisture content, affected surfaces are treated. We do not release a job until readings confirm the structure is dry — not when the equipment looks like it might be done.
