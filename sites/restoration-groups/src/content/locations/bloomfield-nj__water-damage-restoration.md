## Our Water Damage Restoration Process in Bloomfield

Every job begins with a thorough moisture assessment using thermal imaging and calibrated moisture meters — essential in Bloomfield's older homes, where water migrates behind plaster and lathe in ways that aren't visible to the eye. We map the full extent of saturation before a single piece of equipment is placed.

**Water extraction** comes next. Truck-mounted and portable extractors pull standing water from basements, crawl spaces, and finished living areas. For sewer-backup losses — common in the Watsessing and Brookdale areas after heavy rain — we treat the affected zone as a Category 3 (black water) event, which means full containment protocols, appropriate PPE, and EPA-registered antimicrobial application before any drying equipment is set.

**Structural drying** follows the IICRC S500 standard. We use commercial-grade desiccant and refrigerant dehumidifiers alongside high-velocity air movers, positioning equipment based on the psychrometric readings taken at setup. Drying logs are documented daily — a record your insurance adjuster will need and that we maintain as an IICRC Certified Firm (#210213).

Where materials cannot be dried in place — saturated subfloor sheathing, compromised insulation, or plaster that has delaminated — we perform controlled demolition and prepare the structure for reconstruction. ## Local Note

Bloomfield's older plaster-and-lathe interior walls — common throughout the Brookdale and Watsessing neighborhoods — behave differently from modern drywall during a water loss. Plaster is denser and absorbs moisture more slowly, but it also releases that moisture more slowly during the drying phase. In practice, this means drying cycles in pre-war Bloomfield homes often run 30 to 50 percent longer than in a comparable postwar cape or a newer construction. We account for this in our initial drying plan rather than discovering it on day three when readings aren't moving. It also means we're more conservative about calling a structure "dry" — pulling equipment too early in a plaster home is a reliable way to end up with a mold problem two weeks later.
