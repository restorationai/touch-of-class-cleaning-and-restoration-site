## Why Woodbridge Properties See Repeated Storm Damage

Woodbridge Township's geography creates a compounding set of storm vulnerabilities that most inland New Jersey towns don't share. The eastern communities — Sewaren, Port Reading, and Fords — sit at or near tidal elevation, which means a coastal storm doesn't just bring wind and rain; it brings surge. Water arriving from two directions at once, river flooding from the Rahway River corridor and tidal backflow from Arthur Kill, can overwhelm drainage systems that were engineered for a different century.

The housing stock makes recovery harder. The postwar capes and split-levels that line streets in Colonia, Iselin, and Woodbridge Proper were built between the late 1940s and the 1970s, often with finished basements that lack modern waterproofing membranes. When storm runoff enters through window wells or overwhelmed sump pits, it hits carpet, drywall, and wood paneling — materials that absorb quickly and dry slowly. Older cast-iron drain lines, common in this era of construction, can back up under surge pressure, pushing contaminated water into the lowest level of the home rather than away from it.

High-wind events add a separate layer of damage. ## Our Storm Damage Restoration Process in Woodbridge

Every storm job starts the same way: a rapid damage assessment before a single piece of equipment is unloaded. Wind damage and flood damage require different opening moves, and mixing up the sequence — say, running dehumidifiers in a space that still has active roof penetration — wastes time and money. Our IICRC Certified Firm (#210213) technicians document structural breaches, water intrusion points, and any contamination indicators before categorizing the loss.

For wind and tree-strike damage, the first priority is emergency tarping and board-up to stop the weather from continuing to enter. A roof breach left open for 48 hours in a New Jersey summer can produce enough humidity inside the attic to start secondary mold colonization — mold can begin establishing within 24 to 72 hours on wet organic material. Once the structure is secured, we move to interior water extraction, structural drying using commercial-grade desiccant and refrigerant dehumidifiers, and moisture mapping with thermal imaging to find water that has migrated inside wall cavities.

For flood-driven losses, particularly in the tidal zones near ZIP code 07077 (Sewaren) and the river-adjacent streets in Fords (07032), we assess whether the intrusion source is clean stormwater or mixed-source water carrying sewage backflow — a critical distinction that changes the remediation protocol, the required PPE, and what materials can be dried in place versus what must be removed.
