## Our Basement Flooding Cleanup Process in Fair Lawn

Every job starts with a moisture assessment before a single pump runs. We use thermal imaging and calibrated moisture meters to map where water has traveled — under slab, behind finished walls, into subfloor assemblies — because what you can see is rarely the full picture in an older Fair Lawn home.

Once the scope is mapped, the sequence is:

1. **Standing water extraction** using truck-mounted and portable extractors. In finished basements, we remove saturated carpet and pad immediately — they hold water against the subfloor and accelerate structural damage.
2. **Structural drying** with industrial desiccant or refrigerant dehumidifiers and high-velocity air movers, positioned to create directed airflow through wall cavities and under flooring.
3. **Antimicrobial treatment** on affected surfaces. At typical indoor temperatures, mold can begin colonizing wet organic material within 24–48 hours — in a warm Fair Lawn basement in July, that window is on the shorter end.
4. **Moisture verification** before any rebuild begins. We don't close a job until readings confirm materials have returned to acceptable dry standards per the IICRC S500 protocol.

For homes with original plaster or lathe construction — common in the Radburn Historic District — drying timelines run longer than in modern drywall construction. Plaster releases absorbed moisture slowly and unevenly, so we extend monitoring cycles rather than pulling equipment prematurely.
