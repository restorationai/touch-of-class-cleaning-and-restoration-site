## Why Highland Park Properties See Mold Problems

Highland Park's building stock is one of the most telling factors. The borough's 1910s through 1940s colonials, bungalows, and two-family homes — the kind you find throughout Livingston Manor and the Triangle neighborhood — were built with balloon-frame construction and plaster-and-lath walls. That framing style creates uninterrupted vertical cavities from the basement sill plate to the roof line, meaning a slow leak from aging galvanized supply pipes or a failing cast-iron drain can wick moisture upward through wall cavities long before a stain appears on the ceiling. By the time a homeowner notices discoloration or a musty odor in a finished basement, mold growth may already be well established inside the wall assembly.

The Raritan River's proximity compounds the problem. Highland Park sits at or below the 20-foot elevation contour along its southern edge, and the water table in low-lying sections near the river fluctuates significantly after heavy rain. Basement slab moisture intrusion is common even in homes that have never experienced a visible flood, and that chronic dampness — combined with the limited ventilation typical of pre-1950 basements — creates ideal conditions for mold year-round, not just after storm events.

Commercial properties along the Raritan Avenue business district face a different pattern. Restaurants and retail spaces with older sprinkler systems or commercial kitchen plumbing see water losses that saturate concrete subfloors and wall cavities, and the multi-unit rentals near Rutgers University (just across the Landing Lane Bridge) generate their own share of delayed-response water events where mold is already active before a call is placed.

## Our Mold Remediation Process in Highland Park

Every project starts with a thorough inspection — not just the visible surface but the materials behind it. In Highland Park's older homes, that means probing plaster walls with a calibrated moisture meter, checking basement rim joists and sill plates, and using a thermal imaging camera to locate moisture trapped inside wall cavities that look dry on the surface.

Once we've mapped the affected area, we establish negative-air containment using 6-mil poly barriers and HEPA-filtered air scrubbers. This step is especially important in open balloon-frame homes where spores can travel through wall cavities to unaffected rooms. We then remove compromised materials — whether that's plaster, drywall, wood framing, or insulation — following IICRC S520 standard protocols. Affected structural surfaces are treated with an EPA-registered antimicrobial agent, and we run commercial-grade drying equipment until moisture readings return to baseline for the material type.

Before we demobilize, we conduct a post-remediation verification, which includes visual inspection and, when warranted, air sampling to confirm spore counts have returned to normal ambient levels. We can coordinate independent clearance testing with a third-party industrial hygienist if your insurance carrier or a prospective buyer's inspector requires it.
