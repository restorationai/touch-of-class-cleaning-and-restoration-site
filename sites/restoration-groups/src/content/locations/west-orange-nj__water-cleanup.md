## Our Water Cleanup Process in West Orange

Every job starts with a moisture mapping inspection, not a visual sweep. Thermal imaging and calibrated moisture meters tell us what is actually wet behind plaster, under hardwood, and inside wall cavities — surfaces that read dry to the touch can conceal saturated lath or subfloor for days. In older West Orange homes, that step is not optional; it determines where equipment goes and how long drying will take.

From there, the process follows the IICRC S500 standard:

- **Standing water extraction** using truck-mounted and portable units, including tight-access equipment for finished basement stairwells and crawl spaces
- **Controlled demolition** of non-salvageable materials — saturated drywall, insulation, and base trim — documented photographically before removal for insurance purposes
- **Structural drying** with commercial-grade desiccant and refrigerant dehumidifiers and high-velocity air movers, placed according to the moisture map, not by guesswork
- **Daily monitoring** with logged moisture readings so the drying curve is documented and defensible to your carrier
- **Final clearance check** before equipment is removed, confirming materials have reached acceptable moisture content

For sewer backup losses — which are common after heavy rain events in lower-elevation streets — the scope expands to include Category 3 water protocols: appropriate PPE, antimicrobial treatment, and disposal of porous materials that contacted contaminated water.

## Local Note

In West Orange's older Llewellyn Park and surrounding Victorian-era homes, plaster walls behave very differently from modern drywall during a water loss. Plaster is dense and slow to absorb, which can make a wall feel sound at the surface even when the wood lath behind it has been wet for days. That retained moisture feeds mold growth in a space that standard pin meters can miss entirely. We use both penetrating and non-penetrating moisture meters on these walls and extend drying timelines accordingly — what takes two to three days in a postwar ranch may take four to five in a pre-1940 plaster home. Knowing that going in prevents a mold callback weeks later.
