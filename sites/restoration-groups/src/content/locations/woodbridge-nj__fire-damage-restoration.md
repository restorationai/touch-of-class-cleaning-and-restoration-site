## Why Woodbridge's Housing Stock Complicates Fire Damage

Woodbridge Township grew rapidly after World War II, and the bulk of its residential neighborhoods — Woodbridge Proper, Avenel, Iselin — are filled with homes built between the late 1940s and early 1970s. That era of construction carries specific fire-damage challenges that newer builds simply don't have.

Balloon framing, common in homes from that period, creates continuous vertical cavities from the basement sill plate to the roof rafters. When fire or superheated gases enter those cavities, smoke and soot travel upward through the entire wall system — sometimes surfacing in second-floor closets and attic spaces that appear untouched from the outside. Plaster walls, meanwhile, are denser and more absorptive than drywall; they hold smoke compounds longer and require more aggressive odor-neutralization treatment before a space is livable again.

Older knob-and-tube or early aluminum wiring — still present in a meaningful share of pre-1960 homes in the township — also affects the scope of post-fire electrical assessment. Structural fire damage in these homes routinely uncovers wiring that needs evaluation before reconstruction can begin, which is a step we coordinate with licensed electricians as part of the overall restoration scope.

## Our Fire Damage Restoration Process in Woodbridge

Every fire loss is different, but the sequence of work follows a disciplined order — and in Woodbridge's older housing stock, that order matters.

**Emergency stabilization** comes first: board-up, roof tarping if the structure is breached, and a thorough assessment of which areas are structurally safe to enter. For homes near the Rahway River corridor or in low-lying sections of Port Reading and Sewaren, we also check for water intrusion from firefighting efforts, since those areas can have drainage complications that slow water evacuation.

**Soot and smoke removal** is the most labor-intensive phase. Dry soot from fast-burning fires behaves differently than wet, oily soot from smoldering synthetic materials — and Woodbridge homes often contain both, given the mix of original wood framing and decades of renovation using modern materials. We use HEPA-filtered negative air machines, thermal fogging, and hydroxyl generators calibrated to the specific soot type and the home's construction.

**Structural drying and content pack-out** run concurrently when water damage from suppression is present. Contents are inventoried, packed, and transported to our climate-controlled facility for cleaning and storage while the structure dries and reconstruction is planned.

**Reconstruction** is scoped to match the original materials where possible — important in older neighborhoods where matching original trim profiles, plaster finishes, or hardwood flooring species matters to both the homeowner and the insurance adjuster.

Throughout every phase, work is documented per the IICRC S700 standard for fire and smoke damage restoration, and we carry our IICRC Certified Firm credentials (#210213) into every job.
