## Why East Brunswick Homes Are Especially Vulnerable to Smoke Damage

The postwar building stock that defines most of East Brunswick's residential neighborhoods — ranches, colonials, and split-levels built between roughly 1952 and 1978 — shares a few traits that make smoke damage harder to contain and longer to remediate than in newer construction.

First, these homes were built before modern air-sealing standards existed. Wall cavities communicate freely with attic spaces and basement voids, which means combustion byproducts from even a contained kitchen or garage fire can migrate to rooms that appear untouched. A homeowner in the Frost Woods area who had a laundry-room fire told us the master bedroom on the opposite end of the house smelled like smoke for weeks after a previous contractor "cleaned" it — because nobody had addressed the shared wall cavity running the full length of the structure.

Second, many of these homes still have original cast-iron HVAC plenum systems or early-generation forced-air furnaces. Smoke aerosols and soot particles are drawn into the return and re-deposited on every surface the air touches. Skipping duct inspection and cleaning after a fire is one of the most common reasons smoke odor returns months later.

Finally, East Brunswick's humid summers — amplified in low-lying sections near Lawrence Brook — mean residual smoke compounds absorb ambient moisture and off-gas more persistently than they would in a drier climate. Protein smoke from a kitchen fire, which is nearly invisible but intensely odorous, is particularly stubborn in humid conditions.

## Our Smoke Damage Restoration Process in East Brunswick

Every job starts with a structured assessment before any cleaning begins. We identify the fire's origin point, map the smoke migration path through the structure, and test surfaces with a chemical sponge and pH strips to distinguish dry soot from wet smoke residue — the two behave differently and require different chemistry.

For the split-levels and ranches common in neighborhoods like Tanglewood and Farrington, that migration mapping almost always extends to the basement. Finished lower levels in these homes typically share open joist bays with the floor above, and smoke travels down as readily as it travels up when the pressure differential is right.

Once the scope is defined, the process moves through these phases:

- **Structural surface cleaning**: HEPA-vacuuming loose soot before any wet cleaning prevents smearing. Dry chemical sponges lift dry smoke from walls and ceilings without water activation.
- **Odor neutralization**: Thermal fogging and hydroxyl generation reach the same porous surfaces — wood framing, insulation, subfloor — that smoke reached. We do not mask odor with fragrance; we break down the odor compounds chemically.
- **HVAC and duct cleaning**: Soot-contaminated ductwork is cleaned and treated before the system is run again. Running a contaminated system re-distributes smoke residue to every room.
- **Content and pack-out**: Furniture, clothing, and personal items are inventoried, packed, and cleaned off-site when in-home cleaning isn't sufficient.
- **Final air quality verification**: We confirm odor levels have returned to pre-loss baseline before closing the job.

All work is performed to IICRC standards by our certified team (IICRC Certified Firm #210213), and we carry the NJ Licensed Home Improvement Contractor credential required for any structural repair work that follows.
