## Why Clifton Properties See Flood Damage Differently

Clifton's geography puts a significant share of its housing stock in the direct path of recurring flood events. The Passaic River corridor along Route 21 has overrun its banks during major storms — Irene in 2011 and Ida in 2021 both pushed floodwater into homes and industrial lots in the eastern sections of the city. Weasel Brook, which runs through the western neighborhoods before joining the Passaic, creates a secondary flood risk that catches homeowners off guard when heavy rain overwhelms the culvert system.

The housing stock amplifies the problem. Most of Clifton's residential neighborhoods — from the split-levels and capes in Richfield to the brick multi-family buildings in Botany Village — were built between the 1940s and 1960s. Those homes typically have fully finished basements with original cast-iron drain lines and galvanized supply pipes that are at or past their service life. A single pipe failure during a storm event, when the municipal system is already under pressure, can flood 800 square feet of finished living space within minutes. Older floor assemblies with tongue-and-groove subfloor over wood joists trap water in ways that modern OSB construction does not, and that moisture can begin supporting mold colonization within 24 to 48 hours if drying doesn't start promptly.

Along the Route 3 commercial corridor and around Styertowne Shopping Center, the flood picture shifts to commercial sprinkler discharges, roof membrane failures, and overnight losses in retail and warehouse spaces — often discovered when a tenant or property manager arrives in the morning.

## Our Flood Damage Restoration Process in Clifton

Every job starts with a moisture mapping inspection using thermal imaging and calibrated moisture meters. In Clifton's older homes, this step matters more than in newer construction: water migrates differently through plaster-and-lath walls, masonry block foundations, and the poured concrete floors common in postwar basements. We document affected materials with photos and written moisture readings before a single piece of equipment is placed — that documentation becomes the backbone of your insurance claim.

Extraction comes next. For standing water in a finished basement, we use truck-mounted and portable extraction units to pull bulk water before transitioning to the drying phase. Structural drying in Clifton's 1950s and 1960s homes often requires more drying days than a newer build because plaster releases absorbed moisture slowly and masonry block walls hold water deep in the substrate. We calibrate the number of air movers and dehumidifiers to the actual square footage and material types on-site — not a generic formula — and we monitor readings daily until the structure reaches its pre-loss moisture baseline.

Demolition of unsalvageable materials — saturated drywall, buckled hardwood, compromised insulation — is performed selectively. Where original plaster is structurally sound and drying data supports it, we preserve it. Where materials must come out, we document scope for your adjuster before work begins.
