## Why Bloomfield's Older Housing Stock Complicates Smoke Damage

Most of the single- and two-family homes in the 07003 ZIP code were built before modern vapor barriers, sealed wall cavities, or synthetic insulation existed. When smoke travels through a structure, it follows air pathways — and in a pre-war Bloomfield colonial, those pathways run through balloon-framed walls, uninsulated floor joist bays, and plaster that was mixed and applied in multiple coats directly onto wood lath. Each layer is porous.

Soot deposits in these homes tend to be thicker and more chemically complex than in newer construction. Cooking fires and electrical fires — the most common residential calls in dense urban neighborhoods — produce wet, oily smoke that bonds to plaster and painted woodwork differently than the dry smoke from a fast-burning structural fire. That distinction matters because it determines which cleaning chemistry works and which will permanently set the stain.

Homes near the Bloomfield Green Historic District also carry an additional layer of consideration: original millwork, built-in cabinetry, and decorative plaster details are irreplaceable. Aggressive cleaning methods that work fine on drywall can dissolve or abrade historic finishes. Restoration here requires a lighter hand and more time on detailed surfaces.

## Our Smoke Damage Restoration Process in Bloomfield

Every job starts with a structured assessment — not just the room where the fire occurred, but the full air-movement path through the building. We document affected materials with photographs and moisture readings before anything is touched, which creates the paper trail your insurance adjuster will need.

From there, the process moves in a defined sequence:

- **Dry soot removal first.** Vacuuming and dry sponging lift loose carbon particles before any wet cleaning begins. Wetting soot before this step drives it deeper into porous surfaces.
- **Chemical sponge and degreaser application.** Oily smoke residue on plaster, trim, and cabinetry is treated with alkaline cleaners calibrated to the surface. We test in an inconspicuous area on historic finishes before full application.
- **Thermal fogging and hydroxyl treatment.** Odor molecules embedded in wall cavities and subflooring require more than surface cleaning. Thermal fogging penetrates the same pathways the smoke traveled; hydroxyl generators run continuously to oxidize residual odor compounds without requiring occupants to vacate for extended periods.
- **HVAC and ductwork decontamination.** Smoke pulled into a forced-air system recirculates until the ducts are cleaned. In Bloomfield's older homes, many HVAC systems were retrofitted into buildings not designed for them, which means ductwork often runs through unfinished spaces where soot accumulates heavily.
- **Final air quality check and documentation.** Before we close out, we verify odor levels and surface cleanliness, then provide written documentation of completed scope — required by most carriers and useful if you sell the property.

The Restoration Group is an IICRC Certified Firm (#210213) and a licensed NJ Home Improvement Contractor, so the documentation we produce carries weight with adjusters and inspectors.
