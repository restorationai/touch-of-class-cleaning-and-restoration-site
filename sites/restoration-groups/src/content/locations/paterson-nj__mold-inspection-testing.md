## Why Paterson Properties See Elevated Mold Risk

Paterson's housing stock is among the oldest in New Jersey. The dense 19th-century construction that lines streets from South Paterson through the Totowa Section was never designed with today's understanding of indoor air quality in mind. Knob-and-tube wiring remnants mean walls often can't be opened casually; cast-iron drain stacks sweat condensation in humid summers; and unventilated basement bathrooms in two- and three-family frame houses create exactly the dark, damp environment where mold spores settle and multiply.

Beyond the building stock, geography matters. Paterson sits directly on the Passaic River, and the flooding pattern is well-documented — Irene in 2011, Ida in 2021, and smaller events in between. Homes in the 07501 and 07502 ZIP codes that took on water even once carry a higher baseline risk for residual moisture trapped inside structural assemblies. Converted silk-era industrial buildings near the Great Falls National Historical Park add another layer of complexity: sprinkler and standpipe discharges in those apartment conversions can saturate concrete and masonry that takes weeks to dry without intervention, giving mold a long window to establish itself.

Overcrowded rental stock compounds the problem. Tenant water damage — an overflowed tub, a slow supply-line leak behind a vanity — often goes unreported for weeks. By the time a landlord or property manager calls, the mold colony is already well past the surface.

## Our Mold Inspection and Testing Process in Paterson

A thorough mold assessment in a Paterson property isn't a single-step walkthrough. The process is methodical because the buildings demand it.

**Visual inspection first.** A trained inspector surveys the full property — basement to roof line — looking for moisture staining, efflorescence on masonry, soft drywall or plaster, and visible fungal growth. In older Paterson construction, this includes checking inside mechanical chases, around cast-iron plumbing, and along any wall that shares a boundary with a previously flooded space.

**Moisture mapping.** Non-invasive moisture meters and thermal imaging cameras identify elevated readings behind walls and under flooring without unnecessary demolition. This matters especially in plaster-wall construction, where water migrates differently than it does in modern drywall assemblies.

**Air sampling and surface sampling.** Spore trap cassettes capture what's circulating in the indoor air column. Surface swabs or tape-lift samples are collected from suspect areas. All samples are sent to an accredited third-party laboratory — results typically return within 24 to 48 hours and are compared against outdoor baseline samples taken at the same time.

**Written assessment report.** You receive a clear report identifying mold species, spore concentrations, affected areas, and recommended next steps — documentation that holds up with insurance carriers and satisfies NJ landlord-tenant disclosure requirements.
