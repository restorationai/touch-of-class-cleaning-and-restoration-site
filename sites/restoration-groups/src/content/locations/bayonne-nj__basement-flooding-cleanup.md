## Why Bayonne Properties See Basement Flooding So Often

The geology and history of Bayonne work against dry basements. ## Our Basement Flooding Cleanup Process in Bayonne

Every flooded basement job starts with a moisture mapping inspection before any equipment is staged. We use thermal imaging and calibrated moisture meters to trace water migration through concrete block walls, wood subfloors, and any finished framing — because in Bayonne's older homes, water rarely stays where it entered. A backed-up floor drain in Bergen Point can saturate the base plates of a finished wall cavity ten feet away without leaving a visible waterline.

Once the extent of saturation is confirmed, we extract standing water with truck-mounted and portable extraction units, remove wet materials that cannot be effectively dried in place (carpet, pad, compromised drywall), and establish a structured drying system using commercial-grade desiccant or refrigerant dehumidifiers and high-velocity air movers. Drying progress is logged daily against IICRC S500 standard drying goals — not estimated by feel. We don't close out a job until moisture readings in walls, subfloors, and concrete return to acceptable baselines, which in Bayonne's humid coastal climate can take longer than inland jobs at the same time of year.

For properties with finished basements, we document all affected materials with photographs and moisture readings before demolition, which feeds directly into the insurance claim package.

## Local Note

In Bayonne's older rowhouses and two-families — especially those built before 1940 in the blocks running between Midtown and Bergen Point — basement walls are frequently unreinforced concrete block rather than poured concrete. Block walls absorb and hold moisture differently than poured walls: water wicks laterally through the mortar joints and can saturate a large section of wall from a single entry point at the base. Standard drying protocols designed for poured concrete will underestimate drying time in these assemblies. We account for this in our drying calculations and typically extend monitoring cycles on block-wall basements to avoid a false clearance.
