## Why Linden Properties Are Prone to Sewage Backups

Linden's plumbing history is written in cast iron. The capes, colonials, and split-levels that line the streets from the West End toward the Bower neighborhood were plumbed with cast-iron drains that are now 60 to 80 years old. Cast iron corrodes from the inside out — scale buildup narrows the pipe diameter over decades until a single heavy rain event or a root intrusion causes a full blockage and sewage pushes back up through floor drains and toilet bases into finished lower levels.

The geography compounds the problem. The Rahway River and Morses Creek run through the city, and during major storm events — Ida in 2021 being the most vivid recent example — low-lying areas near Tremley Point and the Arthur Kill waterfront can experience combined sewer surcharges where the municipal system simply cannot move volume fast enough. When the city main surcharges, it doesn't matter how healthy your private lateral is: sewage finds its way back into the lowest fixture in the house. Homes with finished basements, which describes most of Linden's residential stock, take the hit directly in their most-used living space.

## Our Sewage Cleanup and Sanitization Process in Linden

Sewage cleanup is not the same as water damage mitigation. Category 3 contamination — raw sewage, septic overflow, sewer line backup — requires a containment-first approach before any drying equipment is staged.

Here is how the process works on a typical Linden residential loss:

1. **Containment and PPE staging** — We establish a contamination perimeter before entering the affected area. Cross-contamination into unaffected rooms is a primary concern in Linden's split-level layouts where the basement opens directly onto a finished family room or laundry area.
2. **Solid waste and standing water extraction** — Truck-mounted extraction removes standing sewage. Portable units reach tight utility rooms and under stair storage common in postwar construction.
3. **Removal of unsalvageable materials** — Porous materials (drywall, carpet, insulation, ceiling tile) that have direct sewage contact are removed and bagged per EPA Category 3 protocols. Subfloor assemblies are evaluated; original tongue-and-groove subfloor found in older Linden homes holds contamination differently than OSB and often requires full removal.
4. **EPA-registered disinfection and sanitization** — Hard surfaces, framing, and concrete are treated with EPA-registered antimicrobial agents. We do not skip this step to save time on a job.
5. **Drying and clearance** — Once sanitization is complete, the space is dried to IICRC S500 standards using commercial dehumidifiers and air movers before any reconstruction begins.

As an IICRC Certified Firm (#210213), our process follows the industry standard for Category 3 water loss — not a modified version of it.

## Local Note: What Linden's Older Basements Tell Us Before We Start

Crew leads working in Linden's postwar housing learn to check for a specific condition before staging equipment: many finished basements from the 1950s and 1960s were built with a thin concrete skim coat poured directly over original clay-tile or cinder-block foundation walls. When sewage backs up and sits even briefly, that skim coat wicks contamination laterally into the block cavities behind it — contamination that a surface wipe-down will not reach.
