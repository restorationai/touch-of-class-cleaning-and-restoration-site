## Our Smoke Damage Restoration Process in Ridgewood

The first step on any smoke loss is a full structural survey before any cleaning begins. Smoke travels with air movement, which means residue is rarely confined to the room where combustion occurred. We map affected zones using thermal imaging and air sampling, then build a scope that reflects what the building actually absorbed — not a standard checklist.

For Ridgewood's plaster-wall homes, dry chemical sponging precedes any wet cleaning. Introducing moisture too early on heavily sooted plaster can drive particles deeper into the substrate. Once surface residue is removed, we apply hydroxyl generators and thermal fogging to neutralize odor compounds embedded in porous materials — wood framing, subfloor, upholstery, and insulation. HVAC systems are cleaned and sealed before the system is run again to prevent redistribution. All work is performed to IICRC S520 and S500 standards by our IICRC Certified Firm team (Firm #210213).

For contents — furniture, textiles, documents, artwork — we inventory and assess on-site, then coordinate pack-out to a controlled facility when items require ultrasonic cleaning or ozone treatment that can't be safely done in an occupied structure.
