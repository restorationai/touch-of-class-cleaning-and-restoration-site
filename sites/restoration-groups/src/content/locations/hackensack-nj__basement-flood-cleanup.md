## Our Basement Flood Cleanup Process in Hackensack

The first step on any flooded basement is measurement, not extraction. Moisture meters and thermal imaging cameras show where water has traveled behind block walls or under slab edges — areas that look dry to the eye but will grow mold within days if left untreated. Standing water is extracted with truck-mounted or portable units depending on access, and then the slower work of structural drying begins.

For Hackensack's older masonry construction, drying timelines run longer than they would in a newer home with poured concrete and modern drywall. Block walls are porous and hold moisture at depth; we position desiccant or refrigerant dehumidifiers and axial air movers to drive evaporation from the wall surface inward, monitoring daily with calibrated instruments until readings reach the IICRC S500 standard for acceptable moisture levels. No work is signed off until the numbers confirm it — not just until the basement feels dry.

If sewage or tidal backflow is involved, the cleanup scope expands. Category 3 water (sewage-contaminated or flood-sourced water) requires removal of all porous materials that contacted it — drywall, insulation, carpet — and antimicrobial treatment of structural surfaces before any drying equipment is placed. We document every affected material and photograph conditions throughout, which matters when an insurance claim is filed.
