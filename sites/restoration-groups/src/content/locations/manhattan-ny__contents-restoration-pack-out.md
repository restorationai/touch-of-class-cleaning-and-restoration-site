## Why Manhattan Properties See Elevated Contents Losses

Manhattan's housing stock spans more than a century of construction, and that range matters when a loss occurs. Pre-war buildings throughout the Upper East Side and Upper West Side were built with cast-iron risers and steam radiator systems that, when fittings fail, can release significant water volume before a resident notices. Modern condo towers in Midtown and Chelsea rely on pressurized plumbing that travels dozens of floors — a single failed washing-machine hose or supply line can affect multiple units in the time it takes to locate the shut-off.

Beyond plumbing, Manhattan's geography creates its own risk profile. The Financial District and Lower East Side sit on low-lying ground that proved vulnerable during Hurricane Sandy's surge, and the remnants of Ida overwhelmed the borough's storm drains from Harlem south through Chelsea, sending water into basements and ground-floor commercial spaces where inventory and equipment were stored. In a city where closets double as storage rooms and square footage is at a premium, contents are rarely out of reach of a water or smoke event.

## Our Contents Restoration & Pack-Out Process in Manhattan

The pack-out process begins with a detailed room-by-room inventory — every item photographed, barcoded, and logged before it leaves the property. In a Manhattan apartment or commercial space, that documentation serves two purposes: it supports your insurance claim and it creates the chain of custody your building's managing agent may require before allowing a moving crew through the lobby.

Once inventoried, contents are transported to a climate-controlled facility where the restoration work happens away from the active loss site. Depending on the material, that work may include ultrasonic cleaning for hard goods, ozone or hydroxyl treatment for smoke and odor, document drying and digitization, textile cleaning, and electronics assessment. Items that cannot be restored are documented for replacement value claims. When the unit is cleared and dried, contents are returned, unwrapped, and repositioned — again coordinated around freight elevator availability and building access windows.

For retail, restaurant, and office spaces — common in ZIP codes like 10001 and 10016 — we schedule pack-out and return work during overnight or off-hours windows to minimize disruption. Every closed day in a Manhattan commercial space carries a real revenue cost, and the timeline matters.
