## Why Clifton Properties Are Prone to Mold Growth

Clifton's housing stock tells a lot of the story. When those pipes fail slowly — a pinhole leak behind a wall, a drain collar that seeps for months — the moisture never fully dries because the wall cavities weren't designed for airflow.

## Our Mold Inspection and Testing Process in Clifton

A mold inspection is not a visual scan. Our IICRC Certified Firm (#210213) technicians begin with a moisture mapping phase — using thermal imaging cameras and calibrated moisture meters to trace elevated readings back to their source, whether that's a failed sump in a Delawanna split-level or a slow roof leak above a Botany Village flat. We document conditions with photographs and moisture readings before anything is disturbed.

From there, we collect air samples and, where surface growth is visible or suspected behind materials, swab or tape-lift samples. Those samples go to an accredited third-party laboratory for spore-count analysis and species identification. The lab report tells you not just whether mold is present, but which species, at what concentration, and how that compares to outdoor baseline levels — context that matters when you're deciding whether remediation is urgent or whether a drying intervention alone may resolve the issue.

For Clifton's older homes with original plaster walls — common in the Athenia and Richfield sections — we adjust our sampling approach. Plaster absorbs and holds moisture differently than modern drywall, which means active growth can exist behind a wall that reads visually clean. Thermal imaging often catches temperature differentials in plaster that standard visual inspection misses entirely.
