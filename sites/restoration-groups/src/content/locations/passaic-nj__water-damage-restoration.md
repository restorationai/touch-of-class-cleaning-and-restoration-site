## Our Water Damage Restoration Process in Passaic

Every job starts with moisture mapping, not assumptions. We use thermal imaging cameras and calibrated moisture meters to trace where water has traveled — inside wall cavities, under flooring, into subfloor assemblies — before a single piece of drying equipment is placed. In Passaic's older brick and plaster construction, water wicks laterally through masonry and plaster lath in ways that don't follow the same path it would in modern drywall. Missing that lateral migration is how jobs get closed out too early and mold shows up six weeks later.

Once the affected area is mapped, we extract standing water using truck-mounted and portable extraction units, then establish a drying system calibrated to the specific materials present. Plaster walls, original hardwood flooring, and old-growth dimensional lumber subfloors all have different equilibrium moisture content targets and different drying timelines than modern materials. We monitor daily with logged moisture readings so the drying phase has a documented endpoint — not a guess.

For sewage-involved losses, which are common in Passaic's aging drain infrastructure, affected materials are treated as Category 3 contamination. That means antimicrobial treatment, controlled demolition of non-salvageable porous materials, and air scrubbing before reconstruction begins.
