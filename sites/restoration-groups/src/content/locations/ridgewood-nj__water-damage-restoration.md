## Our Water Damage Restoration Process in Ridgewood

Every job starts with moisture mapping. Before a single piece of equipment is placed, technicians use thermal imaging and calibrated moisture meters to trace water migration through walls, subfloors, and ceiling assemblies. In Ridgewood's older homes, water rarely stays where it entered — it follows plaster lath, travels along balloon-frame stud bays, and pools inside finished basement soffits that look dry from the outside.

Once the scope is documented, water removal begins with truck-mounted extraction units capable of pulling hundreds of gallons from carpet, hardwood, and concrete in a single pass. Structural drying follows the IICRC S500 standard: desiccant or refrigerant dehumidifiers, high-velocity air movers, and daily moisture readings until affected assemblies reach the target equilibrium moisture content for the material type. We don't pull equipment early because a surface feels dry — we pull it when the numbers confirm it.

For homes with finished basements — common throughout the Willard and Somerville neighborhoods — this often means drilling small weep holes at the base of drywall to release trapped moisture and allow air circulation behind the assembly. The holes are patched before the job closes.
