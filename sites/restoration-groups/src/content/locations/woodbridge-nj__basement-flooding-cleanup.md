The process on-site follows the IICRC S500 standard for water damage restoration, which means every decision is driven by moisture readings, not guesswork.

**Step 1 — Water extraction.** Truck-mounted and portable extractors pull standing water from the basement floor, including water trapped beneath floating laminate or engineered wood panels. In homes with finished basements, we remove baseboards and probe wall cavities before assuming the visible surface is the full extent of the damage.

**Step 2 — Material assessment.** We use thermal imaging cameras and calibrated moisture meters to map how far water has wicked into framing, drywall, and insulation. Fiberglass batt insulation against a wet foundation wall is almost always a removal — it holds water against wood framing and accelerates decay.

**Step 3 — Structural drying.** Industrial desiccant or refrigerant dehumidifiers, high-velocity air movers, and targeted heat drying bring structural moisture levels back to regional equilibrium. We set equipment, log readings daily, and adjust placement until the assembly is dry — not until it looks dry.

**Step 4 — Documentation.** Every moisture reading, photo, and equipment log is formatted for insurance submission. We work directly with most major carriers and can communicate with your adjuster on your behalf.
