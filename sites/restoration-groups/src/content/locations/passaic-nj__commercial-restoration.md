Passaic sits directly on the Passaic River, and the city's topography means that low-lying blocks — particularly along the riverfront and in sections of the Fourth Ward — have flooded catastrophically twice in the last fifteen years, during Hurricane Irene in 2011 and again during Ida in 2021. Those events pushed water into ground-floor retail spaces, restaurant kitchens, and mixed-use building basements that were never designed to drain that volume. But river flooding is only part of the picture.

The city's commercial stock is dominated by early 20th-century brick construction — storefronts along the Main Avenue business district, multi-story walkups with ground-floor retail, and converted industrial spaces with original cast-iron drain lines that corrode, root-intrude, and back up with little warning. Flat or low-slope roofs on these older buildings pond water and develop membrane failures that go undetected until a ceiling collapses. Sprinkler systems in buildings that were retrofitted rather than purpose-built sometimes discharge from a single corroded head and soak an entire floor before anyone is on-site to shut the riser. These are not hypothetical scenarios — they are the calls we take regularly from Passaic property owners and managers.

## Our Commercial Restoration Process in Passaic

Commercial losses in Passaic require a different pace and scope than residential work. When we arrive, the first priority is life-safety and containment: shutting utilities if needed, stabilizing any structural concerns, and establishing a clear perimeter so your employees or tenants can safely access unaffected areas while work proceeds.

For water losses, we deploy truck-mounted extraction and commercial-grade low-grain refrigerant dehumidifiers calibrated to the moisture loads typical in Passaic's older brick and concrete construction. Brick cavity walls hold water longer than modern stud framing — drying protocols are extended accordingly, and we use thermal imaging to confirm moisture has cleared before equipment is removed. For fire and smoke losses, we assess the difference between direct char damage and the secondary smoke residue that travels through HVAC systems and settles on inventory, fixtures, and structural surfaces — a distinction that matters enormously when your adjuster is scoping the claim.

Throughout the job, we maintain a written scope of work, daily moisture logs, and photo documentation formatted for commercial insurance carriers. Our team coordinates directly with your property manager, your tenant contacts, and your adjuster so you are not playing telephone between three parties.

## Local Note

One pattern we see repeatedly in Passaic's older mixed-use buildings — especially along the blocks running off Main Avenue toward St. Mary's General Hospital — is that ground-floor commercial tenants share a basement mechanical room with residential units above. When a water loss hits that basement, it simultaneously affects the commercial tenant's electrical panel and the residential units' heat and hot water. Restoration work has to be sequenced carefully to restore utilities to occupied residential floors before completing commercial drying, which changes the job timeline. Property owners who are not aware of this dynamic sometimes push for a faster commercial close-out and inadvertently leave residential tenants without heat mid-job. We flag this at the initial walkthrough so the schedule is set realistically from the start.
