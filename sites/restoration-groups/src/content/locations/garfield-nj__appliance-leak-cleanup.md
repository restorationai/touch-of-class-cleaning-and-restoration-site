## Why Garfield Homes See Appliance Leaks Differently

Garfield's housing stock is the main factor. Water from a dishwasher overflow or a washing machine flood doesn't just wet the surface; it migrates laterally between layers and can travel several feet from the source before showing up as a stain or soft spot. Finished basements compound the problem. Garfield's postwar capes and two-families commonly converted their basements into living space — drop ceilings, carpet over concrete, drywall partitions. ## Our Appliance Leak Cleanup Process in Garfield

Every job starts with a moisture mapping inspection — not a visual walk-through, but calibrated readings with thermal imaging and non-penetrating moisture meters across the floor, walls, and ceiling in the affected zone. In Garfield's layered subfloor construction, the thermal camera often reveals wet areas that look dry to the eye.

From there, the process follows a defined sequence:

- **Source confirmation and isolation** — We verify the appliance is fully shut off and the supply line is secured before any drying equipment goes in.
- **Standing water extraction** — Truck-mounted or portable extraction units pull free water from hard surfaces and carpet. In finished basements, we remove wet carpet and padding immediately; holding it in place extends drying timelines significantly.
- **Structural drying** — IICRC S500-standard drying with commercial-grade desiccant or refrigerant dehumidifiers and high-velocity air movers. In multi-family buildings, we calculate equipment placement to dry the affected unit without over-pressurizing shared walls into adjacent units.
- **Daily monitoring** — Moisture readings are logged each visit. Drying is not called complete until readings return to baseline for the material type and local ambient conditions.
- **Documentation** — Photo logs, moisture logs, and equipment records are packaged for your insurance carrier.
