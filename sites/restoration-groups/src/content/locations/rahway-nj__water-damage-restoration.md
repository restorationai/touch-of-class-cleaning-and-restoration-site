## Why Rahway Properties See Water Damage So Often

Rahway's housing stock tells the whole story. The blocks closest to Downtown Rahway and the Rahway Train Station corridor are dense with homes built between the 1890s and the 1930s — Victorian-era and early Colonial Revival structures with original cast-iron drain lines, galvanized supply pipes, and plaster-over-lath walls. Cast iron corrodes from the inside out over decades; galvanized steel accumulates mineral scale until flow is restricted and pressure spikes. Neither announces failure before it happens.

Postwar capes and colonials in Hazelwood and out toward the Clark border were built on crawl spaces and slab-on-grade foundations that weren't engineered with today's storm intensity in mind. Most have finished basements — carpeted recreation rooms, drywall partitions, drop ceilings — that become sponges the moment a floor drain backs up or a sump pump loses power during a nor'easter. The Rahway River watershed compounds everything: the river rises quickly during heavy rain events, and the flat topography means water has nowhere to drain except into the lowest point of your property.

Transit-oriented development around the train station and the Main Street business district has also added multi-unit residential buildings and commercial spaces to the mix, where a single sprinkler head activation or a third-floor pipe failure can cascade through multiple units and trigger complex multi-party insurance claims.

## Our Water Damage Restoration Process in Rahway

Every job starts with a moisture inspection — not a visual scan, but thermal imaging and calibrated moisture meters mapped against a floor plan. In Rahway's older homes, water travels. A supply line failure in a second-floor bathroom can saturate a first-floor ceiling, run inside a plaster wall cavity, and pool under a subfloor without leaving an obvious wet patch on the surface. We find the full extent of the moisture before we extract anything.

Water removal comes next. We use truck-mounted and portable extraction units to pull standing water from carpets, hardwood, and concrete, then transition to the drying phase. Structural drying in Rahway's pre-1950 homes requires a calibrated approach: plaster walls release absorbed moisture more slowly than modern drywall, and the dense old-growth framing lumber common in these houses holds water longer than contemporary dimensional lumber. We set drying targets based on the IICRC S500 standard, monitor psychrometric readings daily, and don't pull equipment until the structure reads dry — not just surface-dry.

For finished basements, we document material conditions before any demolition, which matters when you're working with an insurance adjuster on a Category 2 or Category 3 loss. Affected drywall, insulation, and flooring are removed to the flood cut line, treated if needed, and the cavity is dried and verified before reconstruction begins.
