## Why Hillside Properties Flood the Way They Do

Hillside's geography and housing age work against property owners in a few specific ways. The Elizabeth River cuts across the township's eastern edge, and the low-lying ground on that side drains slowly. Tropical remnants and nor'easters that drop two or more inches of rain in a short window — Ida in September 2021 being the clearest recent example — push the river over its banks and force municipal storm sewers to back up simultaneously. Basements that were dry for decades can take on a foot of water in a single evening.

Older galvanized supply lines and cast-iron drain stacks common in pre-1960 construction add a second failure mode that has nothing to do with weather. Galvanized pipe corrodes from the inside out; when a section finally gives way, it often does so at a joint hidden inside a finished wall or below a concrete slab. Cast-iron drains crack under ground movement and root intrusion, and a cracked lateral under a basement floor can quietly introduce groundwater and sewage over months before anyone notices the smell. Homes near Coe's Corner and the Winans neighborhood, where a lot of the postwar cape-cod and colonial stock sits, see this pattern regularly.

## Our Flood Damage Restoration Process in Hillside

Every job starts with a moisture survey before a single piece of equipment is placed. In Hillside's older homes, that means probing behind finished basement walls and under floating subfloors, not just reading surface readings. Water migrates into the cavity between a 1940s concrete block foundation and interior framing quickly; if that cavity isn't identified and addressed, drying logs will look complete while structural moisture climbs.

Once the scope is mapped, we extract standing water with truck-mount or portable extractors sized to the loss, then set a drying system — commercial-grade desiccant or refrigerant dehumidifiers, axial air movers positioned to create a directed airflow pattern — calibrated to the specific materials present. Concrete block, plaster, and older dimensional lumber all release moisture more slowly than modern OSB and drywall. Drying timelines are set accordingly, typically monitored with daily psychrometric readings rather than a fixed number of days.

Demolition of unsalvageable materials — saturated insulation, compromised drywall, buckled flooring — is performed to the minimum necessary to achieve drying goals, which matters in Hillside because many homeowners have invested in finished basements that represent real living space. We photograph and document everything before removal for insurance purposes.

## Local Note

One thing that catches homeowners off guard in Hillside's older two-family and colonial stock: the original basement floor drain often connects directly to the sanitary sewer rather than to a sump pit. During a combined sewer overflow event — exactly what happened in parts of the township during Ida — that drain becomes an entry point rather than an exit. Any loss involving a floor-drain backup requires Category 3 (black water) handling protocols: full PPE, antimicrobial treatment of all affected surfaces, and disposal of porous materials that contacted the water.
