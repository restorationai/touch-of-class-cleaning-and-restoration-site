## Local Note: What Clifton Contractors Know About Postwar Basements

In the split-levels and cape cods that dominate Clifton's residential blocks, finished basements were often converted by homeowners in the 1970s and '80s — not by licensed contractors, and not with permits. That means the framing may be sitting directly on a concrete slab with no pressure-treated bottom plate, the insulation may be fiberglass batts stuffed against a cold concrete wall (a reliable recipe for mold), and the electrical may be running through unprotected Romex stapled to joists. Before any renovation in these spaces, we assess what's there and bring it to current code as part of the project — not as a surprise add-on, but as a line item in the original estimate. It protects the homeowner, it protects the investment, and it's the only way to pass a Clifton building inspection.
