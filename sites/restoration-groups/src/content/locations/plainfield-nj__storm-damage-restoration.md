## Why Plainfield Properties See Severe Storm Damage

Plainfield's housing stock is one of its defining assets and one of its greatest liabilities when severe weather arrives. The Van Wyck Brooks Historic District and surrounding neighborhoods are lined with late-Victorian and Queen Anne homes built between the 1880s and 1920s — structures with original slate and clay-tile roofing, wide overhanging cornices, and wood-framed dormers that were never engineered for the kind of wind loads a fast-moving nor'easter or post-tropical cyclone can generate. When a gust peels back a section of original slate, the water intrusion that follows isn't just a roofing claim — it's a cascading event that soaks through horsehair plaster, wicks into old-growth lumber, and pools in deep, often finished basements that were never designed with modern waterproofing membranes.

The city's topography compounds the problem. Cedar Brook and the Green Brook run through Plainfield's low-lying neighborhoods, and the bowl-shaped terrain around ZIP code 07060 funnels runoff toward residential streets faster than the combined sewer infrastructure can handle it. During heavy rain events, that means basement flooding in areas that feel nowhere near a floodplain — including parts of the Netherwood and Sleepy Hollow neighborhoods where homeowners are often surprised to find standing water in finished lower levels.

On the commercial side, the Front Street and Park Avenue corridors add a different set of claims: flat and low-slope roofs on mixed-use buildings that pool water, aging HVAC curbs that fail under wind pressure, and sprinkler systems that can activate or leak when structural movement occurs during a storm.

## Our Storm Damage Restoration Process in Plainfield

Every storm loss is different, but the sequence matters — and in Plainfield's older housing stock, cutting corners on the early steps creates hidden damage that surfaces months later as mold or structural failure.

**Immediate stabilization** comes first. If a tree has come through a roof or a wall section has been breached, we board, tarp, and secure the structure before any interior work begins. For the Victorian and Craftsman homes common around the Drake House Museum area, this means working carefully around original millwork and masonry that can't simply be replaced with modern materials.

**Water extraction and moisture mapping** follow. We use thermal imaging and calibrated moisture meters to trace water migration through plaster, lathe, and old-growth subfloors — materials that hold moisture differently than modern drywall and OSB. Plaster walls in particular can appear dry on the surface while retaining significant moisture several inches deep, which is why drying timelines in pre-1950 Plainfield homes routinely run longer than industry averages.

**Structural drying** uses commercial-grade desiccant and refrigerant dehumidifiers alongside high-velocity air movers, with drying logs documented to IICRC S500 standards. For insurance purposes, that documentation is the difference between a fully covered claim and a dispute.

**Debris removal and reconstruction** close the job — from tree limb extraction and roof repair to full interior rebuild where water damage has compromised framing, insulation, or finished surfaces.
