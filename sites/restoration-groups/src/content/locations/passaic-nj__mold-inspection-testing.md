## Why Passaic Properties See Elevated Mold Risk

Passaic is one of the densest small cities in New Jersey, and its housing stock reflects more than a century of continuous occupation. Most of the residential buildings in the 07055 ZIP code were constructed before 1950 — brick multi-family walkups, wood-frame two-families, and attached rowhomes that were never designed with modern vapor barriers or mechanical ventilation. Cast-iron drain stacks corrode and weep slowly for years before anyone notices. Knob-and-tube remnants in older walls create gaps in the building envelope that funnel humid air into unconditioned cavities.

Then there is the river. The Passaic River flooding during Hurricane Irene in 2011 and again during Ida in 2021 pushed water into basements and crawlspaces on riverfront blocks and in low-lying pockets throughout the city. Flood water doesn't just drain away — it saturates subfloor sheathing, wicks into wall bottom plates, and raises the relative humidity in enclosed spaces for weeks. Mold can begin colonizing damp organic material in as little as 24 to 48 hours. Properties that were "dried out" after those storms without professional moisture mapping may still harbor hidden colonies years later.

The dense rental market compounds the issue. High tenant turnover means water damage events — an overflowing tub, a slow supply-line drip, a sewer backup from a failing cast-iron lateral — often go unreported until the next tenant moves in and notices a musty odor or visible staining.

## Our Mold Inspection and Testing Process in Passaic

A thorough mold assessment in Passaic requires more than a visual walkthrough. Because so much of the city's housing stock has plaster walls over wood lath — rather than modern drywall — moisture can be trapped deep inside a wall assembly without any visible surface sign. Our process starts with calibrated moisture meters and thermal imaging to map wet zones before a single sample is taken.

From there, we collect air samples from affected rooms and an outdoor baseline, then take surface swab or tape-lift samples from any visible growth. All samples are submitted to an accredited third-party laboratory for spore identification and concentration analysis. The lab report tells you exactly which species are present, at what levels, and how those levels compare to outdoor ambient counts — giving you objective data rather than a technician's opinion.

For multi-unit buildings near the Main Avenue business district or mixed-use storefronts where a roof leak or sprinkler discharge has affected multiple floors, we scope the assessment unit by unit and document findings per address, which is often required when multiple tenants or a commercial insurer is involved.

As an IICRC Certified Firm (#210213) and NJ Licensed Home Improvement Contractor, we carry the credentials that insurance adjusters and property managers typically require before authorizing remediation work.
