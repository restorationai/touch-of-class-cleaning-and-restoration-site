Rahway's housing stock tells the story. ## Our Water Cleanup Process in Rahway

Water cleanup in an older Rahway home requires more than a shop vac and a few fans. The process starts with moisture mapping — using thermal imaging and calibrated moisture meters to find water that has migrated behind plaster walls, under hardwood floors, and into subfloor assemblies. In homes with plaster-over-lath construction, water wicks laterally through the lath before it ever shows on the surface, which means the visible wet area is almost always smaller than the actual affected zone.

Once the scope is confirmed, extraction comes first: truck-mounted and portable extraction units pull standing water and saturated material from flooring, carpet, and pad. Then drying equipment goes in — commercial-grade desiccant and refrigerant dehumidifiers paired with high-velocity air movers, positioned to create a controlled drying envelope calibrated to the affected materials and the ambient conditions. Every job is documented against the IICRC S500 standard for water damage restoration, which matters both for the quality of the work and for the insurance claim that typically follows.

In finished basements — one of the most common loss locations in Rahway's postwar capes and colonials in the 07065 ZIP code — we assess whether drywall, insulation, and flooring can be dried in place or need to be removed to prevent mold colonization behind the wall cavity. That call is made on data, not assumption.

## Local Note

In Rahway's older Victorian and Craftsman homes — particularly on the blocks radiating from the Union County Performing Arts Center toward the river — plaster walls behave very differently from modern drywall during a water loss. Plaster is dense and slow to release moisture, which means a wall can read nearly dry at the surface on day two while the lath behind it is still holding significant moisture content. If drying equipment is pulled too early based on surface readings alone, that trapped moisture becomes the foundation for mold growth inside the wall cavity. The Restoration Group uses deep-wall probes and extended monitoring intervals on these properties rather than relying on surface moisture readings to call a job complete.
