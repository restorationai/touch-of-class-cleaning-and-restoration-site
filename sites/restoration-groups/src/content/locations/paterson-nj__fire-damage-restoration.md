## Why Paterson Properties See Elevated Fire Damage Risk

Paterson's housing stock skews old — much of it built before 1940, when knob-and-tube wiring was standard and fire blocking between wall cavities was rarely installed. In the blocks surrounding Hinchliffe Stadium and through much of South Paterson, you'll find attached rowhomes where a fire in one unit can push superheated gases into adjacent party walls before a neighbor even smells smoke. Converted silk-era industrial buildings — now apartments in the 07501 and 07505 ZIP codes — add another layer of complexity: older sprinkler standpipe systems can discharge hundreds of gallons during a fire event, leaving crews to address both fire and water damage simultaneously.

Overcrowded rental units with deferred maintenance also contribute. Grease fires from overloaded circuits, space heaters running on extension cords, and dryer vents that haven't been cleaned in years are recurring causes we see across Paterson's rental corridors. The result is rarely a contained, single-room loss — it's a building that needs systematic assessment from the roof deck to the basement.

## Our Fire Damage Restoration Process in Paterson

Every job starts with a documented scope before a single piece of debris moves. We photograph and catalog structural damage, map soot migration through wall cavities and HVAC ductwork, and identify salvageable materials — because in a Paterson rowhome, ripping out plaster that can be cleaned costs the homeowner money and adds weeks to the rebuild.

From there, the process moves through four phases:

1. **Emergency board-up and tarping** — securing windows, doors, and roof penetrations against weather and unauthorized entry while the structure is vulnerable.
2. **Controlled demolition** — removing char, compromised framing, and unsalvageable insulation. In balloon-frame walls common to pre-1950 Paterson construction, we open cavities to confirm soot hasn't migrated two or three floors up.
3. **Smoke and odor neutralization** — thermal fogging and hydroxyl generation to break down odor molecules embedded in plaster, brick, and wood substrate. Masking agents don't work long-term; we eliminate the source.
4. **Structural drying and reconstruction** — if sprinkler discharge or firefighting water is present, we run IICRC S500-standard drying protocols before any rebuild begins, preventing mold from establishing in wet framing before the walls close.

## Local Note

Paterson's older plaster-and-lath walls behave differently in a fire loss than modern drywall. Plaster is denser and more alkaline, which means dry soot — the fine, powdery residue from fast-burning fires — can actually be easier to clean off plaster than off porous drywall. But wet smoke residue from slow, smoldering fires (common in overloaded electrical fires) penetrates plaster deeply and requires chemical sponge cleaning followed by an oil-based primer to seal odor before any finish coat. Crews who treat Paterson's older interiors the same way they'd treat a 2005 vinyl-sided colonial will miss the odor on the first pass — and the homeowner will smell it the moment the heat kicks on in October.
