## Why Morristown Properties Hold Odors Longer

The housing stock in and around the Speedwell corridor and the blocks flanking Morristown Green includes homes built as far back as the 1880s. Those structures share a few traits that make odor removal genuinely harder than in newer construction: original horsehair plaster on interior walls, unlined brick chimneys that wick smoke residue, and finished basements with limited airflow sitting on slab or rubble-stone foundations. When the Whippany River's low-lying areas flooded during the remnants of Ida in 2021, dozens of those finished basements took on standing water. The microbial odors that follow a flood event — musty, earthy, sometimes sharp — can persist for months if the affected materials are dried without proper deodorization treatment.

Commercial properties along South Street and the restaurant cluster near Morristown Green face a different set of odor sources: grease-saturated HVAC ductwork, overnight roof-leak losses that soak insulation above drop ceilings, and recurring drain backups in high-volume kitchens. Odors in commercial spaces spread faster because HVAC systems actively distribute them through the building.

## Our Deodorization Process in Morristown

Every job starts with source identification — not equipment deployment. We trace the odor to its origin material before selecting a method, because the wrong treatment on the wrong substrate wastes time and can drive volatile compounds deeper into porous surfaces.

For smoke odor in older Morristown homes, **thermal fogging** is often the right first tool. Thermal fog converts a deodorizing solvent into a fine aerosol that penetrates the same pathways smoke traveled — into wall cavities, behind baseboards, inside plaster voids — and pairs chemically with odor molecules rather than masking them. For spaces that cannot be vacated for thermal fogging (occupied apartments in the downtown mid-rise towers, for example), **hydroxyl generators** run continuously without requiring evacuation and are safe around electronics, fabrics, and occupied adjacent units.

**Ozone treatment** is reserved for unoccupied spaces with severe smoke or biological contamination — it is the most aggressive oxidizing method available and requires proper dwell time followed by full ventilation before re-occupancy. For biological odors from sewage backups or flood losses, we combine enzymatic treatments on affected surfaces with HEPA air scrubbing to capture airborne particulate before sealing the space.

All deodorization work is performed to IICRC standards by our IICRC Certified Firm (#210213) technicians, and we document conditions before and after with moisture readings and air quality notes for your records.
