## Our Burst Pipe Cleanup and Repair Process in Wayne

When we arrive at a Wayne property, the first priority is source control — locating and isolating the broken line before anything else. In older colonials and capes, shutoff valves are often in awkward locations: under basement stairs, behind finished walls, or corroded to the point where they won't fully close. We carry portable valve tools and can work with the homeowner to identify the main shutoff at the meter if interior valves are compromised.

Once water flow is stopped, we move into extraction. Standing water on a finished basement slab pulls into carpet padding, wall framing, and insulation within hours. We use truck-mounted and portable extraction units to remove bulk water, then deploy industrial air movers and desiccant or refrigerant dehumidifiers calibrated to the room's cubic footage and material types. Moisture readings are logged at every session — we dry to IICRC S500 standard, not to a visual estimate.

Structural drying in Wayne's older homes often takes longer than in newer construction because mid-century builders used thicker dimensional lumber, dense plaster-and-lath assemblies, and wood subfloors that hold moisture longer than OSB. We account for this in the drying timeline and don't close out a job until readings confirm equilibrium moisture content across all affected assemblies.

Repair work — replacing the failed pipe section, patching drywall, reinstalling flooring — is coordinated as part of the same scope. As an NJ Licensed Home Improvement Contractor, we can carry the job from emergency response through finished restoration without handoffs to a separate contractor.
