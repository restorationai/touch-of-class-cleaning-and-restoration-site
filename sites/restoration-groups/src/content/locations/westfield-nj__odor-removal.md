## Our Odor Removal and Deodorization Process in Westfield

Every job starts with a source identification walkthrough — because deodorization that skips this step is just masking. We trace odors to their origin: charred framing behind a fireplace surround, a slow drain leak inside a plaster wall, or a sump failure that saturated a finished basement slab. Once the source is confirmed and any underlying damage is addressed, we select the appropriate treatment method.

For smoke odor removal in older Westfield homes, **thermal fogging** is often the right tool. It produces a dry fog with particle sizes small enough to follow the same pathways smoke traveled — into wall cavities, behind built-ins, and through HVAC ductwork — neutralizing odor compounds rather than coating surfaces. For occupied or sensitive environments, **hydroxyl deodorization** uses UV-generated hydroxyl radicals to break down odor molecules without requiring residents to vacate. **Ozone treatment** is reserved for unoccupied spaces where a high-concentration cycle is safe to run; it's highly effective on heavy smoke odor but requires careful post-treatment ventilation before re-occupancy.

All drying and deodorization work is performed to IICRC standards by our IICRC Certified Firm (#210213) technicians. Equipment placement, dwell times, and post-treatment air quality checks are documented throughout.

## Local Note: Plaster Walls and Odor Penetration Depth

One thing that surprises homeowners in older Westfield neighborhoods like Wychwood and The Gardens is how deeply smoke and sewer odors penetrate lime-based plaster compared to modern drywall. Plaster is denser but more chemically reactive — it can actually bond with certain odor compounds, particularly the aldehydes in smoke residue, in a way that gypsum board does not. This means a thermal fogging treatment that would fully clear a 1990s ranch house may need to be followed by a second pass or a hydroxyl cycle in a 1920s Tudor with original plaster throughout.
