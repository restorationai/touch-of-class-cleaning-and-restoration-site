## Why Bloomfield Basements Flood the Way They Do

Bloomfield's housing stock tells the story. The colonials, two-families, and Tudor-style homes built between roughly 1910 and 1940 that line streets throughout Brookdale and North Center were constructed with finished basements as living and utility space — not as water-resistant voids. Original galvanized supply lines in those homes corrode from the inside out; you won't see the failure coming until a pinhole becomes a split. ## Our Basement Flooding Cleanup Process in Bloomfield

The first priority on any flooded basement call is water classification. A sump failure pulling in groundwater is a different job than a sewer backup near Watsessing Park — the latter requires Category 3 (black water) protocols, including full PPE, antimicrobial treatment of all affected surfaces, and disposal of porous materials that can't be safely restored. We assess on arrival and explain what we're dealing with before any work begins.

From there, the process follows a deliberate sequence:

- **Extraction** — truck-mounted and portable extractors pull standing water from the floor, including water trapped under floating laminate or tile.
- **Structural drying** — industrial desiccant dehumidifiers and high-velocity air movers are positioned to dry concrete, framing, subfloor, and wall cavities. Drying is monitored with moisture meters and thermal imaging, not estimated by feel.
- **Demolition if required** — saturated drywall, insulation, and baseboards that can't be dried in place are removed to prevent hidden mold growth. We document everything photographically before removal.
- **Antimicrobial treatment** — applied to all affected surfaces, calibrated to the water category.
- **Clearance monitoring** — we don't close a job until moisture readings return to regional baseline, typically between 10 and 13 days for a finished Bloomfield basement depending on the extent of saturation.

All drying is performed per the IICRC S500 standard for water damage restoration.
