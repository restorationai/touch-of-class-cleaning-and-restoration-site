## Why Clark Properties See Water Cleanup Issues

Clark was largely built out between the late 1940s and the mid-1960s, which means the majority of its housing stock carries aging cast-iron drain lines and original copper supply plumbing. Cast iron corrodes from the inside out — pinhole failures and joint separations are common, and they often go unnoticed until a finished basement ceiling shows a stain or standing water appears on the carpet. Copper supply lines from this era are prone to pinhole leaks at solder joints, especially where water pressure fluctuates.

The geography compounds the risk. Robinson's Branch threads through low ground near Lake Avenue before joining the Rahway River along Clark's northern and western edges. During heavy events — Ida in 2021 being the sharpest recent example — both waterways have exceeded their banks and pushed water backward through municipal sewer connections, sending sewage-contaminated water into basements off Valley Road. That's a different cleanup problem than a burst pipe: it requires Category 3 water protocols, not just extraction and drying.

Sump pumps are the routine failure point. Clark's soil drains slowly after saturation, and a pump that loses power during a summer thunderstorm can let a foot of groundwater into a finished basement within hours.

## Our Water Cleanup Process in Clark

Every call starts with a moisture assessment before any equipment moves. In Clark's older homes, water behind plaster or inside block foundation walls can read dry at the surface while the substrate stays saturated — a standard pin meter reading at the drywall face misses it. We use thermal imaging and penetrating moisture meters to map the actual wet boundary, not just the visible one.

From there, the process follows a structured sequence:

- **Standing water extraction** using truck-mounted and portable units, including water trapped under floating floors and inside wall cavities
- **Material evaluation** — determining what can be dried in place versus what needs to come out (wet insulation, for example, holds moisture too long to dry efficiently and typically gets removed)
- **Drying system placement** — calibrated dehumidifiers and air movers positioned based on the moisture map, not a generic room-count formula
- **Daily monitoring** — moisture readings logged each visit so drying progress is documented, not assumed
- **Final clearance readings** before equipment is pulled

For losses that involve sewage backup — a real pattern in the Valley Road area after heavy rain — we follow Category 3 containment and disposal protocols before any drying work begins.

## Local Note

Clark's split-level homes present a specific drying challenge that doesn't come up as often in towns with slab-on-grade construction. The intermediate floor level — typically a few steps below the main living area, often housing the laundry room, a bathroom, and direct garage access — sits partially below grade. When water enters at that level, it wicks laterally into the framing that connects to the upper floor deck. Drying that assembly requires equipment placed both below and above the wet zone simultaneously, and it takes longer than a straightforward basement flood because the framing is enclosed on multiple sides.
