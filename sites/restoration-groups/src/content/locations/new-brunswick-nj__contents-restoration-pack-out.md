The Fifth Ward and Robeson Village carry blocks of early-1900s rowhouses where cast-iron drain stacks corrode from the inside out — a slow leak behind a kitchen cabinet can go undetected long enough to wick into wood furniture, plaster walls, and stored belongings on lower shelves before anyone notices a water stain. Downtown high-rises and the George Street business corridor introduce a different exposure. The contents in those spaces are dense, mixed in material, and often high in replacement value.

Then there is the Raritan. The river's flood history is long, and Tropical Storm Ida in 2021 pushed water into low-lying riverfront areas near Boyd Park with little warning. Flood-affected contents carry a contamination burden that goes beyond surface moisture — sediment, sewage backflow, and biological material require a more thorough decontamination protocol than a clean-water pipe burst.

## Our Contents Restoration & Pack-Out Process

The process begins with a detailed inventory before anything leaves the property. Every item is photographed, logged, and assigned a condition rating — this documentation matters both for insurance claims and for returning items to the right room when restoration is complete. Fragile and high-value items are wrapped and crated separately from general household goods.

Once contents reach our climate-controlled facility, the work separates by material type. Upholstered furniture and textiles go through ultrasonic or immersion cleaning depending on the soiling level. Hard goods — ceramics, metals, plastics — are cleaned with appropriate solvents and dried under controlled conditions. Electronics are assessed by a qualified technician before any cleaning attempt, because moisture inside a circuit board requires different handling than moisture on a surface.

Deodorization is a step that gets skipped in cheaper pack-out operations, and it shows. Smoke odor from a kitchen fire in a Rutgers Village apartment or mildew smell from a Raritan flood event embeds in porous materials at a molecular level. Ozone treatment and hydroxyl generation address odor at the source rather than masking it.

Final storage is climate-controlled and secured until the structure is ready to receive contents back — a timeline that depends on how long structural drying and repairs take, not an arbitrary schedule.

## Local Note: What Cast-Iron Plumbing Means for Your Contents

In New Brunswick's pre-war multifamily buildings, cast-iron drain lines don't fail all at once — they develop pinhole corrosion that weeps slowly, often inside wall cavities or under subflooring. By the time water reaches a closet floor or a bookshelf, it has typically picked up rust sediment and organic material from inside the pipe. Contents affected by this type of leak carry a gray-water contamination classification even if the source looks like a clean drain. That distinction matters for cleaning protocols: items that appear only lightly soiled may require full decontamination rather than surface cleaning. Knowing to ask about the source — not just the visible damage — is the difference between a restored item and one that fails an odor check six months later.
