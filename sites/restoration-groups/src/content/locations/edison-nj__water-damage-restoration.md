## Our Water Damage Restoration Process in Edison

Every job starts with a thorough moisture assessment — not just the visible wet area, but the full migration path water traveled. In Edison's older split-levels and colonials, water commonly tracks from a second-floor bathroom through the subfloor into a finished basement ceiling before it ever shows on a surface. We use thermal imaging and calibrated moisture meters to map that path before any drying equipment is placed.

Once the scope is documented, water extraction comes first. Standing water is removed with truck-mounted and portable extraction units, followed by targeted drying using commercial-grade desiccant and refrigerant dehumidifiers and high-velocity air movers positioned to the IICRC S500 standard for structural drying. Drying logs are recorded daily — both for your insurance adjuster and to confirm that materials are reaching their target moisture content before any rebuild begins.

Demolition is kept to the minimum necessary. In a finished basement, that often means removing only the lower portion of drywall to allow airflow behind the wall cavity, rather than gutting an entire room. When asbestos-containing materials are a possibility — a real consideration in Edison homes built before 1980 — we pause and coordinate testing before disturbing those assemblies.

## Edison Insurance Coordination

Most water damage claims in Edison run through standard homeowner or commercial property policies, but the cause of loss matters enormously. A burst pipe is typically a covered sudden-and-accidental loss. Groundwater intrusion from a Raritan-area flood event may fall under a separate flood policy — or may not be covered at all without one. We document the cause of loss carefully from the first hour on-site: photographs, moisture readings, and a written scope that gives your adjuster a clear picture of what happened and what it will take to restore the property.
