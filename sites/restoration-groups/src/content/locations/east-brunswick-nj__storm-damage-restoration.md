## Our Storm Damage Restoration Process in East Brunswick

The first priority on any storm call is making the structure safe and dry. That means tarping breached rooflines before additional rain enters, boarding compromised windows and doors, and extracting standing water before it wicks further into framing and insulation. Our crews carry truck-mounted extraction units capable of pulling hundreds of gallons from a finished basement quickly — important in a township where the typical affected space is a fully built-out lower level, not an open concrete slab.

Once the immediate threat is controlled, we conduct a full moisture mapping survey using thermal imaging and calibrated moisture meters. In East Brunswick's older homes, this step matters more than it might in newer construction: plaster ceilings and original wood-framed walls hold moisture in ways that standard drywall does not, and a reading that looks acceptable at the surface can conceal elevated moisture deeper in the assembly. Drying equipment — commercial dehumidifiers and directed-airflow drying systems — is placed based on those readings, not on a generic room-count formula.

Documentation runs parallel to every physical step. We photograph affected materials, record moisture baselines and daily readings, and produce a scope of loss that insurance adjusters can work from directly. For multi-unit properties, we coordinate with property managers and HOA boards to sequence work across affected units without displacing more residents than necessary.
