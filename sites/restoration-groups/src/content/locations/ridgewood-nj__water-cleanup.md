## Why Ridgewood Properties See Water Damage Differently

Ridgewood's architectural character — the thing that makes the 07450 zip code so desirable — is also what makes water losses here more complicated than in newer suburbs. Homes built before World War II often have plaster-and-lath interior walls rather than modern drywall. Plaster is dense and slow to release moisture: a pipe leak behind a plaster wall can read nearly dry at the surface on a moisture meter while the lath and framing behind it remain saturated for days. Standard drying timelines don't apply.

Slate roofs and low-pitch flat sections on historic homes in the Willard and West Side neighborhoods are a recurring source of storm intrusion claims. When ice dams form along those slate edges in a hard Bergen County winter, meltwater backs up under the roofline and works its way into attic framing and top-floor ceilings — often without the homeowner noticing until a stain appears weeks later. Dense mature tree cover across the borough compounds the problem: clogged gutters and debris-blocked drains accelerate runoff directly against foundation walls.

## Our Water Cleanup Process in Ridgewood

Every water cleanup engagement follows the same structured sequence, calibrated to what we actually find in Ridgewood's housing stock.

**Inspection and moisture mapping.** We use thermal imaging cameras and penetrating moisture meters to locate water that has migrated behind plaster, under hardwood floors, and into subfloor assemblies — common travel paths in older colonial construction. We document everything photographically before any material is moved.

**Standing water extraction.** Truck-mounted and portable extraction units remove standing water from basements, crawl spaces, and finished living areas. In deep finished basements — a hallmark of Ridgewood homes — we stage equipment to reach below-grade areas without damaging stairwells or narrow egress points.

**Structural drying.** Desiccant and refrigerant dehumidifiers, high-velocity air movers, and targeted heat drying are positioned based on the moisture map. Drying logs are recorded daily and compared against the IICRC S500 standard for Category and Class of loss. For plaster walls specifically, we extend monitoring cycles because surface readings can normalize before the assembly is actually dry.

**Antimicrobial treatment and clearance.** Once materials reach target moisture levels, affected surfaces are treated and a final moisture check confirms the structure is ready for reconstruction.

## Ridgewood Insurance Coordination

Most homeowner policies in Bergen County cover sudden and accidental water discharge — a burst pipe, a failed appliance supply line, a roof intrusion from a named storm. Flood losses tied to the Ho-Ho-Kus Brook or Saddle River typically fall under a separate NFIP or private flood policy. We document the loss origin clearly so your adjuster can apply the correct coverage from the start, and we provide moisture logs, photo documentation, and scope of work in the format most major carriers expect. We work directly with your insurance company and can communicate with your adjuster throughout the process.

In Ridgewood's older Tudors and colonials — particularly those with deep, multi-room finished basements in the Heights and Old Country Club — the basement slab is often below the municipal sewer invert. That means a backed-up floor drain during a heavy rain event isn't just a nuisance; it's a sewage-category loss (Category 3 under IICRC classification) even if the water looks relatively clean. Homeowners sometimes assume a floor drain backup is straightforward water cleanup and delay calling.
