## Our Sewage Cleanup and Sanitization Process in Garfield

Sewage is classified as Category 3 water — the most contaminated classification — and every step of the process reflects that. Here's how a typical job unfolds in a Garfield home:

**1. Safe entry and containment.** Before anything is touched, we establish containment barriers to prevent cross-contamination to unaffected living areas. In multifamily buildings, this means isolating the affected unit from shared hallways.

**2. Solid waste and standing water removal.** We extract standing sewage using truck-mounted and portable extraction units, then remove and bag all contaminated porous materials — drywall, insulation, carpet, padding — that cannot be effectively sanitized. In Garfield's older homes, this often includes plank subfloor and original lath that has absorbed sewage deeply.

**3. Antimicrobial treatment.** All affected hard surfaces — concrete block, masonry, framing lumber — are treated with EPA-registered disinfectants rated for Category 3 contamination. We don't skip this step on "just the floor drain area" calls; sewage aerosols travel farther than the visible stain line.

**4. Structural drying.** After sanitization, we deploy commercial desiccant and refrigerant dehumidifiers along with high-velocity air movers, calibrated to the specific moisture load. Garfield's older cast-concrete and block basement walls hold moisture longer than poured concrete, so drying timelines here often run 4–5 days rather than the 2–3 days typical in newer construction.

**5. Post-remediation verification.** We document moisture readings throughout the process and provide a written drying log — something your insurance adjuster will ask for.
