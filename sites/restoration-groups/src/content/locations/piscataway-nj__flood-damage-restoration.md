## Why Piscataway Properties See Flood Damage Differently

The housing stock here creates complications you won't find everywhere. Older tracts in Possumtown and the Stelton corridor were built in the postwar decades with galvanized steel supply lines and cast-iron drain stacks — materials that have long since corroded or scaled to near-closure. When a flood event stresses those systems, a pipe that was holding on suddenly isn't. Finished basements in those same homes were built without modern vapor barriers, so floodwater doesn't just pool on the floor; it wicks into framed partition walls and insulated ceiling assemblies that were never designed to dry out.

Society Hill's townhome and condo developments present a different challenge: shared wall assemblies and common mechanical chases mean water that enters one unit can migrate laterally before anyone notices. Structural drying in those buildings requires coordinating access across multiple units and, often, working with HOA management on building-wide moisture mapping before any one owner can close out their claim.

On the commercial side, the Centennial Avenue and Stelton commercial corridors include warehouse and lab-space tenants where an overnight sprinkler discharge or roof-leak event can compromise inventory and sensitive equipment. ## Our Flood Damage Restoration Process in Piscataway

Every job starts with a full moisture survey — thermal imaging and pin-type meters across every affected surface — because visible water is rarely the whole story. In Piscataway's older split-levels, we routinely find elevated readings inside wall cavities two rooms away from the visible flood line.

From there, the process follows the IICRC S500 standard for water damage restoration:

- **Water extraction**: truck-mounted and portable extractors pull standing water from hard floors, carpet, and subfloor assemblies. In basements with poured concrete slabs, we use weighted extraction tools to draw water from the slab surface before it migrates into block foundation walls.
- **Controlled demolition**: saturated drywall, insulation, and flooring that cannot be dried in place are removed to the flood cut line — typically 12 inches above the highest moisture reading. This is not cosmetic; it's how you stop secondary mold colonization, which can begin within 24 to 48 hours in New Jersey's summer humidity.
- **Structural drying**: commercial-grade desiccant dehumidifiers and high-velocity air movers run in a calibrated drying system. We log temperature, relative humidity, and material moisture content daily and adjust equipment placement until readings reach dry standard.
- **Post-dry verification**: before any rebuild begins, we document final moisture readings across all affected materials. That documentation matters for your insurance adjuster and protects you if a moisture-related issue surfaces later.
