## Our Water Damage Restoration Process in Plainfield

Every job starts with a moisture survey before a single pump is placed. We use thermal imaging cameras and calibrated moisture meters to map how far water has traveled — because in a Victorian home with plaster-over-lath walls and original hardwood floors, water migrates horizontally through wall cavities in ways that aren't obvious from the surface. Skipping this step leads to hidden pockets of moisture that feed mold growth within 48 to 72 hours.

Once we know the full extent of the loss, we begin water extraction using truck-mounted and portable extraction units, followed by targeted structural drying with industrial air movers and desiccant or refrigerant dehumidifiers sized to the affected area. Drying targets are set to IICRC S500 standard — not pulled from the air, but calculated from the material types and ambient conditions present in your specific home.

For Plainfield's older homes, we pay particular attention to subfloor assemblies. Many properties in the Sleepy Hollow neighborhood and along the Cedar Brook corridor have original tongue-and-groove subfloor boards over floor joists — a construction style that traps water underneath finished flooring and requires directed airflow from below, not just above. We pull readings daily and adjust equipment until every monitored point reaches its dry standard.

Plainfield's Victorian and Edwardian homes frequently have plaster walls finished with horsehair-reinforced base coats — a material that behaves very differently from modern drywall during a water loss.
