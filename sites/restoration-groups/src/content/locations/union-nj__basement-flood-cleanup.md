## Our Basement Flood Cleanup Process in Union

The first step on any flooded basement call is containment and assessment — not just pulling water. A crew member walks the space with a thermal imaging camera and moisture meter before any equipment runs, because what reads dry at the surface of a 1950s plaster-and-lath wall can be fully saturated two inches in. That distinction changes the drying plan entirely.

From there, the process follows the IICRC S500 standard for water damage restoration:

- **Extraction** — truck-mounted and portable extractors pull standing water from carpet, subfloor, and slab joints. In finished basements, this includes removing saturated carpet and pad, which hold far more water than they appear to.
- **Structural drying** — commercial-grade desiccant and refrigerant dehumidifiers run continuously, paired with high-velocity air movers positioned to dry wall cavities, not just open air.
- **Moisture monitoring** — readings are logged at each visit so drying progress is documented, not estimated. Insurance carriers expect this data.
- **Controlled demolition if needed** — saturated drywall below the flood line typically has to come out. In Union's older split-levels, that often means opening framed walls to dry the wood stud behind the finish material.

The goal is to reach dry standard before any reconstruction begins — skipping that step is how mold takes hold inside a wall cavity weeks after cleanup looks complete.

## Union Insurance Coordination

Most standard homeowner policies cover sudden and accidental water discharge — a burst pipe, a failed appliance supply line, an overflowing fixture. Gradual seepage through a foundation wall is a different claim category and often excluded, which is why the initial documentation matters. The Restoration Group photographs affected materials, logs moisture readings, and prepares scope documentation that insurance adjusters can work from directly. That process reduces back-and-forth and keeps the claim moving while drying is still underway.

If your policy includes loss-of-use coverage and the basement is a finished living space, that's worth raising with your adjuster early — especially in homes where the lower level functions as a rental unit or in-law suite.
