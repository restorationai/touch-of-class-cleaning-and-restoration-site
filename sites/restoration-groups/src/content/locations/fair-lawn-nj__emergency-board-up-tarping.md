## Our Emergency Board-Up and Tarping Process in Fair Lawn

When a call comes in, the first thing we do is ask two questions: what type of opening, and what's the weather doing right now. Those answers determine whether we roll with plywood and framing hardware for a long-term board-up or deploy a heavy-duty polyethylene tarp system rated for sustained wind — or both on the same job.

For residential board-up, we cut exterior-grade plywood to fit each opening, secure it with structural screws into the framing (not just the trim), and seal the perimeter with foam backer rod where the fit isn't flush. On older Radburn homes with non-standard window proportions — many of those 1930s casements don't match any modern stock size — we cut custom panels on-site rather than forcing an ill-fitting sheet that leaves gaps at the corners.

For roof tarping, we use reinforced poly tarps anchored with 2×4 battens screwed through the tarp and into the roof deck, not bungee cords or sandbags that shift in wind. We overlap the ridge by at least two feet so wind-driven rain can't work back under the leading edge. On steeper-pitch roofs common in Fair Lawn's colonial stock, we use fall-arrest equipment — the pitch that gives a house its curb appeal is the same pitch that makes an unprotected tarp job dangerous.

Every job is photographed before, during, and after installation. That documentation goes directly to you and, if you authorize it, straight to your insurance adjuster.
