## Our Mold Remediation Process in Linden

Every job starts with a thorough moisture mapping and visual inspection — not just the room with visible mold, but adjacent cavities, the HVAC system, and any crawlspace or basement slab. In Linden's older homes, we pay particular attention to the band joist area where the floor framing meets the foundation wall; it's consistently one of the wettest spots in a postwar split-level and one of the most commonly overlooked.

Once we've identified the full affected area, we establish negative-air containment using 6-mil poly barriers and HEPA-filtered air scrubbers. This keeps spores from migrating to clean areas of the home during removal. Affected porous materials — drywall, insulation, wood sheathing — are removed, bagged, and disposed of per New Jersey DEP guidelines. Non-porous surfaces are HEPA-vacuumed and treated with an EPA-registered antimicrobial agent.

Drying is not optional after remediation. We leave commercial-grade dehumidifiers and air movers on-site until moisture readings in structural materials return to baseline — typically under 16% for wood framing. In Linden's below-grade finished spaces, that drying phase can run longer than homeowners expect, particularly in late summer when outdoor dew points are high. We monitor daily and don't pull equipment early.
