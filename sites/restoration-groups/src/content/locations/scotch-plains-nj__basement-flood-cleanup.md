## Our Basement Flood Cleanup Process in Scotch Plains

Every job starts with moisture mapping, not assumptions. We use thermal imaging cameras and calibrated moisture meters to locate water that has traveled beyond the visible wet area — behind finished drywall, beneath vinyl plank flooring, inside the cavities of drop ceilings. In Scotch Plains's mid-century homes, that step is especially important: the original plaster-and-lath construction common in the older colonials near Downtown Scotch Plains holds moisture differently than modern drywall. Plaster can read dry at the surface while the lath behind it stays saturated, which means drying timelines run longer than they would in a newer build.

Once we understand the full extent of the loss, we extract standing water with truck-mounted and portable extraction units, then set a calibrated drying system — commercial-grade desiccant or refrigerant dehumidifiers paired with air movers positioned for the specific room geometry. We monitor daily with psychrometric readings and adjust equipment placement as materials dry. Nothing is signed off until moisture readings return to regional baseline, not just until the floor feels dry underfoot.

For finished basements, we perform selective demolition only where necessary: removing saturated drywall to the flood cut line, preserving structural framing where it's sound, and documenting every removed material with photographs for your insurance claim file.
