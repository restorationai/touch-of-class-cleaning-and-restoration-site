## Our Mold Inspection and Testing Process in Paramus

A mold inspection is not a visual walkthrough with a flashlight. The process starts with a structured interview about the property's water history — past floods, plumbing repairs, HVAC issues, any musty odors — because mold often lives where the last water event happened, not necessarily where the current smell is strongest.

From there, the inspection moves through the structure systematically. Moisture meters and thermal imaging cameras identify elevated readings behind walls, under flooring, and above ceilings without requiring demolition. If moisture anomalies or visible growth are found, air samples and surface swab samples are collected and sent to an accredited third-party laboratory. The lab identifies the species present and quantifies spore concentrations, comparing indoor levels against outdoor baseline samples taken at the same time.

The written report you receive specifies exactly where samples were collected, what the lab found, and — critically — whether the results indicate a remediation need or simply elevated background spores that can be addressed through ventilation improvements. That distinction matters when you're deciding whether to call a remediation contractor or simply improve airflow in a basement storage room.

Because The Restoration Group holds IICRC Firm Certification (#210213) and is licensed through the NJ Division of Consumer Affairs as a Home Improvement Contractor, the inspection report carries documentation weight that insurers and real estate attorneys recognize.

## Coordinating with Insurance After Paramus Water Events

Mold inspections in Bergen County frequently follow insurance claims — a burst pipe, a sump pump failure during a nor'easter, or storm-related flooding. Insurers want documentation that links the mold growth to a covered water event, and the timing of that documentation matters. Mold discovered months after an undocumented leak is harder to tie to a specific cause, which is why getting an inspection on record quickly after any water intrusion is worth the cost.

## Local Note

One pattern that comes up repeatedly in Paramus's postwar ranches: the original builders often ran supply plumbing through exterior wall cavities on the north-facing side of the house — a practice that made sense for layout but left pipes vulnerable to freezing and, over decades, to pinhole corrosion. When those lines fail slowly, the insulation in the wall cavity absorbs the moisture before it ever reaches the drywall surface. Thermal imaging during a mold inspection will catch the temperature differential that signals a wet wall cavity, even when the drywall itself looks and feels dry to the touch. If your home was built before 1970 and you've never had the north-facing walls checked, it's worth including that in the scope of an inspection.
