Roselle's housing stock is one of the densest concentrations of pre-1950 construction in Union County. ## Our Flood Damage Restoration Process in Roselle

When we arrive, the first priority is stopping ongoing intrusion if it hasn't been addressed — shutting the water source, confirming the sump is operational, or sandbagging if the Elizabeth River is still running high. From there, the process follows a consistent sequence calibrated to what we actually find in Roselle homes:

**Water extraction** — Truck-mounted extractors pull standing water from finished basement floors, carpeting, and subfloor cavities. In older homes with tongue-and-groove subfloors, water migrates laterally under the boards faster than it does under modern OSB, so we use moisture mapping to trace the full spread before we set equipment.

**Structural drying** — Industrial desiccant and refrigerant dehumidifiers, paired with high-velocity air movers, are positioned based on psychrometric readings taken at the start and monitored daily. Drying timelines in plaster-wall construction — common in Roselle's Victorian and early colonial stock — run longer than in drywall homes because plaster releases absorbed moisture slowly and unevenly.

**Contamination treatment** — Any event involving sewer backup receives EPA-registered antimicrobial application to all affected surfaces. Porous materials that absorbed Category 3 water are removed and documented for your insurance claim.

**Documentation** — Every step is photographed and logged. We produce a moisture-dry log and a scope of loss that your insurance adjuster can use directly.
