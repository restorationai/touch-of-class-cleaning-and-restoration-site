## Our Deodorization Process, Calibrated for Paramus Conditions

Effective odor elimination isn't a single product — it's a sequence of methods matched to the source, the material, and the severity. The process typically moves through these stages:

**Source removal first.** No deodorization technology works long-term if the odor source is still present. That means removing saturated materials, cleaning smoke residue from surfaces, or clearing the failed plumbing component before any treatment begins.

**Thermal fogging** for smoke and fire odors. A petroleum-based or water-based fogging agent is heated to create a dry fog that penetrates the same porous pathways smoke traveled — inside wall cavities, under subfloor sheathing, into HVAC returns. It's particularly effective in the open floor plans common to Paramus ranches and split-levels where smoke migrates freely between living zones.

**Hydroxyl generation** for occupied or sensitive environments. Hydroxyl generators produce hydroxyl radicals that oxidize odor molecules without requiring the space to be vacated, making them the right tool for commercial properties along Route 17 that can't afford a full closure, or for households where occupants need to return quickly.

**Ozone treatment** for severe, unoccupied spaces. High-concentration ozone is the most aggressive option for deeply embedded odors — fire losses, long-term pet contamination, or decomposition events — but it requires full evacuation of people, plants, and pets for the treatment period and a thorough airing-out before reoccupancy.

All drying and deodorization work is performed per IICRC standards, and the crew documents conditions before and after with moisture readings and air sampling where applicable.

## Local Note: What Paramus's Finished Basements Do to Odor Treatment

One pattern that comes up repeatedly in Paramus: homeowners in the Farview and Spring Valley neighborhoods finished their basements in the 1970s and 1980s using wood-paneled walls furred out from the foundation — a construction method that creates a hidden air gap between the paneling and the concrete block. When sewer gas, mold, or flood odors get into that cavity, surface-only treatments don't reach them. Thermal fogging or hydroxyl treatment needs to be introduced into that void space directly, either through drilled access points or by temporarily removing a section of paneling.
