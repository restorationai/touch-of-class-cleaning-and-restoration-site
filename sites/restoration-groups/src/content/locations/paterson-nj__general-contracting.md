## Why Paterson's Building Stock Shapes Every Renovation

Renovating in Paterson is not the same as renovating in a suburb built after 1980. The city's proximity to the Passaic River means that low-lying blocks near the Great Falls have flooded repeatedly — Hurricane Irene in 2011 and Ida in 2021 both left standing water in basements and first floors across the Riverside and downtown sections. Homes that absorbed those floods often have subfloor rot, compromised rim joists, and moisture-damaged framing that only becomes visible once a kitchen or bathroom gut begins.

Beyond flood history, the housing stock itself creates complexity. Knob-and-tube wiring remnants in pre-1940 frames require coordination with a licensed electrician before walls close. Cast-iron drain stacks in multi-family walkups are brittle after a century of use — a bathroom remodel that ignores the stack condition can produce a failure inside a finished wall six months later. Lead paint and asbestos-containing materials are routine in pre-1978 construction, and New Jersey's Department of Health requirements for lead-safe work practices apply to any renovation disturbing painted surfaces in homes built before that year.

## Our Renovation and General Contracting Process in Paterson

Every project starts with a documented scope walkthrough — not a phone estimate. For post-damage rebuilds, that walkthrough cross-references the adjuster's scope of loss with what the structure actually shows: hidden moisture, compromised structural members, or code-deficiency items that an insurance estimate may not have captured. For elective remodels, it means understanding which walls are load-bearing in a rowhouse configuration, where the knob-and-tube runs, and whether the existing drain stack can handle a reconfigured bathroom layout.

Permitting is handled through the City of Paterson's Division of Construction Code Compliance. Paterson enforces the New Jersey Uniform Construction Code, and permit timelines can run longer for multi-family properties or projects in historically sensitive blocks near Lambert Castle or the Great Falls National Historical Park corridor. We file the applications, coordinate inspections, and keep the project on schedule around those windows rather than treating permits as an afterthought.

For kitchen and bathroom remodels specifically, we source materials with Paterson's climate in mind — tile and grout selections that hold up in humid, poorly ventilated spaces, and cabinetry that won't delaminate in a basement-level unit that sees seasonal moisture swings. Finished work is only as durable as the substrate and ventilation behind it.
