## Why North Bergen's Housing Stock Complicates Fire Damage

The building fabric here is the central challenge. A significant portion of North Bergen's residential inventory was built before 1950, and those structures carry characteristics that change how fire and smoke damage behaves. Balloon-frame construction — common in the two- and three-family homes that line the streets between the Bergenline corridor and the Woodcliff neighborhood — lacks the fire-blocking between floor cavities that modern platform framing provides. Smoke, heat, and soot travel vertically through wall voids, contaminating units that had no direct flame exposure. Owners and tenants in unaffected floors are often surprised to find a persistent acrid odor and fine soot deposits on surfaces two stories from the origin point.

Older plaster-and-lath walls, still common throughout the 07047 ZIP code, absorb smoke residue differently than modern drywall. The porous lime-based plaster holds odor-causing compounds deeper in the substrate, which means standard surface cleaning is rarely sufficient — affected sections often require HEPA-vacuuming, chemical sponge treatment, and sealing before any repainting. Cast-iron heating systems and older ductwork in these buildings can also carry smoke particulate throughout a structure, requiring duct cleaning as part of the scope.

## Our Fire Damage Restoration Process in North Bergen

Every fire loss is different, but the sequence is consistent and matters. The first step on arrival is a thorough structural and safety assessment — checking load-bearing integrity, identifying compromised electrical panels (a real concern in older North Bergen rentals that may still have fuse boxes or undersized service), and documenting all affected areas with photos and moisture readings before anything is touched. That documentation is the foundation of the insurance claim.

Once the structure is confirmed safe to work in, the process moves through board-up and tarping of any breached openings, controlled demolition of unsalvageable materials, and HEPA air scrubbing to begin reducing airborne particulate. Soot removal follows a strict protocol — dry chemical sponges before any wet cleaning, because introducing moisture to protein-based smoke residue (common in kitchen fires) can permanently set staining into surfaces. Thermal fogging and hydroxyl generation address embedded odor in porous materials including plaster, wood framing, and contents. Structural drying runs concurrently where water from suppression efforts has saturated subfloors or wall cavities, which is nearly universal in multi-family buildings where firefighting water migrates between units through shared framing.

The Restoration Group is an IICRC Certified Firm (#210213), and the work is scoped and documented to meet carrier requirements — we work directly with adjusters to keep the claim moving.
