Piscataway's housing stock tells a complicated story for anyone planning a renovation. The postwar capes and split-levels scattered through Arbor and Possumtown were built when galvanized supply lines and cast-iron drains were standard — and after seven decades, those systems are failing inside finished basements and behind tiled walls. Add the Raritan River's documented flood history along River Road and the Johnson Park flatlands, and a significant share of remodel projects here begin not with a design wish list but with damage that forced the homeowner's hand. The Restoration Group handles both ends: the remediation that clears the way and the licensed general contracting that brings the space back better than it was.

## Why Piscataway Homes Present Distinct Renovation Challenges

The age and construction type of a home shapes every phase of a remodel, and Piscataway's inventory is unusually varied. The split-levels and raised ranches built between the late 1940s and early 1970s — common throughout the Stelton and New Market corridors — frequently have knob-and-tube or early aluminum wiring that must be addressed before walls close. Insulation in those homes is often compressed fiberglass or, in some cases, vermiculite in attic spaces, which requires testing before any demo work begins.

Society Hill's townhome and condo developments introduce a different set of constraints. Shared walls, HOA architectural guidelines, and building management approval processes add lead time to scopes that would be straightforward in a detached single-family home. Permit timelines through Middlesex County and the Piscataway Township Construction Office can run longer than neighboring municipalities, so realistic scheduling matters from the first conversation.

For properties in the River Road flood corridor — homes that took on water during Ida in 2021 or during Floyd and Irene before that — the subfloor and lower wall assemblies often carry hidden moisture damage that a cosmetic remodel would simply seal over. We scope those projects with moisture mapping before any finish work is priced.

## Our Renovation and General Contracting Process in Piscataway

Every project starts with a documented walkthrough — not a sales visit. For post-damage rebuilds, that means moisture readings, material sampling where age suggests hazardous content, and photographic documentation that supports an insurance claim if one is open. For elective remodels, it means understanding what's behind the surfaces before committing to a budget.

From there, the process follows a consistent sequence:

- **Permit and HOA coordination** — We pull all required Piscataway Township permits and, for Society Hill or similar HOA communities, submit architectural review packages on the homeowner's behalf.
- **Hazardous material abatement** — Older homes in the 08854 ZIP code frequently contain lead paint in pre-1978 layers and asbestos in floor tile adhesive or pipe wrap. Our IICRC Certified Firm (#210213) handles remediation before any renovation begins.
- **Structural and mechanical rough-in** — Framing, electrical, plumbing, and HVAC rough-in are inspected before walls close, keeping the project code-compliant at every stage.
- **Finish work and final inspection** — Tile, cabinetry, flooring, and fixtures are installed to the agreed scope, followed by a township final inspection and a client walkthrough.

For commercial properties along the Centennial Avenue or Stelton commercial corridors — lab space, warehouse, or retail — we schedule trades to minimize operational downtime and coordinate with building management on access and after-hours work windows.

## Local Note: What Older Piscataway Basements Hide Behind the Drywall

One pattern we see repeatedly in the split-levels and raised ranches between Possumtown Road and Stelton Road: a finished basement that was renovated sometime in the 1980s or 1990s, with drywall furred directly against the foundation wall and no drainage plane behind it. When those homes flood — even a modest few inches — the water wicks up into the wall cavity and stays there, hidden behind paint that looks fine. By the time a homeowner calls about a musty smell, the framing has been wet for weeks. On any Piscataway basement remodel following a water event, we open the wall assembly to the foundation before pricing the rebuild. It adds a day to the assessment but prevents a mold callback six months later.
