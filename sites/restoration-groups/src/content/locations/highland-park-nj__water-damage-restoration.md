## Our Water Damage Restoration Process in Highland Park

The first thing a crew does on arrival is identify the water source and stop active flow — shutting a supply valve, capping a drain line, or sandbagging a point of entry. Once the source is controlled, the focus shifts to measurement: moisture meters and thermal imaging cameras map how far water has traveled through walls, subfloor assemblies, and insulation. In Highland Park's older homes, that spread is often wider than it looks. Plaster-and-lath walls, common in pre-1950 colonials, absorb water more slowly than drywall but release it slowly too, meaning drying timelines run longer than a homeowner might expect from reading generic estimates online.

Extraction comes next — truck-mounted and portable units pull standing water from basements, crawlspaces, and above-grade rooms. After extraction, the drying phase begins with industrial air movers and low-grain refrigerant dehumidifiers calibrated to the actual moisture load in the structure, not a one-size estimate. Readings are logged daily against the IICRC S500 standard to confirm drying progress is on track. Affected materials that cannot be dried in place — saturated insulation, compromised subfloor panels, sections of plaster that have delaminated — are removed and documented for the insurance claim before the structure is closed up.
