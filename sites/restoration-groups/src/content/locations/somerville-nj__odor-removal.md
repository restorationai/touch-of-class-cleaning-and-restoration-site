## Why Somerville Properties Hold Odors Longer

The borough's housing stock is the first factor any honest deodorization contractor has to reckon with. Homes in the West End and along Gaston Avenue were largely built between 1900 and 1940, when builders used old-growth pine framing, plaster-and-lath walls, and minimal vapor barriers. Those materials are porous in ways that modern OSB and drywall are not. Smoke from a kitchen fire or a chimney backdraft doesn't just coat surfaces — it migrates into wall cavities and subfloor assemblies, where it off-gasses for weeks.

The flooding history compounds the problem. Low-lying streets near the Raritan River took on water during Tropical Storm Ida in 2021, and older events like Floyd and Irene left similar marks. When a basement floods and isn't fully dried within 24 to 48 hours, microbial activity begins producing musty mercaptans and aldehydes that bond to porous concrete block and wood framing. By the time a homeowner notices the smell, the odor compounds have already polymerized into the material — surface sprays accomplish nothing.

The Main Street commercial corridor adds a different odor profile. Restaurants, dry cleaners, and the occasional sprinkler-discharge event in a storefront near the Somerset County Courthouse create concentrated odor losses in mixed-use buildings where residential units sit directly above commercial kitchens or utility spaces. Grease-laden smoke travels through shared HVAC chases faster than most building owners expect.

## Our Deodorization Process in Somerville

Every job starts with an odor-source assessment — not just a walk-through with a nose, but a systematic inspection using thermal imaging and moisture meters to locate residual dampness that is still off-gassing. Wet material is the engine of most persistent odors; no deodorization technology works long-term if the source is still wet.

Once the source is confirmed dry and structurally addressed, we select the treatment method based on the odor type, the materials involved, and whether the space is occupied:

- **Thermal fogging** — a petroleum-based or water-based deodorant is vaporized into particles that penetrate the same porous pathways smoke traveled, neutralizing odor compounds on contact. Effective in older plaster-wall homes where odors have migrated into wall cavities.
- **Hydroxyl generation** — UV-spectrum hydroxyl generators produce the same oxidizing radicals the atmosphere uses to break down pollutants. Safe for occupied spaces and ideal for multi-unit downtown apartment buildings where full evacuation isn't practical.
- **Ozone treatment** — high-concentration ozone is deployed in unoccupied spaces for fire and severe mold odors. Ozone is the most aggressive oxidizer in the toolkit; re-occupancy protocols are strictly observed.
- **HEPA air scrubbing** — continuous negative-pressure air scrubbing removes particulate odor carriers from the air column throughout treatment.

For Somerville's older homes, we often combine thermal fogging into wall cavities with hydroxyl generation in living areas — a sequenced approach that addresses both the embedded and airborne odor fractions.

## Local Note

Somerville's Victorian and colonial homes along Mercer Street and in the Fairview area frequently have finished basements with drop ceilings installed over original wood-beam ceilings. When a sewage backup or flood odor is treated, those drop-ceiling tiles trap the deodorizing agent and prevent it from reaching the original ceiling above — and the original ceiling is where the odor has actually migrated. We pull drop-ceiling tiles before fogging in these configurations rather than treating through them, which cuts re-treatment callbacks significantly. It's a small step that makes a real difference in homes built the way Somerville's were.
