## Why Edison Properties Take a Hard Hit in Severe Weather

Edison is a sprawling township — roughly 30 square miles — and its geography creates a few specific vulnerabilities. The Raritan River forms the southern boundary, and the Mill Brook tributaries that thread through lower-lying sections of the township turn into overflow channels during heavy rain events. Residents in the Oak Tree and Nixon areas saw this firsthand during the remnants of Hurricane Ida in 2021, when streets flooded faster than storm drains could handle the volume.

Beyond flooding, the housing stock matters. A large share of Edison's single-family homes were constructed between the late 1940s and early 1970s. That era of construction used dimensional lumber that is actually stronger than modern engineered wood in some respects, but the roof sheathing was often thinner, and ridge vents and soffit ventilation were minimal or absent. When a storm forces water under compromised flashing or through a wind-lifted ridge cap, it pools in unventilated attic cavities — and mold can begin colonizing wet insulation in as little as 48 to 72 hours. Acting fast is not a sales pitch; it's biology.

The Route 1 corridor adds a commercial dimension. ## Our Storm Damage Restoration Process in Edison

Every job starts with a documented scope — not a verbal estimate. We photograph and log every affected surface before any material is moved, because your insurance adjuster will need that record. In Edison, where a significant share of homeowners carry flood endorsements through the National Flood Insurance Program in addition to standard homeowners policies, having two separate documented scopes (wind damage vs. water intrusion) can prevent claim disputes that delay your payout by weeks.

Once the scope is set, the process follows a logical sequence:

1. **Emergency stabilization** — tarping exposed roof sections, boarding broken windows, and extracting any standing water before secondary damage compounds the loss.
2. **Structural drying** — commercial-grade desiccant dehumidifiers and high-velocity air movers run on a schedule calibrated to the material types present. Plaster walls common in older Edison homes dry more slowly than drywall and require extended monitoring.
3. **Debris removal and cleaning** — storm-driven debris carries contaminants. We treat affected areas accordingly and dispose of materials per Middlesex County guidelines.
4. **Reconstruction coordination** — as an NJ Licensed Home Improvement Contractor, we can carry the job from mitigation through finished repairs, which means one point of contact instead of three.

All drying work is performed to IICRC S500 and S520 standards by our IICRC Certified Firm (#210213) technicians.

## Local Note

One thing that catches homeowners off guard in the older split-levels common in Stelton and parts of North Edison: the finished basement level sits partially below grade, and the exterior foundation walls are often uninsulated block. After a storm flood event, that block wicks moisture inward for days after the visible water is gone. Standard moisture meters read the surface; we use thermal imaging cameras to map the full wet zone behind finished walls before we set drying equipment. Skipping that step means pulling drywall twice — once when it looks dry and again when the mold shows up six weeks later.
