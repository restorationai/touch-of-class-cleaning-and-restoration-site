## Why Edison Properties See Smoke Damage Differently

Edison's housing stock is a patchwork of eras. The postwar capes and split-levels concentrated around Stelton and Oak Tree were built with minimal vapor barriers and open stud bays, which act as chimneys during a fire — pulling smoke deep into wall cavities long before a sprinkler or suppression system activates. That same construction pattern means smoke odor can persist in insulation and subfloor assemblies even after visible soot is removed.

The Route 1 retail corridor and the industrial and logistics properties near Menlo Park Mall introduce a different challenge: commercial fires involving synthetic materials — foam insulation, PVC conduit, warehouse racking — produce oily, protein-heavy smoke that bonds aggressively to HVAC ductwork and metal surfaces. Protein smoke is odorless to most people at first, but it oxidizes over days and becomes increasingly difficult to neutralize if cleaning is delayed.

Edison's climate adds pressure to the timeline. Hot, humid summers accelerate the rate at which smoke residue penetrates porous surfaces — brick, grout, unfinished wood — and the freeze-thaw cycle in winter can reactivate odors trapped in exterior walls when temperatures fluctuate. Waiting even 48 to 72 hours after a fire to begin professional cleaning measurably increases the scope of work.

## Our Smoke Damage Restoration Process in Edison

Every job begins with a structured assessment — not a sales walk-through. A technician documents the fire origin point, maps the smoke migration path through the structure, and identifies which materials are salvageable versus which need controlled demolition. For Edison's older colonials and split-levels, that assessment often includes probing wall cavities with a borescope to confirm whether smoke has tracked behind finished surfaces.

From there, the process moves through four phases:

**Containment and ventilation.** Negative air pressure units isolate affected zones and begin pulling contaminated air out of the structure. This is especially important in multifamily buildings — common in the Nixon and Menlo Park sections of the township — where smoke can migrate into adjacent units through shared ductwork or plumbing chases.

**Surface cleaning and deodorization.** Dry-ice blasting, chemical sponges, and HEPA-filtered vacuums remove loose soot from walls, ceilings, and structural framing. Thermal fogging and hydroxyl generator treatments penetrate porous materials — wood framing, insulation, carpet backing — to neutralize odor compounds at the molecular level rather than masking them.

**HVAC decontamination.** Smoke travels through return-air systems and coats duct interiors with a thin, sticky film. Skipping this step is the single most common reason smoke odor returns weeks after a fire. Every duct run in the affected zone is inspected and cleaned before the system is returned to service.

**Clearance verification.** Air quality readings and odor checks confirm the structure meets acceptable thresholds before the job is closed out. The Restoration Group is an IICRC Certified Firm (#210213), and the restoration process follows IICRC S700 standards for smoke and fire damage.

In the sections of Clara Barton and Oak Tree where homes date to the late 1940s and 1950s, original plaster-and-lathe wall systems are still common behind later layers of drywall or paneling.
