## Our Storm Damage Restoration Process in Fair Lawn

Every job starts with a full damage assessment before any material is moved or cut. For storm losses, that means documenting the breach point — whether it's a tree impact, a failed roof section, or a flooded foundation — and tracing every path water has traveled from there. In Fair Lawn's older homes, that path is rarely obvious. Water follows framing cavities, travels along subfloor sheathing, and pools in areas that look dry from the surface.

Once the scope is mapped, the sequence is:

1. **Emergency tarping and board-up** — closing the structure against further weather exposure before restoration work begins.
2. **Water extraction and controlled demolition** — removing saturated materials that cannot be dried in place. In Radburn-era homes with plaster walls, we assess each section individually; plaster holds moisture longer than drywall and requires extended drying cycles.
3. **Structural drying** — commercial-grade desiccant dehumidifiers and high-velocity air movers, placed to IICRC S500 drying standards, run until moisture readings in framing and subfloor reach target levels.
4. **Debris removal and tree damage cleanup** — coordinating with licensed tree services when large limbs or full trees are involved, then clearing the site for reconstruction.
5. **Reconstruction and finish work** — framing, insulation, drywall, roofing, and interior finishes restored to pre-loss condition.

All work is performed under our NJ Licensed Home Improvement Contractor status, which matters when Bergen County inspections are required for structural repairs.
