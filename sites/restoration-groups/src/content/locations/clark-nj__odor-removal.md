## Our Odor Removal and Deodorization Process in Clark

Effective deodorization isn't a single step — it's a sequence calibrated to the odor source, the affected materials, and the structure itself. Here's how we approach a typical Clark job:

**Source removal first.** No deodorization method works if the source material is still present. Charred wood, saturated drywall, or contaminated carpet padding gets removed before any treatment begins. Masking an odor without removing the source is a temporary fix that fails within weeks.

**Surface cleaning and sealing.** Smoke residue and biological odors bond to porous surfaces — bare concrete in a basement slab, the wood framing behind drywall, ductwork lining. We clean accessible surfaces and apply odor-blocking sealers where appropriate before moving to atmospheric treatment.

**Atmospheric treatment matched to the job.** We use hydroxyl generators, thermal fogging, and ozone treatment depending on what the structure needs. Hydroxyl generators work continuously without requiring occupants to vacate for extended periods — useful in occupied homes near Arthur L. Johnson High School where families can't always leave for days. Thermal fogging reaches into wall cavities and HVAC systems by producing a deodorizing fog that follows the same pathways smoke traveled. Ozone treatment is highly effective for severe smoke odor but requires the structure to be fully vacated and sealed during the process. We'll explain which method fits your situation before we start.

**Verification.** We don't close a job based on what we can't smell in the room — we verify with odor meters and a structured walkthrough before signing off.
