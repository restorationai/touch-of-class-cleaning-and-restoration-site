## Our Commercial Restoration Process in Bridgewater

Every commercial job begins with a documented scope — not a verbal estimate. When we arrive at a Bridgewater property, our first step is a moisture mapping survey using thermal imaging and calibrated meters to establish exactly where water has migrated. In slab-on-grade buildings common along the Route 22 corridor, that means checking under vinyl composition tile and floating floors before any drying equipment is placed, because trapped moisture under impermeable surfaces will not evaporate upward — it has to be mechanically extracted or the slab dried from below with desiccant systems.

For fire and smoke losses, we conduct a room-by-room odor and residue assessment before any cleaning begins. Dry smoke from fast-burning electrical fires behaves differently from wet smoke produced by smoldering materials — the cleaning chemistry and dwell times are not interchangeable, and using the wrong approach on the wrong residue type drives the odor deeper into porous surfaces. We document every affected material with photographs and moisture readings that are formatted for direct submission to commercial property carriers.

Drying timelines in commercial spaces are governed by the IICRC S500 standard. We set daily drying goals, log psychrometric readings each visit, and adjust equipment placement when a space is not tracking toward dry. Business owners get written progress reports — not just a technician's verbal update — because your property manager, your insurer, and your tenant all need the same information.
