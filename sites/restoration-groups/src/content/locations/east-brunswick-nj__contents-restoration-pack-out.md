## Why East Brunswick Properties See Contents Losses at Higher Rates

East Brunswick's postwar housing stock — the ranches, split-levels, and colonials built through the 1950s, '60s, and '70s in neighborhoods like Tanglewood and Farrington — was designed with finished basements as a matter of course. That's a generation of original supply plumbing and cast-iron drain lines now at or past their service life. When those pipes fail, they fail into finished space: carpet, drywall, wood paneling, stored belongings, and home-office setups that weren't part of the original build. The contents exposure in a typical East Brunswick basement loss is substantial.

The Lawrence Brook watershed adds a second layer of risk. ## Our Contents Pack-Out and Restoration Process

The process begins with a room-by-room inventory before anything leaves the property. Every item is photographed, catalogued, and assigned a condition code — that documentation is what your insurance adjuster needs, and it's what protects you if a dispute arises later. Items that can be restored are packed using appropriate materials for their category: electronics and artwork travel differently than upholstered furniture or textiles.

From your East Brunswick home, contents move to a climate-controlled facility where restoration technicians work by category. Soft goods go through HEPA-filtered laundering or dry-cleaning depending on fiber content and contamination type. Hard surfaces — furniture, cabinetry components, decorative items — are cleaned with methods matched to the material. Electronics are assessed and, where recoverable, dried and tested before return. Documents and photographs that have been wet for fewer than 48 hours often respond well to air-drying and freeze-drying protocols; after that window, the options narrow.

When the structure is ready, contents are returned, unwrapped, and placed according to the room diagram created at pack-out. Nothing is left for you to sort through.

## Insurance Coordination for East Brunswick Contents Claims

Most homeowner and renter policies in New Jersey cover sudden and accidental water losses, and many include contents coverage as a separate sublimit. The pack-out inventory we create at your property is formatted to support a contents claim directly — line-itemized, photographed, and condition-coded. We work with adjusters from the major carriers regularly and can provide scope documentation in formats they recognize. If your policy includes ALE (additional living expense) coverage, having a professional pack-out on record also supports the claim that your home was genuinely uninhabitable during the restoration period.

For condo and townhome owners in East Brunswick, the question of which policy covers which contents — the unit owner's HO-6 versus the HOA master policy — comes up often in multi-unit losses. In East Brunswick's older split-levels and ranches, finished basements frequently have drop-ceiling systems installed decades after the original build — and those ceiling tiles are among the first things to fail in an overhead leak or flooding event.
