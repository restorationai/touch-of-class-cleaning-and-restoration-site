## Why Staten Island Properties See More Water Damage

The borough's housing stock creates conditions that amplify water losses in ways that aren't obvious until you're pulling up flooring. The postwar Cape Cods and split-levels packed into neighborhoods like Great Kills and Port Richmond were built with shallow basement slabs and minimal waterproofing — standards that made sense in the 1950s but leave today's homeowners vulnerable when aging cast-iron supply lines finally give way. Those original galvanized and cast-iron pipes corrode from the inside out, often showing no exterior warning before they fail under pressure.

The East Shore geography compounds the problem. Inland, the clay-heavy soil across much of the South Shore slows surface drainage, directing runoff toward foundations long after the rain stops. On the commercial side, roof-leak and sprinkler-discharge claims along the Hylan Boulevard corridor add a different category of loss — larger square footage, finished tenant spaces, and tighter timelines driven by lease obligations.

## Our Water Damage Restoration Process in Staten Island

Every job starts with a moisture survey before a single piece of equipment is placed. In Staten Island's older frame homes — particularly the early-1900s wood-construction buildings near St. George and Historic Richmond Town — water migrates through wall cavities and subfloor assemblies in ways that thermal imaging catches but a visual inspection misses. We map the full moisture boundary first, then extract standing water using truck-mounted units capable of handling the volume a flooded basement produces.

Structural drying follows the IICRC S500 standard, which sets specific psychrometric targets rather than relying on a fixed number of days. In practice, that means we're reading temperature, relative humidity, and material moisture content daily and adjusting airflow and dehumidification accordingly. Plaster walls — still common in pre-1960 homes throughout the borough — release moisture more slowly than drywall and require extended drying cycles even after surface readings look normal. We document every reading with timestamped reports, which your insurance adjuster will need for the claim file.
