## Our Smoke Damage Restoration Process in Englewood

Every job begins with a room-by-room scope assessment before any cleaning starts. We use particle counters and thermal imaging to map where smoke has traveled — often revealing contamination in closets, attic spaces, and ductwork that isn't visible to the eye. In Englewood's older East Hill homes, that thermal scan frequently shows smoke migration into wall cavities that would be missed by a visual inspection alone.

From there, the process moves through four stages:

**Dry residue removal** — Chemical sponges and HEPA-filtered vacuuming lift loose soot from ceilings, walls, and surfaces before any wet cleaning is introduced. Introducing moisture too early on a heavy soot deposit smears the residue deeper into porous materials.

**Surface cleaning and neutralization** — pH-balanced cleaning agents break down the acidic smoke film on hard surfaces. On Englewood's plaster walls, we test the surface first; aggressive alkaline cleaners can lift historic paint and damage lime-based plaster finishes that are difficult or impossible to replicate.

**Odor elimination** — Thermal fogging and hydroxyl generators run in affected spaces to reach the same porous materials — wood framing, subfloor, upholstery — that surface cleaning cannot access. In multifamily buildings, we coordinate with property managers to treat shared spaces and affected adjacent units simultaneously.

**HVAC and duct cleaning** — Smoke that enters a forced-air system redistributes every time the system runs. We seal and clean ductwork as a standard step, not an add-on, because skipping it is the most common reason odor returns weeks after a job is closed.

All drying and decontamination work follows IICRC S520 guidelines. The Restoration Group holds IICRC Certified Firm status (#210213) and is licensed as an NJ Home Improvement Contractor through the NJ Division of Consumer Affairs.
