## Our Flood Damage Restoration Process in Millburn

Every job starts with a thorough moisture mapping of the affected structure. In Millburn's older homes, that means probing beyond the obvious wet zone — water travels differently through plaster-and-lath assemblies than through modern drywall, and it migrates into wall cavities, under hardwood floors, and into subfloor systems before the surface shows any visible sign of saturation. We use thermal imaging and calibrated moisture meters to trace the full extent of intrusion before a single piece of flooring comes up.

From there, the process follows a defined sequence:

- **Extraction**: Truck-mounted and portable extractors remove standing water and pull moisture from carpet, padding, and subfloor assemblies.
- **Controlled demolition**: Saturated drywall, insulation, and flooring that cannot be dried in place are removed to the flood cut — typically 12 to 24 inches above the waterline — to expose wall cavities and allow airflow.
- **Structural drying**: Industrial desiccant dehumidifiers and high-velocity air movers are positioned according to a drying plan calibrated to the room volume and material types. In plaster-walled rooms, drying cycles run longer because plaster releases stored moisture more slowly than gypsum board.
- **Monitoring**: We return daily to log moisture readings and adjust equipment placement until all structural materials reach their target dry standard.
- **Documentation**: Every reading, photo, and equipment log is formatted for insurance submission.

As an IICRC Certified Firm (#210213), our drying protocols follow the S500 Standard for Professional Water Damage Restoration — which matters when an adjuster reviews the claim file.
