## Why East Orange's Building Stock Shapes Every Renovation

Working in ZIP codes 07017 and 07018 means working inside buildings that predate modern building codes by fifty or sixty years. Galvanized steel risers corrode from the inside out, narrowing to a fraction of their original diameter before they finally split — and when they do, water travels down through multiple floors before anyone notices. Cast-iron stacks are durable but brittle, and the vibration from decades of street traffic on Central Avenue and Main Street accelerates hairline fractures. Flat roofs on brick apartment buildings pond water at the parapet edges, and the underlying roof deck is often original wood sheathing that has absorbed and released moisture through countless freeze-thaw cycles.

Basements and finished cellars in lower-lying sections of the city flood during heavy rain events — the Second River tributaries that run through and around East Orange proved that clearly during Hurricane Ida in 2021, when low ground took on water faster than sump systems could handle it. Sewer backups compound the problem in older combined-sewer neighborhoods. All of this means renovation scopes here routinely include work that wouldn't appear on a comparable job in a newer suburb: replacing galvanized supply lines with PEX, lining or replacing cast-iron stacks, waterproofing basement walls, and rebuilding subfloor assemblies that have been wet more than once.

## Our Renovation and General Contracting Process in East Orange

Every project starts with a thorough scope assessment — not a quick walkthrough, but a systematic review of structural members, mechanical systems, and finish surfaces. ## Local Note: What Contractors Miss in East Orange's Older Multifamily Buildings

One detail that catches out-of-market contractors in East Orange's older brick buildings: the floor-to-floor framing is often a mix of original balloon-frame interior partitions inside a load-bearing masonry shell. That combination means interior walls that look non-structural can still be carrying point loads transferred from the roof through decades of settled framing. Before opening any wall in a pre-1940 three-story walk-up — especially in the Brick Church or Ampere neighborhoods where the housing stock is densest — it pays to trace the load path carefully.
