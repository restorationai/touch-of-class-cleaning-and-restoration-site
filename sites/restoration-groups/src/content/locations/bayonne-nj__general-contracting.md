## Why Bayonne's Housing Stock Makes Renovation Work Different

Most of Bayonne's residential fabric predates 1960. That means knob-and-tube or early Romex wiring tucked inside plaster-and-lath walls, cast-iron drain stacks that have been patching themselves with mineral scale for seventy years, and original galvanized supply lines that fail quietly inside finished basement ceilings. When a kitchen remodel or bathroom renovation opens up a wall in a Bergen Point rowhouse or a Constable Hook cape, it almost always uncovers at least one of these conditions — and a contractor who isn't prepared to coordinate the electrical, plumbing, and structural trades on the fly will stall the job for weeks.

The waterfront exposure compounds things. Properties near Newark Bay and the low-lying blocks that flooded during Sandy in 2012 often have basements that were rebuilt with materials that look fine on the surface but harbor residual moisture problems. A finished basement remodel in those areas isn't just a framing-and-drywall job — it requires vapor management, proper drainage rough-in, and material choices that can tolerate the humidity levels a peninsula climate produces year-round.

## Our Renovation and General Contracting Process in Bayonne

Every project starts with a scoped walkthrough, not a phone estimate. For post-damage rebuilds, we pull the existing restoration documentation — moisture logs, demolition photos, structural notes — so the rebuild scope is grounded in what was actually found, not what a homeowner remembers. For elective remodels, we walk the space with the homeowner, identify what the existing structure will and won't support, and produce a written scope before any contract is signed.

From there, the process runs in coordinated phases:

- **Permitting and inspections**: Bayonne requires permits for structural, electrical, plumbing, and HVAC work. We file with the City of Bayonne's Construction Office and schedule inspections so trades don't leapfrog each other and create rework.
- **Demolition and rough-in**: Older homes often require selective demo to expose conditions before finish trades can be scheduled. We document as we go.
- **Finish work**: Tile, cabinetry, flooring, fixtures, and paint are sequenced to protect completed work — a detail that matters in tight Bayonne rowhouses where a hallway is both the work corridor and the only path to the rest of the house.
- **Final walkthrough and punch list**: Nothing is closed out until the homeowner has signed off on every item.

We are an NJ Licensed Home Improvement Contractor through the NJ Division of Consumer Affairs, and our IICRC Certified Firm status (Firm #210213) means post-damage rebuilds follow the same documentation standards as the remediation work that preceded them.
