## Our Mold Inspection and Testing Process in Roselle

A thorough mold assessment is a two-part process: a physical inspection to locate moisture sources and visible growth, followed by laboratory-analyzed sampling to identify species and quantify spore concentrations. Here's how that unfolds on a Roselle property:

**Step 1 — Moisture mapping.** Using thermal imaging cameras and calibrated moisture meters, the inspector traces water intrusion pathways through walls, floors, and ceilings. In Roselle's older homes, this often means checking behind plaster walls in finished basements and around original cast-iron cleanouts where condensation collects.

**Step 2 — Visual assessment.** The inspector documents all visible microbial growth, staining, and water damage with photographs. Finished basements and crawl spaces receive particular attention given local flood history.

**Step 3 — Air sampling.** Non-viable spore-trap cassettes are collected at multiple indoor locations and compared against an outdoor baseline. This tells us not just whether mold is present, but whether spore counts indoors are elevated relative to the outside environment — the key metric for indoor air quality concerns.

**Step 4 — Surface sampling (when indicated).** If visible growth is present, tape-lift or swab samples are collected for species identification. Knowing whether you're dealing with Cladosporium, Aspergillus, or Stachybotrys affects the scope and protocol of any remediation that follows.

**Step 5 — Written report.** Lab results are compiled into a clear written report suitable for your insurance carrier, a prospective buyer's attorney, or a remediation contractor. The report identifies affected areas, probable moisture sources, and recommended next steps.
