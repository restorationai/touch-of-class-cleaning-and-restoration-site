## Why Irvington's Housing Stock Makes Smoke Damage Harder to Clear

Irvington's residential fabric is unlike suburban Essex County. The majority of homes in ZIP code 07111 were built before World War II, and many haven't been fully renovated since. That matters for smoke damage in a few specific ways.

First, original plaster-and-lath walls are porous in a way that modern drywall simply isn't. Smoke particles — particularly the oily, protein-based residue from structural fires — wick into plaster and can off-gas odor for weeks if the surface is only wiped down rather than properly treated. Second, balloon-frame construction, common in Irvington's prewar two- and three-family homes, creates continuous void spaces running from the basement to the attic. Smoke travels those channels and deposits residue in areas that look untouched. Third, many homes still have original cast-iron radiator systems with multiple penetrations through floors and walls — each one a pathway for smoke to migrate between units.

In multifamily buildings near Irvington Center, a fire in one unit can mean smoke odor in two or three others, which complicates both the scope of work and the insurance claim.

## Our Smoke Damage Restoration Process in Irvington

Every smoke job starts with a structured assessment before any cleaning begins. We map the fire origin, trace smoke migration paths through the structure — including those balloon-frame voids — and document all affected materials with photos and moisture readings. That documentation matters when you're filing a claim with your carrier.

From there, the process moves in a deliberate sequence:

**Ventilation and air scrubbing.** We deploy HEPA air scrubbers and negative air pressure containment to pull airborne particulates out of the structure before technicians work in the space. This is especially important in Irvington's older homes where HVAC ductwork may have distributed smoke throughout the building.

**Surface cleaning and deodorization.** Dry chemical sponges handle loose soot on ceilings and walls first. Oily residues — common after kitchen fires and structural fires involving synthetic materials — require alkaline cleaners applied in a specific sequence. Plaster surfaces that have absorbed smoke deeply may need sealing with an odor-blocking primer before any repainting.

**Thermal fogging and hydroxyl treatment.** For persistent odor in void spaces and porous materials, we use thermal fogging or hydroxyl generators to reach areas that surface cleaning can't. In a balloon-frame home, this step is not optional — it's how you eliminate odor rather than just mask it.

**Content evaluation.** Soft goods, furniture, and personal items are assessed for restorability. Items that can be cleaned are documented and inventoried; items that cannot are photographed for your insurance adjuster.
