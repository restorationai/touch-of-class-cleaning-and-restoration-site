## Why Wayne Properties Take Storm Damage Hard

Wayne's geography stacks the deck against homeowners when severe weather arrives. The Passaic and Pompton Rivers converge just south of town, and FEMA flood maps place wide swaths of the 07470 ZIP code in high-risk zones — a fact that became impossible to ignore after Tropical Storm Irene in 2011 and again when Ida dropped more than eight inches of rain in a single night in September 2021, triggering mandatory evacuations along both river corridors.

Beyond the rivers, the lake communities create their own exposure. Homes on Pines Lake and Lionshead Lake were built mostly in the 1950s through 1970s, many with finished lower levels that sit at or just below the water table. When storm surge combines with saturated ground, water doesn't just enter through window wells — it wicks through block foundation walls and rises through floor drains. The housing stock in the Preakness and Mountain View neighborhoods adds another layer: older colonials and cape cods with original roof decking, minimal attic ventilation, and gutters that were never sized for the kind of rainfall intensity the region now sees regularly.

## Our Storm Damage Restoration Process in Wayne

Every job starts with a full damage assessment before any debris is moved or materials are touched. For Wayne properties, that means checking not just the obvious — torn shingles, broken windows, downed tree limbs — but also the secondary damage that develops within 24 to 48 hours: wet insulation in attic cavities, moisture trapped between roof decking and drywall ceilings, and saturated subfloor assemblies in finished basements.

Once the scope is documented with photos and moisture readings, the process moves through four phases:

- **Emergency stabilization** — tarping compromised roof sections, boarding openings, and extracting standing water before it migrates further into wall cavities or structural framing.
- **Structural drying** — deploying commercial-grade desiccant dehumidifiers and high-velocity air movers calibrated to the square footage and construction type. IICRC S500 drying standards guide every setup; we monitor psychrometric readings daily until materials reach target moisture content.
- **Debris and damaged material removal** — including tree limb extraction from roofs and attics, removal of saturated insulation, and controlled demolition of materials that cannot be dried in place.
- **Reconstruction and finish work** — framing repairs, sheathing replacement, roofing, drywall, and interior finishes, coordinated under our NJ Licensed Home Improvement Contractor credentials so the homeowner deals with one point of contact from first call to final walkthrough.
