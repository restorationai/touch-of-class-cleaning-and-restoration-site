## Why East Brunswick Properties See Mold Issues

East Brunswick's geography sets up a recurring mold pattern that's different from drier inland suburbs. Lawrence Brook and the Farrington Lake watershed thread through low-lying sections of town, and those areas absorbed serious groundwater intrusion during Tropical Storm Irene and the remnants of Ida. Homes near the brook that took on water during those events often dried improperly — either because remediation was rushed or skipped entirely — leaving dormant mold colonies behind wall cavities and beneath flooring that re-activate when humidity climbs in summer.

The housing stock compounds the risk. The 1950s–70s ranches and colonials throughout neighborhoods like Farrington and Tanglewood were built with minimal attic ventilation by today's standards, and many have had bathroom fans routed into the attic rather than through the roof. That mistake turns attic sheathing into a mold substrate over time. Newer townhome and condo clusters add a different problem: shared wall assemblies mean a slow leak in one unit can drive moisture — and eventually mold — into an adjacent unit before either owner notices anything.

## Our Mold Remediation Process in East Brunswick

Every job starts with a visual inspection and moisture mapping using thermal imaging and pin-type meters. In East Brunswick's older finished basements, we pay particular attention to the perimeter where block foundations meet the slab — that joint is a chronic moisture pathway that standard visual checks miss.

Once we've defined the affected area, we establish negative-air containment using 6-mil poly barriers and HEPA-filtered air scrubbers, which keeps spores from migrating to unaffected living spaces during removal. Affected drywall, insulation, and wood framing are removed to clean margins, and all surfaces are HEPA-vacuumed before an EPA-registered antimicrobial treatment is applied. We follow the IICRC S520 standard for mold remediation throughout — that's the same protocol that drives post-remediation clearance testing.

Drying and reconstruction are scheduled in sequence, not simultaneously. Rushing the drying phase is the single most common reason mold returns, and we don't close a job until moisture readings confirm the structure is back to normal range.
