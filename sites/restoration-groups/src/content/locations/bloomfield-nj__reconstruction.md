Galvanized supply lines and cast-iron waste stacks — standard in Bloomfield homes built between 1910 and 1950 — corrode on a predictable schedule. It requires rebuilding to current code, which in New Jersey means updated framing fasteners, vapor barriers, and in many cases egress window upgrades if a basement bedroom is involved.

## Our Reconstruction Process in Bloomfield

Every reconstruction project begins with a detailed scope-of-loss document that separates emergency stabilization work — temporary shoring, board-up, roof tarping — from the permitted rebuild phase. For Bloomfield properties, that distinction matters because Essex County permit timelines can affect how quickly framing and mechanical work can begin; we submit permit applications as early as possible to avoid scheduling gaps.

Once permits are in hand, the rebuild sequence follows the damage type. For fire-damaged structures, that means removing char and smoke-affected framing down to sound material, replacing structural members, and then working through insulation, rough mechanicals, drywall, and finishes in a single managed progression. For flood-damaged basements — a common call in the Watsessing and Brookdale areas — it means addressing any remaining moisture in the slab and foundation walls before framing begins, so the new assembly doesn't trap residual humidity behind fresh drywall.

Throughout the process, an IICRC Certified Firm (#210213) handles the moisture verification side, ensuring that drying standards are met before reconstruction materials go in. This matters especially in Bloomfield's older homes, where original masonry foundations can continue to release moisture for weeks after a flood event.

## Local Note: Balloon Framing and Hidden Void Spaces

One thing that catches out-of-area contractors in Bloomfield's pre-1940 housing stock is balloon framing — a construction method where exterior wall studs run continuously from the foundation sill to the roof rafters without the fire-blocking present in modern platform framing. In a fire loss, flames travel vertically through these open stud bays with almost no resistance, meaning the visible char on a first-floor wall often understates how far the damage has traveled into the second floor and attic. On reconstruction jobs in the Watsessing and Brookdale neighborhoods especially, we open wall cavities beyond the obvious burn line to verify the actual extent before framing begins — a step that prevents costly surprises mid-project and gives the insurance adjuster an accurate picture of the true scope.
