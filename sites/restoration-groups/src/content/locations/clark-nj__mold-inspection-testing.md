## Why Clark Properties See Elevated Mold Risk

Clark's geography does a lot of the work for mold. Robinson's Branch, the tributary that threads through low ground near Lake Avenue, keeps the water table close to the surface in a wide swath of the township. When sump pumps fail during a summer downpour — and in Clark's clay-heavy soil, they fail more often than homeowners expect — that moisture doesn't just drain away. It lingers in wall cavities, under subfloor sheathing, and behind the drywall that was used to finish basements in the 1970s and 1980s renovation wave.

The aging infrastructure compounds the problem. Many of the ranches and split-levels built in the 1950s and 1960s still run original cast-iron drain lines and copper supply lines. Cast iron corrodes at joints; copper develops pinhole leaks at fittings. Either failure can feed a slow, hidden moisture source for months before it's visible — and mold can begin colonizing a wet surface in as little as 24 to 48 hours. By the time a homeowner notices staining or odor, the colony is often well established behind finished surfaces.

The Raritan Road business district and the Westfield Avenue corridor add a commercial dimension. Restaurant kitchens, retail storage rooms, and office suites in those strips share the same moisture vulnerability as residential properties, with the added complication of food odors masking early mold indicators.

## Our Mold Inspection and Testing Process in Clark

A mold assessment isn't a single swab and a phone call. The process starts with a thorough visual inspection of the areas most likely to harbor growth in a Clark home: the basement perimeter, the rim joist cavity, under-sink cabinets, and any wall or ceiling surface that was wet during a past flood or leak event. We use thermal imaging to identify temperature differentials that indicate moisture behind finished surfaces — a critical step in homes where the original 1960s framing is now buried under decades of renovation layers.

Air sampling for mold spores is collected at multiple points: typically one outdoor baseline sample and two or more indoor samples in areas of concern. Tape-lift or swab samples are taken from any visible growth to identify the genus and species present. All samples are submitted to an accredited third-party laboratory. The written report we provide includes spore counts by species, a comparison to outdoor baseline levels, and a clear statement of whether remediation is warranted — language that holds up with insurance adjusters and real estate attorneys alike.

For indoor air quality testing beyond mold, we can extend sampling to include VOCs and other particulates, which is relevant for properties near the Raritan Road commercial corridor where prior tenant activities may have left residual contamination.
