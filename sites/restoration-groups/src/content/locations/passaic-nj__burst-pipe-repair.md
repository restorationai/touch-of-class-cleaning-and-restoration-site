## Why Passaic Properties Are Especially Vulnerable to Burst Pipes

Passaic is one of the densest small cities in New Jersey, and its housing stock reflects that history. The overwhelming majority of residential buildings in the 07055 ZIP code were constructed before 1950 — two- and three-family walkups with galvanized steel supply lines that have been corroding quietly for decades, cast-iron drains that crack under root pressure or ground shift, and in some older units, remnants of knob-and-tube wiring that complicate any wall-opening work. When those galvanized pipes finally fail, they don't leak slowly — they burst.

The city's position on the Passaic River adds another layer of risk. Groundwater tables in low-lying blocks near the riverfront stay elevated for extended periods after heavy rain, increasing hydrostatic pressure on basement walls and floor slabs. Residents in the Third Ward and Fourth Ward know this pattern well — Ida in 2021 and Irene in 2011 both inundated riverfront blocks and left basements saturated for days. That persistent moisture accelerates corrosion on older supply lines and weakens the joints most likely to fail during a hard freeze.

Winter is the most dangerous season. When temperatures drop into the teens — which happens several times each January and February in northern New Jersey — uninsulated pipes running through exterior walls, unheated crawl spaces, or the cold side of a garage are at serious risk. A pipe that freezes overnight and bursts before dawn can discharge hundreds of gallons before anyone notices.

## Our Burst Pipe Cleanup and Repair Process in Passaic

The first priority when we arrive is stopping the water at its source. In multi-family buildings — the dominant housing type throughout Downtown Passaic and along the Main Avenue corridor — that often means locating a shared shutoff in a utility room or basement that tenants may not know exists. We carry shutoff keys and work with building owners to isolate the break without cutting water to the entire structure any longer than necessary.

Once the flow is stopped, we assess the full extent of the damage using moisture meters and thermal imaging cameras. In Passaic's older plaster-and-lath construction, water migrates further than it appears on the surface — a burst pipe on the second floor can saturate the subfloor, the first-floor ceiling, and the top of a basement wall before the visible staining catches up. We map the wet zone completely before any drying equipment is placed.

Extraction comes next: truck-mounted and portable units pull standing water from floors, and desiccant or refrigerant dehumidifiers are positioned to draw moisture out of structural cavities. Air movers are placed in patterns calibrated to the room geometry — important in the narrow, compartmentalized layouts common to Passaic's older walkups. Drying typically runs three to five days, with daily moisture readings to confirm progress and adjust equipment placement.

Structural repairs — replacing burst copper or galvanized sections, patching drywall or plaster, restoring flooring — are handled by our licensed crews under our NJ Licensed Home Improvement Contractor registration, so the job doesn't hand off to a separate contractor mid-stream.

## Insurance Documentation for Passaic Burst Pipe Claims

Most homeowner and landlord policies cover sudden and accidental pipe bursts — the key word being sudden. Gradual leaks that went unaddressed are frequently disputed by carriers. We photograph the break point, the affected materials, and the moisture readings at intake, creating a documented record that supports your adjuster's assessment. We work directly with most major carriers and can provide a detailed scope of loss in the format adjusters expect, which shortens the back-and-forth on multi-unit claims — a common scenario in Passaic's rental-heavy building stock.

## Local Note

In Passaic's older multi-family buildings — particularly the two- and three-families densely packed between Pulaski Park and the Prospect Park border — balloon-frame wall cavities run uninterrupted from the basement sill plate to the roof rafters. When a pipe bursts inside one of those walls, water channels the full height of the building before it hits a horizontal fire block (if one exists at all). We've learned to probe these walls from the basement up, not just at the floor where the staining appears, because the actual saturation zone is almost always taller than it looks. Missing that upper moisture pocket is one of the most common reasons secondary mold problems develop weeks after a "completed" dry-out in this housing type.
