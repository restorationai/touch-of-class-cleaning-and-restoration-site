## Our Water Damage Restoration Process in Bayonne

Every loss starts with a thorough inspection using moisture meters and thermal imaging. In Bayonne's older frame construction, that step matters more than it does in newer builds — water travels unpredictably through plaster and lathe, original hardwood subfloors, and uninsulated rim joists. We map the full moisture boundary before any equipment is placed, so drying targets are accurate rather than estimated.

Extraction comes next. For standing water in a finished basement, truck-mounted extraction units pull volume quickly. For water that has migrated into wall cavities — common in the brick rowhomes and postwar capes throughout Bayonne — we use injectidry systems and wall cavity drying panels to move air through enclosed spaces without unnecessary demolition.

Structural drying follows IICRC S500 standards, with daily moisture readings logged to document progress. Drying timelines in Bayonne's older plaster-and-lathe homes typically run longer than in modern drywall construction — plaster holds moisture and releases it slowly, which means equipment stays on-site until readings confirm the structure has returned to acceptable equilibrium moisture content, not just until surfaces feel dry to the touch.
