## Why Garfield Properties See Water Damage So Often

Garfield's housing stock was built for density, not drainage. Most lots in neighborhoods like Belmont and Plauderville are narrow, with homes set close together and downspouts that discharge onto shared driveways or directly against foundation walls. Cast-iron drain lines — common in homes built before the 1960s — corrode from the inside out, and a hairline crack in a stack inside a finished basement wall can go undetected until a wet season pushes enough pressure through the system to cause a visible failure.

The Saddle River tributaries that feed into the Passaic add another layer of risk. Low-lying blocks away from the river itself can still collect runoff after a hard storm, especially where the grade has been altered by decades of additions, paved yards, and infill construction. Finished basements — a near-universal feature in Garfield's postwar capes and two-families — turn what might be a crawl-space nuisance elsewhere into a full living-space loss.

Along the Passaic Street corridor and Belmont Avenue, the pattern shifts toward commercial and mixed-use losses: overnight pipe failures in retail spaces, roof-drain backups in flat-roof buildings, and shared-wall water migration in attached structures where one unit's leak becomes a neighbor's ceiling stain.

## Our Water Cleanup Process in Garfield

Water cleanup in Garfield's older housing stock requires a different approach than a newer suburban build. Modern drywall wicks moisture quickly and dries relatively fast. Plaster-and-lath walls — still common in pre-1950 Garfield homes — absorb water more slowly but hold it longer and deeper. Surface readings on a moisture meter can look acceptable while the lath behind the plaster stays saturated. We use thermal imaging alongside pin and pinless meters to map moisture through wall assemblies before we set drying equipment, so we're not pulling gear based on surface numbers alone.

The process follows the IICRC S500 standard for water damage restoration:

- **Extraction first** — standing water is removed with truck-mounted and portable extractors before any drying equipment is placed. In finished basements, this includes water trapped under carpet and pad.
- **Moisture mapping** — walls, floors, and structural cavities are documented with readings at multiple depths so drying targets are based on actual conditions, not estimates.
- **Drying system placement** — commercial-grade desiccant or refrigerant dehumidifiers and high-velocity air movers are positioned based on the moisture map, not a generic room layout.
- **Daily monitoring** — readings are logged each day so the drying curve is tracked and equipment is adjusted or removed based on data.
- **Documentation for insurance** — every reading, photo, and equipment placement is recorded in a format carriers recognize, which matters when you're filing a claim through a standard homeowner's policy.

Because we operate 24/7, we can begin extraction the same night a loss is reported — which is often the difference between saving flooring and replacing it.
