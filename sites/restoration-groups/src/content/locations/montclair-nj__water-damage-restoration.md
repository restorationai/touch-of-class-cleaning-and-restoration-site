## Why Montclair Properties See Water Damage Differently

Montclair's charm is inseparable from its vulnerability. The grand Victorians and Tudor revivals that line the streets around Upper Montclair and the Estate Section were built with plaster walls over wood lath, cast-iron drain stacks, and slate roofs that have been patched and re-patched for decades. When an aging supply line finally gives way inside a plaster wall, the wall doesn't sag or bubble the way drywall does — it holds moisture invisibly for days while the framing behind it saturates. By the time a homeowner notices a soft spot or a stain, the water has often traveled two or three floor cavities down.

The hillside grading off the Watchung ridge compounds the problem. ## Our Water Damage Restoration Process in Montclair

Every job starts with a moisture mapping inspection — thermal imaging and calibrated moisture meters across walls, subfloors, and ceiling cavities — before a single piece of equipment is placed. In Montclair's older homes, that step is non-negotiable. Water in a plaster-and-lath wall reads completely differently than water in half-inch drywall, and setting up drying equipment without knowing the true wet boundary wastes time and misses pockets of saturation that will feed mold growth within 48 to 72 hours.

Once the scope is mapped, water extraction comes first: truck-mounted and portable extractors pull standing water from flooring and subfloor assemblies, including the tongue-and-groove hardwood floors common in pre-1950 Montclair construction. We then establish a drying system calibrated to the structure — commercial-grade LGR dehumidifiers, high-velocity air movers positioned to create directed airflow through wall cavities, and in-wall drying panels for plaster assemblies where opening the wall isn't immediately necessary. Moisture readings are logged at every visit so drying progress is documented, not estimated.

Where sewer backup is involved — a frequent scenario in the South End and Frog Hollow after heavy rain — affected materials are handled under Category 3 (black water) protocols, which means additional containment, appropriate PPE, and antimicrobial treatment of structural surfaces before any drying begins. This is not optional; it's the IICRC S500 standard, and skipping it creates a liability problem for the homeowner down the road.

## Local Note

One thing that catches out-of-area crews in Montclair: plaster walls in the older Victorians and Tudors don't respond to standard drying timelines. Modern half-inch drywall typically reaches drying goals in three to five days under proper airflow. Original plaster — which can be an inch and a half thick over wood lath — releases moisture slowly and unevenly, and it's not uncommon for drying to run seven to ten days in a heavily saturated wall cavity. Pulling equipment too early because the surface reads dry is a mistake; the lath behind it may still be holding enough moisture to sustain mold colonization. We extend monitoring cycles on plaster assemblies and don't close out a job until subsurface readings confirm the entire assembly is dry.
