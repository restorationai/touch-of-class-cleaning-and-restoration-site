## Why Wayne Commercial Properties Face Elevated Risk

The geography here is unforgiving. A single failed sprinkler head in a retail bay can discharge hundreds of gallons before the system is isolated — saturating inventory, subfloor assemblies, and adjacent tenant spaces simultaneously.

## Our Commercial Restoration Process in Wayne

Commercial losses are operationally different from residential ones. Tenants need partial access maintained, inventory must be documented before it moves, and building owners often have lease obligations that affect how quickly a space must be returned to service. The process is structured around those constraints.

The first step on arrival is containment and documentation — moisture mapping with thermal imaging across the full affected footprint, photographic inventory of damaged materials, and identification of any secondary systems (HVAC ducts, electrical panels, elevator shafts) that may have been compromised. For multi-tenant retail or office buildings, we isolate the loss area without cutting off adjacent tenants where structurally possible.

Drying in commercial spaces requires industrial-scale equipment: large-capacity desiccant or refrigerant dehumidifiers, high-velocity axial air movers, and in some cases negative air pressure containment to prevent cross-contamination during mold-risk situations. Drying timelines are validated against the IICRC S500 standard for water damage and documented daily — that documentation matters when a property manager is reporting to an insurance adjuster or a commercial landlord is tracking a tenant's business interruption claim.

For fire and smoke losses — common in restaurant and kitchen-adjacent commercial spaces — odor neutralization and structural cleaning follow a separate protocol. Smoke residue in HVAC ductwork can redistribute odor throughout an entire building long after visible soot is cleaned; duct cleaning and sealing is part of the scope, not an afterthought.
