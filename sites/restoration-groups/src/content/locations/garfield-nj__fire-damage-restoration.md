## Why Garfield's Housing Stock Complicates Fire Damage

Garfield's residential neighborhoods — particularly the dense blocks running off Passaic Street and through the Plauderville section — were built primarily between 1910 and 1955. That era of construction means balloon-frame walls with no fire blocking between floors, original horsehair plaster over wood lath, and cast-iron drain stacks that have been patched and re-patched for decades. In a fire, balloon framing acts like a chimney: flames and superheated gases travel vertically through wall cavities from basement to attic with little resistance, which is why even a contained kitchen fire can leave smoke damage two floors up.

Older plaster walls also hold odor compounds differently than modern drywall. The lime-and-sand matrix is porous, and smoke particulates — especially the oily residue from burning synthetic materials common in postwar kitchens — bond deeply into the surface. Cleaning these walls requires pH-matched chemical sponges and dry-chemical soot removal before any wet cleaning, or you drive the residue further in. Rushing that step is one of the most common reasons homeowners in 07026 end up with lingering odor six months after a fire.

## Our Fire Damage Restoration Process in Garfield

Every job starts with a structured assessment before any cleaning begins. We document the fire origin point, map smoke migration paths through the structure, and identify materials by composition — original plaster, wood paneling, vinyl flooring, fiberglass insulation — because each requires a different cleaning protocol. Soot on a painted plaster ceiling in a 1920s Garfield two-family is not treated the same way as soot on a foam-backed carpet in a finished basement.

From there, the process moves in a deliberate sequence:

- **Emergency board-up and tarping** to secure the structure and prevent weather intrusion through fire-compromised roof or wall sections
- **Dry soot removal** using HEPA-filtered vacuums and dry chemical sponges on walls, ceilings, and structural members before any moisture is introduced
- **Wet cleaning and deodorization** with alkaline or acidic agents matched to the soot type — protein-based residue from kitchen fires needs a different chemistry than petroleum-based residue from electrical fires
- **Thermal fogging or hydroxyl treatment** for deep odor neutralization in wall cavities and HVAC pathways
- **Structural drying** if firefighting water is present, coordinated with soot remediation so neither process undermines the other
- **Controlled demolition and rebuild** of materials that cannot be cleaned to pre-loss condition, documented for your insurance adjuster

All work is performed by our IICRC Certified Firm (#210213) technicians and is licensed under the NJ Division of Consumer Affairs as a Home Improvement Contractor — which matters when the scope crosses into structural repairs.
