## Why Irvington Properties See Basement Flooding Issues

Irvington's housing stock is one of the oldest in Essex County. Most of the rowhouses and prewar frame homes throughout the 07111 ZIP code were built before modern drainage standards, with cast-iron drain lines that corrode and collapse over decades and galvanized supply pipes that fail suddenly — often overnight, when no one notices until morning. Finished basements are common in these homes because families converted them for extra living space, which means a single pipe failure or a backed-up floor drain doesn't just wet a concrete slab — it saturates drywall, carpet, drop ceilings, and stored belongings.

Storm events compound the problem. When Ida swept through in September 2021, widespread basement flooding hit Essex County hard, and Irvington's low-lying streets took some of the worst of it. The combination of aging municipal storm infrastructure, heavy clay soil that slows absorption, and tightly packed housing leaves little margin when the rain comes fast. Sump pump failures during power outages are another recurring cause — a pump that runs fine all year can fall behind in a 2-inch-per-hour rain event.

## Our Basement Flooding Cleanup Process in Irvington

The first priority when we arrive is stopping the source — whether that's a burst galvanized line, a failed sump, or groundwater intrusion through a cracked foundation wall. Once the water source is controlled, we use truck-mounted and portable extraction units to pull standing water from the floor, then transition to targeted drying with industrial air movers and desiccant or refrigerant dehumidifiers calibrated to the basement's square footage and the ambient humidity typical of a New Jersey summer or winter.

In Irvington's older homes, we pay close attention to what's behind the walls. Many finished basements in this housing stock used 1×3 furring strips and plaster board rather than modern fiberglass-batted stud walls — materials that hold moisture differently and require longer drying cycles. We use thermal imaging cameras and penetrating moisture meters to map the wet zone accurately before we close anything up. Drying is documented against the IICRC S500 standard so your insurance adjuster has a clear record of scope and completion.

When structural materials — subfloor, framing, drywall — have absorbed enough water that drying in place isn't viable, we perform controlled demolition, removing only what can't be saved and leaving the rest. We photograph and document every step, which matters when you're filing a claim.
