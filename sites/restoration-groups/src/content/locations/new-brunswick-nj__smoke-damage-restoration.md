## Our Smoke Damage Restoration Process in New Brunswick

Every smoke damage job starts with a structured assessment rather than a visual sweep. Technicians use thermal imaging and air sampling to map where smoke has traveled — especially important in the multifamily rowhouses common through the Fifth Ward, where a fire in one unit can push smoke into shared wall cavities and ceiling voids serving neighboring apartments.

From there, the process moves through four core phases:

**Containment and ventilation** — Negative air pressure is established to prevent cross-contamination while fresh-air exchange begins drawing smoke particulates out of the structure.

**Dry and wet chemical sponge cleaning** — Soot type determines the method. Protein-based residues from kitchen fires (common in the dense rental stock near campus) require enzymatic treatments, while petroleum-based soot from electrical fires responds better to chemical sponges and alkaline cleaners.

**HVAC and ductwork decontamination** — In older New Brunswick buildings with original forced-air or steam systems, ductwork is a primary smoke reservoir. We clean or seal register openings and assess whether duct lining needs replacement.

**Thermal fogging and hydroxyl treatment** — Odor molecules that have bonded to porous materials — wood framing, subflooring, plaster — are neutralized using hydroxyl generators or thermal fogging, depending on occupancy status and material sensitivity.
