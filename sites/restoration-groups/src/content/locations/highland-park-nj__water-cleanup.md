## Why Highland Park Properties See Water Damage Differently

Highland Park's housing stock is part of what makes water events here particularly stubborn. Many of the colonials and two-family homes built between the wars still carry original galvanized supply lines and cast-iron drain stacks. Galvanized pipe corrodes from the inside out — the exterior can look intact while the interior bore has narrowed to a fraction of its original diameter, building pressure until a joint lets go. When that happens inside a finished basement, water spreads under laminate flooring and behind drywall before anyone notices the smell.

The borough's topography compounds the risk. Livingston Manor and the Triangle neighborhood sit close enough to the Raritan floodplain that a sustained rain event raises the water table quickly, pushing groundwater through foundation cracks and floor drains even when the storm itself doesn't produce visible surface flooding. That kind of hydrostatic intrusion is slower and quieter than a burst pipe, but it saturates concrete block and the framing sitting on top of it just as thoroughly.

Commercial properties along the Raritan Avenue business district face a different pattern: aging sprinkler systems and commercial dishwasher lines that, when they fail, can flood a dining room or retail floor in minutes and wick into adjacent tenant spaces through shared walls.

## Our Water Cleanup Process in Highland Park

The first priority on any water call is stopping ongoing intrusion — shutting the source, whether that's a supply valve, a sump that's overwhelmed, or a drain that's backed up. Once the water is no longer entering, the extraction phase begins using truck-mounted and portable units capable of pulling standing water from finished floors, carpet, and low-lying crawl spaces.

After bulk extraction, the work shifts to what you can't see. In Highland Park's older homes, that means probing plaster walls and checking the lath behind them — plaster absorbs water more slowly than modern drywall but releases it more slowly too, which means drying timelines here often run longer than in newer construction. Thermal imaging cameras help locate moisture pockets behind finished surfaces without unnecessary demolition. Where materials are beyond recovery, controlled removal creates the airflow channels that allow structural drying to proceed efficiently.

Drying equipment — industrial air movers and refrigerant or desiccant dehumidifiers — is calibrated to the specific moisture readings taken at the start and monitored daily until the structure reaches documented dry standard per the IICRC S500. Every reading is logged, which matters when you're filing an insurance claim.

## Local Note: Finished Basements and Hidden Moisture in Pre-War Homes

One thing that comes up repeatedly in Highland Park's older housing stock: finished basements that were renovated decades after original construction often have a layer of rigid foam or fiberglass batt tucked between the block foundation wall and a wood-framed interior wall. When water intrudes through the block — common during high water-table events in the Meadow and Livingston Manor areas — that insulation traps moisture against the framing where you can't see it. Standard surface moisture readings come back low while the cavity stays wet for weeks. Probing and thermal imaging behind the finished wall is the only reliable way to catch it, and skipping that step is how a manageable water event becomes a mold remediation job two months later.
