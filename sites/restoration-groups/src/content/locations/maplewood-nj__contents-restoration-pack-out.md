## Why Maplewood Homes Face Distinctive Contents Risks

The housing stock along the Hilton and Jefferson neighborhoods — many of them built between 1910 and the 1940s — wasn't designed with modern flood resilience in mind. Stone foundations with original mortar allow groundwater to seep through during prolonged rain events, and finished basements that were converted decades after original construction often hold the things families care about most: media rooms, wine storage, vintage furniture, file archives. Cast-iron drain systems that have been in service for 80 or more years are prone to back-ups when storm volumes overwhelm municipal capacity, and when they fail, the water carries contaminants that make contents recovery more complex than a clean-water loss.

Plaster walls — still original in many homes near Maplewood Village — also behave differently from modern drywall. They absorb moisture more slowly but hold it longer, which means contents stored against interior walls can show secondary damage days after the initial event if the structure isn't drying fast enough. That slow moisture migration can reach furniture, artwork, and electronics that looked unaffected on day one.

## Our Contents Restoration & Pack-Out Process in Maplewood

The process starts with a detailed room-by-room inventory before anything is moved. Every item is photographed, catalogued by category and condition, and assigned a tracking number — documentation that becomes part of your insurance file. Items that can be safely restored on-site stay in place. Everything else is carefully packed, loaded, and transported to a climate-controlled facility where the actual restoration work happens.

At the facility, contents go through a triage process: textiles to ozone and thermal treatment for odor, hard goods to ultrasonic cleaning where appropriate, documents and photographs to freeze-drying if water exposure has been significant. Electronics are assessed by technicians before any power is applied. Once items are cleaned, dried, and documented as restored, they're stored until your home is structurally ready — then returned and placed back in the rooms they came from, with the same inventory used to verify every item.

For losses tied to contaminated water (a sewer back-up through an aging cast-iron line, for example), the pack-out crew works in appropriate PPE and separates potentially affected items from clean ones from the first moment of contact.
