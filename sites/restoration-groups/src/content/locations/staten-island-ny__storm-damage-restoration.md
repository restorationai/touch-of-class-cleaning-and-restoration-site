## Why Staten Island Properties See Repeated Storm Damage

The borough's housing stock creates compounding problems after severe weather. The dense rows of postwar Cape Cods and split-levels in Great Kills and New Dorp were built before modern wind-load standards, and their shallow roof pitches shed water poorly when gutters clog with debris. Further north, the early-1900s wood-frame homes near Port Richmond carry aging cast-iron drainage that backs up fast when storm runoff overwhelms the system — a burst or cracked line during a heavy event can turn a roof-leak call into a combined wind-and-water loss inside a few hours.

The island's position at the confluence of the Arthur Kill, Kill Van Kull, and Lower New York Bay means storm surge and tidal flooding aren't abstract risks. Neighborhoods below the 10-foot elevation line face repeated exposure, and the clay-heavy soil in many South Shore areas doesn't drain quickly, keeping crawlspace moisture elevated for days after the storm passes. That sustained dampness is exactly the window — 24 to 48 hours — in which mold colonization begins on wood framing and insulation.

## Our Storm Damage Restoration Process in Staten Island

Every job starts with a full exterior and interior assessment before any debris moves. Wind damage to a roof in the 10306 ZIP code often looks contained from the street but has driven water into the attic insulation and down interior wall cavities — we use thermal imaging and moisture meters to map the actual loss boundary before scoping the repair.

From there, the process follows a consistent sequence calibrated to what we find on Staten Island properties:

- **Emergency tarping and board-up** — roof breaches and broken windows are sealed to stop active water intrusion, typically within hours of your call.
- **Structural drying** — commercial-grade desiccant dehumidifiers and high-velocity air movers are positioned based on the moisture map, not guesswork. In crawlspace-heavy South Shore homes, we deploy dedicated crawlspace drying systems rather than relying on equipment staged above the subfloor.
- **Debris removal and damage documentation** — every affected material is photographed and itemized in a format your insurance adjuster can use directly.
- **Reconstruction coordination** — once the structure is dry and stable, our crews handle framing, roofing, drywall, and finish work so you're dealing with one point of contact, not three separate contractors.

The Restoration Group is an IICRC Certified Firm (#210213), and all drying work is performed to the IICRC S500 and S520 standards — documentation that matters when your carrier reviews the claim.

Storm claims on Staten Island frequently involve multiple damage types — wind, water intrusion, and sometimes a downed tree through a roof — and carriers often want to separate the losses for coverage purposes.
