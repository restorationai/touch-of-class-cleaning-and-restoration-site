## Why Somerville Properties See Water Damage Issues

Somerville is Somerset County's compact seat, and its building history is written in its pipes. Most of the residential blocks radiating out from the Somerset County Courthouse were built between 1900 and 1950. When those systems fail — and they do, especially after a hard freeze or a prolonged dry spell followed by heavy rain — water moves fast through plaster-and-lath walls and old-growth subfloor.

## Our Water Damage Restoration Process in Somerville

Every job starts with moisture mapping, not assumptions. We use thermal imaging and calibrated moisture meters to trace where water has actually traveled — not just where it's visible. In Somerville's older homes, water routinely migrates behind plaster walls and under original hardwood floors before it shows on the surface. Knowing the true boundary of saturation determines how much material needs to come out and how many drying days to plan for.

Once the scope is confirmed, extraction comes first. High-capacity truck-mounted and portable extractors pull standing water and saturated material from floors, subfloor assemblies, and wall cavities. We then set a drying system calibrated to the structure — axial and desiccant air movers positioned to create directed airflow through wall cavities, paired with commercial dehumidifiers sized to the actual cubic footage being dried, not a rule-of-thumb estimate. Drying is monitored daily with documented moisture readings so the record is clear for your insurer and, if needed, for any future buyer inspection.

For Category 2 or Category 3 losses — sewage backup, floodwater from Peters Brook, or contaminated sprinkler discharge — affected porous materials are removed and the structure is treated with an EPA-registered antimicrobial before drying begins. We do not dry contaminated assemblies in place.

## Somerville Insurance Coordination

Water damage claims in Somerset County follow standard HO-3 and commercial property policy structures, but the details matter. Flood losses — water that enters from outside the structure, as happened to many Somerville properties during Ida — are typically excluded from standard homeowners policies and fall under separate NFIP or private flood coverage. Supply-line failures and appliance leaks are usually covered, subject to deductible and policy language around maintenance.
