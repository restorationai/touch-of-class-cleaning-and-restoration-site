## Our Water Damage Restoration Process in Paramus

Every job starts with a moisture survey before any equipment is placed. We use thermal imaging cameras and penetrating moisture meters to map exactly where water has traveled — because in a finished Paramus basement, water that entered near the sump pit may have wicked 20 feet under laminate flooring before it becomes visible. That map drives the extraction and drying plan, not guesswork.

Extraction comes first: truck-mounted and portable units pull standing water from carpet, subfloor, and hard-surface flooring. Once bulk water is removed, we set a calibrated drying system — commercial-grade desiccant or refrigerant dehumidifiers paired with high-velocity air movers — sized to the affected square footage and the specific materials involved. Drying targets follow the IICRC S500 standard, and we log temperature, relative humidity, and material moisture readings on every visit until the structure reaches documented dry standard.

Demolition is limited to what's genuinely unsalvageable. We remove wet insulation, saturated drywall, and compromised flooring, but we don't tear out materials that can be dried in place — a distinction that matters when you're filing an insurance claim and need documented justification for every line item.

Most sudden water losses in Paramus — burst pipes, appliance failures, accidental discharges — are covered events under standard homeowners policies. ## Local Note

Paramus's 1950s and 60s split-levels frequently have a quirk that slows drying: the half-level below grade was often finished with furring strips and paneling directly against the concrete block wall, with no vapor barrier and minimal airspace.
