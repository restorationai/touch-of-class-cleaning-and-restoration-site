## Why Roselle's Housing Stock Complicates Fire Damage

Roselle's building inventory is its charm and its challenge. Most of the residential blocks in East Roselle and the streets running off Fifth Avenue were built between 1910 and 1955, when balloon-frame construction was standard. In balloon framing, stud cavities run continuously from the foundation sill to the roof ridge — no fire blocking, no horizontal barrier. When a kitchen fire vents upward, smoke travels those open channels to the attic in minutes, depositing oily soot on rafters and insulation that homeowners never see until the smell returns weeks later.

Original galvanized supply lines and cast-iron waste stacks are common in these homes, and a fire suppression event — whether from the homeowner's extinguisher or the Roselle Fire Department's hose lines — can push hundreds of gallons of water into finished basements and crawl spaces that were already marginal before the fire. ## Our Fire Damage Restoration Process in Roselle

Every job starts with a same-visit scope assessment. Our IICRC Certified Firm (#210213) technicians document structural char, map smoke migration through wall cavities and HVAC ductwork, and test surface pH to determine which soot residue type — dry, wet, or protein-based — is present. Protein soot from kitchen fires is nearly invisible but produces the most persistent odor; it bonds to painted surfaces and cabinet interiors and requires enzymatic treatment, not just wiping.

After board-up and emergency stabilization, we extract standing suppression water, then begin the structural drying phase before any smoke remediation starts — wet materials hold odor compounds and prevent encapsulants from bonding. Thermal fogging and hydroxyl generation run concurrently with content pack-out, so restorable items leave the structure while deodorization is active. We coordinate directly with your insurance adjuster throughout, providing photo documentation, moisture logs, and line-item scoping in the format most carriers require.
