## Our Contents Restoration & Pack-Out Process

The process begins with a room-by-room inventory before a single box is moved. Every item is photographed, described, and assigned a condition code — this documentation is the backbone of your insurance claim and your guarantee that nothing is lost in transit. Items are then sorted into three streams: restore, clean-and-store, or total loss. Only items that genuinely cannot be brought back to pre-loss condition are flagged as losses.

Pack-out moves your belongings to a climate-controlled facility where technicians work through each category: ultrasonic cleaning for hard goods and non-porous items, ozone and hydroxyl treatment for soft goods and documents carrying smoke or microbial odor, freeze-drying for water-saturated papers and photographs. Electronics are evaluated by technicians before any power is applied — energizing a wet circuit board destroys it. Throughout this phase, your inventory stays live and accessible so your adjuster can review it without a site visit.

Once structural drying and any necessary remediation is complete at your Piscataway address, contents are returned in the same documented order they left — nothing is unboxed until you've confirmed placement.

## Piscataway Insurance & HOA Coordination

Contents claims in Piscataway follow a few predictable patterns worth knowing before you file. Riverine flood losses — the kind that come off the Raritan — are typically covered under a separate NFIP or private flood policy, not a standard homeowner's policy. That distinction matters for pack-out documentation: flood adjusters and property adjusters use different forms, and the inventory needs to be structured to satisfy both if a loss straddles both policies.

For Society Hill and similar HOA communities, the master policy usually covers the building envelope while the unit owner's HO-6 covers personal property and interior finishes. Pack-out documentation that clearly separates structural contents (built-ins, cabinetry) from personal property simplifies the coverage split and prevents items from falling into a gap between the two policies. The Restoration Group works directly with adjusters on both sides of that line.

## Local Note

Homes in the older Possumtown and New Market tracts — many built in the postwar decades — frequently have cast-iron drain stacks that have partially collapsed or are running at reduced capacity. When a sewage backup or drain overflow occurs in these finished basements, the contents damage carries a Category 3 contamination designation, which changes the restoration calculus significantly: porous soft goods that have contacted sewage-affected water generally cannot be restored to a safe condition regardless of cleaning method. Identifying this early during pack-out prevents the frustrating situation where a homeowner expects cleaned furniture to be returned and the adjuster later disputes the total-loss claim. Flagging contamination category at intake, before any cleaning is attempted, protects both the claim and the homeowner's expectations.
