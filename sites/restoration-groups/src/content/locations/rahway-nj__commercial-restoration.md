## Our Commercial Restoration Process in Rahway

Commercial restoration isn't just residential work scaled up — the documentation requirements, the tenant coordination, and the pressure to reopen on a business timeline all demand a structured approach. When we arrive at a Rahway commercial property, the first hour is spent on containment and documentation: thermal imaging to map moisture migration behind walls and under flooring, written scope notes for the insurance adjuster, and a clear triage of what can be salvaged versus what needs to come out.

For water losses, we deploy truck-mounted extraction followed by desiccant or refrigerant dehumidifiers sized to the square footage — commercial drying is governed by the IICRC S500 standard, and we track psychrometric readings daily to confirm the structure is actually drying, not just appearing to dry. For fire and smoke losses, the challenge in older Rahway commercial buildings is odor migration through original plaster and masonry; we use thermal fogging and hydroxyl treatment in addition to surface cleaning because smoke penetrates porous historic materials in ways that surface wipes alone won't resolve.

Throughout the process, a single project manager handles communication with the business owner, the insurance carrier, and any municipal contacts — including any required permits for structural work under New Jersey's contractor licensing framework.

One pattern we've seen repeatedly along the Main Street business district and the blocks surrounding the Union County Performing Arts Center: commercial spaces that occupy the ground floor of a mixed-use building share mechanical systems — and sometimes structural walls — with residential units above.
