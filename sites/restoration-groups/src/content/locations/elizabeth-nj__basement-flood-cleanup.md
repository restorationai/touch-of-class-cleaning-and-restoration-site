## Our Basement Flood Cleanup Process in Elizabeth

The process starts with a moisture and contamination assessment before any equipment goes in. ## Local Note: Combined-Sewer Backups and What They Mean for Your Cleanup

One thing anyone working basements in Elizabeth learns quickly: a floor drain that's backing up during a rainstorm is almost never just rainwater. In neighborhoods served by Elizabeth's combined sewer system — which carries both stormwater and sanitary sewage in the same pipe — heavy precipitation overwhelms capacity and sewage surcharges back through the lowest available opening, which is almost always a basement floor drain. Homeowners sometimes assume they can mop it up themselves, but sewage-contaminated water requires Category 3 handling: proper PPE, controlled demolition of porous materials that absorbed the water, and documented disinfection. Attempting to dry it in place without that protocol can leave pathogens behind in the subfloor or wall cavity. If your drain backed up during the last big storm and your basement has any odor at all, treat it as a sewage loss until proven otherwise.
