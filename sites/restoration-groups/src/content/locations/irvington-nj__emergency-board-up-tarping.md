## Our Emergency Board-Up and Tarping Process in Irvington

When we arrive on-site, the first priority is a rapid exterior assessment: we identify every opening — fire-damaged windows, kicked-in doors, collapsed soffits, missing roof sections — and rank them by weather and security exposure. In Irvington's attached and semi-attached housing, we also check whether the breach creates any exposure to an adjacent unit, because that changes the scope immediately.

For window and door board-ups, we use 3/4-inch plywood cut to fit and secured with structural screws rather than nails, which hold better against wind load and are easier to remove cleanly when reconstruction begins. We pre-drill and countersink fasteners on finished surfaces where the framing will be reused. For roof breaches, we deploy reinforced polyethylene tarps rated for sustained wind, weighted and mechanically anchored — not just draped — so they hold through a multi-day weather event without shifting and creating new water pathways.

On Irvington's older wood-frame structures, we pay particular attention to the condition of the framing around the breach. Prewar lumber is often denser and more durable than modern dimensional lumber, but it can also be more brittle at the edges of fire or impact damage. We probe and sound the framing before anchoring anything into it, and we document everything photographically for your insurance file.
