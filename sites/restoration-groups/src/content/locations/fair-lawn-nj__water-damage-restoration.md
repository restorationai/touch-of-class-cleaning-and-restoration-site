## Our Water Damage Restoration Process in Fair Lawn

Every job starts with moisture mapping — not a visual inspection, but thermal imaging and calibrated moisture meters that find water inside wall cavities and under flooring before it becomes a mold problem. In Fair Lawn's older homes, that step matters more than most people realize: plaster-and-lath walls common in Radburn-era construction hold water differently than modern drywall, releasing moisture slowly even after standing water is gone.

Once we know where the water is, extraction comes first. Truck-mounted and portable extraction units pull water from carpet, hardwood subfloors, and concrete before it wicks further into structural materials. After extraction, we set drying equipment — commercial-grade desiccant and refrigerant dehumidifiers, high-velocity air movers — positioned specifically for the room geometry and material types involved. Drying targets are set to the IICRC S500 standard, and we monitor readings daily until the structure reaches documented dry standard. Nothing gets closed up until the numbers confirm it.

For homes near the Saddle River or in flood-prone zones, we also document the loss thoroughly from the start — photographs, moisture logs, scope of damage — because insurance carriers handling repetitive-loss properties in this area scrutinize claims carefully.
