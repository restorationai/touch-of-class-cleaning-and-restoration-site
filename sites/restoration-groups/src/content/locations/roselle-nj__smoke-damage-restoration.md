## Why Roselle Properties Are Especially Vulnerable to Smoke Damage

Roselle's housing stock is overwhelmingly pre-1960, and that era of construction creates specific challenges after a fire. Plaster walls — common in the colonials and Victorians clustered near Warinanco Park and throughout East Roselle — are far more porous than modern drywall. Smoke particles and soot penetrate deeply into the substrate, meaning surface wiping alone leaves odor and residue behind. Original wood-framed balloon construction, typical of homes built before the 1940s, allows smoke to travel vertically through interior wall cavities from the basement to the attic in minutes.

Finished basements, another fixture of Roselle's dense residential blocks, trap smoke at low levels where it lingers longest. Older HVAC systems with ductwork that hasn't been replaced in decades can distribute smoke residue throughout the entire house within a single burn event. In the ZIP code 07203, where lot sizes are tight and homes sit close together, smoke from a neighbor's fire can also infiltrate through shared attic spaces or open foundation vents — a loss pattern that surprises homeowners who weren't even the ones who had the fire.

## Our Smoke Damage Restoration Process in Roselle

Every smoke job starts with a structured assessment before any cleaning begins. The chemistry of smoke residue varies depending on what burned — synthetic materials like carpet and cabinetry produce a wet, smearing soot that requires different chemistry than the dry, powdery residue from burning wood framing. In Roselle's older homes, we frequently encounter both in the same structure because the original wood framing sits alongside decades of renovation materials.

Once we've mapped the residue types and the migration path smoke took through the structure, the work proceeds in a defined sequence: HEPA-filtered air scrubbers are placed to create negative pressure and capture airborne particles; dry sponge and chemical sponge methods are applied to walls and ceilings based on surface type; contents are inventoried and either cleaned on-site or packed out for off-site restoration; ductwork is inspected and cleaned if contaminated; and thermal fogging or hydroxyl generation is used to neutralize embedded odor molecules in porous materials. As an IICRC Certified Firm (#210213), our process follows the S700 standard for smoke and fire restoration — not a proprietary checklist.

Structural repairs — replacing smoke-saturated insulation, refinishing plaster, repainting — follow only after air quality readings confirm residue levels are within acceptable limits.
