Staten Island's geography sets it apart from the other boroughs. The housing stock adds another layer of complexity. ## Our Flood Damage Restoration Process in Staten Island

Every flood job starts the same way: stop the water source, assess the extent, and document everything before a single piece of material is touched. For insurance purposes, that documentation — moisture readings, thermal imaging, photo logs — needs to be thorough and timestamped. Our IICRC Certified Firm (#210213) crews carry industrial moisture meters and infrared cameras on every truck, so we're mapping hidden saturation in wall cavities and under subfloors within the first hour on site.

Once the scope is clear, we move through a structured sequence:

- **Water extraction** using truck-mounted and portable extractors sized to the space — a flooded Cape Cod basement in the 10306 ZIP code needs different equipment staging than a ground-floor commercial unit on Forest Avenue.
- **Controlled demolition** of saturated materials that cannot be dried in place — baseboards, lower drywall sections, wet insulation — removed and documented before disposal.
- **Structural drying** using desiccant or refrigerant dehumidifiers and high-velocity air movers, positioned per IICRC S500 drying science, with daily moisture readings to track progress.
- **Antimicrobial treatment** of affected cavities, because in Staten Island's humid summers, mold colonization can begin within 24 to 48 hours of a loss if cavities aren't treated promptly.
- **Reconstruction coordination** — framing, insulation, drywall, and finish work handled by our own crews so the handoff from mitigation to repair doesn't stall the job.

One thing that catches out-of-borough crews in older Staten Island neighborhoods: many pre-1950 wood-frame homes in Port Richmond and St. George were built with board sheathing under the exterior cladding rather than modern plywood or OSB.
