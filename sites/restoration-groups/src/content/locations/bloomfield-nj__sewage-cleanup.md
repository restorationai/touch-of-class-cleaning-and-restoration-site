## Why Bloomfield Properties See Sewage Backup So Often

Bloomfield's housing stock tells the story. The colonials, two-families, and Tudors built between 1900 and 1940 that line streets in Brookdale, North Center, and the blocks around Bloomfield Green Historic District were plumbed with cast-iron drain lines and galvanized supply pipes that are now well past their service life. Cast iron corrodes from the inside out — roots from the mature street trees common in these older neighborhoods find every hairline crack — and a single heavy rain event can push enough hydrostatic pressure through a compromised lateral to flood a finished basement in under an hour.

The geography compounds the problem. The Second River and the Yantacaw (Third River) both thread through the western and southern edges of town near Watsessing Park and Brookdale Park. ## Our Sewage Cleanup and Sanitization Process in Bloomfield

Sewage is classified as Category 3 water — the most hazardous class — because it carries bacteria, viruses, and parasites that survive on porous surfaces long after the visible water is gone. Our process is built around that reality, not around speed alone.

**Containment and assessment first.** Before extraction begins, we establish containment barriers to prevent cross-contamination from the affected basement or utility area into living space above. We identify the source — floor drain backflow, a cracked lateral, a failed ejector pump — and confirm it's been stopped or isolated before we start moving material.

**Extraction and controlled demolition.** Standing sewage is extracted with truck-mounted units. Porous materials — drywall, carpet, insulation, wood base trim — that absorbed Category 3 water are removed. In Bloomfield's older two-families and colonials, that often means original tongue-and-groove subfloor or plaster on wood lath, both of which hold contamination differently than modern OSB and drywall. We document everything photographically for your insurance file before anything leaves the property.

**EPA-registered disinfection.** Structural surfaces — concrete block, poured foundation walls, floor joists, subfloor — are treated with EPA-registered disinfectants rated for Category 3 contamination. We do not skip this step to save time. The IICRC S500 standard governs our drying and documentation protocol as an IICRC Certified Firm (#210213).

**Drying and clearance.** Commercial desiccant dehumidifiers and air movers run until moisture readings in structural assemblies return to baseline. We verify with thermal imaging and pin/pinless meters before equipment is pulled.
