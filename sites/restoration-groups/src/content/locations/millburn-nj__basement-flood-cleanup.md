## Why Millburn Basements Flood — and Why They're Harder to Dry

Millburn and Short Hills are defined by their housing stock: large colonials, Tudors, and estate homes, many built between the 1910s and 1950s, sitting on deep lots with mature landscaping that can slow surface drainage toward foundation walls. Basements in these homes were often finished decades after original construction, layering modern drywall, carpet, and built-ins over original stone or poured-concrete foundations that were never designed to be living space.

That layering is what makes water losses here more complex than in newer construction. When a storm pushes groundwater through a foundation crack — or when an aging cast-iron drain line backs up — moisture travels behind finished walls and under floating floors before it ever becomes visible. By the time a homeowner in the Wyoming or Glenwood sections notices a damp smell or a bubbling floor, water may have been sitting against the framing for hours.

## Our Basement Flood Cleanup Process in Millburn

Every job starts with moisture mapping, not assumptions. Before any equipment is placed, the crew uses thermal imaging cameras and calibrated moisture meters to trace exactly where water has traveled — behind plaster, under tile, inside wall cavities. In Millburn's older homes, this step is especially important because pre-war plaster holds moisture differently than modern drywall: it can feel dry at the surface while the lath behind it stays saturated for days.

Once the full extent of the loss is documented, standing water is extracted using truck-mounted and portable units sized to the space. Structural drying follows the IICRC S500 standard, with desiccant or refrigerant dehumidifiers and high-velocity air movers positioned based on the psychrometric readings, not a generic room count. Moisture logs are recorded daily so the drying curve is visible and defensible — something carriers increasingly require for claims in the 07041 and 07078 ZIP codes.

If sewage or river water is involved, the scope expands to include antimicrobial treatment and, where contaminated materials cannot be adequately dried, controlled demolition and removal. The team is IICRC Certified (Firm #210213) and operates under NJ Division of Consumer Affairs licensing as a Home Improvement Contractor, which matters when permits are required for any structural work.

## Local Note

Millburn's finished basements in the Short Hills and Old Short Hills sections frequently include wine cellars and climate-controlled storage rooms built with spray-foam insulation and vapor barriers — features that are excellent for preservation but can trap moisture inside a wall assembly after a flood event. Because spray foam is vapor-impermeable, drying cannot happen through the wall surface; affected sections typically need to be opened from the interior to allow airflow. Homeowners who skip this step and simply run a dehumidifier often see mold colonization behind intact walls within two to three weeks. Identifying these assemblies early in the moisture-mapping phase is one of the most consequential things a crew can do on a Short Hills job.
