## Why Somerville Properties See Elevated Mold Risk

Somerset County's borough seat sits between the Raritan River and Peters Brook — two waterways that have pushed floodwater into low-lying streets more than once in living memory. Ida alone displaced dozens of residents in 08876 and left basements damp for weeks. But flooding is only part of the story. The majority of Somerville's residential blocks are early-1900s frame two-families and Victorians with balloon-frame wall cavities that channel water silently from a roof leak or a failed supply fitting down to the sill plate before anyone notices a stain. Mold can colonize porous materials in as little as 24 to 48 hours under the right humidity conditions — and a finished basement that smells "a little musty" after a wet spring may already have active growth behind drywall that a visual inspection alone won't catch.

The Main Street commercial corridor adds a separate layer of exposure. ## Our Mold Inspection and Testing Process in Somerville

Every inspection starts with a thorough visual assessment of the areas you're concerned about — and the areas you aren't. Inspectors check attic sheathing, basement rim joists, bathroom exhaust paths, and any wall cavity adjacent to a known plumbing run. In Somerville's older Victorians and colonials, that means paying close attention to the spaces behind clawfoot tub surrounds and the soffits above galley kitchens, where supply lines often lack adequate insulation.

When visual evidence or moisture readings suggest hidden growth, we collect air samples and, where appropriate, surface swab or tape-lift samples. Those samples go to an accredited third-party laboratory — not an in-house bench — so the results carry the independent credibility you need for insurance documentation, real estate transactions, or a landlord-tenant dispute. You receive a written report that identifies mold genera and spore concentrations, compares indoor counts to outdoor baseline samples, and flags any readings that exceed thresholds associated with indoor air quality concerns.

The IICRC Certified Firm credential (Firm #210213) behind this work means the inspection follows recognized industry protocols, not a proprietary checklist.

One thing that comes up repeatedly in post-flood inspections in Somerville's low-lying neighborhoods is a false sense of resolution: the standing water was pumped out, the dehumidifiers ran for a week, and the basement looks dry.
