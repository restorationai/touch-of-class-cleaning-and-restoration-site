## Why Garfield Properties See Mold Problems

The housing stock in ZIP code 07026 is a near-perfect environment for mold growth. Pre-WWII frame construction means balloon-framed walls with minimal vapor barriers, plaster over wood lath in older units, and crawl spaces that ventilate poorly through small foundation vents. Cast-iron drain stacks — common in the two- and three-family homes that line streets throughout Plauderville and the Harrison Avenue district — corrode at joints and seep slowly for months before anyone notices. That slow seep behind a finished basement wall is exactly the scenario where mold colonies establish themselves well before the damage is visible.

The Saddle River tributaries that drain into low-lying parts of the city add another layer of risk. After a hard storm, groundwater rises quickly in below-grade spaces, and sump pumps in older homes often lack battery backup. A pump failure during a two-inch overnight rain is a routine call in Garfield — and routine doesn't mean minor. Saturated fiberglass batt insulation against a concrete foundation wall can harbor active mold growth within a week.

## Our Mold Remediation Process in Garfield

Every remediation starts with a thorough visual inspection and moisture mapping using thermal imaging and calibrated moisture meters. In Garfield's older multifamily buildings, this step matters more than usual: moisture migrates laterally through shared framing, meaning a leak in one unit can feed mold growth in the wall cavity of the unit next door. We identify the full moisture boundary before any demolition begins.

Once the scope is clear, we establish containment using 6-mil poly barriers and negative air pressure with HEPA-filtered air scrubbers. This is non-negotiable in attached housing — negative pressure prevents cross-contamination to adjacent units or floors. Affected materials (drywall, insulation, wood framing where necessary) are removed, bagged, and disposed of per New Jersey DEP guidelines. Structural surfaces are HEPA-vacuumed, treated with an EPA-registered antimicrobial agent, and dried to documented moisture levels before any rebuild begins. We are an IICRC Certified Firm (#210213), and our process follows the IICRC S520 Standard for Professional Mold Remediation throughout.
