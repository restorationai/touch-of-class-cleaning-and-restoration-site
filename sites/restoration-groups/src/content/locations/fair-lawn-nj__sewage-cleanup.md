## Our Sewage Cleanup and Sanitization Process in Fair Lawn

Sewage cleanup is not the same as water damage drying. The contamination level requires a specific sequence, and skipping steps creates health and liability exposure.

**1. Containment and PPE staging.** Before extraction begins, the affected area is isolated to prevent cross-contamination into clean zones. In Fair Lawn's older homes, that often means sealing off finished basement rooms from mechanicals and laundry areas that share the same open floor plan.

**2. Solid waste and standing water removal.** Industrial pumps and wet-vac equipment remove standing sewage. Solid waste is bagged and disposed of per New Jersey Department of Environmental Protection guidelines for Category 3 material.

**3. Controlled demolition of non-salvageable materials.** Porous materials — drywall, carpet, insulation, wood subfloor — that absorbed raw sewage cannot be dried and reused. They are removed to the flood cut line. In finished Radburn basements, this frequently means removing paneling that conceals original plaster or concrete block walls behind it.

**4. EPA-registered disinfection.** Hard surfaces, framing, and concrete are treated with EPA-registered antimicrobial agents rated for Category 3 contamination. This is not a single spray pass — it requires dwell time, a second application on porous concrete, and documentation.

**5. Structural drying and verification.** After sanitization, the space is dried using commercial dehumidifiers and air movers calibrated to the actual cubic footage and material types present. Moisture readings are logged daily until the structure reaches dry standard per the IICRC S500 framework.

**6. Clearance documentation.** We provide written documentation of the remediation scope, materials removed, and post-treatment moisture readings — the package your insurance adjuster and any future buyer's inspector will ask for.
