## Why Summit Properties See Water Damage Differently

Elevation protects Summit from the river flooding that hits Millburn and Chatham after a heavy storm, but it creates its own drainage dynamic. Steep grades across the Northside and the Franklin School area funnel stormwater directly toward foundation walls. In older homes, that means water migrating through stone or brick foundations that were never designed to be fully waterproofed — just drained. Once it's inside, it contacts plaster, old-growth hardwood floors, and finished basement spaces that may include original cabinetry, built-in bars, or mechanicals that predate modern flood-resistant materials by decades.

Aging original plumbing is the other persistent source. Many homes in the 07901 ZIP code still carry supply lines installed before World War II. Galvanized steel corrodes from the inside out; when a section finally fails inside a wall cavity, water can travel several feet before it's visible. The same clay sewer laterals that have served Summit's older streets for generations are now cracked, root-invaded, and prone to backing up during heavy rain — sending contaminated water into lower levels without warning.

## Our Water Cleanup Process in Summit

The first step on any Summit job is understanding what's behind the surface. Plaster walls read dry at the face long after the lath behind them has absorbed significant moisture — a standard pin meter pass isn't enough. Thermal imaging and deep-penetration moisture mapping let us locate saturation in wall assemblies, under original hardwood floors, and in the framing above finished ceilings before we commit to a drying plan.

Extraction comes next: truck-mounted units for standing water, followed by targeted desiccant or refrigerant dehumidification calibrated to the square footage and air exchange rate of the specific space. In Summit's larger homes — many of which run 4,000 to 6,000 square feet across multiple finished levels — equipment placement matters. We stage units to create consistent airflow across the affected zone rather than concentrating drying in one room and leaving adjacent spaces under-treated.

Content handling is a real part of the job here. Antique furniture, original artwork, and period architectural elements require careful documentation, protective wrapping, and in some cases off-site storage during the drying phase. We treat that process with the same attention we give the structural work.

Throughout drying, we log moisture readings daily and share them with the homeowner and, when applicable, the insurance adjuster. Drying is complete when readings return to established baselines for the material type — not when the space looks or smells dry.
