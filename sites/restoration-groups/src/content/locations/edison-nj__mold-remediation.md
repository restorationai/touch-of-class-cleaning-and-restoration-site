## Why Edison Properties See Elevated Mold Risk

Middlesex County's humid continental climate means Edison rarely gets a prolonged dry stretch in summer — relative humidity regularly climbs above 60 percent indoors without active dehumidification, which is exactly the threshold at which dormant spores on wood framing and paper-faced drywall begin to activate. That baseline humidity problem is compounded by Edison's specific geography: the Raritan River's southern border and the Mill Brook drainage network running through the interior of the township create low-lying pockets where groundwater sits high. Neighborhoods near those waterways saw significant basement intrusion during Hurricane Ida in 2021, and homes that were dried quickly still sometimes develop mold months later in wall cavities that were never fully opened and dried.

Older homes in the Stelton and Oak Tree areas frequently have original fiberglass batt insulation packed against foundation walls — a combination that traps moisture against concrete and creates persistent mold conditions that surface slowly, often as a musty odor long before visible growth appears. Commercial properties along the Route 1 retail corridor face a different pattern: overnight sprinkler discharges and roof membrane failures on flat-roofed big-box buildings can saturate large areas of ceiling tile and structural decking before anyone arrives in the morning.

## Our Mold Remediation Process in Edison

Every job starts with a thorough inspection — moisture mapping with thermal imaging and pin meters to locate wet building materials that aren't yet visibly affected, followed by air sampling if the scope is unclear. In Edison's older finished basements, this step matters more than most: mold is frequently behind original wood-panel wainscoting or inside the framing cavities of 1960s drop ceilings, invisible until the surfaces are opened.

Once the scope is confirmed, the affected area is sealed under negative air pressure using HEPA-filtered air scrubbers to prevent cross-contamination to living spaces. Contaminated materials — drywall, insulation, wood trim — are removed, bagged, and disposed of per New Jersey DEP guidelines. Remaining structural surfaces are HEPA-vacuumed and treated with an EPA-registered antimicrobial. Drying equipment stays in place until moisture readings return to baseline, which in Edison's climate often means running dehumidifiers longer than the national average — especially in below-grade spaces where concrete continues to release absorbed moisture for days after a flood event.

The Restoration Group is an IICRC Certified Firm (#210213), and remediation is performed in accordance with the IICRC S520 Standard for Professional Mold Remediation.
