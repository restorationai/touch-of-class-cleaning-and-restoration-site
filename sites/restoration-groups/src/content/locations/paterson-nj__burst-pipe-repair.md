## Why Paterson Properties See Burst Pipe Issues More Than Most

Paterson's housing stock is among the oldest in New Jersey. The dense mill-worker rowhomes and brick multi-family walkups that line blocks from the Eastside to South Paterson were built in the late 1800s and early 1900s, and their original plumbing — galvanized steel, cast iron, even remnants of lead supply lines — was never designed to last this long. Galvanized pipe corrodes from the inside out, narrowing over decades until water pressure finally splits the wall. You won't see it coming.

The city's converted silk-era industrial buildings add another layer of risk. Many of those structures were retrofitted with sprinkler systems and standpipes as code required, and aging wet-pipe sprinkler heads or corroded standpipe fittings can discharge without warning — flooding multiple units in a single event. In dense rental buildings near Passaic County Community College or along the corridors closer to Hinchliffe Stadium, a single standpipe failure can affect four or five apartments before the water main is located and shut.

Cold weather accelerates everything. Paterson sits in a climate zone where hard freezes arrive by late November and can persist through February. Exterior walls in uninsulated frame houses, pipes running through unheated garages, and supply lines tucked against the rim joist in basement units are all vulnerable. When the temperature drops fast — as it does during a northwest wind event off the Passaic River valley — the margin between "cold pipe" and "burst pipe" can be a matter of hours.

## Our Burst Pipe Cleanup and Repair Process in Paterson

When we arrive, the first priority is stopping the loss — not paperwork. If the water main hasn't been shut yet, we help locate the shutoff, which in older Paterson multi-families is sometimes buried behind a finished wall or in a shared utility room with no clear labeling. Once flow is stopped, we document the affected area with moisture mapping and thermal imaging before anything is moved or torn out. That documentation matters for your insurance claim.

Extraction comes next. Standing water in basements — common in the low-lying blocks of the Lakeview section and near the downtown flats — is removed with truck-mounted extraction equipment. We don't stop at visible water. Moisture readings guide us into wall cavities, under hardwood floors, and into the subfloor framing, where water travels faster than most people expect.

Drying is calibrated to the building, not a generic formula. Paterson's older plaster-and-lath construction releases moisture more slowly than modern drywall — the plaster itself holds water and continues off-gassing for days after extraction. We set commercial desiccant and refrigerant dehumidifiers accordingly and monitor daily until readings confirm the structure has reached equilibrium. IICRC S500 drying standards govern the process from day one through final clearance.

Pipe repair or replacement is coordinated with a licensed plumber. We work alongside the trade contractor so the repair and the restoration move in parallel — you're not waiting for one to finish before the other starts.
