## Our Water Cleanup Process in Woodbridge

Every water cleanup job starts with a moisture assessment, not a guess. Before any equipment goes down, the crew maps the affected area using thermal imaging and calibrated moisture meters — tools that reveal water migration behind walls and under subfloor materials that look dry at the surface. In Woodbridge's older homes, water routinely travels further than the visible damage suggests, especially in homes with plaster-and-lath construction or original hardwood floors over uninsulated crawl spaces.

From there, the process follows a defined sequence:

- **Standing water extraction** using truck-mounted and portable extraction units, including specialty tools for water trapped in carpet padding or beneath hardwood planks
- **Structural drying** with industrial air movers and refrigerant dehumidifiers, positioned to create a controlled drying environment rather than just moving air around
- **Moisture monitoring** at regular intervals — typically daily — until readings reach the dry standard required by the IICRC S500 protocol
- **Documentation** of moisture readings, equipment placement, and drying progress for insurance reporting

## Local Note: What Woodbridge's Postwar Basements Hide

In the split-levels and raised ranches that dominate Colonia and Fords, finished basement walls are frequently built with wood furring strips nailed directly against the concrete-block foundation — no air gap, no vapor barrier. When water enters through the block (which is porous by nature), the furring and drywall wick moisture inward and hold it. The surface can read nearly dry within 24 hours while the wall cavity stays saturated for days. Standard moisture meters placed on the drywall face miss this entirely. Thermal imaging and penetrating probes placed at the base of the wall tell a different story. Skipping that step leads to mold growth behind a wall that was declared dry — and a second remediation job weeks later.
