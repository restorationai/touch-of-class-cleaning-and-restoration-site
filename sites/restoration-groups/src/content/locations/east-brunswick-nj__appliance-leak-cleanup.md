## Our Appliance Leak Cleanup Process in East Brunswick

When we arrive, the first priority is stopping ongoing water introduction — confirming the supply valve is fully closed and that the appliance itself is isolated. From there, the process follows the IICRC S500 standard for water damage response, calibrated to what we actually find on-site.

In East Brunswick's older ranch and split-level homes, that typically means:

- **Moisture mapping** with thermal imaging and pin-type meters across the subfloor, adjacent wall cavities, and any finished ceiling below the affected area. Appliance leaks are deceptive — water travels along joists and vapor barriers before it pools visibly.
- **Controlled demolition** when necessary. Wet drywall, baseboards, and flooring that cannot be dried in place are removed to the flood cut line. We document everything photographically before any material comes out.
- **High-capacity extraction** of standing water followed by placement of commercial desiccant dehumidifiers and axial air movers. In finished basements — which are nearly universal in the older Frost Woods and Tanglewood tracts — drying times run longer because there are more enclosed cavities holding moisture.
- **Daily monitoring** with written moisture readings until all structural materials reach target dryness. We do not pull equipment early because a number looks close.
