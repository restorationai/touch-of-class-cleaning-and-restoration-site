## Our Basement Flooding Cleanup Process in Englewood

The first step on any flooded basement call is a rapid moisture mapping of the space — walls, subfloor, framing, and any stored contents. We use thermal imaging cameras and penetrating moisture meters to find water that has migrated behind finished surfaces, which is especially common in the older balloon-frame construction found throughout the Fourth Ward and West Englewood.

Once the scope is documented, we extract standing water with truck-mounted and portable extraction units, then set a calibrated drying system: industrial-grade desiccant or refrigerant dehumidifiers paired with high-velocity air movers positioned to create a directed airflow pattern. The number of units, their placement, and the target grain depression are calculated to the room's cubic footage and the material types present — concrete dries on a different curve than OSB subfloor or 1950s tongue-and-groove.

We monitor drying progress with daily moisture readings and adjust equipment until all structural materials reach the IICRC S500 standard for acceptable moisture content. Nothing gets closed up — no new drywall, no fresh flooring — until those numbers confirm the assembly is dry. That discipline matters in Englewood's older housing stock, where trapped moisture behind a prematurely replaced wall is how a $4,000 water loss becomes a $25,000 mold remediation two years later.
