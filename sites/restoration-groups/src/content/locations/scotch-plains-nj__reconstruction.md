## Our Reconstruction Process in Scotch Plains

Reconstruction begins where mitigation ends: once affected materials are removed and the structure is confirmed dry, we conduct a scope-of-work assessment that documents every structural and finish element requiring replacement. For the mid-century homes common in neighborhoods like Willow Grove and Ashbrook, that often means specifying materials that match the original construction — dimensional lumber framing rather than engineered I-joists, for instance, or plaster-skim finishes over new drywall to blend with intact original walls.

From there, the process moves through a defined sequence: structural framing and sheathing first, then mechanical rough-ins (plumbing, electrical, HVAC), insulation and moisture barrier, drywall or plaster finish, and finally trim, flooring, and paint. Each phase requires a Union County inspection before the next begins. We pull all required permits, schedule inspections, and keep the homeowner or property manager informed at each gate so there are no surprises at certificate-of-occupancy.

For commercial properties on the Route 22 corridor, we coordinate directly with the building owner's insurance adjuster and, where applicable, with individual tenants whose spaces are affected. Phased reconstruction — completing one tenant bay at a time — is often the practical approach when full closure isn't an option.

## Local Note

One pattern we see repeatedly in Scotch Plains split-levels: the lower level is framed on a partial slab with a wood-framed section cantilevered over the foundation wall, and that cantilever area is almost always insulated with original fiberglass batts that have no vapor retarder facing the exterior. After a flood or prolonged moisture event, that framing cavity holds moisture far longer than the rest of the structure — sometimes weeks after the main floor reads dry. On reconstruction scopes in these homes, we factor in additional drying time and often specify closed-cell spray foam in that cavity on reassembly, which solves the moisture-retention problem and improves the thermal envelope at the same time.
