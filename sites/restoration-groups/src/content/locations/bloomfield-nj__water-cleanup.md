## Our Water Cleanup Process in Bloomfield

Water cleanup in an older Bloomfield home follows a different rhythm than work in a newer construction suburb. The first step on arrival is containment and source control — shutting off supply, confirming the sewer isn't still actively backing up, and identifying whether the water is clean supply, gray, or category 3 sewage. That distinction matters immediately because it changes how materials are handled and what PPE the crew wears.

Extraction comes next. In finished Bloomfield basements, that typically means truck-mounted or portable extraction units pulling standing water from carpet, engineered flooring, or tile, followed by targeted extraction along wall bases where water wicks upward into framing cavities. Moisture mapping with thermal imaging and calibrated meters follows — because in plaster-and-lath construction common to Brookdale Park-area colonials and North Center Tudors, a wall can read surface-dry while the lath behind it holds significant moisture. Drying equipment — desiccant or refrigerant dehumidifiers, air movers positioned to create directed airflow — is then placed according to a drying plan documented to IICRC S500 standards. Monitoring readings are logged on each visit until structural materials reach acceptable equilibrium.

One thing that catches homeowners off guard in Bloomfield's older two-family and colonial stock: plaster walls absorb water more slowly than modern drywall, which can make a wall look fine for the first 24 to 48 hours after a loss.
