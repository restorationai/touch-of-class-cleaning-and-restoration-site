## Why Paramus Properties See Basement Flooding So Often

Paramus's housing stock is almost entirely postwar construction, which means the supply plumbing and drain systems in many homes are approaching or past their expected service life. Galvanized steel supply lines corrode from the inside out; cast-iron waste stacks crack and allow groundwater intrusion at the joints. These aren't cosmetic problems — they're the reason a dry basement in October becomes a flooded one in February with no storm in sight.

Geography compounds the issue. The Saddle River runs through the Van Saun County Park corridor and through low-lying residential areas nearby. During sustained rain events — the kind that Bergen County sees several times a year — the water table in those zones rises quickly, overwhelming footer drains and sump pits that were sized for average rainfall, not the increasingly intense storms the region has experienced since Ida. ## Our Basement Flooding Cleanup Process in Paramus

Every job starts with moisture mapping, not guesswork. Technicians use thermal imaging cameras and calibrated moisture meters to trace water migration through concrete block walls, under finished flooring, and into wall cavities before a single piece of equipment is placed. In Paramus's split-level and raised-ranch layouts, water frequently travels laterally under the slab before surfacing — finding it early prevents secondary damage in rooms that look dry.

Extraction comes next. Truck-mounted and portable extraction units pull standing water from the floor; weighted extraction tools address water trapped in carpet padding and subfloor assemblies. Once bulk water is removed, the drying phase begins: industrial-grade desiccant and refrigerant dehumidifiers, axial air movers, and — where necessary — structural drying panels that press directly against concrete walls to pull moisture from the block itself. Drying is documented daily with moisture readings logged against the IICRC S500 standard so there's a clear record for your insurance adjuster.

Contaminated losses — sewage backups, groundwater intrusion carrying surface runoff — require antimicrobial treatment and, in some cases, controlled demolition of saturated materials that cannot be dried in place. The Restoration Group is an IICRC Certified Firm (#210213), and all affected materials are handled and disposed of per New Jersey DEP guidelines.

## Local Note: Finished Basements and Hidden Moisture in Paramus Ranches

One thing that comes up repeatedly in Paramus's postwar ranch and split-level homes: finished basements with drop ceilings and wood-paneled walls installed decades ago. Homeowners often assume the damage is limited to what they can see — wet carpet, a puddle near the utility room. In reality, the fiberglass batt insulation tucked behind that paneling acts like a sponge, and the paper-faced drywall or plaster behind it can hold moisture for weeks without visible staining. By the time a musty smell appears, mold is already established. Thermal imaging on the first visit — not after the visible materials are removed — is the step that catches this before it becomes a much larger remediation project.
