## Why Highland Park Basements Flood the Way They Do

The Raritan River defines Highland Park's southern edge, and that geography has consequences. During Tropical Storm Ida in 2021, the low-lying stretches along River Road and the grounds of Donaldson Park were submerged — but the flooding didn't stop at the riverbank. Groundwater saturation spread inland, overwhelming the hydrostatic pressure that older foundation walls were never engineered to resist. Homes in Livingston Manor and the Triangle neighborhood, many built between the 1910s and 1940s, sit on poured-concrete or stone-rubble foundations that are porous by today's standards. Water doesn't announce itself through a dramatic breach; it seeps through mortar joints, wicks up through the slab, and pools quietly behind finished walls.

The plumbing compounds the problem. Original cast-iron drain lines common in these colonials, bungalows, and two-family homes corrode from the inside out over decades. When a lateral cracks or root intrusion causes a partial blockage, a heavy rain event can reverse-flow sewage back through floor drains — a category of loss that requires a different cleanup protocol than clean water alone. On the Raritan Avenue corridor, commercial properties face a related but distinct risk: aging sprinkler systems and high-volume plumbing in restaurant kitchens can fail under pressure surges, sending water cascading into below-grade storage and prep areas.

## Our Basement Flood Cleanup Process in Highland Park

The first thing that matters after a basement flood is an honest assessment of what you're dealing with — water source, contamination category, and what's already hidden inside the structure. A finished basement in a 1930s colonial can look like a simple wet-carpet situation on the surface while the lath and plaster behind the paneling holds moisture for weeks. We use thermal imaging and calibrated moisture meters to find those pockets before they become mold colonies.

From there, the process follows a clear sequence:

- **Water extraction** — truck-mounted and portable extractors remove standing water from concrete floors, carpet, and subfloor assemblies.
- **Structural drying** — industrial desiccant dehumidifiers and high-velocity air movers are positioned to dry the space to IICRC S500 standards, not just until it feels dry to the touch.
- **Contamination treatment** — if the source involves sewage backup or river intrusion, affected materials are treated with EPA-registered antimicrobials and, where necessary, removed entirely.
- **Documentation** — moisture readings, photos, and drying logs are compiled throughout for your insurance carrier.

In older Highland Park homes, we pay particular attention to the transition zones: where finished drywall meets an original stone foundation, where a dropped ceiling hides original floor joists, where a sump pit was retrofitted into a slab that wasn't designed for one. Those are the spots that look fine and aren't.

## Highland Park Insurance Coordination

Most standard homeowners policies cover sudden and accidental water losses — a burst pipe, an appliance failure, a drain backup if you carry the rider. What they typically don't cover is gradual seepage or flood damage from a rising river, which falls under NFIP (National Flood Insurance Program) policies. Given Highland Park's documented flood history along the Raritan corridor, a meaningful number of borough homeowners carry both. We document losses in the format carriers expect: moisture mapping, category classification, itemized scope of work.
