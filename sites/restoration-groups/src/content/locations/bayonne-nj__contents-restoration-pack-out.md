## Our Contents Restoration & Pack-Out Process in Bayonne

The process starts before anything leaves the property. A technician walks the affected rooms and categorizes every item: salvageable with cleaning, salvageable with specialized treatment, or a documented total loss for insurance purposes. Nothing is moved without that inventory, because your adjuster will need it.

Pack-out follows a room-by-room sequence designed to protect items during transit. Fragile or high-value pieces — artwork, electronics, upholstered furniture — are wrapped and crated separately from textiles and documents. Once off-site, contents go into a climate-controlled facility where each category gets the appropriate treatment: ultrasonic cleaning for hard goods, ozone or hydroxyl treatment for smoke-affected soft goods, freeze-drying for water-saturated documents and photographs. Items are tracked individually through the process so nothing gets lost in the shuffle between pack-out and return.

Return happens after the structure itself is dry and cleared — we coordinate that timing with the structural drying crew so contents aren't moved back into a space that still has elevated moisture readings.

## Local Note

In Bayonne's older brick rowhomes, particularly on the blocks running inland from the Newark Bay waterfront, basement walls are often uncoated brick or cinder block with no interior membrane. When those walls take on water during a surge or a heavy rain event, the moisture wicks laterally into stored contents sitting directly against the wall — cardboard boxes, fabric bins, wooden furniture legs. By the time the floor looks dry, items against the wall may already be supporting mold growth. During pack-out in these properties, we pull everything at least a foot from the perimeter before cataloging, because contact with a wicking masonry wall changes the condition assessment for almost every item it touched.
