## Why Livingston's Housing Stock Complicates Fire Damage Cleanup

Livingston's residential neighborhoods were built in waves. The post-war split-levels and ranches that dominate much of the town — particularly around Collins and the older streets feeding into Memorial Park — were constructed with materials that simply don't exist in new builds: horsehair plaster, solid-wood millwork, original hardwood subfloors, and cast-iron drain stacks. When fire or heat moves through these structures, smoke particles don't just coat surfaces — they penetrate into plaster substrates and settle inside wall cavities where modern drywall would have stopped them.

The newer teardown McMansions present a different challenge. Many were built on expanded finished basements, which means a kitchen or living-room fire can push smoke and soot through HVAC systems into below-grade living space — doubling the affected square footage and complicating structural drying if sprinkler discharge or firefighting water has soaked into the slab or framing. Both housing types demand a restoration approach calibrated to what's actually in the walls, not a one-size process.

## Our Fire Damage Restoration Process in Livingston

The first hour on-site is documentation. Before a single item is moved, we photograph and catalog every affected surface, material, and structural element — a step that directly supports your insurance claim and protects you if scope disputes arise later. From there, the process follows a clear sequence:

**Emergency board-up and stabilization.** Openings left by firefighting — broken windows, compromised roof decking, doors forced during entry — are secured against weather and unauthorized access. In Livingston's older homes, original single-pane windows and wood-frame storm doors often need full replacement rather than temporary cover.

**Smoke and soot removal.** We use dry-chemical sponges on porous surfaces before any wet cleaning, which prevents soot from being ground deeper into plaster or masonry. HEPA-filtered air scrubbers run continuously to capture airborne particulates. Protein smoke from kitchen fires — the kind that leaves an almost invisible film but a persistent odor — requires enzyme-based treatments that differ from the approach used on structural fires.

**Odor neutralization.** Thermal fogging and hydroxyl generation are deployed based on the fire type and the home's construction. Plaster walls in older Livingston homes off-gas smoke odor for weeks if not treated at the substrate level, not just the surface.

**Structural assessment and reconstruction.** Our IICRC Certified Firm (#210213) technicians document what can be restored versus what requires replacement, coordinating directly with your adjuster. We hold a New Jersey Licensed Home Improvement Contractor license through the NJ Division of Consumer Affairs, which means reconstruction work — framing, drywall, finish carpentry — is handled under the same project rather than handed off.
