## Why Roselle Properties See Mold Issues

Roselle's housing stock tells the story. The borough is dense with Victorian two-families, postwar capes, and colonials built between roughly 1910 and 1955 — many of them on tight lots with finished basements, minimal crawlspace clearance, and original galvanized supply lines and cast-iron waste lines that are well past their service life. When those pipes fail slowly (and they often do), water saturates framing, subfloor, and insulation for weeks before anyone notices.

The geography compounds the risk. The Elizabeth River corridor along the eastern edge of town — running past Warinanco Park — means that heavy rain events don't just cause surface flooding. Storm-drain backpressure and sump-pump overload push water and sewage into lower-level spaces that were never designed to drain quickly. Sewage-contaminated water (Category 3 in the IICRC classification system) carries bacteria and organic matter that accelerates mold growth and requires a more aggressive remediation protocol than a clean-water pipe burst.

Along the Chestnut Street business district and the St. Georges Avenue corridor, we also see commercial mold events driven by overnight roof leaks in flat-roofed retail buildings — water that sits undiscovered until Monday morning, by which point mold has had 48-plus hours of uninterrupted growth time.

## Our Mold Remediation Process in Roselle

Every job starts with a visual inspection and moisture mapping using thermal imaging cameras and pin-type meters. In Roselle's older homes, we pay particular attention to original plaster walls, balloon-frame cavities, and the junction between finished basement ceilings and the subfloor above — areas where moisture hides long after the source is repaired.

Once we identify the affected zone, containment goes up: heavy-duty poly barriers, negative air pressure using HEPA-filtered air scrubbers, and sealed HVAC registers to prevent spore migration to clean areas of the home. We then remove compromised materials — drywall, insulation, wood framing where necessary — following the IICRC S520 standard for mold remediation. Surfaces are treated with an EPA-registered antimicrobial agent, and structural cavities are dried to target moisture levels before any reconstruction begins.

For Category 3 (sewage-affected) losses, which are common in the Warinanco-area flood zone, PPE protocols and disposal procedures are more stringent. We document every step with photographs and moisture readings, which matters both for insurance claims and for any future real estate disclosure requirements under New Jersey law.

## Local Note

One pattern we see repeatedly in Roselle's pre-1950 homes: original horsehair plaster over wood lath. Unlike modern drywall, plaster doesn't wick moisture to the surface in a way that's easy to read with a surface meter — the lath behind it can be saturated while the plaster face feels dry to the touch. We use deep-penetration moisture meters and thermal imaging on every plaster-wall inspection in this housing stock. It takes longer to assess, but it prevents the scenario where a homeowner gets a clearance and then finds mold returning inside the wall cavity three months later.
