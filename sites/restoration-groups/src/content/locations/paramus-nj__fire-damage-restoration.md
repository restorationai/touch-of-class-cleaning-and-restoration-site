## Why Paramus Homes Are Particularly Vulnerable After a Fire

The postwar housing stock that defines most of Paramus — ranches, colonials, and split-levels built through the 1950s and '60s — creates specific challenges after a fire that newer construction simply does not. Many of these homes were built with plaster-and-lath walls rather than modern drywall. Plaster is dense and porous; it absorbs smoke particulates and odor molecules deeply, requiring more aggressive chemical sponging and longer deodorization cycles than a standard drywall surface. Attic spaces in these homes often contain original blown-in insulation that acts like a sponge for smoke, holding odor even after visible soot has been cleaned from living areas.

The aging supply plumbing common in West Paramus and Arcola neighborhoods adds another layer of risk: when firefighters suppress a kitchen or utility-room fire, the thermal shock from cold water hitting old copper or cast-iron pipes can crack fittings, introducing water damage on top of fire damage. We scope for both during our initial assessment so nothing is missed on your insurance claim.

## Our Fire Damage Restoration Process in Paramus

Every job begins with a full structural and contents assessment — we document damage room by room with photos and moisture readings before a single item is moved. That documentation matters: Bergen County insurers see a high volume of residential fire claims, and a thorough initial report reduces the likelihood of a disputed supplement later.

From there, the process moves through board-up and roof tarping if the structure is compromised, controlled demolition of unsalvageable materials, and HEPA-filtered air scrubbing to pull fine soot particles out of the breathing zone. Smoke odor in Paramus homes with forced-air HVAC systems requires duct inspection and cleaning — smoke travels through return vents and deposits residue throughout the entire duct network, not just in the rooms where the fire occurred. We use thermal fogging and hydroxyl generation for odor neutralization rather than masking agents, which means the smell does not return weeks later when the weather warms.

Structural drying runs concurrently with fire cleanup when suppression water is present, following the IICRC S500 standard for drying documentation.
