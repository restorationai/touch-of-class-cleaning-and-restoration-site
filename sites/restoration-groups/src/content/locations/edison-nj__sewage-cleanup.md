## Why Edison Properties Are Especially Vulnerable to Sewage Backups

Edison's housing stock tells a lot of the story. The township filled in rapidly after World War II with cape cods, split-levels, and colonials — many of them in ZIP code 08820 and 08837 — and those homes are now carrying original cast-iron drain lines that are 60 to 70 years old. Cast iron corrodes from the inside out, and as the pipe wall thins, root intrusion, grease buildup, and sediment accumulation accelerate. The result is a sewer line that looks intact from the outside until it isn't.

The geography compounds the problem. Low-lying areas near the Raritan River's northern bank and the Mill Brook tributary network — particularly in the southern reaches of the township — saw significant flooding during Hurricane Ida in 2021. When stormwater overwhelms the municipal sewer system, it doesn't just flood streets; it reverses flow through floor drains and toilet bases in basements that residents use as living space, home offices, and storage. A finished basement in North Edison can go from dry to contaminated in under an hour during a heavy rain event that saturates already-stressed infrastructure.

Multifamily complexes and the commercial corridor along Route 1 add a different dimension. Restaurants, food-service tenants, and logistics warehouses generate grease-heavy wastewater that accelerates drain line failure, and when a shared lateral backs up in a multi-unit building, the contamination can affect multiple units on the ground floor simultaneously.

## Our Sewage Cleanup and Sanitization Process in Edison

Every sewage job starts with containment, not extraction. Before any equipment goes into the affected space, the crew establishes a contamination boundary, puts on appropriate PPE, and assesses whether the source — a failed lateral, a blocked municipal main, or a septic overflow — has been stopped. Cleaning a space before the source is resolved is a waste of everyone's time and money.

Once the source is confirmed stopped, we extract standing sewage using truck-mounted and portable units, then remove all Category 3 (grossly contaminated) materials that cannot be reliably sanitized: drywall below the flood line, saturated carpet and pad, compromised insulation. These materials are bagged, manifested, and disposed of per New Jersey solid waste handling requirements — not left curbside.

After demo, all hard surfaces — concrete, block, treated framing — are treated with EPA-registered disinfectants applied at dwell times sufficient to address bacterial and viral load. We then set drying equipment and monitor moisture levels in the structural assembly until readings confirm the space is dry before any reconstruction begins. The IICRC S500 standard governs our drying protocol, and as an IICRC Certified Firm (#210213), our documentation reflects that at every step.

One thing crews working in Edison's older split-level homes encounter regularly: the finished basement floor is often a thin concrete slab poured directly over compacted fill, with no vapor barrier between the slab and the soil.
