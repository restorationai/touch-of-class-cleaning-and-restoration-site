Most of Irvington's residential fabric dates to the early twentieth century. In the prewar frame houses near Grove Park and the denser multifamily blocks closer to Civic Square, opening a wall for a kitchen or bathroom remodel almost always surfaces at least one of these conditions — and sometimes all three.

## Our Renovation and General Contracting Process in Irvington

Every project in the 07111 ZIP code starts with a thorough pre-construction assessment. We document existing conditions — structural, mechanical, electrical — before scoping work, because surprises mid-project cost more than surprises on day one. For post-damage rebuilds, that assessment integrates with the remediation closeout so reconstruction doesn't begin until affected materials are genuinely dry and cleared.

From there, the process runs in a defined sequence:

- **Permitting and code compliance**: Irvington sits within Essex County jurisdiction, and permit requirements for structural changes, electrical upgrades, and plumbing replacements are enforced. We handle the filings as your licensed contractor of record.
- **Selective demolition**: In older multifamily homes, demo is methodical, not aggressive. We protect adjacent units, identify hazardous materials (lead paint and asbestos are common in pre-1978 stock), and coordinate abatement before proceeding.
- **Rough trades**: Plumbing, electrical, and HVAC rough-in happens before walls close. For kitchens and bathrooms in century-old homes, this phase often includes full supply-line replacement from the main to the fixture.
- **Finish work and inspections**: We schedule inspections at each required phase rather than batching them at the end, which keeps the project moving and avoids the backlog that comes from trying to close out everything at once.

## Local Note: What Irvington's Multifamily Stock Teaches You Fast

Contractors who work Irvington's two- and three-family rowhouses learn quickly that shared party walls are almost never insulated to modern standards, and that a renovation on one unit almost always affects the thermal and acoustic envelope of the adjacent unit. In blocks near Union Avenue, we've found original horsehair plaster on the party-wall side even when the street-facing walls were already drywalled by a previous owner. That plaster absorbs and holds moisture differently than gypsum board — it dries slower, and it can mask elevated moisture readings if you're only checking surface temperature. We probe and meter both sides of shared walls before closing any framing, because a moisture problem that migrates into a neighbor's unit after the job is closed is nobody's idea of a finished project.
