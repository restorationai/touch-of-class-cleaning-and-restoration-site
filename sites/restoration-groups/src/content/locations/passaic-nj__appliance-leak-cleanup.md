## Our Appliance Leak Cleanup Process in Passaic

When we arrive, the first priority is stopping ongoing water intrusion — confirming the supply valve is fully closed and the appliance is isolated. From there, the process follows a structured sequence calibrated to what we actually find in Passaic homes:

**Moisture mapping.** We use thermal imaging cameras and calibrated moisture meters to trace where water has traveled — under flooring, into wall cavities, along baseboards. In a multi-family building, that often means checking the unit below as well.

**Extraction.** Truck-mounted or portable extraction equipment removes standing water and pulls moisture from carpet padding, hardwood gaps, and subfloor seams. In buildings with original wide-plank hardwood floors, we work carefully to preserve the material where drying is still viable.

**Structural drying.** Commercial-grade desiccant and refrigerant dehumidifiers, paired with high-velocity air movers, are positioned to dry the affected cavity to pre-loss moisture levels. Drying timelines are documented per the IICRC S500 standard — typically three to five days depending on material depth and ambient humidity.

**Affected material removal.** Where subfloor, drywall, or cabinetry has absorbed water beyond the drying threshold, we remove and document the material for insurance purposes before reconstruction begins.

**Clearance verification.** Before we close out the job, we re-test moisture readings across the affected area to confirm the structure is dry and no secondary mold risk remains.
