## Why Teaneck Properties See Water Damage Differently

Teaneck's geography works against its housing stock in a specific way. The Hackensack River runs along the township's eastern edge, and Teaneck Creek winds through low-lying areas near the Teaneck Creek Conservancy before draining toward the river. During heavy rainfall, that drainage corridor backs up — and the water has nowhere to go except into finished basements that homeowners have spent years renovating. The problem isn't just surface flooding. Saturated clay-heavy soil along those low corridors holds moisture against foundation walls long after the rain stops, creating hydrostatic pressure that pushes water through block and mortar even when the storm has passed.

Older cast-iron sewer laterals throughout the township are another factor. As they corrode and root-intrude over decades, they're prone to backups during high-volume rain events — turning a drainage problem into a sewage contamination problem that requires a different level of cleanup than clean water extraction alone.

## Our Water Cleanup Process in Teaneck

Every water loss is different, but the sequence is consistent: stop the source if it hasn't been stopped, assess what category of water you're dealing with, extract standing water, and then dry the structure — not just the surface.

In Teaneck's older homes, that drying phase deserves particular attention. Plaster-over-lath walls, common in pre-war construction throughout the Bryant School area and West Teaneck, behave differently than modern drywall. The surface can read dry on a moisture meter while the lath behind it remains saturated. We use thermal imaging cameras alongside pin and pinless meters to map moisture through walls and floors before placing drying equipment, so we're not pulling equipment based on how things look — we're pulling it based on what the readings confirm.

Finished basements, which are nearly universal in Teaneck's residential stock, add another layer. Carpet, padding, and subfloor over a concrete slab trap moisture in ways that require careful extraction sequencing. Drying equipment placement matters: dehumidifiers and air movers need to be positioned to create airflow through the assembly, not just across the surface.

All structural drying follows the IICRC S500 standard. The Restoration Group holds IICRC Certified Firm status (#210213), and our technicians document moisture readings throughout the drying cycle so there's a clear record for your insurance carrier.

Most homeowner policies in New Jersey cover sudden and accidental water losses — a burst pipe, a washing machine failure, an appliance supply line that lets go.
