## Our Reconstruction Process in Ridgewood

Reconstruction starts where mitigation ends — once affected materials are dry, decontaminated, and documented. For a typical Ridgewood loss, that process moves through several defined phases.

**Structural assessment and permitting.** Bergen County and the Borough of Ridgewood both require permits for structural work, and the village's Historic Preservation Commission may have input on exterior elements for properties in designated areas near the Village center. We pull the appropriate permits before any framing, roofing, or exterior cladding work begins, keeping the project compliant from day one.

**Framing and structural repairs.** Damaged or rotted framing members — rim joists, floor joists, wall studs, roof rafters — are sistered or replaced to current IRC standards. On balloon-frame homes common in Ridgewood's older neighborhoods, we take care to maintain fire-blocking continuity that may have been absent in the original construction.

**Mechanical, electrical, and plumbing rough-in.** Reconstruction is the right moment to bring aging systems up to code. We coordinate licensed subcontractors for electrical, plumbing, and HVAC rough-in work, scheduling inspections in sequence so the project doesn't stall waiting on a single trade.

**Insulation, drywall, and finishes.** We match finish materials to the existing home where possible — critical in Ridgewood, where mismatched trim profiles or ceiling heights stand out in pre-war interiors. On commercial losses along East Ridgewood Avenue, we prioritize timeline to minimize business interruption.

**Final inspection and punch-list.** Every project closes with a walk-through against the original scope, photo documentation for the insurance file, and sign-off from the relevant municipal inspector.
