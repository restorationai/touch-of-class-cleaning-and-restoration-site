## Why Irvington Properties See Elevated Mold Risk

The housing stock along the Springfield Avenue corridor and into neighborhoods like Grove Park and Chancellor Avenue was largely built between 1910 and 1945. Those homes rely on galvanized steel supply pipes that corrode from the inside out — a pinhole leak inside a wall cavity can weep silently for weeks before a stain appears on the plaster. Cast-iron drain lines develop root intrusion and joint failures that back up into finished basements, which are extremely common in Irvington's tight, attached housing. Add the Elizabeth River watershed's tendency to flood low-lying lots during heavy storms, and you have a community where moisture events are frequent, sometimes invisible, and almost always reach organic material — wood joists, plaster lath, paper-faced insulation — that mold thrives on.

Prewar construction also means balloon-frame wall cavities that run floor-to-ceiling without fire blocking. Water that enters at the roof or a second-floor window can travel all the way to the basement sill plate before anyone notices it, spreading mold spores through the entire cavity along the way.

## Our Mold Remediation Process in Irvington

Every job starts with a visual inspection and moisture mapping using thermal imaging and calibrated moisture meters. In Irvington's older plaster-and-lath walls, moisture readings behave differently than in modern drywall — plaster holds water longer and releases it more slowly, so we extend our drying assessment window accordingly before declaring an area ready for remediation.

Once the affected zone is identified, we establish negative-air containment using 6-mil poly barriers and HEPA-filtered air scrubbers. This is especially important in multifamily rowhouses — common in Irvington Center and along Union Avenue — where an open wall cavity can share air with an adjacent unit. Negative pressure keeps disturbed spores from migrating to neighboring living spaces.

Remediation itself follows the IICRC S520 standard: controlled removal of unsalvageable porous materials, HEPA vacuuming of framing and surfaces, application of an EPA-registered antimicrobial treatment, and encapsulation where structurally appropriate. We photograph every step for insurance documentation. Before containment comes down, air samples confirm that spore counts have returned to normal ambient levels.
