## Our Basement Flooding Cleanup Process in Linden

The first thing we do on arrival is assess the water source and stop it if it hasn't been stopped already. An active supply-line break requires isolation at the shutoff; a backed-up drain may need a plumber on-site before extraction begins. Once the source is controlled, we use truck-mounted and portable extractors to pull standing water, then transition to air movers and industrial dehumidifiers calibrated to the space.

Finished basements — which are extremely common in Linden's postwar housing stock — require more careful work than open concrete utility spaces. Framed walls with fiberglass batt insulation trap moisture invisibly; we use thermal imaging and moisture meters to map the full extent of saturation before deciding what can be dried in place and what needs to come out. Wet insulation almost always has to be removed. Drywall with wicking damage typically does too. We document every affected material photographically, which matters when you're filing with your homeowner's carrier.

Here's something that comes up repeatedly on jobs in Sunnyside and the West End: the original basement walls in many of these homes were finished with a thin layer of plaster over masonry block rather than standard drywall over framing. Plaster absorbs water more slowly than drywall, so it doesn't always show obvious surface damage — but it also releases moisture more slowly during the drying phase.
