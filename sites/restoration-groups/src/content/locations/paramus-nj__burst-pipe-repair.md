## Why Paramus Properties See Burst Pipe Failures More Than You'd Expect

Paramus was largely built out between 1950 and 1975. ## Our Burst Pipe Cleanup and Repair Process in Paramus

The first priority when we arrive is stopping the source. If the homeowner hasn't already shut the main, we locate and close it — in most Paramus ranches and colonials the main shutoff is in the utility room or near the water meter at the front foundation wall. From there the process follows a defined sequence calibrated to what we actually find:

**1. Standing water extraction.** Truck-mounted and portable extractors pull bulk water from finished basements, hardwood floors, and carpet. In slab-on-grade homes — less common in Paramus but present in some 1950s ranches — we pay close attention to water that has migrated under flooring with nowhere to drain.

**2. Structural moisture mapping.** Thermal imaging cameras and calibrated moisture meters let us trace water migration inside wall cavities and under subfloors before we open anything. This matters in the older homes here because water travels farther than it appears to on the surface.

**3. Controlled demolition and drying.** We remove only the material that can't be dried in place — saturated drywall, compromised insulation, buckled flooring — and set a drying system sized to the affected area. Drying is documented daily against the IICRC S500 standard until readings confirm the structure has returned to baseline moisture.

**4. Pipe repair or replacement coordination.** We work alongside licensed plumbers to repair or repipe the failed section before we close any walls. We can coordinate that trade if you don't have a plumber, or work alongside yours.

**5. Rebuild.** Once the structure is dry and the plumber has signed off, our NJ Licensed Home Improvement Contractor crews restore drywall, insulation, flooring, and finish work to pre-loss condition.

Most homeowner policies in New Jersey cover sudden and accidental water discharge — which is exactly what a burst pipe is.
