## Why Hillside Properties See Basement Flooding So Often

Hillside is almost entirely built out with housing stock from the 1920s through the 1950s — colonials, two-families, and postwar capes, most of them sitting on full basements that were finished or partially finished decades after original construction. That era of building used galvanized steel supply lines and cast-iron drains, both of which corrode from the inside out. A galvanized pipe that looks intact from the outside can be running at a fraction of its original diameter, and when pressure spikes during a cold snap or a main-line surge, it fails without warning.

The town's topography compounds the problem. Low-lying ground near the Elizabeth River collects runoff from the surrounding area, and Hillside's combined sewer system — common in Union County municipalities of this age — can reverse-flow during heavy rain events, pushing sewage-contaminated water up through floor drains. Homes near Evergreen Cemetery and along the lower sections of Winans have seen this pattern repeatedly. That contamination category (IICRC Category 3) changes how cleanup must proceed: affected materials require removal rather than drying in place, and the space needs antimicrobial treatment before any reconstruction begins.

## Our Basement Flooding Cleanup Process in Hillside

The first thing that happens when the crew arrives is a moisture and contamination assessment. Knowing whether the water source was a clean supply-line break, a sump failure, or a sewer backup determines every decision that follows — what stays, what goes, and how aggressively the space needs to be treated.

From there, the process moves through four phases:

**Extraction** — Truck-mounted and portable extractors pull standing water from the floor. In Hillside's older basements, that often means working around finished wall assemblies, drop ceilings, and stored belongings. We move what we can and document what we cannot.

**Demolition of unsalvageable materials** — Saturated drywall, insulation, and wood framing that has been in contact with Category 3 water comes out. Trying to dry contaminated materials in place creates a mold and odor problem within days.

**Structural drying** — Commercial-grade desiccant dehumidifiers and high-velocity air movers run continuously, calibrated to the square footage and construction of the space. Drying logs are recorded daily and benchmarked against the IICRC S500 standard for water damage restoration.

**Post-dry verification** — Before any reconstruction, moisture readings in framing, concrete, and remaining wall assemblies confirm the space is dry enough to close up. Skipping this step is how mold problems start.

## Hillside Insurance Coordination

Most standard homeowner policies cover sudden and accidental water losses — a burst pipe or appliance failure — but draw a hard line at flooding from surface water or sewer backup unless the homeowner added a rider. In Hillside, where sewer backups are a documented recurring event, that distinction matters at claim time. The Restoration Group documents the loss thoroughly from arrival: photos, moisture readings, contamination classification, and an itemized scope of work. That documentation package is what adjusters need to process the claim, and we work directly with most major carriers so the homeowner is not left managing the paperwork alone during an already stressful event.
