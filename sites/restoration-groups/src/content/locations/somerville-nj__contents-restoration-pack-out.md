## Why Somerville Properties Face Elevated Contents Risk

Somerville's character is inseparable from its age. The borough's residential neighborhoods — the West End, East Main, Fairview, and the blocks radiating out from the Somerset County Courthouse — are dominated by early-1900s frame construction: Victorians, colonials, and converted two-families with finished basements that were never designed with modern flood loads in mind. Galvanized supply lines in these homes corrode from the inside out, often failing without warning. Cast-iron drains back up when roots infiltrate joints that haven't been inspected in decades.

The geography compounds the risk. Peters Brook and the Raritan River flank the borough, and low-lying streets have seen repeated inundation — most recently during Tropical Storm Ida in 2021, which pushed water into basements and ground-floor units across the borough. When floodwater enters a finished basement full of stored furniture, holiday items, file boxes, and appliances, the contents clock starts immediately. Mold colonization on porous materials can begin within 24 to 48 hours of saturation, which means waiting until the structure is dry before addressing contents is not a viable option.

The Main Street commercial corridor adds a different category of loss. Sprinkler discharges, overnight pipe failures in older mixed-use buildings, and roof leaks above tenant spaces routinely affect merchandise, fixtures, and equipment that cannot simply be moved to a dry corner and left.

## Our Contents Restoration & Pack-Out Process in Somerville

A pack-out is not just loading boxes into a truck. Every item we remove from a Somerville property is inventoried, photographed, and assigned a condition rating before it leaves the site. That documentation matters for insurance claims and for returning items to the right room when restoration is complete.

The process moves in stages:

**Assessment and triage** — We identify what can be restored versus what is a total loss, separating salvageable contents from materials that pose a contamination risk if stored alongside recoverable items.

**Pack-out and transport** — Items are wrapped, boxed, and labeled using a chain-of-custody inventory system. Fragile or high-value pieces — artwork, antiques, electronics — receive individual handling documentation.

**Cleaning and restoration** — Depending on the loss type, contents are processed using ultrasonic cleaning, ozone treatment, thermal fogging for smoke odor, or specialized textile cleaning. Electronics are evaluated by a qualified technician before any power-on attempt.

**Climate-controlled storage** — Items remain in a controlled environment during structure drying and reconstruction, protecting them from secondary damage.

**Return and placement** — Once the structure is cleared, contents are returned and placed per the original inventory, room by room.

Somerville's early-1900s two-families and Victorians frequently have horsehair plaster walls and original wood-lath ceilings in upper floors. When a roof leak or supply-line failure saturates those assemblies, the water wicks laterally much farther than it would in modern drywall construction — which means contents in rooms adjacent to the visible damage are often affected too.
