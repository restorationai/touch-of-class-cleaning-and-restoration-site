When that happens, the clock matters: mold can begin colonizing wet framing and drywall in as little as 24 to 48 hours.

## Why Edison Homes See More Appliance Leak Damage Than You'd Expect

Edison's housing pattern is part of the story. The Clara Barton and Stelton neighborhoods are dense with cape cods and ranches that were finished out over decades — original kitchens dropped onto concrete slabs, laundry rooms tucked into partially below-grade basements, and refrigerators pushed against exterior walls where condensation from ice maker lines has nowhere to drain. Older cast-iron drain stacks in these homes corrode from the inside out, so even a modest dishwasher overflow can back up rather than drain cleanly.

The other factor is the water itself. Edison draws from the Middlesex County municipal system, which delivers moderately hard water. Over years of use, that mineral content accelerates corrosion inside braided steel supply hoses — the kind behind washing machines and under refrigerators — making hose failure more likely in homes that haven't had those connections replaced in a decade or more. A hose that looks fine on the outside can be compromised at the fitting.

Multifamily buildings along the Route 1 corridor and in North Edison add another layer of complexity: when an appliance leak on an upper floor migrates through a subfloor, it becomes a multi-unit loss that involves both the property owner and individual tenants' insurance policies simultaneously.

## Our Appliance Leak Cleanup Process in Edison

The first priority on any appliance leak call is stopping the source — shutting off the supply valve behind the machine or at the main if the valve is seized. From there, the process follows a structured drying protocol calibrated to what we actually find:

**Moisture mapping** — Thermal imaging and calibrated moisture meters trace exactly where water traveled. In Edison's finished basements, that often means water has wicked up into drywall several inches above the visible waterline and migrated under laminate or engineered hardwood flooring before it's visible.

**Extraction and material assessment** — Standing water comes out first with truck-mounted or portable extraction equipment. We assess whether flooring, baseboard, and lower wall sections can be dried in place or need to be removed to prevent hidden mold growth. Saving materials is always the goal, but trapping moisture behind intact drywall creates a worse problem weeks later.

**Structural drying** — Industrial air movers and low-grain refrigerant dehumidifiers run in a calculated configuration based on the room's cubic footage and measured humidity. We monitor daily with written moisture readings so the drying curve is documented — that documentation matters for your insurance claim.

**Post-dry verification** — We don't close out a job until moisture readings return to normal range for the material type. In Edison's older homes, that sometimes means an extra day of drying in a basement that holds ambient humidity from the surrounding soil.

## Insurance Coordination for Edison Appliance Leak Claims

Sudden and accidental appliance leaks are generally covered under standard homeowners' policies — the key word being "sudden." A hose that failed this morning is a different claim than a slow drip that's been staining the cabinet floor for months. We photograph and document the loss thoroughly from the moment we arrive, producing the moisture logs, equipment records, and scope of work that adjusters need to process the claim. We bill most major carriers directly and can communicate with your adjuster on your behalf, which matters when you're also dealing with a displaced refrigerator and a wet basement.
