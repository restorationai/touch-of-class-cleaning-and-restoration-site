## Why Paterson Properties See Basement Flooding So Often

Paterson's geography sets the stage. The city sits directly on the Passaic River, and the low-lying streets below the Great Falls drop toward the riverbank in a way that gives stormwater nowhere to go during heavy rain events. The combined sewer system — common throughout older Passaic County municipalities — backs up under sustained rainfall, pushing sewage and groundwater up through floor drains and cast-iron pipe connections that were never designed to handle modern storm volumes.

The housing stock amplifies the problem. Dense mill-worker rowhomes built before 1920, the kind you'll find throughout Sandy Hill and the Eastside, were constructed with stone rubble or unreinforced brick foundations. Those foundations wick groundwater laterally, especially after a wet winter or a fast spring thaw. Knob-and-tube remnants in the walls mean electrical safety checks are part of any basement entry. Converted silk-era industrial buildings — now apartments throughout the downtown ZIP codes — carry standpipe and sprinkler systems that generate their own flooding losses when fittings fail or pipes freeze.

## Our Basement Flooding Cleanup Process in Paterson

The first thing we do when we arrive is assess the water category. A slow foundation seep is Category 1; a sewer backup pushing up through a floor drain is Category 3 — contaminated water that requires full PPE, antimicrobial treatment, and different disposal protocols. In Paterson's rental stock, sewer backups are common enough that we treat any drain overflow as contaminated until we can confirm otherwise.

Once the source is identified and safe to enter, we extract standing water using truck-mounted and portable extraction units. In Paterson's narrow rowhouse basements — often under 600 square feet with low clearance — portable units do the heavy lifting because large truck-mount hoses can't always reach the back corners. After bulk extraction, we place commercial desiccant dehumidifiers and high-velocity air movers calibrated to the actual square footage and material types present. Drying logs are recorded daily so you — and your insurance adjuster — have documentation of moisture readings from start to finish, dried per the IICRC S500 standard.

Demolition is selective. We remove only what the moisture readings and visual inspection confirm is unsalvageable. In older Paterson homes with plaster walls and original wood framing, that judgment call matters: plaster is dense and slow to release moisture, so we use thermal imaging and pin-type moisture meters to find hidden saturation before we decide what stays and what comes out.
