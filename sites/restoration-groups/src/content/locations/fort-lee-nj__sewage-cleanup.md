## Our Sewage Cleanup and Sanitization Process in Fort Lee

Raw sewage is classified as Category 3 water — the most contaminated classification under the IICRC S500 standard — which means the removal and sanitization protocol is more involved than a standard water loss. The process begins with containment: we isolate the affected area to prevent cross-contamination to clean spaces, which is especially important in high-rise buildings where HVAC systems can distribute airborne pathogens across multiple units.

Extraction comes next. Standing sewage is removed using truck-mounted and portable extraction equipment, followed by the removal of any porous materials — drywall, flooring, insulation — that cannot be adequately disinfected. In Fort Lee's older multifamily buildings, this often includes original hardwood subfloors or plaster assemblies that have absorbed contaminated water deeply. After physical removal, all affected surfaces are treated with EPA-registered disinfectants rated for Category 3 losses. We apply antimicrobial agents to structural cavities, not just visible surfaces, because sewage contamination wicks into wall framing and concrete block faster than most homeowners expect.

Drying and dehumidification follow sanitization, using commercial-grade desiccant and refrigerant dehumidifiers. In Fort Lee's high-rise units, where concrete construction slows evaporation compared to wood-frame homes, drying timelines typically run longer — we monitor moisture readings daily and document every reading for insurance purposes.
