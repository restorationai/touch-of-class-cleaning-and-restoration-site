## Why Rahway Properties Are Particularly Vulnerable to Flood Damage

Rahway's geography works against homeowners during heavy rain events. The Rahway River winds through the western side of the city and through Rahway River Park, and its floodplain extends into residential blocks that don't look like riverfront property on a dry day. Downtown Rahway and the streets closest to the Main Street business district sit at elevations where stormwater has nowhere to go quickly — the city's combined sewer system, like most in older Union County municipalities, can back up under sustained rainfall and push water into basements through floor drains and toilets.

The housing stock compounds the risk. The Victorian-era and 1920s homes near downtown and in the Hazelwood and Leesville neighborhoods were built before modern waterproofing standards existed. Most have original cast-iron drain lines that have corroded or partially collapsed over a century of use, and finished basements that were added decades after original construction — often without proper sump pits or vapor barriers. When those basements flood, you're dealing with water that has soaked into horsehair plaster, old-growth framing, and concrete block foundations that wick moisture for days after the visible water is gone.

The transit-oriented development around Rahway Train Station adds a different category of loss: multi-unit residential buildings and commercial spaces with fire suppression systems, elevator pits, and shared mechanical rooms where a single sprinkler malfunction or roof drain failure can affect multiple tenants simultaneously.

## Our Flood Damage Restoration Process in Rahway

When we arrive at a flooded property in Rahway, the first step is a moisture mapping survey — not just the obvious wet areas, but the walls, subfloors, and structural cavities where water has migrated. In older downtown homes, water travels farther than it does in newer construction because aged framing and masonry are more porous.

From there, the process follows a structured sequence:

- **Water extraction** using truck-mounted and portable extractors, including specialty tools for crawl spaces and tight basement layouts common in pre-war Rahway homes
- **Controlled demolition** where necessary — removing saturated drywall, flooring, and insulation to the flood cut line so drying equipment can reach the structure itself
- **Drying and dehumidification** using commercial-grade desiccant and refrigerant dehumidifiers, with daily moisture readings documented to IICRC S500 standard until structural materials reach target values
- **Antimicrobial treatment** on affected surfaces before reconstruction begins
- **Reconstruction coordination** — framing, drywall, flooring, and finish work handled in-house so you're not managing a separate contractor

As an IICRC Certified Firm (#210213) and NJ Licensed Home Improvement Contractor, we document every phase with photos and moisture logs, which your insurance adjuster will need for a complete claim.
