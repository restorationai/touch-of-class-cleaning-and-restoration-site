## Our Mold Inspection and Testing Process in West Orange

A thorough mold assessment here isn't a quick visual walkthrough. Given the age and construction of most West Orange homes, the inspection starts with a systematic review of the building envelope — foundation walls, rim joists, crawl spaces if present, and any area that received water intrusion during past storm events.

The process typically follows these steps:

**Visual inspection and moisture mapping.** Using calibrated moisture meters and thermal imaging, we identify areas of elevated moisture content behind finished surfaces — the places where mold hides long before it's visible. In older plaster-and-lath construction common in the Llewellyn Park area, moisture readings behave differently than in modern drywall, so we adjust baseline thresholds accordingly.

**Air sampling.** We collect indoor air samples from affected zones and a control sample from outside or an unaffected room. Samples are sent to an accredited third-party laboratory. Results identify mold genera, spore concentrations, and whether indoor counts are elevated relative to outdoor baseline — the core data you need for any insurance claim or remediation decision.

**Surface and bulk sampling.** Where visible growth or staining is present, tape lift or swab samples confirm species identification. Bulk samples from suspect building materials help determine whether remediation requires surface treatment or full material removal.

**Written report.** You receive a documented findings report with lab results, moisture readings, photographs, and a clear summary of what was found, where, and what it means — written in plain language, not just lab codes.

The IICRC Certified Firm credential (#210213) means the assessment follows recognized industry standards, which matters when you're presenting findings to an insurance adjuster or a prospective home buyer.
