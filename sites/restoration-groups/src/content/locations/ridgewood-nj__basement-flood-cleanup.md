## Our Basement Flood Cleanup Process in Ridgewood

Every basement flood cleanup begins with a thorough moisture assessment — not just the visible standing water, but the readings behind finished walls, under subfloor assemblies, and inside stud cavities. In Ridgewood's older homes, this step matters more than it might in newer construction. Plaster-and-lath walls, common in pre-war homes near Van Neste Square and throughout the Willard neighborhood, absorb water differently than modern drywall. The surface can test dry while the lath behind it remains saturated, which means extraction timelines need to account for that slower moisture release.

Once moisture mapping is complete, standing water is removed with truck-mounted extraction equipment. Structural drying follows using commercial-grade desiccant and refrigerant dehumidifiers, with air movers positioned to pull moisture through wall cavities rather than just across surfaces. Our work follows the IICRC S500 standard for water damage restoration — the industry benchmark for drying validation — and we document readings throughout so there's a clear record for your insurance carrier.

If flooring, insulation, or framing has absorbed water beyond recoverable thresholds, controlled demolition removes only what's necessary. We photograph everything before and after for the claim file.
