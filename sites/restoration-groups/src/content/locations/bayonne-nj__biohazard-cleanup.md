## Why Bayonne Properties Present Unique Biohazard Challenges

Bayonne's housing stock tells you a lot before you open the front door. Early 20th-century frame homes and brick rowhomes — common throughout Uptown and Downtown Bayonne — were built with plaster walls, unfinished subfloor cavities, and cast-iron drain stacks that have been patched and re-patched over decades. Porous materials like plaster, older hardwood, and unsealed concrete absorb biological contamination more deeply than modern surfaces, which means surface cleaning alone is never sufficient. Proper remediation requires removing affected material to a confirmed clean substrate, not just treating what is visible.

Dense rental occupancy adds another layer. Multi-unit buildings mean that a loss in one unit can affect common hallways, shared mechanical rooms, or adjacent apartments. ## Our Biohazard Cleanup Process in Bayonne

Every job begins with a thorough assessment — identifying the full extent of contamination, including areas that are not immediately visible. Biological material can migrate into subfloor gaps, wall cavities, and HVAC returns, particularly in older Bayonne homes where construction tolerances were loose and gaps between structural members are common.

Once the scope is confirmed, the work area is contained and the team works in appropriate personal protective equipment throughout. Affected porous materials are removed, bagged, and transported as regulated waste through licensed disposal channels — not left curbside or handled informally. Hard surfaces are cleaned with EPA-registered disinfectants, and the space is treated to address any residual biological hazard before a final verification step.

Documentation is produced throughout: photographs, a written scope of work, and disposal records. That paperwork matters when a property owner needs to demonstrate to a landlord, an insurer, or a future tenant that the space was properly remediated by a certified firm.
