## Why New Brunswick Properties See Severe Storm Damage

The Raritan River defines the city's southern edge at Boyd Park, and its flood history is not ancient — Tropical Storm Ida in September 2021 pushed water into low-lying riverfront areas, leaving behind saturated foundations and contaminated crawl spaces that owners discovered weeks later when the smell arrived. That same storm exposed how quickly stormwater overwhelms the city's older combined sewer infrastructure, backing water into basement apartments and ground-floor retail in the 08901 ZIP code.

Above grade, the housing stock creates its own vulnerabilities. Pre-1950 rowhouses throughout the Fifth Ward and near Rutgers Village were built with wood-framed roofs and minimal attic ventilation — conditions that let wind-driven rain migrate deep into roof assemblies before a single interior stain appears. Mature street trees planted close to those structures mean that a 60-mph gust doesn't just strip shingles; it drops 40-foot limbs onto rooflines that were never engineered for that kind of point load. Downtown and the Robert Wood Johnson University Hospital corridor add a different risk category: flat or low-slope commercial roofing where ponding after a storm can exceed design load within hours.

## Our Storm Damage Restoration Process in New Brunswick

Every storm job starts with a documented damage assessment before any debris is moved. We photograph and measure structural breaches, map water intrusion paths, and identify secondary hazards — downed utility lines, compromised load-bearing members, exposed insulation — so nothing is missed in the insurance claim and nothing is disturbed that could worsen the loss.

From there, the work breaks into three overlapping phases:

**Emergency stabilization** covers the first hours: tarping and board-up for breached roofs and windows, temporary shoring if a tree strike has shifted a wall or rafter, and extraction of any standing water before it wicks into subfloor assemblies. In New Brunswick's older rowhouses, that extraction step matters — original hardwood and plank subfloors can absorb a surprising volume of water before the surface looks wet.

**Structural drying and debris removal** follows. We use industrial desiccant and refrigerant dehumidifiers calibrated to the building type, with moisture mapping logged daily so drying targets are met rather than estimated. Tree debris removal is coordinated with a licensed tree service when root systems or large canopy sections require rigging — we manage that handoff so you have one point of contact.

**Repair and reconstruction** closes the project: roofing, siding, windows, interior framing, insulation, and finish work completed under our NJ Licensed Home Improvement Contractor credentials, with all permits pulled through the City of New Brunswick's building department.
