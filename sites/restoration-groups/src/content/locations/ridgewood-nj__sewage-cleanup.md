## Our Sewage Cleanup and Sanitization Process in Ridgewood

Sewage water is classified as Category 3 — the most contaminated category recognized by the IICRC S500 standard — which means the cleanup protocol is fundamentally different from a clean-water pipe burst. Every material the sewage contacted must be treated as potentially contaminated, and porous materials that cannot be fully decontaminated need to be removed.

Here's how the process unfolds on a typical Ridgewood job:

**Containment and safety assessment.** Before extraction begins, the affected area is isolated to prevent cross-contamination to upper floors or adjacent rooms. Technicians wear appropriate PPE throughout.

**Extraction and removal.** Standing sewage water is extracted using truck-mounted or portable units. Saturated porous materials — carpet, pad, drywall, insulation, wood trim — are removed and bagged for disposal per NJ Department of Environmental Protection guidelines.

**Antimicrobial treatment.** All affected hard surfaces, framing, and concrete are treated with EPA-registered antimicrobial agents. This step is not optional on a Category 3 loss — it's the difference between a sanitized space and one that will develop dangerous microbial growth within 24 to 48 hours.

**Structural drying.** After removal and treatment, commercial desiccant dehumidifiers and air movers are positioned to dry the structural cavity. In Ridgewood's older homes, this often means drying exposed stone or brick foundation walls in addition to wood framing — materials that hold moisture differently than modern poured concrete.

**Post-remediation verification.** Moisture readings are documented before equipment is removed. We provide a written scope and photo documentation, which your insurance adjuster will need.
