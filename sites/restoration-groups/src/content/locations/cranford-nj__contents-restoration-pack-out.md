## Why Cranford Homes Face Distinctive Contents Challenges

Cranford's housing stock is largely colonials and capes built between roughly 1900 and the 1940s — full-basement homes with plaster walls, original hardwood floors, and decades of accumulated furniture and personal property. That construction era means a few things for contents when a loss event hits.

Plaster walls absorb and hold moisture longer than modern drywall, which means the interior environment stays humid well after surface readings look acceptable. Upholstered furniture and paper goods left inside during that extended drying window absorb secondary moisture damage even if the initial flood didn't reach them directly. Older homes also tend to have more irreplaceable items — built-in cabinetry, antique furniture passed down through families, original hardwood pieces — where restoration is worth attempting rather than defaulting to replacement.

The repeat-flood history here also means many homeowners have navigated NFIP claims before. They know that thorough documentation of contents — photographs, itemized inventories, condition notes — is what separates a fair settlement from a frustrating one. We build that documentation into every pack-out from the start.

## Our Contents Restoration & Pack-Out Process in Cranford

The process begins with a room-by-room contents inventory before anything is moved. Each item is photographed, logged by category, and assessed for restorability. Items with active contamination risk — anything touched by category 3 floodwater from the Rahway River, for instance — are handled and packaged separately under protocols consistent with IICRC standards.

From there, contents are carefully packed and transported to a climate-controlled facility for cleaning and restoration. Depending on the item, that may involve ultrasonic cleaning for hard goods, ozone or thermal treatment for odor in soft goods and documents, or specialized textile cleaning for upholstery and rugs. Electronics and artwork are assessed individually.

Throughout the process, the inventory stays accessible to you and to your insurance adjuster. When structural drying and repairs are complete, contents are returned, unpacked, and placed — so you're not managing a warehouse of boxes on top of everything else.

## Coordinating with Insurance After a Cranford Flood

Because so many properties near Nomahegan Park and along the Riverside Drive corridor carry both standard homeowners policies and NFIP flood policies, contents claims here often involve two separate adjusters working from different coverage structures. We document losses in a format that works for both: line-item inventories with pre-loss value estimates, condition photographs, and restoration cost records that carriers can evaluate against replacement cost.

For NFIP claims specifically, the contents coverage is separate from the building coverage and has its own documentation requirements. Getting that paperwork right from the pack-out stage — rather than reconstructing it later — makes a measurable difference in how claims resolve.
