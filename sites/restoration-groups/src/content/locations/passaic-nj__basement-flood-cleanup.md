## Why Passaic Basements Flood — and Why Cleanup Is More Complicated Here

Passaic is one of New Jersey's densest small cities, built up largely in the early twentieth century. The housing stock — two- and three-family frame houses, brick multi-family walkups, older rowhomes — was designed long before modern waterproofing standards existed. Foundations are often poured concrete or stone rubble with no interior drainage membrane. When the water table rises after a heavy rain, hydrostatic pressure pushes moisture through every crack.

The drainage infrastructure compounds the problem. Many blocks still rely on combined sewer systems, meaning heavy rainfall can overwhelm the line and send sewage-laced water back up through floor drains. That changes the cleanup category entirely — what looks like a simple flood is often a Category 3 (grossly contaminated) loss that requires full personal protective equipment, antimicrobial treatment, and in many cases the removal of porous materials that cannot be safely dried in place.

Rental density adds another layer. In neighborhoods like the Third Ward and Rutherford Heights, a single flooded basement can affect multiple tenant units above it, and landlords face both habitability obligations and the clock — mold colonization in an unventilated Passaic basement can begin within 24 to 48 hours of saturation.

## Our Basement Flood Cleanup Process in Passaic

Every job starts with a moisture assessment before a single pump is placed. Older plaster walls and original hardwood subfloors common in pre-war Passaic construction hold water differently than modern materials — surface readings can look acceptable while the structural framing behind them stays saturated. We use thermal imaging and penetrating moisture meters to map the actual wet zone, not just the visible one.

From there, the process follows a structured sequence:

- **Water extraction** — truck-mounted and portable extractors remove standing water, including from below floor-level cavities where it pools under concrete slabs.
- **Contamination assessment** — if sewer backup is involved, affected materials are documented and handled under Category 3 protocols per IICRC S500 standards.
- **Structural drying** — commercial desiccant or refrigerant dehumidifiers and high-velocity air movers run in a calculated configuration based on the basement's square footage, ceiling height, and material types. In Passaic's older brick structures, drying timelines often run longer than in newer construction because masonry releases absorbed moisture slowly.
- **Documentation** — daily moisture readings are logged and shared with you and your insurance adjuster throughout the drying cycle.
- **Antimicrobial treatment** — applied to affected surfaces after drying targets are met, not before.

## Passaic Insurance Coordination

Most standard homeowner policies cover sudden and accidental water intrusion but exclude flood damage from rising surface water — a distinction that matters enormously in a Passaic riverfront loss. Sewer backup coverage, if you carry it, is typically a separate rider.
