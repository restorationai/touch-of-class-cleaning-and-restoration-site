## Why Hoboken Properties Flood the Way They Do

Hoboken's vulnerability isn't just about storms. The city's elevation rarely exceeds a few feet above sea level, and its aging combined sewer infrastructure — designed for a much smaller population — regularly surpasses capacity during moderate rainfall, let alone events like Superstorm Sandy in 2012 or the remnants of Ida in 2021, which submerged basement units across the 07030 ZIP code citywide. Below-grade apartments and garden units in the brownstone rowhouses that line streets from Uptown down through Downtown Hoboken are structurally exposed: their floor drains connect directly to the same lines that carry stormwater, so when the system backs up, water enters from below rather than above — and no sump pump stops that.

The housing stock compounds the challenge. Most of Hoboken's residential buildings predate 1940. That means original cast-iron plumbing stacks shared between units, brick foundations without modern waterproofing membranes, and wood-framed floor assemblies that wick moisture laterally through an entire floor plate before visible damage appears. ## Our Flood Damage Restoration Process in Hoboken

Every flood loss starts with a thorough assessment before any equipment is placed. In Hoboken's narrow rowhouses and multi-unit buildings, that means mapping moisture migration through shared walls and floors using thermal imaging and calibrated moisture meters — not just treating the visible wet area. Water in a ground-floor unit frequently travels into the subfloor and up into the unit above through wall cavities before anyone notices.

Once the scope is documented, we extract standing water with truck-mounted and portable units suited to tight stairwells and below-grade spaces. Drying follows the IICRC S500 standard, using industrial desiccant and refrigerant dehumidifiers alongside high-velocity air movers positioned to address cavity drying — not just surface evaporation. In pre-war plaster construction, which is common throughout Downtown Hoboken and Midtown, drying timelines run longer than in modern drywall builds; plaster releases absorbed moisture slowly and requires more monitoring cycles before readings stabilize. We document moisture readings daily and share them with you and your adjuster throughout the process.

If structural materials — subfloor sheathing, baseboards, lower wall sections — have absorbed beyond salvageable thresholds, we perform controlled demolition and prep the space for reconstruction. ## Local Note: What Hoboken's Sewer Backups Mean for Your Drain and Floor Assembly

Here's something that matters specifically in Hoboken: sewer backup events introduce Category 3 — or "black water" — contamination, which carries a different remediation protocol than a clean-water pipe burst. When the combined sewer system reverses into a basement, the water entering through floor drains is not rainwater. It contains sewage. That means porous materials that contacted the water — concrete block, carpet, drywall below the flood line, wood framing — are treated as contaminated and typically require removal rather than drying in place. Homeowners who attempt to dry out a sewer backup with shop vacs and fans are spreading aerosolized contamination, not cleaning it. This distinction matters enormously in Hoboken's dense housing stock, where the same event can affect four units stacked vertically. If your building flooded during a heavy rain event and you're not certain whether the water came from above or below, assume Category 3 until a professional assessment says otherwise.
