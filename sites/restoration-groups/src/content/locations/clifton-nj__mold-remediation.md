## Why Clifton Properties See Mold Issues

Clifton's housing stock is a significant part of the equation. When those lines weep or fail slowly behind a finished wall, moisture accumulates for weeks before anyone notices. Botany Village adds another layer of complexity. ## Our Mold Remediation Process in Clifton

Every job starts with a thorough visual inspection and moisture mapping using thermal imaging and pin-type meters. In Clifton's postwar basement-heavy homes, that means checking not just the visible wall surface but the rim joist cavity, the subfloor above, and the HVAC return plenum — all common secondary spread points after a flood event.

Once the affected area is identified, we establish negative-air containment using 6-mil poly barriers and HEPA-filtered negative air machines. This keeps disturbed mold spores from migrating to unaffected rooms during removal. Contaminated drywall, insulation, and porous building materials are bagged and removed per EPA guidelines before any antimicrobial treatment is applied to the structural framing.

Drying comes next. We use commercial-grade desiccant and refrigerant dehumidifiers alongside high-velocity air movers, and we monitor daily with calibrated moisture meters until the structure reaches dry standard per the IICRC S520. In Clifton's humid continental climate — where summer dewpoints regularly climb into the mid-60s — achieving and holding those dry targets takes longer than it would in a drier region, and we don't close out a job until the readings confirm it.

Final steps include a post-remediation visual inspection and, where the scope warrants it, coordination with an independent industrial hygienist for air clearance testing before any reconstruction begins.
