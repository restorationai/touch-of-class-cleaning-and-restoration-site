Clifton's housing stock tells a big part of the story. ## Our Storm Damage Restoration Process in Clifton

Every storm loss is different, but the sequence we follow is consistent and calibrated to what we actually find in Clifton properties.

**1. Emergency stabilization.** The first priority is stopping active intrusion — tarping breached roofs, boarding compromised windows, and pumping standing water before it wicks further into structural cavities. In split-level homes, water that enters at grade can travel down into a finished lower level within minutes.

**2. Moisture mapping.** We use thermal imaging cameras and calibrated moisture meters to trace water migration behind walls and under flooring. In Clifton's postwar homes, original hardwood subfloors and plaster-over-lath wall assemblies hold water in ways that modern drywall doesn't, and a visual inspection alone will miss saturated cavities that will grow mold within 24 to 48 hours.

**3. Structural drying.** Industrial desiccant dehumidifiers and high-velocity air movers are positioned according to the IICRC S500 standard for water damage restoration. Drying timelines in finished basements with concrete block walls typically run longer than above-grade spaces — we monitor daily and adjust equipment placement based on psychrometric readings, not a fixed-day schedule.

**4. Debris removal and damage documentation.** Wind-driven tree limbs, displaced roofing materials, and saturated insulation are removed and photographed systematically. That documentation package is what your insurance adjuster needs to process the claim accurately.

**5. Reconstruction.** Once the structure is dry and the adjuster has signed off, our crews handle framing, drywall, insulation, and finish work so you're dealing with one contractor from first call to final walkthrough.
