## Why Hillside's Building Stock Shapes Every Renovation Decision

Most of the residential streets running between Route 22 and Evergreen Cemetery were built between 1925 and 1955. That era of construction means a few things that show up on every job estimate: knob-and-tube or early Romex wiring that can't simply be patched around, cast-iron drain stacks that have been slowly corroding for decades, and plaster-over-lath walls that hide moisture damage far longer than modern drywall would. A kitchen remodel in a Winans-area colonial isn't just a cabinet swap — it's often a discovery process that uncovers a failing drain line or a rim joist that absorbed years of condensation before anyone noticed.

The two-family homes common throughout 07205 add another layer. Renovation work in an owner-occupied unit frequently borders a tenant's living space, which means sequencing trades carefully, maintaining egress, and coordinating inspections without displacing a household. We plan for that from the first walkthrough, not after demo day.

## Our Renovation and General Contracting Process in Hillside

Every project starts with a scoped assessment — not a sales visit. For post-damage rebuilds, that means reviewing the adjuster's scope line by line and flagging anything the initial estimate missed (concealed structural damage, code-upgrade requirements triggered by the permit, hazardous material abatement). For elective remodels, it means understanding what the homeowner actually uses the space for before drawing a single layout.

From there, the process moves through permitting with the Hillside Township building department, which requires specific documentation for structural changes and any work touching the building envelope. We pull permits, coordinate inspections, and manage the subcontractor schedule so the homeowner isn't acting as a general contractor by default. Rough-in trades — plumbing, electrical, HVAC — are sequenced before insulation and drywall close everything back up. Finish work follows: tile, cabinetry, flooring, paint, trim. On post-damage jobs, we photograph every phase for the insurance file.

## Coordinating with Insurance on Post-Damage Rebuilds

A significant share of renovation calls in Hillside come after a covered loss — a sump-pump failure during a summer downpour, a sprinkler discharge at a commercial property along the Conant Street industrial corridor, or a kitchen fire that left smoke residue embedded in every cabinet and ceiling joist. In those cases, the rebuild scope is driven partly by the insurance estimate and partly by what the structure actually needs once demo reveals conditions the adjuster couldn't see from the surface.

The Restoration Group documents the loss from day one — photographs, moisture readings, material inventories — and supplements the claim when hidden damage surfaces during demo. We communicate directly with adjusters so the homeowner isn't translating between contractor and insurance company throughout a process that can run weeks. Being an IICRC Certified Firm (Firm #210213) means our documentation follows a standard that carriers recognize, which reduces back-and-forth on scope disputes.

## Local Note: What Hillside Inspectors Look for on Older Remodels

Hillside Township inspectors routinely flag two issues on renovations in pre-1960 homes that catch out-of-town contractors off guard. First, any permitted kitchen or bathroom remodel that disturbs more than a threshold of existing material can trigger a lead-paint compliance requirement under NJ state rules — relevant in nearly every home near Hillside Center or along the older residential blocks off Conant Street. Second, finished basement conversions that were done without permits in prior decades (common in the cape-style homes throughout Saybrook) often need to be brought to current egress and electrical code before the new work can be signed off. Budgeting time for that remediation upfront prevents the kind of mid-project stop-work order that stalls a job for weeks.
