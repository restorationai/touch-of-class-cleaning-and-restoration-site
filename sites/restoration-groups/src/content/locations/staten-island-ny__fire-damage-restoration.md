## Why Staten Island Properties See Distinct Fire Damage Challenges

Staten Island's housing ranges from early-1900s wood-frame colonials near St. George and Port Richmond to dense postwar Cape Cods and split-levels throughout the South Shore, to newer townhome clusters closer to the Staten Island Mall corridor. That variety creates layered fire and smoke damage patterns that differ from borough to borough.

Older homes — particularly those built before 1960 — commonly feature original plaster-and-lath wall assemblies, balloon-frame construction, and uninsulated interior cavities. In a balloon-frame house, fire and smoke travel vertically inside the wall cavity from the basement to the attic with almost no resistance, meaning visible char on a first-floor wall may be the smallest part of the story. Smoke odor compounds this: plaster is highly porous and absorbs volatile organic compounds from smoke more aggressively than modern drywall, requiring more aggressive deodorization protocols — thermal fogging, hydroxyl generation, or ozone treatment — rather than surface cleaning alone.

Statens Island's climate adds another layer. High summer humidity, combined with the moisture introduced during fire suppression, accelerates secondary mold colonization — mold can begin establishing within 24 to 48 hours in wet, soot-contaminated materials. Coordinating fire cleanup and moisture mitigation simultaneously is essential, not sequential.

## Our Fire and Smoke Restoration Process on Staten Island

Every job starts with a structured assessment before any debris is moved. We document the full extent of char, smoke migration, and water intrusion from suppression — photographing affected materials room by room, which also creates the foundation of your insurance claim file.

From there, the process follows a defined sequence:

**1. Emergency board-up and stabilization.** Broken windows, compromised rooflines, and open wall sections are secured to prevent weather intrusion and unauthorized entry — important in densely populated neighborhoods where adjacent properties are close.

**2. Controlled demolition of unsalvageable materials.** Char-damaged framing, saturated insulation, and smoke-saturated drywall or plaster are removed. In older homes, this phase requires testing for lead paint and asbestos before any disturbance — both are common in Staten Island's pre-1978 housing stock. Our IICRC Certified Firm (#210213) protocols address hazardous material handling as part of scope.

**3. Structural drying.** Water from suppression is extracted and affected cavities are dried using commercial desiccant dehumidifiers and air movers. We monitor moisture readings daily until substrates reach target levels.

**4. Smoke and soot remediation.** Hard surfaces are cleaned with pH-appropriate agents matched to the soot type — wet smoke from slow-burning fires leaves a different residue than dry smoke from fast, high-temperature fires. Ductwork is inspected and cleaned if smoke infiltrated the HVAC system.

**5. Deodorization.** Odor is treated at the molecular level, not masked. For plaster-heavy homes, we extend dwell times and may run hydroxyl generators over multiple days.

**6. Reconstruction.** Framing, insulation, drywall, and finishes are rebuilt to pre-loss condition, coordinated with your insurance adjuster's scope.

## Local Note

In Staten Island's older frame neighborhoods — Port Richmond, St. George, and parts of New Dorp — balloon-frame construction is more common than most homeowners realize. Unlike platform framing, balloon frames have continuous stud cavities running from the sill plate to the roof rafters, with no fire blocking between floors. After a fire, we probe these cavities with borescope cameras before closing walls, because smoldering material can persist inside a cavity for hours after surface flames are out. It is a step that adds time to the assessment but has prevented rekindles on jobs where the initial fire appeared fully extinguished.
