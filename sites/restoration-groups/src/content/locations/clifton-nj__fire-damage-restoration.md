## Why Clifton's Housing Stock Complicates Fire and Smoke Restoration

The bulk of Clifton's residential neighborhoods — the capes and colonials off Van Houten Avenue, the brick two-families in Botany Village, the split-levels throughout Richfield — were built between 1945 and 1965. That era of construction means original horsehair-and-plaster interior walls in many homes, cast-iron supply and drain lines, and minimal vapor barriers between living spaces and unfinished cavities.

After a structural fire, those plaster walls behave differently than modern drywall. They're denser, so they absorb smoke particulates and odor compounds more deeply. Thermal fogging and surface sealing alone won't reach the contamination — content pack-outs, controlled demolition to expose cavities, and HEPA-filtered air scrubbing are typically required before any reconstruction begins. In older multi-family buildings like those along the Route 3 commercial corridor, a single-unit fire can push smoke through shared wall assemblies into adjacent units, expanding the scope of the loss significantly.

The age of the electrical systems in these homes is also a factor worth noting. Knob-and-tube wiring, while less common than it once was, still turns up in Clifton attics and wall cavities during fire restoration teardowns. When it does, it changes the reconstruction sequence — electrical work has to be permitted and inspected before walls close.

## Our Fire Damage Restoration Process in Clifton

Every fire loss is different, but the process follows a documented sequence that keeps the work defensible for your insurance adjuster and safe for your family.

The first step on-site is a full structural and content assessment — identifying what's salvageable, what needs to be packed out for off-site cleaning, and what has to come out entirely. We photograph and document every affected area before anything moves, which matters when your carrier's adjuster reviews the claim.

Once the structure is secured and utilities are addressed, we move into controlled demolition — removing char, compromised insulation, and smoke-saturated materials down to clean substrate. In Clifton's older homes, that often means pulling original plaster in fire-affected rooms rather than attempting to seal over it. After demolition, air scrubbers and hydroxyl generators run continuously to address airborne particulates and odor compounds embedded in the structure.

Odor treatment in a fire-damaged home is not a single step. Thermal fogging, applied to penetrate porous surfaces, combined with encapsulant sealers on framing and subfloor, is the standard approach for persistent smoke odor in older construction. We follow the IICRC S700 standard for smoke and fire restoration throughout this process.

Reconstruction — framing, insulation, drywall, painting, finish work — is handled under our NJ Licensed Home Improvement Contractor license, so you're dealing with one company from emergency response through the final walkthrough.
