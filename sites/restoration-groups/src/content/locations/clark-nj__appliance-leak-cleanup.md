## Our Appliance Leak Cleanup Process in Clark

The first step on any appliance leak call is source confirmation. Technicians identify whether the loss is still active — a supply line, a drain connection, an ice maker feed — and isolate it before any drying equipment goes down. In Clark's finished basements, that often means pulling back carpet and pad to assess whether the subfloor has delaminated and checking the perimeter wall framing with a moisture meter, because water that travels laterally under a slab edge is easy to miss on a visual inspection.

Once the scope is mapped, the team sets commercial-grade desiccant or refrigerant dehumidifiers and axial air movers calibrated to the room's cubic footage. Drying is documented daily with psychrometric readings — temperature, relative humidity, and material moisture content — so there's a defensible record for your insurance adjuster. Affected materials that can't be dried in place (saturated insulation, delaminated subfloor panels, wet drywall below the flood cut line) are removed and disposed of before the drying phase begins, not after, because leaving them in place extends drying time and raises the mold risk. In Clark homes with cast-iron drain lines, the team also checks whether the drain itself is the source of a recurring backup rather than the appliance, since misdiagnosed drain failures account for a notable share of repeat calls in older suburbs like this one.
