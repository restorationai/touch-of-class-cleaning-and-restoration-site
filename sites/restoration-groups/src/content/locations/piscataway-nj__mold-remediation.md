## Why Piscataway Properties See Mold Issues

Piscataway's housing stock spans several distinct eras, and each era carries its own mold risk profile. The postwar capes and split-levels in neighborhoods like Possumtown and Stelton were built with galvanized steel supply lines and cast-iron drain stacks that are now well past their service life. Galvanized pipe corrodes from the inside out, narrowing the bore and eventually pinholing — often inside a finished wall where a slow leak goes undetected for months. By the time a homeowner notices the stain on the drywall, mold has already colonized the paper facing and the wood framing behind it.

The Society Hill townhome and condo developments present a different challenge. Shared wall assemblies and stacked units mean a leak originating on one floor can wick laterally and vertically before anyone traces the source. HOA common-area plumbing, roof membrane failures, and HVAC condensate lines are frequent culprits. On the commercial side, the Stelton corridor's warehouse and lab buildings deal with sprinkler-discharge events and flat-roof ponding — both of which can saturate insulation above drop ceilings and create hidden reservoirs for mold growth that aren't visible from below.

Piscataway's position in central Middlesex County also means it sits in a humid continental climate corridor where summer dew points routinely push into the uncomfortable range. Crawl spaces and slab-on-grade additions that lack adequate vapor barriers become moisture traps from May through September.

## Our Mold Remediation Process in Piscataway

Every project starts with a moisture mapping inspection — not just a visual check, but thermal imaging and pin-type moisture meters used on walls, subfloor assemblies, and ceiling cavities. In Piscataway's older split-levels and capes, we pay particular attention to the band joist area at the foundation line, which is frequently where Ida-era flood moisture was trapped and never properly dried.

Once the affected boundaries are established, we build negative-air containment using 6-mil poly barriers and HEPA-filtered air scrubbers running at a minimum of one air change every 15 minutes inside the work zone. This keeps spores from migrating into unaffected living areas during the removal phase. Affected drywall, insulation, and porous materials are removed, bagged, and disposed of per New Jersey DEP guidelines. Structural framing that tests clean on moisture but shows surface mold is treated with an EPA-registered antimicrobial and allowed to dry to below 16% moisture content before any encapsulation.

Final clearance is documented with post-remediation air sampling. We work with independent industrial hygienists for third-party clearance testing when required by an HOA, a lender, or a property manager — which is increasingly common in Society Hill and similar condo associations.

## Local Note

In the split-levels and raised ranches common to Possumtown and the older Stelton tracts, the original builders often furred out concrete block foundation walls with a narrow wood-stud cavity and then drywalled directly over it — no vapor barrier, no insulation gap. After a flood event, that cavity traps water against the block and the back of the drywall simultaneously. Standard drying protocols designed for modern framed walls underestimate the drying time needed here by 30 to 40 percent. We account for this by leaving dehumidification equipment in place longer and re-reading moisture levels at the block surface before signing off on structural dryness.
