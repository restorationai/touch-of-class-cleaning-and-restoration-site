## Why Passaic Properties See Repeated Water Damage

Passaic is one of New Jersey's most densely built small cities, and the housing stock tells the whole story. The vast majority of residential buildings went up between 1900 and 1950 — brick multi-family walkups, two- and three-family frame houses, and attached rowhomes that were never designed for modern water loads. Knob-and-tube wiring remnants complicate drying decisions because heat cannot be applied freely near old insulated conductors. Cast-iron drain lines corrode from the inside out, and by the time a backup surfaces in a basement laundry room, the pipe has often been failing for years.

The city's topography compounds the problem. Passaic sits in a low bowl along the river, and even storms that don't produce major flooding push groundwater up through foundation walls and floor drains in below-grade units. The rental density — buildings that house multiple families under one roof — means a leak from a second-floor bathroom can travel through a shared wall cavity and appear as a ceiling stain in a first-floor unit that belongs to a different tenant, creating both a remediation challenge and a landlord-tenant documentation headache.

## Our Water Cleanup Process in Passaic

Arriving on-site, the first priority is finding water that isn't visible. In Passaic's older plaster-and-lath construction, moisture reads dry at the surface long before the lath behind it is actually dry — thermal imaging and calibrated moisture meters tell the real story. We map the full extent of saturation before a single piece of equipment is placed.

From there, the process follows a defined sequence:

- **Standing water removal** — truck-mounted extraction pulls standing water from basements, unit floors, and commercial spaces quickly, reducing the window for microbial activity.
- **Structural drying** — industrial air movers and low-grain refrigerant dehumidifiers are positioned based on the moisture map, not guesswork. In multi-family buildings, we account for shared cavities and adjacent units.
- **Monitoring** — daily readings track the drying curve. We document every reading, which matters when an insurance adjuster asks for proof of due diligence.
- **Content and material assessment** — saturated drywall, insulation, and flooring that cannot be dried in place are removed and documented before mold has a chance to colonize. In Passaic's climate, that window is shorter than most homeowners expect — mold can begin to establish in porous materials within 24 to 48 hours of sustained saturation.

As an IICRC Certified Firm (#210213), drying protocols follow the S500 standard for water damage restoration, which sets the benchmark for when a structure is genuinely dry versus when it just feels dry.

## Local Note

In Passaic's older brick walkups — particularly the three-story buildings common through the Third Ward — plaster walls behave very differently from modern drywall during a water loss. Plaster is dense and slow to release absorbed moisture, which means surface readings can drop to acceptable levels while the wood lath behind the plaster stays saturated. If drying equipment is pulled too early based on surface readings alone, that trapped moisture becomes a mold problem inside the wall cavity within weeks. Crews working in this housing stock need to extend drying cycles and verify with penetrating probes, not just pin meters held against the face coat.
