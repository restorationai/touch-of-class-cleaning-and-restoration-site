## Why Scotch Plains Homes Present Unique Renovation Challenges

The housing stock in Scotch Plains tells a very specific story. Most of the residential neighborhoods — from Willow Grove out toward Ashbrook — were developed between the late 1940s and the mid-1960s. That era produced well-built frames, but it also produced galvanized steel supply lines that have long since reached the end of their service life, cast-iron drain stacks that corrode from the inside out, and finished basements laid directly over concrete slabs with no vapor barrier. When those systems fail — and they do fail — the resulting water intrusion doesn't just damage drywall. It exposes knob-and-tube wiring, asbestos floor tile, and original plaster that behaves nothing like modern 5/8" drywall during a remodel.

The Green Brook and Cedar Brook corridors along the township's southern edge add a flood-exposure layer that became impossible to ignore after Ida in 2021. Homeowners near the Plainfield border who took on water during that event are now making smart decisions: raising mechanical systems, installing sump pits with battery backup, and finishing basements with moisture-resistant materials from the floor up. That kind of post-damage rebuild is exactly where a general contractor with restoration roots earns its keep — because the renovation plan has to account for what the water revealed, not just what the homeowner wants cosmetically.

Commercial properties along the Park Avenue business district and the Route 22 corridor face a different set of issues: roof-leak damage to drop ceilings and flooring, sprinkler-discharge losses that soak drywall partitions, and tenant-improvement timelines that can't slip.
