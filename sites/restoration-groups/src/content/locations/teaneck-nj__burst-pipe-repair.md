## Local Note: What Plaster Walls Mean for Your Drying Timeline

Many of the center-hall colonials and brick Tudors in Teaneck's older neighborhoods — particularly around the Bryant School area and West Englewood — still have original plaster-and-lath interior walls rather than modern drywall. Plaster is denser and absorbs moisture more slowly than gypsum board, but it also releases that moisture more slowly during the drying phase. In practical terms, a room with plaster walls may need 30 to 50 percent more drying time than an equivalent space finished with drywall, and moisture readings at the surface can look acceptable while the lath and framing behind the plaster are still wet. We account for this by taking deep-wall readings with penetrating probes rather than relying solely on surface meters — it's the difference between a genuinely dry wall and a mold problem three weeks later.
