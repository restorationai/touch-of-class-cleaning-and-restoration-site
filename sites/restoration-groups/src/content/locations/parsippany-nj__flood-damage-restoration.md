## Why Parsippany Properties See Flood Damage Issues

That housing stock carries two chronic vulnerabilities. First, the original cast-iron drain stacks and galvanized supply lines in those older lake-tract homes corrode from the inside out; a pinhole failure in a finished basement can run undetected behind paneling for days before the floor gives it away. Second, the low-lying geography around Lake Hiawatha and Troy Brook means that even a moderate storm event can push stormwater into window wells and crawlspaces that were never designed for that kind of hydrostatic pressure.

## Our Flood Damage Restoration Process in Parsippany

Every job starts with a moisture mapping survey — thermal imaging and calibrated moisture meters across walls, floors, and ceilings to establish exactly where water traveled, not just where it's visible. In the split-level and bi-level homes common around Rainbow Lakes and Troy Hills, water often migrates through floor assemblies to the level below before it shows up as a stain, so that mapping step is not optional.

Once the scope is documented, standing water is extracted using truck-mounted units capable of removing hundreds of gallons quickly. Saturated materials — carpet, pad, damaged drywall, compromised insulation — are removed and documented before drying begins. Industrial desiccant and refrigerant dehumidifiers, paired with high-velocity air movers, are positioned according to the IICRC S500 standard for structural drying. Moisture readings are logged daily so drying progress is measurable, not estimated. When readings reach target levels across all monitored points, equipment is removed and the rebuild phase can begin.

As an IICRC Certified Firm (#210213) and NJ Licensed Home Improvement Contractor, the team can carry the project from emergency extraction through finished reconstruction — one point of contact rather than two separate contractors.
