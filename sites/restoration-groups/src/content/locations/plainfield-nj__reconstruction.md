## Our Reconstruction Process in Plainfield

Every reconstruction engagement starts with a documented scope of loss, not an estimate built on assumptions. For Plainfield properties, that typically means a structural assessment before demolition begins — particularly in pre-1940 homes where removing a damaged wall section can reveal compromised headers or bearing points that weren't visible in the initial walkthrough.

The sequence we follow:

1. **Damage documentation and scope development** — photographs, moisture mapping, and structural notes that feed directly into the insurance claim file.
2. **Selective demolition** — removing only what cannot be dried, treated, or structurally retained. In historic-district properties near the Drake House Museum corridor, we work to preserve original millwork and trim wherever the material is sound.
3. **Structural framing and sheathing** — replacing compromised framing members, sistering joists where needed, and bringing the structure into current IRC compliance before any finish work begins.
4. **Mechanical rough-in coordination** — electrical, plumbing, and HVAC subcontractors are sequenced so inspections don't bottleneck the rebuild timeline.
5. **Finish work and final inspection** — drywall, insulation, flooring, painting, and trim reinstallation, followed by the municipal inspection required for permit closeout.

For commercial properties along the Front Street business corridor, we build the schedule around operational continuity where possible — phasing work so that portions of a building can remain in use during reconstruction.
