## Why Clark's Housing Stock Shapes Every Renovation

Most of the homes in the 07066 ZIP code were framed between 1952 and 1968, which means a few things that matter before a single wall goes up. Knob-and-tube wiring occasionally still hides inside older insulation cavities. Asbestos-containing floor tiles and pipe wrap are common in finished basements — the same finished basements that took on sewer backup during the Ida-era flooding along the Lake Avenue lowlands. Lead paint is a realistic expectation on any pre-1978 surface, which triggers New Jersey's lead-safe renovation rules under the NJ Division of Consumer Affairs framework.

None of this makes a kitchen remodel or bathroom renovation impossible. It does mean the pre-construction walkthrough has to be honest about what's there, and the scope of work has to account for it — not discover it mid-demo. That kind of upfront assessment is standard practice here, not an upsell.

## Our Renovation and General Contracting Process in Clark

Every project starts with a documented scope. For post-damage rebuilds — the category that makes up a meaningful share of work in flood-prone corridors near the Rahway River — that scope is built off the adjuster's estimate and the drying logs, so there are no gaps between what insurance approved and what the crew is actually rebuilding.

For elective renovations, the process looks like this:

- **Assessment and permit planning**: Clark Township's building department requires permits for structural changes, electrical upgrades, plumbing reroutes, and HVAC modifications. We pull them. Homeowners who skip this step on a kitchen remodel often discover the problem at resale.
- **Material and subcontractor coordination**: We work with licensed plumbers and electricians who know Union County inspectors and their inspection scheduling windows, which keeps projects from stalling between rough-in and finish.
- **Demo with hazmat awareness**: In homes built before 1978 along the Raritan Road business district or the older residential blocks near Arthur L. Johnson High School, we test before we demo. Disturbing asbestos tile or lead paint without proper containment creates a liability that outlasts the renovation.
- **Finish and final inspection**: We don't consider a job closed until the certificate of occupancy or final inspection sign-off is in hand.

For commercial clients on the Westfield Avenue corridor or in the Raritan Road retail strip, the same sequence applies — with the addition of ADA compliance review and occupancy-load documentation where required.

A significant portion of renovation work in Clark is insurance-driven — homeowners rebuilding after a sump failure, a burst supply line in a finished basement, or smoke damage from a kitchen fire. As an IICRC Certified Firm (#210213) and NJ Licensed Home Improvement Contractor, the company can bridge the gap between the mitigation phase and the rebuild phase under a single project file.
