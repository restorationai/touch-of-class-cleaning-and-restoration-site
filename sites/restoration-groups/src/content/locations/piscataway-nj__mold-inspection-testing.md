## Why Piscataway Properties See Elevated Mold Risk

Piscataway's housing stock is unusually varied, and that variety matters for mold risk. The postwar capes and split-levels concentrated in neighborhoods like Possumtown and Arbor were built before vapor barriers were standard practice. Many of those finished basements still run on original galvanized supply lines and cast-iron drain stacks — both known for slow, hidden leaks that saturate wall cavities for months before a homeowner notices a stain or odor. Mold can begin colonizing damp organic material within 24 to 48 hours of a moisture event, so a pinhole leak behind a utility wall can produce a significant hidden colony long before it's visible.

The Society Hill condo and townhome developments present a different pattern: shared plumbing chases and stacked units mean one unit's slow leak becomes a neighbor's mold problem. HOA boards in those developments frequently require documented inspection results — not just a contractor's verbal assessment — before authorizing remediation work or releasing reserve funds.

## Our Mold Inspection and Testing Process in Piscataway

A mold inspection is not a visual walk-through with a flashlight. The process starts with a structured moisture mapping of the affected areas using thermal imaging and calibrated moisture meters — tools that locate elevated readings inside wall assemblies, under flooring, and above drop ceilings without opening anything unnecessarily. In older Piscataway homes with plaster walls over wood lath, moisture readings behave differently than in modern drywall construction; plaster holds and releases moisture slowly, which can mask an active problem or make a resolved one appear ongoing without careful interpretation.

Air sampling and surface sampling follow the moisture assessment. Air cassettes are collected in affected rooms and in an outdoor control location — the outdoor sample is the baseline against which indoor spore counts are compared. Surface samples (tape lifts or swabs) target visible suspect growth to identify genus and species. All samples are sent to an accredited third-party laboratory. The written report you receive identifies the species present, the relative concentration, and whether the indoor environment is elevated compared to the outdoor baseline. That report is the document your insurance adjuster, your HOA board, or your remediator needs to move forward.

Because The Restoration Group holds IICRC Firm Certification (#210213), the inspection protocol follows industry-standard methodology — which matters when an insurance carrier or an HOA management company scrutinizes the documentation.
