## Our Contents Restoration & Pack-Out Process

The first step on-site is a room-by-room contents inventory before anything moves. Every item is photographed, catalogued, and assigned a condition code. This documentation matters for insurance claims — carriers want a line-item record, not a general description — and it protects you if a dispute arises later about what was present and in what condition.

Once the inventory is complete, affected contents are carefully packed and transported to our climate-controlled facility, where the restoration work happens away from the active drying and reconstruction zone. Depending on the loss type, that may mean ultrasonic cleaning for hard goods and collectibles, ozone or hydroxyl treatment for smoke and odor, document drying and digitization for paper records, or textile cleaning for upholstered furniture and clothing. Items that cannot be restored are documented and flagged for your insurance adjuster before disposal — nothing is discarded without your knowledge.

When the structure is cleared and dry, contents are returned and placed according to the original inventory. The goal is a room that looks like your room, not a storage unit dropped back in.

## Local Note: What Older Rahway Homes Do to Smoke Odor

Here is something that comes up repeatedly in the older housing near Downtown Rahway and the Clark border: plaster walls and original hardwood floors are far more porous than modern drywall and engineered flooring. After a kitchen fire or even a contained appliance fire, smoke particulates penetrate deeply into plaster and wood grain. Contents in those rooms — upholstered furniture especially — absorb odor compounds at a higher rate than the same items would in a newer home, because the ambient concentration of smoke in the air stays elevated longer before the structure itself off-gasses.
