## Why Bridgewater Properties Develop Persistent Odors

Bridgewater's housing stock creates conditions that make odor removal harder than average. The Finderne flats and the low-lying areas near the Raritan River flooded badly during Tropical Storm Floyd and again during Ida in September 2021. Homes that took on water — even a few inches in a finished basement — often developed mold and musty odors that owners masked with air fresheners rather than eliminated at the source. Cast-iron drain lines common in pre-1980 construction corrode from the inside, and slow sewer-gas leaks can permeate a finished lower level for months before anyone traces the smell.

On the other end of the township, the larger colonials and townhome developments near Chimney Rock and Green Knoll deal with a different pattern: fireplace and wood-stove smoke that migrates into attic insulation and wall cavities during heating season. Smoke odor is particularly stubborn because the volatile organic compounds bond chemically to porous surfaces — drywall, insulation batts, carpet padding — rather than simply sitting on top of them. Ventilation alone will not remove them.

## Our Deodorization Process in Bridgewater

Effective odor removal is not a single treatment. It is a sequenced process calibrated to what caused the odor and what materials absorbed it.

**Source removal first.** No deodorization technology works reliably if the contaminated material is still present. We remove charred debris, saturated insulation, or mold-colonized drywall before any equipment goes in.

**Thermal fogging** for smoke and fire losses. A petroleum-based deodorant is vaporized into a fog that penetrates the same microscopic pathways smoke traveled — behind baseboards, into wall cavities, through subfloor gaps. The fog particles bond with odor-causing residues and neutralize them chemically.

**Hydroxyl generation** for occupied or sensitive spaces. Hydroxyl generators produce hydroxyl radicals that break down odor molecules at the molecular level without the occupancy restrictions that ozone treatment requires. For Bridgewater homeowners who cannot vacate for 24–48 hours, hydroxyl is often the right tool.

**Ozone treatment** for severe, unoccupied spaces. High-concentration ozone is the most aggressive option for deeply embedded smoke or animal odors in vacant homes. Ozone must be applied only in unoccupied spaces and requires thorough post-treatment ventilation — a step that is sometimes rushed by less careful operators.

**HVAC deodorization.** Because Bridgewater's older homes often have ductwork running through finished spaces, we treat the air handler and accessible duct runs directly. Odors recirculated through a contaminated HVAC system will return within days if this step is skipped.

## Local Note

One pattern we see specifically in Bridgewater's older Finderne and Martinsville neighborhoods: homes built in the 1950s and 1960s frequently have original cellulose insulation in the walls and attic. Cellulose is highly absorbent — it holds smoke and moisture odors far longer than fiberglass batts and releases them slowly over months as temperatures change with the seasons. Homeowners sometimes think a deodorization job failed because the smell returns the following summer; in most cases the insulation was never treated. When we scope an odor job in one of these older tracts, we include the attic and wall cavity condition in our assessment before recommending a treatment plan.
