## Why Montclair Properties See Mold Problems

Montclair's housing stock is part of what makes the town beautiful — and part of what makes mold remediation here more involved than in a newer suburb. Homes built before 1950 were constructed with materials that behave differently under moisture: plaster walls absorb and hold water longer than modern drywall, old-growth framing sits tight against masonry foundations with minimal vapor barrier, and unfinished crawl spaces under center-hall colonials often have bare soil floors that wick ground moisture upward year-round.

The town's topography compounds the problem. Properties on the slopes below Eagle Rock Reservation and along the Watchung ridge shed stormwater fast; that water has to go somewhere, and it often finds its way through foundation walls or overwhelms sump pits that were sized for a quieter era of storms. The Third River (Yantacaw) and its tributaries run through lower-lying neighborhoods, and in heavy rain events those waterways back up. Ida was the most dramatic example, but any significant nor'easter can push groundwater into the finished basements that Montclair homeowners depend on as living space. Mold can begin colonizing porous materials — drywall paper, wood framing, carpet backing — in as little as 24 to 48 hours after a water intrusion event.

On the commercial side, the Bloomfield Avenue and Walnut Street corridors see their own pattern: flat or low-slope roofs on retail buildings that develop slow leaks, and restaurant kitchens where ventilation issues create chronic high-humidity conditions behind walls.

## Our Mold Remediation Process in Montclair

Every job starts with a thorough visual inspection and moisture mapping. We use thermal imaging cameras and pin-type moisture meters to trace moisture migration through walls and floors — critical in older Montclair homes where a leak behind a plaster wall may have traveled several feet from the source before becoming visible.

Once the scope is established, we set up negative-air containment using 6-mil poly barriers and HEPA-filtered air scrubbers to prevent spores from migrating to unaffected areas of the home during work. This step matters especially in the large, open floor plans common in Estate Section colonials and Tudors, where an unsealed work area can contaminate an entire floor.

Physical removal follows: affected drywall, insulation, and porous materials are HEPA-vacuumed, bagged, and removed per EPA guidelines. Structural wood surfaces that can be preserved are treated with an EPA-registered antimicrobial agent and dried to confirmed moisture levels before any rebuild begins. We document every step with photographs and moisture readings — the same documentation package your insurance adjuster will need.

Final clearance testing is performed by an independent industrial hygienist (not our own crew), so the air quality confirmation is unbiased. We coordinate that scheduling directly so you are not managing a second contractor.

## Local Note: Plaster Walls and Hidden Mold Migration

One thing our crews have learned working in Montclair's older housing stock: plaster walls mask mold migration in ways that modern drywall does not. A standard moisture reading on the surface of a plaster wall can look acceptable even when the wood lath behind it is actively colonized. We probe deeper — into the lath cavity and against the masonry or framing behind — before concluding a wall is clean. Skipping that step is how a remediation job gets called back three months later. In homes near Anderson Park and throughout the Estate Section, where original plaster is often intact and owners want to preserve it, this careful approach also helps us avoid unnecessary demolition.
