## Our Basement Flooding Cleanup Process in Clark

The first thing we do on arrival is assess the water category. A failed supply line is clean water; a backed-up floor drain connected to a combined sewer is Category 3 — contaminated, requiring different protective protocols and disposal procedures. In Clark, where sewer backup is a real and recurring event, we treat ambiguous sources as contaminated until testing or direct observation says otherwise.

Once the source is confirmed and stopped, water removal begins with truck-mounted extraction units capable of pulling hundreds of gallons per hour. Porous materials — carpet padding, drywall below the flood line, insulation inside stud bays — are documented and removed if they cannot be dried in place within the IICRC S500 standard's drying window. We set industrial-grade desiccant dehumidifiers and axial air movers in a calculated pattern based on the square footage and construction of the space, then monitor moisture readings daily until the structure reaches its dry standard.

For Clark's finished basements, that often means pulling baseboards and drilling small evaporation holes at the base of drywall to let wall cavities dry without a full tearout — a technique that saves material and shortens rebuild time significantly.
