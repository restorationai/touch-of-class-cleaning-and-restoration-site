## Why Teaneck Homes See More Appliance Leak Damage

Teaneck's housing stock — heavily concentrated in brick Tudors, center-hall colonials, and two-family homes built between the 1920s and 1940s — carries a specific set of vulnerabilities that newer construction doesn't share. Supply lines to dishwashers and refrigerators were often retrofitted into kitchens that were never designed for them, running through cabinet toe-kicks and wall cavities with minimal clearance. When those braided steel or plastic lines fail, water follows the path of least resistance: down through original hardwood subfloors, into rim joists, and into finished basement spaces below.

Cast-iron drain lines, common throughout the Bryant School area and older streets near Cedar Lane, corrode from the inside out over decades. A slow dishwasher drain leak can go undetected for months because the water exits through a hairline crack in the drain rather than pooling visibly. By the time it registers — a stain on the basement ceiling, a musty odor in the utility room — secondary mold colonization can begin in as little as 48 to 72 hours on organic materials like wood framing and drywall paper.

Water heater failures add another layer of risk. Many Teaneck homes have their water heaters in basement utility rooms that share walls with finished living space. A tank that fails at capacity — typically 40 to 80 gallons — can saturate concrete block foundation walls and migrate under slab areas before it reaches a floor drain.

## Our Appliance Leak Cleanup Process in Teaneck

When we arrive on-site, the first priority is source confirmation — not just stopping the visible water, but identifying whether the appliance itself is the only source or whether a secondary failure (a drain line, a supply valve, a corroded fitting) contributed. After source control, we conduct a full moisture mapping of the affected area using thermal imaging cameras and calibrated moisture meters. This matters especially in homes with plaster-over-lath walls, which are still present in many pre-war Teaneck properties. Extraction of standing water is followed by the placement of commercial-grade desiccant dehumidifiers and axial air movers, sized to the actual cubic footage of the affected space.
