## Our Water Damage Restoration Process in Fort Lee

Every job starts with a moisture survey — thermal imaging and calibrated meters to map exactly where water has traveled, not just where it's visible. In high-rise units, that often means checking the unit below and the shared corridor walls before we set a single piece of equipment. We extract standing water with truck-mounted and portable units, then place commercial-grade desiccant dehumidifiers and axial air movers in a calculated drying configuration sized to the space.

Structural drying in Fort Lee high-rises requires coordination with building management. We communicate directly with property managers to access mechanical rooms, confirm riser shutoffs, and meet HOA or condo board documentation requirements before demolition of any shared wall or ceiling assembly. Drying logs are recorded daily and handed off to your adjuster in the format most carriers request — moisture readings, equipment placement maps, and timestamped photos of affected materials.

For single-family and multifamily homes in Coytesville or along the streets climbing toward Fort Lee Historic Park, the process is similar but the building materials differ. Older plaster-on-lath walls common in pre-1950 construction absorb water more deeply than modern drywall and release it more slowly — drying timelines typically run longer, and we adjust chamber configurations accordingly rather than pulling equipment prematurely.
