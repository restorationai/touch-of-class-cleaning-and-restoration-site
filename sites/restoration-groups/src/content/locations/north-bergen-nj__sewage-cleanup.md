## Our Sewage Cleanup and Sanitization Process in North Bergen

Sewage is classified as Category 3 water — the most contaminated type — which means standard water extraction protocols aren't enough. Every job follows a structured sequence designed to eliminate biological hazards, not just remove visible waste.

The process begins with containment. Affected areas are isolated using physical barriers and negative air pressure where cross-contamination is a risk — especially important in multi-family buildings where HVAC systems or open stairwells can spread airborne pathogens between units. All standing sewage and saturated porous materials (drywall, insulation, flooring underlayment, carpet) are removed and bagged for proper disposal per New Jersey solid waste regulations.

Once the space is cleared, surfaces are treated with EPA-registered disinfectants rated for Category 3 contamination. This isn't a single spray-and-wipe step — it involves dwell times, mechanical agitation on porous substrates, and a second application on high-contact surfaces. Structural drying follows using commercial-grade desiccant dehumidifiers and air movers, calibrated to ambient conditions. In North Bergen's older brick and masonry buildings, concrete block foundation walls absorb moisture deeply and require extended monitoring before a space can be considered dry and safe.

The job isn't closed until moisture readings confirm the structure has returned to baseline and there's no residual odor — the sulfur and ammonia compounds in raw sewage bind to surfaces and will return if drying is incomplete.
