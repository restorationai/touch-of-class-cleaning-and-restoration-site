## Our Basement Flood Cleanup Process in Westfield

The first thing we do on arrival is classify the water. A failed sump pump during a clean rain event is a very different job than a sewer backup — the latter is Category 3 contaminated water under the IICRC S500 standard, requiring full containment, PPE, and disposal protocols that go well beyond simple extraction. In Westfield's older homes, that distinction matters enormously because finished basements here often combine carpet, engineered hardwood, built-in cabinetry, and drywall — materials that respond differently to contaminated water and require different salvage decisions.

Once water is extracted with truck-mounted and portable units, we use thermal imaging and calibrated moisture meters to map what's wet behind the walls and under the subfloor. In homes with plaster-over-lath construction — common in the older sections of Westfield — the surface can read dry while the lath behind it holds significant moisture. We set drying equipment to IICRC standards and monitor daily, adjusting airflow and dehumidification as readings change. Nothing is closed up until the structure reaches target moisture levels.

For Category 3 losses, all porous materials that contacted contaminated water — drywall, insulation, carpet and pad — are removed, bagged, and disposed of per New Jersey DEP guidelines before drying begins.
