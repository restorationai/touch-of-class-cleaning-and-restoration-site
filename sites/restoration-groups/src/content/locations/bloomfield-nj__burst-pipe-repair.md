## Why Bloomfield Properties See Burst Pipe Issues More Than Most

The town's building age is the root cause. The majority of single-family homes, two-families, and Tudors in Bloomfield Center, Watsessing, and Brookdale were built between 1900 and 1940. Galvanized steel supply lines from that era corrode from the inside out — the pipe looks intact on the outside until the day it doesn't. ## Our Burst Pipe Cleanup and Repair Process in Bloomfield

The first thing we do on arrival is locate and isolate the break. In Bloomfield's older homes, shutoff valves are sometimes buried behind finished drywall or tucked into crawl spaces under additions — we carry the tools to find them fast rather than waiting for the municipal water shutoff at the curb. Once flow is stopped, we document the affected area with moisture mapping and thermal imaging before anything is moved or touched, because that documentation is what your insurance adjuster needs.

Once structural moisture is back to baseline, we coordinate or perform the pipe repair itself — and as an NJ Licensed Home Improvement Contractor, we can handle both the remediation and the repair under one scope rather than leaving you to manage two separate contractors.

## Local Note: What Bloomfield's Plaster Walls Mean for Drying Time

A detail that catches homeowners off guard: a significant share of Bloomfield's pre-1940 colonials and Tudors still have original horsehair plaster walls rather than drywall. Plaster is denser and absorbs water more slowly than gypsum board, but it also releases that moisture more slowly during the drying phase. On jobs in older Watsessing and Brookdale homes, we routinely extend our drying monitoring window by one to two days compared to a comparable postwar house, and we set equipment placement differently to account for the slower moisture migration through plaster. Skipping that adjustment and pulling equipment on a standard three-day schedule is how you end up with secondary mold growth inside a wall that read dry on the surface.
