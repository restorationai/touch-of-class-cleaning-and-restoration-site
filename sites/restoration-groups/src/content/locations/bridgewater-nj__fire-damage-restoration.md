## Why Bridgewater Homes Are Particularly Vulnerable After a Fire

Bridgewater is a patchwork of construction eras. The Finderne and Bradley Gardens tracts closest to the Raritan River flats were built out in the 1950s and '60s — ranches and split-levels with original plaster ceilings, cast-iron ductwork, and wood-framed soffits that absorb smoke deeply. Newer colonial and townhome developments along the Route 22/287 corridor are built tighter, which means smoke and combustion gases pressurize into wall cavities faster when a fire occurs.

Older homes in the Martinsville and Green Knoll areas frequently still have original forced-air systems with metal plenums and flex runs that act as highways for smoke particulates. A fire that appears contained to one room can deposit odor-causing soot throughout a 2,400-square-foot home within minutes. Identifying every affected air pathway — not just the visible char — is what separates a complete restoration from a job that smells fine in January and reactivates every summer when humidity rises.

## Our Fire Damage Restoration Process in Bridgewater

The first step on any fire loss is a thorough scope — not a sales call. A certified technician walks the structure to document char depth, soot type (wet, dry, or protein-based), and any structural compromise before a single item is moved. This documentation matters for your insurance adjuster and for the permit process with the Bridgewater Township Building Department, which requires inspection sign-off on structural repairs before walls are closed.

From there, the process moves through defined phases:

- **Board-up and stabilization** — securing the structure against weather and unauthorized entry, critical in a township where a fire in a dense neighborhood like Bradley Gardens can attract attention quickly.
- **Soot and smoke removal** — dry-chemical sponging of walls and ceilings, HEPA vacuuming of contents, and thermal fogging or hydroxyl treatment for odor neutralization. Protein smoke from kitchen fires requires enzymatic cleaning that standard soot sponging cannot address.
- **HVAC decontamination** — duct cleaning and coil treatment to prevent recontamination of restored surfaces every time the system cycles.
- **Structural drying and reconstruction** — fire suppression water from sprinklers or hose lines creates a secondary moisture problem. Affected materials are dried to IICRC S500 standards before any reconstruction begins.
- **Reconstruction coordination** — framing, drywall, finish carpentry, and painting brought back to pre-loss condition with permits pulled and inspections scheduled.

The Restoration Group is an IICRC Certified Firm (#210213) and a licensed NJ Home Improvement Contractor, which means reconstruction work is covered under the same contract — no hand-off to a separate GC mid-project.

Most homeowners in Bridgewater carry standard HO-3 policies that cover sudden and accidental fire losses. The documentation phase — photo evidence, moisture readings, scope of damage, and itemized estimates — is the piece most policyholders underestimate. Carriers will scrutinize whether smoke damage to secondary rooms was caused by the fire event or pre-existing conditions, and a thorough initial scope protects your claim. ## Local Note

Bridgewater's postwar split-levels were commonly built with a half-story below grade — a finished rec room or laundry space with a drop ceiling over the lower level. After a fire on the main floor, smoke and fire-suppression water both migrate down into that below-grade space through stairwells and mechanical chases. Contractors who scope only the visible fire floor routinely miss soot deposits on the drop-ceiling tiles and moisture in the concrete block foundation walls below. On any Bridgewater split-level loss, the below-grade level gets scoped as a matter of course — not as an add-on.
