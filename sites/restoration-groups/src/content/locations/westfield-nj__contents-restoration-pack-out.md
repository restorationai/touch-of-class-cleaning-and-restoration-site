## Why Westfield Homes Present Unique Contents Challenges

The housing stock along the tree-lined streets near Mindowaskin Park and throughout The Gardens tells the story plainly: homes built between the 1890s and 1930s were constructed with materials that behave differently under stress than modern building products. Original plaster walls wick moisture laterally before showing surface damage, meaning a slow leak behind a cast-iron stack can saturate a lath cavity — and everything stored against that wall — long before a homeowner notices discoloration. Finished basements, which are nearly universal in Westfield's larger colonials and Tudors, compound the problem. A failed sump pump or a backed-up clay sewer lateral can flood a home theater, a gym, or a home office in hours, submerging electronics, upholstered furniture, and irreplaceable documents.

The Robinson's Branch of the Rahway River and Westfield's dense old-growth tree canopy create a recurring storm-damage pattern as well. ## Our Contents Restoration & Pack-Out Process in Westfield

The process begins with a detailed inventory before a single item leaves the home. Every piece is photographed, logged, and assigned a condition rating — documentation that matters both for your insurance claim and for the orderly return of your belongings. Fragile or high-value items, including artwork, ceramics, and electronics common in Westfield's larger estates, are packed using material appropriate to their category.

From there, contents move to a controlled facility environment where the actual restoration work happens: ultrasonic cleaning for hard surfaces, ozone and hydroxyl treatment for odor-bearing soft goods, freeze-drying for water-damaged documents and photographs, and specialized dry-cleaning circuits for textiles. Throughout the process, the inventory log tracks each item's status so you're never left guessing where a particular piece stands.

When the structure is cleared and dried, contents are returned in the same organized sequence they left — coordinated with any reconstruction schedule so that flooring, paint, and finish work are complete before furniture re-enters the space.
