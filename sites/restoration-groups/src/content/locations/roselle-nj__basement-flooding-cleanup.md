## Our Basement Flooding Cleanup Process in Roselle

The first step on any flooded basement call is moisture mapping — not just the standing water you can see, but the water that has already wicked into wall cavities, under subfloor panels, and into the base of any framing. We use thermal imaging and calibrated moisture meters to establish a drying baseline before a single fan is placed. In Roselle's older homes, this step routinely reveals that water has traveled six to eight feet laterally from the visible wet zone.

Once the scope is documented, extraction comes next. Truck-mounted and portable extractors pull standing water from the slab, and then we transition to structural drying: commercial-grade desiccant or refrigerant dehumidifiers, high-velocity air movers positioned to drive airflow through wall cavities, and daily moisture readings to confirm the drying curve is tracking correctly. If sewer backup is involved, affected materials — drywall, insulation, carpet — are removed and properly disposed of before drying begins. We photograph every step for your insurance file.

For homes with finished basements, we try to preserve what can be saved. Solid hardwood flooring sometimes dries in place with aggressive airflow; laminate and engineered products almost never do. We are direct about what is salvageable and what is not, because leaving compromised material in place to save a weekend of demo work is how mold colonies establish themselves within 48 to 72 hours.
