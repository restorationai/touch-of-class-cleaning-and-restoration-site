Irvington's commercial corridors — particularly along Springfield Avenue — are largely built on early-1900s masonry construction. Beyond storm events, the density of Irvington's built environment creates a compounding problem: a supply line failure in an upper-floor unit of a mixed-use building on Union Avenue can saturate the commercial tenant below before anyone notices. Finished and semi-finished basement spaces — common throughout the township — trap moisture and accelerate mold colonization. ## Our Commercial Restoration Process in Irvington

Every commercial job begins with a thorough scope assessment — not a sales walkthrough. We use thermal imaging and moisture mapping to identify where water has traveled inside wall cavities, under flooring systems, and into ceiling assemblies before any demolition begins. In older Irvington commercial buildings, that step often reveals migration paths that aren't obvious from a visual inspection alone.

From there, the process follows a defined sequence:

- **Emergency water extraction** using truck-mounted and portable extraction units sized to the loss
- **Structural drying** with industrial-grade desiccant or refrigerant dehumidifiers and high-velocity air movers, calibrated daily using psychrometric readings
- **Controlled demolition** of unsalvageable materials — limited to what the drying data actually supports, not a default tear-out
- **Antimicrobial treatment** of affected assemblies where moisture levels and dwell time indicate microbial risk
- **Reconstruction** scoped to match the existing finish level and return the space to pre-loss condition

For fire and smoke losses, the sequence shifts: we prioritize board-up and weatherproofing first, then move to soot removal, odor neutralization using thermal fogging or hydroxyl generation, and content pack-out if the business needs to operate from a temporary location during restoration.
