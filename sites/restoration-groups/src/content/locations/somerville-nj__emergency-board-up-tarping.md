## Why Somerville's Housing Stock Makes Rapid Securing Critical

Somerville is Somerset County's compact seat, and its residential fabric reflects more than a century of layered construction. The blocks radiating off the Main Street business district — particularly the West End and the streets around the Somerset County Courthouse — are dense with frame two-families, Victorians, and colonials built between roughly 1890 and 1940. These structures share a few characteristics that matter the moment a loss occurs.

First, their roof decks are often original skip-sheathing or tongue-and-groove boards rather than modern plywood panels. When a section of roofing is compromised, water infiltrates faster and spreads laterally along the framing before it ever drips to the ceiling below. A tarp installed within the first few hours can prevent what would otherwise be a roof-and-attic claim from becoming a full interior gut. Second, the exterior wall assemblies — clapboard over board sheathing with no housewrap — offer almost no secondary moisture barrier once a window is broken or a wall is breached. Boarding a broken window on one of these homes is not optional; it is the only thing standing between the interior and the weather.

The downtown apartment redevelopment corridor adds a different risk profile: multi-unit mid-rise projects where a single sprinkler discharge or overnight roof-leak can affect multiple tenants and trigger commercial property claims simultaneously.

## Our Board-Up and Tarping Process in Somerville

Every job starts with a documented assessment — photographs and measurements of every breach before a single sheet of OSB goes up. This documentation matters for your insurance claim and for the scope of any subsequent repair work.

For window and door board-ups, we use minimum ½-inch OSB cut to fit the rough opening and secured with structural screws into the surrounding framing, not just the trim. On Somerville's older homes, we probe the surrounding framing for rot before anchoring — a board screwed into punky wood is not secure. For storefronts along the Main Street corridor, we carry commercial-grade plywood and the hardware to span larger glazed openings.

For roof tarping, we use heavy-duty polyethylene tarps rated for extended outdoor exposure, weighted and secured at the ridge and eaves with lumber battens and fasteners driven into sound decking — not just sandbags draped over the surface. On the skip-sheathed roofs common in Somerville's older neighborhoods, we locate solid nailing points carefully to avoid creating new penetrations that could leak around the fasteners. The tarp is lapped over the ridge when possible so wind-driven rain cannot force water back underneath.

We photograph the completed installation before we leave, and we leave a written summary of what was secured and how — something your adjuster will ask for.
