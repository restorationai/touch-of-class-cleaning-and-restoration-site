## Why Somerville Properties Are Particularly Vulnerable to Flood Damage

Somerville's geography puts a significant portion of the borough in the path of recurring flood events. The Raritan River and Peters Brook converge near the borough's edges, and residents in the West End and along Gaston Avenue have watched their streets turn to rivers during major storms — Ida in 2021 cut off access to parts of the borough and pushed water into basements that had never flooded before. Floyd and Irene left similar marks on a generation of homeowners who thought they were safely uphill.

Beyond riverine flooding, the borough's aging housing stock creates a second category of risk entirely. Early-1900s homes throughout the East Main and Fairview neighborhoods were built with galvanized steel supply lines that corrode from the inside out over decades, eventually failing without warning. Cast-iron drain stacks develop hairline cracks that let sewer gas and backflow water into finished lower levels. When a supply line lets go overnight in a home with a finished basement, the water has often been sitting for six or more hours before anyone notices — long enough for subfloor sheathing to begin delaminating and for mold colonization to begin within 24 to 48 hours of saturation.

The Main Street commercial corridor adds another loss pattern: sprinkler-system discharges triggered by minor heat events, overnight roof leaks into ground-floor retail spaces, and multi-unit supply-line failures in the newer mid-rise apartment buildings going up near downtown. ## Our Flood Damage Restoration Process in Somerville

Every job starts with a moisture mapping survey — thermal imaging and calibrated moisture meters across every affected surface, including walls, subfloors, and ceiling assemblies below any upstairs source. In Somerville's older homes, this step matters more than it does in newer construction because plaster walls and tongue-and-groove subfloors hold water in pockets that aren't visible to the eye and won't register on a surface reading.

Once the scope is documented, standing water is extracted with truck-mounted and portable extraction units. Structural drying follows using industrial-grade desiccant and refrigerant dehumidifiers and high-velocity air movers positioned to create a directed airflow through wall cavities and floor assemblies — not just across room surfaces. Drying is monitored daily against a psychrometric drying log until affected materials reach the target moisture content specified by the IICRC S500 standard for water damage restoration.

Materials that cannot be dried in place — saturated insulation, compromised drywall below the flood line, buckled hardwood flooring — are removed and documented with photographs and itemized logs for the insurance claim. Antimicrobial treatment is applied to framing and substructure before any rebuild begins. The Restoration Group is an IICRC Certified Firm (#210213) and a licensed NJ Home Improvement Contractor, so the documentation we produce is formatted to meet carrier requirements and withstand adjuster review.

## Local Note: What Somerville's Plaster Walls Mean for Drying Timelines

Contractors who don't work regularly in pre-war housing sometimes underestimate drying times in Somerville's older Victorians and colonials. Plaster-over-lath walls absorb water more slowly than paper-faced drywall, but they also release it more slowly — a wall assembly that reads dry at the surface can still carry elevated moisture 3/4 of an inch in, behind the lath. In our experience, full structural drying in these homes routinely runs 30 to 40 percent longer than the same square footage in a post-1980 home with standard drywall. We account for this in our drying projections upfront so homeowners near the Wallace House historic district or along Mercer Street aren't caught off guard when the equipment needs to run an extra day or two.
