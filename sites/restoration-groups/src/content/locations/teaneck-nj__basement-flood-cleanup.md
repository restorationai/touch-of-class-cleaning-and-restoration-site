## Our Basement Flood Cleanup Process in Teaneck

The first step on any Teaneck job is category identification — clean water from a supply line failure behaves very differently from a Category 3 sewer backup, and the extraction and disinfection protocols differ accordingly. Sewer events, which are common in Teaneck's older housing stock, require EPA-registered disinfectants applied to all affected surfaces after water removal, not just drying equipment.

After extraction, we place calibrated drying systems — commercial-grade dehumidifiers and air movers positioned to create airflow patterns that pull moisture out of wall assemblies, not just off surface materials. Moisture readings are taken at multiple depths using penetrating and non-penetrating meters, because concrete block foundations and older plaster-over-lath walls hold water differently than modern construction. We document readings daily and adjust equipment placement based on the drying curve, not a fixed schedule.

For finished basements, we perform targeted demolition only where the data shows it's necessary — removing wet drywall to the flood cut line, pulling saturated carpet and pad, and opening wall cavities that are reading elevated moisture. Unnecessary tear-out adds cost and time; insufficient tear-out leaves hidden moisture that becomes a mold problem weeks later.
