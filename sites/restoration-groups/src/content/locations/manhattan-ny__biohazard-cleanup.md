## Why Manhattan Properties Present Unique Biohazard Challenges

Vertical living compresses risk. In a pre-war building on the Upper West Side or a post-war tower near Grand Central Terminal, a situation on one floor can create exposure concerns in shared mechanical spaces, elevator lobbies, and stairwells within hours. Unlike a suburban single-family home where the scope is usually contained to one structure, Manhattan buildings involve resident managers, co-op boards, managing agents, and sometimes commercial tenants on the ground floor — all of whom have a legitimate stake in how remediation is handled.

Older building stock adds another layer. Many Manhattan co-ops and rental buildings constructed before World War II have narrow service corridors, freight elevators with restricted hours, and HVAC systems that recirculate air across multiple units. These conditions shape every decision about containment, access, and the sequencing of work — and they require crews who understand building operations, not just remediation chemistry.
