## Why Maplewood Properties Are Especially Vulnerable to Water Damage

Maplewood's architectural character is also its liability. The craftsman bungalows along Jefferson and the larger colonials in Tuscan and Hilton were built with stone or rubble-block foundations that were never designed to be fully waterproof — they were designed to breathe. That works fine in dry seasons, but when a nor'easter stalls over Essex County or a sump pump fails during a spring thaw, those foundations wick groundwater directly into finished basement spaces.

Older cast-iron drain lines throughout the housing stock are another recurring culprit. They corrode from the inside out, and a section can fail without warning — releasing a slow, hidden leak inside a wall cavity that may go undetected for days. Knob-and-tube-era supply plumbing, still present in some of the older homes near Maplewood Village, runs through uninsulated wall chases where a hard freeze can split a fitting overnight. The result is the same whether the source is a river, a drain, or a burst pipe: standing water in spaces that were never meant to get wet, against materials — plaster, old-growth wood lath, horsehair insulation — that hold moisture far longer than modern construction.

## Our Water Damage Restoration Process in Maplewood

Every job starts with a thorough moisture assessment using thermal imaging and calibrated moisture meters, because in a plaster-walled home the visible water line rarely tells the whole story. Water migrates laterally through lath cavities and can appear two rooms away from the actual source.

From there, the process follows a deliberate sequence:

- **Water extraction** — truck-mounted and portable extractors pull standing water from flooring, subfloor assemblies, and low-lying areas. In finished Maplewood basements, this often means moving around built-in cabinetry and mechanicals.
- **Structural drying** — commercial-grade desiccant and refrigerant dehumidifiers, paired with high-velocity air movers, are positioned to dry the structure — not just the surface. Drying targets are set per the IICRC S500 standard and verified with daily moisture readings.
- **Selective demolition when needed** — plaster that has delaminated or framing that has exceeded safe moisture thresholds gets removed. We document everything photographically before and after, which matters when an insurance adjuster reviews the scope.
- **Antimicrobial treatment** — applied to affected cavities before any enclosure to inhibit secondary growth in the drying period.

We do not close walls or sign off on drying until readings confirm the structure has returned to its pre-loss moisture baseline.

Storm-driven flooding from the Rahway River is typically governed by flood insurance (NFIP or private), while internal losses — burst pipes, appliance failures, sump overflows — fall under standard homeowners policies. The distinction matters because documentation requirements differ. ## Local Note

In Maplewood's older plaster homes, drying timelines routinely run longer than what homeowners expect from reading general guidance online. Lime-based plaster over wood lath holds significantly more moisture per square foot than modern drywall, and it releases that moisture slowly — a wall that reads dry at the surface can still be elevated at the lath layer for days.
