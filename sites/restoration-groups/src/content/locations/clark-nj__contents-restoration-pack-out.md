## Our Contents Restoration & Pack-Out Process in Clark

The process begins with a room-by-room contents inventory before anything moves. Every item is photographed, catalogued by condition, and assigned a restoration category — restorable, questionable, or non-salvageable — so your insurance adjuster has a defensible line-item record and you know exactly what is leaving your home and where it is going.

From there, the pack-out itself is methodical. Soft contents — upholstered furniture, bedding, clothing, rugs — go to our climate-controlled facility for ultrasonic cleaning, ozone treatment, or thermal deodorization depending on the loss type. Hard goods and electronics are assessed separately. Documents and photographs, which Clark homeowners often store in basement filing cabinets, can frequently be freeze-dried or air-dried if they reach us quickly enough.

Storage at our facility is tracked by item, not by box. When your home is ready, contents are returned in the same documented condition they left — nothing gets lost in the shuffle between pack-out and move-back.

Because Clark's split-level floor plans often mean tight stairwells and low clearances between the basement and the main floor, our crews assess access before staging any equipment. A sofa that came in through the garage door in 1987 may not leave through the front hallway in 2024 without planning.

## Local Note

Clark's postwar split-levels often have a quirk worth knowing: the basement and the half-level above it share interior walls that aren't always insulated or vapor-sealed. When a basement floods, moisture migrates upward into those shared wall cavities and can affect the living level above before any visible damage appears on the main floor. During a contents pack-out, we flag items stored against those shared walls for closer inspection — a bookshelf that looks fine may have a back panel that's been wicking moisture for days.
