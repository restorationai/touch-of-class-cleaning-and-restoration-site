## Why Paterson Properties See More Appliance Leak Damage

Paterson's housing stock is one of the oldest in New Jersey. Many of the multi-family walkups near Hinchliffe Stadium and throughout the Sandy Hill neighborhood were constructed before modern moisture barriers existed. Subfloor sheathing is often original tongue-and-groove pine — absorbent and prone to cupping — rather than the plywood or OSB used in newer construction. Dishwasher leak cleanup in these homes is more involved than a typical suburban job because water migrates laterally under cabinetry before it ever reaches a visible surface.

Appliance connections in older Paterson buildings also tend to reflect decades of patchwork repairs. Refrigerator ice maker lines are sometimes still connected with compression fittings that have never been replaced, and washing machine hoses in basement laundry setups may run against uninsulated exterior walls — a combination that accelerates wear. Water heater leak cleanup adds another layer of complexity: many units sit in tight utility closets or unfinished basements with minimal drainage, so a slow tank failure can saturate concrete block foundations before the tenant or landlord notices standing water.

## Our Appliance Leak Cleanup Process in Paterson

When we arrive at a Paterson property, the first step is stopping the source — shutting off the supply valve behind the appliance or, when that valve is corroded or inaccessible, isolating the unit at the main. In older Paterson homes, main shutoffs are sometimes located in shared basement utility rooms in multi-family buildings, which means coordinating access with neighboring units before any drying work begins.

Once the water source is controlled, we use thermal imaging cameras and calibrated moisture meters to map exactly how far saturation has traveled.
