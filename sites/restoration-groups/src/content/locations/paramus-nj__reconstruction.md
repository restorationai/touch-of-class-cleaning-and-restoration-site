## Why Paramus Properties Face Distinct Reconstruction Challenges

The housing stock along the Farview and West Paramus corridors is largely one-story ranch and bi-level construction built between 1952 and 1968. That era of construction used dimensional lumber that is now undersized by current IRC standards, meaning a fire or flood that compromises even a partial wall assembly can trigger a broader structural upgrade requirement during the permit process. Bergen County enforces current code on any reconstruction that touches more than 50% of a structural element — a threshold that catches many homeowners off guard when they expect a simple patch-and-paint scope.

On the commercial side, the Route 17 retail corridor and the properties surrounding Westfield Garden State Plaza generate a specific category of loss: overnight sprinkler discharges, roof membrane failures on flat big-box roofs, and tenant-improvement water intrusion. These commercial reconstruction projects run under tight landlord and anchor-tenant timelines, and they often involve coordinating with mall property management, the tenant's own contractor, and the building's base-building MEP systems simultaneously.

The Saddle River's low-lying floodplain, which cuts through Van Saun County Park and edges into residential streets to the south and east, adds another layer. Homes that have flooded more than once often have compromised vapor barriers and deteriorating rim-joist framing that only becomes visible once drywall is removed — work that has to be scoped honestly before a reconstruction contract is signed.

## Our Reconstruction Process in Paramus

Every project starts with a documented scope-of-loss walkthrough. For residential jobs in neighborhoods like Arcola or East Paramus, that means photographing every affected cavity, identifying pre-existing conditions that could affect the permit, and producing a line-item estimate formatted for insurance carrier review. For commercial losses near Paramus Park Mall, it means coordinating access windows with property management before any demolition phase begins.

From there, the process moves through four phases:

1. **Structural stabilization** — temporary shoring, roof tarping, or emergency board-up where needed to stop further deterioration while permits are pulled.
2. **Permitted demolition and rough-in** — licensed subcontractors handle framing, electrical, plumbing, and HVAC rough-in under Bergen County building permits. The Restoration Group carries NJ Licensed Home Improvement Contractor status through the NJ Division of Consumer Affairs, which is required for this scope of work in New Jersey.
3. **Insulation and moisture barrier installation** — critical in Paramus's mixed-humid climate, where summer dew points routinely push into the mid-60s. Vapor retarder placement depends on wall assembly and exposure, and we specify to current NJ Energy Code requirements.
4. **Finish work and final inspection** — drywall, paint, flooring, trim, cabinetry, and fixture reinstallation, followed by Bergen County final inspection sign-off.

The IICRC Certified Firm credential (Firm #210213) governs the drying and remediation phases that precede reconstruction; the rebuild itself follows the same documentation discipline so the insurance file stays clean from first notice of loss through certificate of occupancy.

## Paramus Insurance and Permit Coordination

Bergen County requires permits for virtually all structural reconstruction work, and the permit timeline — typically two to four weeks for residential projects — affects how insurance carriers schedule depreciation holdback releases. We build that timeline into the initial estimate so adjusters are not surprised by a gap between mitigation completion and reconstruction start.

For homeowners in 07652 and 07653, we provide the carrier with a photo-documented scope, a signed contractor estimate, and permit application confirmation before the first board goes up. That paper trail is what gets supplemental claims approved when hidden damage — rotted sill plates, corroded cast-iron drain sections — surfaces during demolition, which it frequently does in homes of this age.

## Local Note

One thing that comes up repeatedly on Paramus split-level jobs: the half-story intermediate floor — the landing level between the garage and the main living area — is often framed with a non-standard joist depth that does not match anything at a modern lumber yard. When fire or water damage requires sistering or replacing those joists, the repair has to be engineered to match the existing load path rather than defaulting to a standard replacement size. We flag this during the initial walkthrough so the structural scope is accurate before the permit application goes in, not after the framing inspector visits.
