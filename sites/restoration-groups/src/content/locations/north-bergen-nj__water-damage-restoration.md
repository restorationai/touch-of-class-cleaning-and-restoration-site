## Our Water Damage Restoration Process in North Bergen

Every job starts with a moisture mapping inspection — thermal imaging and calibrated moisture meters applied to walls, subfloors, and ceiling assemblies — before a single piece of extraction equipment is placed. In North Bergen's older multi-family buildings, this step is especially important because water migrates through plaster and masonry in ways that aren't visible on the surface. A wet reading behind a first-floor baseboard often means the floor joist cavity above the basement is already saturated.

Once the scope is documented, we extract standing water with truck-mounted and portable units, then transition to structural drying using commercial-grade desiccant and refrigerant dehumidifiers and high-velocity air movers positioned to dry cavities, not just surfaces. Drying targets are set to the IICRC S500 standard and verified with daily moisture readings — we don't pull equipment until the numbers confirm the structure has returned to acceptable moisture content, not just until it feels dry.

For basement losses in the Tonnelle Avenue flood zone, we also assess whether the water source was clean supply water, gray water, or category 3 groundwater intrusion — the answer changes both the extraction protocol and what materials can be dried in place versus removed.
