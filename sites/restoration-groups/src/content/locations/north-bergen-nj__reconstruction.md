## Why North Bergen Properties See Reconstruction Challenges

North Bergen's building stock is one of the densest in New Jersey, and that density creates a specific set of reconstruction headaches. The town is packed with early 20th-century two- and three-family homes built on hillside lots along the Palisades ridge — structures with balloon-frame or platform-frame construction, plaster-and-lath walls, cast-iron drain stacks, and knob-and-tube wiring that was long ago spliced into modern panels. When fire or water damages one of these buildings, reconstruction isn't a matter of swapping out standard components. Framing members are often non-standard dimensions. Plaster walls that absorbed moisture during a pipe failure need full removal before new drywall can go in, because residual moisture trapped behind intact plaster will feed mold colonies within 48 to 72 hours.

The low-lying western flats near the Tonnelle Avenue commercial corridor add another layer of complexity. Heavy rain events — including the remnants of Ida in 2021 — overwhelmed storm drains throughout that area, pushing water into basements and ground-floor units. Post-flood reconstruction in those zones often uncovers compromised sill plates and floor joists that weren't visible during initial mitigation. Structural reconstruction in these cases goes deeper than cosmetic repair.

## Our Reconstruction Process in North Bergen

Reconstruction begins where mitigation ends. Once affected materials are dried, removed, or treated, our project managers conduct a scope-of-work assessment that documents every damaged structural and finish component — load-bearing walls, subfloor, ceiling joists, windows, doors, insulation, and mechanical systems. That scope drives the estimate submitted to your insurance carrier.

From there, work proceeds in a logical sequence: structural repairs first (framing, sheathing, subfloor), then rough mechanical (plumbing, electrical, HVAC where applicable), then insulation and vapor barrier, then drywall and plaster-match where the existing finish requires it, and finally paint, flooring, trim, and fixtures. On multi-family properties near the Bergenline Avenue business district, we coordinate work schedules to minimize disruption to occupied units above or below the loss area. On commercial properties along Tonnelle Avenue, we work with building managers on phased timelines that keep retail or office space partially operational where code and safety allow.

All work is performed or supervised under our NJ Licensed Home Improvement Contractor credential and carried out by tradespeople familiar with Hudson County permit requirements.
