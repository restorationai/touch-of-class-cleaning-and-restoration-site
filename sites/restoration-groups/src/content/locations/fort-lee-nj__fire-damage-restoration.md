## Why Fort Lee Properties See Distinct Fire Damage Challenges

The building stock here doesn't behave like a typical suburban neighborhood. The dense condominium towers clustered near the George Washington Bridge and the Hudson Lights mixed-use complex were built with interconnected mechanical systems — a fire in one unit means smoke infiltration and suppression-water migration into adjacent units, sometimes affecting three or four floors before the fire department clears the scene. That creates a multi-party loss that requires careful documentation before any demolition begins.

On the other side of town, Coytesville and The Hill contain a significant number of prewar multifamily homes and older single-family wood-frames. These structures often have balloon-frame construction, where wall cavities run uninterrupted from the basement to the roof line. Fire and smoke travel those cavities fast, and soot deposits in areas that look undamaged from the outside. Thermal imaging and systematic cavity inspection are not optional on these jobs — they're the difference between a complete restoration and a callback six months later when hidden char is discovered.

## Our Fire and Smoke Restoration Process in Fort Lee

Every post-fire restoration starts with a structured assessment, not a quick walk-through. The Restoration Group documents the loss with photographs, moisture readings, and air quality samples before a single piece of debris is moved. That documentation package is what your insurance adjuster needs, and it's what protects you if scope disputes arise later.

Once the assessment is complete, the process moves through demolition of unsalvageable materials, HEPA-filtered negative air pressure containment to prevent soot cross-contamination, structural drying if suppression water is present, and then odor neutralization using thermal fogging and hydroxyl generation. Smoke odor in a Fort Lee high-rise is particularly stubborn because HVAC recirculation embeds particles into ductwork — we treat the mechanical system, not just the visible surfaces.

Content pack-out, cleaning, and storage are coordinated for residents who need their belongings protected while structural work continues. Final reconstruction is handled under the same NJ Licensed Home Improvement Contractor license, so you're not managing two separate contractors.
