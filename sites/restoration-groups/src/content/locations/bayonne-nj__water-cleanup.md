## Why Bayonne Properties See Water Damage Differently

The housing that defines most of Bayonne's residential blocks was built in the early to mid-20th century. That means cast-iron drain lines that have been corroding quietly for decades, galvanized supply pipes that constrict and fail without warning, and finished basements beneath two- and three-family frame homes that were never designed with modern waterproofing in mind. When a supply line lets go behind a kitchen wall in a Midtown multi-family, the water doesn't stop at one unit — it travels down through floor assemblies and into the unit below.

The peninsula geography compounds the problem. Low-lying blocks near the Newark Bay waterfront and around Constable Hook carry the memory of Hurricane Sandy, when storm surge pushed into residential and industrial areas alike. Even in a routine nor'easter, those same low blocks can see groundwater intrusion through foundation walls and floor drains that simply can't keep pace with a saturated water table. Bayonne's tight rental market also means tenant-reported losses often sit unreported for days before a landlord is notified — by which point what started as a slow leak has become a mold-ready environment.

## Our Water Cleanup Process in Bayonne

Every water cleanup starts with moisture mapping, not assumptions. We use thermal imaging cameras and calibrated moisture meters to trace water migration through walls, subfloors, and ceiling assemblies — because in a brick rowhouse or a wood-frame three-family, water travels laterally through structural cavities in ways that a visual inspection will miss entirely.

Once we understand the full extent of saturation, we extract standing water with truck-mounted and portable extraction units, then place commercial-grade drying equipment — industrial air movers and refrigerant dehumidifiers — in a configuration calculated for the specific square footage and material density of your space. Drying logs are recorded daily and shared with your insurance adjuster. We don't pull equipment until the structure reads dry on the meters, not just dry to the touch. Throughout the process, we follow the IICRC S500 standard for water damage restoration, which governs everything from contamination classification to final clearance.

## Local Note

In Bayonne's older two- and three-family frame homes, the original plaster-and-lath wall construction behaves very differently from modern drywall when it gets wet. Plaster absorbs moisture slowly but releases it even more slowly — surface readings can show acceptable numbers while the wooden lath behind the plaster remains saturated. We extend drying cycles and take deeper-wall moisture readings in these structures rather than relying on surface meters alone. Pulling equipment too early in a plaster-wall home is one of the most common reasons a water loss turns into a mold remediation job weeks later.
