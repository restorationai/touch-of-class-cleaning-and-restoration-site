## Our Basement Flooding Cleanup Process in Ridgewood

Every flooded basement job starts with moisture mapping, not guesswork. Before a single piece of equipment is placed, a technician walks the space with a thermal imaging camera and calibrated moisture meters to identify where water has traveled — under tile, behind paneling, into the subfloor above. In Ridgewood's older finished basements, water routinely wicks into plaster-and-lath partition walls that were never meant to get wet, and those materials hold moisture differently than modern drywall.

Once the scope is documented, we extract standing water using truck-mounted and portable extraction units, then establish a structured drying system calibrated to the actual cubic footage and material types in the space. IICRC S500-compliant drying protocols govern how many air movers and dehumidifiers are placed and how long the system runs — we don't pull equipment early because the floor feels dry to the touch. Moisture readings are logged at every visit so you have a written record for your insurance adjuster. When contamination is present — from a sewer backup or groundwater intrusion — affected materials are removed, the space is treated with EPA-registered antimicrobials, and clearance readings are documented before any rebuild begins.

## Ridgewood Insurance Coordination

Most standard homeowners policies cover sudden and accidental water losses — a burst pipe, a failed sump pump, an appliance supply line failure — but exclude gradual seepage and surface water flooding. That distinction matters in Ridgewood, where many losses involve both: a sump pump overwhelmed by brook flooding may be a covered equipment failure even if the underlying cause was rising groundwater. We document the loss thoroughly — photographs, moisture logs, scope of affected materials — and provide your carrier with the structured format adjusters expect. We bill most major carriers directly, which means you're not fronting the full restoration cost while waiting for a claim to settle.
