## Why Manhattan Properties See Water Cleanup Emergencies

The borough's plumbing infrastructure spans more than a century. Pre-war buildings — many of them constructed before World War II with cast-iron risers and steam heating systems — develop failures at joints, valves, and supply lines that modern PEX plumbing simply doesn't share. When those systems fail, water doesn't follow a straight path. It pools inside hollow-core plaster walls, soaks into original hardwood subfloors, and wicks into masonry that was never designed to be dried with mechanical equipment.

More recent events have layered on top of that aging infrastructure. Hurricane Sandy's storm surge inundated the Financial District and the Lower East Side, exposing how quickly below-grade spaces — parking garages, mechanical rooms, lobby levels — can fill when the drainage system is overwhelmed. Tropical Storm Ida overwhelmed street drains from Harlem down through Chelsea, turning basement apartments into catch basins in under an hour. Climate-driven rainfall events are no longer rare in Manhattan; they're a recurring risk for any property with below-grade square footage or street-level retail.

## Our Water Cleanup Process in Manhattan

Water cleanup in a Manhattan high-rise or brownstone isn't the same job it is in a single-family suburban home. The process has to account for vertical water migration, building management protocols, and occupied adjacent units.

We start with thermal imaging and calibrated moisture readings across every affected surface — walls, ceilings, subfloors, and baseboards — before any extraction begins. That baseline map tells us where water has traveled beyond the visible wet zone, which in a plaster-and-lath building is almost always farther than it looks. Extraction follows using truck-mount and portable units suited to the building's access constraints; many Manhattan buildings require freight elevator coordination and after-hours staging agreements with the resident manager.

Drying equipment — commercial-grade desiccant dehumidifiers and high-velocity air movers — is placed according to the moisture map, not by formula. We maintain unit-by-unit drying logs throughout the process, which building management and insurance adjusters both require. No equipment comes out until readings confirm the structure has returned to pre-loss moisture levels across all monitored points.

## Local Note

In Manhattan's pre-war co-ops and brownstones, original plaster walls behave very differently from modern drywall when they've been saturated. Plaster is dense and slow to release moisture — surface readings can drop to acceptable levels within a day or two while the lath behind the plaster and the masonry behind the lath remain significantly elevated. A crew that pulls equipment based on surface readings alone will leave a building that looks dry but isn't. We extend monitoring in plaster-wall buildings as a matter of course, because the alternative is a mold call three weeks later.
