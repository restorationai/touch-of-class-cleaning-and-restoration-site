## Our Commercial Restoration Process in Edison

Commercial losses require a different operational rhythm than residential work. When we arrive at a loss site — whether it's a retail tenant space near Menlo Park or a multi-suite office building in the Stelton Road corridor — the first priority is stopping secondary damage while preserving the documentation chain your insurance adjuster will need.

Here is how the process unfolds on a typical commercial water loss:

1. **Rapid assessment and source control** — We identify the water source, coordinate with your building engineer or property manager to confirm shutoff, and photograph affected areas systematically before any materials are moved.
2. **Category and class determination** — Water from a clean supply line is handled differently than a backed-up floor drain or storm intrusion. We classify the loss per IICRC S500 standards, which directly affects the drying protocol and any required antimicrobial treatment.
3. **Extraction and structural drying** — Commercial-grade truck-mount extractors pull standing water from hard floors, carpet systems, and wall cavities. We deploy desiccant dehumidifiers and high-velocity air movers calibrated to the square footage and ceiling height of the affected space, and we monitor moisture readings daily with calibrated meters until the structure reaches documented dry standard.
4. **Contents and equipment protection** — Office furniture, racking systems, inventory, and electronics are inventoried, moved to a dry zone, or wrapped in place depending on the scope.
5. **Scope documentation for insurance** — We produce a line-item Xactimate estimate, a photo log, and a drying record that your carrier's adjuster can review directly. We work with most major commercial carriers and can communicate with your broker or risk manager throughout the project.

## Local Note: Flat Roofs, Drains, and the Monday-Morning Discovery

One pattern we see repeatedly on Edison's older commercial strips: a roof drain partially blocked by debris backs up during a weekend rainstorm, water pools on the membrane, finds a seam failure, and drips steadily into a drop-ceiling grid over a weekend when no one is in the building. By Monday morning, ceiling tiles have collapsed, the carpet is saturated, and the water has been sitting long enough for microbial activity to begin — typically within 24 to 48 hours of initial saturation. If your property has flat-roof drainage that hasn't been inspected since the last major storm, that inspection is worth scheduling before the next heavy rain. It won't prevent every loss, but it significantly reduces the severity when one does occur.
