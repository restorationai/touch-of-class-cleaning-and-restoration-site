## Why East Brunswick Properties See Sewage Backup Issues

East Brunswick's housing stock tells the story. The postwar tracts — many of them concentrated around areas like Frost Woods and Farrington — were built with cast-iron drain lines that are now 50 to 70 years old. Cast iron corrodes from the inside out, and root intrusion from mature oaks and maples accelerates the process. When those lines fail, they fail into finished basements: carpeted family rooms, wood-paneled walls, and drop ceilings that were never designed to survive a sewage event.

The Lawrence Brook watershed adds another layer. Low-lying sections of town along the brook and near the Route 18 corridor saw significant flooding during the remnants of Hurricane Irene and Ida. When stormwater overwhelms the municipal system, it can reverse-pressurize aging lateral lines and push sewage back through floor drains and toilets — even in homes that have never had a plumbing problem. That combination of aging infrastructure and periodic hydraulic overload makes sewage backup a recurring reality in East Brunswick, not a freak event.

Newer townhome and condo clusters introduce a different failure mode: shared lateral lines and stacked plumbing that can propagate a single clog or pipe failure across multiple units simultaneously.

## Our Sewage Cleanup and Sanitization Process in East Brunswick

Every sewage job follows a structured sequence calibrated to the contamination level and the specific materials involved.

**1. Containment and assessment.** We establish a containment perimeter before any extraction begins, preventing cross-contamination into unaffected areas. In East Brunswick's finished basements, that often means isolating the mechanical room from the living space.

**2. Extraction and removal.** Standing sewage is extracted with truck-mounted and portable units. Porous materials — carpet, pad, drywall below the flood line, insulation — that have contacted Category 3 water are removed and bagged per EPA disposal protocols. There is no cleaning porous material that has been saturated with raw sewage; it comes out.

**3. Antimicrobial treatment.** Structural surfaces — concrete slab, framing, subfloor — are treated with EPA-registered antimicrobial agents. We do not use bleach as a primary sanitizer; we use hospital-grade disinfectants formulated for porous and semi-porous substrates.

**4. Drying and documentation.** After sanitization, affected structural materials are dried to IICRC S500 standard moisture targets using desiccant dehumidifiers and air movers. Moisture readings are logged daily and provided to your insurance carrier.

**5. Clearance and rebuild coordination.** Once the structure passes moisture and contamination clearance, we coordinate with our reconstruction team or your preferred contractor for finish restoration.
