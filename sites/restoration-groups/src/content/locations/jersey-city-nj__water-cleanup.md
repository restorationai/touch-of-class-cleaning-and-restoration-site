## Why Jersey City Properties Face Distinct Water Damage Risks

Jersey City's geography works against it. Much of the city sits on low-lying ground that was exposed during Hurricane Sandy's surge and flooded again during Ida, when basements citywide took on water faster than sump pumps could cycle. The Hudson River waterfront looks resilient from the outside, but the soil beneath older blocks retains moisture long after a storm passes, keeping foundation walls under hydrostatic pressure for days.

The building stock compounds the problem. Rowhouses in Bergen-Lafayette and Greenville were built when combined sewer systems were standard — storm water and sanitary waste share the same pipe. A heavy rain event can reverse flow and push contaminated water into basements through floor drains, which is a different cleanup problem than a clean-water pipe burst. Waterfront towers at Newport and Exchange Place face a separate dynamic: riser failures or HVAC condensate overflows on an upper floor migrate through concrete slab assemblies and show up as ceiling stains two or three units below the source, making moisture mapping essential before any drying begins.

## Our Water Cleanup Process in Jersey City

The first thing we do on arrival is identify the water category. Clean supply-line water, gray water from an appliance, and sewage-contaminated water each require a different extraction and treatment protocol — and in Jersey City, combined-sewer backups mean we encounter category 3 contamination more often than in newer suburban developments.

Once the source is confirmed and stopped, we extract standing water using truck-mounted and portable units, then deploy thermal imaging and calibrated moisture meters to map where water has migrated inside wall cavities, under flooring, and into subfloor assemblies. In older plaster-and-lath construction — common throughout the Heights and Journal Square — water wicks into the lath and reads dry at the plaster surface well before the structure is actually dry. We set drying equipment based on what the meters show, not what the walls look like.

Drying timelines are monitored daily, and we document every reading. That documentation matters in Jersey City's dense multifamily environment, where a landlord or condo board may need to show an insurer or building association exactly which units were affected and when they reached drying goals.

## Jersey City Insurance and HOA Coordination

Multifamily ownership in Jersey City means water damage almost always involves more than one party — a unit owner, a building owner or management company, a condo association, and frequently two separate insurance policies. We produce moisture logs, photo documentation, and scope-of-loss reports formatted to support both the unit owner's claim and the building's master policy, so adjusters are working from the same set of facts.

For high-rise buildings along the waterfront, building management typically needs unit-by-unit moisture documentation before they will authorize repairs in common areas or adjacent units. We build that documentation into every job from day one rather than assembling it after the fact.
