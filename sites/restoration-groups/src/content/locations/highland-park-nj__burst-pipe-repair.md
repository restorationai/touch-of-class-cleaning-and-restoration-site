## Why Highland Park Properties See Burst Pipe Issues

The housing stock tells most of the story. The colonials and bungalows that line the streets of Livingston Manor and the Triangle neighborhood were built when galvanized steel supply pipe was standard. After 80-plus years, galvanized corrodes from the inside out — the interior diameter narrows, pressure spikes, and a cold snap or water-hammer event is all it takes to split a fitting or blow a joint. Cast-iron drain stacks in the same homes are similarly aged and prone to cracking under root intrusion or ground movement.

The climate compounds the risk. Central New Jersey's winters bring repeated freeze-thaw cycles through February and into March. Pipes in unheated crawl spaces, garage walls, and the rim-joist cavities common in these older homes are especially exposed. When temperatures drop sharply overnight — as they do along the Raritan River corridor — an uninsulated supply line in an exterior wall can freeze and rupture before sunrise.

Commercial properties along the Raritan Avenue business district face a different version of the same problem: aging sprinkler systems and supply mains in buildings that haven't had a full plumbing audit in decades. A head that freezes or a main that corrodes through can flood a restaurant kitchen or retail floor in minutes.

## Our Burst Pipe Cleanup and Repair Process in Highland Park

The first priority when we arrive is stopping the loss. If the homeowner hasn't already shut the main, we locate the shutoff — in older Highland Park homes it's often in the basement near the front foundation wall or, in some two-families, in a shared utility room — and isolate the affected zone. From there, the process follows a documented sequence:

**1. Moisture mapping.** We use thermal imaging cameras and calibrated moisture meters to trace water migration behind walls, under flooring, and into subfloor assemblies. In homes with plaster-over-lath construction — common in pre-1950 Highland Park — water wicks laterally along the lath before it ever shows on the surface, so this step is non-negotiable.

**2. Extraction and controlled demo.** Standing water is extracted with truck-mounted and portable units. Saturated drywall, insulation, or flooring that cannot be dried in place is removed to the flood cut line. We document every affected material with photographs before anything is touched — critical for insurance claims.

**3. Structural drying.** Commercial-grade desiccant dehumidifiers and high-velocity air movers are set to drying specifications calibrated to the affected materials and ambient conditions. As an IICRC Certified Firm (#210213), drying plans follow the S500 standard for water damage restoration.

**4. Pipe repair coordination.** We work alongside licensed plumbers to repair or replace the failed section before drying is complete, so the structure isn't re-wetted after remediation begins.

**5. Rebuild.** Once moisture readings confirm the structure is dry, our NJ Licensed Home Improvement Contractor crews handle framing, drywall, insulation, and finish work to return the space to pre-loss condition.
