## Why Somerville Properties See Sewage Backup So Often

Somerville's housing stock tells the story. The borough's compact grid — from the blocks flanking the Somerset County Courthouse out through East Main and Fairview — is built largely on homes constructed between 1900 and 1950. Those properties share a common plumbing profile: galvanized supply lines that have already been replaced once or twice, and original cast-iron drain stacks that have not. Cast iron corrodes from the inside out; by the time a homeowner notices slow drains, the pipe wall may already be paper-thin or cracked at a hub joint.

The Raritan River and Peters Brook add a second layer of risk. During heavy rain events — Somerville experienced significant flooding during both Tropical Storm Irene and Hurricane Ida — the municipal sewer system can surcharge, pushing sewage backward through floor drains and basement toilets in low-lying areas. Homes within a few blocks of the river corridor are especially vulnerable to this kind of combined sewer backup, which differs from a simple clog: the contamination load is far higher, and the volume can arrive fast.

## Our Sewage Cleanup and Sanitization Process in Somerville

Raw sewage is classified as Category 3 water — the highest contamination level in the IICRC S500 framework — and it requires a structured response, not just a wet-vac and a mop.

When we arrive, the first step is containment. We establish negative air pressure in the affected area using HEPA-filtered air scrubbers to prevent aerosolized contaminants from migrating into living spaces above the basement or into adjacent units. In Somerville's older two-family homes, where basement ceilings are often open-joist construction with no vapor barrier, this step is critical — contaminated air can move freely into the first-floor living area if the space isn't isolated.

Extraction follows: truck-mounted and portable units pull standing sewage and saturated material. Porous materials — drywall below the flood line, carpet, insulation, wood subflooring — are removed. There's no safe way to dry and restore Category 3-contaminated porous material; it has to come out. We document everything photographically before removal, which matters for your insurance claim.

Once the space is cleared to hard surfaces, we apply EPA-registered disinfectants rated for sewage contamination, paying particular attention to concrete block walls (common in Somerville basement construction), floor drains, and any HVAC returns that may have drawn contaminated air. A final ATP surface testing pass confirms sanitization before we begin the drying phase with commercial dehumidifiers and air movers.
