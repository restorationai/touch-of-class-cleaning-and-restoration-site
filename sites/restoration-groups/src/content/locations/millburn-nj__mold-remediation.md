## Why Millburn Properties See Elevated Mold Risk

Millburn's housing stock is part of the story. Many of the homes in Wyoming, Glenwood, and the older sections near the Millburn train station were built between the 1910s and 1940s. That era of construction relied heavily on rubble-stone foundations, brick cavity walls, and plaster applied over wood lath — materials that absorb and hold moisture in ways modern construction simply doesn't. A slow leak behind a finished basement wall in one of these homes can saturate structural framing for months before it becomes visible.

The river geography compounds the problem. The Rahway's West Branch passes directly through Taylor Park and the heart of downtown Millburn. Properties within a few blocks of the corridor sit on soil that stays saturated longer after heavy rain events, which drives relative humidity into crawl spaces and lower levels even when there's no direct flooding. New Jersey's humid summers — regularly hitting dew points above 65°F from June through September — mean that unventilated basements in these older homes rarely dry out completely between rain events.

Commercial properties along Millburn Avenue and the retail corridor near The Mall at Short Hills face a different but related exposure: sprinkler-discharge events and HVAC condensation losses in large, compartmentalized spaces where mold can spread across ceiling tiles and ductwork before it's detected.

## Our Mold Remediation Process in Millburn

Every remediation begins with a thorough assessment — not a visual scan, but moisture mapping using thermal imaging and calibrated meters to find wet material behind finished surfaces. In Millburn's older homes, that step is critical because plaster walls mask moisture readings that standard probes miss at the surface.

Once the extent of contamination is confirmed, the affected area is physically isolated with negative-air containment barriers. Air scrubbers running HEPA filtration run continuously throughout the project, capturing airborne spores before they can migrate to unaffected rooms — particularly important in the open floor plans common in Short Hills estate homes where a single HVAC return can distribute spores across multiple levels.

Affected porous materials — drywall, insulation, wood framing where warranted — are removed and bagged for disposal following EPA guidelines. Structural surfaces are then treated and dried to verified moisture levels per the IICRC S520 standard before any reconstruction begins. The Restoration Group is an IICRC Certified Firm (#210213), and the drying protocol doesn't close until readings confirm the substrate is within normal range — not just dry to the touch.

Mold claims in Millburn frequently involve a prior water loss — a burst pipe in an older copper or cast-iron system, a sump failure during a Rahway River flood event, or a slow roof leak that went undetected through a winter.
