## How to Compare Mold Remediation Companies in Texas

Choosing a mold remediation contractor in Texas is not the same as hiring a general handyman. The state has specific licensing requirements, and the [EPA's mold guidance](https://www.epa.gov/mold) recommends using contractors who follow established remediation protocols. Here is what to verify before you sign anything.

**1. Confirm the Texas TDLR license.** Go to the TDLR license lookup and search the company name. A mold remediation contractor working in Texas without a current license is operating illegally. This is the single most important check.

**2. Ask for the scope of work in writing.** A legitimate contractor will provide a written remediation plan before work begins. It should describe the affected areas, the containment approach, the disposal method, and whether post-remediation clearance testing is included or separate.

**3. Check whether clearance testing is independent.** In Texas, the mold assessor and the mold remediator are required to be separate parties on jobs above a certain threshold. If a company is doing both the assessment and the remediation without a separate licensed assessor, ask questions.

**4. Understand what insurance covers.** Homeowners policies typically cover mold remediation when it results directly from a covered water loss, such as a burst pipe. Mold from long-term humidity or a slow undetected leak is often excluded. Your adjuster will want documentation: photos, moisture readings, and a written scope. A licensed contractor who understands the insurance process will help you build that file.

**5. Do not skip containment.** Mold remediation without proper containment and negative air pressure can spread spores to unaffected areas of the property. The IICRC S520 standard specifies containment requirements. Ask the contractor how they handle containment before you hire them.
