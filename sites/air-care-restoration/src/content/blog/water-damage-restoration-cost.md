## How Much Does Water Damage Restoration Cost?

Water damage restoration in Texas typically costs $1,500 to $12,000 for a standard residential loss, with the national average landing around $3,500 to $5,000 for a single-room event. The number swings hard based on water category, affected square footage, materials involved, and how quickly mitigation started.

Here is a breakdown by common loss scenario:

| Loss Scenario | Typical Cost Range |
|---|---|
| Single room, clean water (Cat 1), caught within 24 hours | $1,200 - $3,500 |
| Single room, gray water (Cat 2, e.g., washing machine overflow) | $2,500 - $5,500 |
| Two to three rooms, clean water, 24-48 hour delay | $3,500 - $7,500 |
| Finished basement, clean water | $4,000 - $9,000 |
| Category 3 (sewage backup, floodwater), any size | $5,000 - $15,000+ |
| Whole-house event (roof failure, major pipe burst) | $8,000 - $20,000+ |

These are industry-typical ranges, not quotes. Air Care Restoration provides a written scope before any work begins so you know the exact cost for your specific loss.

## What Factors Drive the Cost Up or Down?

The biggest cost driver in water damage restoration is the IICRC water category, which determines how much contamination is present and how aggressively the crew must work. The [IICRC S500 standard](https://www.iicrc.org/page/IICRCS500) defines three categories that every certified restorer uses to scope a job.

**Category 1 (clean water)** comes from a supply line, a water heater, or a rain intrusion with no contamination. It is the least expensive to remediate because porous materials can often be dried in place rather than removed.

**Category 2 (gray water)** carries biological contamination from sources like a washing machine drain, dishwasher overflow, or toilet overflow with no solid waste. Affected drywall below the flood cut typically comes out. Costs run 30 to 60 percent higher than a comparable Cat 1 loss.

**Category 3 (black water)** is sewage, rising groundwater, or any water that has been sitting long enough to become grossly contaminated. All porous materials in the affected area must be removed. Cat 3 jobs require full PPE, containment, and antimicrobial treatment. Expect costs at the top of the range or beyond.

Beyond category, these factors move the number:

- **Square footage affected.** Drying costs are often quoted per square foot. More area means more air movers, more dehumidifier runtime, more monitoring visits.
- **Materials involved.** Hardwood floors, tile with mortar beds, and spray-foam-insulated walls all dry differently and some require removal. Standard drywall and carpet are cheaper to address than engineered hardwood or custom cabinetry.
- **How long the water sat.** Water that sat for 48 hours or more has likely wicked into wall cavities and subfloor assemblies. What would have been a $2,500 job at 12 hours can become a $6,000 job at 72 hours. Mold growth can begin within 24 to 48 hours in warm, humid conditions, which adds a remediation scope on top of the drying scope.
- **Structural drying time.** Most residential drying jobs take 3 to 5 days under the [IICRC S500](https://www.iicrc.org/page/IICRCS500) standard. Dense materials like concrete slabs or thick wood framing take longer. Each additional day of equipment runtime adds cost.
- **Reconstruction required.** Mitigation (drying and removal) is a separate scope from reconstruction (replacing drywall, flooring, cabinetry). Many restoration companies handle both. Get a clear line in the estimate between what is mitigation and what is rebuild.

## Will Insurance Pay for Water Damage Restoration?

Homeowners insurance typically covers sudden and accidental water damage but excludes gradual leaks and maintenance-related failures. This is the single most important thing to understand before you file a claim.

The [Texas Department of Insurance](https://www.tdi.texas.gov/pubs/consumer/cb030.html) explains that standard homeowners policies cover water damage that is sudden and accidental, such as a burst pipe, a failed appliance hose, or an HVAC condensate line that lets go unexpectedly. The policy generally does not cover water damage that resulted from a slow leak the homeowner could have detected and repaired over time.

**What is typically covered:**
- Burst pipes (sudden failure, not freeze-related neglect in most policies)
- Appliance supply line failures (washing machine, refrigerator, dishwasher)
- HVAC condensate overflow
- Water damage from an accidental overflow (left the tub running)
- Storm-related water intrusion through a damaged roof or wall

**What is typically NOT covered:**
- Gradual leaks behind walls or under slabs that went undetected for weeks or months
- Sewer backup (often requires a separate endorsement)
- Flood damage from rising groundwater or storm surge (requires separate NFIP or private flood policy)
- Damage caused by lack of maintenance

Documentation is what protects your claim. A certified restorer should provide moisture readings at intake, a written drying log, and a photo record of all affected materials before any removal. That paper trail is what an adjuster needs to approve the full scope. For a deeper look at how coverage works, the [homeowners insurance and water damage guide](/blog/does-homeowners-insurance-cover-water-damage/) covers the policy language in plain terms.

Your deductible applies regardless of the total loss size. If your deductible is $2,500 and the total mitigation cost is $3,200, you are paying $2,500 out of pocket and insurance covers $700. On smaller losses, it sometimes makes financial sense not to file a claim at all, since claims can affect your renewal rate. ## What Does the Restoration Process Actually Cost at Each Phase?

Water damage restoration happens in phases, and each phase has its own cost component. Understanding the breakdown helps you read an estimate without surprises.

**Emergency water extraction** is the first step: removing standing water with truck-mounted or portable extractors. For a single-room loss this typically runs $300 to $800. For a flooded basement with several inches of standing water, expect $800 to $2,000 just for extraction.

**Structural drying** is usually the largest line item. Air movers and LGR dehumidifiers run continuously, and the restorer monitors moisture levels daily with calibrated meters. Industry pricing runs roughly $100 to $200 per air mover per day and $50 to $150 per dehumidifier per day. A three-room job with six air movers and two dehumidifiers running for four days adds up quickly.

**Demolition and removal** covers flood cuts in drywall, removal of wet insulation, and pulling saturated flooring that cannot be dried in place. This is billed by the square foot or by the scope of materials removed. Disposal fees apply.

**Antimicrobial treatment** is applied to structural surfaces after drying, especially on Cat 2 or Cat 3 losses. This is a per-square-foot cost, typically $0.50 to $2.00 per square foot depending on the product and application method.

**Reconstruction** is quoted separately after the structure is dry and cleared. Replacing drywall, insulation, flooring, and paint in a single room typically runs $2,000 to $6,000 depending on materials. Kitchen or bathroom reconstruction with tile, cabinetry, or specialty finishes runs higher.

A full written scope from your restorer will itemize all of these phases so you and your adjuster are working from the same document.
