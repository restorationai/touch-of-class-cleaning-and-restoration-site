## When Do You Call a Restoration Company vs a Plumber First?

Call the plumber first only if water is actively flowing and you cannot find or reach the shutoff valve yourself. Once the source is stopped, whether by you turning the main valve or a plumber closing off a broken line, the next call is the restoration company, because standing water starts causing secondary damage within hours, not days.

A plumber's job ends at the pipe, fixture, or appliance that failed. They are not equipped to extract hundreds of gallons of water, run commercial dehumidifiers, or document moisture readings room by room for an insurance file. A restoration company picks up exactly where the plumber's scope ends.

If the situation involves downed power lines, a gas smell, or standing water near an electrical panel, call 911 or your utility company before anyone else. Life-safety hazards always come first.

## What Happens After You Call for Water Damage Help?

The first call is a short intake: your address, what caused the water, how long it has been sitting, and whether the source is stopped. From there the company schedules a technician to assess the loss and begin mitigation, working from what is called a category and class of water loss under the [IICRC](https://www.iicrc.org) S500 standard, which determines how the drying plan is built.

On arrival, expect a walkthrough with a moisture meter to map how far the water traveled behind walls and under flooring, not just what's visible. Extraction equipment removes standing water first. Air movers and low-grain-refrigerant (LGR) dehumidifiers go in next to dry the structure over several days, with daily moisture checks to confirm progress.

## What Should You Have Ready Before the Crew Arrives?

Have your insurance policy information and the source of the water ready to describe, since both shape how quickly mitigation starts and how the claim gets documented. Photos of the damage before anything is moved help your claim later, so take a few with your phone if it's safe to do so.

A few things that speed up the visit:

- Shut off electricity to any affected outlets or rooms if water is near them and it's safe to reach the breaker
- Move furniture, rugs, or electronics out of standing water if you can do so without wading through it
- Note the time you first noticed the water, which matters for the category classification
- Keep pets and kids out of the affected area until the crew clears it

If the water came from a toilet backup, sewer line, or any source outside a clean supply line, treat it as [Category 3, or black water](/blog/category-3-water-damage/), and don't attempt cleanup yourself. That water carries contamination that needs PPE and containment protocols a homeowner shouldn't try to manage alone.

## How Does Insurance Fit Into the Call You Make?

Call your insurance company after mitigation is underway, not instead of the restoration company. Most homeowners policies cover sudden, accidental water damage, and the adjuster will want to see the moisture documentation and drying logs a certified crew produces during the job.

A restoration company that works claims regularly in the Abilene area typically photographs, measures, and logs the loss daily, then provides that file directly to your adjuster. That paperwork is often the difference between a smooth claim and a disputed one.
