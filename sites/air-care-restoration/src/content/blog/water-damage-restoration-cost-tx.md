## How Much Does Water Damage Restoration Cost in Texas?

Water damage restoration in Texas costs between $1,500 and $8,000 for most single-family homes, with the national average around $3,500 according to industry data. Jobs involving sewage backup or long-delayed discovery regularly exceed $10,000 once structural drying, antimicrobial treatment, and partial reconstruction are factored in.

The single biggest cost driver is the IICRC water category. The [IICRC S500 standard](https://www.iicrc.org) defines three categories: Category 1 (clean water from a supply line), Category 2 (gray water from appliances or overflow), and Category 3 (black water, which includes sewage and floodwater). Each step up the scale adds labor, PPE, disposal costs, and drying time.

## What Factors Move the Price Up or Down?

Six factors account for most of the variance you'll see between two seemingly similar jobs.

**1. Water category.** As noted above, Category 1 clean water from a supply line is the least expensive to remediate. Category 3 black water (sewage, floodwater, or water that has sat long enough to grow bacteria) requires full PPE, antimicrobial treatment, and disposal of porous materials that cannot be saved. That adds cost at every step.

**2. IICRC water class (extent of saturation).** The S500 standard also defines four classes of water intrusion based on how much material is wet. Class 1 (partial room, minimal absorption) dries fast. Class 4 (specialty drying, concrete, hardwood, plaster) requires low-grain refrigerant dehumidifiers running for days and drives costs up sharply.

**3. Square footage affected.** Restoration pricing scales with the area that needs drying equipment, antimicrobial treatment, and monitoring. A 200-square-foot bathroom loss is a different job than a 1,200-square-foot first floor.

**4. Materials involved.** Wet drywall that has been saturated for less than 24 hours can often be dried in place. Wet drywall that has been wet for 72 hours typically needs to be cut out and replaced. Hardwood floors, cabinetry, and insulation each carry their own cost profile for removal, disposal, and replacement.

**5. Response time.** The [EPA recommends](https://www.epa.gov/mold/mold-course-chapter-2) that wet materials be dried within 24-48 hours to prevent mold growth. Jobs called in within hours of the event almost always cost less than jobs where the homeowner waited a few days. Mold remediation on top of water damage can double the total cost. See our post on [what to do in the first 24 hours after water damage](/blog/what-to-do-first-24-hours-water-damage/) for the steps that protect your wallet as much as your home.

**6. Reconstruction scope.** Mitigation (drying and cleaning) and reconstruction (replacing drywall, flooring, cabinets) are often billed separately. Air Care Restoration handles both through its [general contracting service](/services/general-contracting/), which simplifies the insurance process considerably.

## Will Homeowners Insurance Cover This?

Most standard Texas homeowners policies cover sudden and accidental water damage but exclude gradual leaks and flooding. The distinction matters a lot for your out-of-pocket cost.

**Typically covered:** Burst pipe, failed water heater, washing machine supply line failure, accidental overflow from a tub or sink. These are sudden and accidental events, which is the language most policies use.

**Typically not covered:** Slow leak behind a wall that went undetected for months, foundation seepage, or rising floodwater from a storm. Flood damage from rising water requires a separate [National Flood Insurance Program](https://www.fema.gov/flood-insurance) policy or a private flood endorsement.

**Your deductible reality:** Most Texas homeowners carry a $1,000 to $2,500 deductible. On a $4,000 mitigation job, you're paying a meaningful portion out of pocket even with coverage. That's why documentation from the first hour matters, photos, moisture readings, and a written scope of work are what protect your claim.

## What Does the Restoration Process Actually Look Like?

Knowing the steps helps you understand why the cost is what it is. Here's what a standard residential water damage job looks like from the first call through completion.

**Step 1: Emergency response and assessment.** Air Care Restoration responds 24/7. On arrival, technicians identify the water source (if still active), categorize the water, and map the affected areas with moisture meters. This assessment drives the scope of work.

**Step 2: Water extraction.** Standing water is removed first using truck-mounted or portable extraction equipment. The faster this happens, the less secondary damage occurs.

**Step 3: Structural drying setup.** LGR (low-grain refrigerant) dehumidifiers and air movers are placed according to a drying plan. The number of units and the duration they run are calculated based on the IICRC S500 standard for the class of loss. Typical residential drying takes 3-5 days.

**Step 4: Daily moisture monitoring.** Technicians return each day to log moisture readings and adjust equipment placement. Drying is not done when the floor feels dry to the touch, it's done when the readings reach the IICRC-defined dry standard for that material type.

**Step 5: Antimicrobial treatment.** Once dry, affected surfaces receive EPA-registered antimicrobial treatment, particularly on Category 2 and Category 3 losses.

**Step 6: Demolition of unsalvageable materials.** Wet drywall, insulation, and flooring that cannot be dried to standard are removed, bagged, and disposed of properly. This is often called "flood cut" work.

**Step 7: Reconstruction.** Once the structure passes final moisture readings, reconstruction begins. New drywall, insulation, flooring, and finishes are installed. Air Care Restoration handles this phase directly through its [general contracting service](/services/general-contracting/) so you don't need a separate contractor.

**Step 8: Final documentation.** A complete moisture log and photo record is provided for your insurance file.
