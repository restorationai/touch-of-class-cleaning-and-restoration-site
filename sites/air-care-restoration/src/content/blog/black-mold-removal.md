## How to Compare Mold Removal Companies in Abilene

Choosing the right mold remediation contractor comes down to three verifiable factors, not marketing claims.

**1. Texas TDLR Mold License.** This is non-negotiable. Texas law requires any company performing mold remediation on more than 25 contiguous square feet to hold a TDLR Mold Remediation Contractor license. You can verify any company's license at [tdlr.texas.gov](https://www.tdlr.texas.gov/mold/mold.htm). Ask for the license number before signing anything.

**2. IICRC Certification.** The [IICRC S520 Standard](https://www.iicrc.org) is the technical benchmark for mold remediation, it governs how containment is built, what air filtration equipment is required, how clearance testing is done, and when a job is actually finished. An IICRC-certified firm has technicians trained to this standard.

**3. Written scope before work begins.** A reputable mold remediation company provides a written scope of work that describes exactly what will be removed, how containment will be set up, and what clearance testing will confirm the job is done. Never authorize work based on a verbal quote alone, especially if insurance is involved.

If you want a deeper look at how to evaluate restoration contractors, see our post on [how to choose a restoration company](/blog/choosing-a-restoration-company/).

## What "Black Mold" Actually Means

The term "black mold" is commonly used to mean *Stachybotrys chartarum*, a dark-colored mold that grows on cellulose materials (drywall paper, wood framing, ceiling tiles) after prolonged moisture exposure. But mold color alone does not identify the species, and not all dark mold is *Stachybotrys*. The only way to confirm species is laboratory testing of a physical or air sample.

What matters more than color is the moisture source and the extent of growth. Any mold colony larger than 10 square feet is classified by the [EPA as requiring professional remediation](https://www.epa.gov/mold/mold-cleanup-your-home). Smaller patches may be addressable by a homeowner, but if the mold returned after a previous cleanup attempt, or if you can smell mold but can't see it, call a professional.

For more on what mold color actually tells you (and what it doesn't), see our post on [black mold vs. regular mold](/blog/black-mold-vs-regular-mold/).

## What the Mold Removal Process Looks Like

A properly executed mold remediation job follows a defined sequence. Here's what to expect when you hire a licensed contractor:

1. **Moisture assessment and source identification.** Mold is a symptom. The moisture source, a slow leak, condensation, a past flood, must be identified and corrected before remediation begins. If the moisture isn't fixed, the mold comes back.
2. **Containment setup.** The affected area is sealed with poly sheeting and negative air pressure is established using HEPA-filtered air scrubbers. This prevents cross-contamination to unaffected areas of the home.
3. **Removal of affected materials.** Porous materials (drywall, insulation, carpet) with active mold growth are removed and bagged for disposal. Non-porous surfaces are HEPA-vacuumed and cleaned with an EPA-registered antimicrobial.
4. **HEPA air filtration.** Air scrubbers run continuously during and after removal to capture airborne spores.
5. **Clearance testing.** An independent third-party inspector (separate from the remediation contractor) collects air samples after the job is complete. The lab results confirm spore counts are within acceptable limits before the space is reopened.
6. **Reconstruction.** Once clearance is confirmed, removed drywall and materials are replaced. Air Care Restoration handles this in-house through their general contracting service.

If a company skips containment or doesn't mention clearance testing, that's a red flag.
