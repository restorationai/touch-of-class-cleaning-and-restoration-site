## What Should You Look for in a Water Damage Restoration Company?

The most important criteria when choosing a water damage restoration company are IICRC certification, 24/7 availability, and the ability to handle the full scope of a water loss from extraction through drying to any necessary mold remediation.

Here is what each of those actually means in practice:

**IICRC Certification:** The [IICRC](https://www.iicrc.org) (Institute of Inspection, Cleaning and Restoration Certification) is the restoration industry's primary standards body. Their S500 standard defines how water damage should be assessed, categorized, and dried. A company that holds IICRC Certified Firm status and employs WRT-credentialed technicians is working from a documented, peer-reviewed process. This matters for your insurance claim and for the quality of the dry-out.

**24/7 Emergency Response:** Water damage compounds by the hour. Category 1 water (clean water from a supply line) can become Category 2 or 3 (contaminated) within 24-48 hours as it contacts building materials and organic matter. A company that only operates business hours may not be able to respond before secondary damage sets in.

**Full-Scope Capability:** Many water losses involve more than one trade. A burst pipe near a wall may require water extraction, structural drying, and later mold remediation. Sewage backups require biohazard-level cleanup. A company that can handle all of it under one scope avoids the coordination gap between multiple contractors.

**Insurance Documentation:** Restoration companies that work with insurance regularly know how to produce the moisture logs, drying records, and scope documentation that adjusters require. Ask whether the company provides written documentation of pre- and post-drying moisture readings.

## What Happens During a Water Damage Restoration Job?

A professional water damage restoration job follows a defined process: emergency extraction, moisture mapping, structural drying, monitoring, and clearance testing. Each phase has specific equipment and documentation requirements.

1. **Emergency extraction:** Standing water is removed using truck-mounted or portable extraction units. The goal is to remove bulk water before it migrates further into building assemblies.
2. **Moisture mapping:** Technicians use calibrated moisture meters and thermal imaging to identify where water has traveled inside walls, under flooring, and into subfloor materials. This defines the drying zone.
3. **Structural drying:** Air movers and LGR dehumidifiers are placed according to a drying plan. Air movers accelerate evaporation from wet surfaces; dehumidifiers capture that moisture and remove it from the air. The IICRC S500 standard defines target drying goals for different material categories.
4. **Daily monitoring:** Moisture readings are logged each day. Equipment is adjusted based on the readings. This documentation is what the insurance adjuster reviews.
5. **Clearance:** When materials reach their target moisture levels, equipment is removed and a final moisture reading is recorded. If mold has begun to develop, remediation is scoped separately.
