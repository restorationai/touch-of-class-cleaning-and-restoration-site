## How to Compare Water Damage Repair Companies in Abilene

Choosing the right company comes down to five factors that actually affect your outcome:

**1. IICRC certification.** The [IICRC S500 Standard for Professional Water Damage Restoration](https://www.iicrc.org) is the industry baseline. Ask any company you call: "Are you an IICRC Certified Firm, and does your lead tech hold WRT certification?" If they can't answer yes to both, keep calling.

**2. Texas licensing for mold.** Texas requires a separate Mold Remediation Contractor License for any company performing mold remediation work. Water damage almost always creates mold risk, you want a company that can legally handle both under one roof. Verify licenses at the [Texas Department of Licensing and Regulation](https://www.tdlr.texas.gov).

**3. True 24/7 response.** "We have an emergency line" is not the same as "a crew can be on-site tonight." Ask: "If I call at 2 a.m. on a Sunday, will a crew come out?" The answer tells you what you need to know.

**4. Insurance documentation.** A good restoration company doesn't just dry your house, they produce the moisture readings, drying logs, and scope of work your adjuster needs to process the claim. Ask if they use industry-standard moisture mapping and whether they'll communicate directly with your insurance carrier.

**5. Scope of services.** If your water loss also caused structural damage, mold growth, or sewage contamination, a company that only does extraction and drying will hand you off mid-job. Confirm they can handle the full scope before you sign anything.

## What Does Water Damage Repair Actually Involve?

Water damage repair is not just drying. The full process, when done to the IICRC S500 standard, has distinct phases:

1. **Emergency extraction.** Standing water is removed with truck-mounted or portable extractors. The faster this happens, the less secondary damage occurs.
2. **Moisture mapping.** Technicians use thermal imaging cameras and penetrating moisture meters (such as a Tramex) to locate water migration behind walls, under flooring, and in ceiling cavities. What you can't see is often worse than what you can.
3. **Structural drying.** LGR (low-grain refrigerant) dehumidifiers and high-velocity air movers are placed in a calculated pattern based on the affected square footage and material type. Drying typically takes 3-5 days for Category 1 (clean water) losses in standard drywall construction.
4. **Mold prevention assessment.** If drying is delayed or the water was Category 2 (gray water, such as a washing machine overflow) or Category 3 (black water, such as sewage), mold risk escalates quickly. The [EPA recommends](https://www.epa.gov/mold) beginning mold prevention steps within 24-48 hours of a water event.
5. **Demolition of unsalvageable materials.** Wet drywall, insulation, and flooring that can't be dried in place are removed. This is called "flood cuts" in the industry.
6. **Reconstruction.** Once the structure is dry and cleared, rebuild begins, drywall, flooring, paint, trim.
