Stop the water first. If a pipe burst or an appliance failed, find your main shutoff valve and turn it off before you do anything else. In most Abilene homes, that valve is near the water meter at the street or on an exterior wall facing the meter. Once the source is off, you have a narrow window — roughly 24 to 48 hours — before wet building materials become a mold problem. Here is exactly what to do with that time.

## Stop the Source and Make the Space Safe

Before you wade in, check two things: electricity and structural stability.

If water is near any outlet, panel, or appliance, cut power to the affected area at the breaker box. Standing water conducts electricity, and a wet floor near a live outlet is a genuine hazard. If you are not certain which breakers control the wet area, shut off the main.

Next, look up. A ceiling that is bulging or sagging has water pooling above it. Do not stand under it. A saturated ceiling can hold hundreds of pounds of water before it lets go — and when it does, it comes down fast. Puncture a small hole at the lowest point with a screwdriver to let it drain in a controlled way, and keep a bucket ready.

Once the space is safe to enter:

1. Shut off the water supply if you have not already.
2. Cut power to the affected rooms at the breaker.
3. Photograph everything before you move or remove anything — your insurance claim depends on documentation.
4. Open windows and interior doors to start air moving through the space.

## Document Before You Touch Anything

This step costs you nothing but a few minutes, and skipping it can cost you thousands on your insurance claim.

Walk the affected area with your phone and shoot video first — a slow, continuous sweep of every wall, floor, and ceiling surface. Then take still photos: close-ups of the water source, the waterline on walls, soaked flooring, and any personal property that was damaged. If you have a second story involved, document both levels.

Note the time and date in your first photo, or check that your phone's metadata is recording timestamps. Adjusters look at documentation timelines. A gap between when the loss happened and when photos were taken can raise questions.

Save the damaged materials. Do not throw out wet carpet, baseboards, or drywall until an adjuster or restoration professional has seen them. Insurers sometimes require physical evidence of the loss.

## Remove Standing Water and Begin Drying Immediately

Every hour water sits, it moves further into materials. Water does not stay on the surface — it wicks up drywall, travels under flooring, and saturates insulation inside walls. The clock matters.

If there is less than an inch of standing water over a small area, a wet/dry shop vac can help you pull the bulk of it up. For anything more than that, or for water spread across multiple rooms, you need extraction equipment that goes deeper than a shop vac can reach.

Once visible water is removed:

- Run every fan you own across the wet surfaces, pointed low and angled toward walls.
- If you have a dehumidifier, run it continuously and empty it as often as needed.
- Remove soaked area rugs — they hold moisture against the subfloor and slow drying significantly.
- Pull furniture off wet carpet and put aluminum foil or plastic cups under the legs to prevent rust and dye transfer.
- Open cabinet doors under sinks and in adjacent rooms to let air circulate into those cavities.

One thing worth knowing about West Texas conditions: Abilene's dry climate can be deceptive. The low ambient humidity means surfaces dry faster than in humid regions, but that same dryness can cause wet drywall to crack and separate from framing before the interior moisture has fully escaped. Surface readings can look dry while the wall cavity is still saturated. A moisture meter — not a visual check — is the only reliable way to confirm drywall and subfloor are actually dry.

## What Not to Do in the First 24 Hours

Some of the most common instincts after a water loss actually make things worse.

**Do not use a regular household vacuum.** Standard vacuums are not designed for water and can be damaged or cause electrical hazards when used on wet surfaces.

**Do not run the HVAC system to dry things out.** If water entered the ductwork or the air handler, running the system spreads contaminated air through the entire house. It can also pull mold spores — if any have already begun forming — into circulation. Leave the HVAC off until the system has been inspected.

**Do not assume the damage is only where you can see it.** Water behind plaster or drywall can read dry at the surface while the lath or framing stays saturated. If the leak ran for any length of time before you noticed it, assume the damage extends further than the visible waterline.

**Do not discard anything before your insurance company has been notified.** Call your carrier early — many policies require prompt notice of a loss, and some have language about mitigation steps the homeowner must take to preserve coverage.

## When to Call a Water Damage Professional

Some water losses are manageable with what you have on hand. A small appliance leak caught immediately, confined to a hard-surface floor, can often be dried without professional help if you act fast and monitor moisture levels.

Call a professional restoration company when:

- The water source was a toilet, sewage line, or any drain — that water is considered contaminated and requires different handling than clean supply-line water.
- The water reached drywall, insulation, hardwood floors, or a crawl space.
- You cannot identify where all the water went.
- The affected area is larger than roughly 10 square feet of flooring.
- You smell anything musty within the first day — mold can begin colonizing porous materials within 24 to 48 hours under the right conditions.
- The structure involves older construction with potential lead paint or asbestos-containing materials, which require specific handling before any demolition.

Professional water damage restoration uses truck-mounted extractors, industrial air movers, and refrigerant or desiccant dehumidifiers that pull moisture at a rate household equipment cannot match. More importantly, restoration technicians use thermal imaging and calibrated moisture meters to find water that has migrated behind walls and under floors — the water you cannot see is usually the water that causes the most damage.

## The Longer Recovery: What Comes After Day One

If the structure dries completely — typically three to five days with proper equipment in place — and no mold colonization occurred, repairs can begin. That usually means replacing drywall that was cut for drying access, reinstalling baseboards, and addressing flooring.

If drying was delayed or incomplete, remediation comes before reconstruction. Mold that has begun growing in a wall cavity does not stop because the visible surface looks fine. Remediation involves containing the affected area, removing colonized material, treating surfaces, and verifying air quality before closing the wall back up.

Documentation from the drying phase — daily moisture readings, equipment logs, photos — becomes part of the claim file and helps establish that the work was done correctly. Reputable restoration companies provide this as a standard part of their process.
