## Why Eastland Properties Are Especially Vulnerable After a Storm

Eastland sits in a corridor of North-Central Texas where supercell thunderstorms track regularly through spring and early summer, bringing large hail, straight-line winds, and occasional tornadoes. That combination is hard on roofing — particularly the older wood-frame homes that make up a significant portion of Eastland's residential stock. Many of these homes were built in the mid-twentieth century with roof pitches and framing standards that predate modern wind-load requirements. A single hail event can crack ridge caps and displace flashing; a strong gust can peel back a section of shingles entirely, leaving decking exposed.

Fire damage compounds the problem differently. Structure fires in older wood-frame construction often leave window openings, door frames, and roof sections compromised even after the flames are out. Soot and smoke residue begin migrating into unaffected rooms within hours if openings aren't sealed. Board-up isn't just about keeping rain out — it's about containing the loss footprint.

## Our Emergency Board-Up and Tarping Process in Eastland

When we arrive at a property in Eastland, the first step is a rapid exterior assessment: we identify every compromised opening — broken windows, kicked-in doors, fire-damaged walls, missing roof sections — and prioritize by exposure risk. Wind direction matters here; a gap on the south or southwest face of a structure takes on water fast during a typical Eastland storm track.

For roof tarping, we use heavy-duty polyethylene tarps secured with wood battens and screws driven into undamaged decking — not bungee cords or sandbags, which shift under wind load. We overlap seams by at least two feet and run the tarp past the ridge line when possible to prevent water from wicking underneath. For board-up, we cut plywood to fit each opening and fasten it with structural screws rather than nails, which pull out under repeated wind pressure. Every opening gets photographed before and after installation to support your insurance documentation.

If a property has sustained fire damage, we coordinate with the fire marshal's release before entering — standard practice in Texas — and we can begin board-up immediately once the scene is cleared.

## Working with Your Insurance Carrier on an Eastland Loss

Most homeowner policies in Texas cover emergency protective measures — board-up and tarping — as part of the covered loss, not as a separate out-of-pocket expense. That said, carriers want documentation: photographs of the damage before any work begins, a written scope of what was installed, and receipts for materials. We provide all of that as a standard part of every job.

If your carrier requires a preferred vendor or wants to send their own adjuster before work starts, call us anyway — we can walk you through what you're entitled to under Texas insurance code and help you understand your options. Delaying protective measures while waiting for adjuster approval can result in a carrier disputing subsequent water or vandalism damage as "preventable."
