## Why Brownwood Properties See Significant Storm Damage

Brown County's position in West-Central Texas puts it squarely in a collision zone for dry continental air from the northwest and Gulf moisture pushing up from the south. That combination produces severe thunderstorm cells capable of large hail, damaging winds, and isolated tornadoes — sometimes within the same storm system. Brownwood's housing stock adds another layer of vulnerability. Many homes in and around the city were built in the mid-twentieth century, when roof decking was thinner and fastener patterns less robust than modern codes require. An aging roof that has already weathered decades of Texas sun and freeze-thaw cycling can fail under hail impact or wind uplift in ways a newer roof might not. Mature trees — a genuine asset in a city where summer shade matters — become a liability when sustained winds exceed 60 mph and limbs or whole trunks come down on structures.

Flat or gently sloping lots common across much of Brown County also mean that storm surge from heavy rainfall has fewer places to go. When soil is already saturated from earlier rain events, water finds the path of least resistance: crawl spaces, garage slabs, and the gaps around aging foundation penetrations.

## Our Storm Damage Restoration Process in Brownwood

Every storm loss is different, but the sequence of decisions that limits long-term damage is consistent. When we arrive, the first priority is emergency stabilization — tarping compromised roof sections, boarding breached windows and doors, and removing any standing water from interior spaces before it migrates into subfloor assemblies or wall cavities. We document everything with photos and written scope before a single material is removed, because that documentation is what your insurance adjuster will use to evaluate the claim.

Once the structure is stabilized, we move into systematic assessment. Moisture meters and thermal imaging cameras reveal water that has traveled behind walls or under flooring — damage that looks minor at the surface but is actively feeding mold colonization. The IICRC S500 standard guides our drying protocols, and our team holds IICRC certification as a firm alongside IICRC WRT credentials, so the drying plan is built on tested science rather than guesswork. Structural repairs — framing, sheathing, roofing, drywall — follow once the building envelope is dry and verified.
