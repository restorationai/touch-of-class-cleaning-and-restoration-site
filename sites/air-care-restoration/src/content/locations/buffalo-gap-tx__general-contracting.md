## Why Buffalo Gap's Building Stock and Climate Shape Renovation Work

The housing in and around Buffalo Gap tends toward older ranch-style construction — single-story, pier-and-beam or slab-on-grade, with framing and finish materials that reflect the era they were built in. That matters for remodeling because what's behind the walls often surprises even experienced contractors. Plumbing may be galvanized steel rather than copper or PEX. Insulation may be minimal or missing in exterior wall cavities. Electrical panels in homes built before the 1980s frequently predate modern load requirements, meaning a kitchen remodel that adds a dishwasher, microwave circuit, and under-cabinet lighting can expose a panel that needs upgrading before the finish work begins.

The expansive clay soils common across this part of Taylor County also create a specific challenge for any renovation touching flooring or foundation-adjacent spaces. Seasonal moisture swings cause the soil to swell and contract, which can telegraph into tile cracking, door frames that rack out of square, and gaps at baseboards. A remodel that ignores these movement patterns — installing rigid tile over a slab that hasn't been assessed for levelness and stability — is setting up the homeowner for callbacks. The process here accounts for that from the start.

## The Renovation and General Contracting Process in Buffalo Gap

Every project begins with a thorough walkthrough that goes beyond the visible scope. Before a single cabinet is ordered or a tile is selected, the structural and mechanical conditions get assessed — foundation levelness, moisture readings in wall cavities, condition of existing electrical and plumbing rough-ins. For post-damage rebuilds, this assessment also documents the loss for insurance purposes, with photographs and written scope that carriers can review.

From there, the project moves through permitting with Taylor County, material procurement, and phased construction sequenced to minimize the time a home is disrupted. In West Texas heat, sequencing matters practically: exterior work gets scheduled to avoid peak afternoon temperatures where possible, and interior finishes like paint and caulk are applied in conditioned spaces to ensure proper curing. Final walkthroughs confirm that every trade — framing, mechanical, finish — meets both code requirements and the homeowner's expectations before the project closes out.

## Local Note

One thing that catches out-of-area contractors working in this part of Taylor County: the clay soil shrinkage during dry summers can leave visible gaps around exterior door frames and window casings that look like a framing or installation failure but are actually seasonal movement. A renovation that addresses those gaps with rigid caulk alone will see them reopen within a year. The right approach uses a flexible, paintable sealant rated for high-movement joints and, where the gap reflects a deeper racking issue, addresses the underlying frame before any finish work.
