## Why Eden Properties Are Vulnerable After a Storm or Fire

The Concho County landscape offers little in the way of natural windbreaks. Open rangeland surrounds much of Eden, and when a storm system tracks through the region, gusts can strip shingles, snap tree limbs into windows, and lift lightweight metal roofing that is common on older agricultural outbuildings and rural residential structures in the area. Homes built in the mid-twentieth century — a significant portion of Eden's housing stock — often have original wood-frame windows and aging roof decking that can fail faster under impact than modern construction.

Fire damage compounds the problem differently. A kitchen fire or electrical fire that scorches a wall can also blow out window glazing from heat pressure, leaving the interior exposed to wind-driven rain within minutes of suppression. Without a board-up, smoke-damaged contents get wet, and a restoration job that could have been contained becomes a multi-trade reconstruction.

## Our Emergency Board-Up and Tarping Process in Eden

When a call comes in from an Eden address, the crew loads pre-cut plywood panels, heavy-duty polyethylene tarps rated for high-wind conditions, cap nails, and structural fasteners before leaving Abilene. The process on-site follows a consistent sequence:

1. **Damage assessment** — Every opening, compromised roof section, and structurally weakened wall is documented with photographs before any material goes up. This documentation feeds directly into the insurance claim file.
2. **Roof tarping** — Tarps are anchored with wood battens and fasteners driven into solid decking, not just laid over the surface. In West Texas wind conditions, a tarp that is not mechanically secured is a liability, not protection.
3. **Window and door boarding** — Openings are measured and panels are cut to fit flush, then secured to the framing rather than to the siding. On older Eden homes with narrow or non-standard window dimensions, panels are sometimes custom-cut on-site.
4. **Perimeter check** — The crew walks the full structure before leaving to confirm no secondary openings were missed — crawl space vents, attic louvers, and garage door gaps are common oversights that allow wind and moisture to enter.

All work is performed by an IICRC-certified team with OSHA training and EPA Lead-Safe certification, which matters on pre-1978 homes where disturbing painted surfaces during board-up requires specific handling protocols.

## Local Note

One detail that catches out-of-area contractors working in Eden: metal roofing — standing seam and corrugated panels — is far more common here than in urban markets, and tarping over metal requires a different anchor strategy than tarping over asphalt shingles. Driving fasteners through metal panels to secure a tarp batten can create new penetration points that cause leaks after the tarp is removed. The correct approach is to anchor battens to the roof's structural members at the ridge or eave, bridging over the metal surface rather than puncturing it. Crews familiar only with shingle roofs sometimes skip this step and create a secondary damage problem.
