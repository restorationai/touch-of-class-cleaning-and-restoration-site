## Why Snyder Properties See Duct Buildup Faster Than You'd Expect

The Llano Estacado climate that defines this part of Texas is not gentle on HVAC equipment. Summers push cooling systems to run nearly continuously from May through September, and the region's persistent southwesterly winds carry agricultural dust — soil, crop residue, and cottonseed fiber during harvest season — that infiltrates homes even with windows closed. Older homes in Snyder, many built during the mid-twentieth-century oil-boom era, often have ductwork installed before modern air-sealing standards existed. Flex duct connections that have loosened over decades, or original sheet-metal runs with unsealed joints, allow attic air — which in a West Texas summer can exceed 140°F and carries its own load of insulation fibers and dust — to bleed directly into the supply stream. The result is ductwork that accumulates debris faster than a comparable home in a more temperate, less agricultural climate.

Dryer vents are a separate but related concern. In Snyder's single-story ranch-style homes, dryer vent runs are often routed through interior walls rather than short exterior paths, making lint traps more likely to clog and reducing airflow efficiency — a fire-risk issue that a thorough duct cleaning visit should also address.

## Our Air Duct Cleaning Process in Snyder

The approach starts before any equipment enters the home. A technician walks the system — locating every supply register, return grille, and the air handler itself — and photographs the interior of representative duct sections with an inspection camera. That baseline matters: it documents what's actually present before work begins, which is useful if you're filing a homeowner's insurance claim tied to a covered event like storm infiltration or fire smoke.

Cleaning uses a truck-mounted or portable negative-air machine that places the entire duct system under continuous negative pressure while rotating brush heads and compressed-air whips dislodge debris from duct walls. Every register is sealed except the one being actively cleaned, so loosened material travels toward the collection unit rather than back into living spaces. After mechanical cleaning, an EPA-registered antimicrobial agent can be applied to interior duct surfaces if microbial growth is present — something worth considering in Snyder homes that have experienced roof leaks or swamp-cooler conversions that introduced excess humidity into the duct system. The air handler coil, blower wheel, and drain pan are inspected and cleaned as part of the same visit, because a clean duct connected to a fouled coil doesn't solve the air-quality problem.
