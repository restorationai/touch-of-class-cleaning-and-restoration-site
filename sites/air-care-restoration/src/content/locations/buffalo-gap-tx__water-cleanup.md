## Why Buffalo Gap Properties See Water Damage Issues

The West Texas climate is deceptive. Long dry stretches cause clay soils to shrink and pull away from foundations; when a significant rain event finally arrives — or a supply line fails — water finds those gaps and travels fast. Buffalo Gap's older ranch-style homes, many built with pier-and-beam or slab foundations that predate modern waterproofing standards, are especially vulnerable. Plumbing in homes from earlier decades often runs through interior walls with minimal insulation, making freeze events during a rare Taylor County cold snap a legitimate burst-pipe risk. The combination of aging infrastructure and expansive soil means water damage here rarely stays where it started.

## Our Water Cleanup Process in Buffalo Gap

The first step on any job is containment and assessment — not just mopping up what's visible. Water wicks through sheetrock, insulation, and subfloor material in ways that a surface inspection misses. We use thermal imaging and calibrated moisture meters to map the full extent of saturation before a single piece of equipment is placed.

From there, the process moves through extraction, structural drying, and monitoring:

- **Extraction**: Truck-mounted and portable extraction units pull standing water and surface moisture from carpet, hardwood, and concrete.
- **Drying**: Commercial-grade air movers and refrigerant dehumidifiers are positioned according to the IICRC S500 standard, which accounts for room volume, material type, and ambient humidity — all relevant in Taylor County's variable conditions.
- **Monitoring**: We return on a set schedule to read moisture levels and adjust equipment until materials reach documented drying goals. Nothing is signed off on a visual guess.

For slab-on-grade homes common in the Buffalo Gap area, we pay particular attention to the seam between flooring and slab, where moisture can persist even after the surface feels dry underfoot.
