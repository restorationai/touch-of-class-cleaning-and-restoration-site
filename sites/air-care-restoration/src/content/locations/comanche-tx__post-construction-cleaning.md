## Why Comanche Properties Present Unique Post-Construction Cleaning Challenges

Comanche sits in the Cross Timbers region of central Texas, where the climate swings hard — dry, windy springs that carry red clay dust through any unsealed opening, followed by humid summer stretches that let that same dust cake onto surfaces and make it harder to remove. Homes and commercial buildings here often sit on pier-and-beam or slab foundations over the region's rocky, clay-heavy soil, which means construction crews track in a distinctive reddish grit that stains grout lines and embeds in carpet fibers if it isn't addressed quickly after the job wraps.

Comanche County also has a significant share of pre-1960 housing stock. Renovations to older homes frequently disturb original materials — plaster walls, original hardwood subfloors, cast-iron plumbing chases — that hold dust differently than modern construction. Plaster, in particular, creates an ultra-fine particulate when cut or patched that settles in layers and reactivates with foot traffic for days after work ends. Cleaning that environment requires more than a mop pass; it requires a sequenced approach that works from ceiling to floor and addresses air quality alongside surface cleanliness.

## Our Post-Construction and Specialty Cleaning Process in Comanche

Every post-construction clean begins with a walkthrough to assess what trades were involved, what materials were used, and what the handoff condition actually looks like — because "broom clean" means something different to a framing crew than it does to a homeowner about to move furniture in.

From there, the process follows a deliberate sequence:

**Rough debris removal** comes first — scrap lumber, packaging, cut tile, wire offcuts, and anything the crew left behind. This clears the field for detail work.

**Top-down dust removal** follows. Drywall compound dust and insulation fibers cling to ceiling fixtures, top plates, window frames, and cabinet interiors. HEPA-filtered vacuums pull that material out before any wet cleaning begins, so it isn't just redistributed.

**Surface cleaning and detail work** addresses adhesive residue, paint overspray, grout haze, caulk smears, and construction film on glass. The right solvent matters here — aggressive degreasers that work on tile can damage new wood trim, so product selection is surface-specific.

**Final air pass and HVAC inspection** closes the job. Construction dust loads HVAC filters fast, and in Comanche's windy spring season, a new build or renovation that sat open during framing can have significant particulate in the duct system. We check filter condition and advise on replacement timing before the system runs continuously.
