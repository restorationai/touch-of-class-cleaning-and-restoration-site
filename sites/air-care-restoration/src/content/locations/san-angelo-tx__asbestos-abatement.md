## Why San Angelo's Older Housing Stock Carries Elevated Asbestos Risk

Tom Green County's growth surged in the postwar decades, and a significant share of the residential and commercial construction from roughly 1945 through the late 1970s incorporated asbestos-containing materials as a matter of course. That era produced homes with vinyl asbestos tile on concrete slab floors, popcorn acoustic ceilings sprayed with chrysotile-bearing compound, and pipe wrap around cast-iron and galvanized plumbing. None of those materials are automatically dangerous while intact, but San Angelo's climate creates the conditions that break them down.

The region runs hot and dry for long stretches, then cycles through rapid temperature swings that cause building materials to expand and contract repeatedly. That thermal stress, combined with the occasional severe hailstorm or West Texas wind event, can crack plaster ceilings, loosen floor tile adhesive, and compromise duct insulation that has been in place for fifty years. Once those materials are disturbed, fibers become airborne. Renovation work, even something as routine as installing a ceiling fan or pulling up old flooring, can trigger the same release if no one has confirmed what is underneath.

## Our Asbestos Abatement Process in San Angelo

The work starts with inspection and bulk sampling before any abatement begins. Samples are sent to an accredited laboratory for analysis, and no material is treated as asbestos-containing until testing confirms it. That distinction matters because not every popcorn ceiling or floor tile from the 1960s actually contains asbestos, and unnecessary abatement costs money and time.

When testing confirms the presence of asbestos-containing material, the abatement process follows a defined sequence. The affected area is isolated with negative air pressure containment using HEPA-filtered air scrubbers to prevent fiber migration to adjacent spaces. Technicians work in full personal protective equipment, wet the material to suppress fiber release during removal, and bag waste in double-sealed, labeled containers for transport to a licensed disposal facility. Texas Department of State Health Services regulations govern notification requirements, disposal manifests, and site clearance, and every project Air Care Restoration completes is documented to satisfy those requirements.

For popcorn ceiling asbestos removal specifically, the ceiling surface is thoroughly wetted before any scraping begins. Dry removal is not an acceptable method under current standards, and any contractor who skips that step is cutting corners that create liability for the property owner.

## Local Note

San Angelo sits in a region where slab-on-grade construction is common, and many homes built in the 1950s and 1960s used vinyl asbestos tile directly over the concrete slab as the finished floor. When homeowners install new flooring over the top of that tile, the old material is often left in place and undisturbed. That is sometimes an acceptable approach under Texas regulations, but it depends on the condition of the tile and the installation method for the new floor. Grinding, sanding, or nailing through intact VAT releases fibers. Before any flooring project in a pre-1980 San Angelo home, it is worth confirming what is underneath rather than assuming the tile is either safe or already gone.
