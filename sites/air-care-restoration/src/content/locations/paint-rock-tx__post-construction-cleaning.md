Paint Rock sits in the heart of Concho County, where the dry West Texas air and caliche-heavy soil create a construction environment unlike most of the state. When a renovation or new build wraps up here — whether it's a ranch property update along the Colorado River corridor or a commercial remodel on Main Street — what gets left behind isn't just drywall dust. It's fine silica particulate from cut caliche block, adhesive residue baked onto surfaces by the summer heat, and construction debris ground into every horizontal surface by the constant wind that rolls across the Edwards Plateau. Air Care Restoration handles the full post-construction and specialty cleaning process so that space is genuinely move-in ready, not just visually tidy.

## Why Paint Rock Properties Face Distinct Post-Construction Cleanup Challenges

Concho County's climate is the first variable any cleanup crew has to account for. Summer temperatures regularly push well past 100°F, which means adhesives, caulks, and sealants cure faster and bond more aggressively to surfaces than they would in a more temperate region. That baked-on residue requires specific solvents and dwell times — a quick wipe-down won't cut it. At the same time, the region's low humidity accelerates the spread of fine dust: drywall compound dust, insulation fibers, and silica from cut masonry migrate farther through HVAC systems and settle into ceiling fixtures, window tracks, and cabinet interiors that look untouched from across the room.

Older structures in and around Paint Rock often feature masonry construction — rock and adobe-style walls common to Central Texas ranch building traditions — that traps particulate differently than standard wood-frame drywall. Grout lines, mortar joints, and rough stone faces hold construction dust tenaciously. A post-construction clean on one of these properties requires different tools and techniques than a new suburban build would.

## Our Post-Construction and Specialty Cleaning Process in Paint Rock

Every job starts with a walk-through to assess what trades were on-site, what materials were used, and what the handoff condition actually looks like — not what the GC's punch list says. From there, the process moves in a deliberate sequence:

**Rough clean first.** Large debris, scrap material, and bulk dust are removed before any detail work begins. Attempting to detail-clean a surface that still has construction waste on it just redistributes the problem.

**HVAC and duct protection or cleaning.** In Paint Rock's wind-driven dust environment, HVAC systems frequently pull construction particulate into ductwork during the build. We inspect return air pathways and, where necessary, coordinate duct cleaning before the system runs continuously.

**Surface-by-surface detail clean.** Windows (inside glass, frames, tracks, and screens), cabinetry (interior boxes, door faces, hardware), lighting fixtures, plumbing fixtures, and flooring each get material-appropriate treatment. Paint overspray, grout haze, and sticker adhesive residue are addressed with targeted methods rather than one-size-fits-all chemicals.

**Final inspection pass.** A second walk-through under strong lighting catches what the cleaning pass missed — dried drips on baseboard edges, dust in electrical box knockouts, smudges on switch plates.

As an IICRC Certified Firm and EPA Lead-Safe Certified operation, the team follows documented protocols, which matters on any property where older painted surfaces may have been disturbed during renovation.

## Local Note

One thing that catches out-of-area crews in Concho County: the caliche dust generated during grading and foundation work has a fine, talc-like quality that settles invisibly on surfaces and then reactivates with any air movement. Property owners sometimes sign off on a visual inspection, move furniture in, and discover within a week that every horizontal surface is re-coated with white powder that migrated from inside wall cavities when the HVAC kicked on.
