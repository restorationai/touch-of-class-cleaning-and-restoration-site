## Why Sweetwater Properties Face Elevated Asbestos Risk

The West Texas construction boom of the mid-20th century left Sweetwater with a housing stock that skews older than many Texas cities. Homes built between the late 1940s and early 1980s — a period when asbestos was used in everything from attic insulation to joint compound — are common throughout town. The region's climate compounds the risk: brutal summer heat causes building materials to expand and contract repeatedly over decades, which can crack and crumble asbestos-containing products that might otherwise remain stable. Roofing materials and exterior siding take particular punishment from the sun and the seasonal hailstorms that roll through Nolan County, and damaged roofing often contains asbestos in older structures.

Popcorn ceilings are another frequent concern. That spray-applied texture was widely used through the 1970s and contains asbestos in a significant share of homes from that era. Sweetwater homeowners planning a remodel — scraping ceilings, opening walls, replacing old vinyl floor tiles — should have materials tested before any demolition begins.

## Our Asbestos Abatement Process in Sweetwater

Abatement is not the same as simply removing material. It is a sequenced, regulated process designed to prevent fiber release at every step.

**Inspection and sampling** come first. A trained technician collects samples from suspect materials — ceiling texture, floor tiles, pipe wrap, attic insulation — and submits them to an accredited laboratory. Results determine whether abatement is required and which materials are involved.

**Containment** is established before any disturbance. The work area is sealed with heavy-gauge polyethylene sheeting, and negative air pressure is maintained using HEPA-filtered air scrubbers so that fibers cannot migrate to adjacent rooms or the HVAC system.

**Wet methods and hand removal** minimize fiber release during extraction. Regulated asbestos-containing material is bagged in labeled, sealed containers on-site — never broken down or compacted in the open.

**Disposal** follows Texas Commission on Environmental Quality (TCEQ) requirements for asbestos waste. Material is transported to a licensed disposal facility; the chain of custody is documented throughout.

**Air clearance testing** is conducted after removal and before containment is dismantled. Fiber counts must fall below regulatory thresholds before the space is re-occupied.

Our team holds EPA Lead-Safe certification and is OSHA trained, which matters on older West Texas properties where lead paint and asbestos frequently appear together in the same structure.
