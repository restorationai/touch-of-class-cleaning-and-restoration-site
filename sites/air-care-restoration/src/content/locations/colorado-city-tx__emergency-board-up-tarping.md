## Why Colorado City Properties Are Vulnerable After Storms and Fires

Mitchell County sits in a corridor where severe weather patterns are both frequent and punishing. The region's semi-arid climate means long dry stretches punctuated by intense, fast-moving storm cells — the kind that produce damaging straight-line winds, large hail, and occasional tornadoes with little warning. Roofing materials already stressed by years of UV exposure and temperature swings from freezing winters to 100°F summers are particularly susceptible to sudden failure when a storm hits.

Colorado City's housing stock leans heavily toward older construction — many homes were built during the mid-20th century oil-field boom, and those structures often have original wood-frame rooflines, aging fascia, and single-pane windows that don't absorb impact the way modern materials do. When a window shatters or a section of decking lifts, the interior is immediately exposed. Fire damage presents the same urgency: a structure that has experienced even a contained room fire is left with compromised windows, door frames, and sometimes roof sections that need immediate closure to prevent weather intrusion and unauthorized entry.

## Our Emergency Board-Up and Tarping Process in Colorado City

When you call (325) 339-8723, the process starts before the crew leaves Abilene. We ask targeted questions — type of opening, approximate size, roof pitch if known, whether there's active fire department or utility activity on site — so the truck arrives with the right materials loaded.

On arrival, the crew assesses every breach point, not just the obvious one. A storm that blows out a front window often damages soffit vents and roof flashing at the same time. Plywood board-up follows IICRC and industry-standard sizing protocols — panels are cut, fitted, and secured to protect the structural opening without causing additional damage to the surrounding frame. For fire-damaged structures, we coordinate with the fire marshal's release before entering. Board-up on a fire loss also means addressing smoke-odor containment — sealed panels slow the migration of smoke residue into unaffected areas of the home.

Most homeowner policies in Texas cover emergency protective measures — board-up and tarping are typically classified as "reasonable steps to prevent further damage" and are reimbursable under the same claim as the underlying loss.
