Abilene's construction season runs hard through spring and into the scorching West Texas summer, and when a build or renovation wraps up, what's left behind is rarely just sawdust. Fine caliche dust — the pale, chalky soil that defines Taylor County — works its way into every gap during framing and drywall, then bakes onto surfaces once the HVAC kicks on. Air Care Restoration is based here in Abilene, and post-construction and specialty cleaning is one of the most detail-intensive services we provide, precisely because of what this climate and this soil do to a freshly finished space.

## Why Abilene Construction Sites Leave a Particular Kind of Mess

Building in West Texas means building in wind. The same gusts that roll across the open range west of town push fine particulate through every unsealed opening on an active job site. By the time the last subcontractor walks out, drywall compound dust has settled on top of caliche grit on top of caulk overspray — and that layered residue behaves differently than the post-construction debris you'd find in a more humid climate. In drier conditions, fine particles stay airborne longer and resettle repeatedly; a single pass with a mop or shop vac won't capture it. Surfaces that look clean in dim light reveal a gray haze the moment afternoon sun comes through the windows.

Abilene also sees a significant volume of renovation work on mid-century residential stock — homes built in the 1950s through 1970s with original plaster-and-lath interior walls that have since been partially drywalled over. Disturbing those walls during a kitchen or bathroom remodel can release decades of embedded dust and, in some cases, older insulation material. That changes the cleaning sequence and the equipment required.

## Our Post-Construction Cleaning Process in Abilene

Every post-construction clean starts with a walk-through to identify what trades were on site and what they left behind. Rough construction debris — cut lumber ends, broken tile, packaging — is staged for disposal before any cleaning begins. From there the work moves top-to-bottom and dry-to-wet:

**Dry phase:** HEPA-filtered vacuuming of all ceiling fixtures, vents, window sills, cabinet interiors, and baseboards before any liquid is introduced. Skipping this step just pushes fine dust into wet surfaces and creates a paste that's harder to remove.

**Detail phase:** Window tracks, door hardware, outlet covers, and grout lines get hand-cleaned individually. Caliche dust packs into grout and window channels in a way that a mop pass won't address.

**Final surface phase:** Hard floors, countertops, and fixtures are cleaned with appropriate solutions for the finish type — important on new hardwood or polished concrete where the wrong product can dull the surface the contractor just spent days perfecting.

**Air quality pass:** Because West Texas dust stays suspended, we run air scrubbers with HEPA filtration during and after the cleaning to pull particulate out of the air column, not just off the surfaces.

For specialty cleaning scopes — post-renovation deep cleans, move-in/move-out cleans on commercial properties, or cleaning after hoarding or deferred-maintenance situations — the process is scoped individually based on what the space actually needs.

## Local Note

One thing that catches out-of-town cleaning crews working in Abilene is the HVAC ductwork. Many homes and light commercial buildings here run evaporative coolers alongside or instead of refrigerated air, and those systems pull unfiltered outside air directly into the structure during the build. By the time construction wraps, the duct interior can carry a significant load of construction dust and caliche. A post-construction clean that doesn't include a duct inspection — and cleaning if warranted — leaves a source of recontamination that will keep settling onto freshly cleaned surfaces for weeks. We flag this during the walk-through so the property owner can make an informed decision before we start.
