## Why Early Properties Present Unique Renovation Challenges

Brown County's expansive clay soils are notorious for swelling after rain and contracting during drought, and Early's residential neighborhoods feel that movement in concrete slabs, door frames, and tile grout lines. Homes built in the post-war decades — many of which still stand throughout Early — often have pier-and-beam foundations that have shifted over time, original cast-iron drain lines that corrode from the inside out, and single-pane windows that no longer meet modern energy code. Before a remodel can move forward, a thorough assessment of what's behind the walls and under the floors is essential. Skipping that step on an older Early home is how a bathroom tile project turns into a subfloor replacement.

The region also sees hail seasons that can compromise rooflines and attic sheathing, and the occasional ice storm that stresses older plumbing. Renovation scopes in Early frequently expand once a crew opens up a wall — not because of poor planning, but because the building stock reflects decades of deferred maintenance and incremental repairs done by multiple hands over the years.
