West Texas storms don't announce themselves politely. In Tye, the same flat, open terrain that makes the Big Country feel endless also gives severe weather an unobstructed runway — hail the size of golf balls, straight-line winds that strip shingles in seconds, and the occasional tornado that can drop without much warning from a fast-moving supercell. When a storm tears through and leaves your property exposed, Air Care Restoration responds around the clock from our Abilene base to assess the damage, secure the structure, and start the restoration process before secondary damage compounds the loss.

## Why Tye Properties See Significant Storm Damage

Tye sits in Taylor County at the western edge of Tornado Alley, where the atmospheric conditions that breed severe convective storms are a recurring seasonal reality — not a once-a-decade anomaly. Spring and early summer bring the highest risk, but late-season cold fronts can push damaging wind events well into November.

The housing stock in the Tye area leans toward single-story construction — ranch-style homes and manufactured or HUD-code housing that, while practical for the climate, carries specific vulnerabilities. Low-pitched rooflines common in this region offer less wind resistance than steeper-pitched designs, meaning even moderate hail events can leave granule loss and micro-fractures that won't show up as an obvious leak until the next rain. Manufactured homes are particularly susceptible to wind uplift along roof-to-wall connections, and that kind of structural movement can let water into wall cavities that are difficult to dry without opening the assembly.

The caliche-heavy soils across Taylor County drain poorly after heavy rain events, which means water that pools around a foundation after a storm doesn't dissipate quickly. That standing water creates hydrostatic pressure against slab edges and can wick into interior flooring — a problem that looks minor on the surface but can harbor moisture for weeks if not addressed with proper extraction and drying equipment.

## Our Storm Damage Restoration Process in Tye

Every storm loss is different, but the sequence matters. When we arrive at a Tye property, the first priority is a thorough damage assessment — roof, exterior walls, windows, and any penetrations where water has already entered. We document everything photographically before anything is moved or removed, which matters enormously for the insurance claim process.

If the roof is compromised, we install emergency tarping to stop ongoing water intrusion. Broken windows and doors get boarded. Once the envelope is secured, we move inside to identify all wet materials using moisture meters and thermal imaging. Water that enters through storm damage rarely stays where it lands — it follows wall cavities, runs along subfloor sheathing, and collects in places you wouldn't think to look.

Drying in this part of West Texas has a small advantage: the ambient humidity is generally low, which helps commercial desiccant and refrigerant dehumidifiers work efficiently. We set drying systems calibrated to the specific materials involved — drywall dries differently than OSB sheathing, and manufactured home wall panels behave differently than site-built framing. Daily moisture readings track progress and determine when structural materials have returned to acceptable levels before any rebuild work begins.

For debris removal — downed tree limbs, displaced roofing material, broken glass — we handle the site cleanup as part of the restoration scope, not as a separate call.

Storm damage claims in Texas are governed by the Texas Department of Insurance's prompt-payment rules, which set timelines for how quickly carriers must acknowledge and act on a claim. Knowing those timelines helps you hold your adjuster accountable.
