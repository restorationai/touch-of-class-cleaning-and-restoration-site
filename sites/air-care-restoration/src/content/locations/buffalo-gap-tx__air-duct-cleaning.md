Buffalo Gap sits in a narrow band of Taylor County where the wind rarely stops and the soil is a fine caliche-and-clay mix that turns to dust the moment it dries out. That dust — along with cotton-gin chaff, cedar pollen, and the particulate that blows off the open rangeland surrounding the town — finds its way into every gap in a home's envelope, and eventually into the ductwork. If your HVAC system has been running through a few West Texas summers without a professional cleaning, the inside of those ducts likely tells the story.

## Why Buffalo Gap Properties See Duct Buildup Faster Than Most

The climate here is not kind to air quality. Buffalo Gap experiences hot, dry summers that drive HVAC systems to run long cycles, pulling large volumes of outdoor air through the return side of the system. When that air carries caliche dust, pollen, and the occasional insect debris common to rural Taylor County properties, the buildup inside supply and return ducts accelerates noticeably compared to homes in more sheltered urban environments.

Many of the homes in and around Buffalo Gap are single-story ranch-style construction, often with ductwork running through unconditioned attic spaces. In summer, attic temperatures in this part of Texas can push well past 140°F, which accelerates the off-gassing of older duct insulation and can cause flex duct to sag and trap debris in low spots. If your home was built before the widespread adoption of sealed, insulated flex duct — or if it has been through any renovation that left duct joints disturbed — those sections become collection points for dust, mold spores, and allergens.

## Our Air Duct Cleaning Process in Buffalo Gap

We begin every job with a camera inspection of the main trunk lines and as many branch runs as access allows. This step matters in Buffalo Gap homes because the combination of attic heat and caliche dust often creates a compacted layer of debris that a simple brush pass will not fully dislodge. We use negative-pressure source removal — a high-powered vacuum collection unit attached at the main trunk while agitation tools work through each supply and return register — so that loosened material is captured rather than redistributed into the living space.

After mechanical cleaning, we inspect for microbial growth. The humidity spikes that follow West Texas thunderstorms can introduce enough moisture into a duct system — especially one with any insulation gaps — to support mold colonization. Where growth is present, we apply an EPA-registered antimicrobial treatment to interior duct surfaces. We also clean and inspect the air handler cabinet, blower wheel, and evaporator coil area, because a clean duct connected to a fouled air handler is only half the job.

Dryer vent cleaning is available as an add-on and is worth scheduling at the same visit. The lint loads in a dryer vent serving a working ranch household or a home with livestock-adjacent laundry are considerably heavier than the national average, and the fire risk scales with that load.

## Local Note

Something that comes up specifically in Buffalo Gap and the surrounding rural Taylor County corridor: homes on well water with high mineral content sometimes have humidifier bypass dampers or whole-house humidifiers installed on the air handler to compensate for the dry air. Those components — if they have not been serviced — can introduce calcium scale and standing water directly into the duct system, creating a moisture source that feeds mold growth even during drought conditions. If your home has a humidifier attached to the HVAC, tell us before the appointment so we can inspect that component as part of the cleaning.
