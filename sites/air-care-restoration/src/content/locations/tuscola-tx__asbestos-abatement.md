## Why Tuscola Properties See Asbestos Concerns

Taylor County's building boom in the 1950s through the 1970s coincided with peak asbestos use in American construction. Many homes and farm structures in and around Tuscola were built during that window, meaning asbestos-containing materials were essentially standard at the time. Popcorn ceilings, vinyl floor tiles in 9-inch and 12-inch squares, duct insulation on older HVAC systems, and the textured joint compound around ceiling fixtures are the most common sources we encounter in this era of construction.

The West Texas climate adds a layer of concern specific to this region. Extreme temperature swings between summer highs and winter nights cause building materials to expand and contract repeatedly over decades. That thermal cycling can crack and loosen materials that were once intact and stable, turning a previously non-friable asbestos product into a friable one that releases fibers into living spaces. A ceiling tile that looked fine five years ago may not be today.

## Our Asbestos Abatement Process in Tuscola

Every project begins with a thorough inspection and bulk sampling of suspected materials. Samples are sent to an accredited laboratory for analysis before any abatement work is scheduled. This step is not optional, and any contractor who skips it is not following proper protocol.

Once a material tests positive, the work area is isolated with negative air pressure containment using polyethylene sheeting and HEPA-filtered air scrubbers. Our OSHA-trained technicians wear full personal protective equipment throughout the removal process. Regulated materials are wetted to suppress fiber release, carefully removed, double-bagged in labeled asbestos waste bags, and transported to an approved disposal facility in accordance with Texas Commission on Environmental Quality requirements.

After removal, a final visual inspection and air clearance sampling confirm that fiber levels have returned to acceptable limits before containment is torn down. You receive documentation of the lab results, the scope of work completed, and the disposal manifest, which is useful for insurance purposes and for any future buyer's due diligence.

## Tuscola Insurance Coordination

Asbestos abatement is sometimes covered under homeowner's policies when the disturbance is tied to a covered loss, such as storm damage to a roof or a burst pipe that required tearing into older flooring. ## Local Note

In Taylor County's older agricultural properties, asbestos-containing roofing materials, particularly corrugated transite panels, were widely used on outbuildings, equipment sheds, and storage structures well into the 1980s. These panels are durable and often still intact, but drilling, cutting, or pressure-washing them releases fibers. Property owners in the Tuscola area who are planning to demolish or reroof an older outbuilding should have those panels tested before any mechanical work begins. It is a step that is easy to overlook on a farm structure, and it is one of the more common sources of unintentional asbestos exposure in rural Taylor County.
