## Why Sweetwater Properties Are Especially Vulnerable After a Loss

Nolan County's climate is the defining factor here. Sweetwater averages more than its share of high-wind events — the same open plains geography that made it a national leader in wind energy generation also means there's little to slow a storm front before it hits a residential roof. Hail events in this part of West Texas can strip granules from asphalt shingles in minutes, and a compromised roof left open even overnight will absorb enough rain to turn a roofing claim into a full interior water-damage claim.

The city's housing stock adds another layer of complexity. A significant portion of Sweetwater's homes were built in the mid-twentieth century, when construction methods relied on wood-lath framing and older window configurations that don't match modern board-up panel sizing without custom cuts. Older wood-frame structures also tend to have wider eave overhangs and irregular rooflines that require more anchor points when tarping — a detail that matters when gusts are still running at 30-plus mph after a storm passes.

## Our Emergency Board-Up and Tarping Process in Sweetwater

When a call comes in, the first priority is a fast structural read — not every opening needs the same solution. A fire-damaged window frame that's still structurally sound gets a different treatment than one where the lintel has softened. For board-up work, we use OSB panels cut and fitted to the opening, secured with methods that won't cause additional damage to the surrounding frame. For roof tarping, we anchor to structural members — never just to decking — so the tarp holds through the next wind event rather than peeling off by morning.

Documentation runs parallel to the physical work. Every opening is photographed before and after closure, with measurements recorded. That documentation goes directly to the insurance adjuster and helps establish that no additional water or weather intrusion occurred after our crew secured the property. IICRC FSRT-certified technicians handle fire-related board-up work, which matters when smoke-affected framing is involved and the adjuster needs to know the crew understood what they were working around.

## Local Note

One thing that catches out-of-area crews in Sweetwater: the wind doesn't stop just because the storm does. After a major weather event, sustained winds of 20–30 mph are common for 12 to 24 hours as the front moves through. A tarp anchored only to roof decking — which is standard practice in calmer climates — will fail in those conditions. The correct approach here is to run straps to ridge-board or rafter tails and weight the perimeter, not just the field. It adds time to the job, but a tarp that blows off at 3 a.m. and lets rain into a freshly opened roof cavity turns a contained loss into a much larger one.
