## Why Robert Lee Properties See Water Damage Differently Than Most Texas Towns

Coke County's climate is a study in extremes. Summer temperatures routinely push past 100°F, and the region can swing from months of drought to intense, short-duration storms that dump several inches of rain faster than the hard-packed caliche soil can absorb it. That combination creates two distinct water damage patterns that show up repeatedly in Robert Lee homes and commercial buildings.

First, prolonged drought causes the expansive clay soils beneath many older Robert Lee foundations to shrink and shift. When rain finally arrives, those soils swell unevenly, stressing slab foundations and the plumbing embedded in them. A hairline crack in a slab-embedded supply line can run undetected for weeks, saturating the subfloor from below before it ever shows up as a wet spot on the surface.

Second, Robert Lee's housing stock includes a significant number of homes built in the mid-twentieth century — pier-and-beam construction with wood subfloors, original galvanized plumbing, and minimal vapor barriers. Galvanized pipe corrodes from the inside out; by the time a pinhole leak is visible, the surrounding framing may already be compromised. Moisture meters and thermal imaging cameras are essential here, not optional extras.

## Our Water Cleanup Process in Robert Lee

Every job starts with a thorough moisture mapping survey before a single piece of furniture moves. Technicians use penetrating and non-penetrating moisture meters alongside infrared imaging to locate water that has migrated beyond the obvious wet area — behind baseboards, inside wall assemblies, under cabinetry. In Robert Lee's older homes, water commonly travels farther than expected because original plaster and adobe-influenced construction materials wick moisture laterally rather than containing it.

Once the scope is mapped, standing water is extracted using truck-mounted or portable extraction units, depending on access. Structural drying follows: industrial air movers and low-grain refrigerant dehumidifiers are positioned to create a controlled drying chamber. Drying targets are set using the IICRC S500 standard as the benchmark, and daily moisture readings document progress. Nothing is closed up until readings confirm the structure has returned to acceptable moisture levels for the local climate baseline.

When affected materials — drywall, insulation, subfloor sections — cannot be dried in place within a safe timeline, controlled demolition removes only what is necessary. This matters in Robert Lee, where sourcing replacement materials can involve a drive to San Angelo or Abilene; limiting unnecessary tear-out keeps the project scope realistic.
