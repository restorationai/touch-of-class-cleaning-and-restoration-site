## Why Roby Properties See Sewage Backup Issues

Roby sits in a region where the soil composition shifts between sandy loam and tighter clay layers depending on how close you are to the Double Mountain Fork of the Brazos drainage basin. That clay content is the quiet culprit behind many septic failures: during dry stretches, the ground contracts and shifts, stressing lateral lines and tank connections. When rain finally arrives — often in short, intense bursts — saturated soil can't absorb the surge, and septic systems that were borderline adequate suddenly back up into the home.

The housing stock in Roby skews older, with many homes built in the mid-twentieth century when septic design standards and sewer line materials were very different from today. Cast-iron and Orangeburg pipe — common in homes of that era — corrode, collapse, and root-infiltrate in ways that PVC simply doesn't. A slow drain that's been "manageable" for years can become a full sewage overflow in a single afternoon.

Municipal sewer connections in smaller Fisher County communities like Roby also tend to run on infrastructure that hasn't been replaced at the same pace as larger metro systems, meaning tree-root intrusion and joint separation are recurring issues rather than rare events.

## Our Sewage Cleanup and Sanitization Process in Roby

Raw sewage — Category 3 water in the IICRC classification system — carries bacteria, viruses, and parasites that make standard water damage cleanup protocols insufficient. The process here is more involved:

**Containment first.** Before any extraction begins, the affected area is isolated to prevent cross-contamination to unaffected rooms. In Roby homes with open floor plans or older construction where subflooring is continuous across large areas, this step is especially important.

**Extraction and removal.** Standing sewage is extracted using truck-mounted or portable units rated for contaminated water. Porous materials — carpet, pad, drywall below the flood line, insulation — are removed and bagged for proper disposal. There is no "dry it in place" option with Category 3 contamination.

**Structural disinfection.** Hard surfaces, framing, and concrete are treated with EPA-registered disinfectants. Our OSHA-trained technicians work in appropriate PPE throughout this phase. Multiple applications are typically required to reach the contact time needed for effective pathogen reduction.

**Drying and verification.** Once contaminated materials are out and surfaces are disinfected, the drying phase begins using commercial dehumidifiers and air movers. Moisture readings are logged daily until the structure returns to pre-loss moisture baselines — documentation that matters for both your insurance claim and your peace of mind.
