## Why Robert Lee Properties See Sewage Backup Issues

Robert Lee's housing stock leans heavily toward ranch-style homes and older pier-and-beam construction built through the mid-twentieth century. Many of these homes were plumbed with cast iron or Orangeburg sewer lines that have long since passed their useful life. Orangeburg pipe — a compressed tar-and-wood-fiber product common in postwar rural Texas construction — softens and collapses over time, and when it fails, it fails completely. A slow drain one week becomes a full sewage backup the next.

Septic systems are also common in and around Robert Lee, particularly on larger lots and rural parcels outside the city's utility service area. Septic tanks that haven't been pumped on schedule, or that sit in the heavy clay-and-caliche mix typical of this part of West Texas, can develop drain-field saturation problems that push effluent back toward the structure. During and after the region's sporadic but intense rainfall events, that risk spikes sharply — the ground simply can't absorb water fast enough, and the pressure transfers into the system.

## Our Sewage Cleanup and Sanitization Process in Robert Lee

Raw sewage — whether from a municipal sewer line backup or a septic overflow — is classified as Category 3 water, the most contaminated classification in the IICRC S500 standard. That means the cleanup protocol goes well beyond extraction. Here's what the process looks like on a typical Robert Lee job:

**Containment first.** Sewage-affected areas are isolated to prevent cross-contamination to unaffected rooms. In pier-and-beam homes common in this region, that includes checking the subfloor cavity, where effluent can migrate along joists before it's visible from above.

**Extraction and removal.** Standing sewage is extracted with truck-mounted equipment. Porous materials — carpet, pad, drywall, insulation, and in some cases subfloor sheathing — that have contacted Category 3 water are removed and bagged for disposal. There is no safe way to dry and restore these materials in place.

**Antimicrobial treatment.** After removal, all affected structural surfaces are treated with EPA-registered antimicrobial agents. This step addresses the bacteria, viruses, and pathogens present in raw sewage and is what separates professional sanitization from a mop-and-bleach approach.

**Drying and verification.** Structural drying follows, with moisture readings documented throughout. In West Texas's low-humidity climate, drying times are often shorter than in more humid regions — but that same dry air can cause rapid surface evaporation that masks residual moisture in subfloor framing. We use thermal imaging and calibrated moisture meters, not surface readings alone.
