## Why Sweetwater Properties See Flood Damage

Much of Sweetwater's housing stock was built before modern grading and drainage standards were common practice, which means older slab foundations and crawlspaces sit closer to grade than you'd see in newer West Texas developments. Combine that with the region's caliche and clay-heavy soil, which swells when saturated and pushes water laterally instead of letting it drain straight down, and you get a pattern we see repeatedly: water finding its way in through slab cracks, weep holes, and low points around exterior doors rather than dramatic breaches. Add in a dry climate most of the year, and many homes here have baseboards, drywall, and subfloor that were never built to handle sustained moisture. Once that material gets wet, it holds the water longer than a home designed for a wetter climate would.

## Our Flood Damage Restoration Process in Sweetwater

Extraction comes first, using truck-mounted and portable pumps depending on how much standing water we're dealing with and whether power is still on to the affected area. From there we pull baseboards and, where needed, the bottom few inches of drywall to check what's happening inside the wall cavity, because surface-dry doesn't mean the framing or insulation behind it is dry. Moisture mapping with penetrating meters tells us which materials are salvageable and which aren't, and drying is set up per the IICRC S500 standard, with air movers and dehumidifiers sized to the affected square footage rather than a one-size-fits-all setup. In a region where humidity swings hard between seasons, we adjust drying targets to the outdoor conditions on the day of the job rather than a generic number, because West Texas air in July behaves very differently than it does in January.
