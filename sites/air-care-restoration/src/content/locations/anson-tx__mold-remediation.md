## Why Anson Properties See Mold Issues

Anson sits in a semi-arid climate, but that label can be misleading. Jones County receives concentrated rainfall events — often arriving fast and hard — and the clay-heavy soils common to this part of the Rolling Plains don't drain quickly. Water pools against foundations, seeps under slab edges, and wicks into pier-and-beam structures before it has any chance to evaporate. Once inside, it meets warm interior air and organic building materials, and mold follows.

The housing stock in Anson adds another layer of risk. Many homes in town were built in the mid-twentieth century, when vapor barriers were minimal or absent entirely and insulation was packed tight against exterior walls without any provision for drying. That construction style traps moisture effectively. A slow roof leak or a single plumbing failure can saturate wall cavities for weeks before a homeowner notices discoloration or an earthy, musty odor — by which point mold colonies are well established behind the surface.

## Our Mold Remediation Process in Anson

Every job starts with a thorough inspection using moisture meters and thermal imaging to locate moisture that isn't visible to the eye. Mold that reads dry at the surface can be feeding on saturated framing or insulation just inches deeper. Once the full scope is mapped, the affected area is sealed off with physical containment barriers and negative air pressure — this prevents spores from migrating to unaffected rooms during the removal work.

Contaminated materials are removed, bagged, and disposed of according to industry standards. Structural surfaces are then treated and HEPA-vacuumed. Air scrubbers with HEPA filtration run continuously throughout the project to capture airborne particulates. Before containment comes down, the area is dried to target moisture levels verified by instrument readings — not by feel or visual inspection alone. The goal is to eliminate the moisture condition that allowed mold to grow, not just to address what's visible on the surface.

Homes in Anson built on pier-and-beam foundations — common in older blocks near the town center — have crawl spaces that act as humidity reservoirs during and after rain events.
