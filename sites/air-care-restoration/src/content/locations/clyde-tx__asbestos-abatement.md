## Why Clyde Properties See Asbestos Concerns

Construction in Callahan County tracked national patterns closely through the 1950s, 1960s, and into the early 1980s. Asbestos was cheap, fire-resistant, and widely available, so it ended up in a surprising range of materials: the popcorn texture sprayed on bedroom ceilings, the 9x9 vinyl floor tiles in kitchens and hallways, the joint compound used on drywall seams, and the wrap around HVAC ducts and water heaters. Homes built before 1980 are the most likely candidates, but some products containing asbestos remained in commercial use into the mid-1980s.

Clyde's climate adds a layer of urgency. The region sees dramatic temperature swings, dry summers, and occasional severe freeze events. That thermal cycling causes building materials to expand and contract repeatedly over decades, and it is exactly that kind of mechanical stress that can cause asbestos-containing materials to become friable, meaning they crumble and release fibers into the air. A ceiling texture that looked intact for forty years may begin shedding after a hard freeze or a roof leak that went unaddressed through a dry summer.

## Our Asbestos Abatement Process in Clyde

Every project begins with a thorough inspection and, where materials are suspect, bulk sampling sent to an accredited laboratory. Testing results determine the scope before any removal work begins. This sequence matters because Texas regulations govern asbestos abatement work, and proper documentation protects you as a property owner throughout the process.

Once testing confirms asbestos-containing material, the work area is isolated with negative air pressure containment. Certified technicians wet the material before removal to suppress fiber release, then bag and seal waste according to Texas Commission on Environmental Quality disposal requirements. Air monitoring runs throughout the project, and a final clearance test confirms the space is safe before containment is removed. For popcorn ceiling asbestos removal specifically, the process includes careful surface wetting and hand-scraping rather than dry sanding, which would aerosolize fibers into the living space.

The Air Care Restoration team holds OSHA training and EPA Lead-Safe certification, and operates under Texas Mold Remediation Contractor License MRC2262. Because asbestos and lead-based paint frequently coexist in pre-1980 homes, having both credentials under one contractor simplifies coordination on older Clyde properties.

## Clyde Insurance and Documentation

Homeowner policies vary widely on asbestos abatement coverage. Some carriers cover removal when asbestos-containing material is disturbed by a covered loss, such as a roof breach or a pipe failure that damages insulation. Others treat abatement as a maintenance exclusion. Regardless of how your policy reads, the documentation Air Care Restoration produces, lab reports, containment photos, air monitoring data, and disposal manifests, gives your adjuster the complete picture needed to evaluate the claim. Thorough paperwork also protects you if the property changes hands and a future buyer's inspector raises questions about prior abatement work.
