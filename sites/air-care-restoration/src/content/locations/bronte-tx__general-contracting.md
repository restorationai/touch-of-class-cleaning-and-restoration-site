## Why Bronte Homes Present Unique Renovation Challenges

Coke County's housing stock leans heavily on construction from the mid-twentieth century, when pier-and-beam foundations were common and interior walls were often finished with plaster over wood lath rather than the drywall used today. That distinction matters the moment you open a wall for a kitchen remodel or a bathroom remodel: plaster substrates require different fastening schedules, different moisture management during construction, and different finishing sequences than a modern drywall renovation. Cutting corners on any of those steps produces cracks within a season or two as the structure moves with the heat.

The West Texas climate adds another layer. Summers in this part of the state push ambient temperatures high enough that adhesives, grouts, and caulks cure faster than the manufacturer's standard conditions assume — which means scheduling finish work for early morning and protecting fresh tile work from direct afternoon sun isn't optional, it's just how the job gets done correctly out here.

## Bronte Insurance Coordination for Post-Damage Rebuilds

When a renovation follows a covered loss — fire damage, water damage, a weather event — the scope of work has to satisfy both the homeowner's vision and the insurance carrier's documentation requirements. That means line-item estimates formatted to adjuster standards, photo documentation of demolished materials before disposal, and clear separation between covered restoration work and any elective upgrades the homeowner wants to fold into the project. Carriers vary in how they handle betterment, and getting that conversation right at the estimate stage prevents disputes at the invoice stage.
