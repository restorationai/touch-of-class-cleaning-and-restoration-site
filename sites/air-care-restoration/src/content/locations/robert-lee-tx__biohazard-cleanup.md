## Why Robert Lee Properties Present Unique Biohazard Considerations

Coke County's housing stock skews older, and many homes in and around Robert Lee were built in an era when subflooring, wall cavities, and HVAC systems weren't sealed the way modern construction standards require. That matters in biohazard remediation because porous materials — unfinished concrete, aged hardwood, older drywall compound — can absorb biological material well below the visible surface. A cleanup that looks complete at the surface level may leave contamination behind in the substrate if the crew isn't using the right testing protocols.

The region's arid climate adds a layer of complexity that runs counter to what most people expect. Low humidity in the Concho Valley area accelerates surface drying, which can mislead an untrained eye into thinking remediation is complete when biological residue has simply desiccated rather than been removed. Proper biohazard remediation requires physical removal and verified decontamination — not just drying — and that distinction matters in West Texas conditions.

## Our Biohazard Cleanup Process in Robert Lee

Every job begins with a contained assessment. Our OSHA-trained technicians arrive in unmarked vehicles when requested, establish a containment perimeter, and document the affected area before any removal begins. That documentation serves two purposes: it protects your insurance claim, and it ensures nothing is missed during the remediation itself.

Removal follows established protocols for the specific material type — whether that involves blood and bodily fluids, sharps and needle cleanup, or other infectious material. We don't subcontract that disposal; it stays in our chain of custody from your property to licensed disposal.

After physical removal, affected surfaces are treated with hospital-grade disinfectants rated for the relevant pathogens. We then conduct ATP testing — a rapid surface-contact test that detects biological residue invisible to the eye — before we consider a space cleared. The job isn't done because it looks done.
