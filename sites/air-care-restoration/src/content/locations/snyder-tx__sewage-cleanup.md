## Why Snyder Properties Experience Sewage Backups

The same caliche and clay-rich soils that define West Texas make Scurry County's underground infrastructure work harder than it would in sandier terrain. Soil that expands when wet and contracts in drought cycles puts lateral sewer lines under constant stress, particularly in older residential neighborhoods where cast-iron or clay-tile pipe is still in service. Prolonged dry spells — common across this part of the Permian Basin region — cause the ground to shrink and shift, which can crack or offset sewer laterals at the joints. Then when rain does arrive, roots that have worked their way into those cracks get flushed deeper, and the backup happens fast.

Septic systems on properties outside Snyder's municipal sewer grid face a related problem: the same dense soil that makes installation difficult also limits the drain field's ability to percolate effluent away from the tank. An oversaturated drain field during a wet stretch can force sewage back toward the home just as surely as a blocked main line.

## Our Sewage Cleanup and Sanitization Process in Snyder

Category 3 water — what the IICRC classifies raw sewage as — carries pathogens that survive on porous surfaces long after the visible waste is gone. The cleanup process has to address both the extraction and the microbial contamination left behind.

When the crew arrives, the first priority is containment: isolating the affected area to prevent cross-contamination to adjacent rooms. Standing sewage is extracted using truck-mounted or portable units, depending on access to the structure. All porous materials that have absorbed Category 3 water — drywall, insulation, carpet, pad — are documented, removed, and disposed of according to applicable guidelines. What stays gets treated with EPA-registered antimicrobial agents applied to hard surfaces, subfloor, and framing.

Drying follows using commercial-grade dehumidifiers and air movers calibrated to the ambient conditions. West Texas air is often dry, which can accelerate surface drying — but that same low humidity can mask residual moisture in slab penetrations and wall cavities where a moisture meter is the only reliable indicator. The team uses thermal imaging and pin-type meters to confirm structural drying before any rebuild begins.
