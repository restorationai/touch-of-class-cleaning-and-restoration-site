## Why Coleman Properties Present Unique Biohazard Challenges

Coleman sits in the heart of Coleman County on the Rolling Plains, where the climate swings between dry summer heat and cold, occasionally wet winters. That heat matters in biohazard situations: warm temperatures accelerate biological breakdown and odor penetration into porous surfaces. In many of Coleman's older homes — the kind of wood-frame construction common throughout this part of Central West Texas — subflooring, wall cavities, and plaster or older drywall can absorb contaminants faster than you'd expect, and hold them longer than the surface suggests.

Rural and semi-rural properties around Coleman also tend to have septic systems and older plumbing, which occasionally factor into the source or spread of a biohazard condition. Outbuildings, detached garages, and storage structures on agricultural parcels add another layer of complexity — these spaces often lack climate control, meaning biological material degrades more rapidly and requires faster containment.

## Our Biohazard Cleanup Process in Coleman

Every job begins with a private, no-pressure assessment. A trained technician walks the affected area, identifies the scope of contamination, and explains what the remediation will involve before any work begins. You won't be handed a clipboard and left to figure out the next step.

From there, the process follows a structured sequence:

- **Containment** — the affected space is isolated to prevent cross-contamination to adjacent rooms or HVAC pathways.
- **Personal protective equipment and regulated waste handling** — our crew works under OSHA-trained protocols, treating all biological material as regulated waste from the moment it's collected to the moment it leaves your property.
- **Surface removal and deep cleaning** — porous materials that cannot be safely decontaminated are removed and disposed of per applicable regulations. Non-porous surfaces are treated with EPA-registered disinfectants.
- **Odor control** — in West Texas heat, odor compounds can penetrate deeply. We address this with fogging and hydroxyl treatment rather than masking agents.
- **Documentation** — a written scope of work and photo documentation supports your insurance claim and gives you a clear record of what was done.

As an IICRC Certified Firm with OSHA-trained technicians and EPA Lead-Safe certification, the team is equipped to handle situations that involve older building materials — relevant in a county where a significant portion of the housing stock predates modern construction standards.
