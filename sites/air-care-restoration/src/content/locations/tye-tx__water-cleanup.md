## Why Tye Properties See Water Damage Issues

The soils around Tye are predominantly expansive clay — the kind that swells when wet and contracts when dry. That cycle puts constant stress on slab foundations and the plumbing embedded within them. Slab leaks are among the most common water loss sources in this part of Taylor County, and they are deceptive: the surface floor may feel barely damp while water has been migrating laterally under the concrete for days. By the time a homeowner notices a warm spot on tile or a spike in the water bill, the subfloor and lower wall cavities are often already saturated.

Tye's position along the I-20 corridor also means many structures here were built during the post-war and 1970s–1980s residential booms, when vapor barriers were minimal or absent. Older construction without modern moisture management means water that enters through a roof, window, or foundation has fewer barriers slowing its spread through wall assemblies and insulation.

Severe thunderstorms roll through the Abilene metro area with enough frequency that flash flooding is a real seasonal threat. When storm runoff overwhelms drainage and enters a crawl space or garage, standing water removal needs to happen fast — mold colonization can begin within 24 to 48 hours in warm, humid conditions, and Tye summers provide exactly that warmth.

## Our Water Cleanup Process in Tye

Every water cleanup job starts with moisture mapping. Technicians use thermal imaging cameras and calibrated moisture meters to trace where water has traveled beyond the visible wet zone — behind baseboards, inside wall cavities, under flooring. In slab-on-grade homes common to this area, that means checking the perimeter of the affected room and any adjacent rooms sharing the same slab section.

Once the scope is documented, standing water is extracted using truck-mounted and portable extraction units. Structural drying follows with industrial air movers and low-grain refrigerant dehumidifiers positioned according to the IICRC S500 standard for water damage restoration. Daily moisture readings track the drying progress and determine when materials have reached acceptable equilibrium — a number, not a guess.

If affected materials include drywall, insulation, or flooring that cannot be dried in place, controlled demolition removes only what is necessary. In homes with older plaster or paneling — a common find in mid-century Tye construction — the drying timeline extends because dense materials release moisture more slowly than modern drywall. Technicians adjust equipment placement and drying targets accordingly rather than applying a one-size schedule.
