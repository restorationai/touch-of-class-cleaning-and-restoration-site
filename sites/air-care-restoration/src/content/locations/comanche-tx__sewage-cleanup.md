Comanche sits in the rolling Cross Timbers country of west-central Texas, where the clay-heavy soils that dominate Comanche County swell and shift with every rain cycle. That ground movement puts steady lateral stress on older sewer laterals and septic lines — and when one of those lines fails, raw sewage doesn't just back up into a drain. It saturates subfloor cavities, wicks into wall framing, and leaves behind pathogens that no amount of mopping can address. ## Why Comanche Properties Face Elevated Sewage Backup Risk

The same expansive clay soils that make Comanche County farmland productive are hard on underground plumbing. Clay expands when wet and contracts during dry spells — a cycle that's especially pronounced during the region's periodic drought-and-deluge swings. Over time, that movement can offset pipe joints, crack older clay-tile sewer lines, and allow root intrusion from the live oaks and cedar elms common across the area. Homes built before the 1980s are particularly vulnerable because many still have the original vitrified clay laterals running from the house to the city main or to a septic tank.

Comanche also has a significant number of properties served by private septic systems rather than municipal sewer. When a septic tank reaches capacity, gets flooded by heavy rain, or has a failing drain field, the overflow path is usually back into the home. That type of Category 3 contamination — raw sewage — carries bacteria, viruses, and parasites that require a different response than a clean-water pipe burst.

## Our Sewage Cleanup and Sanitization Process in Comanche

When we arrive, the first priority is stopping ongoing exposure. We identify the source — whether that's a backed-up main line, a failed septic system, or a cracked lateral — and coordinate with a licensed plumber if the line itself needs repair before cleanup can begin.

From there, the process follows a specific sequence:

- **Containment and PPE setup** — sewage loss areas are isolated to prevent cross-contamination to unaffected rooms.
- **Bulk removal** — standing water and solid waste are extracted using truck-mounted and portable extraction equipment rated for contaminated water.
- **Structural material assessment** — porous materials like drywall, insulation, and carpet that have contacted Category 3 water are documented and removed. In older Comanche homes with tongue-and-groove subfloor, we probe carefully before assuming the surface read is accurate — that material holds moisture at the seams long after the top face appears dry.
- **Antimicrobial treatment** — affected framing, concrete, and hard surfaces are treated with EPA-registered disinfectants applied at the correct dwell times, not just sprayed and wiped.
- **Drying and verification** — commercial desiccant and refrigerant dehumidifiers, along with air movers, bring structural moisture levels back to pre-loss baselines. Moisture readings are logged throughout.

Because we are an IICRC Certified Firm with trained technicians, and our owner holds EPA Certified Renovator (Lead RRP) credentials, we can safely handle older homes where lead paint on trim or window casings may be disturbed during material removal. If suspect materials are present, we follow proper containment, removal, and disposal procedures — and if a separate licensed abatement contractor is required for other regulated materials such as asbestos, we will flag that during the walkthrough.

Sewage backup coverage is not automatic on most homeowner policies — it typically requires a separate endorsement.
