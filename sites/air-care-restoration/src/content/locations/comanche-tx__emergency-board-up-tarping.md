Comanche sits in the rolling Cross Timbers country of west-central Texas, where spring storm seasons can arrive fast and leave a roof or window exposed before a homeowner has time to make a single call. When a hailstorm peels back shingles on a 1940s craftsman near the courthouse square, or a kitchen fire blows out a window on a side-street rental, every hour the structure stays open to the elements adds to the final repair bill. Air Care Restoration responds around the clock to board up openings and tarp compromised roofs so the damage stops where it started.

## Why Comanche Properties See Board-Up and Tarping Emergencies

Comanche County sits squarely in a corridor that sees severe thunderstorms, large hail, and occasional straight-line wind events each spring and early summer. The town's older residential blocks — many built between the 1920s and 1960s — carry original wood-frame construction with single-pane windows and aging roof decks that are more vulnerable to impact damage than modern builds. When a hailstone punches through a skylight or a wind gust lifts a section of three-tab shingles, the roof deck underneath can absorb a significant amount of rain before the storm even passes. Fire damage is the other common trigger: a structure fire that vents through a window or burns through a soffit leaves the interior wide open to weather, vandalism, and secondary moisture intrusion the same night.

## Our Emergency Board-Up and Tarping Process in Comanche

When we arrive on site, the first priority is a quick exterior assessment — identifying every breach, from the obvious blown-out window to the less visible lifted flashing along a ridge line. For window and door openings, we cut OSB panels to fit and fasten them with structural screws rather than staples, which hold through wind and resist pry-off attempts. For roof damage, we use reinforced polyethylene tarps rated for the Texas sun and secure them with weighted battens and perimeter fastening rather than just running a few screws through the tarp field, which tears under sustained wind. Every opening gets photographed before and after boarding — documentation that matters when an insurance adjuster reviews the claim. Because we hold IICRC FSRT (Fire and Smoke) and IICRC WRT (Water) certifications, we can also flag secondary concerns — smoke odor migration, moisture already tracking into wall cavities — while we're on site, so nothing surprises you later in the restoration process.

## Comanche Insurance Coordination

Most emergency board-up and tarping work is a covered line item under homeowners and commercial property policies as a "protective measures" or "mitigation" expense. We photograph every opening before we board it, document the tarp placement with measurements, and provide a written scope of the emergency work — the paperwork an adjuster needs to approve reimbursement without a site revisit. Texas wind and hail claims are common enough that most carriers have a straightforward process, but having organized documentation from the first night makes the difference between a smooth claim and a protracted back-and-forth. We work directly with adjusters and can answer questions about the emergency scope on your behalf.

## Local Note

Comanche's older residential housing stock was built during an era when roof decks used skip sheathing — spaced boards rather than a continuous plywood or OSB layer — under composition shingles that were later added during re-roofing. When hail or wind lifts those shingles, the gaps in the original skip sheathing mean rain can enter the attic space through multiple small openings rather than one clean breach. A tarp that only covers the most visible damage may still leave water pathways open. When we work on homes in Comanche that show signs of that older deck construction, we extend tarp coverage well past the visible damage zone and use extra fastening points along the eaves to account for it.
