## Why Baird Properties See Asbestos Concerns

Callahan County's housing stock skews older. Baird served as a railroad town, and the residential neighborhoods that grew up around that economy were largely built before 1980, the period when asbestos use in construction materials was most widespread. Homes with original floor tile, intact duct wrap, or untouched popcorn ceilings are common throughout the city. The region's climate adds another layer of risk: the extreme temperature swings between scorching summers and hard winter freezes cause building materials to expand and contract repeatedly over decades. That thermal cycling can crack or crumble asbestos-containing materials that might otherwise stay intact, pushing them from a stable, non-friable state into a friable one where fibers can become airborne.

Roofing is another concern specific to this part of Texas. Older corrugated roofing panels and roofing mastics used across Callahan County frequently tested positive for asbestos in the era before modern substitutes became standard. Hail and high-wind events, which are a regular feature of West Texas weather, can physically damage those materials and trigger a need for abatement before any repair work begins.

## Our Asbestos Abatement Process in Baird

Every project starts with a thorough inspection and bulk material sampling before any removal begins. Suspect materials are collected following established protocols and sent to an accredited laboratory for analysis. Once results confirm the presence of asbestos-containing material, the work area is isolated with negative air pressure containment to prevent cross-contamination to the rest of the structure. Our OSHA-trained crew removes the identified materials using wet methods that suppress fiber release, then double-bags and labels all waste for transport to a licensed disposal facility.

After removal, air clearance testing confirms that fiber counts have returned to acceptable levels before containment is taken down. That step is not optional, and we do not skip it to save time. The full process, from initial inspection through clearance, follows the framework set by the EPA and Texas Commission on Environmental Quality for residential and light commercial abatement work.

## Local Note

One thing that catches some Baird homeowners off guard is the presence of asbestos in materials they would not immediately suspect, particularly the black mastic adhesive used beneath vinyl floor tiles installed through the 1970s. Even when the tiles themselves have been removed or covered over, that adhesive layer often remains on the concrete slab underneath. In West Texas homes where slab-on-grade construction is the norm, any flooring renovation that involves grinding or scraping the slab surface should include a mastic sample test before work begins. It is a small step that prevents a much larger problem.
