## Why Paint Rock Properties See Asbestos Concerns

Concho County's housing stock reflects a construction boom that ran roughly from the 1940s through the 1970s, when asbestos-containing materials were standard across the industry. Roofing shingles, vinyl floor tiles, duct insulation, and joint compound from that era all have a meaningful likelihood of containing asbestos. The dry, high-desert climate here is hard on building envelopes: thermal cycling between cold nights and intense summer heat causes materials to crack and crumble, which is exactly the condition that makes previously stable asbestos-containing materials friable and hazardous. A ceiling tile that held together for decades can become a source of airborne fibers once it starts to flake. Owners of older properties in Paint Rock should treat any renovation or repair as a potential asbestos event until testing says otherwise.

## Our Asbestos Abatement Process in Paint Rock

Every project begins with a thorough asbestos inspection before any demolition or disturbance work starts. Bulk samples are collected from suspect materials and sent to an accredited laboratory for analysis. That step is not optional and is not something to skip to save time. Once results confirm the presence of asbestos-containing material, the work area is isolated with negative air pressure containment using HEPA-filtered equipment, preventing fibers from migrating into adjacent rooms or the HVAC system.

Removal follows Texas Department of State Health Services regulations for asbestos abatement, which govern everything from worker protective equipment to the packaging and transport of waste to a permitted disposal facility. Air Care Restoration carries an EPA Lead-Safe certification and is OSHA trained, and the crew works under procedures that meet those regulatory requirements throughout. After removal, air clearance sampling confirms fiber levels have returned to acceptable limits before containment is broken down. That clearance step is what separates a compliant abatement from one that simply looks finished.

## Local Note

One thing worth knowing about older construction in this part of Concho County: many homes built in the 1950s and 1960s used a combination of asbestos-containing floor tiles over a concrete slab foundation, which was practical for the climate but creates a layered abatement challenge. The tiles themselves may contain asbestos, and the mastic adhesive used to bond them to the slab frequently does too. Treating only the visible tile without testing the adhesive layer is a common oversight that can leave a regulated material in place and create liability for the property owner. A proper inspection accounts for both layers before any flooring project moves forward.
