## Why Snyder Properties See Water Damage Issues

The West Texas climate creates a specific cycle that works against residential and commercial buildings here. Extended dry periods cause the shrink-swell clay soils common across Scurry County to pull away from foundations and utility penetrations. When seasonal thunderstorms arrive — sometimes dropping several inches in a matter of hours — that same soil can't absorb water fast enough, pushing it toward crawl spaces, slabs, and low-lying entry points. Flat rooflines common on ranch-style homes built across Snyder in the 1960s and 1970s compound the problem: ponding water finds every small gap in flashing or caulking.

Aging plumbing is another consistent factor. Homes built during Snyder's post-war growth era often still carry galvanized steel supply lines. Galvanized pipe corrodes from the inside out, narrowing over decades until a pinhole failure or a full joint separation sends water into wall cavities that may stay wet for days before anyone notices. Because the exterior of the pipe looks intact, these failures tend to be discovered late — after moisture has already begun migrating through insulation and framing.

## Our Water Damage Restoration Process in Snyder

The first step on any water loss is containment and assessment, not just mopping up what's visible. A calibrated moisture meter reads saturation levels through finished surfaces — tile, hardwood, drywall — so the crew maps exactly where water has traveled before any material is moved or removed. In Snyder's older homes, water behind plaster-and-lath walls behaves differently than in modern drywall construction: the lath holds moisture like a sponge even when the plaster face reads dry at the surface, so drying timelines here are calibrated accordingly.

Once the extent of the loss is mapped, standing water is removed with truck-mounted extraction equipment. Structural drying follows using commercial-grade desiccant and refrigerant dehumidifiers placed according to the room's cubic footage and the building's construction type. Air movers are positioned to create a controlled drying chamber rather than just blowing air around. Moisture readings are logged at each visit so the drying curve is documented — a record that matters both for insurance claims and for confirming the structure is genuinely dry before any reconstruction begins.

For losses involving older materials — original hardwood floors, plaster ceilings, or mid-century cabinetry — the crew evaluates what can be dried in place versus what needs to be removed. Saving original material is often the right call structurally and financially, but only when moisture levels confirm it's achievable.
