## Why Tye Properties Are Vulnerable to Water Damage

The shrink-swell clay soils common across Taylor County are a quiet driver of water intrusion that often goes unrecognized until damage is already significant. During dry spells, that clay contracts and pulls away from foundation slabs and pier footings, opening small gaps. When rain finally arrives — sometimes in volume — water channels straight through those gaps rather than draining away from the structure. Slab-on-grade homes, which make up a large share of Tye's residential stock, are particularly exposed because there is no crawl space to act as a buffer; moisture that breaches the perimeter migrates directly under living areas.

Tye's older housing also tends to feature galvanized steel supply lines and cast-iron drain lines that have been working hard for decades. Galvanized pipe corrodes from the inside out, narrowing over time until pressure spikes — from a water heater cycling, a washing machine filling, or a summer freeze event — cause a fitting to let go. That kind of failure behind a wall can saturate insulation and framing for hours before anyone notices the ceiling stain.

## Our Water Damage Restoration Process in Tye

Every job starts with a thorough moisture assessment using thermal imaging cameras and calibrated moisture meters. Surface readings can be deceiving — drywall may feel dry to the touch while the wood framing behind it registers well above safe thresholds. We map the full moisture boundary before any equipment is placed, so drying targets are based on what the structure actually holds, not what it looks like.

Once the scope is clear, water removal begins with truck-mounted extraction units capable of pulling standing water from carpet, hardwood, and subfloor assemblies quickly. After bulk extraction, we set a calculated array of commercial air movers and low-grain refrigerant dehumidifiers sized to the affected area. Drying logs are recorded daily so the process is documentable for your insurance carrier from day one. Structural components are monitored until they reach the dry standard specified in the IICRC S500 — not just until they feel acceptable.

If building materials are beyond recovery — saturated OSB subfloor, wicking drywall, compromised insulation — we handle controlled demolition and debris removal as part of the mitigation scope, keeping the site clean and ready for reconstruction.

## Tye Insurance Coordination

Most homeowner policies in Texas cover sudden and accidental water discharge — a burst pipe, an appliance failure, an HVAC condensate line that backs up. What they typically do not cover is gradual seepage or long-term leaks that a reasonable homeowner should have caught. The distinction matters because adjusters will look at the loss history and the condition of the affected materials when evaluating the claim.

We document the loss thoroughly from the moment we arrive: photographs of source and affected areas, moisture readings with timestamps, material inventories, and drying logs. That documentation travels with the claim and gives your adjuster a clear picture of the scope. We work with most major carriers and can communicate directly with your adjuster to keep the process moving.

## Local Note

One pattern we see in West Texas slab homes that surprises some homeowners: after a water loss soaks the subfloor area, the clay soil beneath the slab can actually retain moisture and slow the drying of the concrete from the underside — even after the interior materials are dry. Monitoring only the flooring surface will miss this. In Tye and the surrounding Taylor County area, we extend drying monitoring to include slab temperature and ambient readings near the perimeter, because a slab that stays cool and damp can re-wet flooring materials that were already reading dry at the surface.
