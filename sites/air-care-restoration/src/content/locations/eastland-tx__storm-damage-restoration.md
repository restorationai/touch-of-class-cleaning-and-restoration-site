## Why Eastland Properties See Storm Damage Differently

Eastland sits in the southern edge of Tornado Alley, where dry air from the Llano Estacado collides with Gulf moisture funneling north through the Concho Valley. That atmospheric setup produces hail events that can go from golf-ball to baseball size within a single storm cell, and straight-line winds that frequently exceed 70 mph without a tornado ever touching down. The result is a specific damage profile: impact punctures in asphalt shingles, cracked ridge caps, bent gutters, and — critically — small roof breaches that let water pour into the attic long before a homeowner realizes anything is wrong.

Eastland County's housing stock leans heavily on wood-frame construction built during the mid-twentieth-century oil-field boom. Older framing often uses dimensional lumber that was cut to full nominal size, which is denser and more resilient than modern engineered wood — but it also holds moisture longer once it gets wet. A roof breach that soaks that framing can take significantly more drying time than a comparable loss in newer construction, and it can hide saturation behind plaster or original-era lath that reads dry at the surface while staying wet inside the wall cavity.

## Our Storm Damage Restoration Process in Eastland

Every storm job starts with emergency stabilization, not paperwork. When the crew arrives, the first priority is stopping the water entry: tarping breached roofs, boarding broken windows, and removing standing water from interior spaces before it wicks into subfloor assemblies. We use thermal imaging and moisture meters — not just visual inspection — to map exactly where water has traveled, because storm-driven rain can travel horizontally through wall cavities in ways that defy gravity.

Once the structure is stabilized, the scope shifts to controlled drying. Industrial desiccant dehumidifiers and high-velocity air movers are positioned based on the moisture map, not placed generically. Readings are logged daily so drying progress is documented — documentation that matters later when your insurance adjuster reviews the claim. Structural repairs, including roof deck replacement, framing repairs, and interior finishes, follow only after moisture levels confirm the assembly is dry. Rushing that sequence is how mold problems develop weeks after a storm job is "finished."

As an IICRC Certified Firm with OSHA-trained technicians, the work follows the IICRC S500 and S700 standards for water and storm damage — the same frameworks insurance carriers use to evaluate whether a restoration was performed correctly.

## Local Note

One pattern that comes up repeatedly in Eastland-area storm work: older homes in this part of Eastland County often have unventilated or minimally ventilated attic spaces that were built before modern energy codes required balanced intake and exhaust. After a storm breaches the roof, that attic traps humidity at levels that can push mold colonization to begin within 48 to 72 hours — faster than the national average because the trapped heat accelerates spore activity. Identifying and addressing attic ventilation as part of the restoration scope, not as an afterthought, is the difference between a job that stays closed and one that generates a callback six weeks later.
