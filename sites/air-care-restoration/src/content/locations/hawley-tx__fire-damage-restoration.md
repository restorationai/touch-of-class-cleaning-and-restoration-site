## Why Hawley Properties Face Particular Fire Damage Challenges

Jones County's climate is defined by low relative humidity for much of the year, which means structural materials like wood framing and drywall are already moisture-depleted when a fire starts. Dry materials ignite faster and char more deeply than their counterparts in humid climates, so fire damage in Hawley homes tends to penetrate further into wall assemblies than a comparable fire might in East Texas. The region also sees frequent high winds, which can push combustion products into attic spaces and through soffit vents before the fire is even extinguished.

Hawley's housing stock includes a meaningful share of older single-story ranch homes built with wood-frame construction and original plaster or early drywall finishes. Those materials absorb smoke particulates differently than modern 5/8-inch drywall: the surface may look cleanable while the substrate beneath holds odor-causing compounds that resurface weeks later if not treated at the source. Identifying the actual depth of contamination requires thermal imaging and air sampling, not just a visual walk-through.

## Our Fire Damage Restoration Process in Hawley

The first step on any fire-damaged property is a thorough structural assessment before any cleaning begins. We document every affected surface with photos and moisture readings, then establish a scope of work that separates salvageable materials from those that need removal. This documentation also forms the foundation of your insurance claim.

Once the scope is set, the sequence moves through board-up and tarping if the structure has openings, debris removal, dry soot cleaning of unaffected surfaces, wet cleaning and chemical sponging of smoke-stained materials, and HEPA-filtered air scrubbing to pull particulates from the air column. Ductwork is inspected and cleaned separately because HVAC systems in West Texas homes run hard and can redistribute soot throughout the house if overlooked. Odor neutralization uses thermal fogging and hydroxyl generation rather than masking agents, targeting the same penetration pathways the smoke used to travel.

Our team holds IICRC FSRT (Fire and Smoke Restoration) certification, which means the process follows a defined technical standard rather than a general-contractor best guess.
