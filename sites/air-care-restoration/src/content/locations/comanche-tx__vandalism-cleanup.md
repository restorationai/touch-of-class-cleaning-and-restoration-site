Comanche sits at the edge of the Edwards Plateau in Comanche County, where dry summers, occasional hail, and a small-town commercial strip along Highway 16 create a particular kind of vulnerability to vandalism. Spray paint bites deeper into the porous limestone and older brick that shows up on so many downtown storefronts and historic homes here than it does on modern concrete block. When a property gets hit, the window for clean removal is short: West Texas sun bakes pigment into masonry fast, and what is removable on a Tuesday morning can be a permanent stain by Friday.

## Why Comanche Properties See Vandalism Damage Differently

Comanche's building stock skews older. The courthouse square and the residential blocks radiating off it include structures built in the early to mid-1900s, and those materials behave differently under chemical cleaners than modern surfaces do. Limestone facades, painted wood siding, and single-pane glass are common, and each one demands a different removal approach. Aggressive solvents that work fine on a poured-concrete retaining wall can strip the finish off painted wood or etch soft limestone permanently.

The climate adds pressure. Summer temperatures in Comanche regularly push past 100 degrees, which accelerates how quickly aerosol paint cures. Graffiti applied overnight can be chemically bonded to the surface by mid-morning. That same heat also means broken glass from a smashed window or door creates an immediate security and comfort problem: without boarding or temporary glazing, interior temperatures spike quickly and the property becomes an open invitation for follow-on damage.

## Our Vandalism Cleanup Process in Comanche

Every job starts with a surface assessment before any chemical or abrasive touches the property. Limestone, brick, stucco, wood, and painted metal each require a different product and pressure level. Using the wrong combination strips coatings, opens up pores in masonry, or pushes pigment deeper into the substrate instead of lifting it.

For graffiti, we work through a tiered approach: chemical dwell time first, low-pressure rinse, then a second pass if needed before any mechanical method is considered. On historic or soft masonry, we avoid high-pressure washing entirely and rely on poultice compounds that draw pigment out without abrasion.

Broken glass cleanup goes beyond sweeping visible shards. Fine glass migrates into carpet fibers, HVAC intakes, and wall cavities. We use HEPA-filtered vacuums and systematic grid sweeps rather than a single pass with a broom. Temporary board-up or security film follows immediately so the opening is secured before we leave.

If the vandalism involved interior intrusion, we document every affected surface for insurance purposes, photograph in sequence, and flag any secondary damage (water intrusion through a broken window, for example) before it compounds.

Vandalism is a covered peril under most standard homeowners and commercial property policies in Texas, but the documentation requirements matter. Carriers want photographs of the damage before any cleanup begins, a written scope of work, and itemized invoices. We handle all three. ## Local Note

Comanche County properties with native limestone exteriors, which appear on older homes and commercial buildings throughout the area, present a specific challenge that out-of-area contractors sometimes handle incorrectly. Limestone is calcium carbonate, and many standard graffiti removers are acidic. Acid on limestone does not just remove paint; it reacts with the stone itself, leaving a bleached, pitted scar that is more visible than the original graffiti. We use pH-neutral or alkaline formulations on any surface we identify as limestone or lime-based stucco, and we test in an inconspicuous area before committing to the full surface.
