## Why Albany Properties See Mold Issues

Shackelford County's clay-heavy soil is slow to drain. After a hard rain, water that pools against a foundation or under a pier-and-beam floor doesn't evaporate quickly — it seeps, and it stays. Many homes in Albany were built in the mid-twentieth century, a period when vapor barriers under crawl spaces were minimal or absent entirely. Plumbing in those homes often runs through interior wall cavities with limited ventilation, which means a slow supply-line drip can saturate wood framing for weeks without any visible sign at the surface. By the time a musty odor becomes noticeable in a bedroom or hallway, the colony behind the wall may already be well established. The region's seasonal humidity spikes — particularly after spring storm systems move through the Rolling Plains — add another layer of risk for attic spaces and around roof penetrations where flashing may have shifted over decades.

## Our Mold Remediation Process in Albany

Every remediation starts with a thorough visual inspection and moisture mapping. Technicians use thermal imaging and pin-type moisture meters to locate wet building materials that read dry at the surface but remain saturated underneath — a common condition in Albany's older plaster-and-lath walls. Once the scope is documented, the work area is sealed with poly sheeting and placed under negative air pressure using HEPA-filtered air scrubbers. This containment keeps spores from migrating to unaffected rooms during the removal phase.

Contaminated drywall, insulation, and wood framing that cannot be cleaned are removed and bagged for disposal. Salvageable structural members are treated with EPA-registered antimicrobial solutions and allowed to dry to acceptable moisture content before any reconstruction begins. Throughout the process, air quality inside the containment zone is monitored. The goal is not just to remove visible growth but to address the moisture source — because mold remediation without fixing the underlying water intrusion is only a temporary fix.

Air Care Restoration holds IICRC certification and is EPA Lead-Safe Certified, which matters for Albany homes built before 1978 where lead-based paint may be present on surfaces that need to be disturbed during remediation work.

## Local Note

Albany's older downtown-adjacent residential blocks include a number of homes with original cellulose insulation in the attic — a material that holds moisture exceptionally well and provides an ideal substrate for mold once wet. Unlike fiberglass batts, which tend to dry out after a roof leak is repaired, cellulose can remain damp at its core for weeks even after the surface feels dry to the touch. When attic mold is found in these homes, Air Care Restoration tests insulation moisture content before deciding whether drying in place is feasible or full removal is the more sound approach.
