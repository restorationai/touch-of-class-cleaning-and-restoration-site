## Why Snyder Properties See Water Damage Issues

Scurry County sits in a semi-arid stretch of the Rolling Plains where rainfall is infrequent but can arrive hard and fast during spring storm season. That combination — compacted caliche-heavy soil with low absorption capacity and periodic intense downpours — means surface water can pool against foundations and push into crawl spaces or slab perimeters faster than most homeowners expect.

Snyder's housing stock adds another layer. A significant portion of the city's residential neighborhoods were built between the 1950s and 1970s, when galvanized steel supply lines were standard. Those pipes corrode from the inside out, and by the time a pinhole leak shows up as a stain on the ceiling, water has often been migrating through wall framing for weeks. Older homes in that era also tend to have original cast-iron drain lines that can crack or belly, sending wastewater back into the lowest point of the house during a blockage.

Agriculture and oil-field activity around Snyder also means a higher-than-average share of metal buildings, shop spaces, and outbuildings — structures where condensation, roof penetrations, and uninsulated walls create chronic moisture problems that accelerate when a supply line or roof flashing finally fails.

## Our Water Cleanup Process in Snyder

When we arrive, the first priority is stopping the source — shutting off the supply at the meter or isolation valve before any water removal begins. From there, the process follows a sequence that doesn't cut corners regardless of how small the loss looks at the surface.

**Moisture mapping.** Thermal imaging cameras and calibrated moisture meters let us read what's happening inside walls, under flooring, and in subfloor cavities without tearing into every surface. In older Snyder homes with plaster-over-lath construction, this step is especially important: plaster can feel dry to the touch while the wood lath behind it holds significant moisture.

**Extraction.** Truck-mounted and portable extraction units pull standing water and water saturated into carpet, pad, and subfloor materials. For slab-on-grade homes — the dominant construction type in this part of Texas — we pay particular attention to the gap between the slab and the bottom plate, where water pools and feeds mold growth if it isn't pulled out completely.

**Structural drying.** High-capacity LGR dehumidifiers and air movers are positioned using a drying plan calibrated to the room's cubic footage, material types, and ambient conditions. Snyder's low relative humidity during dry stretches is actually an asset here — when outdoor air is dry enough, controlled ventilation accelerates drying timelines. During humid spring weather, we rely more heavily on closed-system drying.

**Documentation.** Daily moisture readings are logged and photographed. That record supports your insurance claim and gives the adjuster the evidence they need to process the loss without back-and-forth delays.

The team is IICRC certified in Water Restoration (WRT) and operates as an IICRC Certified Firm, meaning the drying protocol follows the IICRC S500 standard — not a shortcut version of it.
