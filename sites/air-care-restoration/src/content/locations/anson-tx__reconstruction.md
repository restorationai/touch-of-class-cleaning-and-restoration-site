When a kitchen fire or a slow plumbing leak tears into the framing of a Jones County home, the rebuild has to account for what's underneath: the expansive clay soils common across this stretch of the Rolling Plains shift with every wet-dry cycle, and a structure that was already settling before the loss needs more than fresh drywall. Air Care Restoration handles the full arc, from tear-out through framing, insulation, and finish work, so Anson property owners aren't managing separate demolition and rebuild crews on top of an insurance claim.

## Why Anson Properties Need Structural Reconstruction

Anson's housing stock leans older, a mix of mid-century ranch homes and farm-adjacent outbuildings built before modern vapor barriers and engineered foundations were standard. Combine that with West Texas summers that bake the clay hard and then see it heave again after a hard rain, and you get slab cracking, door frames that bind, and wall cavities that were already stressed before a fire or water event ever happened. Add in the county's exposure to grassfires carried by high wind during dry stretches, and reconstruction here often means rebuilding a structure that had pre-existing movement, not just replacing burned or soaked material. We document that distinction for the adjuster before we touch a wall, because the difference between storm damage and long-term settlement affects what a policy actually covers.

## Our Reconstruction Process in Anson

Work starts with a structural walk-through: we separate what failed because of the fire, water, or storm event from what was already compromised in the framing or foundation. From there, demolition is scoped to the actual damage line rather than an entire room, which keeps the rebuild footprint, and the cost, tighter. Framing and subfloor repairs follow local code for the applicable structure type, insulation and moisture barriers get upgraded where the original build didn't have them, and finish work (drywall, trim, flooring) is matched to the home's existing materials wherever that's realistic rather than defaulting to the cheapest available stock. Every phase gets photographed and logged, both for the insurance file and so the homeowner has a record of what changed structurally versus cosmetically.

## Anson Insurance & Permitting Coordination

Structural rebuilds in Jones County require permitting through the appropriate local authority before framing work begins, and we handle that coordination as part of the job rather than leaving it to the homeowner to navigate mid-claim. On the insurance side, we itemize mitigation (extraction, demolition, drying) separately from reconstruction line items, since adjusters typically evaluate those under different coverage categories. If mold remediation is part of the scope before rebuild can start, that work falls under our Texas Mold Remediation Contractor License through TDLR, and we sequence the clearance testing before framing goes back up so nothing gets closed in prematurely.

## Local note

Older Anson homes built on pier-and-beam foundations, common in the pre-1960s housing stock around town, often hide water damage in the crawlspace long after the visible flooring looks dry. We check subfloor moisture readings in those crawlspaces even when the surface floor reads normal, because clay soil movement can open gaps in the foundation skirting that let moisture wick into joists for months without anyone noticing until the reconstruction estimate is already underway.
