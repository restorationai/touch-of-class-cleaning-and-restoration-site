## Why Haskell Properties See Asbestos Concerns

The West Texas plains delivered extreme temperature swings that made builders here lean heavily on insulating materials through the 1940s, 1950s, and 1960s. Asbestos was cheap, fire-resistant, and genuinely effective at keeping heat out in summer and cold out in winter, which made it a practical choice for contractors working in Haskell County during those decades. The result is that older ranch-style homes, downtown commercial buildings, and agricultural outbuildings throughout the area frequently contain asbestos in places that are easy to overlook: the mastic adhesive beneath vinyl floor tiles, the joint compound on interior walls, the insulation wrapped around ductwork and water pipes, and the spray-applied texture on ceilings that homeowners today call popcorn ceilings.

Haskell's climate adds a complicating factor. The region experiences significant moisture swings, from dry summer heat to occasional severe storms that can drive water into building envelopes. Water intrusion that damages older materials can disturb asbestos-containing products that had been stable for years, turning a roof leak or burst pipe into a potential exposure situation. That connection between storm damage and asbestos disturbance is something property owners here need to keep in mind whenever older construction is involved.

## Our Asbestos Abatement Process in Haskell

Every project begins with an asbestos inspection and bulk sampling of suspect materials before any removal work starts. Samples go to an accredited laboratory, and we do not proceed with abatement until results are confirmed. That sequence is not optional under Texas Department of State Health Services rules, and skipping it exposes both the property owner and any contractor to serious liability.

Once a positive identification is made, the affected area is isolated with negative air pressure containment using heavy poly sheeting and HEPA-filtered air scrubbers. Workers wear full personal protective equipment throughout removal, and all asbestos-containing material is wetted during removal to suppress fiber release before being double-bagged in labeled, sealed waste containers. Disposal follows Texas Commission on Environmental Quality requirements for regulated asbestos-containing material, which means transport to an approved landfill with proper manifesting, not curbside pickup.

After removal, air clearance testing confirms fiber counts are within acceptable limits before containment is dismantled and the space is released for reconstruction. That clearance step is what separates a properly closed abatement project from one that leaves lingering risk.
