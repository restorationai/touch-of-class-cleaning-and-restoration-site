## Why Haskell Properties See Asbestos Concerns

Haskell's building history mirrors much of rural West Texas: construction surged in the early and mid-20th century when asbestos was not just permitted but actively promoted as a fireproofing and insulation solution. Homes and commercial buildings put up between roughly 1930 and 1980 frequently contain asbestos in multiple locations simultaneously — not just the obvious popcorn ceilings, but also the vinyl floor tiles beneath them, the joint compound on the walls, the duct wrap on HVAC systems, and the roofing felt underneath aging shingles.

The dry, high-plains climate here also plays a role. Haskell County sees significant temperature swings between summer highs and winter cold snaps, and that thermal cycling causes building materials to expand and contract repeatedly over decades. In older structures, that movement can crack or crumble asbestos-containing materials that were once intact — turning a stable, non-friable product into a friable one that releases fibers when disturbed. A roof that looked fine from the outside may have brittle asbestos-containing felt underneath that fractures the moment a roofer starts pulling shingles.

## Our Asbestos Abatement Process in Haskell

Every project starts with a thorough inspection and material sampling before any removal work is planned. Suspected materials are sampled and submitted to an accredited laboratory; we do not guess at what is or isn't asbestos-containing based on appearance alone. Once testing confirms the presence of regulated materials, the scope of work is defined and the appropriate regulatory notifications are made to the Texas Department of State Health Services (TDSHS), which oversees asbestos abatement licensing and project notifications in the state.

Containment is built before any material is touched. Negative air pressure units with HEPA filtration are established to prevent fiber migration to unaffected areas of the structure. Workers operate in full personal protective equipment, and all removed material is wetted, sealed in labeled disposal bags, and transported to a licensed disposal facility — none of it goes in a standard dumpster or landfill. After removal, air clearance sampling confirms that fiber levels meet the clearance criteria before containment is removed and the area is released for reconstruction.

Because Haskell properties often have multiple asbestos-containing materials present at once, we scope the full structure during the initial inspection rather than addressing only the material that triggered the call. Discovering additional regulated materials mid-project causes delays and added cost; a complete initial survey prevents that.

## Local Note

Older commercial buildings along Haskell's main corridor — the downtown blocks that housed hardware stores, banks, and farm supply operations through much of the 20th century — were frequently built with asbestos-containing floor tile and ceiling tile products that were standard for commercial construction of that era. When these buildings change hands or undergo renovation for new tenants, the asbestos inspection step is sometimes skipped because the materials look intact. In Haskell's dry climate, intact floor tile often *is* non-friable and lower risk — but the mastic adhesive beneath it frequently contains asbestos at higher concentrations than the tile itself, and that mastic becomes friable the moment mechanical removal begins. An inspection that covers both the tile and the substrate prevents a routine floor replacement from becoming a regulatory incident.
