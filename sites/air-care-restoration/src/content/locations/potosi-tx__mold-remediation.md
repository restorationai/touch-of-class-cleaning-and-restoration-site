## Why Potosi Properties See Mold Issues

The dominant soil type across this part of Taylor County is expansive clay, which swells during wet periods and pulls away from foundations during drought. That movement creates micro-gaps along slab perimeters and around utility penetrations, and those gaps are exactly where conditioned indoor air meets warm exterior moisture, producing condensation that feeds mold. Homes built in the 1970s and 1980s, which make up a significant share of the residential stock in the Potosi area, often used paper-faced fiberglass batt insulation and kraft-faced sheathing, both of which are highly receptive to mold once moisture levels climb above roughly 60 percent relative humidity.

Potosi's location also means it catches the leading edge of weather systems that push up from the south before they moderate. A single evening of high humidity following a dry stretch is enough to raise interior moisture readings in poorly ventilated crawl spaces, attics with inadequate ridge ventilation, and bathrooms that share an exterior wall. None of these conditions are unique to one street or subdivision, but they are consistent enough across the area that mold remediation here requires moisture mapping rather than a visual-only inspection.

## Our Mold Remediation Process in Potosi

Every job begins with a thorough moisture assessment using thermal imaging cameras and calibrated moisture meters. Visual mold is only part of the picture; the goal is to locate the moisture source and map its migration path before any physical remediation begins. Skipping that step means the mold returns.

Once the scope is established, the affected area is sealed with negative-air containment using 6-mil poly barriers and HEPA-filtered air scrubbers running continuously throughout the project. Contaminated materials, whether drywall, insulation, or wood framing, are removed, bagged, and disposed of according to Texas Department of Licensing and Regulation (TDLR) requirements that govern licensed mold remediation contractors in this state. Remaining structural surfaces are treated with EPA-registered antimicrobial agents. After drying is confirmed with post-remediation moisture readings, an independent clearance test can be arranged through a third-party industrial hygienist before reconstruction begins.

Our IICRC-certified technicians and OSHA-trained crew follow documented protocols on every project, which matters both for quality control and for insurance documentation.

## Potosi Insurance Coordination

Whether mold is covered under a homeowner's policy in Texas depends heavily on the documented cause. Mold that traces back to a sudden and accidental water loss, such as a burst supply line or a storm-driven roof breach, is typically a covered peril. Mold that developed over time from a slow leak or deferred maintenance is frequently excluded. Air Care Restoration documents the loss thoroughly: moisture readings, photographs of affected materials, and a written scope of work that gives your adjuster a clear record of what was found and what was done. That documentation is often the deciding factor in a coverage dispute.

## Local Note

One pattern that comes up repeatedly in Potosi-area homes is mold growth inside metal HVAC ducts that run through unconditioned attic space. During summer, the temperature differential between the cooled air inside the duct and the superheated attic air outside it causes condensation on interior duct surfaces, especially where duct wrap has separated or degraded. That condensation, combined with the dust and organic debris that accumulates inside return-air ducts, creates a ready growth medium. Homeowners often notice a musty smell only when the system runs, which is the first sign that the problem is duct-based rather than wall- or floor-based. Identifying this early prevents the HVAC system from distributing spores throughout the entire living space.
