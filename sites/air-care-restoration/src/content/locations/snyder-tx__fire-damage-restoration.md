## Why Snyder Properties Are Vulnerable to Fire and Smoke Damage

Scurry County sits in a semi-arid climate zone where summer relative humidity regularly drops into the teens and twenties. That dryness is not just a wildfire concern — it affects how fires behave inside homes. Low-moisture air means wood framing, insulation, and wall cavities ignite faster and burn hotter, which accelerates structural damage and pushes smoke deeper into porous materials before suppression begins.

Snyder's housing stock includes a significant share of mid-century construction — homes built in the 1950s through 1970s when open-plan interiors were less common and wall cavities were often insulated with materials that absorb smoke oils readily. Older wiring configurations in that era, including aluminum branch circuit wiring that was standard through the early 1970s, are a known ignition risk when connections loosen over decades. If your home predates 1980, a post-fire electrical inspection is worth scheduling before restoration work closes up any walls.

Natural gas service is common throughout Snyder, and gas-related ignition — whether from appliance failure or a line disturbed during a structure fire — adds a layer of complexity to the initial safety assessment that has to happen before any restoration crew enters the building.

## Our Fire Damage Restoration Process in Snyder

Every fire loss is different, but the sequence of work follows a logic grounded in the IICRC S700 standard for fire and smoke restoration. Here is what that looks like on a Snyder property:

**Emergency stabilization** comes first — boarding windows and doors, tarping roof penetrations, and confirming utilities are safely isolated. West Texas wind means an unsecured opening is not just a security issue; it is an accelerant for secondary smoke migration and moisture intrusion if weather moves in.

**Scope documentation** follows. We photograph and measure every affected surface and cavity, producing the kind of itemized loss documentation that carriers need to process a claim. Smoke travels through HVAC systems, so the assessment extends beyond the room of origin to every register and return air pathway in the home.

**Structural cleaning and deodorization** addresses soot on framing, subfloor, and wall cavities using dry chemical sponges, HEPA-filtered vacuums, and thermal fogging or hydroxyl generation for odor neutralization. Surfaces that cannot be cleaned to pre-loss condition are documented for replacement.

**Controlled demolition and reconstruction** removes unsalvageable materials and prepares the structure for rebuild. Our EPA Lead-Safe certification matters here — homes built before 1978 require lead-safe work practices during any demolition that disturbs painted surfaces, and Snyder has no shortage of homes in that age range.
