A new build or gut renovation here doesn't just leave the usual drywall powder and adhesive residue — it leaves a layer of that pale, gritty soil worked into every surface before the last nail is driven. ## Why Roby Properties Present Unique Post-Construction Cleaning Challenges

Fisher County's semi-arid climate means construction projects often run through dry, windy stretches where dust infiltration is relentless. Caliche — the calcium carbonate-rich soil common across this part of the Rolling Plains — grinds into subfloor seams, HVAC returns, and window tracks in a way that standard sweeping barely touches. If a renovation ran through late spring or early fall, when winds pick up ahead of storm fronts, that particulate can be embedded in surfaces before finish work even begins.

Older homes in Roby, many built during the mid-twentieth century agricultural boom, often have original hardwood floors, plaster-and-lath interior walls, and single-pane windows with wood frames. Post-construction cleaning in these homes requires a different touch than work in a new stick-frame build: plaster surfaces are more porous and hold fine dust in ways that drywall doesn't, and original hardwood needs careful product selection to avoid finish damage. Renovation debris in these structures also tends to include legacy materials — old caulk, lead-based paint chips in disturbed areas, and asbestos-containing floor tiles in pre-1980 additions — that require handling under EPA Lead-Safe protocols before cleaning can proceed.

## Our Post-Construction and Specialty Cleaning Process in Roby

Every post-construction clean begins with a walk-through to catalog what trades have left behind: overspray on glass, grout haze on tile, adhesive residue on finished floors, construction dust in ductwork and on light fixtures, and debris in corners that brooms push around rather than remove. That assessment shapes the sequence of work.

The process moves from the top down and from the inside out. Ceiling fans, light fixtures, and high ledges are cleaned before floors are touched. HVAC registers and returns are vacuumed with HEPA-filtered equipment so that the first time the system runs, it isn't redistributing drywall dust through a freshly cleaned space. Hard surfaces — countertops, cabinets, appliances, windows — are cleaned with products appropriate to the finish. Floors are the last step, and the method depends on the material: sealed concrete, original hardwood, luxury vinyl plank, and tile each respond differently to cleaning agents and moisture.

For specialty cleaning requests — deep cleaning after a long-term rental turnover, sanitizing after a commercial kitchen renovation, or cleaning a space that has sat unoccupied through a West Texas summer — the scope is adjusted to match the actual condition of the property rather than a fixed checklist.

## Equipment and Methods for Post-Construction Cleaning

Post-construction cleaning requires a different equipment set than routine janitorial work. HEPA-filtered vacuums capture fine particulate rather than recirculating it. Microfiber systems trap dust instead of spreading it. For surfaces with heavy construction residue — grout haze, paint overspray, adhesive — specific chemical dwell times and agitation methods are used rather than general-purpose cleaners that can streak or leave residue of their own.

Where a renovation disturbed materials that may contain lead — common in Fisher County homes built before 1978 — the team's EPA Lead-Safe certification governs how debris and dust are contained and disposed of before the cleaning phase begins.

## Local Note

One pattern worth knowing in this part of West Texas: construction projects that run through the dry season often have HVAC systems that were left running to control humidity during finish work. By the time the build is complete, the filters are clogged with drywall dust and the ducts have a visible coating on the first few feet of every run. A post-construction clean that skips the duct registers leaves that dust to circulate the first time the system cycles at full load — which in a Roby summer means within hours of occupancy. Pulling and cleaning those registers, and replacing filters before the final walkthrough, is a step that makes a measurable difference in air quality on day one.
