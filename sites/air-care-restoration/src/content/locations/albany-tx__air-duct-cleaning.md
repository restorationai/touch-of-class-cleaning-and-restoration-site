West Texas wind doesn't just carry dust — it carries it inside. In Albany, the same dry, gritty air that sweeps across Shackelford County works its way into return vents and settles deep inside ductwork, layering up season after season until your HVAC system is essentially circulating a fine red-clay haze through every room in the house. If you've noticed a musty or chalky smell when the system kicks on, or if allergy symptoms seem worse indoors than out, the ducts are often where the answer lives.

## Why Albany Properties See Duct Buildup

Albany sits in a part of Texas where the soil is fine-grained and the wind is persistent. During dry spells — and dry spells here can stretch for months — particulate matter infiltrates homes through gaps around return grilles, poorly sealed duct joints, and aging flex duct connections. Many homes in Albany were built during eras when duct sealing wasn't a priority, and over decades those small gaps become reliable entry points for dust, pollen, and allergens.

The region also sees sharp seasonal swings: hot, dry summers followed by cold fronts that push humidity levels up quickly. That cycle of dry and damp creates conditions where dust accumulates in summer and moisture can briefly condense on cooler duct surfaces in winter — enough to encourage mold colonization if organic debris is already present. A duct system that looks fine from the register face can be carrying a significant load further back in the trunk lines.

## Our Air Duct Cleaning Process in Albany

The process starts with a visual inspection — camera or direct access where the duct layout allows — so we understand what we're dealing with before any equipment runs. Albany homes with older central systems sometimes have trunk-and-branch layouts that require a different agitation approach than modern flex-duct installations, and we adjust accordingly.

We use negative-pressure extraction: a high-powered vacuum unit is connected to the main trunk, creating suction throughout the system while rotary brush agitation loosens debris from duct walls. Registers are sealed one by one as each branch is cleaned, so loosened material moves toward the vacuum rather than back into living spaces. After extraction, we inspect again. If contamination warrants it — particularly in homes where moisture intrusion has occurred — we apply an EPA-registered antimicrobial treatment to interior duct surfaces. Dryer vent cleaning follows the same appointment when requested, which matters in Albany's dusty environment where lint and fine soil can combine to create a genuine fire risk in longer vent runs.

## Equipment and Methods

The core tool is a truck-mounted or trailer-mounted negative-pressure unit with HEPA filtration — meaning the debris extracted from your ducts is captured and contained, not exhausted into your yard or back into the home. Agitation is handled with flexible rotary brushes sized to the duct diameter, which matters in older systems where rigid metal duct and newer flex duct may coexist in the same run. Contact whip attachments are used for stubborn debris in longer horizontal runs. For sanitization, we use products registered with the EPA for HVAC use — not consumer-grade sprays.

## Local Note

One thing that comes up specifically in Shackelford County homes: many properties here have evaporative coolers or combination systems rather than purely refrigerated air. Evaporative systems pull outside air directly through a wet pad and into the duct system, which means every dust event outside becomes a dust event inside the ducts — and the moisture from the pad can cause that dust to cake onto duct walls rather than stay loose. If your Albany home uses a swamp cooler, even seasonally, duct cleaning intervals should be shorter than the national average recommendation, and the interior of the cooler cabinet itself should be inspected at the same time.
