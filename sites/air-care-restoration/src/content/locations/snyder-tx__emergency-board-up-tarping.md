Snyder sits in the middle of the Southern Plains, where the weather swings hard and fast. The region's housing stock includes a significant number of homes built in the mid-twentieth century — construction eras when roof decking was thinner, window frames were single-pane wood, and attic ventilation details were less robust. That older material profile means a breach opens up a larger vulnerability than it might in a newer build.

Fire damage compounds the problem. After a structure fire, the roof and exterior walls are often compromised in ways that aren't immediately visible — char weakens sheathing, and suppression water accelerates that deterioration overnight. Without a proper board-up and tarp installation, a property that survived the fire itself can sustain a second round of damage from the next rain event.

## Our Emergency Board-Up and Tarping Process in Snyder

Every call starts with a rapid assessment of the breach points: broken windows, kicked-in doors, missing roof sections, or compromised soffits. For window and door openings, the crew installs structural-grade plywood cut and secured to the framing — not stapled sheeting that the next gust pulls loose. When fire damage is involved, the board-up scope expands.
