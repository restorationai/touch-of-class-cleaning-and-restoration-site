## Why Haskell Properties Present Specific Post-Construction Cleaning Challenges

Haskell sits in a semi-arid stretch of the Rolling Plains where wind is a constant variable. During any renovation or new build, that wind pushes fine construction particulate into wall cavities, window tracks, and ductwork in ways that contractors in more sheltered markets rarely contend with. Caliche-heavy soil from the surrounding ranch land is also unusually alkaline — when it dries on sealed concrete or luxury vinyl plank, it leaves a white haze that can be mistaken for a product defect if it isn't addressed with the right pH-balanced cleaners before the owner walks through.

Older commercial buildings and homes in Haskell's downtown corridor often feature original plaster or lathe-and-plaster walls that were disturbed during renovation. Plaster dust behaves differently than standard drywall compound — it's denser, it doesn't float as long, and it embeds in porous grout and natural stone more stubbornly. Sarha Boyd, owner of Air Care Restoration, holds EPA Certified Renovator (Lead RRP) credentials, which matters on pre-1978 structures where disturbed plaster may contain lead-based paint residue that requires containment and proper disposal rather than standard vacuuming.

## Our Post-Construction Cleaning Process in Haskell

Every post-construction clean follows a structured sequence rather than a single pass with a mop and vacuum. The work moves from ceiling to floor and from rough to fine:

**Phase 1 — Rough Clean:** Removal of construction debris, packaging materials, scrap lumber, and bulk dust. HEPA-filtered vacuums run on all horizontal surfaces, window sills, and inside cabinet boxes before any wet cleaning begins. This phase also includes a duct inspection — in Haskell's windy conditions, drywall dust migration into the HVAC system during construction is common enough that we treat it as a default check rather than an add-on.

**Phase 2 — Detail Clean:** Window frames, door hardware, light fixtures, and all millwork are wiped down. Grout lines are scrubbed. Residual construction adhesive is removed from tile and hard flooring using appropriate solvents that won't compromise the finish. Alkaline soil deposits from the job site get treated with a pH-adjusted solution rather than acidic cleaners that can etch newly sealed surfaces.

**Phase 3 — Final / White-Glove Pass:** This is the walk-through-ready stage. Glass is polished, stainless appliances are treated, and every surface is inspected under raking light to catch the fine haze that standard overhead lighting misses. We document the condition with photographs before releasing the space.

## Local Note: Caliche Dust and HVAC Registers

One pattern that shows up repeatedly on Haskell-area construction projects is caliche contamination inside supply registers. Crews prop windows during the build for ventilation — a reasonable call in the Texas heat — but the regional soil is so fine and alkaline that it migrates into the duct system even when the HVAC isn't running. When the system fires up for the first time after a clean, that residue can coat freshly cleaned surfaces within hours. Our standard practice on Haskell jobs is to vacuum and wipe register boots before the final pass, not after, and to recommend the homeowner or property manager run the system for a short cycle before the official walk-through so any remaining loose material settles and can be addressed. It's a small sequencing adjustment that prevents a callback.
