## Why Tuscola Properties See Serious Storm Damage

Taylor County sits squarely in a corridor where cold dry air from the Panhandle collides with Gulf moisture pushing north — conditions that generate large hail, straight-line winds, and the occasional tornado with very little lead time. Tuscola's housing stock reflects the area's agricultural roots: many homes are single-story frame construction on slab foundations, built in eras when roof decking was thinner and roof-to-wall connections weren't engineered to the wind-load standards required today. That older framing can fail at the ridge, at the gable ends, or where an addition was tied into the original structure — spots that look intact from the street but have lost their sheathing nails or flashing integrity after decades of thermal cycling.

Hail here also tends to run large. A storm that produces golf-ball-sized hail will granulate asphalt shingles enough to void manufacturer warranties even when the shingles appear visually intact. That kind of damage lets water infiltrate slowly — not a sudden flood, but a steady seep that saturates decking and attic insulation over weeks before a stain appears on the ceiling below.

## Our Storm Damage Restoration Process in Tuscola

The first step after we arrive is a full exterior and interior assessment — roof, walls, windows, and any outbuildings on the property. We photograph everything before touching it, because insurance documentation starts at the moment of arrival, not after cleanup begins. If the roof or a wall is breached, we install emergency tarping or board-up to stop additional water intrusion before any restoration work starts.

From there, the process moves to moisture mapping. Even when a storm looks like a "wind and hail" event rather than a flooding event, driven rain can push water into wall cavities, attic spaces, and under flooring through gaps that weren't there before the storm. We use thermal imaging and calibrated moisture meters to find those pockets. Wet insulation in an attic in a Taylor County summer — where ambient temperatures routinely exceed 100°F — becomes a mold-risk environment within 24 to 48 hours, so speed matters here.

Once the structure is dried to IICRC S500 standards, we move into structural repairs: decking replacement, framing repairs, window and door reinstallation, and interior finishes. As an IICRC Certified Firm with OSHA-trained technicians and EPA Lead-Safe certification, we handle the full scope rather than handing off to a second contractor mid-job.
