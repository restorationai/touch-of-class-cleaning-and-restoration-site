## Why Paint Rock Properties Are Vulnerable to Water Damage

The same semi-arid climate that makes Concho County feel dry most of the year creates a false sense of security. Rainfall here tends to arrive in concentrated bursts — flash-flood events rather than steady soaking rains — and structures that sit dry for months can be overwhelmed in hours. Older homes common to a small county seat like Paint Rock often feature pier-and-beam foundations or early slab pours that have settled unevenly over decades. Those settlement gaps become entry points the moment standing water accumulates against the foundation. Galvanized supply lines in homes built before the 1970s are also prone to pinhole failures; the corrosion is slow and invisible until a section finally gives way, sometimes behind plaster or original wood-lath walls that hold moisture far longer than modern drywall.

The Colorado River corridor adds another layer of risk. Properties near the river bottom are subject to periodic flooding, and even homes set well back from the bank can experience elevated water tables after sustained upstream rainfall. That saturated soil pushes moisture upward through slab cracks and around stem walls in ways that surface-level inspection won't catch.

## Our Water Damage Restoration Process in Paint Rock

When we arrive, the first priority is stopping the source — whether that means coordinating with you to shut off the main supply or identifying the structural point of entry. From there, the process follows a defined sequence calibrated to what we actually find:

**Moisture mapping.** We use thermal imaging and calibrated moisture meters to trace water migration behind walls and under flooring before a single panel comes off. In older Paint Rock homes with plaster walls, this step is especially important — plaster can read dry at the surface while the lath and framing behind it remain saturated.

**Water extraction.** Truck-mounted and portable extraction units pull standing water from hard surfaces and carpet. In rooms with original hardwood or tongue-and-groove subfloor common to mid-century construction in this area, we adjust suction pressure to avoid pulling the finish off the boards.

**Structural drying.** Commercial desiccant and refrigerant dehumidifiers, paired with high-velocity air movers, are positioned according to a drying plan documented in writing. Drying targets follow the IICRC S500 standard. We monitor daily and adjust equipment placement as readings change.

**Documentation.** Every moisture reading, equipment placement, and daily log is recorded and formatted for insurance submission. We provide detailed damage documentation, estimates, and invoices and can communicate technical scope information directly with your adjuster throughout the process.
