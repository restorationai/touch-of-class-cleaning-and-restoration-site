## Why Hawley Properties Present Specific Biohazard Challenges

Hawley's housing stock leans toward older construction, and that matters in biohazard remediation. Homes built in earlier decades often have porous concrete slab foundations, unsealed subfloor materials, and wood framing that has absorbed decades of humidity cycling through West Texas summers and dry winters. When a biohazard event occurs in a structure like this, contamination does not stay on the surface. Fluids can wick into grout lines, penetrate unsealed concrete, and travel along wood grain in ways that a surface wipe-down will never address.

The region's alkaline caliche soil also affects crawl spaces and pier-and-beam foundations common in older Jones County properties. Moisture from a biohazard event can interact with that substrate in ways that complicate both decontamination and post-cleanup testing. Technicians who work only in urban markets with modern construction may not anticipate these variables. Our crews are familiar with the building patterns across this part of Texas.

## Our Biohazard Cleanup Process in Hawley

Every job begins with a site assessment before any cleaning starts. Technicians identify the full extent of contamination, including areas that are not immediately visible, and establish a containment perimeter to protect unaffected parts of the home or building. Decontamination follows a structured sequence: removal of affected porous materials that cannot be safely restored, treatment of hard surfaces with EPA-registered disinfectants, and verification through ATP or other testing methods appropriate to the situation. We document every step, which matters when an insurance claim is involved. Final clearance is not declared until testing confirms the space is safe for reoccupancy.
