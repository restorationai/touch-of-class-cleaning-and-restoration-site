Early, TX sits in the heart of Brown County, where mid-century residential construction was the norm and building materials from the 1950s through the 1970s are still very much present inside walls, attics, and ceilings across town. That era of construction leaned heavily on asbestos-containing materials — floor tile adhesive, pipe insulation, joint compound, and the textured popcorn ceilings that still cap countless rooms in homes built before the federal restrictions of the late 1970s. If you're planning a renovation, dealing with storm damage, or simply trying to understand what's inside your older Early home, an asbestos inspection is the right first step before any demo work begins.

## Why Early Properties See Asbestos Concerns

Brown County's housing stock tells the story of a region that grew steadily through the post-war decades. Many homes in Early were built during the same window — roughly 1950 to 1980 — when asbestos was a standard ingredient in dozens of building products. Vinyl floor tiles and the black mastic adhesive beneath them are among the most common sources found during inspections. Textured ceiling coatings applied with a spray gun often contained chrysotile asbestos as a binder. Attic insulation, particularly the vermiculite variety, is another concern in homes of that era.

Early's climate adds a layer of complexity. The region sees hot, dry summers and occasional severe weather, including hail and high winds. Storm damage that punches through a roof or siding can disturb materials that were otherwise stable and contained. Once asbestos-containing material is broken, crumbled, or friable, fibers become airborne — and that's when risk escalates. A roofing contractor or general handyman who doesn't test before cutting is creating a hazard, not solving one.

## Our Asbestos Abatement Process in Early

Every project starts with sampling and laboratory analysis — no assumptions, no visual guessing. Suspected materials are collected following EPA-recommended protocols, sent to an accredited lab, and results guide the scope of work. If asbestos is confirmed, the abatement plan is built around the specific materials and their condition.

Containment is established before any removal begins. Negative air pressure units with HEPA filtration keep fibers from migrating to unaffected areas of the home. Workers wear full respiratory protection and disposable protective suits. Removed materials are wetted to suppress fiber release, sealed in labeled poly bags, and transported to a licensed disposal facility — Texas Commission on Environmental Quality (TCEQ) regulations govern how asbestos waste is handled and where it goes, and every step of that chain is documented.

For popcorn ceiling asbestos removal specifically, the process involves careful wetting of the texture coat before any scraping begins, which significantly reduces airborne fiber counts during removal. After abatement, air clearance sampling confirms the space is safe before containment is removed and the area is released for re-occupancy or continued renovation.

Our team is OSHA trained and the firm holds EPA Lead-Safe Certification — relevant because lead-based paint and asbestos frequently coexist in the same pre-1978 homes, and disturbing one without accounting for the other creates compounding hazards.

## Local Note

One thing worth knowing if you own an older home in Early: the combination of pier-and-beam foundations and the region's expansive clay soils means homes shift more than those on slab construction in more stable soil conditions. That movement, over decades, can crack floor tile — and cracked vinyl floor tile from the 1950s or 1960s is one of the situations where previously stable, non-friable asbestos-containing material becomes friable and a potential exposure concern. If you've noticed cracked or lifting old floor tile in a home built before 1980, it's worth having it tested before you pull it up yourself.
