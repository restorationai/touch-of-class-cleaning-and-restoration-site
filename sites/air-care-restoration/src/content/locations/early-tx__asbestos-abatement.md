## Why Early Properties Face Elevated Asbestos Risk

Brown County's growth during the mid-twentieth century aligned almost perfectly with peak asbestos use in American construction. Homes built between roughly 1950 and 1980 commonly contain asbestos in several places that are easy to overlook: the backing on vinyl floor tiles, the joint compound on drywall seams, pipe insulation in utility closets, and the spray-applied texture on ceilings that many people know as popcorn ceilings. In Early specifically, the housing stock along older residential streets reflects that era, and many of those homes have been updated cosmetically without anyone disturbing or testing the materials underneath.

The dry, windy climate of West-Central Texas adds another layer of concern. When asbestos-containing materials dry out and become brittle, they are more likely to release fibers with minor disturbance. A ceiling patch, a floor tile pulled up during a bathroom remodel, or storm damage that cracks an older textured surface can all create an exposure risk that is not visible to the eye. Testing before any demolition or repair work in a pre-1980 Early home is not overcaution; it is the step that determines what kind of project you actually have on your hands.

## Our Asbestos Abatement Process in Early

Every abatement engagement begins with inspection and bulk sampling. A trained technician collects material samples from suspected areas, which are then analyzed by an accredited laboratory. Results determine whether abatement is required and, if so, which materials and how much square footage are involved.

Once abatement is confirmed, the work area is isolated with negative air pressure containment to prevent fiber migration to the rest of the structure. Regulated materials are wetted to suppress fiber release, carefully removed, and sealed in approved disposal bags. All waste is transported and disposed of in compliance with Texas Commission on Environmental Quality requirements for asbestos-containing material. After removal, air clearance sampling is conducted to confirm fiber levels have returned to acceptable thresholds before containment is removed and the space is returned to use.

For popcorn ceiling asbestos removal, a common request in Early homes built before 1978, the process requires particular care because spray-applied texture is friable, meaning it crumbles easily and releases fibers with minimal disturbance. Our OSHA-trained crew treats every popcorn ceiling in that age range as a regulated material until testing says otherwise.
