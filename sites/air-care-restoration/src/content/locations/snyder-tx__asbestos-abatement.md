## Why Snyder Properties Face Elevated Asbestos Risk

Scurry County's petroleum industry shaped the local economy for decades, and that same industrial momentum shaped how buildings went up. Contractors working quickly to house oil field workers and their families used whatever materials were available and code-compliant at the time, which routinely included asbestos in roofing felts, joint compounds, ceiling textures, and vinyl floor tile adhesives. Many of those structures are still standing and still occupied.

The dry, windy climate on the Llano Estacado also plays a role. Repeated cycles of heat and drought cause building materials to contract and crack over time. When asbestos-containing materials become brittle and friable, fibers release more easily into the air, especially during renovation work or after a weather event that stresses the structure. A popcorn ceiling that looks intact may be releasing fibers the moment a ceiling fan runs overhead or a contractor starts cutting into an adjacent wall.

Asbestos testing is the only way to know what you are dealing with before any demolition or remodeling begins. Sampling by a qualified inspector and laboratory analysis establish whether abatement is required before a single tile is pulled or a ceiling is scraped.

## Our Asbestos Abatement Process in Snyder

Every abatement project begins with a thorough inspection and sample collection from suspected materials. Bulk samples go to an accredited laboratory, and results determine the scope of work. No abatement plan is written until the data is in hand.

Once the scope is confirmed, the work area is isolated with negative air pressure containment using polyethylene sheeting and HEPA-filtered air scrubbers. Regulated asbestos-containing material is wetted to suppress fiber release, carefully removed by hand where possible, and double-bagged in labeled, sealed disposal containers. All waste is transported and disposed of in compliance with Texas Commission on Environmental Quality requirements for asbestos-containing material.

After removal, the containment zone undergoes a thorough visual inspection and air clearance testing before barriers come down. The goal is a clean, documented result that satisfies both the property owner and any third-party reviewer, whether that is a lender, a contractor preparing to begin renovation, or a prospective buyer's inspector.

Air Care Restoration holds an EPA Lead-Safe certification and is OSHA trained, which matters on older Snyder properties where lead-based paint and asbestos often coexist in the same structure.

## Local Note

One pattern that comes up repeatedly in Snyder-area properties built during the 1950s and 1960s is the combination of asbestos floor tile over a concrete slab with mastic adhesive underneath. The tile itself may be the regulated material, but the black mastic adhesive used to bond it can also contain asbestos at concentrations that require abatement.
