Snyder sits in the heart of Scurry County on the Llano Estacado, where the oil-field economy drove rapid residential and commercial construction through the 1940s, 1950s, and into the 1970s — precisely the decades when asbestos was woven into floor tiles, pipe insulation, joint compound, and the textured ceilings that still exist in countless homes across town. If you've bought an older property here, inherited one, or are planning a renovation, the question isn't really whether asbestos-containing materials might be present — it's whether anyone has ever tested for them.

## Why Snyder Properties See Asbestos Issues

The Permian Basin building boom that followed mid-century oil activity left Snyder with a dense stock of ranch-style homes and commercial buildings constructed when asbestos was standard practice, not an exception. Builders in this region relied heavily on asbestos-reinforced floor tiles for their durability under the wide temperature swings the South Plains delivers — scorching summers that push attic temperatures well above 130°F and cold snaps that drop overnight lows into the teens. That thermal cycling stresses materials over decades, causing adhesives to fail, tiles to crack, and previously stable asbestos-containing compounds to become friable.

Popcorn ceilings are among the most common discoveries in Snyder's older homes. Spray-applied acoustic texture was widely used through the late 1970s and contained chrysotile asbestos in many formulations. Homeowners planning to scrape, sand, or repaint those ceilings without prior testing are unknowingly putting themselves at risk. The same applies to vermiculite attic insulation, transite pipe in older irrigation systems, and the black mastic adhesive beneath vinyl floor tiles — all materials that appear routinely in Scurry County properties from this era.

## Our Asbestos Abatement Process in Snyder

Every project begins with a formal inspection and bulk material sampling before any abatement work is scheduled. Samples are sent to an accredited laboratory, and no removal work begins until results confirm the presence and type of asbestos fibers. This step protects you, your family, and the crew — and it produces documentation your insurance carrier and any future buyer will expect to see.

Once testing confirms asbestos-containing materials, the work area is isolated using negative air pressure containment with HEPA-filtered air scrubbers running continuously. Regulated waste is wetted, double-bagged in labeled poly sheeting, and transported to an approved disposal facility in compliance with Texas Commission on Environmental Quality (TCEQ) requirements. Air clearance testing is conducted after removal and before containment is dismantled, confirming fiber counts are within acceptable limits before the space is released for reoccupancy or continued renovation.

The team is OSHA trained and EPA Lead-Safe Certified — relevant here because Snyder's older housing stock frequently pairs asbestos-containing materials with lead-based paint in the same walls, ceilings, and trim, and disturbing one without accounting for the other creates compounding hazards.

## Snyder Insurance & Coordination

Asbestos abatement is sometimes covered under homeowner's policies when the presence of asbestos-containing materials is tied to a covered loss — a roof breach that damages a vermiculite attic, for example, or a plumbing failure that requires tearing into asbestos-wrapped pipe. We document the affected materials, photograph conditions before and after, and provide the itemized scope your adjuster needs to process the claim. ## Local Note

One pattern that shows up repeatedly in Scurry County properties: original hardwood subfloors were often covered with a layer of asbestos-containing vinyl tile sometime in the 1960s or early 1970s, and then covered again with a second generation of non-asbestos vinyl or carpet in the 1980s. Homeowners pulling up carpet today sometimes see only the newer layer and assume they're clear — but the original tile and its black mastic adhesive are still underneath. If your home was built before 1975 and the floors have been updated at least once, it's worth having the subfloor assembly tested before any flooring work begins.
