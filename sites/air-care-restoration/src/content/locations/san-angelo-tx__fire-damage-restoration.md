## Why San Angelo Properties Face Distinct Fire Damage Challenges

Tom Green County sits in a semi-arid zone where relative humidity routinely drops into the teens during spring and summer. That low humidity accelerates the spread of range fires and structure fires alike, and it also means smoke residue dries and bonds to surfaces faster than in more humid parts of Texas. Protein smoke — the kind left by kitchen fires — becomes nearly invisible on walls but produces a persistent, rancid odor that intensifies in heat. In San Angelo's summers, interior temperatures in an unoccupied, un-air-conditioned structure can climb high enough to bake that residue deeper into drywall and wood grain within days of the fire.

San Angelo also has a substantial stock of mid-century masonry homes — brick veneer over wood frame construction common to the region from the 1950s through the 1970s. Brick exteriors can mask smoke infiltration at the wall cavity level; the exterior looks intact while the interior framing and insulation have absorbed significant odor and soot. Standard visual inspection misses this. Thermal imaging and air sampling are the only reliable way to map the actual contamination boundary in these homes.

## Our Fire Damage Restoration Process in San Angelo

Every fire loss starts with a documented scope before any cleaning begins. The crew photographs and catalogs affected materials, establishes containment to prevent cross-contamination to unaffected areas, and runs air scrubbers with HEPA filtration to begin reducing airborne particulate. Because San Angelo's low-humidity air can cause smoke-damaged materials to off-gas odor compounds more aggressively, ozone treatment and hydroxyl generation are deployed strategically — not as a shortcut to cleaning, but as a complement to it after surfaces have been physically cleaned.

Structural assessment follows: the team evaluates load-bearing members, checks for heat-compromised framing, and identifies materials that require removal versus those that can be cleaned and sealed. Porous materials — insulation, carpet, fabric wall treatments — almost always require removal after a significant fire. Semi-porous materials like drywall and unfinished wood are evaluated on a case-by-case basis using odor testing and visual inspection under UV light. Reconstruction planning is coordinated with the insurance adjuster's scope so that the rebuild phase can begin without delay once remediation is complete and clearance documentation is in hand.
