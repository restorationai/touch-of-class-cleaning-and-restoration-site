## Why Early Properties See Sewage Backup Issues

Much of Early's residential housing stock was built during the mid-20th century, when cast-iron and clay-tile lateral lines were standard. Those materials have a finite lifespan. Clay tile in particular is vulnerable to root intrusion from the native mesquite and cedar trees common across Brown County — roots seek moisture, and a hairline crack in an aging sewer lateral is all the invitation they need. Over time, root mass can partially or fully block the line, causing sewage to back up into the lowest drain in the home, typically a floor drain, toilet, or washing machine standpipe.

Early also experiences the same intense West Texas thunderstorms that push through the region seasonally. When heavy rainfall saturates the ground rapidly, municipal sewer systems can become overwhelmed, and that pressure can push sewage backward through private laterals into homes — a condition called a sewer surcharge backup. Because Early's soils drain slowly after saturation, that ground pressure can persist for hours after a storm passes.

## Our Sewage Cleanup and Sanitization Process in Early

Every sewage call starts with containment. Before any extraction begins, the affected area is isolated to prevent cross-contamination from spreading to unaffected rooms. Standing sewage is removed using truck-mounted extraction units, and any porous materials — drywall, insulation, subflooring, carpet — that absorbed Category 3 water are documented and removed. There is no safe way to dry and restore materials that have been saturated with raw sewage; removal is the industry standard.

Once the space is cleared down to hard, non-porous surfaces, the real sanitization work begins. We apply EPA-registered antimicrobial agents to all affected surfaces, including wall cavities and subfloor framing that were exposed during the backup. Drying equipment — commercial dehumidifiers and air movers — runs continuously until moisture readings confirm the structural materials have returned to normal levels. A final post-sanitization inspection documents that the space is safe for reconstruction.

## Early Insurance Coordination for Sewage Losses

Sewage backup coverage is one of the most commonly misunderstood items in a homeowner's policy. Standard homeowner's insurance in Texas typically does not cover sewer backup damage unless the homeowner has added a specific sewer backup rider. Many Early homeowners discover this only after a loss occurs. Air Care Restoration documents the loss thoroughly — photographs, moisture readings, material inventories, and a written scope — so that if you do carry the rider, your adjuster has everything needed to process the claim efficiently. ## Local Note

One thing that comes up specifically in Early and the broader Brown County area: homes built on pier-and-beam foundations — common in older neighborhoods throughout the city — can accumulate sewage contamination in the crawl space beneath the subfloor without any visible sign in the living area above. A backup that appears minor at the floor drain level may have already allowed Category 3 water to pool under the home. If your home sits on a pier-and-beam foundation and you've had any sewage event, a crawl space inspection is a necessary part of the assessment, not an optional add-on.
