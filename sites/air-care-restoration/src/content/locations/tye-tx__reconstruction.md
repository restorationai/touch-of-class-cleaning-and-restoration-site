When a house fire, burst water line, or wind event tears into the structure of a Tye home, the damage rarely stops at the surface. Studs get scorched, subfloor swells and delaminates, and load-bearing walls sometimes need more than a patch. Reconstruction services in Tye mean rebuilding what was lost to code, not just covering it up, and doing it in a way that holds up to West Texas heat, wind, and the clay-heavy ground under most of Taylor County.

## Why Tye Properties See Reconstruction Needs

Tye sits along the I-20 corridor west of Abilene, where a lot of the housing stock is a mix of older frame homes, manufactured housing, and newer ranch-style construction built on slab foundations. The expansive clay soils common across this part of Taylor County shift with moisture swings, which means foundations move, slabs crack, and plumbing lines under the slab can fail years before a homeowner notices the signs. Add in West Texas's wind events and occasional severe hail, and roof and siding damage that starts as a covered claim can turn into a full structural rebuild once water intrusion or fire has worked through wall cavities and framing.

## Our Reconstruction Process in Tye

Reconstruction starts after mitigation, once the structure is stable, dry, and safe to rebuild in. We document every removed material with photos before demolition, matching what's replaced to the original framing dimensions and finish where possible, since a lot of Tye homes were built decades apart from their neighbors and don't share a single standard. Framing repairs are inspected before drywall goes back, moisture readings are taken on adjacent materials to confirm the loss didn't spread further than what's visible, and finish work (paint, trim, flooring) is scheduled once the structure passes its own drying or inspection benchmarks. For fire damage reconstruction specifically, we also address smoke odor in framing members that look undamaged but have absorbed particulate, since painting over smoke-affected wood without sealing it first is one of the most common reasons odor returns months later.

## Reaching Tye from Abilene

Our crews run out of Abilene, a short run down I-20 into Tye, and we're set up to take reconstruction calls around the clock. Because reconstruction work is rarely a same-day fix, what matters more than arrival speed is sequencing: getting mitigation crews out fast to stop the damage from spreading, then scheduling the reconstruction crew and materials so the rebuild doesn't sit waiting on a permit or an adjuster sign-off longer than it has to.

## Tye Insurance & Permitting Coordination

Most reconstruction work in and around Tye falls under Taylor County's permitting process for unincorporated areas, and depending on the scope, a rebuild may need inspection sign-off before walls get closed back up. We coordinate that sequencing with the adjuster and, where required, the county so the same wall isn't opened twice. On the insurance side, we document pre-existing conditions (an older roof, prior foundation cracking) separately from storm or fire damage, since carriers scrutinize that distinction closely on structural claims and it's the most common reason a reconstruction estimate gets sent back for revision.

## Local note

A good share of the manufactured and older frame homes around Tye were built before slab-on-grade plumbing layouts were standardized, which means a single pipe failure can run water under interior walls in a direction nobody expects. On a reconstruction job, that's why we open a wider section of wall than the visible stain suggests. It's cheaper to replace a few extra feet of drywall now than to get called back in eight weeks for a wall that's still wicking moisture from a section we didn't check.
