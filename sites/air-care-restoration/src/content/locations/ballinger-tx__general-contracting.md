## Why Ballinger Homes Present Distinct Renovation Challenges

Much of Ballinger's residential housing stock was built in the mid-20th century, when construction practices reflected the materials and codes of that era. That means pier-and-beam foundations are common, original plumbing may still be galvanized steel, and interior walls in older homes often carry lead-based paint — a factor that requires EPA Certified Renovator handling during any renovation that disturbs those surfaces. Owner Sarha Boyd holds EPA Certified Renovator (Lead RRP) credentials, so that compliance step is built into the project rather than bolted on as an afterthought.

Runnels County's expansive clay soils are another variable that affects renovation scope in ways homeowners don't always anticipate. When clay soils dry out through a West Texas summer, they contract; when rains return, they swell. That cycle puts lateral pressure on slab edges and pier footings alike, which can cause door frames to rack, tile to crack, and additions to settle unevenly if the foundation isn't assessed before new work begins. A bathroom remodel that looks straightforward can reveal floor framing that needs sistering before new tile goes down.

## Local Note

One thing worth knowing about renovation work in Ballinger and the surrounding Runnels County area: homes with original mid-century wood-frame construction often have interior walls that were finished with a sand-finish plaster coat rather than drywall. When those walls are opened for electrical updates, plumbing reroutes, or layout changes during a remodel, the plaster removal generates significantly more debris volume than drywall demo, and the underlying lath — whether wood or metal — requires a different approach to patching and re-finishing. Matching a sand-finish plaster texture is a skilled trade step that gets skipped by contractors unfamiliar with the housing stock. We account for it in scope and timeline rather than discovering it mid-demo.
