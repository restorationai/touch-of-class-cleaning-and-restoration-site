## Why Abilene Properties Present Unique Biohazard Challenges

Abiline's housing stock spans a wide range of construction eras. Older homes — particularly those built in the mid-twentieth century — often feature porous materials like original hardwood subfloors, plaster-and-lath walls, and uncoated concrete in utility areas. These surfaces absorb biological material at a different rate than modern vinyl plank or sealed drywall, which means visible contamination on the surface rarely tells the full story. Remediation in these structures requires more thorough assessment of what has migrated below the surface layer before any restoration work begins.

The region's alkaline soil and periodic drought conditions also affect crawl spaces and slab foundations in ways that matter for biohazard work. Cracked slabs or compromised vapor barriers — both common in older Abilene properties — can allow contaminants to reach areas that are difficult to access and harder to verify as clean without proper testing protocols.

## Our Biohazard Cleanup Process in Abilene

Every job begins with a scene assessment conducted by OSHA-trained technicians. No cleaning begins until the scope of contamination is understood. From there, the process follows a structured sequence:

**Containment and PPE establishment** — Affected areas are isolated to prevent cross-contamination to adjacent rooms or HVAC pathways. In Abilene's older homes where ductwork may run through unfinished spaces, this step is especially important.

**Removal of contaminated materials** — Porous materials that cannot be decontaminated to a safe standard are removed and packaged according to Texas Department of State Health Services requirements for regulated medical waste transport and disposal. This is not curbside trash — it requires licensed handling.

**Surface treatment and verification** — Hospital-grade disinfectants are applied to all affected hard surfaces. Air Care Restoration's IICRC-certified team follows established remediation standards, and no job is signed off without verification that treated surfaces meet safety thresholds.

**Odor control** — Biological odors can persist in porous building materials long after visible contamination is removed. We address this with targeted odor neutralization rather than masking agents, which is particularly relevant in Abilene properties where older insulation and subflooring can hold odors for extended periods.
