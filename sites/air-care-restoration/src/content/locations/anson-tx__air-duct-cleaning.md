## Why Anson Properties See Duct Buildup Faster Than You'd Expect

Anson sits in a semi-arid stretch of West Texas where the soil is light, the wind is persistent, and seasonal agriculture stirs up particulate matter that urban HVAC systems rarely encounter. During cotton harvest, the air carries fiber and dust that infiltrates return-air vents — especially on single-story ranch-style homes with low rooflines and returns positioned near exterior walls. Those returns draw from the dirtiest layer of air in and around the house.

Anson's housing stock skews toward mid-century construction — homes built in the 1950s through 1970s with original sheet-metal ductwork that was sized for older, less efficient equipment. That older ductwork tends to have more joints, more flex-connector transitions, and more surface area where dust and debris can cling. When insulation inside older ducts degrades, it also sheds fiberglass particles into the airstream. Cleaning those systems requires more passes and more careful inspection than a newer, tighter duct layout.

Summer temperatures in Jones County regularly push past 100°F, which means air conditioning runs long cycles — sometimes continuously during July and August. Long run times mean more air movement through the duct system, which means more opportunity for particulate to accumulate on duct walls, coil surfaces, and blower components.

## Our Air Duct Cleaning Process in Anson

Every job starts with a camera inspection of the main trunk lines and branch runs. That footage tells us what we're dealing with before a single tool goes into the system — whether it's a light layer of dust, compacted debris near a return plenum, or evidence of pest intrusion that requires a different approach entirely.

From there, we use negative-pressure extraction: a high-powered vacuum unit is connected to the main trunk, creating suction throughout the system, while technicians work register by register with agitation tools that dislodge buildup from duct walls. The debris travels toward the vacuum rather than back into the living space. For systems with visible microbial growth or persistent odor — common after a roof leak or a period of high indoor humidity — we apply an EPA-registered sanitizing agent to duct surfaces after extraction is complete.

Dryer vent cleaning is often scheduled alongside duct cleaning in Anson because the same conditions that load HVAC ducts also load dryer exhaust lines. A partially blocked dryer vent is a fire hazard regardless of how new the appliance is, and it's a quick addition to any service visit.

One thing that comes up specifically in Anson and the surrounding Jones County area: homes that were built with evaporative coolers and later converted to refrigerated air often have ductwork that was never designed for the static pressure of a modern central system.
