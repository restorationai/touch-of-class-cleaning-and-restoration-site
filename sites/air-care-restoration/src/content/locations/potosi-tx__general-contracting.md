## Why Potosi Properties Present Unique Renovation Challenges

The Taylor County area around Potosi is dominated by homes built on post-tension or conventional slab foundations over expansive clay soils. When those soils dry and shrink, or absorb moisture unevenly after a plumbing leak or a hard rain, the slab moves. That movement shows up as sticking doors, diagonal cracks running from window corners, and tile grout that pops loose. A kitchen remodel or bathroom renovation in this environment is not just a cosmetic project. Before new cabinetry or tile goes in, the underlying structure needs to be assessed and, if necessary, stabilized. Skipping that step means the finished work fails faster than it should.

The region also sees hail seasons that can compromise roofing, siding, and window seals in a single storm. Post-damage rebuilds that follow a hail or wind event often uncover deferred maintenance underneath, including outdated wiring, galvanized supply lines, or insulation that was never adequate for West Texas temperature swings. Addressing those items during the rebuild rather than patching around them is almost always the more cost-effective path.

## Local Note

Homes in the Potosi area that were built in the 1970s and 1980s frequently used aluminum wiring for branch circuits, a common practice during that era when copper prices spiked. Aluminum wiring is not inherently dangerous, but it requires specific connectors and outlet devices rated for aluminum conductors. When a kitchen or bathroom remodel opens up walls in one of these homes, the electrician on the project needs to identify the wiring type before adding any new circuits or devices. Missing this detail during a remodel creates a fire risk that a standard inspection may not catch until the work is already closed in. It is a detail that comes up often in West Texas homes of this vintage, and it is worth asking about before demolition starts.
