## Why Rotan Properties See Sewage Backup Problems

Fisher County sits in a stretch of West Texas where the soil profile swings between cracked, dry hardpan in drought and near-impermeable clay after rain — neither condition is kind to aging sewer infrastructure. When the ground heaves and contracts through seasonal cycles, underground lateral lines shift. Joints separate. Root intrusion finds its way in. Older homes in Rotan — many built during the mid-20th century oil and agricultural boom — often have cast iron or Orangeburg sewer laterals that have long exceeded their design life. These materials corrode, collapse, or deform in ways that PVC doesn't, and the result is a sewer line backup that can push raw sewage into bathrooms, utility rooms, and crawl spaces before a homeowner realizes what's happening.

Septic systems on rural parcels outside the city's sewer service area face a different but related problem: when the caliche-heavy soil around a drain field stops percolating — especially after a wet period following a long dry stretch — effluent backs up into the tank and eventually into the home. That kind of septic overflow carries the same Category 3 contamination risk as a municipal sewer backup, and it requires the same level of response.

## Our Sewage Cleanup and Sanitization Process in Rotan

Raw sewage is classified as Category 3 water — the highest contamination tier — which means the cleanup protocol is more involved than a standard water loss. The process Air Care Restoration follows is structured around IICRC guidelines for contaminated water losses and includes:

- **Containment**: Isolating the affected area to prevent cross-contamination into clean spaces, particularly important in open-plan homes where sewage has spread across a slab floor.
- **Extraction and removal**: Pumping standing sewage and removing unsalvageable porous materials — flooring, baseboards, drywall — that cannot be adequately sanitized. In Rotan's older housing stock, this sometimes includes original linoleum over concrete that has wicked contamination into the adhesive layer beneath.
- **Antimicrobial treatment**: Applying EPA-registered disinfectants to all affected structural surfaces. This step addresses bacteria, viruses, and other pathogens present in raw sewage.
- **Drying and dehumidification**: Deploying commercial drying equipment calibrated to the ambient conditions. West Texas summers push indoor humidity in unexpected directions when exterior heat drives moisture through slab edges — equipment placement accounts for that.
- **Post-treatment verification**: Confirming that moisture readings and surface conditions meet restoration standards before any rebuild begins.
