## Why Tye Properties See Flood Damage

Much of Tye sits on expansive clay soil typical of the Big Country region, the kind that swells when saturated and shrinks hard when it dries. That cycle stresses foundations and slab joints over time, which is why flood intrusion here often isn't limited to a single storm event. A hard rain along the I-20 corridor can send runoff pooling against homes with older or undersized drainage, and once water finds a gap in a slab expansion joint or a low point in grading, it tends to keep returning after every heavy rain until the underlying path is addressed, not just the visible water. Homes built before modern vapor barrier standards are especially prone to slow subfloor saturation that isn't obvious from the surface.

## Our Flood Restoration Process in Tye

The first visit focuses on stopping active intrusion and mapping how far moisture has traveled, since Tye's soil conditions mean water often spreads farther laterally than the visible wet line suggests. We pull moisture readings from baseboards, subfloor, and wall cavities rather than relying on what looks dry at the surface, because a wall can read dry at eye level while the plate and insulation below stay wet for days. Extraction equipment removes standing water first, then air movers and dehumidifiers are set according to the IICRC S500 drying standard, with daily monitoring so equipment placement can be adjusted as materials give up moisture at different rates. Where flooring or drywall can't be saved, we document everything with photos and moisture logs before removal, both for the rebuild and for the insurance file.
