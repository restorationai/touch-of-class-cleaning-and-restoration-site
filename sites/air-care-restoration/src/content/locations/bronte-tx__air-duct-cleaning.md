Bronte sits in the heart of Coke County, where West Texas wind pushes fine caliche dust and rangeland particulates through every gap it can find — including the return air vents of your home's HVAC system. That dust doesn't just settle on surfaces; it packs into duct lining, coats blower wheels, and recirculates every time the system kicks on. If your filters are doing their job but the air still smells stale or your registers leave a gritty film on nearby furniture, the ductwork itself is likely the source.

## Why Bronte Properties See Duct Buildup Faster Than You'd Expect

The climate here is no secret to anyone who's lived through a Coke County summer: long stretches of heat, low humidity, and persistent wind. That combination means HVAC systems run hard for months at a time, pulling air — and whatever's suspended in it — through ductwork at high volume. Homes built on the rolling terrain around Bronte often have crawl spaces or pier-and-beam foundations that are difficult to fully seal, giving dust, pollen, and occasionally rodent debris a direct path into the duct system from below.

Older ranch-style homes in this part of West Texas were frequently built with flexible duct runs that develop small tears or disconnections over time, especially after temperature swings that cause materials to expand and contract repeatedly across seasons. A disconnected duct section doesn't just reduce efficiency — it actively pulls unconditioned air, insulation fibers, and attic dust into the supply stream before it reaches your living space.

## Our Air Duct Cleaning Process in Bronte

Before any equipment goes into a duct opening, the process starts with a visual inspection — checking for disconnected sections, signs of moisture intrusion, and the type and condition of duct lining. In West Texas homes, fiberglass-lined flex duct is common, and it requires a gentler approach than sheet metal: high-pressure agitation can damage the lining and release fibers into the airstream, so brush speed and vacuum pressure are calibrated accordingly.

Once the system is mapped, a truck-mounted or portable negative-air machine is connected to the main trunk line to create continuous negative pressure throughout the system. Rotating brushes and compressed-air whips work from each register back toward the collection point, dislodging debris without pushing it deeper. Supply and return sides are cleaned separately. If sanitization is requested — useful after rodent activity or prolonged moisture — an EPA-registered antimicrobial agent is applied by fogging after the mechanical cleaning is complete, not before.

Dryer vent cleaning follows the same appointment when requested. In single-story homes common to this area, dryer vents often run a short horizontal distance through an exterior wall, but lint buildup still occurs at elbows and at the exterior termination cap, which can become partially blocked by wind-driven debris.
