## Why Albany Properties See Water Damage Issues

Albany sits in a stretch of West Texas where the soil shifts between sandy loam and expansive clay depending on how close you are to the Clear Fork of the Brazos. That clay content matters: when it's dry, it pulls away from foundation slabs and pier-and-beam footings, opening gaps where water migrates during a hard rain. When it saturates quickly — which it does during the region's periodic flash-flood events — it holds moisture against foundations longer than sandy soil would, keeping concrete and wood framing damp well after the storm passes.

The housing stock in Albany skews older. Many homes were built in the mid-twentieth century with pier-and-beam construction, wood subfloors, and plumbing that has been repaired and re-routed multiple times over the decades. Galvanized supply lines common in that era corrode from the inside out, often failing without visible warning. When they go, water travels under flooring and into wall cavities before the surface shows any sign of a problem. A moisture meter reads wet long after the floor feels dry underfoot.

The town's position in a semi-arid climate also creates a deceptive drying environment. Low ambient humidity makes rooms feel dry quickly, which can lead homeowners to believe a water event is resolved when structural materials are still holding significant moisture. That residual saturation is where mold colonization begins — typically within 24 to 48 hours of initial exposure under warm conditions.

## Our Water Damage Restoration Process in Albany

Every job starts with a full moisture assessment using thermal imaging and calibrated meters — not a visual walkthrough. Water follows the path of least resistance, and in Albany's older pier-and-beam homes that often means it has traveled further than the visible damage suggests, pooling in crawl spaces or wicking up into wall framing from below.

Once we map the moisture, we extract standing water with truck-mounted and portable extraction units, then set industrial desiccant dehumidifiers and high-velocity air movers calibrated to the actual square footage and material types present. Drying in Albany's climate requires balancing the naturally low outdoor humidity (which can help) against the moisture trapped inside wall assemblies and subfloor systems (which resists evaporation). We monitor drying progress with daily readings and adjust equipment placement until materials reach target moisture content per the IICRC S500 standard.

If building materials — drywall, insulation, subfloor panels — are saturated beyond the threshold for drying in place, we remove them cleanly and document the scope for your insurance claim. We photograph affected areas before, during, and after each phase.
