## Why Clyde Properties See Flood Damage

Callahan County sits on heavy clay that swells when saturated and shrinks when it dries, which is part of why so many homes in and around Clyde were built on pier-and-beam foundations rather than poured slabs. That foundation style breathes well in normal weather, but during a flash flood event it turns into a reservoir. Water pools under the house, wicks into floor joists and subfloor insulation, and keeps the crawlspace damp for days after the yard has drained. Older homes near the creek and lower-lying stretches of town are especially exposed, and even newer construction isn't immune when drainage grading hasn't kept pace with runoff from surrounding pasture and farmland.

## Our Flood Restoration Process in Clyde

We start with extraction, pulling standing water from floors, crawlspaces, and wall cavities before it has a chance to migrate further into framing. From there we map moisture with meters and thermal imaging, because in a pier-and-beam home the surface can look dry while the joists above the crawlspace are still saturated. Drying follows the IICRC S500 standard: air movers and dehumidifiers sized to the affected square footage, with daily monitoring until moisture content in wood framing and subfloor returns to a stable range. Only after materials read dry do we move into repairs, whether that's replacing insulation batts that never recover once wet or addressing drywall that wicked water above the baseboard line.

Flood claims in a small Texas town often move differently than they do in a big metro, partly because adjusters covering rural Callahan County territory may be juggling several counties at once.
