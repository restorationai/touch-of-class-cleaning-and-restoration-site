That combination of intense heat, low humidity, and the fine particulate dust that blows in off Taylor County's open range means the ductwork inside Potosi homes works harder than most systems in the region, and collects a distinctive mix of caliche dust, pollen, and allergens that a standard filter alone cannot address. ## Why Potosi Properties See Duct Buildup Faster Than You'd Expect

The West Texas climate is the primary driver. When outdoor air is consistently dry and windy, fine soil particles find their way into return-air pathways through gaps around filter housings, at duct joints in unconditioned attic spaces, and through the crawlspace connections common in older slab-edge construction. Once inside the duct system, that particulate clings to the interior lining and, during the brief humid stretches Potosi does get in late spring and early fall, creates conditions where biological growth can take hold on accumulated debris.

Homes in the Potosi area also tend to run their HVAC systems for long cooling seasons that stretch from late April well into October. Extended run times mean more air cycling through the same ductwork, more opportunity for debris to accumulate on coil surfaces and in trunk lines, and more strain on blower motors that are already working against high outdoor temperatures. If the home has flex duct in the attic, common in builds from the 1990s and 2000s, kinks and sags in that flex create low spots where dust settles and moisture can pool during the system's off cycles.

## Our Air Duct Cleaning Process in Potosi

The process begins with a camera inspection of the main trunk lines and a representative sample of branch runs. This step matters in Potosi because attic duct systems here are often subjected to extreme temperature swings, well above 130°F in a sealed attic on a July afternoon, which accelerates the breakdown of duct tape mastic and foil facings. Knowing what we're working with before we start determines whether a section needs sealing or replacement rather than cleaning alone.

Once the system is mapped, we connect a high-powered negative-pressure collection unit to the main trunk and work each branch line with agitation tools designed to dislodge compacted debris without tearing flex duct liners. All dislodged material is captured in the collection unit rather than redistributed into the living space. After mechanical cleaning, we apply an EPA-registered sanitizing agent to interior duct surfaces where biological growth or heavy organic debris was present. The process concludes with a post-cleaning airflow check at each register to confirm the system is moving air the way it should.

## Equipment and Methods We Use for Air Duct Cleaning

The core tool is a truck-mounted or trailer-mounted negative-air collection system capable of maintaining continuous negative pressure throughout the duct network while agitation tools work each branch. For Potosi homes with flex duct, we use soft-bristle whip attachments rather than rigid brush heads to avoid perforating the inner liner, a real risk in flex duct that has become brittle after years in a superheated attic. Dryer vent cleaning is handled with a separate rotary brush system and a high-velocity blower to clear lint accumulation from the full run of the vent, including any exterior termination that may have a screen or louver collecting debris.

One pattern worth knowing in Potosi: homes built on the eastern edge of the community, closer to the transition toward established Abilene subdivisions, often have original builder-grade duct systems with undersized return-air chases. A visual inspection of the return-air side almost always reveals more than the supply side alone.
