## Why Anson Properties Face Particular Fire Damage Challenges

Jones County's housing stock skews older, and many homes in Anson were built during the mid-twentieth-century agricultural boom when balloon-frame and pier-and-beam construction were the norm. Those framing styles create open wall cavities that act like chimneys during a structure fire — smoke and combustion gases travel vertically through the wall system and deposit soot in attic spaces and subflooring long before the fire department's report is written. A surface that looks lightly affected at the ceiling line may have heavy char residue inside the wall cavity two feet above it.

Anson's predominantly rural and semi-rural lots also mean that many properties rely on propane rather than natural gas, and propane burns hotter and produces a denser, oilier soot than natural gas does. That oily residue bonds to painted drywall, cabinetry, and HVAC ductwork in a way that standard cleaning agents won't cut — it requires chemical sponges, alkaline cleaners, and in some cases thermal fogging to neutralize odor at the molecular level.

## Our Fire Damage Restoration Process in Anson

Every fire loss is different, but the sequence matters. When we arrive, the first priority is safety assessment — confirming structural integrity, identifying compromised electrical panels, and flagging any remaining hot spots that could reignite in the low-humidity conditions typical of West Texas summers. We document everything photographically before a single item is moved, which protects your insurance claim and establishes a clear pre-remediation baseline.

From there, the process moves through board-up and tarping to secure the structure, followed by controlled demolition of unsalvageable materials. Smoke-affected surfaces that can be cleaned receive dry-chemical sponge treatment first — wiping wet before dry is one of the most common mistakes in fire cleanup, and it permanently sets soot into porous surfaces. Odor neutralization follows, using hydroxyl generators or thermal fogging depending on the material profile of the home. Structural drying runs concurrently if suppression water is present, since the IICRC S500 standard requires moisture documentation before any encapsulation or rebuild begins.
