## Why Tuscola Properties See Vandalism Damage Differently

The building stock in and around Tuscola leans toward materials that punish delayed cleanup. Concrete block, painted cinder block, and older brick facades are common on commercial and agricultural structures throughout Taylor County. These surfaces are highly porous, meaning spray paint and marker ink migrate below the surface layer within hours of application, especially under summer heat that regularly pushes past 100 degrees. A tag that looks shallow on day one can require aggressive chemical treatment or even light abrasive blasting by day three.

Residential properties in the area often feature stucco exteriors or painted wood siding, both of which absorb solvent-based aerosols differently than the smooth surfaces vandals usually prefer. Stucco in particular traps pigment in its texture, requiring a different chemical profile than you would use on glass or metal. Broken window glass cleanup also carries a specific hazard on properties with older single-pane windows, which can shatter into larger, sharper fragments than modern tempered glazing.

## Our Vandalism Cleanup Process in Tuscola

Every job starts with a surface-by-surface assessment before any product touches the property. Graffiti removal chemistry is not one-size-fits-all: the solvent that lifts latex aerosol from a painted concrete wall can damage a powder-coated metal gate or strip a wood stain entirely. We identify the substrate and the type of marking first, then select the appropriate remover, from water-based citrus degreasers for lighter tags to professional-grade gel strippers for heavy enamel applications.

For broken glass, the process goes beyond sweeping. Fine glass dust and micro-fragments embed in carpet fibers, settle into HVAC return vents, and collect in door tracks. We use HEPA-filtered vacuums and systematic surface wipes to clear fragments from areas that look clean but are not. Where a window or door frame has been damaged, we document the structural condition so your insurance carrier has a complete picture of the loss.

If the vandalism involved any forced entry, we also check adjacent surfaces for secondary damage, including door jamb splitting, frame warping, or lock hardware that is compromised but not obviously broken.
