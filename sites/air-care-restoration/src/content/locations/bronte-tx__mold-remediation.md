## Why Bronte Properties See Mold Issues

The West Texas climate feels arid most of the year, but that dryness is deceptive. When moisture does arrive — a roof breach during a hailstorm, a slow supply-line leak under a kitchen cabinet, a swamp cooler that runs condensation into an attic space — the temperature swings between day and night create an on-again, off-again humidity cycle inside wall assemblies. Mold doesn't need standing water to colonize; it needs relative humidity above roughly 60 percent sustained for 24 to 48 hours. In a masonry or adobe-influenced home common to this part of Coke County, thick walls hold that humidity long after the surface feels dry to the touch.

Older homes in the Bronte area — many built during the mid-twentieth century ranching boom — often have original cellulose insulation, wood lath behind plaster, and pier-and-beam foundations with limited vapor barriers. Each of those materials is highly hospitable to mold spores. A pinhole leak in galvanized plumbing that goes unnoticed for a few weeks can saturate lath and subfloor well beyond what a surface inspection reveals.

## Our Mold Remediation Process in Bronte

When we arrive at a Bronte property, the first step is containment — not cleaning. Disturbing mold colonies without isolating the work area spreads spores through the HVAC system and into unaffected rooms. We establish negative air pressure in the affected zone using HEPA-filtered air scrubbers, seal doorways and duct registers, and then begin a methodical inspection using moisture meters and thermal imaging to map the full extent of the damage before any material is removed.

Remediation follows the IICRC S520 standard for mold: controlled removal of contaminated porous materials, HEPA vacuuming of structural surfaces, antimicrobial treatment of framing and masonry, and a clearance inspection before containment comes down. In Bronte's older masonry structures, we pay particular attention to the mortar joints and the interior face of exterior walls — both tend to wick and hold moisture in ways that standard drywall construction does not. As an IICRC Certified Firm with EPA Lead-Safe certification, we follow lead-safe work practices on any pre-1978 structure, which covers a significant portion of the housing stock in Coke County.

## Local Note

In Coke County's older ranch homes, evaporative (swamp) coolers are still common — and they're one of the more overlooked mold contributors in the region. A cooler that's slightly out of level, or whose water distribution pads haven't been replaced in a season or two, can push a fine mist of moisture into the attic or wall cavity around the unit's penetration point. By the time the smell becomes noticeable inside the home, the framing around that opening has often been colonized for months. If your home uses an evaporative cooler and you're investigating a mold concern, that penetration point is one of the first places we check.
