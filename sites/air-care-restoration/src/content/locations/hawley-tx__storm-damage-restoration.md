## Why Hawley Properties Are Vulnerable to Storm Damage

Hawley sits on the rolling plains of Jones County, where the landscape offers little in the way of natural windbreaks. Storms that build over the Caprock Escarpment to the west can accelerate as they descend toward lower elevations, arriving in Hawley with sustained winds that strip roofing materials, shatter windows, and drive rain horizontally into wall cavities. The soil in this part of West Texas is predominantly clay-heavy, which means it swells when saturated and drains slowly. After a heavy storm event, water that pools against a foundation doesn't just evaporate quickly in the summer heat. It sits, it seeps, and it finds every crack in a slab or pier-and-beam footing.

Hawley's housing stock skews older, with many homes built during the mid-twentieth century using construction methods that predate modern moisture barriers and impact-resistant roofing standards. Composition shingles from that era, if they haven't been replaced, can fail in a single hail event. Once the roof deck is exposed, interior damage follows within hours, especially during the spring storm season when rain is most likely to accompany the wind.

## Our Storm Damage Restoration Process in Hawley

When we arrive on site, the first priority is emergency stabilization: tarping compromised roof sections, boarding broken windows, and extracting any standing water before it migrates into subfloor assemblies or wall framing. We use moisture meters and thermal imaging cameras to map where water has traveled, because visible wet areas rarely tell the whole story. Water wicks through insulation and behind drywall, and in an older home with dense plaster walls, it can read dry at the surface while the lath behind it stays saturated for days.

Once the structure is stabilized, we document the full scope of damage with photographs and written assessments calibrated for insurance carrier review. Structural drying follows using commercial-grade air movers and dehumidifiers, with daily moisture readings to confirm that drying is progressing to IICRC S500 standards. If roof or siding repairs fall within our scope, we coordinate those alongside interior restoration so the building envelope is closed before interior finishes are addressed. The goal is a single, coordinated project rather than a handoff between multiple contractors.
