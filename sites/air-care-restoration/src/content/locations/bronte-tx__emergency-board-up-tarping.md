## Our Emergency Board-Up and Tarping Process in Bronte

When we arrive on site, the first priority is a rapid exterior assessment — identifying every breach point before any material is staged. That means walking the full roofline, checking fascia and soffit for hidden uplift, and noting any compromised load-bearing elements that would affect how tarps are anchored. In Bronte's wind environment, a tarp secured only at its edges will fail; we use ballasted anchor systems and ridge-cap attachment methods designed to hold through continued gusting, not just calm conditions.

For window and door board-up, we cut plywood to fit each opening precisely rather than relying on generic panel sizes. Gaps at the edges are the most common failure point — they let wind pressure build behind the board and pop it free. On older wood-frame homes common to this part of Coke County, we also inspect the surrounding framing before fastening, since deteriorated window headers can make a standard screw pattern inadequate.

Every job is documented with timestamped photographs of each opening before and after securing — a record that matters when your insurance adjuster is reviewing the scope of emergency mitigation.

## Local Note

One thing worth knowing about Bronte and the broader Coke County area: the combination of caliche soil and the region's dramatic temperature swings means that foundation movement is a recurring issue in older homes.
