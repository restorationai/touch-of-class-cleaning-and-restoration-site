## Why Robert Lee Properties See Unique Contents Damage Patterns

Robert Lee sits in a semi-arid stretch of the Rolling Plains where temperature swings between seasons are dramatic. Homes here commonly feature pier-and-beam or slab-on-grade construction, and many of the older residences in town were built with wood-framed walls and minimal vapor barriers, a combination that lets fire residue and water migrate into wall cavities and onto stored contents faster than in more tightly sealed modern builds.

The region's caliche-heavy soil also affects drainage. When a roof leak or appliance failure sends water across a floor, it tends to pool rather than dissipate, soaking into rugs, upholstered furniture legs, and the lower shelves of bookshelves or cabinetry before anyone realizes the extent of the spread. Contents sitting on flooring in these situations absorb moisture from below as well as above, which complicates drying and often requires full pack-out rather than in-place treatment.

Summer dust storms, common across Coke County, deposit fine particulate into homes through gaps around windows and doors. After a fire, that same particulate mixes with soot and smoke residue, embedding deeper into textiles and making odor neutralization a more layered process than it would be in a climate with less airborne sediment.

## Our Contents Restoration Process in Robert Lee

Every contents job begins with a detailed inventory before anything leaves your home. Each item is photographed, catalogued, and assigned a condition rating. That documentation matters for your insurance claim and gives you a clear record of what was taken and what condition it was in.

Items that can be treated on-site are handled first. Furniture, appliances, and hard goods that are structurally sound often respond well to ultrasonic cleaning, dry-ice blasting for soot, or ozone and hydroxyl treatment for odor. Textiles, documents, and items with significant smoke or water penetration are packed out to a climate-controlled storage environment where temperature and humidity are held steady regardless of what the West Texas weather is doing outside.

Our team is IICRC certified, and we follow the IICRC S500 and FSRT standards for water and fire/smoke damage respectively. Delicate items, including artwork, electronics, and documents, are handled under separate protocols. Electronics are assessed for corrosion before any power is applied. Documents can go through freeze-drying if water saturation is severe enough to warrant it.

Once your structure is dried, repaired, and cleared, we coordinate the return delivery and place items back in their original locations using the inventory photos as a guide.

## Local Note

One thing that catches homeowners off guard in Robert Lee and the surrounding Coke County area: the combination of low ambient humidity and high summer heat means smoke odors that seem to have cleared can return weeks later when temperatures drop and the home is closed up again. Odor molecules that off-gassed into wall cavities and subflooring during the hot months get trapped as the structure cools and contracts. If you're noticing a recurring smoke smell after a fire loss, it's rarely a sign that restoration was incomplete, it's a regional pattern tied to how these older homes breathe across seasons. Addressing it usually means a second hydroxyl or ozone treatment cycle rather than a full re-remediation.
