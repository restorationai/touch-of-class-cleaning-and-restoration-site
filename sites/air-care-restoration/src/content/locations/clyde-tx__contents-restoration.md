## Why Clyde Properties See Contents Restoration Challenges

Callahan County sits in a climate band that swings hard — dry, windy summers that accelerate mold dormancy, then sudden thunderstorm cells that can push water through roof penetrations or overwhelm older drainage around slab foundations. Clyde's housing stock includes a meaningful share of homes built in the mid-twentieth century, when interior finishes leaned toward natural wood paneling, plaster-and-lath walls, and upholstered furniture built to last decades. Those materials hold smoke odor and moisture differently than modern composites: wood paneling absorbs soot into its grain, and older fabric upholstery can trap particulates deep in the batting where surface cleaning never reaches.

That combination — a climate prone to sudden moisture events and a housing stock full of porous, absorbent materials — means contents losses in Clyde tend to be more involved than a quick wipe-down. Belongings often need to leave the property entirely so the structure can be dried and treated without re-contaminating freshly cleaned items sitting in the same rooms.

## Our Contents Restoration and Storage Process in Clyde

The first step is a room-by-room pack-out inventory. Every item is photographed, tagged, and logged before it leaves the property — a record that matters both for your insurance claim and for returning everything to the right room when the job is done. Items are sorted on-site: things that can be restored travel to our climate-controlled facility; things that are a total loss are documented so your adjuster has the evidence they need.

At the facility, contents go through the appropriate cleaning track:

- **Smoke and soot:** Dry-sponge treatment first to lift loose carbon, followed by ultrasonic cleaning for hard goods and thermal fogging or ozone treatment for soft goods and upholstered furniture.
- **Water damage:** Controlled drying in a low-humidity environment, with mold-inhibiting treatment applied to any item that sat wet for more than 24 to 48 hours — the window in which mold colonization typically begins.
- **Odor:** Hydroxyl generation or ozone chambers depending on material sensitivity. Ozone is effective but off-gasses in ways that require items to air out before return; hydroxyl works continuously and is safe around most materials.

Once the structure is cleared, items are returned, unwrapped, and placed back in their documented positions.
