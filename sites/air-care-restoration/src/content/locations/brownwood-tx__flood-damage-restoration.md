## Why Brownwood Properties See Flood Damage Issues

Much of Brownwood sits in the Pecan Bayou watershed, and the same clay soils that make Central Texas hard on foundations during drought turn the opposite direction during heavy rain: they saturate fast, drain slowly, and push water laterally toward slabs instead of letting it soak straight down. Older homes near downtown and the original townsite, many built well before modern vapor barrier requirements, sit directly on grade with little separation from wet soil after a storm. Add in aging drainage along some of the city's older residential streets, and water that should sheet off toward the bayou instead pools against foundations for hours. When Lake Brownwood releases water upstream during heavy rain events, Pecan Bayou can rise quickly even after the rain itself has stopped, which catches some property owners off guard days after the storm has already passed.

## Our Flood Cleanup Process in Brownwood

The first visit is about getting water out and stopping the clock on secondary damage, not paperwork. Crews extract standing water, pull baseboards and flooring where needed to check for trapped moisture, and use moisture meters to map how far water has traveled into slab, subfloor, and framing. Because Brownwood's clay soils hold groundwater near the surface longer after a flood event, we monitor slab moisture over several days instead of assuming a single reading tells the whole story. Drying follows the IICRC S500 standard, with air movers and dehumidifiers sized to the structure rather than dropped in and left to guess. In homes built before 1978, which still make up a meaningful share of Brownwood's older neighborhoods, any disturbed painted surfaces are handled under EPA Lead-Safe practices before demolition or repair work begins.

## Local note

One thing that trips up flood cleanup in Brownwood: clay soil around a slab doesn't just cause the intrusion, it complicates the fix. As the ground dries out over the following weeks, clay can shrink and pull away from the foundation, sometimes opening small gaps that weren't there during the flood itself. We flag this for homeowners during the final walkthrough so a new crack or gap that shows up a month later isn't mistaken for a new leak.
