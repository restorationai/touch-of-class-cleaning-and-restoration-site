## Why Colorado City Properties Face Elevated Asbestos Risk

Mitchell County's economy rode the Permian Basin oil industry for much of the twentieth century, and that prosperity funded a wave of residential and commercial construction from the 1940s through the early 1980s — precisely the window when asbestos-containing materials (ACMs) were most widely used. Textured "popcorn" ceilings applied before the late 1970s frequently contain chrysotile asbestos. Floor tiles, particularly the 9×9-inch vinyl squares common in mid-century Texas homes, were routinely manufactured with asbestos binders. Pipe insulation, duct wrap, roofing felt, and joint compound in homes built before 1980 are all suspect materials that require testing before any disturbance.

The West Texas climate adds another layer of concern. Extreme temperature swings between summer highs and winter cold fronts cause building materials to expand and contract repeatedly over decades. That cycling can crack and friabilize materials that were once intact — turning a previously stable asbestos-containing floor tile or ceiling texture into a source of airborne fiber release the moment a contractor's tool touches it.

## Our Asbestos Abatement Process in Colorado City

Every project begins with a thorough inspection and bulk sampling of suspect materials. Samples are submitted to an accredited laboratory, and no abatement work begins until results are confirmed. This sequence matters in Colorado City because older properties here often contain multiple ACM types layered across renovation eras — a 1955 home that was updated in 1972 and again in 1988 may have three distinct generations of materials, each requiring its own assessment.

Once testing confirms the presence of asbestos, the work follows a defined protocol:

- **Containment**: Affected areas are isolated with negative-air pressure enclosures and polyethylene sheeting to prevent fiber migration into clean spaces.
- **Wet removal**: ACMs are wetted before and during removal to suppress fiber release — a step that's especially important in Colorado City's dry, windy conditions, where disturbed fibers can travel quickly through an unsealed structure.
- **HEPA filtration**: Air scrubbers with HEPA filtration run continuously throughout the project.
- **Waste disposal**: All asbestos waste is double-bagged, labeled, and transported to a licensed disposal facility in compliance with Texas Commission on Environmental Quality (TCEQ) requirements.
- **Air clearance**: Post-abatement air sampling confirms the space is safe before containment is removed.

The team is OSHA trained and EPA Lead-Safe Certified, which matters when older Colorado City properties combine asbestos with lead-based paint — a common pairing in pre-1978 construction.
