## Why Brownwood Properties See Water Damage Issues

The soil across much of Brown County is a heavy clay mix that sheds water rather than absorbing it. During a fast-moving storm, that clay sheds runoff toward foundations, crawl spaces, and low-lying slabs before drainage infrastructure can keep up. Homes built in the mid-20th century — a significant portion of Brownwood's residential stock — were often constructed on pier-and-beam foundations, which can collect standing water underneath the structure long after the rain stops. That standing water wicks upward into subfloor sheathing and floor joists, and because the space is enclosed and poorly ventilated, drying without mechanical intervention is essentially impossible.

Brownwood also sits within a region prone to hard freezes during winter cold snaps. Older homes with supply lines running through exterior walls or uninsulated crawl spaces are vulnerable to burst pipes — a loss type that can release hundreds of gallons before the shutoff is located. The combination of storm-driven intrusion and freeze-related pipe failures means water damage here isn't a single-season problem; it's a year-round risk.

## Our Water Damage Restoration Process in Brownwood

Every job starts with moisture mapping. Technicians use thermal imaging cameras and calibrated moisture meters to trace exactly where water has traveled — including areas that look dry at the surface but are holding moisture inside wall assemblies or below flooring. In Brownwood's older pier-and-beam homes, that step often reveals saturation in subfloor materials that a visual inspection would miss entirely.

Once the scope is documented, water removal begins using truck-mounted extraction units capable of pulling standing water from hard floors, carpet, and crawl space voids. After extraction, the drying phase follows the IICRC S500 standard: a calculated combination of commercial air movers and low-grain refrigerant dehumidifiers sized to the actual affected volume, not a one-size estimate. Moisture readings are logged daily so the drying curve is tracked and documented — data that also matters when an insurance adjuster reviews the claim.

If building materials have absorbed enough water to require removal — saturated drywall, buckled hardwood, compromised insulation — that work is completed before drying equipment is set, not after. Trapping moisture behind intact surfaces is one of the most common mistakes in DIY or underprepared water cleanup, and it creates the conditions for mold colonization within 24 to 48 hours of initial saturation.

Most homeowner policies cover sudden and accidental water losses — burst pipes, appliance failures, storm-driven intrusion through a compromised roof or window.
