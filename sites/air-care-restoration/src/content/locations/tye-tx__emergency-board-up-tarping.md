## Why Tye Properties Are Particularly Vulnerable After a Storm

Tye sits in Taylor County along the US-83/84 corridor, where the flat, open terrain gives little natural windbreak. Storms that build over the Llano Estacado to the northwest funnel straight through this stretch with little to slow them down. That translates into wind-driven hail that can puncture roof decking in minutes, and straight-line winds strong enough to push in older single-pane windows or lift soffit panels entirely.

The housing stock in the Tye area skews toward ranch-style and pier-and-beam construction built in the mid-to-late twentieth century. Older roof systems in this region often used thinner decking and lighter felt underlayment than modern builds, which means even a moderate hail event can leave gaps that a standard tarp needs to bridge quickly. Fire damage is another common driver — a kitchen fire or electrical event that vents through a gable end leaves the attic fully exposed, and in West Texas, a dry wind through an open attic cavity will desiccate structural members fast while also carrying embers to neighboring properties.

## Our Emergency Board-Up and Tarping Process in Tye

When you call, the first thing we do is ask two questions: what type of opening and roughly how large. That lets the crew load the right materials before leaving — 3/4-inch OSB for window and door board-ups, 6-mil polyethylene tarps in the appropriate dimensions for roof spans, and the fastening hardware to anchor both against West Texas gusts rather than just drape them in place.

On arrival, the crew walks the exterior perimeter before touching anything. Hail events and fire damage often create secondary hazards — compromised fascia, unstable chimney brick, or downed utility lines near the structure. Once the perimeter is clear, window and door openings get framed with dimensional lumber and sheathed with OSB, secured to the surrounding framing rather than just the window casing. Roof tarps are run past the ridge when possible and weighted or screwed at the edges so they shed water rather than pool it. Every opening gets photographed before and after — documentation that matters when your adjuster opens the claim file.

As an IICRC Certified Firm with IICRC FSRT (Fire & Smoke) certification, the team also notes any visible smoke or water intrusion during the board-up walk-through, which gives you a head start on the restoration scope before the adjuster visits.
