## Why Robert Lee Properties See Elevated Duct Contamination

Coke County sits in a semi-arid zone where annual rainfall averages well below the state mean, and the soil is a mixture of caliche, sandy loam, and clay that pulverizes easily in dry, windy conditions. During the spring and early summer, sustained southwest winds push particulate matter through even well-sealed building envelopes. Older wood-frame homes — many built during the mid-twentieth century oil and ranching booms — often have original ductwork with joints sealed only by duct tape that has long since dried out and separated. Those gaps act as direct intake points for outdoor dust, pollen, and the occasional insect nest.

Central air conditioning also runs hard here. Robert Lee summers routinely push well past 100°F, which means systems run long daily cycles and pull enormous volumes of air across filters that may not be rated for fine particulate. When filters are overwhelmed or overdue for replacement, the overflow migrates straight into the duct interior. Combine that with the region's seasonal cedar and mesquite pollen loads, and the interior surfaces of a duct system can accumulate a surprisingly dense layer of debris within just a couple of heating and cooling seasons.

## Our Air Duct Cleaning Process in Robert Lee

Before any equipment enters your home, a technician walks the system — locating every supply and return register, identifying the air handler location, and noting any visible flex-duct sections or older sheet-metal runs. In Coke County homes, it is common to find a mix of original rigid ductwork and later flex-duct additions from previous HVAC upgrades, and each type requires a slightly different approach to avoid tearing liner material.

The core cleaning method uses high-powered negative-pressure vacuum equipment paired with agitation tools — rotary brushes and compressed-air whips — that dislodge debris from duct walls before it is captured at the collection point. Every register is sealed off in sequence so suction is concentrated in the active section rather than dispersed across the whole system. After the mechanical cleaning, an EPA-registered antimicrobial sanitizer is available as an add-on for ducts where mold spores or bacterial growth are a concern — a relevant option in any home that has experienced a roof leak or plumbing event, since moisture intrusion in an otherwise dry climate can create an isolated environment where microbial growth thrives.

Dryer vent cleaning is performed as a separate service but is frequently scheduled at the same visit. Lint accumulation in dryer exhaust lines is a leading cause of residential fires nationwide, and in a town the size of Robert Lee, where fire response distances can be significant, keeping that vent clear is a straightforward precaution.
