West Texas storms don't announce themselves politely. In Clyde, the same open-range geography that makes Callahan County's skies so wide also means there's nothing to slow a supercell down before it arrives over your roof. Hail the size of golf balls, straight-line winds that peel back soffit venting, and the occasional tornado touchdown are seasonal realities here — and when a storm passes through, the damage it leaves behind starts compounding within hours, not days.

## Why Clyde Properties Are Particularly Vulnerable to Storm Damage

Clyde sits in a stretch of West Texas where the climate swings hard. Summers bring brutal heat that dries out wood framing and caulk lines, leaving roofing systems brittle before storm season even peaks. When fall and spring storms roll through, those dried-out materials fail faster than they would in a more temperate climate — shingles crack rather than flex, and aged caulk around windows and vents lets wind-driven rain push straight into wall cavities.

The housing stock in Clyde skews toward single-story ranch and pier-and-beam construction, common throughout small Central West Texas towns. Pier-and-beam foundations, while well-suited to the region's shrink-swell clay soils, leave a crawl space that can trap storm-driven moisture and debris. That moisture doesn't always show up at the surface — wood joists and subfloor sheathing can stay wet long after the yard dries out, which is exactly the window when mold colonization begins.

High-wind events also tend to drive debris horizontally, meaning fascia boards, gutters, and exterior HVAC equipment take hits that aren't always visible from the ground. A damaged condenser or a cracked vent cap can let water migrate into the attic or wall system for days before a homeowner notices a stain on the ceiling.

## Our Storm Damage Restoration Process in Clyde

When we arrive at a Clyde property after a storm, the first priority is stopping active water intrusion. That means emergency tarping of any compromised roof sections and boarding window or door openings before any interior work begins. Moisture readings come next — we use thermal imaging alongside pin and pinless moisture meters to map exactly where water has traveled inside wall assemblies, not just where it's visible.

Once the building envelope is stabilized, we document the full scope of damage with photographs and written notes formatted for insurance carrier review. Structural debris is removed, affected materials are catalogued, and drying equipment — industrial air movers and dehumidifiers calibrated to the low-humidity West Texas air — is deployed to bring moisture levels back to acceptable baselines. Because the region's ambient humidity is typically low, evaporation happens quickly at the surface, but dense materials like OSB sheathing and wood framing hold moisture longer and require careful monitoring over multiple days.

Repairs follow only after moisture readings confirm the structure is dry. We work through the sequence methodically: insulation, sheathing, drywall, roofing, and exterior finishes — so nothing gets closed up wet.

## Local Note

One thing that catches Clyde homeowners off guard after a major hail or wind event: the clay-heavy soils common throughout Callahan County absorb standing water slowly, which means water pools against foundations longer than it would in sandier terrain. After a storm that drops significant rain alongside wind damage, it's worth checking the perimeter of the foundation for pooling before it migrates through any existing cracks in the slab or stem wall. We routinely check the foundation perimeter during our initial assessment — not just the roof and walls — because in this soil type, ground-level water intrusion is a secondary damage pathway that's easy to miss until it shows up as efflorescence or a musty smell in the back bedroom weeks later.
