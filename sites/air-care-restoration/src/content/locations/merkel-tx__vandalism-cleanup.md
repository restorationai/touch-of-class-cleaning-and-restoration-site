## Why Merkel Properties See Vandalism Damage Differently

The building stock in and around Merkel leans toward older masonry construction, painted concrete block, and wood-frame structures with chalky or weathered exterior coatings. Those surfaces are particularly unforgiving when it comes to spray paint. Aerosol pigment wicks into the pores of aged concrete and soft brick the way water soaks into a sponge, and the intense West Texas sun accelerates the bond between the paint solvent and the substrate. What might wipe off a sealed modern surface in a downtown metro becomes a staining problem on a 1960s-era block wall in a rural Texas town.

The region's low humidity also matters in a counterintuitive way. Graffiti removal products that rely on moisture to loosen paint work more slowly in arid conditions, so the chemical dwell time and application method have to be adjusted. Using the wrong approach on Merkel's older exterior finishes can etch the surface or lift the base coat, turning a cleanup job into a full repaint. The process has to be calibrated to what the building is actually made of, not just what the can of remover says on the label.

## Our Vandalism Cleanup Process in Merkel

Every job starts with a material assessment before any chemical or abrasive tool touches the surface. Painted brick, raw concrete, stucco, wood siding, and plate glass all require different approaches, and mixing them up causes secondary damage that costs more to fix than the original vandalism.

For graffiti on masonry, we use a tiered method: a low-pressure chemical application matched to the substrate porosity, followed by controlled pressure washing at a volume and angle that clears the solvent without driving pigment deeper into the wall. On wood-frame or vinyl surfaces, we work with gel-based removers that cling to vertical faces without running onto adjacent materials. Broken glass cleanup follows a structured sweep-and-containment protocol, including a final pass with a fine-bristle magnet and tape inspection to catch shards embedded in weatherstripping, door frames, or gravel near the entry.

If the vandalism involved interior access, such as a forced entry through a door or window, we also assess for secondary damage inside: scuffed flooring, contaminated surfaces, or structural compromise at the point of entry. That full-scope look is what separates a cleanup from a restoration.
