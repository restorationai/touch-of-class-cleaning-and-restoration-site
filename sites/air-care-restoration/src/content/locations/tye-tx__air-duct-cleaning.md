That combination means the ductwork inside a Tye home is working harder than almost anywhere in the state, pulling in fine caliche dust and cotton-gin debris along with conditioned air, and packing it into filters, coils, and duct lining faster than most manufacturers' maintenance schedules anticipate. ## Why Tye Properties See Accelerated Duct Buildup

The Taylor County landscape around Tye is flat, dry, and frequently windy. When fields are being worked or when a dust storm rolls through, particulate counts spike dramatically — and residential HVAC systems are running almost continuously during summer months trying to keep up with the heat load. Every hour the system runs, it's drawing that particulate through the return air path.

Homes in this part of West Texas also tend to be slab-on-grade construction, which means ductwork is typically routed through unconditioned attic space. Attic temperatures in Tye can exceed 140°F on a July afternoon. Flex duct exposed to those temperatures over years can develop micro-tears and loose connections at the boots, creating gaps where attic dust bypasses the filter entirely and deposits directly inside the supply plenum. That's a problem no filter change alone will fix.

Cotton and grain agriculture in the surrounding area adds another layer: harvest season kicks up organic particulate that settles into return grilles and duct interiors, and that organic material provides a food source for mold if humidity ever spikes — which it can, briefly but sharply, during late-summer thunderstorm patterns.

## Our Air Duct Cleaning Process in Tye

We follow a source-removal approach aligned with NADCA guidelines and the standards Air Care Restoration's IICRC-certified team applies across all indoor air quality work.

**Step 1 — System inspection.** Before any equipment goes in, a technician walks the home, checks every register and return grille, and photographs the interior of accessible duct runs with a camera. In Tye's attic-routed systems, we also check flex duct connections at the air handler and at each boot for gaps or collapsed sections.

**Step 2 — Negative pressure containment.** We connect a high-powered vacuum collection unit to the main trunk line, placing the entire duct system under negative pressure. This prevents dislodged debris from migrating into living spaces during agitation.

**Step 3 — Mechanical agitation.** Rotary brush systems and compressed-air whips work through each supply and return branch, breaking loose settled dust, lint, and organic debris from duct walls and driving it toward the collection unit.

**Step 4 — Register and grille cleaning.** Every grille is removed, cleaned, and reinstalled. Boots are wiped and inspected for rust or moisture staining — a useful secondary check in homes that have had any roof or window leak history.

**Step 5 — Air handler and coil inspection.** We inspect the evaporator coil and blower compartment. In West Texas heat, coils can accumulate a dense layer of dust that chokes airflow and reduces efficiency measurably. We note coil condition and advise on cleaning if needed.

**Step 6 — Optional sanitization.** After mechanical cleaning, an EPA-registered antimicrobial fogger can be applied to duct interiors on request. This is particularly relevant for Tye homes that have experienced any moisture intrusion or that have visible discoloration inside supply registers.

## Dryer Vent Cleaning in Tye

Dryer vent cleaning is a natural companion service to duct cleaning, and in Tye it carries a specific risk factor worth noting. Many homes in this area use gas dryers, and the combination of lint accumulation with a gas appliance raises the stakes on a clogged vent. A fully obstructed dryer vent doesn't just extend dry times — it creates a potential ignition scenario. Our technicians clean dryer exhaust lines from the dryer connection point to the exterior termination cap, clear the cap screen of lint and debris, and confirm adequate airflow at the exit point before signing off.

## Local Note

Homes in Tye that were built during the 1970s and 1980s construction boom in this corridor frequently used fiberglass duct board rather than sheet metal for interior trunk lines. Duct board is more susceptible to surface degradation over decades, and aggressive mechanical agitation that works fine in a metal system can damage the interior liner if applied without adjustment. When we encounter duct board construction during the initial inspection, we modify the agitation method — using softer brush heads and lower rotational speeds — to clean effectively without compromising the liner. It's a detail that matters in this housing stock and one worth confirming with any contractor you call.
