Bronte sits in Coke County where the dry West Texas climate and a housing stock that stretches back to the early twentieth century create a quiet but persistent asbestos problem. Many of the older ranch-style homes and commercial buildings in this part of the Rolling Plains were constructed or renovated during the decades when asbestos was standard in floor tile, pipe insulation, roofing felt, and textured ceiling finishes. When those materials age, crack, or get disturbed during a remodel, the fibers they release are invisible and odorless, which means the hazard is easy to underestimate until a licensed inspector confirms it.

## Why Bronte Properties Face Asbestos Risks

Coke County's building history mirrors much of rural West Texas: construction boomed during the mid-twentieth century, materials were sourced regionally, and maintenance often meant patching rather than replacing. Homes and outbuildings built or updated between roughly 1940 and 1980 frequently contain asbestos in multiple locations at once, not just the obvious popcorn ceiling texture but also the mastic adhesive under vinyl floor tiles, the wrap around ductwork and plumbing, and the joint compound in drywall seams.

The climate adds another layer of concern. Bronte's hot summers and occasional freeze-thaw cycles in winter stress building materials over time. Pipe insulation that has been through decades of temperature swings tends to become brittle and friable, meaning it crumbles easily and releases fibers with minimal disturbance. A plumber cutting into a wall or a homeowner pulling up old flooring can unknowingly scatter asbestos throughout a living space in minutes.

## Our Asbestos Abatement Process in Bronte

Every project begins with a thorough inspection. Before any material is disturbed, our OSHA-trained team collects samples from suspect materials and has them analyzed by an accredited laboratory. That testing phase is not optional, and it shapes everything that follows, including which containment methods apply, what personal protective equipment the crew wears, and how waste must be packaged and transported.

Once testing confirms the presence of asbestos-containing materials, we establish negative-air containment using heavy polyethylene sheeting and HEPA-filtered air scrubbers to prevent cross-contamination to unaffected rooms. Removal follows strict regulatory procedures under Texas Department of State Health Services guidelines for asbestos work. All waste is double-bagged in labeled, sealed containers and transported to an approved disposal facility. Air clearance testing after abatement confirms fiber levels have returned to safe thresholds before containment is removed.

Because Air Care Restoration is also EPA Lead-Safe Certified, we can flag and manage lead-based paint hazards discovered alongside asbestos in the same older structures, which is common in Bronte's pre-1978 housing stock and saves property owners from scheduling a second contractor.

## Local Note

One thing that comes up specifically in Coke County's older ranch properties is asbestos in the corrugated roofing panels on outbuildings and equipment sheds. This material was widely used across agricultural West Texas because it was durable and cheap, and it is still present on many working properties around Bronte. It is easy to overlook because it looks like ordinary metal roofing from a distance, but when those panels are cut, drilled, or broken during repairs or demolition, they release fibers just as readily as interior materials do. If you are planning any work on an older barn, shed, or utility structure on your property, have the roofing material tested before anyone picks up a saw.
