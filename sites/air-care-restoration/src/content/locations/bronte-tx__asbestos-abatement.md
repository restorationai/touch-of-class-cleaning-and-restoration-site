## Why Bronte Properties Face Asbestos Risks

Coke County's housing stock tells a clear story: a significant portion of residential and commercial buildings in the Bronte area were constructed between the 1940s and 1980s, the decades when asbestos was woven into virtually every category of building material. Pipe insulation, duct wrap, roofing felt, vinyl floor tiles, joint compound, and spray-applied "popcorn" ceiling texture all commonly contained asbestos during that era.

The dry, high-desert climate around Bronte adds a layer of complication. Decades of heat cycling — scorching summers followed by hard freezes — cause building materials to expand and contract repeatedly. Over time, that movement can crack and crumble materials that were once intact, turning what was "non-friable" asbestos (stable and low-risk) into friable material that releases fibers into the air. A ceiling that looked fine last decade may be shedding fibers today simply because of the climate it has lived through.

Renovation is the other major trigger. Many Bronte homeowners are updating older properties — opening walls, pulling flooring, or removing popcorn ceilings — without realizing those materials may need testing before a single tool touches them. Disturbing asbestos-containing materials without proper containment is where exposure risk becomes real.

## Our Asbestos Abatement Process in Bronte

Every project starts with a professional inspection and bulk sample collection. Samples are sent to an accredited laboratory for analysis — we do not guess, and we do not skip this step. If asbestos is confirmed, we build a site-specific abatement plan before any removal begins.

Containment comes first. We seal the work area with heavy-gauge poly sheeting and establish negative air pressure using HEPA-filtered air scrubbers so that fibers cannot migrate to unaffected parts of the home or building. Workers wear full personal protective equipment throughout. Removal follows strict EPA and Texas Commission on Environmental Quality (TCEQ) protocols — wet methods to suppress fiber release, careful double-bagging of all waste in labeled, sealed containers.

Disposal in West Texas requires transport to a licensed facility approved for asbestos-containing waste — not a standard landfill. We handle the chain of custody documentation so the property owner has a clear paper trail, which matters for both regulatory compliance and future real estate transactions. After removal, a final air clearance test confirms fiber levels have returned to acceptable thresholds before containment is broken.
