## Why Haskell Properties See Sewage Backup Problems

Haskell sits in a part of West Texas where aging municipal infrastructure and private septic systems exist side by side. Many homes in town were built in the mid-twentieth century, when cast-iron and clay-tile drain lines were standard. Those materials corrode, crack, and shift over decades — and when the soil around them swells after a heavy rain event, tree roots and ground movement can collapse a section of line without warning. Septic systems on rural properties outside city limits face their own pressure: when the ground is already saturated from a storm, a drainfield can't absorb effluent fast enough, and the backup travels the path of least resistance — usually into the lowest fixture in the house.

The semi-arid climate here also creates a freeze-thaw pattern in winter that stresses older pipe joints. A line that holds fine through summer can develop a hairline fracture after a hard freeze in January, then fail completely the next time a heavy load moves through it.

## Our Sewage Cleanup and Sanitization Process in Haskell

Sewage is classified as Category 3 water — the most contaminated classification — which means standard water extraction protocols aren't enough. The process has to address both the physical waste and the biological hazard it leaves behind.

When the crew arrives, the first step is containment: sealing off affected areas to prevent cross-contamination to unaffected rooms. Solid waste is removed and disposed of in accordance with applicable health and waste regulations. Then extraction equipment pulls the remaining liquid from flooring, subfloor cavities, and any wall cavities that absorbed backflow. Because many Haskell homes have pier-and-beam foundations with open crawl spaces, sewage can migrate under the structure — the crew inspects beneath the floor deck, not just the visible surface.

After extraction, every contacted surface is treated with EPA-registered antimicrobial agents. Porous materials — insulation, carpet padding, sections of drywall below the flood line — are removed rather than treated in place, because saturated porous material can't be reliably sanitized. Structural wood that can be preserved is treated and dried to documented moisture targets using commercial drying equipment. The process follows the IICRC S500 standard for water damage and the additional contamination protocols that apply to Category 3 losses.

## Haskell Insurance Coordination

Whether a sewage backup is covered depends on the specific policy language — standard homeowners policies often exclude sewer backup unless a rider was added, while some policies cover it under the sudden-and-accidental water damage provision. The crew documents the loss thoroughly: photographs of affected materials, moisture readings before and after, a written scope of the work performed. ## Local Note

In Haskell County, a number of older homes were built on pier-and-beam foundations using untreated wood piers — a construction method that made sense when lumber was inexpensive and the soil conditions were well understood locally. When sewage reaches those crawl spaces, the piers and floor joists absorb contaminated moisture quickly, and the enclosed space traps humidity that accelerates deterioration. Standard drying timelines don't always apply here: the crew takes additional moisture readings at the pier bases and along the joist lines before closing out a job, because a reading that looks acceptable at the subfloor surface can still mask elevated moisture deeper in the wood.
