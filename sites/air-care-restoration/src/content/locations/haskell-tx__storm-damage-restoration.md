## Why Haskell Properties See Severe Storm Damage

The Texas Rolling Plains climate is the defining factor here. Haskell sits in a corridor that sees significant hail events, high straight-line winds, and periodic tornado activity — all three of which produce different damage signatures that have to be addressed separately. Hail pounds roofing materials and punches through soffit vents, creating entry points for rain that can saturate attic insulation and ceiling assemblies within a single storm event. Straight-line winds — which can exceed 70 mph in severe thunderstorm cells across this part of Texas — are responsible for most of the structural damage: lifted roof decking, failed gable ends, and downed trees that land on rooflines or fence lines.

Haskell's housing stock adds another layer of complexity. A significant portion of the homes in town were built in the mid-20th century, when construction standards for wind uplift were considerably less stringent than current Texas Residential Code requirements. Older roof-to-wall connections, original single-pane windows, and wood-framed structures with minimal sheathing can sustain damage in storms that would leave a newer build largely intact. When water enters through a compromised roof on one of these older homes, it often travels farther inside the wall assembly before it becomes visible — which means surface-level inspection alone will miss the full extent of the loss.

## Our Storm Damage Restoration Process in Haskell

The first priority after a storm is stopping additional damage. That means emergency tarping and board-up before any restoration work begins — not after. Once the structure is secured, the process moves through a documented sequence: moisture mapping with thermal imaging and calibrated meters to find water that has migrated beyond the obvious wet areas, controlled demolition of materials that cannot be dried in place, structural drying with commercial-grade desiccant and refrigerant dehumidifiers, and final inspection before reconstruction begins.

For tree damage cleanup specifically, the process involves more than cutting and hauling. A tree that has punched through a roof creates a puncture wound in the building envelope — the framing around the impact zone needs to be assessed for structural compromise before any cosmetic repair happens. We document every affected area with photographs and moisture readings, which becomes the foundation of the insurance claim file.
