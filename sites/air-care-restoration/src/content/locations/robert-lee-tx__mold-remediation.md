## Why Robert Lee Properties See Mold Issues

Coke County's semi-arid climate is deceptive. Long stretches without rain lead homeowners to underestimate moisture risk, but when storms do arrive — particularly the flash-flood-prone events common along the Colorado River corridor west of town — water intrudes quickly and dries slowly inside wall assemblies. Homes built in Robert Lee during the mid-twentieth century frequently used pier-and-beam foundations, which leave a gap between the ground and the subfloor that traps humidity when ventilation is inadequate. That underfloor environment is one of the most common mold discovery points in this area.

Older construction also means older plumbing — galvanized steel supply lines that corrode from the inside out, pinhole leaks that go undetected behind plaster or wood-paneled walls for months. By the time a homeowner notices a musty odor or a discolored ceiling, mold colonies may already extend well beyond the visible stain. The dry surface air can mask elevated moisture readings that only a calibrated moisture meter or thermal camera will catch.

## Our Mold Remediation Process in Robert Lee

Every job starts with a thorough inspection — not a visual scan, but a systematic assessment using moisture meters and infrared imaging to map where water has traveled inside the building envelope. Mold follows moisture, and finding the full moisture boundary is what separates genuine remediation from a surface cleaning that leaves the problem intact.

Once the affected area is mapped, the crew establishes containment: poly sheeting, negative air pressure with HEPA-filtered air scrubbers, and controlled entry points to prevent cross-contamination to unaffected rooms. Porous materials that cannot be adequately cleaned — drywall, insulation, compromised wood sheathing — are removed and bagged for disposal per EPA guidelines. Structural surfaces that can be salvaged are treated with EPA-registered antimicrobial agents and dried to confirmed moisture targets before any reconstruction begins.

Because Robert Lee homes often have original wood framing and subfloor decking rather than engineered lumber, the drying phase requires careful monitoring. Dense old-growth wood holds moisture longer than modern dimensional lumber and can read deceptively dry at the surface while the core remains saturated. Drying logs are maintained throughout, and clearance is confirmed by measurement, not by elapsed time.

## Local Note

One thing that catches homeowners in Robert Lee off guard: evaporative coolers, which are common in this part of West Texas because of the dry climate, introduce a meaningful amount of moisture into interior air when they run. In a well-ventilated home that's fine, but when ductwork has gaps or a cooler runs into a partially closed structure, humidity can accumulate in attic spaces and interior wall cavities without any visible leak ever occurring. If you're finding mold in an area that has never had obvious water intrusion, the evaporative cooler and its ductwork connections are worth inspecting before assuming the source has been identified.
