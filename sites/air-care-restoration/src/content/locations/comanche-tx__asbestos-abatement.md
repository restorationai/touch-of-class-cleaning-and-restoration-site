## Why Comanche Properties Face Elevated Asbestos Risk

Comanche's residential and commercial building stock reflects the construction patterns common across Central West Texas: a significant share of homes were built between the late 1940s and the mid-1970s, precisely the window when asbestos was used most heavily in American construction. Popcorn ceilings were sprayed with asbestos-containing texture compound well into the late 1970s. Floor tiles — the 9-inch and 12-inch vinyl squares still found in older Comanche homes — were routinely bonded with asbestos-laden adhesive. Vermiculite attic insulation, pipe wrap on older forced-air systems, and transite flue pipes on gas appliances are additional materials that turn up during inspections here.

The region's climate adds a layer of urgency. Comanche County experiences wide temperature swings, periodic drought stress, and occasional severe storms that accelerate the deterioration of older building materials. When asbestos-containing materials dry out, crack, or are disturbed by hail damage repairs or a roof replacement, fibers can become airborne. That's the point at which a material that was stable for decades becomes a health concern — and the point at which professional abatement is no longer optional.

## Our Asbestos Abatement Process in Comanche

Every project starts with a thorough inspection and bulk sample collection. Suspected materials are sampled by our OSHA-trained team and sent to an accredited laboratory for polarized light microscopy analysis — the standard method for confirming asbestos content. We do not skip testing and assume; confirmation drives the scope of work.

Once results confirm the presence of asbestos, the abatement process follows a defined sequence:

- **Containment setup**: Polyethylene sheeting and negative air pressure units isolate the work area from the rest of the structure, preventing cross-contamination.
- **Wet removal**: Regulated asbestos-containing materials are wetted before removal to suppress fiber release. Dry removal is not an acceptable method for friable materials.
- **Waste packaging and transport**: All removed materials are double-bagged in 6-mil poly, labeled per Texas Commission on Environmental Quality (TCEQ) requirements, and transported to a licensed disposal facility.
- **Air clearance**: Post-abatement air sampling confirms fiber levels have returned to acceptable limits before containment is removed and the space is released for reconstruction.

In Comanche, where many homes have original ductwork running through unconditioned attic spaces, we pay particular attention to HVAC penetrations — a common pathway for fiber migration if abatement containment is not properly sealed at those junctions.
