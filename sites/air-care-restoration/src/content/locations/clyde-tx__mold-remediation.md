Clyde sits in Callahan County where the semi-arid West Texas climate can feel like mold's worst enemy — until it isn't. A single plumbing failure, a slow roof leak after a spring thunderstorm, or a swamp cooler that runs all summer without proper drainage can push interior humidity high enough for mold to take hold within 24 to 48 hours. When that happens inside the older wood-framed homes that make up a good portion of Clyde's residential stock, the problem can spread behind walls and under flooring long before it's visible or smells like anything other than "musty."

## Why Clyde Properties See Mold Issues

Callahan County's weather is deceptive. Summers are hot and dry, so many homeowners assume mold is a Gulf Coast problem. But Clyde receives enough seasonal rainfall — concentrated in spring and early fall — to saturate crawl spaces, slab edges, and attic sheathing if drainage isn't directing water away from the foundation. Many homes in the area were built in eras when vapor barriers were minimal or absent entirely, and pier-and-beam foundations common in older parts of town create the kind of dark, low-airflow space where moisture lingers.

Swamp coolers, still widely used across this part of West Texas because they're economical in dry heat, introduce a specific risk: when ambient humidity spikes during a storm system, an evaporative cooler can push damp air into ductwork and wall cavities rather than cooling them. If that condition persists for even a few days without correction, mold colonization in the duct lining or behind registers can begin.

## Our Mold Remediation Process in Clyde

Every remediation starts with a thorough review of conditions — not just a visual scan, but moisture readings taken at the wall cavity level using calibrated meters. Mold that looks contained to one corner of a bathroom often has a moisture source two rooms away. Once the extent of the affected area is mapped, the work follows a structured sequence:

**Containment first.** Plastic sheeting and negative air pressure isolate the work zone so spores disturbed during removal don't migrate to unaffected rooms. This step matters especially in homes with open floor plans or shared HVAC systems.

**Source correction before surface treatment.** Removing mold without addressing the moisture source is temporary at best. We identify and document the origin — whether that's a failed supply line, inadequate attic ventilation, or a compromised crawl space — so the remediation holds.

**Controlled removal and HEPA filtration.** Affected porous materials are removed following containment protocols. HEPA air scrubbers run continuously during and after removal to capture airborne particulates.

**Antimicrobial treatment and drying.** Structural surfaces that remain are treated and then dried to documented moisture levels before any encapsulation or reconstruction begins.

The team is IICRC certified, and work on homes built before 1978 is performed under lead-safe work practices — relevant in Clyde where a portion of the housing stock predates that threshold. One pattern specific to Clyde and the surrounding Callahan County area: homes with evaporative cooling systems that share ductwork with a gas furnace sometimes develop mold inside the plenum and return-air box — a spot that's easy to miss during a standard visual review because it's behind the unit cabinet.
