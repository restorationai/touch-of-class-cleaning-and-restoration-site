## Why Albany Properties Carry a Higher Asbestos Risk

West Texas construction from the 1940s through the late 1970s leaned heavily on materials that were affordable, durable in dry heat, and widely available through regional suppliers. That means popcorn ceilings, floor tile adhesive, pipe insulation, roofing felt, and textured drywall compound in Albany homes often contain chrysotile asbestos at concentrations that require licensed abatement before disturbance. The region's low humidity and persistent wind are a compounding factor: once asbestos-containing material dries out and becomes friable, even minor air movement can release fibers. A renovation crew opening a ceiling in an Albany farmhouse or a downtown commercial building without prior testing is a scenario that creates long-term liability for everyone involved.

Shackelford County's older agricultural and ranch-support structures add another layer. Metal buildings with asbestos-containing roofing panels, and older pump houses or equipment sheds with pipe lagging, are common on properties outside the city limits. These structures rarely appear on anyone's renovation checklist until a sale, an insurance inspection, or storm damage forces the issue.

## Our Asbestos Abatement Process in Albany

Every project starts with a thorough inspection and bulk sampling of suspect materials. Samples go to an accredited laboratory, and no abatement work begins until results confirm the presence and concentration of asbestos. That sequence matters under Texas Department of State Health Services regulations, which govern asbestos abatement projects in the state and set specific requirements for notification, containment, and disposal.

Once testing confirms asbestos, the work follows a defined sequence:

- **Containment setup**: Polyethylene sheeting seals the work area. Negative air pressure units with HEPA filtration run continuously so that fibers cannot migrate into adjacent living or working spaces.
- **Wet removal**: Regulated materials are kept wet throughout removal to suppress fiber release. Dry scraping or sanding is never used on asbestos-containing material.
- **Waste packaging and transport**: All removed material is double-bagged in labeled, sealed containers and transported to a licensed disposal facility. Texas requires manifest documentation for every load.
- **Air clearance**: Post-abatement air sampling confirms fiber counts are within acceptable limits before containment is removed and the space is released for other trades.
