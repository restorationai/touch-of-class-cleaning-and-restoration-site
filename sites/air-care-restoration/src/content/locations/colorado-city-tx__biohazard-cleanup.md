## Why Colorado City Properties Present Specific Biohazard Considerations

Mitchell County's housing stock skews older, and many Colorado City homes were built in construction eras when subflooring, wall cavities, and HVAC systems were designed without modern contamination containment in mind. Porous materials — hardwood subfloors, plaster-and-lath walls, older concrete slab foundations common across this part of West Texas — can absorb biological material well below the visible surface. What looks contained at eye level may have already wicked into structural layers that standard cleaning cannot reach.

The region's climate adds another layer of complexity. Colorado City experiences hot, dry summers and occasional hard freezes in winter. Extreme heat accelerates the biological breakdown of organic material, which means that in the warmer months, the window between an incident and the onset of secondary contamination risks is shorter than in more temperate climates. Acting quickly is not just advisable — in a Texas summer, it is essential.

## Our Biohazard Remediation Process in Colorado City

Every job begins with a thorough assessment before any work starts. Technicians identify the full scope of affected materials — visible and hidden — using moisture meters and thermal imaging where appropriate. Containment barriers are established to prevent cross-contamination to unaffected areas of the home or building.

All biological material, sharps, infectious waste, and contaminated porous materials are removed following OSHA bloodborne pathogen protocols and disposed of through licensed medical waste channels — not standard trash removal. Surfaces are then treated with EPA-registered hospital-grade disinfectants, and the space is verified clean before containment is removed. Documentation is generated throughout, which matters when insurance claims or legal proceedings are involved.

Because older Colorado City homes often have original hardwood floors or plaster walls, our technicians assess whether structural materials can be safely restored or need to be removed. That decision is made on evidence, not assumption, and communicated clearly to the property owner before any demolition occurs.
