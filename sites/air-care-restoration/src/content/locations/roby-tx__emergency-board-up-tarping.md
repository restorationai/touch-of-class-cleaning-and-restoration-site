## Our Emergency Board-Up and Tarping Process in Roby

Every board-up and tarping response follows a sequence designed to stop secondary damage first, then document everything for your insurance claim.

**Assessment on arrival.** Before any material goes up, the crew walks the perimeter and identifies all breach points — broken windows, missing or lifted roof sections, compromised doors, and any structural areas at risk of further failure. In Roby's wind environment, a partially lifted roof section can become a fully missing one if it isn't addressed in the right order.

**Temporary roof tarping.** Heavy-duty polyethylene tarps are secured with batten boards and fastened to sound decking, not just draped and weighted. On older structures where decking may be thinner or spaced, the crew adjusts fastener placement to avoid creating new penetrations that could leak. Tarps are overlapped and sealed at ridges and edges to handle the lateral wind that's common across the Fisher County flatlands.

**Board-up of openings.** Windows and doors are measured and cut to fit, then secured with structural screws rather than nails — a detail that matters when afternoon gusts can exceed the initial storm. For fire-damaged openings, the crew works around compromised framing carefully, bracing where needed before boarding.

**Photo documentation.** Every breach point is photographed before and after securing. That documentation goes directly into the claim file and is formatted to meet the standards most Texas carriers expect.
