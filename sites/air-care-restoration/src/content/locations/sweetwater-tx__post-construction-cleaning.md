## Why Sweetwater Construction Sites Need More Than a Broom Finish

Sweetwater sits in a semi-arid stretch of the Rolling Plains where the wind rarely stops. That's great for the wind farms visible from US-84, but it creates a real problem on active and recently completed job sites: fine particulate — gypsum dust, insulation fibers, sanded joint compound — doesn't settle the way it does in calmer climates. It migrates. It gets pulled into ductwork before HVAC filters are seated. It coats light fixtures, cabinet interiors, and rough-in plumbing before the plumber even returns for trim-out.

Older homes in Sweetwater that are undergoing renovation present a second layer of complexity. Much of the residential housing stock in the city dates to mid-century construction — pier-and-beam foundations, original hardwood or linoleum subfloors, and in some cases plaster-and-lath walls that were never replaced during earlier updates. Renovation debris in these homes can include materials that require careful handling under EPA Lead-Safe protocols, particularly in pre-1978 structures where painted surfaces were disturbed during demo. ## Our Post-Construction Cleaning Process in Sweetwater

A true final clean isn't a single pass — it's a sequenced process that respects how construction dust actually moves.

**Rough clean first.** Before any detail work begins, bulk debris — scrap lumber, packaging, leftover materials — is removed and the site is swept or vacuumed with HEPA-equipped equipment. Skipping this step and going straight to detail cleaning just redistributes debris.

**Top-down surface cleaning.** Dust travels downward. Ceiling fans, light fixtures, tops of door frames, and cabinet interiors are cleaned before floors are touched. In Sweetwater's windy conditions, we pay particular attention to any returns or supply registers that may have pulled airborne particulate into the duct system during construction.

**Detail and specialty surfaces.** Windows, mirrors, hardware, tile grout lines, and appliance surfaces require product-specific cleaning — what works on a stainless steel appliance front will streak a low-e window coating. Our IICRC-certified team carries the appropriate agents for each surface type.

**Final floor pass.** Hard floors are cleaned last, after all overhead and wall-surface work is complete. Carpet protection film is removed and the carpet is vacuumed with HEPA equipment. Any adhesive residue from protective coverings is addressed before the walk-through.

**Specialty cleaning add-ons.** Post-construction work sometimes surfaces needs that weren't anticipated — smoke odor from a subcontractor's cutting torch, a mold colony uncovered behind removed drywall, or air quality concerns from spray foam application. Because Air Care Restoration holds IICRC certifications in both water damage restoration and fire and smoke restoration, those specialty scopes don't require a second contractor.
