## Why Robert Lee Properties Face Elevated Asbestos Risk

The building timeline here matters. Much of Robert Lee's residential and commercial stock was built or substantially remodeled during the postwar boom and again during the energy-sector expansion of the 1970s, both periods when asbestos-containing materials were widely used in roofing felts, floor tiles, pipe insulation, joint compound, and the textured "popcorn" ceiling finishes that were applied almost universally in that era. West Texas construction also leaned heavily on materials rated for thermal performance, and asbestos fit that requirement well before safer alternatives were available.

The dry, windy climate of the Concho Valley and Rolling Plains region adds another layer of concern. When asbestos-containing materials age and dry out, they become friable, meaning fibers can be released with very little physical disturbance. A renovation crew pulling up old floor tile in a 1960s home, or a contractor cutting into a wall to run new electrical, can unknowingly send fibers airborne in conditions where the low humidity has already been doing degradation work for years. That is not a hypothetical risk; it is a pattern consistent with this climate and this building era.

## Our Asbestos Abatement Process in Robert Lee

Every project begins with a thorough inspection and material sampling before any abatement work starts. Suspected materials are collected following established protocols and sent to an accredited laboratory for analysis. Results determine the scope of work and the appropriate abatement method, whether that is full removal, encapsulation, or enclosure, depending on the material's condition and location.

Once the scope is confirmed, the work area is isolated using negative air pressure containment with HEPA filtration to prevent fiber migration to unaffected parts of the structure. Regulated materials are wetted during removal to suppress fiber release, then double-bagged and labeled for transport to a licensed disposal facility. Texas requires asbestos waste from abatement projects to be disposed of at approved sites, and Air Care Restoration manages that chain of custody documentation from start to finish.

After removal, a visual inspection and air clearance sampling confirm that fiber levels have returned to background before containment is removed. That final clearance step is not optional; it is the point at which the space is genuinely safe to reoccupy.
