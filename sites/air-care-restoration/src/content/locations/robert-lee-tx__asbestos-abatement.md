## Why Robert Lee Properties See Asbestos Concerns

Coke County's building boom tracked closely with the mid-century oil and ranching economy. Homes and commercial structures built through the 1950s, 1960s, and into the early 1980s routinely incorporated asbestos in floor tiles, pipe insulation, roofing felt, textured "popcorn" ceilings, joint compound, and duct wrap — materials that were affordable, fire-resistant, and widely available at the time. In a region where summers push temperatures well past 100°F and structures expand and contract dramatically between seasons, those older materials have had decades of thermal stress. Cracks, crumbling edges, and friable insulation are common findings in attics and crawl spaces across this part of West Texas. A home that looks solid from the outside may have disturbed asbestos-containing materials in places you'd never think to check — around the furnace plenum, beneath linoleum in the kitchen, or inside the walls where pipe insulation has degraded over time.

## Our Asbestos Abatement Process in Robert Lee

Every project starts with a thorough asbestos inspection before any material is touched. Bulk samples are collected from suspect areas — floor tiles, ceiling texture, pipe wrap, roofing materials — and sent to an accredited laboratory for analysis. Once results confirm the presence of asbestos-containing material, the abatement scope is defined and the appropriate Texas Department of State Health Services notifications are completed prior to work beginning.

On-site, the work area is isolated with negative air pressure containment: heavy-gauge polyethylene sheeting seals off the space, and HEPA-filtered air scrubbers run continuously to prevent fiber migration into adjacent rooms. Technicians wear full personal protective equipment, and all regulated waste is wetted, double-bagged in labeled 6-mil poly, and transported to a licensed disposal facility — not a general landfill. After abatement is complete, air clearance sampling confirms fiber levels have returned to acceptable limits before containment is removed and the space is released for re-occupancy or renovation.

## Local Note

One thing worth knowing about older properties in Coke County: many homes from the 1950s and 1960s were built with a combination of materials sourced locally and through regional suppliers serving the oil patch, which means the same structure might have asbestos-containing floor tile in one room and a completely different vintage of non-asbestos tile in the next. Sampling a single room and assuming the rest of the house matches is a mistake that leads to incomplete abatement. A thorough inspection here means walking the entire structure — attic, utility closets, garage slab, and any additions — because material types genuinely vary room to room in ways that aren't typical of more uniformly built suburban housing stock.
