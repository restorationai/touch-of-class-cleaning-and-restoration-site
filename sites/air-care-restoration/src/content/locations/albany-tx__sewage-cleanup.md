## Why Albany Properties See Sewage Backup Issues

Albany's climate swings hard between dry summers and flash-flood-prone spring storms. During extended dry spells, the clay-heavy soils contract and can shift sewer lines just enough to create low spots where solids accumulate. When the rains return, those same lines get overwhelmed. Homes on older septic systems — common on the rural edges of town and on larger lots closer to the Brazos River drainage basin — face a different but related problem: drain fields that have been baked dry for months can't absorb a sudden surge, and the result is a septic overflow that surfaces in the yard or backs up through the lowest fixture in the house.

Albany's housing stock leans toward mid-century construction, and many of those homes were plumbed with cast-iron drain lines that are now well past their expected service life. Cast iron corrodes from the inside out, and the rough interior walls catch grease and debris in ways that PVC simply doesn't. A partial blockage that causes a slow drain in January can become a full backup in March when spring storms load the system.

## Our Sewage Cleanup and Sanitization Process in Albany

Raw sewage is classified as Category 3 water — the most contaminated type — and the cleanup protocol reflects that. The process doesn't start with a mop; it starts with containment. We isolate the affected area to prevent cross-contamination to adjacent rooms, then remove standing sewage and saturated materials using truck-mounted extraction equipment. Porous materials — carpet, pad, drywall below the flood line, insulation — are removed and bagged for disposal rather than dried in place, because no amount of drying makes contaminated porous material safe to keep.

Once the bulk removal is complete, the exposed structural surfaces are treated with an EPA-registered disinfectant rated for Category 3 contamination. We don't skip the drying phase after sanitization: residual moisture left in subfloor assemblies or wall cavities will support mold growth within days, and in Albany's humid spring months that window is even shorter. Drying equipment — dehumidifiers and air movers — stays on-site until moisture readings confirm the structure has returned to pre-loss levels. The IICRC S500 standard guides our drying protocol throughout.
