## Why Ballinger Properties Face Elevated Board-Up and Tarping Needs

Ballinger sits in a corridor where severe thunderstorms, hail, and straight-line wind events are routine seasonal occurrences rather than rare surprises. The Concho Valley's flat, open terrain gives those storms little friction to slow down before they reach residential neighborhoods and the older commercial blocks along Hutchings Avenue. Hail large enough to punch through asphalt shingles is common in spring and early summer, and the same storm cell that cracks a roof can shatter windows and compromise door frames in a single pass.

The housing stock in Ballinger skews older — a significant portion of single-family homes were built in the mid-twentieth century, when roof sheathing was often board-decked rather than plywood-paneled. That construction style is durable in many respects, but when a section fails, the irregular gaps are harder to seal cleanly with standard tarp systems. Proper tarping on these roofs requires mechanical fastening that bridges the board gaps without driving fasteners through sound decking, a detail that matters when the structure eventually goes to a roofer for permanent repair.

Fire damage is the other primary driver of emergency board-up calls in the area. A structure fire that burns through an exterior wall or collapses a window frame leaves the interior open to weather, animals, and unauthorized entry simultaneously. Boarding up fire-damaged openings with OSB panels, properly framed and fastened, is often a condition of the insurance adjuster's initial site visit.

## Our Emergency Board-Up and Tarping Process in Ballinger

When a call comes in from Ballinger, the first question is the nature of the opening — roof breach, broken windows, fire-compromised wall, or some combination. That determines what materials load onto the truck before departure from Abilene.

For roof tarping, the process starts with a safety assessment of the decking before anyone walks the slope. Heavy-duty polyethylene tarps rated for wind uplift are secured with a cap-and-batten system: lumber runs along the ridge and down the eaves, with the tarp sandwiched underneath, so wind cannot catch an edge and peel the cover back. On older board-decked roofs common in Ballinger, fastener placement is mapped to hit solid lumber rather than gaps.

For window and door board-ups, we cut OSB panels to fit each opening, frame them with dimensional lumber to distribute load, and fasten into the surrounding wall framing rather than just the window casing. This matters on older homes where the casing itself may be softened by age or fire exposure. Every opening gets photographed before and after installation — documentation that goes directly into the insurance file.

For fire-damaged structures, we coordinate with the property owner and, when applicable, the insurance adjuster on which openings require board-up versus which areas need to remain accessible for the adjuster's walk-through. Sealing too much too fast can complicate the claim; sealing too little leaves the structure vulnerable overnight.
