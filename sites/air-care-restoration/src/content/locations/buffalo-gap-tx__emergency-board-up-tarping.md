## Why Buffalo Gap Properties Face Particular Board-Up and Tarping Challenges

The Taylor County climate is the first factor to understand. Buffalo Gap and the broader Abilene region sit in a zone where severe thunderstorms, straight-line winds, and hail events are common from late spring through early fall. A single storm can produce golf ball-sized hail that punches through asphalt shingles and leaves roof decking exposed — not just damp, but open to whatever follows over the next 24 to 48 hours. A crew that hasn't worked this area before can lose significant time adapting.

## Our Emergency Board-Up and Tarping Process in Buffalo Gap

When you call, the first thing our team does is gather enough information to arrive with the right materials — plywood gauge, tarp weight, and fastener type all depend on what's damaged and what weather is approaching. We don't show up with a one-size approach.

On-site, the process follows a deliberate sequence:

1. **Damage assessment** — we walk the full perimeter and interior access points before cutting a single board, identifying every breach including ones that aren't immediately visible from the street.
2. **Temporary weatherproofing** — heavy-duty tarps are anchored with batten strips and mechanical fasteners rather than just draped and weighted; in West Texas wind, a tarp held down by sandbags alone won't survive a gust line.
3. **Window and door boarding** — plywood panels are cut to fit and secured to the structural framing, not just the trim, so they hold against pressure rather than popping out.
4. **Documentation** — every opening is photographed before and after boarding, which gives your insurance adjuster a clear record of the original damage scope and the protective measures taken.

That documentation step matters in Taylor County, where insurance carriers handling hail and wind claims are processing high volumes after a major storm event and need organized evidence to move quickly.
