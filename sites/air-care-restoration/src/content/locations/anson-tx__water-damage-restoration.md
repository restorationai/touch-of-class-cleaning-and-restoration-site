## Why Anson Properties Are Vulnerable to Water Damage

Jones County sits in a semi-arid climate that swings between prolonged drought and intense, short-duration rainfall events. That cycle is hard on residential plumbing: extended dry periods cause the expansive clay soil beneath Anson homes to shrink and shift, stressing supply lines and drain connections. When those pipes finally fail — or when a fast-moving thunderstorm overwhelms a roof or window seal — the resulting water intrusion can be substantial.

Anson's housing stock skews older, and many homes in town were built with construction methods that complicate water damage response. Pier-and-beam foundations, common in mid-century Anson homes, create crawl spaces that collect standing water and hold humidity long after the visible leak is addressed. Plaster-and-lath interior walls, found in homes built before the 1960s, absorb moisture more deeply than modern drywall and release it slowly — meaning moisture readings at the surface can drop while the structural layer behind the wall stays saturated. Missing either of those conditions leads to secondary mold growth, typically appearing within 48 to 72 hours of initial wetting.

## Our Water Damage Restoration Process in Anson

Every job starts with a thorough moisture assessment using thermal imaging cameras and calibrated moisture meters — not a visual walkthrough. In older Anson homes, that means probing plaster walls, checking crawl space joists, and inspecting subfloor assemblies that may have absorbed water through the foundation rather than from above.

Once the scope is mapped, water removal begins with truck-mounted extraction units capable of pulling standing water from carpet, hardwood, and concrete slab surfaces. Structural drying follows immediately: we place commercial-grade desiccant dehumidifiers and high-velocity air movers in a calculated configuration based on the square footage and material types involved. Drying is tracked daily with moisture logs, and equipment is repositioned as readings change — a process aligned with the IICRC S500 standard for water damage restoration. Nothing is signed off until every affected assembly reads within normal range for the local ambient conditions.

If building materials are damaged beyond drying — saturated insulation, buckled subfloor panels, compromised drywall — we document the scope for your insurance carrier and coordinate removal and replacement as part of a full mitigation and reconstruction path.

## Local Note

In Anson, pier-and-beam homes present a specific challenge that slab-foundation properties don't: water that enters the crawl space can wick upward into hardwood flooring from below while the surface of the floor feels only slightly damp. Homeowners sometimes assume the floor is fine after wiping up surface water, but the subfloor and the bottom face of the hardwood may be holding significant moisture.
