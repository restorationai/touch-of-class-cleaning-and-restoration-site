In Snyder and the surrounding Scurry County plains, severe weather can arrive as a fast-moving supercell that drops hail the size of golf balls, peels back roof decking, and drives rain horizontally through window frames — all within a span of thirty minutes. ## Why Snyder Properties Are Particularly Vulnerable to Storm Damage

Snyder sits squarely in the southern edge of Tornado Alley, where warm Gulf moisture collides with dry air pushing down from the Panhandle. That atmospheric setup produces not just tornadoes but the kind of straight-line wind events and baseball-sized hail that tear through neighborhoods with little warning. The region's flat, open terrain gives storms almost nothing to slow them down before they reach residential streets.

The local housing stock adds another layer of complexity. A significant portion of Snyder's homes were built in the mid-twentieth century during the oil-boom decades, when construction methods favored wood-framed walls with little to no exterior sheathing beyond the siding itself. When a storm punches through that envelope — cracking a soffit, splitting a ridge cap, or blowing out a window — water infiltration can reach interior wall cavities within minutes. Older homes also tend to have original single-pane windows and aging roof systems that are simply more susceptible to impact damage than newer construction.

The caliche-heavy soil common across Scurry County drains slowly after heavy rainfall, which means water that pools against a foundation after a storm lingers longer than it would in sandier soils. That extended contact raises the risk of moisture wicking into slab edges and crawl spaces, turning what looks like a roofing problem into a flooring or structural concern if it isn't addressed quickly.

## Our Storm Damage Restoration Process in Snyder

Every storm damage response begins with a thorough exterior and interior assessment before any debris is moved or tarps are placed. We photograph and document all visible damage — displaced shingles, fractured fascia, broken glass, saturated insulation — in a format that supports insurance claims from the start.

Once the scope is clear, the immediate priority is stopping water entry. Emergency tarping and board-up services protect the structure from additional rain or wind while permanent repairs are planned. From there, the process shifts to moisture mapping: we use thermal imaging cameras and calibrated moisture meters to trace where water has already traveled behind walls, under flooring, and into ceiling assemblies. In older Snyder homes with plaster-and-lath interiors, this step is especially important because plaster can appear dry at the surface while the wood lath behind it holds significant moisture.

Drying equipment — commercial-grade dehumidifiers and high-velocity air movers — is positioned based on the moisture map, not guesswork. We monitor readings daily until materials reach their target dryness levels, then move to structural repairs: framing, drywall, insulation, roofing, and exterior finishes. Our team holds IICRC certifications in water damage restoration and fire and smoke restoration, and we are EPA Lead-Safe Certified — relevant for any Snyder home built before 1978 where storm damage may disturb lead-based paint during repairs.
