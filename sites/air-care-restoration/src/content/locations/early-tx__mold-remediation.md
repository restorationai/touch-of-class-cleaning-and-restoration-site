Early, TX sits in the heart of the Concho Valley where summer humidity spikes after thunderstorms and cool-season moisture lingers longer than most Central Texas towns expect. That combination — heat, periodic heavy rain, and the area's mix of slab-on-grade ranch homes and older pier-and-beam construction — creates conditions where mold can colonize a wall cavity within 24 to 48 hours of a slow leak going unnoticed. If you're smelling something musty after a plumbing issue or noticing discoloration around a window frame, that's not a problem to watch and wait on.

## Why Early Properties See Mold Issues

Early's position just west of Brownwood in Brown County means the area catches moisture from Gulf-fed storm systems that push inland through the spring and fall. When those rains soak into soil around older foundations, water finds its way through hairline cracks in slabs or through the crawl spaces common under pier-and-beam homes built in earlier decades. Once inside, moisture meets the warm interior air and mold has everything it needs.

The local housing stock adds another layer of complexity. Many homes in Early were built when vapor barriers were minimal or nonexistent, and HVAC systems were sized for a different era. When a duct sweats or a supply line drips behind drywall, the paper facing on insulation and the wood framing behind it can begin supporting mold growth before the homeowner sees a single spot on the surface. By the time visible colonies appear, the hidden spread is often already significant.

## Our Mold Remediation Process in Early

Remediation is not the same as cleaning. Wiping visible mold without addressing the moisture source and the affected substrate leaves the problem intact beneath the surface. The process Air Care Restoration follows is structured around the IICRC S520 standard for mold remediation — containment first, then controlled removal, then verification.

**Containment** means isolating the affected area with negative-air pressure barriers so spores disturbed during removal don't migrate to clean rooms. In a typical Early home where the living areas open directly into hallways and shared spaces, this step is especially important.

**Removal** involves taking out materials that cannot be effectively cleaned — drywall, insulation, sections of subfloor — rather than treating the surface and leaving compromised material in place. Porous materials that have been colonized are bagged and removed per EPA guidelines.

**Drying and treatment** of the remaining structural members follows, using antifungal agents on exposed framing before any reconstruction begins. Moisture readings are taken at multiple points to confirm the area is within acceptable ranges before the space is closed back up.

Because Air Care Restoration is an IICRC Certified Firm and EPA Lead-Safe Certified, work in older Early homes where lead-based paint may be present is handled with the additional protocols that certification requires — something that matters in a region where homes from the 1960s and 1970s are common.

## Local Note

One thing worth knowing about Early and the surrounding Brown County area: the region's clay-heavy soils expand significantly when wet and contract in dry stretches. That movement puts stress on slab foundations over time, and small cracks that open and close seasonally are common. Those cracks are a recurring moisture entry point that homeowners sometimes attribute to settling rather than active water intrusion. If mold is appearing near baseboards or in closets along exterior walls, the foundation perimeter is worth inspecting — not just the plumbing.
