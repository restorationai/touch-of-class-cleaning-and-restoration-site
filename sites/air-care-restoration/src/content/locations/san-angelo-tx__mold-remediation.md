## Why San Angelo Properties See Mold Issues

San Angelo sits at the confluence of three branches of the Concho River, and low-lying properties near those banks are particularly vulnerable after heavy rain events. The region's clay-heavy soils shed water quickly at the surface but can direct runoff toward foundations in ways that aren't obvious until moisture shows up inside a crawl space or along a slab edge. Older homes — many built in the mid-20th century with pier-and-beam foundations — are especially susceptible because the underfloor space creates a naturally humid microenvironment that rarely dries out on its own after a plumbing leak or flood.

The city also experiences significant temperature swings between seasons. When warm, humid air from a summer storm meets a cooled interior wall surface, condensation forms inside wall cavities. That moisture, invisible from the living space, is exactly the kind of slow, persistent dampness that feeds Cladosporium, Penicillium, and the Stachybotrys strains commonly called black mold. A musty odor in a room that otherwise looks clean is often the first sign that something is growing inside the structure itself.

## Our Mold Remediation Process in San Angelo

Every remediation starts with a thorough moisture mapping of the affected area — not just a visual inspection. Technicians use thermal imaging and calibrated moisture meters to locate saturation behind drywall, under flooring, and inside ceiling assemblies before any demolition begins. This matters in San Angelo homes where older plaster-and-lath construction or original hardwood subfloors can hold moisture at depth while reading relatively dry at the surface.

Once the extent of the contamination is confirmed, the work follows a contained, step-by-step sequence:

1. **Containment** — physical barriers and negative air pressure prevent spores from migrating to unaffected rooms during removal.
2. **Removal of affected materials** — porous materials (drywall, insulation, subfloor sections) that cannot be cleaned to an acceptable standard are removed and bagged for disposal.
3. **HEPA vacuuming and antimicrobial treatment** — structural surfaces are cleaned and treated to address residual contamination.
4. **Drying and dehumidification** — commercial-grade desiccant or refrigerant dehumidifiers run until moisture readings return to normal baselines for the material type.
5. **Post-remediation verification** — clearance testing confirms that spore counts in the treated area are within acceptable limits before containment is removed.

The IICRC S520 standard guides the remediation protocol throughout. Air Care Restoration is an IICRC Certified Firm and carries EPA Lead-Safe Certification — relevant in San Angelo's older housing stock where lead-based paint can be disturbed during necessary demolition.

## Local Note

San Angelo has a notable inventory of mid-century brick veneer homes, particularly in established residential areas closer to the downtown core. Brick veneer looks solid but is not a moisture barrier — there's a small air gap and a wood-framed wall behind it, and when the weep holes at the base of the veneer get blocked by soil buildup or landscaping, water has nowhere to drain. Moisture then wicks into the wall cavity from the outside in, and because the brick face stays dry, the problem can go unnoticed for months. If your home has brick veneer and you're seeing mold near baseboards or window frames on exterior walls, that drainage gap is worth inspecting before any interior remediation work begins.
