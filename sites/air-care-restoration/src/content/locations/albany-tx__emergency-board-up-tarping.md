## Why Albany Properties Face Distinct Board-Up and Tarping Risks

Albany sits in a stretch of West Texas where the weather swings hard and fast. Supercell thunderstorms rolling through Shackelford County can drop baseball-sized hail and produce straight-line winds that strip roofing materials, shatter windows, and leave structures open to the elements in minutes. Because Albany is a smaller community with a modest local contractor pool, property owners often face longer waits for emergency response — time during which an unprotected opening lets rain soak insulation, sub-flooring, and wall cavities.

The town's older housing stock adds another layer of complexity. Many homes in Albany were built in the mid-twentieth century using construction methods that are less forgiving of sudden exposure. Older roof decking, for instance, can absorb water rapidly once the surface layer is compromised, and a single night of rain through an unprotected opening can turn a manageable repair into a full deck replacement. Commercial buildings along the main corridor face similar vulnerabilities — plate glass storefronts that shatter in a hail event leave inventory and interior finishes exposed until proper boarding is in place.

## Our Emergency Board-Up and Tarping Process in Albany

When you call, the first thing we do is triage the opening over the phone — size, location, whether the structure is still occupied, and whether there is an active fire or police scene that affects access. That information shapes what materials we load before leaving Abilene.

On arrival, the crew assesses structural stability before anyone climbs. For roof tarping, we use heavy-duty polyethylene tarps secured with lumber battens and screws driven into undamaged decking — not bungee cords or sandbags that shift in the next gust. The tarp is lapped at least two feet past every damaged area and anchored at the ridge where possible so wind cannot get underneath and peel it back. For window and door board-up, we cut OSB panels to fit and fasten them with exterior screws rather than nails, which hold better under repeated wind pressure and are easier to remove cleanly when permanent repairs begin.

Every opening is photographed before and after boarding — wide shots showing context, close-ups showing fastener placement and material overlap. That documentation goes directly into the insurance claim file.

## Local Note

One thing that matters specifically in Albany and the surrounding Shackelford County area: the persistent West Texas wind does not stop after a storm passes. A tarp installed without proper mechanical fastening into the roof deck — the kind held down only by weight or rope — will frequently fail overnight as post-storm winds continue at 20 to 30 mph. We have seen this pattern repeatedly across this part of the state. Our crews always use screwed lumber battens rather than weight-only systems precisely because of this regional wind behavior, and we size the tarp overlap conservatively to account for the gusts that follow the main storm cell.
