## Why Roby Properties Are Vulnerable to Fire Damage

Roby sits in a stretch of West Texas where low annual rainfall, low relative humidity, and persistent southwesterly winds create fire conditions that can escalate faster than in more temperate parts of the state. Grass and brush fires along county roads and ranch boundaries are a recurring seasonal reality, and wind-driven embers can reach structures well before a fire itself does.

The housing stock in Roby reflects the region's history — many homes were built in the mid-twentieth century using wood-frame construction with minimal fire-resistant sheathing by today's standards. Older interior finishes like wood paneling and plaster-over-lath absorb smoke odor compounds deeply, meaning surface cleaning alone rarely resolves the problem. Attic spaces in these homes often lack the insulation baffling that would slow smoke migration, so soot can travel far from the point of origin before a homeowner even realizes the extent of the damage.

Local utility infrastructure — older wiring in some of the town's aging residential blocks — is also a documented ignition risk. Electrical fires that start inside walls are particularly difficult because the damage path follows framing cavities rather than visible surfaces.

## Our Fire Damage Restoration Process in Roby

Every fire loss is different, but the sequence of work follows a proven structure calibrated to what we actually find in Roby homes.

**Emergency stabilization** comes first: boarding windows and doors, tarping compromised roofing, and shutting off utilities if that hasn't already been done. Structural safety is assessed before any cleaning begins.

**Scope and documentation** follows immediately. We photograph and measure every affected area, log damaged materials, and produce the detailed written scope that your insurance adjuster will need. This step is not rushed — a thorough scope protects you from discovering uncovered items weeks into the job.

**Soot and smoke removal** is where IICRC FSRT training matters most. Different combustion sources — synthetic materials, wood, insulation — leave chemically distinct residues that respond to different cleaning agents and techniques. Dry-sponge wiping, HEPA vacuuming, and thermal fogging are applied based on residue type, not a one-size approach.

**Odor neutralization** in a West Texas home often requires special attention to HVAC systems. Ductwork in older Roby homes can hold smoke particulate that will re-contaminate a cleaned space every time the system runs. Duct cleaning and deodorization are part of the scope, not an afterthought.

**Structural drying and reconstruction** closes the job — replacing charred framing, drywall, flooring, and finishes to pre-loss condition.
