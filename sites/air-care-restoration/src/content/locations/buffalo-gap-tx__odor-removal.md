## Why Buffalo Gap Properties Hold Odors the Way They Do

The Taylor County climate is the first factor. High summer temperatures accelerate the chemical bonding of smoke and organic odor compounds to porous materials, which means a fire or a pet-soiled subfloor in Buffalo Gap is not the same problem as the same event in a cooler, more humid region. Heat drives odor molecules into wood grain, drywall paper, and insulation batting faster than in milder climates.

The second factor is the housing stock itself. Many homes in the Buffalo Gap area were built for ranch and rural use, with wood-framed construction, pier-and-beam foundations, and crawl spaces that collect and hold organic odors from moisture, animals, and soil gases. Pier-and-beam homes have open air space beneath the floor system, which means an animal intrusion or a plumbing leak underneath the house can saturate the subfloor and joists with odor long before anyone notices it inside the living space.

Finally, the dry West Texas air creates a deceptive surface condition. A wall or floor can feel and smell acceptable on a cool morning but release odor again when afternoon temperatures climb. That cycling behavior is one reason odor problems in this area often seem to come back after a cleaning that appeared to work.

## Our Odor Removal and Deodorization Process in Buffalo Gap

Effective odor removal is not a single treatment. It is a sequence of steps calibrated to the source, the material, and the environment.

The process begins with source identification. Odor that has no identified source will return regardless of what treatment follows. The crew traces the odor to its origin, whether that is char behind a wall cavity, urine-saturated subfloor, or smoke residue in an HVAC system that has been circulating the smell through every room.

Once the source is confirmed and any contaminated material is removed or treated, the deodorization phase begins. Depending on what the inspection reveals, the team deploys one or more of the following methods:

- **Thermal fogging** uses a petroleum-based or water-based deodorizing agent heated into a fine fog that penetrates the same porous pathways smoke traveled during a fire. It is particularly effective in wood-framed structures where smoke has migrated into wall cavities.
- **Hydroxyl deodorization** generates hydroxyl radicals that break down odor-causing molecules at the chemical level. Hydroxyls are safe to operate in occupied or partially occupied spaces and work continuously over time.
- **Ozone treatment** is reserved for unoccupied spaces where a high-concentration application is appropriate. Ozone is effective for severe smoke and organic odors but requires the space to be vacated and properly ventilated before re-entry.
- **Encapsulant sealers** are applied to surfaces such as concrete, OSB subfloor, or framing that cannot be removed and still carry residual odor after cleaning.

The team holds IICRC FSRT (Fire and Smoke Restoration Technician) certification, which means the selection of method is based on material type and odor source, not a one-size approach.
