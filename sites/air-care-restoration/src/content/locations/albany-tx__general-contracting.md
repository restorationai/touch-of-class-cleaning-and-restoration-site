## Why Albany Properties Present Unique Renovation Challenges

Shackelford County's clay-heavy soils expand and contract with every wet-dry cycle, and Albany gets enough of both to keep that cycle active. Homes built in the mid-twentieth century — a common vintage in Albany's residential neighborhoods — often sit on pier-and-beam foundations that have shifted over decades of that movement. Before a kitchen remodel or bathroom renovation can begin in earnest, it pays to walk the subfloor carefully: soft spots, out-of-level runs, and compromised joists are common findings in houses of that era, and discovering them mid-project is far more expensive than addressing them in the planning phase.

The region's heat also affects material selection in ways that matter for long-term performance. Exterior cladding, caulks, and adhesives rated for extreme temperature swings hold up better in this climate than products spec'd for milder regions. A general contractor who works West Texas regularly understands those product differences; one who doesn't may deliver a remodel that looks finished on day one but shows stress fractures within a season.

## Local Note

Albany's older commercial and residential buildings near the downtown area frequently used full-dimension lumber — true 2×4s and 2×6s cut to their stated size, unlike the nominal dimensions standard today. When renovation work opens those walls, standard modern framing materials don't swap in on a one-to-one basis. Blocking, shimming, and sometimes custom-milled lumber are needed to maintain structural continuity. It's a detail that adds a half-day to a framing phase but prevents the kind of out-of-square finish work that shows up in door reveals and tile lines for the life of the building.
