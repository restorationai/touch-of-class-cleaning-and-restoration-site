## Why Eastland Properties Require a Thorough Post-Construction Clean

Eastland's building stock spans a wide range of eras. Many homes in the older residential corridors near downtown were built mid-century with plaster walls, cast-iron plumbing, and wood-framed soffits — materials that trap construction particulate differently than modern drywall assemblies. When a kitchen gut-and-rebuild or a bathroom retile happens in one of these older homes, drywall dust and joint compound residue can migrate into wall cavities and settle on plaster surfaces that are already slightly porous from age.

On the commercial side, Eastland's main corridors see periodic retail and office renovation, and contractors working under tight punch-list timelines often leave behind silica dust, adhesive overspray, and caulk smear that standard janitorial crews aren't equipped to address. A proper final clean isn't cosmetic — it's a health and liability issue, especially in spaces that will reopen to the public.

The region's wind patterns compound the problem. West Texas gusts push exterior dust through gaps around temporary door covers and window protections, meaning interior surfaces accumulate a secondary layer of caliche and red clay particulate on top of the construction debris itself.

## Our Post-Construction Cleaning Process in Eastland

Every post-construction clean begins with a walk-through to categorize the scope: rough clean, final clean, or specialty clean. Rough cleans address bulk debris — drywall scraps, fasteners, packaging, adhesive residue — before finish materials are in place. Final cleans happen after flooring, fixtures, and trim are installed, and they require a different touch: HEPA-filtered vacuuming of every horizontal surface before any wet wiping, so abrasive dust particles don't scratch new hardwood or stone.

Specialty cleaning layers on top of that when the project involves circumstances like post-fire odor neutralization after a renovation that disturbed char material, or EPA RRP protocols on pre-1978 homes where sanding or scraping was part of the scope. Air Care Restoration's owner holds EPA Certified Renovator credentials under the RRP rule, which matters on Eastland's older housing stock where lead-based paint is a realistic possibility in any renovation that touches original trim or window casings.

The process sequence we follow:

- **Debris removal and site prep** — all bulk construction waste cleared before cleaning begins
- **HEPA vacuuming** — walls, ceilings, light fixtures, cabinet interiors, HVAC registers, window tracks
- **Surface cleaning** — streak-free glass, grout haze removal from tile, adhesive residue from floors
- **Air scrubbing** — HEPA air scrubbers run during and after cleaning to capture airborne particulate
- **Final inspection** — room-by-room walkthrough against a documented checklist before sign-off

## Local Note

One thing that catches out-of-area cleaning crews in Eastland is the caliche soil that gets tracked into construction sites throughout the project. Caliche — the calcium carbonate-rich hardpan common across Eastland County — dries into a fine white powder that looks superficially similar to drywall dust but behaves differently on porous surfaces like grout and unfinished concrete. It requires a different pH-balanced cleaning approach than standard joint compound residue. Crews unfamiliar with West Texas soils sometimes treat it the same way and end up leaving a haze on tile floors that only becomes visible once the lighting is fully installed. Knowing the difference before you start saves a second mobilization.
