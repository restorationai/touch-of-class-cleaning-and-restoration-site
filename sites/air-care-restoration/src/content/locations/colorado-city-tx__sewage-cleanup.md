## Why Colorado City Properties See Sewage Backup Issues

Mitchell County's soil profile — dense caliche over clay-heavy substrate — is notoriously hard on buried pipe. The ground heaves slightly in wet winters and contracts sharply in the dry summer heat, and that movement stresses clay tile and older cast-iron sewer laterals that were never designed for decades of thermal cycling. Many homes in Colorado City were built in the mid-twentieth century, when 4-inch clay tile was the standard for residential drain lines. Those pipes are now well past their design life, and root intrusion from mature trees compounds the problem.

Septic systems on the rural edges of Mitchell County face a different pressure: drought years drive homeowners to water lawns heavily, which can hydraulically overload a drain field that was already marginal. When the field fails, sewage backs up through the lowest fixture in the house — usually a floor drain, toilet, or washing-machine standpipe — and spreads fast across hard flooring before anyone realizes what happened.

Municipal sewer customers in Colorado City can also experience main-line surcharges during heavy rain events. When the city's collection system receives more flow than it can carry, the pressure reverses and pushes sewage back through private laterals. A single storm can affect multiple homes on the same block within the same hour.

## Our Sewage Cleanup and Sanitization Process in Colorado City

Raw sewage — classified as Category 3 "black water" under the IICRC S500 standard — requires a response protocol that goes well beyond mopping and bleaching. The process Air Care Restoration follows in Colorado City homes and commercial properties moves through these stages:

**Containment and PPE setup.** Affected areas are isolated to prevent cross-contamination to unaffected rooms. Crew members work in full personal protective equipment throughout extraction and demolition.

**Extraction and removal.** Standing sewage is extracted with truck-mounted or portable units. Porous materials — carpet, pad, drywall that wicked contamination, insulation — are removed and bagged for disposal. In West Texas homes with pier-and-beam construction, the crawl space under the floor is inspected; sewage that drains through subfloor gaps can pool in the crawl and go undetected.

**Structural drying.** After contaminated materials are out, the remaining structure is dried using industrial dehumidifiers and air movers. Moisture readings are logged at each visit until affected assemblies reach documented dry standard.

**Sanitization and deodorization.** Hard surfaces, framing, and subfloor decking are treated with EPA-registered disinfectants rated for Category 3 contamination. Odor is addressed at the source — not masked — using hydroxyl or thermal fogging where appropriate.

**Clearance documentation.** Before the job is closed, moisture readings and sanitization records are compiled for the insurance carrier and for the homeowner's records.

Most standard homeowner policies cover sudden and accidental sewage backups, though coverage for sewer backup is sometimes a separate endorsement that not every homeowner knows they have — or don't have.
