## Why Tuscola Properties Need a Different Kind of Final Clean

West Texas construction dust is not the same animal as what you'd find after a remodel in a humid climate. The region's dry, alkaline soil — common throughout Taylor County — becomes airborne easily during grading and framing, and it settles into every horizontal surface, HVAC return, and cabinet interior. Drywall compound dried under low-humidity conditions can produce a finer particulate than in wetter climates, meaning it migrates further and hides in places a visual inspection misses.

Older structures in the Tuscola area present an additional layer of complexity. Ranch-era homes and outbuildings from the mid-twentieth century may have original wood windows, single-pane glazing, and unsealed masonry that absorbs dust differently than modern construction. Renovation work on these properties can also disturb lead-containing paint in homes built before 1978 — Air Care Restoration holds EPA Lead-Safe Certification, which matters when a specialty clean follows work on older materials.

New construction in the area — whether a spec home or an agricultural shop — typically generates a substantial debris load: scrap lumber, packaging foam, fastener strips, and adhesive residue on hard flooring. A final clean that skips the detail work leaves a property that looks finished until a buyer or tenant looks closer.

## Our Post-Construction Cleaning Process in Tuscola

A post-construction clean is not a single pass — it works in phases, because disturbing debris on one surface redeposits particles on surfaces you already cleaned.

**Phase 1 — Rough clean:** Removal of bulk debris, scrap material, and packaging. We sweep and vacuum structural surfaces before any wet cleaning begins. HEPA-filtered vacuums capture fine particulate rather than recirculating it.

**Phase 2 — Detail clean:** Windowsills, door frames, cabinet interiors, light fixtures, outlet covers, and HVAC registers. This is where most post-construction cleans fall short. Drywall dust in a register can cycle back through a new HVAC system for weeks if it's not pulled and cleaned at this stage.

**Phase 3 — Surface clean:** Hard flooring, countertops, appliances, and glass. Adhesive residue from protective film on windows and appliances requires the right solvent — the wrong one can etch glass or dull a stone countertop finish.

**Phase 4 — Final inspection walk:** We check against a punch-list format, surface by surface, before signing off. If a subcontractor returns for touch-up work after our clean, we can schedule a return detail pass.

Our team is IICRC certified and OSHA trained, which means the process is documented and the safety protocols — particularly around dust control and any lead-safe work practices — are followed as a standard, not an exception.

## Local Note

One thing that comes up specifically in Taylor County construction is the behavior of caliche-heavy soil during and after a build. Caliche — the calcium carbonate hardpan common across this part of West Texas — gets tracked onto finished concrete slabs and hard flooring during the final construction phases, and it bonds differently than standard dirt once it dries. Standard mopping smears it; it needs to be loosened with the right pH-appropriate cleaner before it releases. A crew that hasn't worked in this soil type may leave a haze on new flooring that the property owner assumes is a defect in the finish. It isn't — it's caliche residue, and it comes off cleanly with the right approach.
