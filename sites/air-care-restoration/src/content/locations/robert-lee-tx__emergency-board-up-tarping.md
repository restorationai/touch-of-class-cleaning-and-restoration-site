## Our Emergency Board-Up and Tarping Process in Robert Lee

Every call starts with a quick phone assessment so the crew arrives with the right materials. Plywood gauge, tarp dimensions, and anchor method all depend on what failed and what's still structurally sound around it.

On arrival, the crew documents the damage with photographs before touching anything — this record matters for your insurance claim and establishes the pre-mitigation condition. Broken glass is cleared and framing is inspected before plywood is cut and secured; a board installed over a compromised frame can create a false sense of security without actually stopping air or water infiltration. For roof work, tarps are run from ridge to eave when possible, with weighted battens or fastened edges rather than loose corners that the next gust can catch. The IICRC FSRT certification the team carries means fire-damage board-ups follow a process that accounts for smoke-weakened materials — charred framing doesn't hold fasteners the same way undamaged wood does, and the crew adjusts anchor points accordingly.

When the structure is stabilized, a written scope is prepared that documents what was installed, where, and why — information your adjuster will ask for.

## Local Note

One thing that catches out-of-area contractors working in Robert Lee: the caliche soil common across Coke County makes ground anchoring for exterior tarping systems less reliable than it looks. Caliche crusts hard at the surface but can be loose and crumbly underneath, which means a stake driven into a yard may not hold under sustained wind load the way it would in clay-heavy soil. The crew accounts for this by prioritizing structural anchor points — roof decking, fascia, and existing framing — over ground stakes whenever the tarp geometry allows it.
