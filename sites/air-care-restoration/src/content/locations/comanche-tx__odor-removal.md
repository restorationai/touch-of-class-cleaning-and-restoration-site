## Why Comanche Properties Hold Odors Longer

The older housing stock throughout Comanche is a significant factor in how odors behave. Many homes in the area were built with solid wood framing, plaster-and-lath walls, and pier-and-beam foundations, all of which absorb and retain odors far more stubbornly than modern construction materials. Smoke from a kitchen fire, for example, can penetrate the soft plaster substrate and settle into the wood lath behind it. At the surface the wall looks fine. Underneath, the char smell is still there.

Comanche's climate adds another layer. Summer temperatures regularly push past 100°F, and that heat drives volatile organic compounds deeper into porous surfaces. A musty smell that seems mild in spring can become overwhelming by July as heat accelerates off-gassing. Conversely, the region's low winter humidity can cause odor-bearing residues to dry and crystallize inside HVAC ducts, where they re-release every time the system cycles on.

## Our Odor Removal and Deodorization Process in Comanche

Every odor job starts with source identification, because no deodorization method works if the underlying cause is still present. Once the source is confirmed and addressed, we select the appropriate treatment method for the specific odor type and the materials involved.

For smoke odor removal after a fire, thermal fogging is often the most effective first step. A fogging agent is heated into a fine vapor that penetrates the same pathways smoke traveled, bonding with and neutralizing residue inside wall cavities, subfloor spaces, and ductwork. For biological odors, including mold and sewage, we use hydroxyl deodorization, which generates hydroxyl radicals that break down odor molecules without requiring occupants to vacate for extended periods. Ozone treatment is reserved for unoccupied spaces where a more aggressive approach is warranted, such as a vacant home with severe pet or smoke contamination.

After treatment, we verify results with post-treatment air quality checks rather than relying on a technician's nose alone. In Comanche's older homes, we pay particular attention to crawl spaces and pier-and-beam voids, where odors can linger long after the living areas clear.
