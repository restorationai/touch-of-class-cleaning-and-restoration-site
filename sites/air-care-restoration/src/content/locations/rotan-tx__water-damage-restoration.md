## Why Rotan Properties Are Vulnerable to Water Damage

Fisher County's expansive clay soils are a double-edged problem. During drought — which West Texas sees often — the clay shrinks and pulls away from foundations, opening hairline gaps that go unnoticed until a heavy rain fills them with water. Rotan's housing stock leans older, and many homes were built on pier-and-beam foundations that leave a shallow crawl space beneath the living area. That crawl space becomes a reservoir when soil drainage fails, and standing water underneath a house can saturate wood joists and subflooring for weeks before a homeowner notices soft spots underfoot or a musty smell drifting up through the floor registers.

Rotan also sits in a region where plumbing infrastructure in older homes often includes galvanized steel supply lines. Galvanized pipe corrodes from the inside out, and a line that looks intact on the outside can fail suddenly, releasing water inside a wall cavity. Because the failure is hidden, the damage is often well advanced by the time it's discovered — drywall is saturated, insulation is compressed, and wood framing has begun to swell.

## Our Water Damage Restoration Process in Rotan

When a crew arrives at a Rotan property, the first priority is stopping any active water source and documenting the full scope of the loss before anything is moved or removed. Moisture meters and thermal imaging cameras map the boundary of saturation — including areas behind walls and under flooring that look dry at the surface but are holding water inside the assembly.

Extraction comes next. Truck-mounted and portable extraction units pull standing water from carpets, hardwood, and concrete slabs. Once bulk water is out, the drying phase begins: industrial air movers are positioned to create directed airflow across wet surfaces, and low-grain refrigerant dehumidifiers pull the resulting vapor out of the air. Drying logs are recorded daily so the process can be adjusted and documented for your insurance carrier.

In homes with pier-and-beam construction — common in this part of Fisher County — the crawl space gets its own attention. Moisture readings are taken at the joists and subfloor, and if conditions warrant, additional equipment is deployed below grade. Structural drying in a crawl space takes longer than above-grade work because airflow is restricted and ambient humidity can be high, but skipping it leads to rot and secondary mold growth that costs far more to fix later.

## Local Note

One thing that catches Rotan homeowners off guard is how slowly caliche-heavy soil releases moisture back into the air after a flood event. When water soaks into the ground around a slab-on-grade foundation and then the weather turns dry, the soil doesn't dry evenly — it dries at the surface while staying saturated deeper, and that subsurface moisture continues to push vapor through the slab and into the living space. Drying equipment placed only in the visible wet areas can show passing readings while the slab is still off-gassing moisture from below. Crews working in this region need to monitor slab moisture content directly, not just ambient humidity, to know when drying is genuinely complete.
