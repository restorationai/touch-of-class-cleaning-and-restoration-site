## Why Brownwood's Building Stock Carries Real Asbestos Risk

Brown County's growth during the 1940s through the 1970s tracks almost exactly with peak asbestos use in American construction. The housing stock built during that window commonly contains asbestos in several places that are easy to overlook: the textured coating on ceilings, the vinyl floor tiles and the black mastic beneath them, the insulation wrapped around HVAC ducts and hot-water pipes, the joint compound in drywall seams, and the roofing felt under shingles. None of these materials pose an immediate hazard when they are intact and undisturbed. The risk comes when they are cut, sanded, drilled, or broken during a remodel or after structural damage, releasing microscopic fibers into the air.

Brownwood's climate adds a layer of complexity. The region sees significant temperature swings between summer heat and winter cold fronts that push through the Rolling Plains. That thermal cycling causes building materials to expand and contract repeatedly over decades, which can cause older asbestos-containing materials to crack or delaminate over time. A ceiling texture that looked fine last year may be in worse condition after a hard freeze followed by a stretch of 100-degree days.

## Our Asbestos Abatement Process in Brownwood

Every project starts with a proper inspection and bulk sampling before any removal work begins. Samples are sent to an accredited laboratory for analysis. Skipping this step and assuming a material is safe is not an option under Texas Department of State Health Services regulations, and it is not something Air Care Restoration does.

Once testing confirms the presence of asbestos-containing material, the abatement process follows a defined sequence:

- **Containment:** Affected areas are isolated with heavy-gauge polyethylene sheeting and negative air pressure is established using HEPA-filtered air scrubbers. This prevents fibers from migrating to adjacent rooms.
- **Wet removal:** Materials are wetted before and during removal to suppress fiber release. Dry removal is not used.
- **Sealed disposal:** All removed material is double-bagged in labeled, leak-tight containers and transported to a licensed disposal facility. Texas regulations govern both the handling and the disposal destination.
- **Air clearance:** After removal and cleanup, air samples are collected and analyzed to confirm fiber levels are within acceptable limits before containment is removed and the space is released for other trades.

The team is OSHA trained and the company holds EPA Lead-Safe Certification, which matters on older Brownwood properties where lead-based paint and asbestos often coexist in the same structure.
