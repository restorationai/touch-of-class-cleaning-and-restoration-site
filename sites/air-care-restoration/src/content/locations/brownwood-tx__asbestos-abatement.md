## Why Brownwood Properties Carry a Higher Asbestos Risk

Brown County's building boom ran hard through the 1950s, 1960s, and into the early 1970s — the exact window when asbestos use in residential and commercial construction peaked nationally. Homes built during that era commonly contain asbestos in floor tile adhesive (the mastic beneath 9×9 vinyl tiles is almost always suspect), duct wrap on HVAC systems, textured ceiling coatings, and exterior siding products like transite panels. The dry, hot summers and periodic freeze-thaw cycles that Central Texas sees each winter accelerate the deterioration of these materials over time. Brittle floor tiles crack. Duct insulation crumbles. Roofing felt separates at the seams. Each of those failure modes can release fibers into living spaces without any visible warning.

Older commercial buildings along the city's main corridors face the same risk profile, compounded by the fact that many were renovated piecemeal over the decades — new drywall over old plaster, drop ceilings installed below original tile, layers of flooring stacked on top of one another. That kind of layered construction makes asbestos inspection more involved, because the hazardous material may not be the most visible layer.

## Our Asbestos Abatement Process in Brownwood

Every project starts with a thorough inspection and bulk sampling of suspect materials. Samples go to an accredited laboratory — results typically come back within a few business days, though rush turnaround is available when a project timeline is tight. Once the lab confirms the presence of asbestos-containing material, we develop a site-specific abatement plan that accounts for the scope, the occupancy status of the building, and any permitting requirements under Texas Department of State Health Services regulations.

Containment is established before any material is disturbed. That means critical barriers, negative air pressure with HEPA-filtered air scrubbers, and full personal protective equipment for every technician on site. Removal follows regulated wet methods to suppress fiber release, and all waste is packaged, labeled, and transported to a licensed disposal facility — nothing is left on-site or placed in standard waste streams. After abatement is complete, air clearance testing confirms that fiber levels have returned to acceptable thresholds before containment comes down and the space is released for re-occupancy or continued renovation.
