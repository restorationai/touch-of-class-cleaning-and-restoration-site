## Why Tuscola Properties See Sewage Backup Issues

Much of Tuscola's residential development sits outside the municipal sewer grid, which means a large share of homes depend on private septic systems. Those systems are only as reliable as their drain fields — and Taylor County's clay-laced soil does not drain the way sandy loam does. After a period of drought followed by even a moderate rain event, the ground can shift enough to stress lateral lines, crack tank lids, or cause drain fields to become temporarily saturated and push effluent back toward the house.

Homes built in the 1970s and 1980s — a common era for Tuscola's single-family housing stock — often have original cast-iron or Orangeburg sewer pipe that has had decades to corrode, root-intrude, or simply collapse under the weight of compacted caliche. A slow drain that homeowners tolerate for months can become a full sewage overflow in hours when a line finally gives way.

Well and septic properties also carry a secondary risk: a sewage event near a water supply line demands immediate containment to prevent cross-contamination, a concern that is less common in fully sewered urban neighborhoods.

## Our Sewage Cleanup and Sanitization Process in Tuscola

Raw sewage is classified as Category 3 water — the most contaminated classification in the IICRC S500 standard — and the response protocol reflects that. When the crew arrives, the first priority is stopping the source: shutting off water supply, confirming the septic system is pumped or the line is cleared, and establishing a containment perimeter so contaminated material does not migrate into unaffected rooms.

From there, the process moves through these stages:

- **Extraction and removal** — standing sewage water is extracted with truck-mounted or portable units rated for contaminated waste. Saturated porous materials (carpet, pad, drywall below the flood line, insulation) are removed and bagged for disposal per Texas Commission on Environmental Quality guidelines for solid waste containing biological material.
- **Surface cleaning and disinfection** — all hard surfaces in the affected zone are cleaned, then treated with EPA-registered disinfectants at appropriate dwell times. This is not a one-pass wipe-down; it is a documented multi-step sanitization sequence.
- **Structural drying** — once surfaces are disinfected, commercial desiccant dehumidifiers and air movers are placed to drive residual moisture out of subfloor assemblies, wall cavities, and concrete slabs. In Tuscola's summer heat, ambient temperatures can actually accelerate drying — but they also accelerate microbial activity in any area missed during sanitization, which is why the disinfection step cannot be shortened.
- **Post-remediation verification** — moisture readings are logged and compared against dry standard baselines before equipment is pulled. Documentation is prepared for your insurance carrier.
