## Why Potosi Properties Are Vulnerable After Storms and Fires

Potosi sits in a stretch of West Texas where thunderstorms can carry straight-line winds strong enough to lift roofing materials entirely, and where hail the size of golf balls is a genuine seasonal expectation rather than a rare event. Many homes in the area are single-story ranch construction, which puts the roofline close to the ground and makes it easier for wind-driven debris to punch through windows or ridge vents. That same low-slope roofing common to ranch-style homes can pool water rapidly once the underlayment is compromised, turning a missing shingle field into a soaked attic within a single rain event.

Fire damage is the other major driver of emergency board-up calls in this part of Taylor County. A kitchen fire or electrical fire that stays contained to one room still typically blows out windows from heat pressure, and the resulting openings invite both weather and unauthorized entry before a homeowner has even finished talking to the fire marshal. Securing those openings quickly is not just about the structure; it's about protecting whatever contents and salvageable materials remain inside.

## Our Emergency Board-Up and Tarping Process in Potosi

When a call comes in, the first step is a rapid site assessment to identify every breach: broken windows, compromised doors, damaged soffits, and any section of roof where the decking or underlayment is exposed. In West Texas, that assessment has to account for ongoing wind, because a tarp installed without proper ballasting or strapping in this region will be gone by morning.

For roof tarping, we use heavy-duty polyethylene tarps secured with wood battens and fastened to the decking rather than simply weighted at the edges. This matters in Potosi's wind environment. A tarp that flaps loose overnight creates its own damage as it slaps against exposed wood and tears away the very protection it was placed to provide. For window and door board-up, we cut plywood to fit each opening precisely, fastening it to the surrounding framing rather than just the exterior cladding, so the board stays in place through additional weather events while the insurance process moves forward.

We document every opening, every measurement, and every material used with photographs before and after installation. That documentation goes directly into your claim file.

## Insurance Coordination for Potosi Board-Up Claims

Most homeowners' insurance policies in Texas cover emergency protective measures, including board-up and tarping, as part of the initial loss. The key is documentation. Carriers want to see that the work was necessary, that it was performed promptly to prevent further damage, and that the scope was reasonable. Our written scope and photo documentation are formatted to meet those requirements, and we can communicate directly with your adjuster to answer questions about materials and methods.

If your policy requires you to mitigate further damage and you delay securing the property, some carriers will use that delay to dispute a portion of the subsequent damage claim. Getting board-up and tarping done quickly is not just about protecting the structure; it's about protecting your claim.

## Local Note

One thing that catches some Potosi homeowners off guard: the clay-heavy soil common to this part of Taylor County expands and contracts significantly with moisture changes, and that soil movement can affect how exterior door frames sit in their rough openings. After a storm event that soaks the ground, a door frame that was plumb before the storm may have shifted enough that a standard pre-cut board-up panel no longer fits flush. We measure on-site rather than cutting to standard dimensions, because in this area, assuming a square opening is a good way to leave a gap.
