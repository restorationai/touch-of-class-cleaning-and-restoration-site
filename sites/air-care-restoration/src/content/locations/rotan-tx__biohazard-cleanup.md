## Why Rotan Properties Present Unique Biohazard Considerations

Fisher County's housing stock leans older, and many Rotan homes were built in mid-century construction eras when subflooring, insulation, and wall cavities were assembled with materials that absorb biological contamination differently than modern composites. Porous concrete slabs, original hardwood floors, and plaster-over-lath wall systems can hold contamination well below the visible surface — meaning what looks contained at eye level may have already wicked deeper into the structure. The region's low humidity in dry months can accelerate surface drying while leaving saturated materials hidden underneath, a pattern that makes thorough testing and documentation essential before any area is cleared.

The agricultural and ranching character of the area also means that outbuildings, shops, and detached structures are common on Rotan properties — and these spaces often lack the ventilation and climate control of a main residence, which affects how biological material behaves and how remediation equipment is staged.

## Our Biohazard Cleanup Process in Rotan

Every biohazard situation is different, but the process follows a disciplined sequence regardless of the circumstances. Our OSHA-trained, IICRC-certified team arrives in unmarked vehicles to protect your privacy. The first priority is containment — isolating the affected area so that the rest of the structure is not exposed during work. We use hospital-grade EPA-registered disinfectants and follow regulated disposal protocols for all biological and infectious material, including sharps and needles when present.

After physical removal, affected surfaces are treated and tested before any area is released. In older Rotan homes where original flooring or plaster is involved, we assess whether porous materials need to be removed entirely or whether surface treatment is sufficient — that decision is documented and explained to the property owner before any structural material is touched. All waste is transported and disposed of in compliance with Texas Department of State Health Services regulations for biohazardous material.
