West Texas wind doesn't quit, and in Rotan it carries something extra: fine caliche dust, cotton gin particulates during harvest season, and the kind of dry grit that works its way into every gap in a home's envelope. That same air cycles through your HVAC system dozens of times a day, depositing layers inside ductwork that no filter fully catches. Over time, those deposits restrict airflow, stress equipment, and push allergens back into living spaces — a pattern that's especially pronounced in Fisher County's flat, open terrain where there's little to slow what the wind brings.

## Why Rotan Properties See Duct Buildup Faster Than You'd Expect

Rotan sits in a region where the climate works against clean ductwork in two distinct seasons. Summer heat pushes systems to run nearly continuously, pulling air — and whatever rides with it — through ducts for months on end. Then winter arrives with northers that drive dust through every unsealed penetration in older homes. Many residences in and around Rotan were built during the mid-twentieth century oil and agricultural boom, when duct insulation standards were minimal and flex duct wasn't yet common. Sheet metal runs in those homes often have joints that were sealed with mastic or foil tape that has since dried and cracked, creating gaps where dust accumulates and rodents occasionally nest.

Cotton farming is still active in Fisher County, and harvest season — typically late summer into fall — introduces a fine lint and dust load that is noticeably heavier than what urban systems handle. If your system runs during that window without a recently serviced duct system, that material settles into return plenums and branch runs where it stays until someone physically removes it.

## Our Air Duct Cleaning Process in Rotan

The process starts with a camera inspection of main trunk lines and accessible branch runs. This isn't a formality — in older Rotan homes with original ductwork, it's common to find debris accumulation, disconnected joints, or evidence of pest activity before we ever attach equipment. Knowing what's there shapes how we approach the job.

Once the system is mapped, we use negative-pressure equipment — a high-powered vacuum unit connected at the air handler — to create continuous suction through the duct system while agitation tools work through each branch. Rotary brushes and compressed-air whips dislodge material from duct walls and drive it toward the vacuum rather than back into the home. Supply and return sides are cleaned separately to ensure full coverage.

For systems showing microbial growth — something we see in Rotan homes where roof leaks or evaporative cooler condensation has introduced moisture into ductwork — we apply an EPA-registered sanitizing agent after mechanical cleaning. This step is optional, not automatic, and we'll walk you through what we found before recommending it.

Dryer vent cleaning follows the same inspection-first approach. Rotan's dry air means lint dries fast and compacts tightly in vent runs, particularly in homes where the dryer is positioned far from an exterior wall and the vent run is long or has multiple elbows.
