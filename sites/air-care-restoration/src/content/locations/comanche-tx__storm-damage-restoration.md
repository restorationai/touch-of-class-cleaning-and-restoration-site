Comanche sits in the heart of the Texas Cross Timbers, where the open rolling terrain gives spring and summer thunderstorms almost nothing to slow them down before they reach your roof. Hail the size of quarters, straight-line winds that can top 70 mph, and the occasional tornado touchdown are part of life in Comanche County — and when that weather hits, the damage it leaves behind moves fast. Water intrudes through breached shingles within minutes, and structural stress from downed trees or debris can compromise framing that isn't immediately visible from the ground.

## Why Comanche Properties See Serious Storm Damage

The geography here works against homeowners. The Cross Timbers region funnels storm cells from the southwest with little natural windbreak, and Comanche's position in the southern part of the county means it sits squarely in the path of the most active convective corridors in Central Texas. That translates to a higher-than-average frequency of large hail events and damaging wind gusts compared to more sheltered parts of the state.

The local housing stock adds another layer of vulnerability. A significant portion of homes in Comanche were built in the mid-20th century, when roof decking was often 1×6 skip sheathing rather than continuous plywood panels. That construction style is charming and durable under normal conditions, but it leaves more gaps for wind-driven rain to exploit once even a few shingles lift. Older wood-framed structures also tend to have less engineered uplift resistance than post-2000 builds, so fascia, soffits, and gable ends are frequently the first points of failure in a severe storm.

## Our Storm Damage Restoration Process in Comanche

When you call Air Care Restoration after a storm, the first priority is stopping the damage from spreading — not paperwork. Our IICRC-certified team arrives with tarping materials and board-up supplies to close any breach in the building envelope before moisture has time to migrate into wall cavities or subfloor assemblies. That initial emergency stabilization is what separates a manageable repair from a mold remediation job six weeks later.

Once the structure is secured, we conduct a full damage assessment using moisture meters and thermal imaging to map water intrusion that isn't visible to the naked eye. Water that enters through a compromised roof can travel along rafters and top plates before pooling in a ceiling or wall well away from the original entry point. We document every affected area with photographs and measurements — the same documentation your insurance adjuster will need to process your claim.

Drying and structural drying equipment is staged based on what the readings show, not a generic room count. Industrial air movers, desiccant or refrigerant dehumidifiers, and in some cases specialty drying mats for hardwood floors are deployed and monitored daily until the structure reaches its dry standard per the IICRC S500 and S520 guidelines.

## Local Note

One pattern we see repeatedly in older Comanche-area homes: the original interior plaster ceilings act as a temporary dam after a roof breach, holding water in the ceiling cavity long enough that the damage isn't obvious until the plaster begins to sag or stain.
