## Why Robert Lee Properties See Water Damage Issues

Coke County's caliche-heavy soil is notoriously poor at draining water quickly. During a hard rain event, that tight, chalky substrate forces runoff to travel laterally rather than percolate downward — which means it follows the path of least resistance straight toward your foundation. Homes built on slab foundations, which are common throughout Robert Lee, are particularly vulnerable to water intrusion along the perimeter when the ground becomes temporarily saturated.

Aging supply lines are another recurring source of loss here. Many homes in Robert Lee were built during the mid-twentieth century oil and ranching boom, and original galvanized steel or early copper plumbing is now well past its service life. A pinhole leak inside a wall cavity can go undetected for weeks in a climate where surface humidity is low — the wall face stays dry while moisture saturates the framing and insulation behind it. By the time a stain appears, secondary damage is already underway.

Water heater failures and HVAC condensate line backups are also common in the intense summer heat. Equipment that runs hard for months on end wears faster, and a slow drip from a condensate pan can saturate a utility closet floor before the next service visit.

## Our Water Damage Restoration Process in Robert Lee

Every job starts with a thorough moisture inspection — not just the visible wet area, but the adjacent walls, subfloor, and ceiling cavities that water travels into before it stops. We use thermal imaging and calibrated moisture meters to map the full extent of saturation, because in slab-foundation homes the water often migrates further than it appears to on the surface.

Once the scope is documented, we extract standing water with truck-mounted and portable extraction units, then set a calculated drying system: commercial-grade air movers positioned to create a directed airflow pattern, and refrigerant or desiccant dehumidifiers sized to the moisture load of the space. Drying is monitored daily against the IICRC S500 standard — we're pulling readings and adjusting equipment until structural materials reach their target moisture content, not just until the floor feels dry underfoot.

If building materials — drywall, insulation, flooring — have absorbed enough water that drying in place isn't viable, we perform controlled demolition to remove only what can't be saved. Documentation of every removed material goes into your claim file.
