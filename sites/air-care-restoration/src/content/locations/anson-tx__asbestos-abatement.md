## Why Anson Properties Face Asbestos Concerns

Jones County's building boom largely tracked the agricultural and oil-field economy of the mid-twentieth century, which means a significant portion of Anson's residential and commercial properties were constructed during the peak years of asbestos use, roughly 1940 through the late 1970s. During that period, asbestos showed up in floor tiles, pipe insulation, roof shingles, joint compound, duct tape on HVAC systems, and the spray-applied texture coatings that became known as popcorn ceilings.

Anson's dry climate creates a specific risk factor worth understanding. Low humidity and temperature swings between summer highs and winter cold cause building materials to expand and contract repeatedly over decades. That mechanical stress can fracture brittle asbestos-containing materials, shifting them from "non-friable" to "friable" status, meaning fibers can become airborne more easily. A ceiling texture that looked intact for thirty years can begin shedding after a roof leak, an HVAC replacement, or even aggressive cleaning.

Texas also enforces asbestos regulations through the Texas Department of State Health Services and requires licensed contractors for regulated asbestos abatement work. Any renovation that disturbs a threshold quantity of asbestos-containing material in a commercial or public building triggers formal notification and removal requirements. Residential projects carry their own obligations, and homeowners who attempt DIY removal without proper containment and disposal can face liability for improper handling.

## Our Asbestos Abatement Process in Anson

Every project starts with a thorough inspection and, where indicated, bulk sampling sent to an accredited laboratory. We do not guess at whether a material contains asbestos. Confirmed results drive the scope of work, which keeps costs honest and protects you from unnecessary removal.

Once the scope is defined, the process follows a consistent sequence:

- **Containment setup**: Plastic sheeting and negative air pressure units isolate the work area so fibers cannot migrate into adjacent living or working spaces.
- **Regulated removal**: Technicians in appropriate personal protective equipment wet the material before removal to suppress fiber release, then seal waste in labeled, double-bagged containers.
- **Air monitoring**: Clearance air sampling confirms fiber levels have returned to acceptable levels before containment is broken.
- **Proper disposal**: Asbestos waste is transported to a licensed disposal facility in compliance with Texas regulations. We handle the chain of custody documentation.

In Anson's older single-story homes, we frequently encounter asbestos in more than one location during a single project. Floor tile adhesive beneath vinyl flooring, pipe wrap in crawl spaces, and ceiling texture can all be present simultaneously. Identifying all suspect materials before demolition begins prevents mid-project surprises.

## Local Note

One pattern that comes up repeatedly in West Texas towns like Anson is the combination of original asbestos floor tile beneath multiple layers of later flooring. A homeowner pulls up modern vinyl plank, finds 1970s peel-and-stick tile, and assumes that's the bottom layer. In many Jones County homes it isn't. The 1970s tile was often installed directly over original 9x9 asphalt-backed tile from the 1950s or early 1960s, and that lower layer is the one most likely to contain asbestos. Sampling only the visible layer and stopping there can lead to an incomplete scope of work. We always probe for layering before finalizing a removal plan.
