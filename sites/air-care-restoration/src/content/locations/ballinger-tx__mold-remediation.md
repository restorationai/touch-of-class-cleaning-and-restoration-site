## Why Ballinger Properties See Mold Issues

The same West Texas climate that brings scorching July heat also delivers sudden, heavy thunderstorms — sometimes dropping several inches of rain in a matter of hours. Runnels County's expansive clay soils absorb that water slowly, and when the ground is already saturated, moisture migrates toward foundations and through slab edges into living spaces. Older homes in Ballinger, many of which were built with pier-and-beam foundations or early slab construction, are especially vulnerable: gaps around plumbing penetrations, settling cracks, and aging vapor barriers all create entry points.

Once moisture is inside, the region's heat accelerates mold growth dramatically. A slow leak under a kitchen sink or a condensation problem on an aging HVAC unit can produce visible colonies within days during summer months. Attic spaces are a particular concern — inadequate ventilation combined with high ambient temperatures creates a near-ideal environment for mold to establish itself long before a homeowner notices any discoloration or odor.

## Our Mold Remediation Process in Ballinger

In Texas, mold assessment, protocol writing, and post-remediation verification are performed by an independent licensed Mold Assessment Consultant — a separate role from remediation. Air Care Restoration holds Texas Mold Remediation Company License RCO1798 and performs the remediation work defined in the consultant's protocol.

Every remediation begins with a thorough review of the consultant's protocol and our own moisture investigation of affected materials — not just the visible surface, but the materials behind it. Moisture meters and thermal imaging help locate saturation that reads dry at the surface while the substrate underneath remains wet. In Ballinger's older housing stock, that distinction matters: original plaster, wood lath, and early-era insulation hold moisture differently than modern materials, and drying timelines need to reflect that.

Once the scope is established, the affected area is contained using negative air pressure and physical barriers to prevent spores from migrating to clean areas of the home. Contaminated porous materials — drywall, insulation, flooring underlayment — are removed and bagged for disposal in accordance with industry protocols. Structural surfaces are then treated and dried to documented moisture levels before any reconstruction begins. Throughout the process, technicians follow containment and PPE standards that protect both the crew and the occupants. Remediation is carried out to meet the post-remediation verification criteria established in the Mold Remediation Protocol, with final clearance conducted by the independent Mold Assessment Consultant.
