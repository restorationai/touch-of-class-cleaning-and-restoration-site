## Why Brownwood Properties Experience Sewage Backups

Brownwood's housing stock spans a wide range of eras, and the age of a home's plumbing tells a lot about its vulnerability. Older homes in the city's established residential corridors were often built with cast-iron or clay-tile sewer laterals that have spent decades contracting and expanding through Central Texas summers and winters. Clay tile in particular is prone to root intrusion — the cedar elms and live oaks that shade older Brownwood streets are beautiful, but their roots are opportunistic, and a hairline crack in an aging lateral is an open invitation.

Beyond aging pipe materials, the region's shrink-swell clay soils shift enough during drought-and-rain cycles that even newer PVC laterals can develop bellied sections where waste pools instead of flowing. A single heavy rain following a dry stretch — something Brown County sees regularly — can overwhelm both municipal sewer capacity and private septic systems simultaneously, pushing sewage back through the lowest fixtures in a home.

## Our Sewage Cleanup and Sanitization Process in Brownwood

Raw sewage is classified as Category 3 water — the most hazardous contamination category under IICRC standards — and every step of the process reflects that. When we arrive at a Brownwood property, the first priority is containment: stopping the source, isolating affected areas, and protecting uncontaminated spaces from cross-contamination.

From there, the process moves through extraction, removal of porous materials that cannot be decontaminated (drywall, insulation, carpet pad), and thorough cleaning of all hard surfaces with EPA-registered antimicrobials. Brownwood's humidity levels, while lower than East Texas, are still high enough in summer that residual moisture in a structure creates real mold colonization risk within 24 to 48 hours — which is why drying equipment runs concurrently with sanitization, not after it. We document moisture readings throughout the process, and drying validation follows IICRC S500 protocols before any structural materials are replaced.

For properties on private septic systems — common on the rural edges of Brown County — we also coordinate with the property owner on the septic side of the problem, since cleanup inside the home and repair of the system outside need to move in parallel.
