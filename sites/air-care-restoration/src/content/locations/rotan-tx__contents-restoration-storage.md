When a burst pipe, a kitchen fire, or a sudden roof failure damages your home or business here, the contents inside face a second threat just as serious as the structure itself: rapid deterioration from heat, humidity cycling, and smoke residue that bakes into fabric and wood faster in this climate than almost anywhere in the state. ## Why Rotan Properties See Elevated Contents Damage

Fisher County's climate creates a specific set of conditions that accelerate damage to personal property after a loss event. The region swings between dry, desiccating winters and humid storm seasons, and that cycle is hard on older building envelopes. Many homes in and around Rotan were built in the mid-twentieth century with materials that absorb moisture readily, wood-framed walls, plaster ceilings, and pier-and-beam foundations that allow air movement underneath the floor system. When water intrudes, it wicks upward into flooring and furniture quickly. When a fire occurs, smoke travels through those same gaps and coats contents in rooms that never saw a flame.

The region's dust and caliche soil also matter. After a storm or a water loss, fine particulate that was already settled on surfaces combines with moisture to form a thin, abrasive film on electronics and soft goods. That film, if left untreated, etches finishes and degrades fabric fibers. Addressing contents within the first 24 to 48 hours is not a sales pitch; it is the actual window before secondary damage becomes permanent.

## Our Contents Restoration Process in Rotan

Every contents job begins with a room-by-room inventory before anything is moved. Each item is photographed, catalogued, and assigned a condition code. That documentation serves two purposes: it protects your insurance claim, and it ensures nothing is lost in transit.

Items that can be restored on-site, hard-surface furniture, metal fixtures, sealed electronics, are treated in place using IICRC-standard cleaning protocols. Porous items, upholstered furniture, clothing, books, area rugs, are packed and transported to a climate-controlled storage facility where they can be processed without the time pressure of an active loss site. Ozone treatment, ultrasonic cleaning, and thermal dehumidification are applied based on the material and the type of contamination, smoke, water, or biological.

For Rotan properties, we pay particular attention to items stored in detached outbuildings or garages. In this part of West Texas, it is common to find a family's secondary storage, seasonal equipment, archived documents, in a metal building adjacent to the main structure. Those spaces heat to extreme temperatures in summer, and contents stored there after a loss can warp, mold, or off-gas within days if not moved to a controlled environment promptly.

## Rotan Insurance Coordination for Contents Claims

Contents claims are often the most disputed portion of a property insurance claim because adjusters rely on documentation that policyholders rarely have ready. Our inventory process produces a line-item record that most major carriers accept directly. We photograph items before and after treatment, note pre-existing condition where visible, and provide a written scope that your adjuster can review without a site visit.

If your carrier requires a contents specialist or a separate contents rider review, we can communicate directly with the assigned adjuster to keep the process moving. Our team holds an IICRC Firm certification and IICRC FSRT (Fire and Smoke Restoration) credentials, which carriers recognize when evaluating the legitimacy of a contents restoration scope versus a replacement recommendation.

## Local Note

In Fisher County, a meaningful share of residential properties use evaporative coolers rather than refrigerated air conditioning. After a water or fire loss, those cooler systems can pull smoke odor and elevated humidity directly into the living space if they are left running. We flag this on every Rotan job and coordinate with the homeowner to shut the system down before contents processing begins. It is a small detail that makes a measurable difference in how quickly odor clears from soft goods and wall surfaces.
