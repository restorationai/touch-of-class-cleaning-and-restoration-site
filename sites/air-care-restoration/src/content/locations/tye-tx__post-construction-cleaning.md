## Why Tye Properties See Unique Post-Construction Cleaning Challenges

Taylor County's alkaline soil is notoriously fine-grained. When trades are working on a Tye property — whether it's a new build off US-83 or a gut renovation on an older ranch-style home — that pale caliche dust works its way into places a standard broom won't reach. Grout lines, window tracks, recessed lighting cans, and the interior of ductwork all collect construction particulate that, if left behind, circulates through the finished space for months.

The climate adds another layer. Tye sits in a semi-arid zone where low humidity accelerates the curing of adhesives and joint compound, but also means drywall dust becomes airborne again with the slightest air movement. A ceiling fan test-run before a thorough final clean can redistribute a week's worth of settled dust in minutes. Knowing this, a proper post-construction clean in this part of Texas has to work from the top of the structure down, sealing off HVAC systems during the process and cleaning them last.

Older homes in the area — particularly those built during the mid-century growth that shaped communities across Taylor County — often have original ductwork that accumulates construction debris from even minor renovations. A kitchen remodel that never touches the HVAC can still deposit sawdust and insulation fibers into the return plenum if the system runs during the project.

## Our Post-Construction and Specialty Cleaning Process in Tye

Every post-construction clean begins with a walkthrough to identify the scope: how many trades were involved, whether drywall was cut or hung, whether flooring was installed over existing subfloor, and whether the HVAC ran during construction. That information shapes the sequence.

The process moves in phases:

- **Rough clean**: Remove all visible debris — cut lumber, drywall scraps, packaging, tape, and hardware — before any surface cleaning begins. Vacuuming with HEPA-filtered equipment comes before any wiping, so fine dust is captured rather than smeared.
- **Detail clean**: Window tracks, door hardware, cabinet interiors, light fixtures, outlet covers, and grout lines. These are the areas that look clean from a distance and fail on close inspection.
- **Final surface clean**: Hard floors, countertops, appliances, and glass — done after all overhead and vertical surfaces are complete so nothing drips or falls onto finished work.
- **HVAC and vent cleaning**: Registers removed, wiped, and reinstalled. Return air paths checked for debris. If the system ran during construction, we flag duct cleaning as a separate recommendation.
- **Final inspection walk**: We go room by room with the same critical eye a buyer or property manager would use.

For specialty cleaning needs — post-fire odor removal, deep cleaning after a long vacancy, or sanitizing a space before occupancy — the process adapts, but the discipline of working top-to-bottom in sealed phases stays constant.

## Local Note: Caliche Dust and HVAC Systems in West Texas Builds

One thing that catches out-of-area cleaning crews in Tye and the surrounding Taylor County communities: the construction dust here isn't the same as drywall dust in a humid climate. Caliche-heavy soil particles are angular and abrasive, and they don't clump or settle the way dust does in higher-humidity regions. That means they stay airborne longer and embed more deeply into soft surfaces like carpet padding and window screen mesh. On new construction where the landscaping isn't established yet, a single windy day mid-project can undo a rough clean entirely. Timing the final clean close to the actual handoff date — not days before — is the single most practical thing an owner or GC can do to protect the work.
