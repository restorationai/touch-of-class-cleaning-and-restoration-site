Tye sits just west of Abilene in Taylor County, a small community where the housing stock tells a clear story: mid-century construction, ranch-style homes, and commercial buildings that went up during the post-war boom when asbestos was still a standard ingredient in floor tile, pipe insulation, roofing felt, and the textured ceilings that were fashionable through the 1970s. If your home or building in Tye was built before 1980, there is a reasonable chance asbestos-containing materials are present somewhere — and the dry, windy West Texas climate means disturbing those materials without proper containment can spread fibers quickly through an open structure.

## Why Tye Properties See Asbestos Concerns

Taylor County's hot summers and low humidity cause older building materials to dry out and become brittle over decades. Pipe wrap and duct insulation that was once pliable can crumble at the touch — a condition abatement professionals call "friable," and the condition that makes asbestos fibers airborne. Ranch homes common to the Tye area were frequently built with asbestos-backed vinyl floor tile in kitchens and utility rooms, asbestos joint compound in drywall seams, and blown-in attic insulation that may contain vermiculite. Popcorn ceilings applied before 1978 are another frequent find — that rough, cottage-cheese texture was often achieved with chrysotile asbestos, and sanding or scraping it without testing first is a serious exposure risk.

The region also sees a steady stream of renovation and remodel projects driven by aging infrastructure. When a homeowner pulls up old flooring, cuts into a wall to update wiring, or tears out a drop ceiling to open up a space, undisturbed asbestos can become a hazard in minutes. Testing before demolition is not just a safety measure — it is a practical requirement under EPA renovation rules that apply to contractors working on pre-1980 structures.

## Our Asbestos Abatement Process in Tye

Every project starts with a thorough inspection of the affected area. Suspect materials are sampled and sent to an accredited laboratory for analysis before any removal work begins — no guessing, no visual-only assessments. Once results confirm asbestos-containing material, the work area is isolated with negative-air containment: heavy-gauge poly sheeting, sealed penetrations, and HEPA-filtered air scrubbers running continuously to prevent cross-contamination to adjacent rooms.

Removal follows Texas Department of State Health Services (DSHS) guidelines for asbestos abatement, which govern worker protection, waste handling, and disposal at licensed facilities. Materials are wetted during removal to suppress fiber release, double-bagged in labeled asbestos waste bags, and transported to an approved disposal site — not a general dumpster. After removal, the contained area is HEPA-vacuumed and wiped, and air clearance testing confirms fiber levels meet the clearance standard before containment comes down. The Air Care Restoration team is OSHA trained and EPA Lead-Safe Certified, and the process is documented throughout for your records and any insurance or permitting needs.

## Local Note

One pattern worth knowing in the Tye and greater Taylor County area: many homes from the 1950s and 1960s were built on pier-and-beam foundations, and the crawl space beneath them sometimes contains original pipe insulation wrapped in asbestos-containing material. This insulation is often out of sight and out of mind until a plumber goes under the house for a repair. If you are scheduling any plumbing, HVAC, or foundation work on a home of that era, an asbestos inspection of the crawl space before the trade contractor starts is a low-cost step that can prevent a much larger problem — and a work stoppage — later.
