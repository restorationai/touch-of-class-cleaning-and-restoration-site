Tye sits in Taylor County where the West Texas climate swings hard between dry heat and occasional heavy rain, and that cycle has a way of revealing what older construction was hiding. Homes and commercial buildings put up before the mid-1980s along the US-83 corridor often contain asbestos in places that look perfectly ordinary: floor tile adhesive, pipe insulation, attic vermiculite, and the textured ceiling finishes that were standard practice for decades. When renovation, storm damage, or a simple ceiling repair disturbs those materials, the risk becomes immediate and the need for certified asbestos abatement in Tye becomes real.

## Why Tye Properties Show Asbestos Risk

Taylor County's building boom ran hard through the 1950s, 1960s, and into the 1970s, and Tye grew alongside Abilene during that same period. Construction materials sourced during those decades routinely contained asbestos as a fire retardant and insulator. In the dry West Texas heat, those materials can become brittle over time. A cracked floor tile, a failing pipe wrap, or a popcorn ceiling that someone sanded before knowing better can release fibers into the air without any visible warning sign.

The region's wind is another factor worth understanding. Tye is not a sheltered urban environment. When abatement work is done incorrectly or without proper containment, cross-contamination to adjacent spaces or even neighboring properties is a genuine concern. That is why the containment and negative air pressure protocols used in certified abatement matter more here than in a tightly packed urban setting where wind patterns are broken by surrounding structures.

## Our Asbestos Abatement Process in Tye

Every project begins with a thorough inspection and, where materials are suspect, bulk sampling sent to an accredited laboratory. We do not guess. If testing confirms asbestos-containing materials, the work moves through a defined sequence: containment of the affected area using polyethylene sheeting and negative air pressure units with HEPA filtration, wet suppression of materials before removal to prevent fiber release, careful bagging and labeling of all waste per Texas Commission on Environmental Quality disposal requirements, and final air clearance sampling before containment is removed.

For popcorn ceiling asbestos removal, which comes up frequently in Tye-area homes built in the 1960s and 1970s, the ceiling must be kept wet throughout the scraping process and the room must remain sealed until clearance testing confirms fiber counts are within acceptable limits. Rushing that step is where DIY attempts and unlicensed contractors create problems that are far more expensive to fix later.

Our team holds EPA Lead-Safe Certification and is OSHA trained, which matters because older Tye properties often have both asbestos and lead paint present in the same renovation zone. Addressing one without awareness of the other can shift one hazard while disturbing another.

## Tye Insurance Coordination

Asbestos abatement is not always covered under a standard homeowner's policy, but when the asbestos disturbance is tied to a covered loss, such as storm damage that breaches an older roof or a pipe failure that requires cutting into insulated walls, the abatement cost can become part of the broader claim. We document the scope of work, photograph affected materials before and after, and provide the detailed written records that insurance adjusters need to evaluate the claim. If your carrier requires a licensed contractor, our Texas Mold Remediation Contractor License (MRC2262) and associated credentials are on file and available for submission.

## Local Note

One pattern that comes up specifically in the Tye and western Taylor County area: homes with original duct insulation from the 1960s and 1970s sometimes have asbestos-containing wrap on the supply plenum inside the attic. In West Texas attics, summer temperatures routinely exceed 140 degrees Fahrenheit, and that heat accelerates the degradation of older insulation materials. By the time a homeowner notices a problem, the material may already be friable. If your home was built before 1980 and has never had an asbestos inspection, the attic ductwork is worth including in the assessment scope before any HVAC work or attic insulation upgrade begins.
