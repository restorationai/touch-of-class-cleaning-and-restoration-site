## Why San Angelo Properties Present Unique Renovation Challenges

San Angelo sits on the Edwards Plateau, where the clay-heavy soil expands and contracts with moisture changes more dramatically than in most Texas cities. That movement shows up in foundation shifts, door frames that no longer square, and tile floors that crack along grout lines without any obvious cause. Before any remodel begins, it's worth understanding what the slab or pier-and-beam foundation beneath your home has been doing over the years — because a beautiful new kitchen floor installed over an active foundation problem won't stay beautiful for long.

The city's housing stock includes a substantial number of mid-century homes built between the 1940s and 1970s, many of which still carry original galvanized plumbing, knob-and-tube or early aluminum wiring, and single-pane windows that make modern energy efficiency targets nearly impossible without structural intervention. Renovating these homes isn't just a matter of swapping fixtures — it often means coordinating with licensed plumbers and electricians, pulling permits through the City of San Angelo Development Services, and meeting current IRC and NEC code requirements that didn't exist when the house was built.

## Our Renovation and General Contracting Process in San Angelo

Every project starts with a walkthrough that goes beyond what the homeowner wants to change. We look at what the structure needs — moisture intrusion points, load-bearing walls that affect open-concept plans, HVAC duct routing that determines where a bathroom addition can realistically go. From there, the process follows a documented sequence:

- **Structural and mechanical rough-in** — Framing, plumbing, electrical, and HVAC work coordinated with licensed trade partners.
- **Finish work** — Drywall, tile, cabinetry, flooring, and paint sequenced to minimize rework and protect completed surfaces.
- **Final walkthrough and punch list** — We don't close a job until every item on the punch list is resolved and the space is clean.

## San Angelo Insurance and Post-Damage Rebuild Coordination

A significant portion of renovation work in West Texas follows an insurance claim — a burst pipe during a freeze event, hail damage that compromised a roof and the ceiling below it, or smoke damage that requires partial reconstruction after a kitchen fire. Air Care Restoration handles post-damage rebuilds as a natural extension of our mitigation work, which means the adjuster is dealing with one company rather than a handoff between a restoration contractor and a separate GC.

## Local Note

San Angelo's dry climate creates a counterintuitive problem during interior renovations: freshly installed drywall mud and tile grout can cure too fast when interior humidity drops below 20 percent, which is common here in late summer and early fall. Joints that cure unevenly are more prone to hairline cracking within the first year. On larger drywall scopes, we monitor interior humidity during the finishing phase and adjust ventilation accordingly — a small step that prevents callbacks down the road.
