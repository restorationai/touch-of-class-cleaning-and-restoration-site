## Why Early Properties Face Particular Board-Up and Tarping Challenges

Early sits in the Rolling Plains transition zone where the climate swings hard — hail-bearing thunderstorms in spring, triple-digit heat in summer, and the occasional ice event in winter that catches homeowners off guard. That combination is rough on roofing materials. Older composition shingles and the standing-seam metal roofs common on ranch-style homes in this part of Brown County can fail suddenly under hail impact, leaving decking exposed mid-storm. When that happens, a properly weighted and sealed tarp isn't cosmetic — it's the difference between a manageable repair and a gut-renovation.

Fire damage compounds the problem. A kitchen or garage fire that vents through a soffit or blows out a window leaves the structure open to wind-driven rain within minutes. In Early's climate, that rain can arrive the same evening as the fire. Boarding up window and door openings with structural-grade plywood — sized, cut, and secured to the framing rather than just leaned against the opening — is the only way to hold the line until a full restoration plan is in place.

## Our Emergency Board-Up and Tarping Process in Early

Every call starts with a rapid damage assessment. When we arrive, we walk the perimeter and roof line before committing to a tarp or board configuration, because the wrong approach on a compromised structure can create new hazards. For roof tarping, we use heavy-duty polyethylene tarps anchored with lumber cap strips screwed into sound decking — not bungee cords or sandbags, which shift in West Texas wind. The tarp overlaps the ridge by at least two feet on each side and is sealed at the edges to prevent wind lift.

For board-up work — broken windows, kicked-in doors, fire-vented wall sections — we cut plywood to fit the rough opening and fasten it into the structural framing. On older homes with non-standard opening sizes, which are common in parts of Early built in the 1960s and 1970s, that means field-measuring and cutting on site rather than arriving with pre-cut stock. We document every opening we secure with photographs, which feeds directly into the insurance claim file.

Because we hold IICRC FSRT (Fire & Smoke) certification and are an IICRC Certified Firm, the board-up documentation we produce is formatted to support a fire or storm loss claim from the first hour — not retrofitted later.

## Insurance Coordination After Storm or Fire Damage in Early

Most homeowner policies in Texas cover emergency protective measures — board-up and tarping — as part of the dwelling coverage, but the documentation has to be right. Carriers want to see the pre-mitigation condition, the scope of what was secured, and the materials used. We photograph the damage before we touch anything, itemize every sheet of plywood and every linear foot of tarp, and produce a written scope that your adjuster can match against the claim without back-and-forth.

If your policy includes an HOA or a deed-restricted community requirement around exterior materials or appearance, let us know when you call. We can discuss material choices that meet the protective function while staying within community guidelines.

## Local Note

Homes in Early and the surrounding Brown County area that were built in the 1960s and early 1970s often have roof decking made of 1×6 skip sheathing rather than continuous plywood — a framing style common in that era of Texas ranch construction. Skip sheathing creates gaps between boards, which means a standard tarp anchor point can pull through more easily than it would on solid decking. When we encounter this, we widen our cap strip spacing and add additional fastening rows to distribute the load across more boards. It's a small adjustment that makes a significant difference in how well the tarp holds through the next weather event.
