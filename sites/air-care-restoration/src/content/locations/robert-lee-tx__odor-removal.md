## Why Robert Lee Properties Hold Odors Longer

The same climate that makes West Texas summers so brutal also makes odor removal a more technical challenge than it is in humid Gulf Coast markets. In Robert Lee, low ambient humidity means airborne odor molecules bind tightly to porous surfaces rather than staying suspended where ventilation can flush them out. Caliche-heavy soil under older slab foundations can wick moisture upward during the brief rainy season, and when that moisture carries mold or sewage contamination into a crawl space or utility room, the subsequent dry period essentially seals the odor into the structure.

The housing stock in Coke County leans heavily toward ranch-style construction from the 1960s through the 1980s, with fiber-board interior sheathing and original cellulose insulation in many attics. Both materials are highly absorbent. A single smoke event, whether from a kitchen fire, a wildfire smoke intrusion during a dry spring, or a malfunctioning HVAC unit, can saturate those materials in hours. Surface cleaning alone will not resolve it.

## Our Odor Removal and Deodorization Process in Robert Lee

Every job starts with a source assessment, not a spray-down. Before any deodorization equipment is deployed, a technician identifies where the odor is originating, whether that is char residue behind a wall cavity, mold colonies in an attic space, or pet contamination in subfloor material. Skipping this step is the reason store-bought sprays and rental ozone machines produce temporary results.

Depending on what the assessment finds, the process typically involves one or more of the following methods:

- **Thermal fogging**: A petroleum-based or water-based deodorizing fog is heated and dispersed to penetrate the same porous pathways that absorbed the odor. This is particularly effective for smoke odor removal in the ranch-style homes common across Coke County, where smoke travels into wall cavities through gaps around electrical boxes and plumbing penetrations.
- **Hydroxyl deodorization**: Hydroxyl generators produce reactive molecules that break down odor compounds at the molecular level. Unlike ozone treatment, hydroxyl units can operate safely in occupied or partially occupied structures, which matters when a homeowner cannot vacate for an extended period.
- **Ozone treatment**: High-concentration ozone is effective for severe, deeply embedded odors, but it requires full evacuation of people, pets, and plants. Our technicians calculate dwell time based on room volume and material porosity, not a generic timer.
- **Encapsulants and sealers**: When odor-bearing materials cannot be removed, an appropriate sealer locks in residual compounds after deodorization is complete.

All drying and deodorization work is performed to IICRC standards, and our team holds the IICRC FSRT credential specifically covering fire and smoke residue.

## Local Note: Wildfire Smoke and HVAC Systems in Coke County

One pattern that comes up repeatedly in this part of West Texas is wildfire smoke intrusion through HVAC systems. During dry spring and fall conditions, range fires in Coke and adjacent counties can push smoke into homes through return air vents even when windows are closed. The smoke bypasses standard filters and coats evaporator coils, ductwork liner, and air handler cabinets with a fine oily residue. Homeowners often notice the smell weeks later when the system cycles on and reheats that residue. If your home smells like smoke but there was no interior fire event, the HVAC system is the first place to check, and duct cleaning alone will not address the residue on the coil or in the air handler cabinet.
