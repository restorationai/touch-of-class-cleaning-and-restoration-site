## Why Tye Businesses See Water and Fire Damage Issues

Tye's commercial stock leans heavily on pole-barn and metal-building construction, the kind common along rural I-20 corridors where warehouse, retail, and light industrial uses sit close together. Metal roofing and wall panels condense heavily when West Texas heat swings against air-conditioned interiors, and that condensation tracks down into insulation and drywall in ways that aren't always visible until a tenant notices a musty smell near the HVAC returns. Add in Taylor County's clay-heavy soil, which swells and shrinks hard between summer drought and sudden thunderstorm runoff, and slab cracking or perimeter seepage shows up more in commercial buildings here than in newer suburban construction elsewhere. On the fire side, older electrical panels in strip retail spaces and the heavy HVAC draw during triple-digit months put real strain on wiring that wasn't sized for today's equipment loads.

## Our Commercial Restoration Process in Tye

A business loss gets handled differently than a residential one because the clock is tied to revenue, not just comfort. After the initial assessment, we map out which zones can reopen immediately, which need containment, and which require full reconstruction, so a retail floor or office lobby can often stay partially operational while back-of-house drying or smoke remediation continues. Water extraction and structural drying follow the IICRC S500 standard, with moisture readings logged daily so the documentation holds up if a landlord or tenant later needs proof of when the space was safe to reoccupy. Fire and smoke jobs get HEPA air scrubbing and soot removal from HVAC runs first, since a commercial system that keeps recirculating smoke odor will undo containment work in office suites overnight. We also schedule around business hours when a client needs to protect foot traffic or a shift schedule, rather than forcing a one-size-fits-all timeline.

## Tye Insurance & Commercial Claim Coordination

Commercial claims in Tye often involve more than one party, a landlord's property policy, a tenant's business interruption coverage, sometimes an equipment lease. We document room by room and system by system, with moisture maps, photos, and drying logs, specifically so the paperwork can be split between carriers without slowing down the work itself. For metal-building tenants, we also flag pre-existing roof or panel conditions early, since insurers frequently push back on condensation-related claims if there's no clear separation between storm damage and ongoing building wear.

## Local note

Metal commercial buildings around Tye often have exposed purlins and minimal interior insulation, which means a roof leak doesn't stay contained to one bay, it travels along the purlin line and shows up several units away from the actual source. We've learned to trace water intrusion in these buildings by following the structural framing rather than the visible ceiling stains, which saves a day or two of guesswork compared to treating it like a standard drywall ceiling leak.
