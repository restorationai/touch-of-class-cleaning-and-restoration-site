## Why Colorado City Properties See Storm Damage Issues

Mitchell County sits squarely in the southern edge of Tornado Alley, and the flat, semi-arid terrain around Colorado City offers little natural windbreak. Storms that develop over the Caprock Escarpment can accelerate dramatically by the time they reach town, and hail events here tend to run large — golf-ball-sized hail is not unusual during spring and early summer convective season.

The local housing stock adds another layer of complexity. A significant portion of Colorado City's residential neighborhoods were built in the mid-twentieth century, when roofing materials, window framing, and exterior cladding were not engineered to the impact-resistance standards common today. Older composition shingles crack and delaminate under hail stress in ways that aren't always visible from the ground, meaning a roof can be compromised for weeks before a homeowner notices ceiling staining. Single-story ranch-style homes — common throughout the city — also tend to have wide, low-pitch roof planes that collect debris and standing water after a storm, accelerating damage to decking and insulation.

The region's clay-heavy soils compound the problem at ground level. When heavy rain follows a storm, water that can't drain fast enough through saturated clay will migrate toward foundations and into crawl spaces, turning a wind event into a water intrusion problem within the same night.

## Our Storm Damage Restoration Process in Colorado City

Every job starts with a thorough exterior and interior assessment before any material is removed or covered. That documentation matters — photographs, moisture readings, and written scope notes form the foundation of an insurance claim, and adjusters for Texas carriers routinely request this level of detail before approving repairs.

From there, the process follows the damage:

- **Emergency stabilization** — tarping compromised roof sections, boarding broken windows, and securing any structural openings to stop weather from continuing to enter the building.
- **Water extraction and drying** — if rain or hail has breached the envelope, standing water is extracted and structural drying equipment is deployed. Older homes with plaster ceilings or original wood-framed walls hold moisture differently than newer drywall construction, and drying timelines are adjusted accordingly.
- **Debris removal** — downed trees, scattered roofing materials, and damaged siding are cleared and documented before reconstruction begins.
- **Structural repair and reconstruction** — once the structure is dry and stable, repair work begins on roofing, siding, windows, and any interior finishes affected by water intrusion.

All work is performed by an IICRC Certified Firm with OSHA-trained technicians, and Air Care Restoration holds EPA Lead-Safe certification — relevant for Colorado City's older homes where lead-based paint may be present in trim and window components disturbed during storm repairs.

## Local Note

One pattern that comes up repeatedly in Colorado City and the surrounding Mitchell County area: hail damage to roof vents and attic louvers often goes undetected until the next significant rain. West Texas hail hits at a steep angle when driven by the straight-line winds common in these storms, and it can crack or dislodge vent covers without leaving obvious exterior marks on the shingles themselves. Water then channels directly into the attic space along the vent framing — sometimes pooling on insulation for days before it works through to a ceiling. During any post-storm inspection in this area, vent and louver integrity gets checked as a standard step, not an afterthought.
