## Why Comanche Properties See Water Damage Issues

Comanche's climate delivers a wide swing of conditions across a single year: dry, cracked summers that leave soil contracted and unable to absorb sudden rainfall, followed by wet winters where that same soil swells and presses against pier-and-beam foundations. Many homes in Comanche were built in the mid-twentieth century with pier-and-beam construction, a style that offers good ventilation but creates an accessible crawl space where standing water can accumulate undetected for days. The wood joists and subfloor in those crawl spaces are especially vulnerable — once moisture content climbs above roughly 19 percent, wood-decay fungi can begin to establish, and the timeline from a slow leak to structural softening is shorter than most homeowners expect.

The city's older housing stock also means galvanized supply lines are still in service in some properties. Galvanized pipe corrodes from the inside out, and the first sign of failure is often a pinhole leak behind a wall rather than a dramatic burst — by the time water appears at the baseboard, the cavity behind the drywall may have been wet for weeks.

## Our Water Damage Restoration Process in Comanche

Every water damage job starts with moisture mapping, not assumptions. The crew uses thermal imaging cameras and calibrated moisture meters to trace water migration through wall cavities, under flooring, and into subfloor framing before a single piece of equipment is placed. In pier-and-beam homes common to this part of Comanche County, that inspection extends into the crawl space — a step that is easy to skip but almost always necessary.

Once the scope is clear, the process moves through four stages:

1. **Water extraction** — truck-mounted and portable extractors pull standing water from hard surfaces, carpet, and subfloor material. In homes with original hardwood floors, extraction speed matters; the faster free water is removed, the better the odds of saving the wood rather than replacing it.
2. **Structural drying** — high-capacity LGR dehumidifiers and axial air movers are positioned according to a drying plan calibrated to the room's cubic footage and the material types present. Drying targets follow the IICRC S500 standard.
3. **Daily monitoring** — moisture readings are logged at each visit so the drying curve is documented, not guessed. This record matters for insurance claims and for confirming that materials have reached acceptable dry standards before any rebuild begins.
4. **Antimicrobial treatment** — affected cavities receive an EPA-registered antimicrobial application after drying is confirmed, reducing the risk of secondary mold growth in the warm, humid conditions that can linger in a Texas summer.
