## Why Early Properties See Water Damage Issues

The shrink-swell cycle of Brown County's clay-heavy soils puts steady lateral pressure on slab foundations and underground supply lines year after year. When those lines develop even a pinhole leak, water follows the path of least resistance, often migrating beneath the slab before surfacing inside the home. Early also sits in a region where summer thunderstorms can deliver several inches of rain in under an hour, overwhelming gutters, window wells, and crawl spaces that were built to handle a slower pace of precipitation.

Homes in the Early area span a wide range of construction eras, and older builds frequently feature cast-iron drain lines that have corroded to the point of cracking, as well as pier-and-beam foundations that leave a generous cavity for moisture to accumulate beneath living spaces. That hidden accumulation is particularly problematic because surface readings can look dry while the wood framing above a wet crawl space is already absorbing enough moisture to support mold colonization, typically within 24 to 48 hours of a sustained leak.
