## Why Eden Properties See Water Damage Issues

Eden's climate swings hard. Summers push triple-digit heat for weeks at a stretch, which dries and contracts supply lines and the soil around foundations alike. When a monsoon-pattern storm rolls through — the kind that drops two inches in forty minutes on already-baked ground — runoff has nowhere to go quickly, and low-lying structures take on water through window wells, garage slabs, and any foundation crack that opened during the dry stretch. The flip side is a hard freeze in January or February: Concho County sits in a zone where overnight temperatures can drop into the teens with little warning, and older homes in Eden with exposed or minimally insulated pipes under pier-and-beam foundations are especially vulnerable to a burst that floods a crawl space before sunrise.

## Our Water Damage Restoration Process in Eden

The first step on any Eden job is containment and assessment. Technicians use thermal imaging cameras and calibrated moisture meters to map saturation behind walls and under flooring before a single panel comes off — this is especially important in slab-on-grade homes where water migrates along the vapor barrier and can surface ten or fifteen feet from the actual source. Once the extent of the damage is documented, extraction equipment removes standing water from the structure.

Structural drying follows a protocol built around the IICRC S500 standard. In Eden's low-humidity summer conditions, drying times can be shorter than in coastal climates — but that advantage disappears if the home has been closed up and interior humidity has already spiked. The crew sets industrial desiccant or refrigerant dehumidifiers and high-velocity air movers in a calculated pattern, then monitors moisture readings on a daily basis. Nothing is closed up or declared dry based on a visual check alone; the numbers have to confirm it. Documentation of daily readings goes into the claim file, which matters when your adjuster needs to verify that drying was completed to standard.

## Local Note

One detail that catches Eden homeowners off guard: caliche subsoil under a slab holds moisture differently than sandy fill. A moisture meter reading at the slab surface can look acceptable while the caliche layer beneath is still holding water against the underside of the concrete.
