## Why Haskell Properties See Persistent Odor Problems

The same climate that makes West Texas agriculture productive creates conditions that complicate odor removal. Extended heat and low humidity cause wood framing, plaster, and older insulation to dry out and become more porous over time, which means smoke particles, pet dander, and mold-related gases absorb deeper into structural materials than they would in a more humid climate. Many homes in Haskell were built during the mid-twentieth century when construction methods favored plaster-over-lath walls and pier-and-beam foundations. Both of those assemblies hold odor-causing compounds in ways that modern sheetrock does not. A surface wipe-down or a scented product masks the problem temporarily; the smell returns within days once the masking agent dissipates. Effective treatment has to reach the same depth the odor reached.

Haskell's agricultural surroundings add another layer. Homes near grain storage, livestock operations, or fields treated with fertilizer can absorb ambient organic compounds through HVAC systems, especially older systems without adequate filtration. Once those compounds circulate through ductwork, they become a secondary odor source that outlasts any cleaning done at the surface level.

## Our Odor Removal and Deodorization Process in Haskell

Every job starts with a systematic source identification. Odor removal that skips this step almost always fails. Technicians trace the odor to its origin, whether that is charred framing behind a wall, saturated insulation under a floor, or contaminated ductwork, before any deodorization equipment is deployed.

From there, the method is matched to the material and the severity of the loss:

- **Thermal fogging** penetrates the same pathways smoke traveled, reaching voids, wall cavities, and subflooring that surface treatments cannot access. It is particularly effective in older Haskell homes where construction gaps and settled framing create hidden channels.
- **Hydroxyl generation** runs continuously in occupied or semi-occupied spaces, breaking down odor-causing molecules at a molecular level without requiring the building to be vacated for extended periods.
- **Ozone treatment** is reserved for unoccupied structures or isolated spaces where a high-concentration, time-limited treatment is appropriate. Ozone is powerful and effective, but it requires proper clearance protocols before re-occupancy.
- **HVAC and duct deodorization** addresses the secondary odor pathways that surface-only treatments miss entirely.

Work is performed by an IICRC Certified Firm with IICRC FSRT (Fire and Smoke Restoration) credentials, meaning the technicians applying these methods understand how smoke behaves in a structure, not just how to operate the equipment.

One pattern that comes up specifically in Haskell County properties is the combination of pier-and-beam construction and the region's expansive clay soils. When moisture intrudes under a pier-and-beam home, whether from a plumbing leak or a heavy rain event, the crawl space can hold that moisture for weeks in the summer heat, creating a persistent musty odor that travels upward through floor gaps and into living areas.
