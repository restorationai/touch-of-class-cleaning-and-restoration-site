## Why Early Properties See Water Damage Issues

Brown County's climate swings between extended dry spells and sudden, heavy rainfall events that overwhelm gutters, window seals, and crawl space drainage in a matter of hours. Early's residential neighborhoods are largely built on expansive clay soils — the same soils that cause foundation movement across Central Texas. When that clay stays saturated after a storm or a plumbing failure, it holds moisture against foundation walls and under slabs long after the visible water is gone. Moisture meters and thermal imaging cameras are not optional here; they're the only way to confirm whether structural materials are actually dry or just dry at the surface.

Many homes in Early were built during the post-war construction boom of the 1950s through 1970s, a period when vapor barriers were minimal and insulation practices were inconsistent. Water that gets behind the wall cavity in these homes tends to wick upward through wood framing faster than it does in newer construction, and it can reach ceiling joists before a homeowner notices the first stain on the drywall.

## Our Water Damage Restoration Process in Early

Every job starts with a thorough assessment — not a visual walk-through, but a systematic inspection using moisture meters and thermal imaging to map exactly where water has traveled. Standing water is extracted first using truck-mounted or portable extraction units, depending on access. Once bulk water is removed, the drying phase begins: industrial air movers are positioned to create directed airflow across wet materials, and commercial dehumidifiers pull the evaporated moisture out of the air before it can migrate into adjacent rooms or building cavities.

Drying timelines are documented daily. Readings are logged at each monitoring point so that the drying record is available for your insurance adjuster and reflects actual conditions — not estimates. When materials like flooring or drywall have absorbed too much water to dry in place within a safe window, controlled demolition removes only what cannot be saved. The goal is always to protect what can be protected and replace only what has to go.

## Local Note

One thing worth knowing about Early's housing stock: slab foundations built in the 1960s and 1970s often have copper supply lines that run through or just beneath the slab itself. When one of those lines develops a pinhole leak — common as copper ages and as the slab shifts with Brown County's clay soils — water migrates under the flooring for weeks before it surfaces. By the time a homeowner notices a warm spot on the tile or a spike in the water bill, the subfloor and lower wall framing can already be significantly affected. If you suspect a slab leak, the water damage assessment needs to go deeper than the visible wet area.

Most standard homeowners' insurance policies cover sudden and accidental water damage — a burst pipe, an appliance failure, a roof breach during a storm — but the documentation has to be thorough for the claim to move smoothly.
