## Why Rotan Properties Are Particularly Vulnerable After a Loss

Rotan sits in a stretch of West Texas where the weather can shift from clear skies to severe thunderstorms with very little warning. The region sees its share of hail events, straight-line wind damage, and the occasional tornado — all of which can compromise a roof or shatter windows in minutes. Because the landscape is largely open rangeland, there are no tree lines or neighboring structures to buffer the wind, which means an exposed roof opening or broken window faces the full force of whatever follows the initial storm.

The local housing stock adds another layer of complexity. Many homes in and around Rotan were built decades ago, with wood-frame construction, older roofing systems, and single-pane windows that are more susceptible to impact damage than modern builds. When those older structures take a hit, the openings tend to be irregular — not standard window sizes, not clean cuts — which requires custom-fitted plywood panels or heavy-duty tarping rather than off-the-shelf solutions.

Fire damage is the other common driver of emergency board-up calls in this area. A structure fire that burns through an exterior wall or collapses a section of roof leaves the interior completely exposed. Without a rapid response, smoke-damaged contents absorb additional moisture, and the structural integrity of remaining walls can be further compromised.

## Our Emergency Board-Up and Tarping Process in Rotan

When you call, the first thing we do is gather enough information to arrive with the right materials. Board-up and tarping jobs are not one-size situations — a fire-damaged wall requires different anchoring than a hail-punctured roof, and a blown-out commercial storefront needs different plywood thickness than a residential bedroom window.

On arrival, we assess the full perimeter of the structure before touching anything. That assessment documents every opening, every compromised section of roofing, and any structural concerns that could affect crew safety — which matters on older West Texas homes where fire or wind may have weakened load-bearing elements. We photograph everything, both for your insurance claim and to establish a baseline before any protective materials go up.

Roof tarps are anchored with weighted boards or screw-down batten strips rather than simple rope ties, because the open terrain around Rotan means wind loads on a tarp can be substantial even on a calm-looking afternoon. Plywood panels are cut to fit, secured with structural screws, and — where the damage involves a fire loss — sealed at the edges to limit smoke odor migration into unaffected areas of the home.

Because we are IICRC certified and hold IICRC FSRT (Fire & Smoke) credentials, the board-up work is coordinated with any downstream restoration scope, so nothing we install creates a problem for the remediation crew that follows.
