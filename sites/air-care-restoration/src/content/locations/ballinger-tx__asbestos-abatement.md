That durability means the asbestos-containing materials baked into those structures decades ago are still there: in floor tiles, pipe insulation, roofing felt, and the textured ceilings that were standard practice before the late 1970s. ## Why Ballinger Properties See Asbestos Concerns

Runnels County's building boom tracked closely with the broader Texas oil and agricultural economy of the 1940s through 1970s. During that period, asbestos was a standard additive in everything from joint compound and vinyl floor backing to attic insulation and HVAC duct wrap. Many of those structures are still standing and still occupied.

The region's climate adds a layer of complexity. Ballinger experiences significant temperature swings — scorching summers that push attic temps well above ambient air, followed by hard freezes in winter. That thermal cycling causes building materials to expand and contract repeatedly over decades, which can fracture brittle asbestos-containing materials and release fibers into living spaces long before any renovation work begins. A ceiling tile that looked intact last spring may be crumbling by fall. Popcorn ceiling texture applied before 1980 is a particularly common find in single-family homes throughout this area, and it's one of the more friable materials — meaning it breaks apart easily and disperses fibers when disturbed.

## Our Asbestos Abatement Process in Ballinger

Every project starts with a proper inspection and bulk sampling before any removal work is scoped or priced. Samples go to an accredited laboratory; we don't skip that step or estimate based on visual appearance alone, because not every textured ceiling or gray floor tile contains asbestos — and not every material that looks clean is safe.

Once results confirm the presence of asbestos-containing material, the work follows a contained, negative-pressure protocol:

- **Containment setup**: Poly sheeting seals off the work area from the rest of the structure. HVAC registers are closed and taped to prevent fiber migration through ductwork — a step that matters especially in older Ballinger homes where original duct systems may run through multiple rooms.
- **Regulated removal**: Friable materials are wetted before removal to suppress fiber release. Non-friable materials that can be encapsulated rather than removed are assessed on a case-by-case basis.
- **Waste handling**: Asbestos waste is double-bagged in labeled, sealed containers and transported to a permitted disposal facility in compliance with Texas Commission on Environmental Quality (TCEQ) requirements.
- **Air clearance**: Post-abatement air sampling confirms fiber levels are within acceptable limits before containment is removed and the space is returned to use.

Our team is EPA Lead-Safe Certified and OSHA trained, which matters in older West Texas structures where asbestos and lead-based paint frequently coexist in the same renovation scope.
