## Why Coleman Properties Face Asbestos Concerns

Coleman's building stock tells the story of the region's economy. Residential construction that boomed in the mid-twentieth century relied heavily on materials we now know to be hazardous: textured popcorn ceilings, floor tile mastics, pipe insulation, duct tape, and joint compounds all commonly contained asbestos through the late 1970s. Agricultural outbuildings and commercial structures built during the same era often used corrugated roofing panels and insulation board with similarly high asbestos content.

The West Texas climate adds a specific complication. Extreme temperature swings between summer heat and winter cold cause building materials to expand and contract repeatedly over decades. That mechanical stress can fracture older insulation and tile, turning what was once a stable, non-friable material into a friable one, meaning fibers can become airborne with minimal disturbance. A renovation project that seems straightforward, like pulling up old vinyl flooring or scraping a textured ceiling, can release those fibers if the material has not been tested first.

## Our Asbestos Abatement Process in Coleman

Every project begins with a formal inspection and bulk sampling of suspect materials. Samples are sent to an accredited laboratory for analysis, and no abatement work begins until results are confirmed. That sequence matters both for your safety and for regulatory compliance under Texas Department of State Health Services rules governing asbestos work.

When abatement is required, the work area is isolated with polyethylene sheeting and placed under negative air pressure using HEPA-filtered air scrubbers. Workers in full personal protective equipment remove the confirmed materials according to the applicable Texas asbestos regulations, sealing waste in labeled, double-bagged containers for transport to a licensed disposal facility. Air clearance testing follows removal, confirming that fiber counts have returned to acceptable levels before containment is removed and the space is released for reconstruction.

Because Air Care Restoration holds an EPA Lead-Safe Certification and is OSHA trained, the team is equipped to handle projects where asbestos and lead-based paint are found together, a combination that is common in pre-1978 Coleman homes where both hazards were present in the original construction.
