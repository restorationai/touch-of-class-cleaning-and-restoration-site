Coleman sits in the heart of West Texas, where decades of oil-field activity, agricultural expansion, and post-war construction left a building stock that spans nearly every era of asbestos use in American materials. ## Why Coleman Properties See Asbestos Concerns

Coleman's residential and commercial construction history tracks closely with the broader Texas boom cycles — a wave of building in the 1940s and 1950s, another in the 1960s and 1970s, and infill construction that stretched into the early 1980s. Asbestos-containing materials were standard in all of those eras: floor tiles, roof shingles, pipe insulation, joint compound, and the textured "popcorn" ceilings that were nearly universal in Texas homes built before 1980.

The region's climate adds a layer of risk that's easy to overlook. Coleman County experiences significant temperature swings between summer heat and winter cold snaps, and that thermal cycling causes building materials to expand and contract repeatedly over decades. Brittle floor tiles crack. Textured ceiling coatings flake. Pipe wrap around older HVAC and plumbing systems can fray at joints. When those materials contain asbestos, physical disturbance — even minor crumbling — can release fibers into living spaces. The dry, windy conditions common across this part of West Texas mean disturbed material doesn't settle quickly.

## Our Asbestos Abatement Process in Coleman

Every project starts with a thorough inspection and bulk sampling. Suspected materials are collected following EPA sampling protocols and sent to an accredited laboratory for analysis. You receive written results before any abatement work begins — no guessing, no "it probably is" conversations.

If asbestos-containing material is confirmed, the scope of work is scoped to the actual findings:

- **Encapsulation** — where materials are intact and in a location that won't be disturbed, a penetrating sealant can lock fibers in place. This is sometimes the right answer for undisturbed pipe insulation in a crawl space that won't be renovated.
- **Full removal** — required when materials are friable (crumbling), when renovation or demolition will disturb them, or when a property is being prepared for sale. The work area is isolated with negative-air containment, and removed material is packaged and disposed of at a licensed Texas disposal facility.
- **Post-abatement air clearance** — after removal, air samples confirm fiber levels are below clearance thresholds before containment comes down. This documentation matters for insurance claims, real estate transactions, and your own peace of mind.

Our team holds EPA Lead-Safe Certification and is OSHA trained, which matters in older Coleman properties where lead paint and asbestos often coexist in the same renovation scope.

## Local Note

Older homes in Coleman that were built during the post-war construction wave often used a combination of asbestos-containing floor tiles over original hardwood. When homeowners pull up linoleum or vinyl tile to reveal the wood underneath, they sometimes find a layer of mastic adhesive beneath the tile — and both the tile and the mastic can contain asbestos. Testing just the tile isn't enough; the adhesive needs to be sampled separately. It's a detail that's easy to miss on a visual inspection and one that comes up regularly in Coleman's older housing stock.
