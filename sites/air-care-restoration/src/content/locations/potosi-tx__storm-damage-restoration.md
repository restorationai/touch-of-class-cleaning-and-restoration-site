## Why Potosi Properties See Storm Damage Differently Than Urban Abilene

The Taylor County landscape around Potosi is characterized by shallow caliche soils and hardpan clay that drains poorly after a heavy rain event. When a severe storm drops two or three inches in under an hour, that water has nowhere to go quickly. It pools against foundations, saturates crawl spaces, and finds every gap in a slab or pier-and-beam base. Homes in this part of Taylor County tend to be spread out on larger lots with mature trees, and those trees become projectiles in a high-wind event. A single downed pecan or post oak can punch through a roof deck, leaving the interior exposed to rain intrusion within minutes.

Because many properties here are on private well and septic systems rather than municipal utilities, storm damage can also compromise water quality or disrupt drainage fields in ways that don't apply to in-city addresses. That intersection of structural damage and site infrastructure is something that requires careful sequencing during restoration.

## Our Storm Damage Restoration Process in Potosi

The first priority after any storm event is stabilization. That means boarding or tarping breached roof sections, covering broken windows, and identifying standing water before it migrates into wall cavities or subfloor assemblies. Water that enters through a roof breach after a tornado or severe hail event is not the same as a plumbing leak. It often carries debris, contaminants from roofing materials, and in some cases insulation fibers that complicate the drying process.

Once the structure is secured, our IICRC-certified team conducts a full moisture mapping survey using thermal imaging and calibrated moisture meters. This step matters especially in Potosi-area homes that use spray foam or blown-in insulation, which can trap moisture and mask wet readings at the surface. Drying equipment, including commercial-grade desiccant dehumidifiers and high-velocity air movers, is placed based on the actual moisture map, not a generic room count. Structural repairs, including roof decking, framing, and exterior cladding, follow once drying benchmarks are confirmed.

Storm damage claims in Texas are governed by the Texas Department of Insurance's prompt-payment rules, and carriers have specific documentation requirements for wind and hail losses. One pattern specific to the Potosi area worth knowing: the caliche subsoil that underlies much of Taylor County west of Abilene becomes almost impermeable when saturated.
