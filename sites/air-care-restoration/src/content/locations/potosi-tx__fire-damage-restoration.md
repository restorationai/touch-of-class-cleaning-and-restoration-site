## Why Potosi Properties Face Particular Fire Damage Challenges

The housing stock in and around Potosi leans toward ranch-style homes and agricultural properties built in earlier decades, many with wood-framed walls, pier-and-beam foundations, and attic spaces that lack the air-sealing common in newer construction. When fire touches these structures, smoke travels freely through wall cavities, crawl spaces, and uninsulated attics before the flames are even out. That means the visible char on a wall surface is rarely the full story. Behind the drywall or older shiplap, soot and smoke residue can coat framing members, insulation, and HVAC ducts, leaving odors and health concerns that linger long after the visible damage is cleaned.

West Texas's low relative humidity also affects how smoke residue behaves. Dry air causes oily soot particles to bond more stubbornly to porous surfaces like wood, brick, and unsealed concrete, making surface wiping alone inadequate. Proper restoration here requires negative-air containment, HEPA filtration, and chemical sponge or wet-chemical cleaning matched to the surface type.

## Our Fire and Smoke Restoration Process in Potosi

Every job starts with a thorough scope assessment before any cleaning begins. We use thermal imaging and moisture meters to map the full extent of smoke migration, including areas the fire never touched but smoke did. From there, the process follows a deliberate sequence:

- **Emergency board-up and tarping** to secure the structure against weather and unauthorized entry while work is underway.
- **Content inventory and pack-out** for salvageable belongings, which are catalogued, transported, and cleaned off-site when needed.
- **Structural cleaning** using dry chemical sponges on unsealed surfaces, wet-chemical agents on sealed and hard surfaces, and HEPA vacuuming throughout affected cavities.
- **Odor neutralization** with thermal fogging and hydroxyl generation, which penetrate the same pathways smoke used so the odor source is treated, not just masked.
- **Selective demolition** of materials that cannot be cleaned to a restorable condition, followed by documentation for the insurance claim.
- **Reconstruction coordination** to return the home to pre-loss condition, handled under the same project so you are not managing two separate contractors.

Because Potosi properties often include outbuildings, covered porches, and detached garages, we scope those structures separately. Smoke from a house fire frequently migrates into adjacent structures, and leaving them unaddressed means odor returns.

## Local Note

One pattern worth knowing in the Potosi area: older ranch homes built with native limestone or caliche-block construction absorb smoke odor differently than wood-frame walls. The porous surface of these materials holds smoke compounds deep in the stone, and standard fogging alone does not reach them. When we encounter limestone or caliche block in a fire-affected home, we adjust the odor treatment protocol to include longer dwell times and, where necessary, a sealant application over cleaned masonry before any repainting. It is a step that adds time but prevents odor from re-emerging after the job is closed.
