## Why Merkel Properties See Asbestos Concerns

Taylor County's housing stock leans heavily on construction from the 1950s through the 1970s, the exact window when asbestos use in residential materials peaked. During that period, manufacturers added asbestos to products for fire resistance and durability — qualities that seemed ideal for West Texas homes that had to endure scorching summers and occasional hailstorms. The result is that popcorn ceilings, 9×9 vinyl floor tiles, and the black mastic adhesive beneath them are common finds in Merkel homes of that era.

West Texas also has a dry, dusty climate that accelerates the aging of building materials. Thermal cycling — extreme heat in summer, sharp cold snaps in winter — causes expansion and contraction that can crack and friable asbestos-containing materials over decades. Once those materials are friable (crumbling or releasing fibers), the risk of airborne exposure rises. A ceiling that looks merely "old" may be shedding microscopic fibers every time the HVAC system kicks on.

Storm damage is another trigger. When a hailstorm or high-wind event damages a roof or siding on a pre-1980 structure, the repair work can disturb asbestos-containing roofing felt or exterior panels. In those situations, testing before repairs begin is not just prudent — it is the responsible path for anyone working on or living in the structure.

## Our Asbestos Abatement Process in Merkel

Every abatement project starts with inspection and bulk sampling. A trained technician collects small material samples from suspect areas — ceiling texture, floor tile, pipe insulation, drywall joint compound — and sends them to an accredited laboratory for polarized light microscopy (PLM) analysis. No abatement work begins until results confirm what you're dealing with.

If asbestos is confirmed, the affected area is isolated with negative-air containment. Polyethylene sheeting seals doorways and HVAC vents; HEPA-filtered air scrubbers run continuously to capture airborne fibers and maintain negative pressure so contaminated air cannot migrate into clean spaces. Workers wear full personal protective equipment including respirators rated for asbestos fiber size.

Material removal follows regulated wet-method procedures — wetting the material before disturbance suppresses fiber release. All removed material is double-bagged in labeled, sealed waste bags and transported to a licensed disposal facility. Texas Commission on Environmental Quality (TCEQ) regulations govern disposal, and documentation of the waste manifest is provided to the property owner.

After removal, a final visual inspection and air clearance test confirm fiber levels have returned to acceptable thresholds before containment is dismantled. The EPA's OSHA-trained standards guide every step of that clearance process.

One thing that comes up specifically in older Merkel homes: the 9×9 floor tiles common in mid-century West Texas construction were almost universally manufactured with asbestos, and the black cutback adhesive beneath them often contains it as well. Homeowners sometimes assume that because the tiles themselves appear intact and unbroken, there is no risk — but the adhesive layer is frequently more friable than the tile, especially after decades of temperature swings.
