## Why Merkel Properties Are Prone to Asbestos-Containing Materials

Construction practices across Taylor County in the 1950s through the late 1970s relied heavily on materials that were cost-effective and durable for the harsh West Texas climate. Asbestos was used precisely because it resisted heat and moisture, two problems that mattered in a region where summer temperatures regularly exceed 100°F and HVAC systems run hard. The result is that asbestos-containing materials show up in a wide range of locations in Merkel homes and commercial buildings: pipe insulation on older heating systems, vinyl floor tiles and their adhesive backing, joint compound on drywall seams, roofing felt, and the textured "popcorn" ceilings that were standard through the early 1980s.

Merkel's agricultural heritage also means there's a meaningful stock of older outbuildings, shops, and storage structures on rural parcels just outside town. These structures often received minimal renovation over the decades, which means their original materials, including asbestos-containing corrugated roofing panels and insulation board, may still be intact. Intact asbestos is generally not an immediate hazard, but any disturbance during demolition or repair creates risk.

## Our Asbestos Abatement Process in Merkel

Every project starts with a thorough inspection of the affected area before any material is disturbed. Samples are collected from suspect materials and sent to an accredited laboratory for analysis. In Merkel, where older homes may have multiple generations of renovation layered on top of original construction, that sampling phase is especially important: a tile floor installed in the 1990s may be covering a 1960s-era adhesive that tests positive.

Once laboratory results confirm asbestos-containing material, the abatement work follows a defined sequence. The work area is isolated with negative air pressure containment to prevent fiber migration to adjacent spaces. Our OSHA-trained crew wears full personal protective equipment throughout. Asbestos-containing materials are wetted before removal to suppress fiber release, then double-bagged in labeled, sealed disposal containers. Waste is transported to a licensed disposal facility in compliance with Texas Commission on Environmental Quality requirements. A final air clearance test confirms the space is safe before containment is removed and the area is released.
