## Why Hawley Properties Are Vulnerable to Water Damage

The same expansive clay soils that make Jones County farmland productive create real headaches for residential foundations. When clay absorbs water it swells, and when it dries it contracts. That repeated movement stresses slab edges and pier-and-beam footings alike, opening small gaps where water finds its way in during heavy rain events. Hawley's older housing stock, much of it built in eras when vapor barriers and moisture-resistant sheathing weren't standard practice, compounds the problem. Wall cavities and subfloor assemblies in these homes can hold standing water that a surface inspection will never reveal.

North-facing exterior walls and enclosed crawl spaces are common moisture traps here. A slow drip from a corroded galvanized pipe fitting, the kind of fitting common in homes built before copper became standard, can saturate insulation and floor decking for weeks before a homeowner notices a soft spot underfoot or a musty odor in a back bedroom. By that point, secondary damage, including mold colonization, can begin in as little as 24 to 48 hours in the warm, humid conditions that follow a Jones County summer storm.

## Our Water Damage Restoration Process in Hawley

When we arrive, the first priority is stopping any active source and then mapping the full extent of the moisture intrusion. We use thermal imaging cameras and calibrated moisture meters to read behind drywall, under flooring, and inside wall cavities. Surface readings alone miss the water that matters most.

Once we understand the scope, water extraction begins with truck-mounted and portable extraction units capable of pulling hundreds of gallons from carpet, subfloor, and concrete. After bulk water is removed, we set a drying system calibrated to the specific materials in your home. Hawley's clay-soil homes often have concrete slabs, and concrete holds moisture longer than wood-framed assemblies, so drying timelines here can run longer than the national average the IICRC S500 standard uses as a baseline. We monitor moisture readings at every visit and adjust airflow and dehumidification until readings reach acceptable dry standards, not just until things feel dry to the touch.

All affected materials, drywall, insulation, flooring, are documented with photos and moisture logs before any demolition. That documentation matters when you file with your carrier.

## Local Note

Hawley's older homes built on pier-and-beam foundations present a specific drying challenge that slab-construction homes do not. The crawl space beneath the floor system can trap humidity even after the standing water is extracted, because airflow under the house is limited and the soil itself continues releasing moisture upward. We address this by placing desiccant dehumidifiers and directional air movers in the crawl space itself, not just in the living area above. Skipping that step leaves residual moisture that drives mold growth from below, which often goes undetected until flooring starts to buckle or odors become persistent.
