## Why Sweetwater Properties Are Especially Vulnerable to Storm Damage

Nolan County sits at an elevation where cold Canadian air masses collide with Gulf moisture with very little topographic resistance. That combination produces hail events that can strip shingles down to bare decking in minutes, and wind-driven rain that finds every unsealed penetration in a roof or wall assembly. Sweetwater's housing stock spans several decades, and older homes — particularly those built in the mid-twentieth century — frequently feature wood-frame construction with original single-pane windows, aging fascia boards, and rooflines that were not engineered to modern wind-load standards. When a storm peels back even a small section of roofing on one of these homes, rainfall can saturate insulation, ceiling drywall, and wall cavities within a single weather event.

The region's clay-heavy soils compound the problem. After a heavy storm, water that cannot drain fast enough through compacted or saturated ground tends to pool against foundations and push into crawl spaces or slab edges. Sweetwater also sits in an area where large wind farms dot the surrounding landscape — a sign of the persistent, powerful winds that are simply part of life here. Those same winds drive debris at speeds that turn tree limbs, fence panels, and roofing materials into projectiles.

## Our Storm Damage Restoration Process in Sweetwater

Every storm job starts with a thorough damage assessment before any material is touched. Crew members document structural damage, roof breaches, broken windows, and interior water intrusion with photographs and moisture readings — the kind of documentation that matters when an insurance adjuster reviews your claim. Temporary protective measures come next: roof tarping, board-up of broken openings, and perimeter containment to stop additional water entry while permanent repairs are planned.

Once the structure is stabilized, water extraction and drying begin immediately. Standing water is removed with truck-mounted extraction equipment, and industrial air movers and dehumidifiers are deployed to begin the drying process calibrated to the IICRC S500 standard. Readings are logged daily so drying progress is documented and verifiable. Debris removal — downed trees, displaced roofing, shattered glass — is handled in parallel so the property is safe for crews and accessible for follow-on trades. Air Care Restoration is an IICRC Certified Firm with OSHA-trained technicians and EPA Lead-Safe certification, which matters on older Sweetwater homes where disturbing painted surfaces during storm repairs requires proper handling protocols.
