## Why Clyde Properties See Water Damage Issues

Callahan County sits in a band of West Texas where the soil shifts between tight clay and caliche hardpan depending on the lot. Neither drains quickly. After a heavy rain, water that can't percolate downward has to go somewhere — and it often finds its way toward a foundation, through a weep hole, or under a slab. Homes built in Clyde during the post-war decades frequently used pier-and-beam construction, which means a flooded crawl space is a real possibility rather than a rare one: standing water under the floor can go undetected for days while the subfloor and floor joists quietly absorb moisture.

Beyond storms, the region's temperature swings create a second category of loss. ## Our Water Damage Restoration Process in Clyde

Every job starts with moisture mapping — not a visual scan, but thermal imaging and calibrated moisture meters that locate water behind drywall, under flooring, and inside wall cavities before a single fan is placed. That step matters in Clyde's older housing stock because water behind plaster or original hardwood reads dry at the surface while the substrate stays saturated.

Once the full extent of the loss is documented, extraction comes first: truck-mounted and portable units pull standing water from carpet, subfloor, and crawl spaces. Moisture readings are logged at each visit so the drying curve is documented — useful for insurance adjusters and for confirming that materials have reached acceptable moisture content before any reconstruction begins.
