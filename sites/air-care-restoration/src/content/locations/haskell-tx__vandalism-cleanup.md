## Why Haskell Properties Face Particular Challenges After Vandalism

Much of Haskell's commercial and residential building stock dates to the mid-twentieth century, when brick veneer and painted concrete block were the dominant materials. Those surfaces are porous in ways that newer construction is not. Aerosol paint soaks into the microscopic voids of aged brick and unsealed block quickly, and the alkaline chemistry of older mortar can actually bond certain pigments more stubbornly than it would on a sealed modern substrate. The intense UV exposure common across this part of Texas also means that graffiti left even a few days can begin to photo-oxidize into the surface, making removal significantly harder.

Windows are another recurring concern. Haskell's older storefronts often use single-pane plate glass, which shatters into large, irregular shards rather than the smaller fragments tempered glass produces. A proper broken glass cleanup here means careful hand-sorting of large pieces, vacuuming of fine particulate from window tracks and floor gaps, and an inspection of the frame for hidden slivers before any temporary boarding is installed.

## Our Vandalism Cleanup Process in Haskell

Every job starts with a documented walkthrough. We photograph all affected surfaces before touching anything, both to support your insurance claim and to assess what removal method each material can tolerate. Aged brick gets a different approach than painted wood siding or a vinyl-wrapped commercial sign panel.

For graffiti removal, we match the solvent or abrasive method to the substrate. Soft sandblasting or soda blasting works well on brick and concrete without gouging the face. Chemical dwell agents are used on painted wood where abrasion would raise the grain. On glass and metal, we use razor-lift techniques followed by solvent wipe-down. After the surface is cleared, we apply a sacrificial anti-graffiti coating where the property owner wants future incidents to be easier to address.

Broken glass cleanup follows a strict containment sequence: large shards bagged and labeled, fine glass vacuumed with HEPA-filtered equipment, and frame cavities inspected before temporary plywood or polycarbonate boarding is secured. We document board placement so your glazier or insurance adjuster has a clear record.
