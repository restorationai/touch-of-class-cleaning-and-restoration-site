## Why Eastland Properties Are Prone to Asbestos Concerns

West Texas construction from roughly the 1940s through the late 1970s leaned heavily on materials that were cheap, fire-resistant, and widely available through regional supply chains — which, in practice, meant asbestos-containing floor tiles, pipe insulation, roofing felt, joint compound, and spray-applied ceiling texture. Eastland's housing stock reflects that era. Single-story wood-frame homes built during the post-war boom and brick commercial buildings from the same period are common throughout the city, and many haven't seen a gut renovation since they were first built.

The climate adds another layer of risk. Eastland County experiences significant temperature swings between summer heat and winter cold fronts that push through off the Caprock. That thermal cycling causes building materials to expand and contract repeatedly over decades, which can fracture or crumble asbestos-containing materials that were otherwise stable — a condition inspectors call "friable." Friable asbestos releases fibers into the air far more readily than intact material, which is exactly the scenario that turns a routine remodel into an abatement job.

## Our Asbestos Abatement Process in Eastland

Every project starts with a thorough inspection and, where suspect materials are present, bulk sample collection sent to an accredited laboratory. We do not guess at what's asbestos and what isn't — Texas regulations require laboratory confirmation before abatement work begins, and that step protects both the occupants and the project timeline.

Once a positive identification comes back, the work area is isolated using negative-air pressure containment with HEPA-filtered exhaust units. This keeps disturbed fibers from migrating into adjacent rooms or HVAC systems — a particular concern in older Eastland homes where ductwork may run through the same ceiling cavities as the material being removed. Abatement technicians work in full PPE, and all removed material is packaged, labeled, and transported to a licensed disposal facility in accordance with Texas Commission on Environmental Quality requirements.

After removal, air clearance testing confirms fiber levels have returned to acceptable thresholds before containment is broken. We are an EPA Lead-Safe Certified firm and OSHA trained, which matters when a project involves both asbestos and lead-based paint — a combination that's not unusual in pre-1978 Eastland properties.
