## Why Albany Properties Face Distinct Vandalism Challenges

Albany's building stock reflects its history as a ranching and county-seat town. Many commercial buildings along the central business district were constructed with soft limestone block or painted brick, both of which are porous enough to absorb aerosol paint deeply within the first few hours of exposure. Unlike concrete block or modern composite cladding, these older masonry surfaces require a staged approach: chemical dwell time has to be calibrated carefully to lift pigment without etching the face of the stone.

Ranch properties on the outskirts of town present a different set of challenges. Metal outbuildings, corrugated panels, and painted wooden fencing are common targets, and the intense UV exposure in this part of Texas means that even a few days of sun can begin to cure spray paint to a point where mechanical abrasion becomes necessary alongside chemical treatment. The low humidity typical of the region also means that broken glass and debris from forced entry dry out and become more friable quickly, which matters for safe collection and disposal.

## Our Vandalism Cleanup Process in Albany

When Air Care Restoration arrives at a property, the first step is a walk-through assessment to document every affected surface with photographs. This documentation serves two purposes: it creates an accurate scope of work, and it gives your insurance adjuster a clear picture of the damage before any cleaning begins.

Surface type drives every decision from that point forward. For painted masonry common to older Albany commercial buildings, we apply a professional-grade graffiti remover at the correct dwell time, then rinse and neutralize before evaluating whether a second pass is needed. For glass replacement, we board up openings immediately to secure the property, then coordinate with glaziers for permanent installation. Broken glass cleanup follows strict containment protocols to protect anyone entering the space, including collection of micro-fragments that are invisible to the naked eye but hazardous underfoot.

For interior vandalism, including paint on walls, overturned fixtures, or contaminated surfaces, the process mirrors a structured restoration sequence: document, contain, remove damaged material, clean and treat remaining surfaces, and restore to pre-loss condition where possible.
