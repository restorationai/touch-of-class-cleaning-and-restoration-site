## Why Colorado City Properties See Duct Buildup Faster Than Most

Mitchell County sits in a semi-arid stretch of the Rolling Plains where dust events are a seasonal fact of life, not an occasional nuisance. When a dry front pushes through ahead of a blue norther, fine particulate gets driven into every gap in a home's envelope — and residential ductwork is full of gaps. Flex duct connections at supply boots, the seam where a plenum meets an air handler, the return-air chase in a hallway ceiling: each one is a potential entry point.

Colorado City's housing stock adds another layer to the problem. A significant share of homes here were built during the oil-patch construction booms of the mid-20th century, when duct design prioritized reach over sealing. Older galvanized steel ducts develop surface corrosion that gives dust and mold spores a rough texture to cling to. Fiberglass duct liner, common in systems from the 1970s and 1980s, can begin to delaminate over decades, shedding material directly into the airstream. Neither condition is visible from a thermostat — they show up in allergy symptoms, in a fine grit on supply registers, and in utility bills that creep upward as restricted airflow forces the system to run longer cycles.

Dryer vents deserve mention here too. In a climate where outdoor humidity can swing from single digits to the 60s within a day, lint accumulation in dryer exhaust runs creates both a fire hazard and a moisture-introduction point when the vent seal degrades.

## Our Air Duct Cleaning Process in Colorado City

The process begins with a camera inspection of the main trunk lines and a representative sample of branch runs. This gives a clear picture of debris type — whether it's primarily dust and lint, biological growth from a past moisture event, or degraded liner material — before any equipment is connected.

Negative-pressure extraction is the core of the cleaning. A truck-mounted or high-capacity portable vacuum is attached to the main trunk, creating continuous suction while compressed-air whips agitate debris from duct walls and drive it toward the collection point. Supply and return registers are cleaned individually. If the inspection reveals microbial growth — possible after any period of elevated humidity or a prior water intrusion near the air handler — an EPA-registered sanitizing agent can be applied to interior duct surfaces after extraction is complete.

The air handler cabinet itself, including the blower wheel and evaporator coil housing, is inspected and cleaned as part of a full-system service. A dirty coil immediately downstream of a cleaned duct system will re-contaminate the airstream within a single cooling season.

Dryer vent cleaning follows the same negative-pressure principle: full-length brush-and-vacuum from the interior connection to the exterior termination cap, with a flow test to confirm adequate exhaust velocity before the job is closed out.

## Local Note

One thing that catches homeowners off guard in Colorado City: the combination of a tight, energy-efficient home retrofit and West Texas dust is harder on duct systems than either factor alone. When older homes are weatherized — new windows, added attic insulation, foam-sealed penetrations — the building tightens up but the ductwork doesn't change. The system now operates under higher negative pressure on the return side, pulling more aggressively through any small gap or loose connection. That accelerates dust ingestion into the ducts themselves. If your home has been through an energy audit and retrofit in the past several years, it's worth having the duct system inspected even if it was cleaned before the weatherization work was done.
