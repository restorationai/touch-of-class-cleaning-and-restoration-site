That same relentless airflow that makes summer afternoons bearable also means your HVAC system is constantly pulling grit, pollen, and cotton-gin debris into ductwork — and once it's in, it stays. ## Why Clyde Homes See Duct Buildup Faster Than You'd Expect

Callahan County's soil is predominantly sandy loam with a fine clay fraction that dries to a talc-like powder during drought stretches. When wind picks up — and it does, regularly — that dust infiltrates return-air chases, attic-mounted air handlers, and any flex duct with a loose collar. Homes built in Clyde during the post-war boom through the 1980s often have original metal trunk lines with fiberglass lining that has degraded over decades, creating a rough interior surface that traps debris far more aggressively than modern smooth-wall duct. Older construction also tends to have larger gaps around boots and registers, giving outside air a direct path in. The result is a duct system that can accumulate a full cleaning's worth of buildup in two to three years rather than the five to seven a tighter, newer home might see.

Dryer vent lines in these same homes frequently run longer horizontal distances than current installation guidelines recommend, and lint compaction in those runs is a genuine fire risk — not a hypothetical one in a county where summer humidity drops low enough that materials ignite easily.

## Our Air Duct Cleaning Process in Clyde

Before any equipment goes in, a technician walks the home and inspects every accessible register, return grille, and the air handler cabinet itself. In Clyde's older ranch-style homes, attic access to the air handler is sometimes the only way to assess the condition of the plenum and main trunk — that inspection happens before a single tool is connected.

From there, the process follows a source-removal approach consistent with NADCA guidelines:

- **Negative pressure containment** — a high-powered vacuum is connected to the main trunk, placing the entire duct system under negative pressure so dislodged debris travels toward the collection unit rather than into living space.
- **Agitation of each branch** — rotating brushes and compressed-air whips work through every supply and return branch, breaking loose compacted dust, lint, and debris from duct walls.
- **Register and boot cleaning** — each grille is removed, cleaned, and inspected for damage before reinstallation.
- **Air handler cabinet wipe-down** — the evaporator coil area, blower wheel housing, and drain pan are inspected and surface-cleaned as part of the service.
- **Optional sanitization** — an EPA-registered antimicrobial fogger can be applied to the interior duct surfaces after cleaning, which is worth considering in Clyde homes that have had any moisture intrusion or visible mold near registers.

As an IICRC Certified Firm, the team applies the same documentation discipline to duct work that it does to water and fire losses — before-and-after photos of key areas, a written scope, and a clear explanation of what was found.

## Local Note

One pattern that comes up repeatedly in Callahan County homes is dryer vent runs that exit through the eave rather than through the exterior wall. It was a common installation shortcut in the region's older construction, and it creates two problems: the run is longer than ideal, and the termination point is often partially blocked by the soffit overhang, reducing airflow and accelerating lint buildup. If your dryer is taking two cycles to dry a normal load, that vent geometry — not the dryer itself — is frequently the culprit. A cleaning combined with a termination cap inspection usually resolves it.

## What to Expect After Cleaning

Most Clyde homeowners notice the difference within the first full HVAC cycle after service — less dust settling on furniture near registers, improved airflow from vents that had been underperforming, and in some cases a measurable drop in how long the system runs to reach setpoint.
