## Why Haskell Properties Are Vulnerable to Severe Fire Damage

Haskell sits in a semi-arid stretch of the Rolling Plains where summer temperatures regularly push past 100°F and relative humidity can drop into the single digits during a dry stretch. That combination means fires in unoccupied structures — barns, outbuildings, older single-story homes — can reach flashover faster than in more humid climates, and the resulting smoke is denser and more alkaline. Alkaline smoke is particularly destructive: it etches metal fixtures, discolors grout, and begins breaking down synthetic fabrics within hours of a fire being extinguished.

Haskell's housing stock skews older, with a meaningful share of homes built before 1970 using construction methods that are now uncommon — tongue-and-groove subfloors, plaster-over-lath interior walls, and in some cases original knob-and-tube or aluminum branch wiring. In a fire loss, those materials behave differently than modern OSB and drywall. Plaster absorbs smoke particles deep into its matrix, and older wood framing can hold heat long after the visible fire is out, creating hidden hot spots that slow the restoration timeline.

## Our Fire Damage Restoration Process in Haskell

The first thing a crew does on arrival is a room-by-room safety assessment — not a sales walkthrough. Structural integrity, residual heat pockets, and utility status (gas shut-off, electrical panel condition) are confirmed before any contents are moved or documentation begins. In Haskell, where natural gas service is common and older homes may have aging supply lines, that utility check is not a formality.

Once the structure is confirmed safe, the process follows a deliberate sequence:

- **Board-up and tarping** to secure the structure against weather and unauthorized entry
- **Contents inventory and pack-out** — salvageable items are catalogued, tagged, and moved to a clean staging area before smoke residue migrates further
- **Dry ice blasting or chemical sponge cleaning** of structural surfaces, depending on soot type (wet smoke vs. dry smoke behaves very differently on plaster vs. drywall)
- **Thermal fogging and hydroxyl generation** to neutralize embedded odor molecules in wall cavities and HVAC ductwork — techniques grounded in our IICRC FSRT and OCT certifications
- **Structural drying** if firefighting water has saturated subfloors or wall assemblies — our IICRC WRT certification covers that phase
- **Reconstruction coordination**, scoped to what the structure actually needs rather than a blanket gut-and-rebuild

All work is documented with photos, moisture readings, and scope notes — the same documentation your insurance adjuster will need to close the claim.
