When a fire, flood, or storm hits a Roby property, the damage rarely stops at the walls. Smoke odor embeds itself in upholstered furniture, soot coats family heirlooms, and floodwater soaks into the wooden legs of dining sets before a homeowner even has a chance to call for help. Contents restoration and storage is how you recover what matters most — and in a small, tight-knit community like Roby, those belongings often carry decades of history worth protecting.

## Why Roby Properties Face Distinct Contents Damage Challenges

Fisher County's climate creates a particular set of conditions that accelerate damage to personal belongings. Summer heat regularly pushes well past 100°F, which means smoke residue and soot from a fire bake into porous materials faster than in cooler climates. Fabrics, wood finishes, and paper goods can be permanently set within hours if not addressed quickly. On the flip side, the region's periodic heavy rains — especially during spring storm season — can overwhelm older drainage systems and push water into homes with crawl spaces or pier-and-beam foundations, soaking flooring and the furniture resting on it.

Many homes in and around Roby were built in the mid-twentieth century, when construction materials and furniture manufacturing standards were different. Older cabinetry, solid-wood furniture, and vintage upholstery respond differently to water and smoke than modern particle-board or synthetic materials — sometimes better, sometimes worse, but always requiring a technician who understands the difference before deciding what can be restored versus what cannot.

## Our Contents Restoration and Storage Process in Roby

The process starts with a thorough inventory. Every item in the affected area is documented — photographed, catalogued, and assessed — before anything is moved. This documentation matters both for your peace of mind and for the insurance claim that typically follows a major loss.

From there, salvageable contents are carefully packed out and transported to a secure, climate-controlled facility. In West Texas, climate control during storage is not optional — extreme heat and humidity swings can continue damaging belongings even after they leave a damaged structure. At the storage facility, items undergo cleaning and restoration appropriate to their material: ultrasonic cleaning for hard goods and collectibles, ozone and hydroxyl treatment for odor-bearing fabrics and soft furnishings, dry-cleaning methods for delicate textiles, and specialized soot removal for wood and upholstered furniture.

Once your home or property is repaired and ready, contents are returned, unpacked, and placed back where they belong. The goal is that you walk back into your space and recognize it — not into a room full of unfamiliar boxes.

## Working with Your Insurance Carrier After a Roby Loss

Contents losses are frequently the most contested part of a property insurance claim. Carriers want itemized documentation, replacement cost valuations, and evidence that restoration was attempted before replacement was recommended. The inventory and photo documentation completed during pack-out feeds directly into that process. Air Care Restoration works alongside your adjuster — providing detailed damage documentation, estimates, invoices, and technical scope information — so the contents portion of your claim is supported by accurate, thorough records rather than a handwritten list of damaged items from memory. Coverage and settlement decisions remain between you and your carrier.

## Local Note

West Texas dust is a real factor that most out-of-area restoration companies underestimate. In Roby and the broader Fisher County area, fine caliche and red clay dust infiltrates homes constantly — and after a fire or flood, that dust mixes with soot, ash, or moisture to create a compound residue that is harder to remove than either substance alone. Cleaning contents after a loss here often requires an additional decontamination pass that would not be standard protocol in a less arid region.
