## Why Merkel Properties See Flood Damage

Merkel sits on the flat, heavy clay soils typical of the Rolling Plains stretch of Taylor County. That clay is part of why the area supports ranching and cotton farming, but it's also slow to drain. When a summer storm dumps a fast inch or two, the ground can't take it in quickly, so water pools against foundations, backs up in yard drainage, and sits longer than it would on sandier soil. Older homes built closer to grade, common in Merkel's original town core along the I-20 corridor, are especially prone to water finding its way under baseboards and into wall cavities during these events. Add a slow-moving system stalling over Taylor County, which happens a few times most springs and falls, and a property can take on more water than a single storm would suggest.

## Our Flood Damage Restoration Process in Merkel

The first hours matter most. We extract standing water, then move to moisture mapping with penetrating meters so we know what's wet behind walls and under flooring, not just what's visibly wet on the surface. Materials are cleaned and sanitized following the IICRC S500 water damage standard, which governs how contaminated flood water is categorized and handled differently from a clean supply-line break. Structural drying follows, with air movers and dehumidifiers set to the specific square footage and construction type, because a pier-and-beam home with a ventilated crawlspace dries differently than a slab home with saturated carpet pad. We document everything with photos and readings as we go, both for our own drying verification and for your insurance file.

## Merkel Insurance Coordination

Flood damage claims in this part of Texas often get complicated by a simple fact: a standard homeowner's policy typically excludes flood water that enters from the ground up, even though it may cover a burst pipe or roof leak. If the water came from rising ground saturation or overwhelmed drainage rather than a plumbing failure, a separate NFIP flood policy is usually what pays the claim, if one exists. We document the loss thoroughly regardless of which policy applies, with moisture readings, photos, and a clear description of how the water entered, so your adjuster has what's needed either way.

## Local note

A good number of homes in Merkel's older sections were built pier-and-beam rather than slab, with a crawlspace instead of a foundation poured flush to grade. Flood water that gets under a pier-and-beam house doesn't always show itself the way it does in a slab home. It can sit trapped against the subfloor and framing for days, held in by clay soil that won't let it percolate away, while the living space above looks dry. We check crawlspace moisture and airflow on these homes even when the visible damage inside looks minor, because that trapped moisture is exactly what leads to subfloor rot and mold colonization in framing lumber if it's left alone.
