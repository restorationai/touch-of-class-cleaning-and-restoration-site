## Why Coleman Properties Hold Odors Longer

West Texas heat is relentless from late spring through early fall, and Coleman is no exception. When temperatures climb into the triple digits, volatile organic compounds from smoke residue, mold byproducts, and biological contamination off-gas more aggressively into the air and deeper into porous materials. Older homes with wood-plank subfloors, original plaster walls, or uninsulated crawl spaces are especially vulnerable because those materials have micro-porous surfaces that trap odor molecules in ways that modern drywall and concrete slab construction does not.

Coleman also sits in a region where grassland and rangeland fires are a recurring reality. A structure fire or a wildfire that pushes smoke through open windows leaves soot particles and phenolic compounds embedded in insulation, upholstery, and HVAC systems. That residue does not simply air out. Without treatment, it continues to off-gas for months, especially when the house heats up each afternoon.

Seasonal flooding from local creek and river drainages can leave standing water under pier-and-beam homes, and once that moisture is gone, the microbial odor it leaves behind can be just as persistent as smoke. The combination of heat and residual humidity in enclosed crawl spaces accelerates bacterial growth and the musty, ammonia-tinged odors that follow.

## Our Odor Removal and Deodorization Process in Coleman

Effective deodorization is not a single-product solution. The process starts with a thorough assessment to identify odor sources rather than mask them. That means pulling back flooring if necessary, inspecting ductwork, and using moisture meters and thermal imaging to find hidden reservoirs of contamination that are still off-gassing.

From there, the approach is matched to the odor type and the structure:

**Thermal fogging** penetrates the same pathways smoke traveled during a fire, reaching into wall cavities, ceiling voids, and HVAC channels where surface sprays cannot reach. The fogging agent pairs with odor molecules and neutralizes them at the chemical level.

**Hydroxyl deodorization** uses UV-generated hydroxyl radicals to break down odor compounds in the air and on surfaces. Unlike ozone, hydroxyl generators can operate in occupied or partially occupied spaces, which matters when a Coleman property manager needs to maintain access to part of a building during treatment.

**Ozone treatment** is reserved for unoccupied spaces with severe contamination. At high concentrations, ozone oxidizes odor-causing compounds effectively, but the space must be vacated and properly ventilated before re-entry. Our IICRC-certified team follows strict protocols to ensure safe application and clearance.

For biological odors from sewage, pets, or decomposition, enzyme-based treatments are applied directly to affected materials before any deodorization equipment is deployed, because neutralizing the source is always the first step.

## Local Note

One thing that comes up specifically in Coleman's older residential areas is the prevalence of whole-house evaporative coolers, sometimes called swamp coolers, in homes built before central refrigerated air became standard. Those units pull outside air directly through water-saturated pads and circulate it through the house. After a smoke or mold event, the cooler's pads, reservoir, and ductwork can become a secondary odor source that recontaminates a space even after the primary treatment is complete. Any deodorization plan for a Coleman home with an evaporative cooler needs to include inspection and treatment of that system, or the smell will return with the first hot day of the season.
