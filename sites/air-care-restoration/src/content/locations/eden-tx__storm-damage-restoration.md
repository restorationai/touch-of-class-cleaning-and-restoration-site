## Why Eden Properties See Significant Storm Damage

Concho County's geography puts Eden directly in the path of some of the most energetic storm systems in Texas. The flat-to-rolling landscape between the Concho River drainage and the rougher terrain to the south gives thunderstorm cells an almost unobstructed run at the area. Hail is a persistent threat — storms that build over the Permian Basin or the Trans-Pecos regularly track northeast and arrive over Eden with golf-ball-sized stones that punch through asphalt shingles, crack skylights, and dent metal roofing on agricultural outbuildings.

The local building stock adds another layer of complexity. Many homes and commercial properties in Eden were built in eras when vapor barriers and hurricane straps were not standard practice, meaning that once a roof is compromised, water moves fast into wall cavities and ceiling assemblies. The caliche-heavy soils common across this part of West Texas drain poorly after a saturating rain, so water that enters a crawlspace or foundation perimeter can sit for days rather than hours. That standing moisture is all mold needs to begin colonizing — and in warm Texas conditions, that window is shorter than most homeowners expect.

## Our Storm Damage Restoration Process in Eden

Every storm job starts with a thorough exterior and interior inspection before any debris is moved or materials are cut out. Wind-driven rain hides in unexpected places — behind exterior trim, inside wall bays where a soffit was breached, under flooring where a door seal failed. Skipping the inspection to rush straight to cleanup is how secondary damage gets missed and reopened insurance claims get denied.

Once the scope is documented with photos and moisture readings, the process moves in a clear sequence:

- **Emergency stabilization** — tarping or board-up of any roof or wall opening to stop active water intrusion
- **Water extraction and structural drying** — commercial-grade extractors and desiccant or refrigerant dehumidifiers placed per IICRC S500 drying standards
- **Debris and damaged material removal** — storm-compromised insulation, sheathing, and interior finishes removed and disposed of properly
- **Reconstruction coordination** — framing, roofing, drywall, and finish work scoped and scheduled so the property is returned to pre-loss condition

Because Eden is a smaller community with limited local contractor capacity, Air Care Restoration handles the full chain — mitigation through reconstruction — rather than handing off mid-job and leaving homeowners to manage multiple vendors.

## Local Note

One pattern that shows up repeatedly in storm work across Concho County: older metal roofs on ranch-style homes and agricultural buildings are often fastened with exposed screws that back out over years of thermal cycling in the West Texas heat. A hail event or high-wind storm that looks minor from the ground can be enough to lift panels at those loose fasteners, creating gaps that funnel water directly onto ceiling insulation and wood decking below. The damage reads as a slow leak on the interior — staining rather than flooding — which leads some property owners to delay the call. By the time the stain is visible, the decking underneath is often already compromised.

If a storm has passed through Eden and something looks even slightly off on a metal roof, an inspection is worth scheduling before the next rain arrives.
