## Why Robert Lee Properties Are Especially Vulnerable to Storm Damage

Coke County sits in a corridor where Gulf moisture collides with dry continental air, making the area a recurring target for severe thunderstorms from late spring through early fall. The flat, open terrain west of the Concho River offers little natural windbreak, so straight-line winds that might clip a tree in a wooded suburb can instead strip shingles clean off a roof here.

The housing stock in Robert Lee reflects decades of West Texas building tradition — pier-and-beam foundations, wood-frame construction, and metal roofing on older ranch-style homes. Metal roofs handle hail better than asphalt shingles in many respects, but seam separations and fastener failures after a severe storm can allow water intrusion that goes undetected until ceiling drywall begins to sag. Older wood-frame homes with original single-pane windows are particularly susceptible to wind-driven rain penetrating around aged caulk lines and window frames.

The caliche-heavy soil common throughout Coke County also drains slowly after a heavy rain event, meaning floodwater can pool against foundations and work its way into crawl spaces and slab perimeters long after the storm has passed.

## Our Storm Damage Restoration Process in Robert Lee

Every job starts with a thorough damage assessment — roof, exterior envelope, windows, and interior — before any work begins. We document everything with photographs and moisture readings, which serves two purposes: it gives you a clear picture of what happened, and it gives your insurance adjuster the evidence package needed to process your claim efficiently.

For wind and tree damage, the first priority is emergency stabilization: tarping compromised roof sections, boarding broken windows, and removing any debris that poses an immediate safety risk. Once the structure is secured against further weather exposure, we move into the interior — extracting standing water, identifying hidden moisture with thermal imaging and pin-less moisture meters, and setting industrial drying equipment calibrated to the ambient conditions. West Texas heat and low relative humidity can be an asset during drying, but only if equipment placement accounts for the way heat moves through a structure in a region where afternoon temperatures routinely exceed 95°F.

After drying targets are confirmed, structural repairs and finish work — drywall, insulation, roofing, flooring — are coordinated so you're not managing multiple contractors on your own.

## Local Note

One thing worth knowing about storm response in Robert Lee specifically: the town's water infrastructure, like that of many small West Texas communities, can experience pressure fluctuations after a severe storm event — particularly if the storm knocks out power to pumping stations. If your home sustained roof or window damage that allowed water intrusion, and you then lose municipal water pressure, don't assume the interior moisture you're seeing is all storm-related. Secondary leaks from pipes stressed by pressure swings are not uncommon in older plumbing systems. We check for both during our initial assessment, because missing a secondary source means the drying process will stall even with the best equipment running.
