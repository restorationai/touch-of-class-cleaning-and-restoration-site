## Why Rotan Properties Are Prone to Asbestos-Containing Materials

Fisher County's building boom tracked closely with the broader West Texas oil economy of the mid-twentieth century, and the construction methods of that era leaned heavily on materials we now know to be hazardous. Asbestos appeared in virtually every layer of a building: the pipe insulation wrapping the heating system, the vinyl floor tiles in kitchens and bathrooms, the joint compound behind drywall seams, and the spray-applied texture on ceilings that homeowners today call popcorn ceilings. In the dry, low-humidity air common to this part of Texas, those materials can become brittle over time. A brittle asbestos-containing tile or ceiling texture is more likely to release fibers when disturbed than the same material in a more humid climate, which makes testing before any demolition or renovation work especially important in homes built here before the mid-1980s.

Rotan's commercial corridor also includes older structures, some of which have seen multiple rounds of interior renovation without full removal of earlier materials. Layered flooring, patched ceilings, and wrapped ductwork can all hide asbestos beneath more recent finishes, and a visual inspection alone cannot confirm what is present.

## Our Asbestos Abatement Process in Rotan

Every project begins with a thorough asbestos inspection and bulk sampling of suspect materials. Samples are submitted to an accredited laboratory, and no abatement work begins until results are confirmed. That sequence matters: disturbing materials before you know what you are dealing with can spread fibers through an HVAC system or into adjacent rooms in ways that are difficult and expensive to address afterward.

Once a material tests positive, the work area is sealed with negative-air containment using heavy-gauge polyethylene sheeting and HEPA-filtered air scrubbers. Regulated materials are wetted to suppress fiber release, carefully removed, and double-bagged in labeled, sealed disposal bags that meet Texas Commission on Environmental Quality requirements. After removal, the area undergoes a visual clearance inspection and air sampling before containment is broken. That final air clearance step is not optional; it is the documented proof that the space is safe to reoccupy.

For popcorn ceiling asbestos removal specifically, the process requires wetting the texture thoroughly before any scraping begins. Dry removal of spray-applied texture is one of the fastest ways to contaminate a living space with airborne fibers, and it is a shortcut that no properly trained crew takes.

## Local Note

One thing worth knowing about older homes in Rotan and the surrounding Fisher County area: the region's alkaline caliche soil and the temperature swings between summer heat and winter cold cause foundations to shift more than in parts of Texas with more stable soil profiles. That movement stresses floor tile adhesives and ceiling finishes over decades, and cracked or lifting vinyl floor tiles in a pre-1980 home are a reliable indicator that the tile and the mastic beneath it may both contain asbestos. Before any flooring replacement project in a home of that era, sampling both the tile and the adhesive layer separately is the right call, because the two materials were often manufactured differently and may have different asbestos content.
