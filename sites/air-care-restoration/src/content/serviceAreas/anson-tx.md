## Building stock, site conditions, and permits in Anson

Anson's residential housing stock is largely mid-twentieth century construction — many homes date from the post-war decades through the 1970s, with a smaller share of newer builds. That era means wood-framed walls on slab-on-grade foundations are common, and slab construction means water intrusion typically spreads laterally under flooring rather than pooling in a visible basement. Plumbing in homes of that vintage often includes galvanized steel supply lines, which corrode from the inside out and can fail with little external warning. Lead-based paint and asbestos-containing materials — floor tiles, pipe insulation, joint compound — are plausible in pre-1980 structures and must be assessed before any demolition work begins.

The soils across Jones County include expansive clays that shrink and swell with moisture changes, which stresses slab foundations over time and can open gaps where water migrates. Caliche layers beneath the topsoil limit drainage, meaning water from a heavy rain or a broken line has fewer places to go.
