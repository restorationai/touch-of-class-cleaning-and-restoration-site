## Restoration emergencies common in Buffalo Gap

Buffalo Gap sits in a stretch of West Texas where the weather can shift hard and fast. Severe thunderstorms roll through Taylor County with little warning, bringing hail that punches through roofing and drives water into attic assemblies. In winter, hard freezes — often arriving overnight — can split exposed supply lines in older homes that weren't built with deep-freeze protection in mind. Summer heat accelerates mold colonization: once moisture is present, visible growth can appear within 24 to 48 hours in an unventilated space. ### Fire and smoke damage restoration

Grass and brush fires are a real seasonal hazard in Taylor County, and smoke infiltration can happen even when a structure itself isn't directly threatened. Smoke odor binds to porous materials — insulation, wood framing, soft furnishings — and doesn't respond to surface cleaning alone. Restoration here involves controlled demolition of unsalvageable materials, structural cleaning, and odor neutralization at the source. ## Building stock, site conditions, and permits in Buffalo Gap

Much of Buffalo Gap's residential housing dates to the mid-twentieth century or earlier, with a smaller number of newer builds on the town's edges.
