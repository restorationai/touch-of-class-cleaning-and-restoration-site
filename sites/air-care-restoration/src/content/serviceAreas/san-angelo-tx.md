## Restoration emergencies common in San Angelo

San Angelo sits in the heart of the Concho Valley, where the climate swings hard in both directions. Summers push triple digits for weeks at a time, drying out wood framing and causing caulk and flashing to crack — the same gaps that let wind-driven rain pour in when a West Texas thunderstorm rolls through fast and heavy. Winters bring periodic hard freezes that can catch older supply lines off guard, particularly in homes built before modern insulation standards were common. The Concho River and its tributaries can rise quickly after intense rainfall on the rocky, low-permeability caliche and clay soils that cover much of the area; water that has nowhere to soak in runs off fast and can push into low-lying structures before owners have time to react. Hail is a recurring hazard that damages roofing and siding, opening pathways for moisture intrusion that may not show up as a visible leak for weeks.

## Services we provide in San Angelo

### Water damage restoration

Water damage in San Angelo often starts small — a slow leak behind a vanity, a failed water heater, a roof penetration after a hailstorm — and grows quickly because the heat accelerates evaporation at the surface while moisture migrates deeper into wall cavities and subfloor assemblies. Air Care Restoration uses thermal imaging and moisture meters to map what the eye misses, then extracts standing water and sets drying equipment calibrated to the ambient conditions. Structural drying is documented throughout so your insurance carrier has the records they need.

### Fire and smoke damage restoration

After a fire, the visible char is rarely the full story. Smoke and soot travel through HVAC systems and settle into insulation, drywall, and soft materials in rooms that never saw a flame. In San Angelo's older housing stock, that can mean smoke residue in plaster walls or original hardwood floors that require careful cleaning rather than replacement. Air Care Restoration scopes fire losses to separate what can be restored from what needs to be rebuilt, and handles both.

### Mold remediation

Mold can begin colonizing a wet surface within 24 to 48 hours under the right temperature and humidity conditions — and San Angelo's summer heat creates those conditions quickly after any water intrusion. Remediation involves containing the affected area, removing compromised materials, and treating surfaces to address the source rather than just the visible growth. Occupants should not assume a surface that looks dry is safe without professional assessment.

### Reconstruction

Once mitigation is complete, the structure still needs to be put back together. Air Care Restoration handles the rebuild phase — drywall, framing, flooring, cabinetry, and finishes — so property owners work with one point of contact from the first call through the final walkthrough rather than coordinating a separate general contractor.

## Building stock, site conditions, and permits in San Angelo

Much of San Angelo's residential housing was built between the 1940s and the 1980s, when slab-on-grade construction was standard across this part of West Texas. Older homes in that stock commonly have galvanized steel supply lines that corrode from the inside out and cast iron drain lines that crack with age and ground movement. Homes built before the mid-1980s may contain asbestos-containing materials in floor tile, pipe insulation, or textured drywall compound, and pre-1978 construction carries lead paint risk — both of which affect how a restoration scope is written and what abatement steps precede any demolition. The soils across Tom Green County include expansive clays and caliche layers that shift with moisture changes, stressing slab foundations and the plumbing embedded in them. Structural repairs and significant rebuilds in San Angelo fall under the City of San Angelo's building department, which issues permits and inspects work; restoration contractors need to account for that permitting timeline when scoping a rebuild.
