## Services we provide in Snyder

### Water damage mitigation

Water damage in Snyder frequently starts at the roof or the supply lines — hail-cracked shingles let moisture into attic insulation, and aging pipe joints fail quietly behind walls. By the time a homeowner notices a stain or a soft floor, the moisture has often already moved laterally through subfloor material or wall cavities. Air Care Restoration uses thermal imaging and moisture meters to map the full extent of the intrusion, extract standing water, and dry structural assemblies to documented target levels before any rebuild begins.

### Fire and smoke damage restoration

After a fire — whether it started in a kitchen, a utility room, or from an electrical fault — the visible char is only part of the problem. Smoke and soot migrate through HVAC systems and settle into porous surfaces throughout the home. In Snyder's drier climate, residue can become embedded quickly. We clean and deodorize affected materials, assess what can be restored versus what requires replacement, and document everything for your insurance claim.

### Mold remediation

Mold can begin colonizing wet building materials within 24 to 48 hours of a moisture event. In homes with older wall assemblies — plaster over lath, or fiberglass batts without proper vapor management — moisture trapped after a roof leak or a burst pipe creates conditions for mold growth that isn't visible from the surface. Air Care Restoration contains affected areas, removes compromised materials following established remediation protocols, and addresses the underlying moisture source so the problem doesn't return.

### Reconstruction

Once mitigation is complete, the structure still needs to be put back together. We carry the project through reconstruction — replacing drywall, flooring, cabinetry, and structural framing as needed — so you're working with one point of contact from the first call to the final walkthrough.

## Building stock, site conditions, and permits in Snyder

Much of Snyder's residential housing was built between the 1940s and the 1980s, a period when slab-on-grade construction dominated West Texas due to the region's expansive clay and caliche soils. Those soils shift with moisture changes — swelling when wet, contracting in drought — which stresses slab edges and the plumbing penetrations running through them. Homes from the mid-century era often contain galvanized steel supply lines that have reached or exceeded their service life, and properties built before the late 1970s may contain asbestos-containing materials in floor tile, pipe insulation, or textured ceilings.
