## Restoration emergencies common in Haskell

Haskell sits in the Rolling Plains of West Texas, where the climate swings hard in both directions. Summer heat regularly pushes past 100°F, drying out wood framing and causing supply lines and hose bibs to age faster than they would in milder climates. Winter is the other extreme: hard freezes arrive fast across Haskell County, and when a cold snap drops temperatures below the teens, older copper and galvanized supply lines — common in the county's mid-twentieth-century housing stock — can split overnight. That sudden release of water inside a wall cavity or under a slab is one of the most frequent reasons Haskell homeowners call a restoration company.

Severe thunderstorms and hail are a seasonal fixture across this part of the Rolling Plains. A single storm can puncture roofing, drive rain into attic insulation, and leave standing water on ceilings before the clouds clear. Because Haskell County's soils include heavy shrink-swell clay in many areas, saturated ground after a storm can also shift against foundations and allow water to migrate into lower-level spaces over days — long after the rain stops.

Air Care Restoration follows established remediation protocols to contain, remove, and dry affected materials.

## Building stock, site conditions, and permits in Haskell

Much of Haskell's residential housing was built between the 1940s and the 1980s. Homes from that era typically sit on slab-on-grade foundations or, in some older properties, pier-and-beam construction — both common across small West Texas towns. Wall assemblies are usually wood-framed with original plaster or later drywall, and roofs are predominantly low-slope or gently pitched with composition shingles. Plumbing in homes built before the mid-1980s often includes galvanized steel supply lines that corrode from the inside out, or early copper that has thinned with decades of use.

Homes built before 1980 may contain asbestos-containing materials in floor tile, pipe insulation, or textured ceiling coatings, and lead-based paint is a realistic consideration in pre-1978 structures. Any restoration scope on an older Haskell property should account for these hazards before demolition begins. Structural repair and reconstruction work in Haskell falls under the City of Haskell's permitting authority; permits are typically required when work involves structural members, electrical, or plumbing — your restoration contractor should confirm what triggers a permit for your specific scope before work begins.
