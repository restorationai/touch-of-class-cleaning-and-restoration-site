**TL;DR:** A plumber costs roughly $100-$250 to auger a toilet clog from the bowl. If the clog is deeper in the branch drain, expect $200-$400. A pull-and-reset (where the toilet comes off the floor) runs $250-$500. If the clog turns out to be a main sewer line problem, you're looking at $300-$600 or more depending on access. After-hours calls add a premium on top of those ranges.

Your toilet won't flush and the water is sitting high in the bowl. Before you reach for the plunger again, there's one quick test worth doing: flush the toilet and watch your bathtub drain. If the tub gurgles or water backs up into it, stop. That's not a toilet clog. That's a main sewer line problem, and no amount of plunging will fix it. If the tub stays quiet and only the toilet is slow, you're almost certainly dealing with a localized clog that a plumber can clear in under an hour. Here's what that costs, broken down by how deep the problem actually is.

## What does a plumber charge to unclog a toilet?

A plumber charges $100-$250 to clear a standard toilet clog using a closet auger, which is the most common scenario. The auger is a coiled cable tool designed specifically for the toilet's trap and drain geometry. It breaks up or retrieves whatever is blocking the drain without damaging the porcelain. Most straightforward clogs are solved this way in 30-60 minutes, including the service call.

That base price covers the labor to diagnose, auger, and confirm the drain is flowing. It does not include any parts, which you won't need for a simple clog. Most plumbers charge a flat service call fee (typically $75-$150) that gets applied toward the repair if you proceed, so ask about that structure before anyone starts work.

## What are the five cost tiers for a toilet clog?

Toilet clogs escalate in a predictable pattern. Each tier reflects how far down the drain system the problem sits.

**Tier 1: Auger from the bowl ($100-$250)**
The clog is in the toilet trap or just past it. The plumber inserts a closet auger through the bowl, breaks up the blockage, and flushes to confirm. No disassembly needed. This is the most common call.

**Tier 2: Branch drain clearing ($200-$400)**
The clog is past the toilet but still in the 3- or 4-inch branch line that connects to the main stack. The plumber accesses a cleanout fitting or uses a longer drain machine. Takes longer and may require working in a crawlspace or under the house. Older Bakersfield homes with cast-iron drains sometimes have partial blockages here from decades of scale buildup.

**Tier 3: Pull-and-reset ($250-$500)**
Some clogs can't be cleared without removing the toilet from the floor. Common causes: a child's toy, a hygiene product, or a hard object lodged in the trap that the auger can't retrieve. The plumber pulls the toilet, clears the obstruction, replaces the wax ring, and resets the toilet. Add $50-$100 if the closet bolts are corroded and need replacement, which is common in older homes.

**Tier 4: Main sewer line clearing ($300-$600+)**
If the tub gurgles when the toilet flushes, or if multiple fixtures are slow at the same time, the blockage is in the main sewer line, not the toilet. This is a different job entirely. The plumber accesses the main cleanout and runs a sewer machine or hydro-jetter to clear the line. Pricing depends on the length of the run and the severity of the blockage. Root intrusion is a common cause in older Bakersfield neighborhoods with mature tree canopy. Our [drain cleaning service page](/services/drain-cleaning/) covers this scope in more detail, and we have a companion post pricing [drain and sewer lateral clogs by type](/blog/how-much-does-a-plumber-cost-to-unclog-a-drain/) if you want to compare.

**Tier 5: Camera inspection ($150-$350 standalone, often bundled)**
If a toilet keeps clogging every few months, a camera inspection tells you why. The plumber runs a small camera through the drain and records what it finds: root intrusion, a collapsed section, an offset joint, heavy scale. Without a camera, you're guessing. Most plumbers will bundle the camera fee into the repair quote if they find something that needs fixing.

## How do you tell if it's a toilet clog or a main line problem?

A toilet-only clog shows up in one fixture. A main line problem shows up in multiple fixtures at once or causes cross-fixture reactions. Here are the clearest signs:

- **Tub gurgles when you flush the toilet.** This is the most reliable indicator. The gurgle means air is being pushed back through the system because the main line is blocked downstream.
- **Multiple drains are slow at the same time.** If the toilet, the bathroom sink, and the shower are all draining slowly, the problem is downstream of all of them.
- **Sewage backs up into the tub or shower when you flush.** This is a main line emergency. Stop using water in the house and call a plumber immediately.
- **Only the toilet is affected, other drains are fine.** This points to a localized toilet or branch drain clog.

Getting this diagnosis right before a plumber arrives saves time and sets accurate expectations on cost. If you describe the gurgling tub on the phone, a good plumber will bring the right equipment for a main line call rather than showing up with just an auger.

## Why does my toilet keep clogging?

A toilet that clogs repeatedly is telling you something beyond "flush less paper." Common causes include a partial blockage that was never fully cleared, a low-flow toilet that lacks the flush volume to move waste through a long branch run, a drain line with inadequate slope (especially in slab-foundation homes where the pipe pitch can't be easily adjusted), or root intrusion into the sewer lateral that keeps rebuilding after each clearing.

If you've had the same toilet augered more than twice in a year, a camera inspection is the right next step. It's cheaper than another service call that only buys you another few months. The [EPA's WaterSense program](https://www.epa.gov/watersense/residential-toilets) notes that older toilets using 3.5 gallons per flush or more are both water-wasters and more prone to partial clogs in aging drain systems. A modern 1.28 gpf WaterSense-labeled toilet paired with a properly cleared drain line often solves chronic clogging for good. ## What should you check before hiring any plumber?

Before you authorize work, ask three things: Is the contractor licensed in California? What is the total cost, flat-rate or time-and-materials? And is there a written invoice before work starts?

You can verify any California contractor's license at the [CSLB license lookup](https://www.cslb.ca.gov/OnlineServices/CheckLicenseII/CheckLicense.aspx). Enter the license number and confirm the license is active, the classification covers plumbing work (C-36), and there are no disciplinary actions. This takes two minutes and protects you from unlicensed operators who carry no bond and no workers' compensation insurance.
