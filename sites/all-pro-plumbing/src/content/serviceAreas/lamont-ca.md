## Restoration emergencies common in Lamont

Lamont sits in the southern San Joaquin Valley, where the climate creates a specific and recurring set of plumbing and mechanical problems. Summers push temperatures well above 100°F, which accelerates pipe joint failure, stresses water heaters working against high incoming water temperatures, and drives heavy air conditioning loads that can overwhelm aging HVAC systems. Winter nights do drop below freezing often enough that exposed supply lines in older homes and mobile units are vulnerable to freezing and splitting.

The area's agricultural heritage means irrigation infrastructure and older residential plumbing share the same soil, and that soil matters. Expansive clay common across Kern County's valley floor shifts seasonally, placing stress on slab penetrations and underground supply lines. When those lines crack or joints separate, the first sign is often a spike in a water bill or a soft spot in a yard rather than visible flooding. Inside the home, slow leaks behind walls in older construction can go unnoticed until drywall is already compromised.

Lamont also sees a higher proportion of older housing stock and manufactured homes than nearby Bakersfield neighborhoods, which means plumbing materials and mechanical systems that were installed decades ago and are now reaching the end of their service life.
