## Local Note

One thing that comes up repeatedly in this part of the Valley: the region's agricultural dust is finer than typical urban particulate, and it loads furnace filters much faster than the manufacturer's replacement schedule assumes. A filter rated for 90 days in a typical suburban home may be choked in 30 to 45 days during harvest season when field activity kicks up the surrounding air. If your furnace is short-cycling or the blower is running but heat output feels weak, a completely clogged filter is often the first thing to check, and it is the one fix a homeowner can do before a technician even arrives. Keeping a spare filter on hand through the fall and winter is a small habit that prevents a surprising number of service calls in this area.

## Equipment and Methods We Use for Furnace Repair

Diagnostic work on modern furnaces requires more than a visual inspection. Technicians use combustion analyzers to verify burner efficiency and CO output, digital manometers to measure static pressure across the duct system, and clamp meters to test electrical draw on the blower motor and inducer. For heat exchanger inspections, a camera or mirror check at the burner compartment can reveal cracks that would otherwise go undetected until carbon monoxide becomes a concern. These tools allow for a repair decision based on measured data rather than guesswork.

When parts are replaced, the work is done to manufacturer specifications for the specific make and model, not a generic approach. Furnace brands vary significantly in how ignition systems, pressure switches, and control boards are configured, and a repair that works on one platform may not translate to another.
