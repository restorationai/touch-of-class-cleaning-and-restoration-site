## Why McFarland's Climate Makes Heat Pump Sizing Critical

The San Joaquin Valley's temperature extremes are not evenly distributed across the year, and McFarland's position in Kern County puts it squarely in a zone where a heat pump must perform at both ends of the spectrum. In peak summer, an undersized unit cycles constantly, driving up electricity costs and wearing out the compressor ahead of schedule. In January and February, when overnight lows approach freezing, a heat pump without an appropriate supplemental heat strip or dual-fuel backup loses efficiency precisely when you need it most.

Kern County's flat, open terrain also means wind-driven dust is a persistent issue. Dust and fine particulate from surrounding farmland accumulate on outdoor condenser coils, reducing heat transfer and forcing the compressor to work harder. Coil cleaning is not optional maintenance here, it is a seasonal necessity. When we install a new system, we position the outdoor unit to minimize exposure to prevailing dust drift and select equipment with coil guards rated for agricultural-area conditions.

## Our Heat Pump Installation and Repair Process in McFarland

Every installation starts with a load calculation calibrated to the actual square footage, insulation quality, and window orientation of the specific building, not a rule-of-thumb estimate. Older single-story homes common in McFarland often have minimal attic insulation, which shifts the load calculation significantly compared to a newer build. We account for that before recommending equipment size.

For repairs, our diagnostic process follows the refrigerant circuit from the outdoor unit through the reversing valve and into the air handler. In the Valley's heat, refrigerant charge drift is one of the most common causes of reduced cooling capacity, and it is frequently misdiagnosed as a failing compressor. We verify charge with manifold gauges before recommending any major component replacement.

Because we operate around the clock, a heat pump that stops cooling on a 108°F afternoon or stops heating on a January night is a call we can take and dispatch on the same day.

## Heat Pump vs. Furnace: What Makes Sense in McFarland

The heat pump vs. furnace question comes up often in a region where natural gas has historically been inexpensive and furnaces are the default install in older housing stock. The calculus is shifting. California's utility rate structure, combined with available rebates through programs tied to the state's building decarbonization goals, has made all-electric heat pump systems financially competitive in many McFarland properties, particularly those where the existing ductwork is in good condition.

For properties with older duct systems that leak significantly, a ductless mini-split heat pump is often the more practical path. It avoids the cost of duct remediation and delivers zoned comfort to individual rooms. We carry and install both ducted and ductless configurations and will walk you through the payback comparison before any contract is signed.

One pattern we see repeatedly in McFarland and the surrounding communities along the Highway 99 corridor: homes built in the 1970s and 1980s were often wired with electrical panels sized for the loads of that era.
