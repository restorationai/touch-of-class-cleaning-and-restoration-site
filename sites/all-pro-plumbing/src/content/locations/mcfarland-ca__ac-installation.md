## Why McFarland's Climate and Housing Stock Make AC Sizing Critical

The San Joaquin Valley's extreme summer heat is not evenly distributed across a structure. Homes in McFarland built during the agricultural expansion of the mid-20th century often have low-slope rooflines, minimal attic insulation by current standards, and ductwork that was sized for older, lower-efficiency equipment. Installing a new high-SEER2 air conditioner into that existing system without reassessing duct capacity is one of the most common mistakes in the region, the equipment runs short cycles, the home never reaches setpoint, and the compressor wears prematurely.

Proper Manual J load calculations account for McFarland's intense solar gain, the prevalence of stucco-and-frame construction that absorbs and radiates heat through the afternoon, and the dust that infiltrates duct seams during the valley's dry, windy spring months. Getting the tonnage right the first time avoids a costly second visit and ensures the system qualifies for available utility rebates.

## Our AC Installation and Replacement Process in McFarland

Every installation starts with a site assessment, not a sales pitch. We look at the existing air handler location, refrigerant line condition, electrical panel capacity, and duct condition before recommending equipment. In older McFarland homes, the electrical service sometimes needs a dedicated circuit added before a modern system can be safely connected, we identify that upfront so there are no surprises on installation day.

Once equipment is selected, the process moves in a clear sequence:

- Remove and responsibly dispose of the old unit, including proper refrigerant recovery
- Inspect and seal duct connections, replacing flex duct sections that have collapsed or separated
- Set the new condenser on a level pad with adequate clearance from walls and vegetation
- Connect refrigerant lines, charge the system to manufacturer specification, and verify static pressure
- Commission the thermostat, confirm airflow at each register, and walk you through filter maintenance and warranty registration

For homes receiving central air for the first time, we plan the duct layout to minimize the number of ceiling penetrations and work with the existing attic access points common to single-story McFarland construction.

## Equipment Selection: High-SEER2 Units for Valley Conditions

California adopted updated SEER2 efficiency standards, and all new residential equipment installed in the state must meet those minimums. In a climate like McFarland's, where a central air system may run eight to ten hours a day from May through September, moving up to a higher-efficiency unit pays back meaningfully on monthly PG&E bills compared to a minimum-compliant system. We carry and install equipment from manufacturers with strong parts availability in the Central Valley so that a warranty repair does not require a weeks-long wait for a component to ship.

For homes with gas furnaces sharing the air handler, we also evaluate whether the existing heat exchanger and blower motor are compatible with the new coil and refrigerant type, a mismatch there can void the equipment warranty and reduce efficiency.

## Local Note

McFarland's prevailing winds come out of the northwest through the valley corridor, which means condensing units placed on the south or west side of a home accumulate significantly more airborne dust and agricultural particulate than those on sheltered sides. Over a season, that buildup restricts airflow across the condenser coil and forces the compressor to work harder. When siting a new outdoor unit, we factor in that wind exposure and recommend a placement that balances shade access with airflow, and we note the coil cleaning interval in the maintenance plan accordingly.
