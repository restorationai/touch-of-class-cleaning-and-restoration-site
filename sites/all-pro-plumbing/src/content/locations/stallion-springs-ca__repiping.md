Stallion Springs sits at roughly 4,000 feet in the Tehachapi Mountains, where hard well water, wide seasonal temperature swings, and a housing stock built largely during the 1970s and 1980s tract-development boom create a specific kind of plumbing stress. Galvanized steel pipes installed in those decades have had fifty-plus years to rust from the inside out, and the mineral-heavy water common to this part of Kern County accelerates that process. If your water pressure has been dropping steadily, or you're seeing rust-colored water at the tap, the pipes themselves are likely the source.

## Why Stallion Springs Homes Are Prone to Pipe Failure

The Tehachapi Mountains experience genuine winters, with overnight temperatures that regularly drop below freezing from November through March. That freeze-thaw cycle puts mechanical stress on older metal pipe joints year after year. Galvanized steel, which was the standard material for residential supply lines through most of the 1970s, is especially vulnerable: corrosion builds up on the interior walls, narrowing the pipe diameter and eventually causing pinhole leaks or full-section failures. Polybutylene pipe, used in some homes built in the late 1970s through the early 1990s, has its own failure mode, becoming brittle over time and cracking at fittings.

The water chemistry here compounds the problem. Stallion Springs draws on well and municipal sources that carry elevated levels of calcium and magnesium. That mineral load accelerates scale buildup inside galvanized and older copper lines alike, reducing flow and creating the conditions where corrosion takes hold faster than it would in softer-water areas.

## Our Whole-House Repiping Process in Stallion Springs

A whole-house repipe replaces every supply line from the main shutoff to each fixture, so the finished system is entirely new rather than patched. The work typically runs two to three days for a standard single-family home, depending on square footage, the number of bathrooms, and whether the existing layout routes pipes through accessible crawl spaces or through finished walls.

For most Stallion Springs homes, we use PEX tubing as the primary material. PEX handles freeze-thaw cycles better than rigid copper because it has some flex, which matters at this elevation. It also resists the scale buildup that shortens the life of metal pipe in hard-water conditions. Where local code or customer preference calls for copper, we carry that material as well and can discuss the tradeoffs for your specific home.

The process starts with a full inspection of the existing system, mapping which lines are original and which may have been partially replaced over the years. We shut off the water, work section by section to minimize the time any part of the house is without service, and patch drywall or access panels before we leave. Kern County permit requirements apply to this work, and we pull the necessary permits so the installation is inspected and on record.
