## Why Lamont Properties See Repiping Issues

The San Joaquin Valley's hard water is well documented, and Lamont is no exception. High mineral content accelerates the interior scaling of galvanized steel pipe, narrowing the bore over years until flow is noticeably restricted and rust flakes reach your fixtures. Homes built in Kern County during the postwar agricultural boom of the 1950s and 1960s were routinely plumbed with galvanized supply lines that are now well past their practical service life.

A second concern is polybutylene pipe, a gray plastic material installed widely through the 1980s and into the early 1990s. Polybutylene degrades when it contacts oxidants present in treated municipal water, and the failure mode is sudden: the pipe splits internally before showing any visible exterior damage. If your home was built or replumbed during that window, a camera inspection of the supply lines is worth scheduling before a failure forces the issue.

The clay-heavy soils common across this part of Kern County also shift seasonally. Slab foundations move slightly as soil moisture changes between wet winters and dry summers, and that movement can stress older, more brittle pipe materials at joints and bends.

## Our Whole-House Repiping Process in Lamont

Every repipe starts with a full assessment of the existing system: material identification, pressure testing, and a review of the home's layout to plan the most efficient routing for new lines. From there the process follows a clear sequence.

**Material selection.** Most Lamont homes are well served by PEX tubing. It handles the temperature swings of a high-desert climate, resists the scale buildup that shortens copper's lifespan in hard-water conditions, and is flexible enough to route through walls with fewer access cuts than rigid pipe. Copper remains a strong choice where local code or a homeowner's preference calls for it, and the crew can walk you through the tradeoffs for your specific home.

**Access and installation.** The crew opens walls and ceilings at strategic points, removes the old pipe, and runs new supply lines throughout the home. Access points are kept as small as practical, and drywall patching is included in the scope so you are not left coordinating a separate repair.

**Pressure testing and inspection.** Before any wall is closed, the new system is pressure-tested and inspected. Kern County requires a permit for whole-house repiping work, and All Pro Plumbing Heating and Air pulls that permit and schedules the inspection as part of the job.

**Restoration.** Once the inspection passes, access points are patched and the water is restored. Most single-family homes in Lamont can be repiped within one to two days.
