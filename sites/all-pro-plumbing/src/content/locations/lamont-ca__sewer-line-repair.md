## Why Lamont Properties See Sewer Line Problems

The San Joaquin Valley floor around Lamont is predominantly clay-heavy soil that expands when wet and contracts sharply during dry spells. That cycle of swelling and shrinking puts lateral stress on buried pipe joints every season, year after year. Older homes in the area, many built during the mid-twentieth century agricultural expansion, were typically plumbed with Orangeburg pipe or vitrified clay, both of which become brittle over time and are especially vulnerable to root intrusion from the large shade trees common on residential lots here.

Agricultural irrigation infrastructure in and around Lamont also raises the local water table at certain times of year, which can create hydrostatic pressure against sewer laterals and accelerate joint separation. A line that reads fine in summer may show a partial collapse by late winter after the soil has moved through a full wet season.

## Our Sewer Line Repair and Replacement Process in Lamont

Every job starts with a sewer camera inspection. A high-resolution camera travels the full length of the lateral from the cleanout to the municipal connection, giving us exact footage of where a crack, offset, root mass, or belly in the pipe is located before any ground is opened. That footage also becomes documentation for your insurance claim if one applies.

Once we know what we are dealing with, we choose the right method for the site:

- **Trenchless pipe lining (CIPP):** A resin-saturated liner is pulled into the existing pipe and cured in place, creating a smooth new pipe inside the old one. No excavation, no disruption to driveways or landscaping.
- **Pipe bursting:** Where the existing pipe is too deteriorated to line, a bursting head fractures the old pipe outward while simultaneously pulling a new HDPE pipe into position. Still trenchless, still minimal surface disturbance.
- **Open-cut replacement:** When a line has fully collapsed or when access requires it, we excavate, remove the failed section, and install new PVC to current code. We restore the surface after backfill and compaction.

Kern County requires a permit for sewer lateral work that connects to the public main, and we pull that permit before any work begins.

## Equipment and Methods We Use for Sewer Line Work

Trenchless repair is the right fit for most Lamont laterals because it avoids disturbing the compacted, clay-dense soil that took years to settle around existing pipe. Reopening that soil and recompacting it improperly can create new settlement problems. Our pipe lining and pipe bursting equipment lets us restore full flow capacity without that risk. Where open-cut is unavoidable, we use laser-level grade checks during backfill to make sure the new pipe has proper slope, because a low spot in a sewer line becomes a recurring backup problem regardless of how new the pipe is.
