## Why Lamont Properties See Elevated Water Treatment Needs

The San Joaquin Valley's geology does most of the explaining here. Water moving through the alluvial soils of Kern County picks up calcium, magnesium, and in some areas elevated nitrates before it ever reaches a home's meter. Hardness levels in this corridor regularly run high enough that water heaters accumulate scale within a few years of installation, shower heads clog, and soap never quite lathers the way it should.

Lamont's housing stock adds another layer. A significant portion of homes here were built during the mid-20th century agricultural boom, with galvanized steel supply lines that have been slowly corroding for decades. Hard water accelerates that corrosion, and the mineral deposits that build up inside older pipe walls narrow the effective diameter over time. Installing a softener or conditioner in these homes isn't just a comfort upgrade; it extends the useful life of plumbing that would be expensive to repipe.

Agricultural runoff is also a real consideration in this part of Kern County. Nitrate levels in some local water sources have drawn attention from state regulators, and a reverse osmosis system at the kitchen tap is one of the few treatment methods that meaningfully reduces nitrates at the point of use.

## Our Water Softener and Filtration Installation Process in Lamont

Every installation starts with a water test, not a sales pitch. We pull a sample from your tap, measure hardness in grains per gallon, check for iron, test pH, and note any odor compounds. That data determines what equipment actually makes sense for your home, because a softener sized for a two-bedroom house on a well-fed lot will underperform in a larger home with high daily water use.

For whole-house softeners, we select the bypass valve location, set the brine tank in a utility area with adequate drainage, and program the regeneration cycle to match your household's actual consumption rather than a factory default. For whole-house carbon filtration, we account for the specific compounds present in your supply. Reverse osmosis systems at the kitchen sink get a dedicated faucet, a storage tank sized for your household, and a connection to the refrigerator line if the layout allows.

We pull the appropriate permits through Kern County when the scope of work requires it, and we test flow rate and pressure before and after installation to confirm the system isn't creating a pressure drop that would affect the rest of the house.

## Local Note: Older Homes and Whole-House Filtration Sizing

One thing that comes up repeatedly in Lamont's older residential neighborhoods is undersized service lines. Homes built in the 1950s and 1960s often have three-quarter-inch or even half-inch main supply lines running to the meter. Adding a whole-house filtration system with a standard one-inch port to a half-inch line creates a pressure restriction that can make the filtration feel like it's hurting performance rather than helping it. When we assess a Lamont home for whole-house treatment, we check the service line diameter first. Sometimes the right answer is a point-of-entry system matched to the existing line size, with a high-capacity reverse osmosis unit at the kitchen for drinking water, rather than forcing a larger system onto plumbing that can't support it.
