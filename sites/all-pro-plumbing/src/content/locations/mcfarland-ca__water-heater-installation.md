## Why McFarland Homes See Water Heater Problems Earlier

The water delivered to McFarland properties carries a high mineral load, a characteristic shared across much of Kern County's agricultural corridor. Calcium and magnesium compounds settle at the bottom of a tank over time, forming a layer of sediment that forces the burner to work harder, raises your gas bill, and creates that popping or rumbling noise you might hear during a heating cycle. In a climate where the unit runs hard to keep up with demand during scorching Central Valley summers, that wear compounds quickly.

Older homes in McFarland, particularly those built during the mid-20th century agricultural expansion of the area, often have water heater closets or utility spaces sized for the standard 40-gallon tanks of that era. Upgrading to a 50-gallon unit or making the switch to a tankless system sometimes requires modifying the venting configuration or gas line sizing, work that needs to be done to current California code before the installation is complete.

## Tankless vs. Tank: What Makes Sense in McFarland

The tankless vs. tank decision comes down to a few practical factors that are worth thinking through before you commit.

A **tankless water heater** heats water on demand, which eliminates standby heat loss and can reduce energy costs meaningfully in a home where hot water use is spread across the day. In McFarland's hard-water conditions, tankless units do require periodic descaling, typically once a year, to keep the heat exchanger clear. Skipping that maintenance shortens the unit's life and reduces flow rate.

A **tank water heater**, particularly a 50-gallon model, remains the more straightforward and lower upfront-cost option for households with simultaneous high-demand needs, such as multiple bathrooms running at the same time. Modern high-efficiency tank units have improved significantly and hold up well when an inline sediment filter or water softener is part of the setup.

All Pro walks through both options with you before any work begins so the choice fits your household size, budget, and existing plumbing configuration.
