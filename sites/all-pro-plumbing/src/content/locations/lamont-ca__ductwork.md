Lamont sits in the southern San Joaquin Valley where summer temperatures routinely push past 100°F and the surrounding agricultural fields push fine particulate dust into the air year-round. That combination is hard on duct systems: heat causes flex duct to expand and contract until joints loosen, and airborne soil particles work their way into any gap, coating coil surfaces and lining duct walls. If your home's air conditioning is running constantly but certain rooms stay stuffy, or if you notice a gritty film on supply registers, the ductwork is likely the culprit rather than the equipment itself.

## Why Lamont Properties See Duct Problems

The housing stock in and around Lamont skews toward single-story ranch-style construction built during the mid-twentieth century agricultural boom, with attic-run duct systems that were sized for window units or smaller central systems than what most households run today. When a larger tonnage system is added without resizing the duct trunk lines, static pressure climbs, flex duct connections strain, and return air becomes restricted. The result is uneven cooling, higher utility bills, and compressor wear from restricted airflow.

Kern County's clay-heavy soils also affect crawl-space duct runs. During dry stretches the soil shrinks and shifts; after irrigation or the occasional winter rain it swells. Duct straps and hangers anchored to floor joists in older crawl spaces can pull loose over repeated cycles, leaving sections of duct sagging or kinked. A kinked flex duct loses a significant portion of its rated airflow capacity even when the duct itself is intact.

## Our Ductwork Repair and Installation Process in Lamont

Every job starts with a pressure test. Before any duct replacement or duct sealing work begins, the system is pressurized with a calibrated blower to measure how much conditioned air is escaping before it reaches a register. That number tells us whether targeted air duct repair will solve the problem or whether a full duct replacement makes more economic sense for the home.

For repair work, mastic sealant and metal-backed tape are applied at joints, boots, and panned returns. Aeroseal duct sealing is available for systems where access is limited and leaks are distributed throughout the duct network rather than concentrated at a few obvious points. For full ductwork installation, we size the new system to the actual load of the home, not to the equipment's nominal tonnage, and we use insulated flex duct rated for the attic temperatures common in Lamont's summers.

After the work is complete, a second pressure test confirms the leakage rate has dropped to an acceptable level. You get a before-and-after comparison in plain numbers.

## Local Note

Attic temperatures in Lamont during July and August regularly exceed 150°F by early afternoon. Standard flex duct is rated for those temperatures, but the radiant heat accelerates the breakdown of the duct jacket and inner liner over time, particularly on runs that rest directly on attic insulation rather than being properly suspended. When we install or replace ductwork in Lamont attics, we pay close attention to duct support spacing and insulation contact, because a duct that sags into blown-in insulation traps heat and degrades faster than one that is properly hung. It is a detail that does not show up on a basic inspection but makes a measurable difference in how long the new duct system holds up in this climate.

## Leaky Duct Testing and What It Means for Your Energy Bill

Leaky duct testing is not just a diagnostic step; in Kern County's climate it is one of the highest-return investments a homeowner can make. When conditioned air leaks into an attic that is 150°F, that air is effectively lost. The system runs longer cycles to compensate, and the compressor accumulates hours faster than it should. Duct sealing and duct replacement work that reduces leakage to current California Title 24 standards can meaningfully reduce cooling runtime. If your home was built before those standards were in place, and most Lamont homes were, there is a reasonable chance the duct system has never been tested for leakage at all.
