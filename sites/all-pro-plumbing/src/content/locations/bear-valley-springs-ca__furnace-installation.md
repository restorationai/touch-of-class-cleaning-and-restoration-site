## Why Bear Valley Springs Homes Have Unique Furnace Demands

The Tehachapi range creates a climate that sits in its own category: colder and drier than the San Joaquin Valley floor, with temperature swings that stress HVAC equipment more than the mild coastal conditions most California furnaces are rated against. Homes in Bear Valley Springs also tend to be on larger rural lots with propane rather than natural gas service, which changes the equipment conversation considerably. A furnace sized and configured for a valley home may underperform here, running longer cycles and wearing components faster because it was never matched to the altitude and fuel type.

Older homes in the community often have ductwork that was designed around lower-efficiency systems. When a high-efficiency furnace is installed without addressing duct sizing and sealing, the efficiency gains on paper disappear in practice. Getting the load calculation right for a Bear Valley Springs property means accounting for the elevation, the typical construction era of the home, and whether the structure has adequate insulation to hold heat overnight when temperatures drop sharply.

## Local Note

Propane-fueled homes in Bear Valley Springs require a furnace configured specifically for LP combustion, not a natural gas unit with a field conversion kit added as an afterthought. The altitude also affects combustion air requirements in ways that matter at installation time. A unit installed at sea-level specifications and then operated at 4,000 feet can run rich, produce more carbon monoxide, and cycle inefficiently. We verify combustion performance with instruments during commissioning rather than assuming the factory settings are adequate for the elevation. It is a step that adds time to the job and is easy to skip, but at this altitude it is not optional.
