## Why Lamont Properties See Gas Line Problems

Lamont sits on alluvial soils deposited over centuries by runoff from the Tehachapi foothills to the south. These soils drain well in summer but absorb significant moisture during the wet season, and the resulting expansion and contraction cycles apply lateral stress to underground gas supply lines. Older homes in the area, many built during the mid-20th century agricultural boom, often have original black iron pipe that has never been replaced. That pipe corrodes from the outside in, and the first sign of trouble is sometimes nothing more than a faint odor near a foundation penetration or a meter connection.

Kern County also sits in one of California's highest seismic-risk zones, and the valley floor experiences ground shaking regularly. Flexible gas connectors behind stoves and dryers degrade over time, and older rigid connections at appliance hookups are especially vulnerable. If your home does not have an earthquake-actuated automatic gas shutoff valve, that is a gap worth closing before the next significant shake.

## Our Gas Line Installation and Leak Repair Process in Lamont

When a gas leak call comes in, the first priority is always safety: confirm the meter is shut off, ventilate the structure, and get everyone outside. Once on site, we use combustible gas detectors to pinpoint the source rather than guessing. Leaks behind walls or under slabs show up on the detector before any digging or cutting starts, which keeps the repair targeted.

For new gas line installation, whether you're running a dedicated line for a range, an outdoor grill, or a whole-house generator, the work follows a defined sequence: pressure-test the existing supply, size the new branch correctly for BTU load, route the pipe to avoid soil contact where corrosion is a risk, and schedule the inspection with Kern County Building and Safety before covering anything. We pull the required permits so the work is on record, which matters when you sell the home or file an insurance claim.

For leak repairs on older iron pipe, we assess whether a section repair or a full repipe makes more sense. A single corroded joint on an otherwise sound line is a different conversation than a system where multiple fittings are showing the same age-related degradation.

## Equipment and Methods We Use for Gas Lines

Electronic combustible gas detectors let us trace a leak to its source without opening walls unnecessarily. For underground lines, we use pressure-decay testing: the line is isolated, pressurized, and monitored over time. A drop in pressure confirms a leak; a stable reading confirms integrity. This method works on both new installations before backfill and on existing buried lines when a homeowner suspects a problem but can't locate it visually.

For new installations, we use materials appropriate to the application: CSST (corrugated stainless steel tubing) for interior runs where flexibility is an advantage, and coated steel or approved polyethylene for underground sections where soil contact is unavoidable. Every installation is pressure-tested before the gas is turned back on.
