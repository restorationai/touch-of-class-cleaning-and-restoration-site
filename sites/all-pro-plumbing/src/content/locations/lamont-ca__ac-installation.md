## Why Lamont's Climate Makes Equipment Selection Critical

The San Joaquin Valley's extreme summer heat is well documented, but what's easy to underestimate is how long the cooling season actually runs here. Lamont residents can see temperatures climb into the 90s as early as May and stay there well into October. That extended runtime puts far more annual hours on a compressor than the same unit would log in a coastal city.

That operating reality has two direct consequences for anyone buying a new system. First, efficiency ratings matter more here than almost anywhere in California. A high SEER2 air conditioner that costs more upfront will pay back the difference faster in Lamont than it would in Fresno or Sacramento because the equipment simply runs more hours per year. Second, proper load calculation is non-negotiable. An oversized unit short-cycles, never adequately dehumidifies the air, and wears out faster. An undersized unit runs continuously and still can't keep up on a 108-degree afternoon. Getting the tonnage right for your specific square footage, ceiling height, insulation quality, and window exposure is the first thing we do before any equipment is ordered.

## Our AC Installation and Replacement Process in Lamont

Every installation starts with a Manual J load calculation, the industry-standard method for determining the correct system size for your home or building. We measure the structure, assess insulation, and account for the intense solar gain that south- and west-facing walls absorb during long San Joaquin Valley afternoons.

From there, the process moves through equipment selection, permit coordination with Kern County, and scheduling the installation itself. Permit requirements for HVAC work in unincorporated Kern County areas like Lamont are real, and skipping that step creates problems at resale and can void manufacturer warranties. We handle permit submission as part of the job so that detail doesn't fall on you.

On installation day, the crew removes and properly disposes of the old equipment, sets the new condenser on a level pad, connects or replaces refrigerant lines, verifies electrical connections at the disconnect and panel, and commissions the system with a full startup check before leaving. If ductwork is undersized or leaking, we identify that during the assessment and discuss repair or replacement options before work begins, not after.
