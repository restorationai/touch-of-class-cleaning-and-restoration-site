## Equipment and Methods for Mountain-Climate Installations

For Keene homes, we most often recommend sealed-combustion, two-stage or modulating gas furnaces with AFUE ratings in the high-efficiency range. Sealed combustion draws outside air directly into the burner rather than pulling from the living space, which eliminates the backdrafting risk that open-combustion units can face in tightly sealed or high-wind environments. Two-stage operation lets the furnace run at a lower output on milder cold days, which reduces temperature swings and extends equipment life in a climate where the furnace may run for months at a stretch.

We also pay close attention to thermostat placement. In homes where the living area and bedrooms are on different levels or separated by the home's original layout, a single thermostat in the wrong location can leave parts of the house consistently under- or over-heated.

## Local Note

Homes along the Keene area's older ranch-style corridors were frequently built with gravity-fed or early forced-air systems that used oversized sheet-metal trunk lines running through unconditioned crawl spaces. When we replace the furnace in one of these homes, we often find that the original duct system was sized for a unit with much lower static pressure than a modern high-efficiency furnace produces. Connecting a new furnace to that old duct layout without modification can cause the heat exchanger to overheat and trip the high-limit switch repeatedly. We check static pressure before finalizing equipment selection, not after the new unit is already on the floor.
