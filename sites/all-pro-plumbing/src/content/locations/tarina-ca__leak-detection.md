## Why Tarina Properties See Hidden Leak Problems

The San Joaquin Valley's expansive clay soils are a known aggressor on underground plumbing. As the ground heats and dries through a long Kern County summer, it pulls away from pipe joints; when winter rains return, it swells back. That cycle stresses copper and galvanized lines at their fittings, and older homes in the Tarina area that were built with galvanized supply lines are especially vulnerable. Galvanized pipe corrodes from the inside out, narrowing over decades until pinhole leaks form along the walls of the pipe rather than at a visible joint. You may never see a drop of water on the surface.

Slab foundations, which are common throughout this part of the valley, create a second layer of difficulty. A pressurized supply line running beneath a concrete slab can lose water steadily into the subgrade without producing any visible moisture at the surface for weeks. The first sign is often warm flooring above a hot-water line, a faint sulfur or mineral smell near a baseboard, or a water meter that keeps spinning after every fixture in the house is shut off.

## Our Leak Detection Process in Tarina

Finding a leak without tearing up flooring or excavating a yard requires the right combination of methods, matched to what the structure is telling us.

**Meter and pressure testing** comes first. Isolating the supply side and watching for pressure drop over a timed interval tells us whether the loss is on the pressurized line or somewhere downstream. If the meter dial moves with all fixtures off, there is an active leak on the supply side.

**Electronic listening equipment** picks up the acoustic signature of water escaping under pressure. Pressurized water moving through a breach in a pipe produces a distinct frequency that travels through soil and concrete. A trained technician can walk a grid pattern across a slab and identify the loudest point, narrowing the excavation target from a full slab to a precise area.

**Thermal imaging** is useful when a hot-water line is involved. Infrared cameras read temperature differentials through flooring and wall surfaces, flagging areas where warm water is migrating into the structure.

**Tracer gas detection** is available for situations where acoustic methods are inconclusive, particularly on deep underground lines or where ambient noise makes listening difficult. A non-toxic gas is introduced into the line; a sensor at the surface detects where it escapes.

The goal throughout is to locate the breach with enough precision that the repair is surgical, not exploratory.

## Local Note

One pattern worth knowing in the Tarina area: properties on agricultural-adjacent parcels sometimes have a secondary irrigation or domestic well line running alongside the municipal supply, and the two systems can share a meter pit or be plumbed closer together than a homeowner realizes. When a leak is detected near the property perimeter, it's worth confirming which line is losing pressure before any repair work begins. Confusing an irrigation lateral for a domestic supply line is an easy mistake that adds time and cost to a job. Our technicians ask about secondary water sources as part of the initial site assessment.
