## Why Valley Acres Homes See Furnace Problems

The Central Valley's climate is deceptively hard on HVAC equipment. Summer heat pushes air conditioners to their limits, but the same systems that run constantly in July sit mostly idle until November. That long off-season means ignitors corrode, gas valves stiffen, and blower wheels collect a season's worth of dust before the furnace is asked to perform again. When you finally flip the thermostat to heat and nothing happens, or the unit blows cold air instead of warm, it is often that accumulated neglect catching up all at once.

Valley Acres also has a significant share of homes built during the post-war tract-housing boom, many of which still run on original duct layouts that were never sized for modern forced-air equipment. Undersized return ducts restrict airflow, causing the heat exchanger to overheat and the high-limit switch to trip, which shuts the furnace off as a safety measure. The symptom looks like a broken furnace; the root cause is airflow, and misdiagnosing it leads to parts replacements that do not fix anything.

## Our Furnace Repair Process in Valley Acres

Every call starts with a systematic diagnostic rather than a parts swap. A technician will check the thermostat signal, verify gas pressure at the manifold, inspect the ignitor and flame sensor, test the inducer motor, and confirm that the heat exchanger is not cracked before touching anything else. A cracked heat exchanger is a carbon monoxide risk, and it changes the repair path entirely.

Once the fault is confirmed, we explain what failed, why it failed, and what the repair involves before any work begins. If the part is on the truck, the repair typically happens the same visit. If it requires a sourced component, we give you an honest timeline and, if the overnight temperature is a concern, talk through interim options.

After the repair, we run the furnace through a full heating cycle, verify temperature rise across the heat exchanger, and check that the blower is moving enough air to keep the system from tripping on high limit again. The goal is a furnace that runs reliably through the rest of the heating season, not one that holds together until the next cold snap.

## Local Note

One pattern that comes up repeatedly in the Valley Acres area involves older furnaces installed in homes with low-clearance utility closets or tight garage alcoves. These installations sometimes do not meet current California combustion-air requirements, which specify minimum free-air volume around the appliance. When a furnace is starved for combustion air, it runs rich, produces more carbon monoxide, and trips the rollout switch as a safety response. The homeowner sees a furnace that keeps shutting off; the actual issue is a ventilation deficiency that has been there since the original install. Correcting it is straightforward, but only if the technician knows to look for it, and it is something we check on every Valley Acres service call involving repeated nuisance shutdowns.
