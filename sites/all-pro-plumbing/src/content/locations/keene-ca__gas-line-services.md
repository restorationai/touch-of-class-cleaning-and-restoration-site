## Why Keene Properties See Gas Line Issues

Keene's position along the Tehachapi Pass places it directly within a seismically active zone. Small fault-related ground shifts, even ones that don't register as noticeable earthquakes, can stress buried gas line connections over time. Fittings that were properly installed years ago can develop micro-leaks as the soil around them settles and shifts. The canyon's temperature swings compound the problem: summer days push well above 90°F while winter nights can drop near freezing, and that thermal cycling causes metal pipe and fittings to expand and contract repeatedly across the seasons.

Many of the residential properties in this area were built during mid-century rural development, when gas line materials and installation standards were considerably different from what California code requires today. Black iron pipe, once standard, corrodes from the inside out in conditions where moisture infiltrates the line, and older flex connectors behind ranges and dryers have a finite service life that many homeowners don't track. If your home still has its original gas infrastructure and hasn't been inspected since a major appliance was last replaced, that's worth knowing before a problem announces itself through smell or a detector alarm.
