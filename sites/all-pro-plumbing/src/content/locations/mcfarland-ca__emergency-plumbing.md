## Why McFarland Properties See Emergency Plumbing Issues

The San Joaquin Valley's agricultural water infrastructure and the region's clay-heavy soils create conditions that push residential plumbing harder than most homeowners realize. Expansive clay soil shifts seasonally as it wets and dries, and those ground movements place lateral stress on buried supply and drain lines. Older homes in McFarland built during the mid-twentieth century agricultural boom often have galvanized steel supply lines that have been narrowing with mineral buildup for decades. When a galvanized pipe finally fails, it tends to fail completely rather than slowly, turning a gradual pressure drop into a sudden flood.

The valley's hard water also accelerates scale buildup inside water heaters and at shutoff valves. A valve that has not been exercised in years may not close fully when you need it most, which is exactly the wrong moment to discover that. High summer temperatures add a separate layer of stress: thermal expansion in copper runs causes fittings to work loose over many heat cycles, and outdoor hose bibs left under direct sun can develop pinhole leaks that go unnoticed until water is wicking into a wall cavity.
