## Why Lamont Homes See Furnace Failures When They Do

The San Joaquin Valley's climate creates a specific wear pattern on residential furnaces. Because heating season in Lamont is compressed, mostly running from late November through February, homeowners often discover a failed heat exchanger or a cracked igniter on the first genuinely cold night of the year, after the unit has sat idle for eight or nine months. Dust accumulation during those dormant months is heavier here than in coastal climates because valley air carries fine agricultural particulate year-round. That dust loads filters, restricts airflow, and forces heat exchangers to run hotter than they were rated for, accelerating fatigue.

Lamont's housing stock also matters. A significant share of the community's single-family homes were built in the postwar decades as the agricultural workforce expanded, and many of those structures were fitted with original ductwork that was never designed around modern high-efficiency equipment. Installing a 96 AFUE modulating furnace into a duct system sized for a 1970s unit can create static pressure problems that reduce comfort and shorten equipment life if the duct system is not evaluated and corrected as part of the project.

## Our Furnace Installation and Replacement Process in Lamont

Every replacement starts with a load calculation, not a guess based on what was there before. Kern County homes in communities like Lamont often have inadequate insulation in attic spaces, which means the outgoing furnace was likely oversized to compensate, and simply dropping in the same BTU rating repeats the same mistake. We size the new unit to the actual heat loss of the structure.

From there, the process moves through these steps:

- **Duct inspection and static pressure test** before any equipment is ordered, so surprises surface before installation day.
- **Gas line verification**, confirming that existing supply line diameter and pressure are compatible with the new unit's input rating. Older Lamont properties sometimes have undersized gas branch lines that worked fine with a pilot-light furnace but cannot deliver adequate pressure to a modern two-stage burner.
- **Equipment selection**, matched to the home's square footage, ceiling height, insulation level, and whether the structure has a sealed or vented crawlspace.
- **Installation and commissioning**, including combustion analysis and airflow balancing before we leave the property.
- **Permit coordination** with Kern County Building and Safety, which requires a mechanical permit for furnace replacement. We handle the permit application so you are not navigating that process yourself.

## Equipment and Efficiency Options for This Climate

For most Lamont homes, a single-stage or two-stage gas furnace in the 80 to 96 AFUE range is the practical choice. The valley's mild shoulder seasons mean a variable-capacity modulating furnace delivers real comfort benefits, running at lower capacity on cool nights rather than short-cycling on and off. High-efficiency 96 AFUE units also vent through PVC pipe rather than a metal flue, which matters in older homes where the existing chimney flue is shared with a water heater and cannot easily be relined for a new appliance.
