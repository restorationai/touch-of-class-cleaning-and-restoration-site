## Why Arvin Properties See Ductwork Problems

The San Joaquin Valley's heat cycle is relentless. Ducts in unconditioned attic spaces in Arvin can reach temperatures well above 140°F on a July afternoon, then cool sharply overnight. That daily expansion and contraction stresses flex duct connections, loosens mastic seals, and eventually pulls duct sections apart at the joints. Homes built during Arvin's growth periods often have original duct layouts that were sized for smaller, less efficient HVAC equipment, meaning the ductwork itself may be undersized for a modern system replacement even if it looks intact.

Agricultural dust is a factor most inland valley contractors understand but rarely discuss openly. Fine particulate from surrounding farmland infiltrates attic spaces through soffit vents and gaps in rooflines, accumulating inside leaky ducts and reducing airflow. If your supply registers are delivering noticeably less air than they used to, or if rooms on the far end of the duct run feel significantly warmer than rooms near the air handler, a leaky duct system is a likely cause worth testing before replacing the equipment.

## Our Ductwork Repair and Installation Process in Arvin

Every ductwork job starts with a diagnostic, not an assumption. We use pressure testing to measure how much conditioned air your system is actually losing before it reaches your living space. That number tells us whether targeted duct sealing will solve the problem or whether duct replacement is the more cost-effective path.

For duct sealing, we apply aeroseal or mastic sealant at joints and connections, then retest to confirm the leakage rate has dropped to an acceptable level. For partial or full duct replacement, we size the new duct runs to match your current equipment's airflow requirements, not the original builder-grade specs. In Arvin's attic environments, we use duct board or insulated flex duct rated for the temperature extremes common here, and we secure connections with metal draw bands and mastic rather than relying on tape alone, which degrades quickly in high-heat attic spaces.

After installation, we do a final airflow check at each register to confirm balanced delivery across the system.

## Local Note

One thing that catches Arvin homeowners off guard: attic duct systems in homes built on slab foundations here often have very little clearance between the duct runs and the roof decking. In extreme summer heat, ducts running close to an uninsulated roof deck are exposed to radiant heat that standard duct insulation ratings do not fully account for. When we replace or reroute ducts in these attic configurations, we add supplemental insulation wrap and, where possible, reposition runs away from the hottest zones near the ridge. It is a small adjustment that makes a measurable difference in system efficiency when outdoor temps are at their worst.
