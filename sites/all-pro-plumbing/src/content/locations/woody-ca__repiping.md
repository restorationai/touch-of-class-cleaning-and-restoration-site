Woody sits in the southern San Joaquin Valley, where summer heat regularly pushes well past 100°F and the region's agricultural water history has left many older properties tied to supply lines that were never built to last this long. If your home or commercial building in Woody is showing rust-colored water, a noticeable drop in pressure at the tap, or pinhole leaks that keep reappearing in the same wall, the pipe material itself is likely the problem, and patching it one joint at a time only delays the inevitable.

## Why Woody Properties See Repiping Issues

The San Joaquin Valley's water supply runs through a complex mix of surface water from the State Water Project and local groundwater, and the mineral content shifts depending on the season and source. That variability is hard on older metal pipe. Galvanized steel, which was the standard in homes built through the 1960s and into the 1970s, corrodes from the inside out. The rust builds up as scale, narrows the interior diameter, and eventually flakes into the water stream. By the time a homeowner notices orange-tinted water at the kitchen faucet, the pipe walls are often already compromised across a significant portion of the system.

Polybutylene pipe, installed widely during the 1980s and into the early 1990s, presents a different problem. It reacts poorly to chlorine and chloramine disinfectants used in treated municipal water, becoming brittle and prone to sudden failure at fittings. Homes built in Woody during that era may still be running on polybutylene if no one has looked at the supply lines since original construction.

The valley's temperature swings compound the issue. Pipes in unconditioned crawl spaces and attics cycle through extreme heat in summer and occasional hard freezes in winter, accelerating the fatigue on any pipe material that is already aging.

## Our Whole-House Repiping Process in Woody

A full repipe is a multi-day project, and the sequence matters. The work begins with a thorough inspection of the existing system, mapping where supply lines run through walls, under slabs, or through crawl spaces, and identifying which materials are in place. In Woody's older ranch-style homes, supply lines frequently run through exterior walls that face west, where afternoon heat is most intense, and that routing affects how we plan the new installation.

Once the scope is confirmed, the water supply is shut off and the old pipe is removed or abandoned in place depending on its location and the wall construction. New copper or PEX tubing is run to every fixture, including hose bibs, the water heater, and any irrigation connections tied to the domestic supply. PEX is particularly well-suited to the valley's temperature extremes because it expands rather than cracks under stress, and its flexibility allows it to be routed through tight spaces without as many fittings, which are typically the first points of failure in any system.

After the new lines are pressure-tested and confirmed leak-free, drywall patches are completed and the system is flushed before water is restored. Kern County requires a permit for whole-house repiping, and the inspection is scheduled through the county building department. We pull the permit and coordinate the inspection so you are not managing that process yourself.

One detail that comes up repeatedly in the Woody area: homes on agricultural parcels or at the edge of town sometimes have a secondary water source, either a private well or an older irrigation connection, that was informally tied into the domestic supply at some point in the past.
