## Why Tarina Properties See Slab Leak Issues

The Central Valley's seasonal pattern is a significant factor. Summers here are long and dry enough to shrink the clay subsoil, and the winter rains that follow cause it to swell back. Homes built on expansive clay experience that cycle year after year, and the slab moves with it in small but cumulative ways. Copper lines embedded in or beneath the slab flex at fittings and elbows until the metal fatigues. Older homes built before copper was replaced with more flexible materials are especially susceptible, but even newer construction is not immune when the soil conditions are this active.

Kern County's hard water compounds the problem. High mineral content accelerates pitting corrosion on the inside of copper pipe, thinning the wall from within while soil pressure works from outside. The two processes together shorten the lifespan of under-slab plumbing considerably compared to regions with softer water supplies.

## Our Slab Leak Repair Process in Tarina

Locating the leak precisely before any concrete is touched is the step that separates a clean repair from an expensive guessing game. We use electronic listening equipment and thermal imaging to pinpoint the break, which means the opening in the slab is kept as small as possible. Once confirmed, there are two primary repair paths.

For an isolated failure in an otherwise sound line, a direct-access repair exposes only the damaged section, replaces it, and patches the concrete. For lines that have corroded along their length or have failed more than once, a full reroute is the more durable solution. A reroute abandons the compromised under-slab pipe entirely and runs a new line through the walls or attic, eliminating future access through the foundation. We walk through both options with you before any work begins so you understand the trade-offs in cost, disruption, and long-term reliability.

## Tarina Insurance and HOA Coordination

Homeowners insurance often covers the cost of locating and accessing a slab leak, even when the pipe repair itself is not included in the policy. ## Local Note

Homes built on post-tension slabs, a construction method that became common in the Central Valley during the 1980s and 1990s, require extra care during any concrete cutting. Post-tension cables run through the slab under tension, and cutting into one without knowing its location can cause structural damage that goes well beyond the plumbing repair. Before any saw work on a Tarina home from that era, we confirm the slab type and, when necessary, use ground-penetrating radar to map cable positions. It is a step that adds a short amount of time to the job but protects the integrity of your foundation.
