Tarina sits in the southern San Joaquin Valley, where summer temperatures regularly push past 100°F and the air conditioning runs hard from May through October. That kind of sustained thermal load puts real stress on duct systems, and in a region where many homes were built during the tract-housing booms of the 1970s and 1980s, the original flex duct and mastic seals are often decades past their useful life. If your home feels uneven room to room, your utility bills have crept up without explanation, or your system runs constantly without cooling the house down, the ductwork is a likely culprit.

## Why Tarina Properties See Ductwork Problems

The Central Valley's climate is the defining factor here. Attic temperatures in Tarina can exceed 150°F on a hot July afternoon, and flex duct that bends, sags, or was never properly supported degrades faster in that heat than it would in a milder coastal climate. Older homes in the area frequently have duct runs that were sized for smaller, less efficient systems and then patched or extended as HVAC equipment was replaced over the years. The result is a patchwork of materials, connection types, and duct diameters that leak conditioned air into attic spaces rather than delivering it to the rooms where you need it.

Slab-on-grade construction, common throughout this part of Kern County, means most duct systems run through attics rather than crawl spaces or basements. Attic duct runs are exposed to the worst of the summer heat, which accelerates liner deterioration and causes the foil facing on older insulated flex to crack and separate. When that happens, the duct is losing both air and thermal efficiency at the same time.

## Our Ductwork Repair and Installation Process in Tarina

Before any repair or replacement work begins, the system gets tested. Leaky duct testing uses a calibrated blower to pressurize the duct system and measure how much air escapes before it reaches the registers. That number tells us whether targeted duct sealing will solve the problem or whether a section, or the whole system, needs to be replaced. It also gives you a before-and-after comparison so you can see the actual improvement.

For duct sealing, we use mastic sealant at joints and connections rather than relying on duct tape, which dries out and fails in high-attic-temperature environments. For duct replacement, we size the new runs to match the actual output of your current equipment, not the original system specs from 30 years ago. In Tarina's attics, proper support spacing and insulation wrap matter as much as the duct material itself, so installation includes securing flex runs at the correct intervals and verifying that R-value meets current California Title 24 requirements for this climate zone.

## Equipment and Methods We Use for Ductwork

Leaky duct testing requires a duct blaster or similar calibrated pressure-measurement device, not a visual inspection or a hand held near a register. We use that data to make decisions, not guesses. For sealing, aeroseal duct sealing is an option for systems where access to every joint isn't practical, injecting sealant particles into the pressurized duct system so they adhere to leak points from the inside. For full duct replacement, we work with insulated flex duct and rigid sheet metal depending on the run geometry, and we pull the appropriate permits through Kern County when the scope of work requires it.
