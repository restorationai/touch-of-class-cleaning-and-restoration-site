## Why Woody Properties See Furnace Problems

The San Joaquin Valley's climate creates a specific stress pattern for heating equipment. Summers are long and brutal, which means furnaces sit completely idle for five or six months. When temperatures finally dip in late October or November, a unit that has been dormant since March gets called into full-duty service overnight. Dust and debris accumulate in heat exchangers and burner assemblies during that long rest, and the first hard run of the season is when cracked heat exchangers, failed igniters, and clogged flame sensors reveal themselves.

Woody is also surrounded by active agricultural operations, and the fine particulate matter that drifts through the valley during harvest and tillage seasons loads up furnace filters faster than in urban areas. A filter that should last 90 days in a suburban tract home may be fully blocked in 30 to 45 days here. A choked filter starves the blower of return air, causes the heat exchanger to overheat, and trips the high-limit switch, which is one of the most common reasons a furnace in this area blows cold air or shuts off mid-cycle.
