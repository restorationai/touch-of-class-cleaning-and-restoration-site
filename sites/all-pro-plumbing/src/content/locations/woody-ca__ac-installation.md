## Why Woody's Climate Makes AC Sizing So Critical

The San Joaquin Valley's heat is dry and relentless, but that description undersells what it does to residential cooling loads. Woody's agricultural surroundings mean daytime temperatures spike fast and stay elevated well into the evening, and homes that were built or converted without a full Manual J load calculation are often running equipment that's either too small to keep up or too large to dehumidify effectively. An oversized unit short-cycles: it cools the thermostat quickly, shuts off, and never runs long enough to pull moisture out of the indoor air. The result is a clammy, uncomfortable home even when the temperature reads acceptable.

Older ranch-style homes common in this part of Tulare County frequently have attic spaces with minimal insulation depth, which dramatically increases the cooling load on any new system. Before recommending equipment, a proper installation starts with measuring the home's envelope, not just swapping out the old tonnage for the same number on a new box.

## Our AC Installation and Replacement Process in Woody

Every installation starts with a site assessment: duct condition, attic insulation, existing electrical capacity, and the orientation of the home relative to afternoon sun exposure. In Woody's climate, west-facing rooms with single-pane windows can add meaningfully to peak load calculations, and that gets accounted for before any equipment is specified.

Once the right system is selected, the process moves through:

- **Equipment selection**: We match SEER2-rated systems to your home's actual load. Higher-efficiency units carry a larger upfront cost but pay back quickly when a cooling season runs five or six months.
- **Electrical verification**: New high-efficiency systems often require a dedicated circuit or a panel upgrade. We confirm capacity before installation day so there are no surprises.
- **Duct inspection and sealing**: In Valley homes, leaky duct connections in unconditioned attics are one of the most common reasons a new system underperforms. Sealing those connections before commissioning the new unit is not optional.
- **Refrigerant line and drain line installation**: Properly sloped condensate lines matter in a climate where the system runs continuously for months. A drain line that pools water creates mold risk inside the air handler.
- **System commissioning**: We verify airflow, refrigerant charge, and thermostat calibration before leaving the site.

## Equipment and Efficiency: What to Know Before You Buy

California's Title 24 energy code sets minimum efficiency standards for residential HVAC equipment, and those requirements apply to replacements as well as new installations. Systems sold and installed in California must meet current SEER2 minimums, which are higher than federal baselines. For Woody homeowners, this is actually good news: the efficiency floor is higher, which means even the entry-level replacement option performs meaningfully better than equipment from ten or fifteen years ago.

For homes with existing ductwork in good condition, a standard split system replacement is usually the most cost-effective path. For homes without ducts, or with duct systems in poor shape, ductless mini-split systems are worth a serious look. They avoid the duct loss problem entirely and allow zone control, which is useful in homes where one wing faces west and heats up faster than the rest of the house.

## Local Note

Homes in the Woody area that were originally built as farm labor housing or converted agricultural structures sometimes have non-standard ceiling heights and minimal interior wall insulation, which changes how supply air needs to be distributed. A conventional duct layout designed for a tract home won't move air correctly in a long, low structure with open floor plans. When we encounter these layouts, we adjust register placement and duct sizing rather than forcing a standard template onto a building that doesn't fit it. It's a detail that doesn't show up in a quote from someone who hasn't looked at the actual structure.
