## Why McFarland Homes See Furnace Problems When the Cold Hits

The San Joaquin Valley's agricultural economy shaped how McFarland was built. A significant portion of the housing stock here consists of modest tract homes and older ranch-style construction, many of which were fitted with builder-grade forced-air furnaces that were never upgraded. These units often share a few common failure points: cracked heat exchangers from years of thermal cycling, dirty flame sensors coated with the fine particulate that drifts in from surrounding farmland, and clogged filters that restrict airflow until the high-limit switch shuts the system down as a safety measure.

Tule fog is another factor unique to this stretch of the valley. When dense fog settles in for days at a time, outdoor temperatures stay suppressed and furnaces run longer and harder than they would in a drier winter climate. That extended run time accelerates wear on igniters, inducer motors, and blower belts in ways that a homeowner in a coastal city rarely encounters.

## Our Furnace Repair Process in McFarland

When a technician arrives at your McFarland home, the first step is a full system diagnostic rather than a quick guess at the most obvious part. ## Local Note: Valley Dust and Flame Sensors

One thing that comes up repeatedly in McFarland and the surrounding agricultural communities is flame sensor failure caused by particulate buildup rather than sensor age. The fine dust that moves through the valley during harvest season and dry windy periods coats everything, including the flame sensor rod inside the burner compartment. A sensor that reads as failed will often clean up and function correctly once it is lightly abraded with fine steel wool, buying a homeowner another season before replacement is actually necessary. A technician who jumps straight to a replacement part without checking the sensor condition first may be solving the wrong problem. Knowing that pattern is part of working in this part of Kern County.

## What to Do Before the Technician Arrives

A few quick checks can help the repair go faster. Confirm that the furnace switch on the unit itself is in the ON position and that the circuit breaker for the air handler has not tripped. Check the filter: a filter so clogged it has collapsed can cause a no-heat call that resolves the moment airflow is restored. If the system is attempting to ignite but shutting off after a few seconds, do not keep cycling the thermostat repeatedly. Repeated failed ignition attempts can flood the heat exchanger with unburned gas, and most modern furnaces will lock out after three attempts anyway, requiring a manual reset.

If you smell gas at any point, leave the house, do not operate any switches, and call SoCalGas or 911 before calling for furnace service.
