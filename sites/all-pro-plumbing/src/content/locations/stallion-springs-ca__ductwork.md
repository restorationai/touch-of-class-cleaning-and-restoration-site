## Why Stallion Springs Properties See Ductwork Problems

The elevation and climate here create a combination that accelerates duct wear faster than most Central Valley homeowners expect. Daytime heat causes flexible duct liner to expand; overnight cold contracts it. Over several seasons, that cycling loosens the mastic or tape at every connection point. Homes built in the 1980s and 1990s, which make up a large share of the residential stock in Stallion Springs, were often fitted with flex duct runs that are now approaching the end of their useful life. Older installations sometimes used duct tape rather than UL-listed mastic sealant, and that tape dries out and peels in arid mountain air far faster than manufacturers ever intended.

Attic spaces in this area also see significant temperature extremes. An unconditioned attic in Stallion Springs can reach temperatures that degrade duct insulation wrap, leaving bare metal or bare flex liner exposed to the heat load. When duct insulation fails, the system works harder, energy bills climb, and rooms farthest from the air handler never reach the set temperature.

## Our Ductwork Repair and Installation Process in Stallion Springs

Every job starts with a thorough inspection before any repair work begins. We use a combination of visual assessment and pressure testing to locate leaks, this matters in Stallion Springs homes where duct runs often travel long distances through unconditioned attic space, making a single small gap surprisingly costly in lost efficiency.

For leaky duct testing, we depressurize the system and measure total duct leakage against the volume the system should be moving. That number tells us whether targeted sealing will solve the problem or whether sections of ductwork need full replacement. When sealing is the right call, we apply aerosol-based sealant or brush-grade mastic at joints and collars, materials rated to hold up in the temperature extremes this area produces. When replacement is warranted, we size new duct runs to the actual load of the home, not just the footprint of what came out.

Installation work in Stallion Springs attics requires attention to insulation depth around new duct runs. California's Title 24 energy code sets minimum R-values for duct insulation in climate zones like this one, and meeting that standard matters both for code compliance and for the long-term performance of the system.
