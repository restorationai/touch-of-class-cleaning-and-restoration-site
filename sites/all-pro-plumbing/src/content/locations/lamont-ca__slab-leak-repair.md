## Why Lamont Properties See Slab Leak Issues

The San Joaquin Valley floor beneath Lamont is composed largely of fine-grained alluvial sediments that behave differently depending on moisture content. During dry summers, the soil shrinks and pulls away from foundation edges. When winter rains arrive, it swells back. That repeated cycle creates micro-movement in slabs that were never designed to flex, and over years it works the fittings and straight runs of under-slab copper until a pinhole or joint failure opens up.

Housing stock in the Lamont area skews toward mid-century construction, with many homes built in the 1950s through 1970s using copper supply lines that are now past their expected service life. Original galvanized steel drain lines in that same era corrode from the inside out, sometimes collapsing inward before any surface sign appears. The combination of aging pipe material and active soil movement makes under-slab plumbing repair a recurring need here, not a rare one.

Kern County's agricultural irrigation infrastructure also affects the local water table in ways that can accelerate corrosion on the exterior surface of buried lines. Seasonal fluctuations in soil moisture, especially in areas near irrigation canals and fields, mean the ground around a slab is rarely at a stable moisture level for long.

## Our Slab Leak Detection and Repair Process in Lamont

Finding the leak before cutting concrete is the part that separates a clean repair from a costly one. We use electronic amplification equipment and thermal imaging to locate the leak point without opening the slab unnecessarily. Thermal cameras pick up the temperature differential created by a hot-water line leak, while acoustic listening devices isolate the sound of pressurized water escaping through a pinhole in a cold-water line.

Once the leak is confirmed and mapped, we present two primary repair paths:

**Direct-access repair** is appropriate when the leak is isolated to a single point and the surrounding pipe is otherwise sound. We open the slab at the smallest footprint possible, make the repair, pressure-test the line, and restore the concrete and finish flooring.

**Pipe rerouting** is the better choice when the pipe material itself is degraded along a longer run. Rather than chasing future failures through the slab, we route a new supply line through the walls or attic, bypassing the under-slab section entirely. For Lamont homes with widespread original copper, this approach often prevents a second slab leak within a few years of the first repair.

After any repair, we run a final pressure test and document the results before we leave the site.
