## Why McFarland Homes Have Specific Furnace Needs

The Central Valley's agricultural economy shaped how McFarland was built. A significant portion of the housing stock consists of compact single-family homes constructed during the mid-20th century, many with original ductwork that was sized for older, lower-efficiency equipment. Installing a modern high-efficiency furnace into one of these homes without reassessing the duct system is one of the most common mistakes in the region. Undersized returns and leaky trunk lines can reduce a new furnace's effective output by a meaningful margin, leaving homeowners paying for equipment that never performs as rated.

McFarland also sits in a part of Kern County where natural gas service is the standard fuel source for residential heating. That's generally an advantage for efficiency and operating cost, but it means installation work must account for gas line sizing, proper combustion air requirements, and flue venting that meets current California code. The California Energy Commission's Title 24 standards apply to replacement equipment, which affects which units qualify and how the installation must be documented.

## Our Furnace Installation and Replacement Process in McFarland

Every installation starts with a load calculation. McFarland's climate zone determines the heating and cooling loads your home actually needs, and that number drives equipment selection. A furnace that's oversized short-cycles, wears out faster, and leaves rooms feeling unevenly heated. One that's undersized runs constantly on the coldest nights and still can't keep up.

After sizing, the process moves through these steps:

- **Existing system assessment**: We inspect the current furnace, flue, and ductwork before the old unit comes out. If the duct system has problems, we identify them now rather than after the new equipment is running.
- **Equipment selection**: We match the unit to your home's load, your existing fuel and electrical connections, and California's efficiency minimums for replacement furnaces.
- **Safe removal and disposal**: The old unit is disconnected from gas, electrical, and flue connections and removed from the home. Refrigerant handling and disposal follow applicable regulations.
- **Installation and commissioning**: The new furnace is set, connected, and tested through a full heating cycle. We verify gas pressure, check for combustion issues, and confirm the thermostat is communicating correctly.
- **Documentation**: California Title 24 replacement installations require permit documentation in most jurisdictions. We handle the paperwork so the installation is on record with the appropriate local authority.

## Equipment and Efficiency Options for McFarland Installations

For most McFarland homes, an 80% AFUE gas furnace meets California's current minimums for replacement installations, but 96% AFUE two-stage models are increasingly common and make sense for homeowners planning to stay in the home long-term. Two-stage units run at lower capacity most of the time, which means quieter operation, more even heat distribution, and lower monthly gas bills during the valley's cool winters. Variable-speed air handlers paired with higher-efficiency furnaces also improve air filtration, which matters in an agricultural area where dust and particulate levels can be elevated during harvest season.
