## Why McFarland Properties See Ductwork Problems

The San Joaquin Valley's climate is the primary driver. Daytime heat in McFarland expands duct materials; overnight temperatures drop enough to contract them. That daily thermal cycling loosens joints, degrades mastic sealant, and eventually opens gaps that bleed conditioned air into unconditioned spaces. Older homes in McFarland built in the 1960s and 1970s often have original flex duct that has become brittle over decades of this cycle. When that flex duct kinks, collapses, or tears, airflow to entire rooms can drop to nearly nothing.

Agricultural dust is the second factor. Fine particulate matter common throughout Kern County infiltrates duct systems through return-air grilles, loose connections, and small punctures. Over time it coats duct interiors, reduces airflow, and in some cases contributes to indoor air quality issues. Leaky duct testing, sometimes called a duct blaster test, can quantify exactly how much air your system is losing before you commit to a full replacement versus targeted sealing.

## Our Ductwork Repair and Installation Process in McFarland

Every job starts with a visual inspection and, where conditions warrant, a pressure-based leaky duct test. That test tells us whether you need localized air duct repair, comprehensive duct sealing, or a full duct replacement. We don't default to the most expensive option; the test data drives the recommendation.

For repairs, we access the affected duct runs through attic hatches or crawl space entries, remove damaged sections, and replace them with properly sized flex duct or sheet metal depending on the application. All connections are sealed with mastic compound and mesh tape rather than duct tape, which degrades quickly in Kern County's heat. For new ductwork installation, we size the system to match your HVAC equipment's specifications and the actual square footage and layout of your home, not a generic rule of thumb.

After any repair or installation, we run a post-work pressure test to confirm the system is performing within acceptable leakage limits before we close up the attic or crawl space.

## Local Note

Homes in McFarland built during the agricultural boom years of the 1960s through the 1980s frequently have duct systems routed through interior wall cavities rather than the attic, a design choice that made sense for insulation at the time but creates complications today. Wall-cavity ducts are harder to access, harder to seal, and harder to replace than attic runs. If your home was built in that era and you're seeing uneven room temperatures or high utility bills, it's worth having those wall runs inspected specifically, not just the attic ductwork, before assuming the HVAC equipment itself is the problem.
