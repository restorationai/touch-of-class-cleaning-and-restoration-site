## Why McFarland Properties See Burst Pipe Problems

The San Joaquin Valley's clay-heavy soil expands when wet and contracts sharply during the dry stretches that define Kern County summers. Underground water lines, particularly older galvanized or polybutylene runs common in homes built through the 1980s and early 1990s, flex with that movement until a joint or fitting gives way. Frost is less frequent here than in mountain communities, but the nights in December and January cold enough to freeze an uninsulated exterior hose bib or a pipe running through an unheated garage are more common than most homeowners expect. A pipe that freezes and thaws even once can develop a hairline crack that weeps for weeks inside a wall before the stain appears on drywall or the water bill spikes.

Agricultural irrigation infrastructure throughout this part of Kern County also means local water pressure can fluctuate more than it does in denser urban systems. Pressure surges after irrigation-season shutoffs are a known stressor on older residential supply lines, and the connection between those surges and sudden pipe failures is something a plumber working regularly in this corridor learns to look for.
