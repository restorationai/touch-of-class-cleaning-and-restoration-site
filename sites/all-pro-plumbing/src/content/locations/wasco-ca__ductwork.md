Wasco sits in the heart of the San Joaquin Valley, where summer temperatures regularly push past 100°F and dry, dusty air is a fact of life from June through September. That combination puts unusual stress on duct systems: heat causes flexible ductwork to expand and contract with every cycle, while the fine agricultural dust that settles across this part of Kern County works its way into gaps and joints over time. If your home feels unevenly cooled, your utility bills have crept up, or you notice a faint earthy smell when the system kicks on, the ductwork is often the place to start looking.

## Why Wasco Properties See Duct Problems

Much of Wasco's residential housing stock was built during the agricultural expansion of the mid-20th century, when duct design standards were far less rigorous than they are today. Homes from that era were often fitted with duct runs that snake through unconditioned attic spaces, which in a Central Valley summer can reach temperatures well above 140°F. At those temperatures, duct tape adhesives fail, foil-faced insulation delaminates, and flexible duct liner collapses inward at bends, choking airflow to back bedrooms and far corners of the house.

The region's soil conditions add another layer of complexity. Expansive clay soils common throughout Kern County shift seasonally as the ground wets and dries, and that movement can stress any ductwork that passes through crawl spaces or is anchored to framing near the foundation. A duct that reads tight in February may be pulling conditioned air into the crawl by August.

## Our Ductwork Repair and Installation Process in Wasco

Every job starts with a diagnostic walk-through rather than a guess. We use pressure testing to measure how much air the duct system is actually losing before we open a single panel. Leaky duct testing gives us a baseline number, so when the work is done you can see the improvement in measurable terms, not just feel it.

From there, the scope determines the path. Minor duct sealing jobs typically involve cleaning the joint surfaces, applying mastic sealant, and wrapping connections with UL-listed tape rated for the temperature swings Wasco attics produce. Full duct replacement means pulling the old runs, sizing the new system to the actual load of the house (not the old system's footprint), and installing properly supported flexible or sheet-metal duct with insulation rated for the local climate zone. California's Title 24 energy code governs duct installation in the state, including insulation R-values and duct leakage thresholds for new and replacement work, so every installation we complete is built to meet those requirements.

## Local Note

One pattern that comes up specifically in Wasco's older ranch-style homes: the original duct systems were often sized for evaporative coolers, not the central air conditioning units that replaced them in later decades. When a homeowner upgrades to a modern split system without revisiting the duct layout, the new equipment moves air at a different static pressure than the old system was designed for. The result is a unit that short-cycles, struggles to dehumidify, and wears out faster than it should. If your central AC was added after the house was built and the ducts were never redesigned to match, that mismatch is worth testing for before you replace the equipment a second time.

## Equipment and Methods We Use for Ductwork

For leaky duct testing, we use a duct blaster setup that pressurizes the system and measures total leakage in cubic feet per minute. That number tells us whether sealing is sufficient or whether replacement makes more economic sense. For sealing, mastic is our default over tape alone because it stays flexible through the temperature extremes Wasco attics see year after year. For new installations, we size duct runs using Manual D calculations so that every room in the house gets the airflow the equipment is designed to deliver.
