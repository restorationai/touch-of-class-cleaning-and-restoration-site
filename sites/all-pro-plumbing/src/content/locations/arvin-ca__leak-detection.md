## Why Arvin Properties Experience Hidden Leaks

The agricultural heritage of the Arvin area means the soil beneath many residential lots has a high clay content that expands when wet and contracts sharply during the dry summer months. That constant movement stresses underground supply lines and sewer laterals, particularly on properties where original galvanized or cast-iron pipe has never been replaced. Homes built during Arvin's mid-century growth periods often have plumbing configurations that were standard at the time but are now well past their expected service life.

High summer temperatures also accelerate joint fatigue on plastic supply lines in attics and crawl spaces. When daytime heat pushes pipe temperatures to extremes and overnight temperatures drop, repeated expansion and contraction works fittings loose over time. The result is a category of leak that reads as nothing more than a slightly elevated water bill for months before it announces itself through a stain on the ceiling or a soft patch in the yard.

## Our Leak Detection Process in Arvin

Finding a leak without tearing open walls or digging up a yard requires layered diagnostic tools, and the approach we use is calibrated to the types of construction common in this part of Kern County.

The process typically starts with a pressure test on the supply system to confirm a leak is present and to isolate which branch is affected. From there, electronic listening equipment is used to trace the acoustic signature of escaping water through concrete slabs, compacted soil, and finished wall assemblies. In cases where a slab leak is suspected, thermal imaging can reveal temperature differentials at the surface that point to the leak's location with a high degree of precision before any concrete is touched.

For underground line leaks in yards or driveways, ground microphone technology lets us walk the line and mark the leak location within inches. That matters in Arvin because many lots have irrigation infrastructure running parallel to domestic supply lines, and distinguishing between the two without excavation saves significant time and cost.
