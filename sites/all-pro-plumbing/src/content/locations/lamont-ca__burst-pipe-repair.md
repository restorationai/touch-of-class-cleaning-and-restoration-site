## Why Lamont Properties See Burst and Leaking Pipe Issues

The San Joaquin Valley's clay-heavy soil is a significant factor that rarely gets discussed until something goes wrong. Clay expands when it absorbs water and contracts during dry spells, and that constant ground movement puts lateral stress on underground water lines and the joints connecting them to a home's interior plumbing. Lamont's agricultural surroundings also mean irrigation infrastructure nearby can shift soil moisture levels seasonally, accelerating that cycle.

Inside the home, the issue is often age. A large portion of Lamont's residential housing was built in the 1950s through 1970s, when galvanized steel pipe was standard. Galvanized pipe corrodes from the inside out, narrowing over decades until pressure builds and a joint or section gives way. The failure rarely announces itself loudly. More often, a homeowner notices a soft spot in drywall, a warm section of floor, or a water bill that has quietly doubled before the pipe fully lets go.

Winter cold snaps compound the risk. When overnight temperatures fall into the low 30s, pipes running through uninsulated exterior walls or beneath homes with minimal crawl space insulation are vulnerable to freezing. A frozen pipe does not always burst immediately. It can thaw, develop a hairline fracture, and leak slowly inside a wall for days before the damage becomes visible.

## Our Burst and Leaking Pipe Repair Process in Lamont

When you call, the first step is locating the source accurately. Water follows the path of least resistance, which means the visible stain on a ceiling or wall is often not directly above or beside the break. The crew uses electronic leak detection and thermal imaging to trace the line without opening walls unnecessarily. In Lamont's older homes, where plumbing may have been rerouted or modified over the decades without updated documentation, that diagnostic step saves significant time and avoids unnecessary damage.

Once the break is located, the repair approach depends on the pipe material, its age, and its accessibility. A corroded galvanized section in a wall cavity may warrant replacing a longer run rather than patching a single joint, because the surrounding pipe is often in similar condition. For copper or PEX lines, a precise section repair is usually appropriate. After the repair, the crew pressure-tests the line before closing any access points.

Water extraction and drying follow immediately if the leak has been active long enough to saturate building materials. In Lamont's climate, the dry heat can create a false sense that materials are drying on their own, but moisture trapped inside wall cavities or beneath flooring does not evaporate without airflow directed at it.
