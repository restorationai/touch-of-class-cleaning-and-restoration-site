## Why McFarland Properties See AC Repair Issues

The San Joaquin Valley's climate is hard on cooling equipment in ways that differ from coastal California. McFarland experiences extreme diurnal swings in summer, with daytime highs that stress compressors and condenser coils for months at a stretch, followed by cooler nights that cause refrigerant pressures to fluctuate. Dust from surrounding agricultural fields coats condenser coils and clogs filters faster than in urban areas, restricting airflow and forcing the system to work harder than it was designed to. That sustained strain accelerates wear on capacitors, contactors, and fan motors.

Many homes in McFarland were built during the mid-century agricultural expansion of Kern County, and their duct systems were sized for equipment that is now two or three generations old. An aging duct layout that was adequate for a 1970s central air unit may be undersized for a modern high-efficiency system, creating pressure imbalances that show up as rooms that never cool evenly. Identifying whether the problem is the unit itself or the distribution system is a diagnostic step that shapes every repair recommendation we make here.

## Our Air Conditioning Repair Process in McFarland

When a technician arrives, the first priority is a full system diagnostic rather than a quick part swap. That means checking refrigerant charge and inspecting for leaks at the evaporator coil and line-set connections, testing capacitor and contactor condition, measuring supply and return air temperatures, and verifying that the thermostat is communicating correctly with the air handler.

Refrigerant leak repair deserves particular attention in this climate. A system that is low on refrigerant will run continuously without reaching setpoint, which is exactly the "AC not cooling" complaint that spikes in McFarland every June through September. Simply topping off refrigerant without finding and sealing the leak is a short-term fix that leaves the underlying problem in place. We locate the leak, repair it, and recharge to manufacturer specification before the job is closed out.

If a compressor or coil replacement is needed, we source parts and schedule the return visit as quickly as supply allows. We walk you through what was found, what was repaired, and what to watch for going forward, in plain language rather than technical shorthand.

## Equipment and Methods We Use for AC Repair

Valley heat demands accurate refrigerant measurement, not guesswork. We use digital manifold gauges to read system pressures precisely, electronic leak detectors to find refrigerant loss at fittings and coil surfaces, and clamp meters to verify that motors and compressors are drawing correct amperage. Overamping is one of the earliest signs that a compressor is failing, and catching it before a full burnout can save the cost of a full system replacement.

For condenser coil cleaning, we use a low-pressure coil cleaner rather than high-pressure water, which can bend aluminum fins and reduce airflow permanently. In an agricultural area like McFarland, coils can accumulate a season's worth of field dust in a few weeks during harvest, so the cleaning step is not cosmetic.

## Local Note

One pattern that comes up in McFarland and the surrounding Kern County agricultural corridor: evaporative coolers and central air systems sometimes share the same duct network in older homes, and switching between them seasonally can leave dampers in the wrong position when cooling season starts. A system that appears to have lost capacity in early summer sometimes turns out to have an evaporative damper partially blocking return airflow rather than a refrigerant or mechanical fault. It is worth mentioning to a technician if your home has both systems, because it changes where the diagnostic starts.
