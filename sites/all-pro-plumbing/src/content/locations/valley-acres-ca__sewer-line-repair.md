## Why Valley Acres Properties See Sewer Line Problems

The San Joaquin Valley's clay-heavy soil is notoriously expansive. During the dry summers, it contracts and pulls away from buried pipe. When the rains arrive in winter, it swells back and exerts lateral pressure on whatever is underground. Over years, that cycle cracks joints, misaligns pipe sections, and creates low spots where solids accumulate. Properties in this part of Kern County that were developed in the 1950s through 1970s are especially vulnerable because they were commonly plumbed with cast iron or orangeburg, a fiber-and-tar composite that was never meant to last more than a few decades. Those materials are now well past their design life, and a camera inspection often reveals the same picture: sagging bellies, root intrusion at every joint, and sections that have gone from round to oval under ground pressure.

Agricultural irrigation in the surrounding area also raises and lowers the local water table more than homeowners expect. When the water table is elevated, hydrostatic pressure can force groundwater into cracked sewer lines, adding volume to the system and accelerating pipe deterioration from the outside in.

## Our Sewer Line Repair and Replacement Process in Valley Acres

Every job starts with a sewer camera inspection. A high-resolution camera goes into the line from the cleanout, and we record the full run so you can see exactly what we see: root intrusion, offset joints, cracks, or a collapsed section. That footage also matters for insurance documentation and for any permit pulled through Kern County.

From there, the repair path depends on what the camera shows:

- **Trenchless sewer repair** is the right call when the pipe is structurally sound enough to serve as a host. Pipe lining (CIPP) installs a resin-saturated liner inside the existing pipe and cures it in place, restoring flow without excavation. Pipe bursting is the trenchless option when the old pipe needs to be replaced entirely: a bursting head fractures the old line outward while simultaneously pulling new HDPE pipe through the same path.
- **Open-cut replacement** is necessary when the pipe has collapsed, when there are multiple offset sections close together, or when the line runs under a structure that makes trenchless access impractical. In Valley Acres, this sometimes means working around irrigation laterals and utility crossings that aren't always reflected on older as-built drawings.

Once the new or lined pipe is in place, we backfill, compact, and restore the surface. If a permit was required, we coordinate the inspection before closing out.
