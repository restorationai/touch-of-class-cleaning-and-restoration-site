## Why Lamont Properties See Leak Detection Issues

The soils across this part of Kern County contain a significant clay fraction. Clay swells when it absorbs moisture and shrinks when it dries out, and that cycle repeats every year through the wet winters and scorching summers. Underground supply lines, particularly older galvanized or copper runs, flex with that movement. Over time, small stress fractures develop at joints and elbows. Because the leak is below grade, the only early sign is often a water meter that keeps spinning after every fixture in the house is shut off.

Housing stock in and around Lamont skews toward mid-century construction, with many homes built between the 1940s and 1970s. Galvanized steel pipe from that era corrodes from the inside out, narrowing the bore and eventually pinholing. A pinhol leak inside a wall cavity or under a slab can lose dozens of gallons a day while the surface stays bone dry. Electronic leak detection finds those losses without a single exploratory cut.

## Our Leak Detection Process in Lamont

The process starts at the water meter. With every fixture closed, a meter that still registers flow confirms active loss somewhere in the system. From there, the diagnostic sequence moves inward:

- **Pressure testing** isolates whether the leak is on the supply side, the drain side, or both.
- **Electronic amplification** uses sensitive listening equipment placed against the slab or soil surface to pick up the acoustic signature of water escaping under pressure. Clay soil actually transmits that signal well, which works in our favor in this part of the valley.
- **Thermal imaging** can reveal temperature differentials behind drywall or under flooring where a slow leak has cooled or warmed a surface zone.
- **Tracer gas** is introduced into the line when acoustic methods leave ambiguity; the gas escapes through the breach and is detected at the surface with a calibrated sensor.

Once the breach is located to within inches, we mark the spot and discuss the least invasive repair path before any concrete or drywall is touched.

## Equipment and Methods We Use for Leak Detection

Not every leak announces itself with the sound of running water. The equipment we carry is chosen specifically for losses that hide:

- **Ground microphones and correlators** compare acoustic signals from two points on a pipe run and calculate the leak's position by the difference in arrival time.
- **Infrared cameras** read surface temperature variations that indicate moisture migration behind finished surfaces.
- **Video pipe inspection** threads a camera through the line to visually confirm corrosion, joint separation, or root intrusion in drain and sewer lines.
- **Non-invasive moisture meters** verify whether a suspicious area is actively wet or simply stained from a past event.

Using the right tool for the specific pipe material and burial depth matters. Galvanized lines common in older Lamont homes behave differently under acoustic testing than modern PEX, and we adjust the approach accordingly.
