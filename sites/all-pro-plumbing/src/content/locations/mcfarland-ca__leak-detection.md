## Why McFarland Properties See Leak Detection Issues

The clay-heavy soils common throughout this stretch of the San Joaquin Valley are particularly hard on underground supply lines. Seasonal expansion and contraction puts lateral pressure on pipe joints year after year, and in a town like McFarland where much of the housing stock dates to mid-century construction, galvanized steel and early copper plumbing is still in service under a lot of slabs and yards. Those older materials corrode from the outside in, and the first sign of failure is often a slow, invisible seep rather than a dramatic break.

Irrigation infrastructure throughout the area also means water pressure in municipal lines can fluctuate more than homeowners in denser urban areas typically experience. Pressure spikes accelerate wear on fittings and valve seats, and a pinhole leak under a concrete slab can saturate the substrate for weeks before moisture wicks up to a floor surface you can actually see.

## Our Leak Detection Process in McFarland

Finding a hidden leak without tearing up flooring or landscaping requires the right equipment and a methodical approach. The process starts with a pressure test on the supply line to confirm a leak exists and get a rough sense of its severity. From there, electronic amplification equipment is used to listen for the specific acoustic signature of water escaping a pressurized pipe, even through a concrete slab or compacted soil.

For underground leaks where acoustic methods narrow the location but soil depth or ambient noise complicates pinpointing, thermal imaging adds a second layer of confirmation by detecting temperature differentials where cold water is migrating through warm ground. In McFarland's summer months, when soil surface temperatures can exceed 100 degrees Fahrenheit, thermal contrast between the leak path and surrounding dry soil is often pronounced and makes imaging especially effective. The goal throughout is to mark the leak location precisely before any excavation or repair begins, keeping disruption to your yard, driveway, or slab to the minimum necessary.

## Equipment and Methods We Use for Leak Detection

Electronic leak detection equipment does the work that used to require opening walls or trenching yards on a best-guess basis. Acoustic listening devices amplify the sound of water under pressure escaping through a breach, filtering out ambient noise to isolate the leak signal. Correlating equipment can compare signals from two access points and calculate the leak's position along the line mathematically, which is particularly useful on longer underground runs.

For slab leaks, where the pipe is encased in concrete, non-invasive detection is especially valuable. Identifying the precise location before cutting means a smaller, targeted opening rather than a wide exploratory trench. In homes with older plumbing common to McFarland's mid-century residential areas, accurate pre-repair location also helps determine whether a spot repair makes sense or whether the line's overall condition warrants a more comprehensive solution.
