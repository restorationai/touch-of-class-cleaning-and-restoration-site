## Why Lamont's Climate and Building Stock Drive Mini-Split Demand

The San Joaquin Valley's heat is relentless from May through October, but Lamont's housing mix creates specific challenges that a one-size system rarely solves. A large share of the residential stock here consists of smaller wood-frame homes built for agricultural workers, many with minimal attic insulation and no existing duct infrastructure. Trying to retrofit traditional ducted central air into those structures means cutting through finished ceilings, adding a furnace plenum, and losing closet space to trunk lines. A ductless system sidesteps all of that: the indoor air handler mounts high on the wall, a three-inch conduit run connects it to the outdoor compressor, and the room is cooling or heating within a day.

Commercial and light-industrial properties along the Highway 184 corridor face a different version of the same problem. Warehouses, small shops, and agricultural support buildings often have open floor plans and no practical way to zone a ducted system. A multi-zone mini-split lets an owner condition the office separately from the shop floor, running only what's needed rather than cooling the entire footprint all day.

## Our Mini-Split Installation Process in Lamont

Every installation starts with a load calculation tied to the actual room dimensions and sun exposure of the space, not a rule-of-thumb BTU guess. Lamont's western-facing walls absorb heat well into the evening, which often means the western rooms of a home need a higher-capacity head unit than the east side. We size accordingly.

From there, the process runs in a predictable sequence:

1. **Site walk and bracket placement**, we confirm wall structure, locate studs or concrete anchors, and mark the conduit path before any drilling begins.
2. **Line set and conduit installation**, refrigerant lines, electrical, and condensate drain are bundled and run through the exterior wall, then covered with a line-set cover for a finished appearance.
3. **Outdoor unit placement and pad or bracket mounting**, the compressor needs clearance for airflow and protection from direct afternoon sun where possible.
4. **Vacuum, charge, and startup**, the system is evacuated, charged to manufacturer spec, and tested through both heating and cooling modes before we leave.
5. **Walkthrough**, we show you how to use the remote or app controls, explain filter cleaning intervals, and answer questions before closing out.

For multi-zone installs, we map the head-unit locations in advance so conduit runs stay clean and the outdoor unit is sized to handle the combined load without short-cycling.

## Local Note: Afternoon Heat Load on West-Facing Walls

One thing that comes up consistently in Lamont's smaller residential properties: the afternoon sun hits west and southwest walls hard, and many of these homes have little to no exterior shading from trees or overhangs. When we're placing indoor head units, we factor that in by positioning the air handler on an interior or north-facing wall where possible, so it's drawing from the coolest air in the room rather than fighting radiant heat coming through the wall behind it. It's a small placement decision that makes a measurable difference in how efficiently the system runs through a 108-degree August afternoon.
