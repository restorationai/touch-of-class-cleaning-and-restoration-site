## Why Buttonwillow Properties See Fixture Problems

The water delivered to Buttonwillow carries a high mineral load typical of the southern San Joaquin Valley. That hardness accelerates scale buildup inside fill valves, corrodes cartridge seals in single-handle faucets, and leaves calcium deposits on showerheads that restrict flow long before the fixture itself wears out. Properties that have been on well water face a related but different problem: iron and sediment in the supply line grind away at valve seats and washers faster than municipal water does.

The extreme heat cycle here also matters more than most people realize. Plastic supply lines and wax toilet rings expand and contract sharply between a cool winter morning and a July afternoon when an unshaded crawl space can reach temperatures that would surprise most plumbers from coastal California. That thermal stress is one reason toilet bases in older Buttonwillow properties sometimes develop slow seepage at the floor flange long before the toilet itself shows any visible damage.
