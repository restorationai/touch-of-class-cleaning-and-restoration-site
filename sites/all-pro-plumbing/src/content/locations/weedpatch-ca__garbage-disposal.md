## Why Weedpatch Properties See Garbage Disposal Problems

The water coming out of taps in this part of Kern County carries a high mineral load. Over time, those dissolved solids coat the impellers and grinding ring inside a disposal, reducing cutting efficiency and putting extra strain on the motor. Homeowners often notice the unit running louder than usual or taking two or three cycles to clear what it used to handle in one. That is not a sign the disposal is aging out of service necessarily; in many cases it is a sign the unit needs a thorough cleaning, a reset, or a targeted repair before the motor burns out entirely.

Older homes in the Weedpatch area also tend to have narrower drain lines and P-traps that were sized for simpler kitchen use. Adding a disposal to one of those setups, or replacing an undersized unit with a higher-horsepower model, sometimes requires adjusting the drain configuration so the new unit does not back up into the sink basin. That is work that goes beyond swapping hardware, and it is worth having a licensed plumber assess the drain stack before any installation begins.

## Our Garbage Disposal Repair and Installation Process in Weedpatch

Every service call starts with a diagnosis, not an assumption. A disposal that hums but will not spin is almost always a jammed turntable, and that is usually fixable on the spot with the right hex wrench and a few minutes of work. A disposal that leaks from the bottom flange is a different problem entirely, often a failed internal seal that makes replacement more cost-effective than repair. We walk through that assessment with you before any work begins so you understand what you are paying for and why.

For installations, we match the motor size to your household's actual usage and your existing drain plumbing. A half-horsepower unit is adequate for a single-person household with light food prep; a larger family kitchen that handles fibrous vegetables, citrus rinds, and heavier scraps will benefit from a three-quarter or full horsepower motor. We also verify that the electrical circuit serving the disposal meets current requirements, since some older Weedpatch-area kitchens have circuits that were wired before disposals were standard equipment.
