## Why Buttonwillow Properties See Furnace Problems

The climate pattern here is the core issue. Buttonwillow's heating season is short but intense, which means furnaces spend most of the year sitting dormant in dusty, dry air. San Joaquin Valley dust is fine and pervasive, and it accumulates inside heat exchangers, burner assemblies, and blower compartments during the off-season. When the furnace fires up for the first time in fall, that buildup can trigger limit switches, choke airflow across the heat exchanger, or cause the burner to ignite unevenly and then shut down on a safety lockout.

Agricultural operations around Buttonwillow also affect air quality in ways that accelerate filter clogging. A filter that might last 90 days in a suburban Bakersfield home can reach capacity in half that time here, and a severely restricted filter is one of the most common reasons a furnace blows cold air rather than warm. Homes and commercial properties near the I-5 corridor see additional particulate load from truck traffic, compounding the issue.

## Local Note

One thing that catches homeowners in the Buttonwillow area off guard: older agricultural-era homes in this part of Kern County were sometimes built with oversized furnaces relative to the conditioned square footage, because original construction assumed less insulation and more infiltration.
