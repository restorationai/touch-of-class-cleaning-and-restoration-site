## Why McFarland Properties See Sewer Line Problems

The clay soils throughout this stretch of the valley behave almost like a slow-motion vise around buried pipe. During a wet winter or a heavy irrigation season, the ground swells and shifts. When summer heat returns and the soil dries, it contracts. Cast iron and older clay tile sewer lines, common in homes built in the mid-twentieth century, are especially vulnerable to that movement because they rely on gasketed or cemented joints rather than the flexible couplings used in modern PVC installations. A joint that shifts even a fraction of an inch can allow roots from nearby trees or shrubs to find their way in, and once roots establish inside a line they grow quickly toward the moisture and nutrients flowing through it. The result is a partial blockage that gets worse with every flush until the line is fully obstructed or collapses.

McFarland's agricultural setting also means some residential parcels are served by older lateral connections that were installed decades ago with minimal cover depth, making them more susceptible to damage from surface loading, heavy vehicles, or even routine landscaping work.

## Our Sewer Line Repair and Replacement Process in McFarland

Every job starts with a sewer camera inspection. A flexible camera head is fed through a cleanout or access point and transmits live video of the pipe interior, showing exactly where a crack, offset, root intrusion, or collapse has occurred and how far it extends. That footage drives every decision that follows and eliminates guesswork about scope.

Once the condition of the line is documented, the repair method is matched to what the camera reveals. For a localized crack or a section with heavy root intrusion, trenchless sewer repair is often the right call. Pipe lining involves pulling a resin-saturated liner through the existing pipe and curing it in place, creating a smooth, seamless interior that seals cracks and blocks future root entry without requiring a trench the length of the yard. Where a line has collapsed or the pipe wall is too deteriorated to host a liner, pipe bursting can replace the old line by pulling a new pipe through the same path while fracturing the old one outward.

When a full open-cut replacement is necessary, the crew locates utilities before any digging begins, a step that matters in McFarland where irrigation infrastructure and agricultural supply lines can run close to residential property boundaries.
