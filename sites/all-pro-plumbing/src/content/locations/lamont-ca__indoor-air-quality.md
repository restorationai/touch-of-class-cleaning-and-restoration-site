## Why Lamont Properties See Elevated Indoor Air Quality Concerns

The San Joaquin Valley consistently ranks among the worst air quality regions in the United States, and Lamont's position in the southern end of the valley puts it squarely in that pattern. During harvest season, field dust, crop residue, and equipment exhaust move across the area in visible waves. When HVAC systems pull in outdoor air for ventilation, they can introduce those particles directly into living spaces.

Lamont's housing stock adds another layer. Many homes in the area were built in the mid-twentieth century, when vapor barriers and tight building envelopes were not standard practice. Gaps around duct connections, aging insulation, and crawl spaces with limited airflow all create conditions where moisture, mold spores, and accumulated dust circulate more freely. Older evaporative coolers, still common in the area, can also introduce outdoor particulates and humidity in ways that central air systems with proper filtration do not.

For families with asthma, allergies, or young children, these aren't abstract concerns. The Valley's high rates of respiratory illness are well documented at the county health level, and the indoor environment is one of the few factors a homeowner can actually control.

## Our Indoor Air Quality Services Process in Lamont

Every project starts with an assessment rather than an assumption. Before recommending equipment, a technician evaluates your existing HVAC system, ductwork condition, current filtration, and the specific layout of your home. A house with a single-zone system and original ductwork from the 1970s needs a different approach than a newer build with a high-efficiency air handler.

From there, the work typically falls into a few categories:

**HVAC filtration upgrades** replace standard filters with higher-MERV media filters or whole-system filtration units that capture fine particulates, including the sub-micron particles that standard filters pass right through. For Lamont homes dealing with agricultural dust, this upgrade alone can make a measurable difference in what settles on surfaces and what gets inhaled.

**UV air purifiers for HVAC systems** mount inside the air handler and use ultraviolet light to neutralize biological contaminants, including mold spores and bacteria, before they circulate through the home. In a climate where summer humidity spikes can encourage mold growth inside ductwork, UV systems add a layer of protection that filtration alone doesn't provide.

**Whole home humidifier installation** addresses the opposite problem: Kern County winters are dry, and low indoor humidity causes its own set of issues, from cracked woodwork to increased susceptibility to airborne illness. A whole-house humidifier integrated with your HVAC system maintains consistent humidity levels without the maintenance burden of portable units.

**Indoor air quality testing** gives you a baseline reading of what's actually present in your air before and after any equipment installation, so the improvement isn't just theoretical.
