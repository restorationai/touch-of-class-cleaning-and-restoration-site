## Our Furnace Repair Process in Bear Valley Springs

When a technician arrives at your property, the diagnostic sequence is methodical rather than guesswork. The first step is a visual and electronic check of the ignition system: hot surface ignitors and flame sensors are common failure points in systems that sit idle through the spring and summer months. A sensor coated with oxidation will cause the burner to light and then shut down within seconds, which is exactly what homeowners describe as a furnace "short cycling."

From there, the technician checks the heat exchanger for cracks, verifies that the pressure switch is reading correctly, and tests the blower motor and capacitor. At elevation, combustion air supply matters more than it does at sea level, so the flue and air intake are inspected for blockage from debris, bird nests, or collapsed flex sections. ## Local Note

One detail that catches homeowners off guard in Bear Valley Springs: propane tank levels drop faster than expected during a cold snap, and a furnace that suddenly stops firing in January may not have a mechanical fault at all. Low tank pressure causes the regulator to cut off flow as a safety measure, and the furnace will behave exactly like it has an ignition problem. Before assuming the worst, check your tank gauge. If the level is below 20 percent, that is the likely culprit, and a fuel delivery call solves it faster than a repair visit. That said, if the tank is full and the furnace still won't run, call for a technician rather than resetting the system repeatedly, as repeated failed ignition attempts can flood the heat exchanger with unburned fuel.
