## Why Lamont Properties See Water Heater Problems

Lamont sits in the southern San Joaquin Valley, where the groundwater and municipal supply carry a notably high mineral load. Hard water is not a minor inconvenience for a water heater. Calcium and magnesium deposits settle at the bottom of a tank over time, forming a layer of sediment that forces the burner to work harder to heat the water above it. The result is a rumbling or popping sound during heating cycles, rising energy bills, and a tank that wears out years ahead of schedule.

The region's agricultural roots also mean a significant share of Lamont homes were built during mid-century construction booms, when standard practice was to install water heaters in detached garages or exterior utility closets with minimal insulation. Those locations expose the unit to extreme temperature swings, from scorching summer afternoons to the cold, damp nights that roll through Kern County in January and February. That cycle of expansion and contraction accelerates wear on fittings, supply lines, and the tank itself.

When a technician arrives, the first step is a full diagnostic rather than a quick guess.
