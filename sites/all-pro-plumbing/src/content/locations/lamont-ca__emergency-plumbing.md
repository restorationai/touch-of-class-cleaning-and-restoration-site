## Why Lamont Properties Experience Plumbing Emergencies

Lamont's housing stock skews older, with a significant share of single-family homes and agricultural worker residences built during the mid-twentieth century boom that followed the expansion of farming operations in this part of Kern County. Galvanized steel supply lines from that era corrode from the inside out, often showing no external warning before they fail. The local water supply, drawn from groundwater sources, carries mineral hardness that accelerates scale buildup inside water heaters and narrows the bore of older copper fittings over time.

Below grade, the expansive clay soils common throughout this stretch of the valley contract sharply during dry spells and swell again after irrigation season or a rare winter rain event. That cycle exerts lateral pressure on buried drain lines and can crack cast-iron sewer laterals at the joints, producing slow leaks that go unnoticed until a slab shows moisture or a cleanout backs up. Homes on slab foundations, which are common in this part of Kern County, give that moisture nowhere to drain, so a slab leak can saturate subfloor materials quickly once it starts.
