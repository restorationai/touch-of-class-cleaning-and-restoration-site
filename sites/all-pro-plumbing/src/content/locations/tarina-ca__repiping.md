Tarina sits in Kern County's southern San Joaquin Valley, where decades of agricultural irrigation, alkaline soil, and hard water have quietly taken a toll on the plumbing inside older homes. If your water pressure has been dropping room by room, or if a rust-colored tint shows up when you first run the tap in the morning, the pipes themselves are likely the source. Whole-house repiping replaces every supply line in one coordinated project, eliminating the cycle of single-pipe repairs that never quite solves the underlying problem.

## Why Tarina Properties Show Repiping Problems Early

The San Joaquin Valley's water is notoriously hard. High mineral content accelerates scale buildup inside galvanized steel pipes, narrowing the interior diameter over years until water pressure at the fixture is a fraction of what it should be. In homes built between the late 1950s and early 1980s, galvanized supply lines were standard, and those pipes are now well past the point where patching makes financial sense.

Polybutylene pipe is a separate concern in homes built or replumbed between roughly 1978 and 1995. The material reacts over time with chlorine compounds used in municipal water treatment, becoming brittle and prone to sudden failure at fittings. Kern County's water supply, drawn from both surface and groundwater sources, is treated to meet state standards, and that treatment chemistry is part of what degrades polybutylene from the inside out. If your home was built during that window and has never been repiped, it is worth having the supply lines identified before a fitting lets go behind a wall.

Soil movement is a quieter factor. The valley floor experiences seasonal expansion and contraction, and in areas with clay-heavy soil, that movement stresses underground service lines and the connections where supply lines meet the slab. Copper and PEX both handle minor movement better than rigid galvanized or aging plastic, which is one reason both materials are preferred for full repipes in this region.

## Our Whole-House Repiping Process in Tarina

A repipe starts with a walk-through of every fixture location: kitchen, bathrooms, laundry, exterior hose bibs, and any utility sinks. That inventory determines the linear footage of new pipe, the number of access points needed in walls and ceilings, and whether the existing shutoff valves and main are in good enough condition to stay.

For most Tarina homes, the choice comes down to copper or PEX. Copper is the longer-proven material and is required by some HOA governing documents for visible or accessible runs. PEX is flexible, resists scale buildup better in high-mineral water, and requires fewer fittings, which means fewer potential leak points. Both materials meet California plumbing code requirements. We discuss the tradeoffs with each homeowner before any pipe is ordered.

Once materials are confirmed, the crew opens the minimum wall area needed to route new lines, installs and pressure-tests the new supply system, and restores drywall. Water is typically off for one day on a standard single-family home. We coordinate the sequence so that at least one bathroom remains functional as long as possible during the work.

## Local Note

Homes in this part of Kern County that were built on slab foundations in the 1960s and 1970s sometimes have original copper supply lines that were run through or under the slab rather than through interior walls. That routing made sense at the time, but slab-embedded copper is vulnerable to pinhole leaks caused by soil chemistry and the slight movement the valley floor produces over decades. When we find slab-embedded lines during a repipe assessment, we typically recommend rerouting the new lines through interior walls rather than trenching the slab, which keeps the project cost lower and avoids disturbing the foundation. It is a detail that matters specifically in this region's construction era and soil conditions.
