## Why Wasco Properties See Water Heater Problems

The water delivered to Wasco homes through the Kern County Water Agency system carries a notably high mineral content. Calcium and magnesium settle at the bottom of tank-style water heaters over time, forming a layer of sediment that forces the burner to work harder, raises energy bills, and eventually cracks the glass lining of the tank. You may hear it first: a popping or rumbling sound when the burner fires is almost always sediment boiling under the buildup. Left alone, that stress leads to premature tank failure or a slow leak at the base.

Wasco's housing stock adds another layer of complexity. Many homes built during the agricultural expansion decades of the mid-20th century were plumbed with galvanized steel supply lines. As those pipes age and corrode internally, the rust and scale they shed accelerate wear on water heater components downstream, particularly the dip tube and the inlet valve. A water heater that seems to be failing may actually be reacting to what the supply lines are sending into it.

## Our Water Heater Repair Process in Wasco

Every repair call starts with a full diagnostic rather than a parts swap. A technician will check the thermostat and thermocouple on gas units, inspect the heating elements and anode rod, test the pressure relief valve, and look for corrosion at the inlet and outlet connections. On older tank units common in Wasco's mid-century homes, we pay particular attention to the dip tube, which can disintegrate and send plastic fragments through the hot water lines.

If sediment is the culprit, we flush the tank completely before reassembling. If the issue is a failed heating element or a faulty gas valve, we carry the most common replacement components on the truck so repairs can be completed in a single visit. When a unit is beyond economical repair, we walk you through replacement options, including tankless systems that handle Wasco's hard water better with the right pre-filtration setup.
