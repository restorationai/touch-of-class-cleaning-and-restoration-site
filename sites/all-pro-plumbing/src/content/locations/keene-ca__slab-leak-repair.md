## Why Keene Properties See Slab Leak Issues

The geology along the Tehachapi Pass corridor is not uniform. Properties closer to the canyon floor deal with soil that can hold moisture after seasonal rains, then dry and contract sharply during the long dry stretches that define this part of Kern County. That cycle of expansion and contraction puts lateral stress on embedded plumbing that flat-terrain homes rarely experience.

Many of the residences in this area were built during mid-century railroad and agricultural expansion, when copper pipe was the standard and installation depth varied widely. Older copper lines are now past their expected service life and are especially vulnerable where the pipe contacts alkaline soil or sits near a point where the slab has micro-settled. Galvanized pipe, where it still exists under older slabs, corrodes from the inside out and can fail with almost no visible warning at grade level.

The Tehachapi Mountains also create localized freeze-thaw cycles at higher elevations that the Bakersfield valley floor rarely sees. Even a few nights of hard frost can stress a slab and the plumbing beneath it in ways that accumulate over years before a leak finally opens.

## Our Slab Leak Detection and Repair Process in Keene

Before any concrete is touched, the source has to be confirmed. We use electronic amplification listening equipment and pressure-isolation testing to narrow the leak to a specific zone, then cross-reference with thermal imaging where floor coverings allow. This matters especially in Keene, where slab thickness and aggregate composition can vary between properties built in different decades, and where a single misread can mean cutting in the wrong location.

Once the leak is located, the repair approach depends on what the pipe looks like and where it runs. A pinhole in an accessible straight run can often be repaired with a targeted saw cut, a sleeve repair, and a concrete patch. A pipe that has corroded across a longer section, or one that runs beneath a load-bearing interior wall, is often a better candidate for rerouting: running a new supply line through the attic or wall cavity and abandoning the compromised under-slab section entirely. Rerouting avoids repeated concrete work and is frequently the more durable long-term solution for older plumbing systems.

We document the location, depth, and condition of the affected pipe before and after repair, which matters when you are filing an insurance claim or need a record for a future property transaction.
