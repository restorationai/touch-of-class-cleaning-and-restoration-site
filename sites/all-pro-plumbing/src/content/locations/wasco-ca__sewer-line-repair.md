## Why Wasco Properties Experience Sewer Line Problems

The San Joaquin Valley's soil profile is the root cause of most sewer failures in this area. Wasco's surrounding farmland relies on heavy irrigation, and that moisture infiltrates residential lots, causing the clay-dominant soil to swell against pipe joints and then shrink away during dry stretches. Over time, that cycle cracks older clay or cast-iron pipe, allows root intrusion from the ornamental trees common in residential neighborhoods, and causes pipe sections to shift out of alignment.

Housing stock in Wasco includes a significant number of homes built in the mid-20th century, when clay vitrified pipe was the standard material for sewer laterals. Those pipes are now well past their design life. A camera inspection often reveals multiple failure points on a single lateral: root masses at joints, a belly or sag where the pipe has settled, and hairline cracks that let groundwater in and sewage out. Addressing one section without inspecting the full line is a short-term fix that usually leads to a repeat call within a year or two.

## Our Sewer Line Repair and Replacement Process in Wasco

Every job starts with a sewer camera inspection. A high-resolution camera travels the full length of the lateral from the cleanout to the city main connection, and the footage is recorded so you can see exactly what we see. That documentation also matters if you are filing an insurance claim or selling the property.

Once we know what we are dealing with, we recommend the least invasive method that will actually solve the problem:

- **Trenchless pipe lining (CIPP):** A resin-saturated liner is pulled through the existing pipe and cured in place, sealing cracks and restoring flow capacity without excavating the yard. This is the right call when the pipe is cracked but structurally intact enough to hold a liner.
- **Trenchless pipe bursting:** The old pipe is fractured outward while a new HDPE pipe is pulled through simultaneously. This works well when the existing line has collapsed sections or severe offset joints that a liner cannot bridge.
- **Open-cut replacement:** When pipe depth, soil conditions, or the extent of collapse makes trenchless methods impractical, we excavate, remove the failed pipe, and install new material to current code. We pull the required permits through Kern County and coordinate any work near the city main with the appropriate utility.

After any repair or replacement, we run a final camera pass to confirm the line is clear, properly graded, and sealed before we close out the job.
