## Why East Niles Properties See Leak Detection Issues

The Kern County clay soils that underlie much of East Niles are classified as expansive, meaning they swell when wet and shrink when dry. Over years of irrigation cycles and seasonal rain, that movement puts lateral stress on underground supply lines and sewer laterals. Galvanized pipe, common in homes built before the 1970s, is especially vulnerable: the zinc coating corrodes from the inside out, narrowing the bore and eventually developing pinhole leaks that are invisible at the surface but detectable with the right equipment.

Hard water is another local factor. The groundwater in this part of Kern County carries high mineral content, and scale buildup inside older copper lines can accelerate pitting corrosion at joints and elbows. A water bill that has risen steadily over several billing cycles, soft spots in a concrete slab, or a patch of lawn that stays green without irrigation are all signs worth investigating before the damage spreads to flooring, framing, or foundation.

## Our Leak Detection Process in East Niles

Finding a hidden leak without tearing open walls or breaking up a slab requires a layered approach. The process typically starts with a pressure test on the supply system to confirm a loss is actually present, then moves to acoustic listening equipment that picks up the frequency of water escaping under pressure. In slab-built homes, which are common throughout East Niles, a thermal imaging camera can map temperature differentials in the concrete that point toward the leak's location before any concrete is touched.

Electronic leak detection adds another layer of precision for underground lines. A tracer gas is introduced into the pipe, and a surface sensor tracks where it escapes, narrowing the excavation zone to a matter of inches rather than feet. That matters in East Niles yards where irrigation systems, gas lines, and electrical conduit often share the same trench corridor. Locating the leak accurately before digging protects those adjacent utilities and keeps the repair scope as small as possible.
