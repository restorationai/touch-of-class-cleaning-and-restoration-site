## Why Arvin Properties See Repiping Issues

Arvin's housing stock skews toward mid-century construction built during the agricultural boom years that shaped Kern County's southern communities. Homes from that era were typically plumbed with galvanized steel pipe, which has a functional lifespan of roughly 40 to 70 years depending on water chemistry. The water delivered through this part of the valley tends to carry higher mineral content, and that hardness accelerates interior pipe scaling and corrosion from the inside out. By the time rust shows up at a tap, the pipe walls are often already compromised across multiple runs, not just at the visible leak point.

Polybutylene pipe is a separate issue that shows up in homes built or replumbed between the late 1970s and mid-1990s. That gray plastic material reacts poorly to the chlorine used in municipal treatment and becomes brittle and prone to sudden failure with age. If your home falls in that construction window and you have not had the pipe material confirmed, it is worth a visual inspection before a failure forces the issue.

Soil movement is a compounding factor in Arvin. Expansive clay soils common to the southern valley shift with seasonal wet and dry cycles, and that ground movement puts lateral stress on pipe runs beneath slabs and in crawlspaces. A pipe already weakened by corrosion does not need much additional stress to split.

## Our Whole-House Repiping Process in Arvin

A whole-house repipe is a multi-day project, and the process matters as much as the materials. The job starts with a full assessment of your existing system: identifying the pipe material in place, mapping the supply runs, and checking where access points are cleanest given your home's wall and ceiling construction. In Arvin's older ranch-style homes, that often means working through stucco exteriors and original drywall, so the access plan is specific to the structure.

Once the scope is confirmed, water service is shut down for the active work days. PEX is the most common choice for repipes in this climate because it handles thermal expansion better than rigid copper in extreme heat, resists scale buildup, and is flexible enough to route through existing wall cavities with fewer access cuts. Copper remains a strong option where local code, HOA rules, or homeowner preference calls for it. After new lines are run and connected, the system is pressure-tested before walls are closed, and a final flow check confirms pressure and volume at every fixture.

Permit requirements for repiping in Kern County apply to this work, and All Pro Plumbing Heating and Air pulls the necessary permits as part of the job. Inspections are scheduled and coordinated so the project moves without unnecessary delays.
