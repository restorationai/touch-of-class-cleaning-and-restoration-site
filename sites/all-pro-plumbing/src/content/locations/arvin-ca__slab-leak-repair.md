## Why Arvin Properties See Slab Leak Issues

The soils around Arvin are a mix of alluvial clay and sandy loam deposited over centuries by runoff from the Tehachapi Mountains to the south. Clay-heavy soil expands when it absorbs moisture and contracts sharply during the dry season. That seasonal movement exerts lateral pressure on the concrete slab itself, which in turn shifts the copper or galvanized supply lines embedded within it. Over years, that repeated flexing work-hardens the pipe walls, eventually producing pinhole leaks or outright fractures.

Arvin's housing stock skews toward mid-century construction, with a significant number of homes built during the agricultural boom of the 1950s through 1970s. Galvanized steel supply lines from that era are well past their expected service life. When galvanized pipe corrodes from the inside out, the scale buildup restricts flow and the outer wall thins until even modest soil movement opens a leak. Copper lines from the same period fare better, but copper is vulnerable to formicary corrosion when soil chemistry is slightly acidic, a condition that can occur near fields where fertilizer applications have altered the ground over decades.

## Our Slab Leak Detection and Repair Process in Arvin

Finding a slab leak without tearing up flooring starts with electronic listening equipment and thermal imaging. A technician pressurizes the suspect line and uses acoustic amplification to isolate the leak point through the slab. Thermal cameras can confirm a hot-water line leak by mapping the heat signature spreading outward from the breach, which shows up clearly against the cooler concrete even in Arvin's warm summers.

Once the leak is located, there are typically three repair paths:

- **Direct access repair**: A targeted saw-cut opens the slab at the confirmed leak point, the damaged section is replaced, and the slab is patched. This works well when the leak is isolated and the rest of the line is in good condition.
- **Pipe rerouting**: If the line is aging throughout or the leak is in a position that makes direct access destructive to finished flooring, the old line is abandoned in place and a new supply line is run through the attic, walls, or crawl space. In Arvin's single-story ranch-style homes, attic reroutes are often the least invasive option.
- **Epoxy pipe lining**: For certain line configurations, an epoxy liner is pulled through the existing pipe, sealing pinhole corrosion without excavation. This is evaluated case by case based on pipe diameter and condition.

The right method depends on the pipe material, the leak location, and what's above the slab. A technician will walk you through the tradeoffs before any work begins.
