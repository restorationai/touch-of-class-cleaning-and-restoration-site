## Why Lamont Properties See Sump Pump Problems

The San Joaquin Valley's hardpan and clay-dominant soils are the root cause of most sump pump calls in this part of Kern County. Unlike sandy soils that drain quickly, clay holds moisture at or just below grade for extended periods. When rain arrives after a long dry stretch, the compacted ground sheds water laterally rather than absorbing it, pushing that water toward foundations, crawl spaces, and below-grade mechanical rooms.

Lamont also sits in an agricultural corridor where irrigation canals and field drainage can raise the local water table temporarily during growing season. Homes and commercial properties near active farmland can experience groundwater intrusion at times of year that feel counterintuitive, well outside the rainy season. A sump pump that only runs in January and February may still be called on in late spring when neighboring fields are being flooded for irrigation.

Older residential construction in the area, much of it built for agricultural workers and their families across several decades, often features minimal waterproofing below the slab. That means the sump pump is frequently the only line of defense between the ground and the interior.

## Our Sump Pump Installation and Repair Process in Lamont

Every job starts with an assessment of where water is entering and why. We check the pit liner, float switch, discharge line, and check valve before recommending a course of action. A sump pump that is not working is not always a failed motor; a stuck float or a frozen discharge line can produce the same symptom.

For new installations, we size the pump to the actual volume of water the pit is likely to see based on the property's grade, soil type, and proximity to drainage infrastructure. In Lamont's clay-soil conditions, we typically recommend a higher-capacity primary pump paired with a battery backup sump pump. Power outages during Kern County storm events are not rare, and a battery backup unit keeps the pit draining even when the grid goes down.

For sewage ejector pump work, below-grade bathrooms or laundry areas that sit lower than the main sewer line require a sealed ejector system rather than a standard sump. We handle both the pump and the discharge piping to the main line, and we test the system under load before we leave the property.

## Local Note

One thing that comes up specifically in Lamont and the surrounding unincorporated Kern County communities is that many properties were built without a dedicated sump pit at all. The assumption in drier decades was that the climate simply did not require one. When those homes are retrofitted, the pit has to be core-drilled through an existing slab, which requires a different tool setup and a longer job window than a new-construction install. If your home has never had a sump pump and you are starting to see seasonal moisture in a crawl space or utility room, the absence of an existing pit is not a barrier; it just means the installation conversation starts a step earlier.

## Equipment and Methods We Use for Sump Pumps

We install submersible and pedestal-style primary pumps depending on pit depth and access. For battery backup sump pump systems, we use units with sealed lead-acid or maintenance-free AGM batteries that hold a charge through extended outages. All discharge lines are run with a check valve to prevent backflow into the pit when the pump cycles off, a step that is sometimes skipped on quick replacements and that causes premature motor wear.

For sewage ejector pump replacements, we match the horsepower rating to the fixture count being served and inspect the basin lid seal, which is the most common source of odor complaints on ejector systems.

If a pump is not working and the unit is relatively new, we diagnose before recommending replacement. A failed float switch, a tripped GFCI outlet, or a blocked impeller can restore a pump to full function without the cost of a new unit.
