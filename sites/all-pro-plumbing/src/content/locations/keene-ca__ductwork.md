If your system is struggling to keep up, the problem is often not the unit itself but the ducts running through unconditioned attic and crawl space cavities where temperature swings can exceed 50 degrees between seasons. ## Why Keene Properties See Ductwork Problems

The geography here works against duct integrity in ways that flatter valley towns never experience. Keene's elevation and position in the Tehachapi Pass means wind-driven pressure differentials push against building envelopes constantly. Flexible duct runs that are perfectly sealed at installation can develop micro-tears at collar connections when a structure flexes under that sustained pressure load. Attic temperatures in summer routinely climb high enough to degrade the foil-and-fiberglass wrap on older duct sections, leaving the inner liner exposed and the insulation value compromised.

The housing stock along this corridor includes a mix of older ranch-style construction and manufactured homes, both of which tend to have long duct runs with multiple bends. Every additional bend is a potential weak point, and in a climate that cycles between freezing winters and scorching summers, those joints work loose over time. The result is conditioned air bleeding into unconditioned space before it ever reaches a register, which shows up as uneven room temperatures and utility bills that keep climbing even when the thermostat stays the same.

## Our Ductwork Repair and Installation Process in Keene

When we arrive at a Keene property, the first step is a thorough inspection rather than a guess. We use pressure testing to measure how much air the duct system is actually losing, which gives us a baseline before any work begins and a benchmark to verify against when the job is done. Leaky duct testing takes the guesswork out of the diagnosis and helps justify the scope of work to a homeowner or property manager who wants to understand what they are paying for.

From there, the process depends on what the inspection reveals. Minor breaches at collar joints and elbow connections are addressed with mastic sealant and reinforced mesh tape, the materials that hold up in temperature-extreme environments far better than standard duct tape ever will. Sections where the duct liner has collapsed or the insulation wrap has deteriorated are replaced outright rather than patched, because a patch on a compromised section rarely lasts a second season in this climate. When a full ductwork installation is the right call, we size the new system to the actual load of the structure, accounting for the ceiling heights, window exposure, and the specific heat gain and loss patterns that come with Keene's mountain-pass location.

## Equipment and Methods We Use for Ductwork

Duct sealing in a climate like Keene's demands materials rated for wide temperature ranges. We apply two-part mastic systems at all connections and use foil-backed insulation wrap with appropriate R-values for attic installations in high-altitude, high-swing environments. For duct replacement, we work with both rigid sheet metal and flexible duct depending on the run geometry, and we support flexible sections at the intervals required to prevent sagging, which is one of the most common causes of airflow restriction in older installations.

## Local Note

One thing that comes up specifically in Keene and the surrounding Tehachapi Pass communities is the effect of sustained wind on crawl space vapor barriers and the duct runs that pass through them. Wind infiltration into a crawl space can displace a vapor barrier enough to expose duct sections to ground moisture, which accelerates corrosion on metal fittings and promotes mold growth on duct insulation. When we inspect ductwork in this area, we make a point of checking the crawl space barrier condition alongside the ducts themselves, because fixing the ducts without addressing that moisture pathway means the repair may not hold through the next wet season.
