## Why Dustin Acres Properties See Emergency Plumbing Issues

The geology and climate here work against residential plumbing in ways that aren't always obvious until something fails. Kern County's expansive clay soils shift measurably between the wet winter months and the dry, scorching summers. That seasonal movement puts lateral stress on underground service lines and the joints where they enter slab foundations. Homes built in the 1970s and 1980s, which make up a large share of the housing stock in this part of Bakersfield's southwest corridor, were often plumbed with galvanized steel or early-generation copper that has now reached or exceeded its service life. Galvanized pipe corrodes from the inside out, so the exterior can look fine while the interior diameter has narrowed to a fraction of its original size, and pressure spikes from the water main can split it without warning.

Hard water is another compounding factor. The water delivered through this part of Kern County carries high mineral content, and scale buildup inside water heater tanks and at valve seats accelerates wear. A water heater that fails catastrophically, flooding a garage or utility room, is a common emergency call in this area, especially in late summer when units have been running hard for months.
