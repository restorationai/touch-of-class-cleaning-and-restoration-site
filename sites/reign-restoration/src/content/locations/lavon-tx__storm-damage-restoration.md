## Why Lavon Properties See Storm Damage

Lavon sits in Collin County on the edge of the Lavon Lake watershed, and that geography shapes how storms hit the area. Severe thunderstorm cells that roll across North Texas tend to intensify as they cross the lake and the open farmland surrounding it, dropping large hail and generating straight-line winds that outbuildings and newer-construction roofs aren't always built to shed. Spring and early summer bring the added risk of tornado-warned supercells moving through Collin County, and when those systems stall, the ground around Lavon, much of it clay-heavy and slow to drain, holds water long enough to push it toward foundations and crawlspaces. Because so much of Lavon has been built out in the last two decades, a lot of homes share the same roofing materials, siding, and fencing from the same production builders, which means one bad hailstorm often damages an entire subdivision at once rather than scattered individual homes.

## Our Storm Damage Restoration Process in Lavon

The first visit is about stopping further loss: tarping open roof sections, boarding broken windows, and extracting any water that's made it past the building envelope. From there we document everything with photos and moisture readings, because storm claims in growing areas like Lavon often involve adjusters handling a backlog of similar claims across the same neighborhood, and a clear, thorough record moves your file faster. Structural drying follows IICRC S500 guidelines, with equipment placement adjusted for slab-on-grade construction, which is common in Lavon's newer subdivisions and dries differently than homes with raised foundations or basements. Once materials read dry, we move into reconstruction, matching roofing, siding, and drywall to current materials wherever the product is still available from the original builder's supplier.
