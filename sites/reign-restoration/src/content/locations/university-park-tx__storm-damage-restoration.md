## Our Storm Damage Restoration Process in University Park

Every job starts with a full-perimeter inspection before anyone touches interior materials. Wind damage is easy to undercount — a missing ridge cap or a lifted flashing seam may look minor from the street but admits gallons of water per hour into wall cavities. We photograph and document every affected surface for insurance purposes before extraction begins.

From there, the process follows the IICRC S500 and S520 standards that govern water and structural drying:

- **Emergency tarping and board-up** to stop active water intrusion at the roof or broken windows
- **Standing water extraction** using truck-mounted and portable units rated for high-volume removal
- **Structural drying** with commercial-grade desiccant dehumidifiers and air movers, monitored by daily moisture readings until materials reach target levels
- **Debris removal and content pack-out** for salvageable furnishings
- **Reconstruction coordination** — framing, drywall, roofing, and finish work managed through a single point of contact

For older University Park homes with original plaster walls, drying timelines run longer than modern drywall construction. Plaster releases moisture slowly and holds it deep in the substrate, so we extend monitoring intervals rather than close out a job prematurely.
