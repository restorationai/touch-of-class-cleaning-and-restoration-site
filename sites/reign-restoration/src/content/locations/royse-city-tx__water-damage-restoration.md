## Why Royse City Properties See Water Damage Issues

Royse City's growth has been explosive — Rockwall County has been one of the fastest-growing counties in Texas for over a decade, and large portions of Royse City's housing stock were built within the last fifteen years on former farmland. Rapid subdivision development means homes built on fill soil that hasn't fully settled, shallow drainage swales that weren't sized for the neighborhood's eventual impervious cover, and builder-grade supply lines that are now approaching the age when they start to fail.

At the same time, older homes closer to the historic downtown core were built on pier-and-beam foundations, which are more forgiving in some ways but create a hidden cavity where standing water can sit undetected for days. The combination of a wet North Texas spring — where Royse City can absorb several inches of rain in a 24-hour period — and clay soils that shed water laterally rather than vertically means that water finds its way into structures from directions homeowners don't expect: through foundation seams, up through slab cracks, and along exterior wall bases.

HVAC condensate drain lines are another frequent culprit. In the long cooling season that runs from April through October, a clogged condensate line can overflow into an attic or ceiling cavity and go unnoticed until drywall begins to sag.

## Our Water Damage Restoration Process in Royse City

The first step on any job is a thorough moisture assessment — not just of the visible wet area, but of adjacent cavities, the subfloor, and wall assemblies. In Royse City's newer construction, engineered wood subfloors and OSB sheathing absorb water quickly and begin to swell and delaminate within 24 to 48 hours, so the scope of affected material is often wider than it looks on the surface.

Once the source is confirmed controlled, water extraction begins using truck-mounted and portable extraction units capable of pulling standing water from carpet, hardwood, and subfloor assemblies. Structural drying follows using commercial-grade desiccant or refrigerant dehumidifiers and high-velocity air movers positioned to create a calculated airflow pattern across wet assemblies. Drying targets are set using psychrometric data — temperature, relative humidity, and material moisture content — and tracked daily against the IICRC S500 standard until all readings reach acceptable dry levels.

Reign Restoration holds IICRC certification as a firm, and the lead technicians carry Water Restoration Technician (WRT) credentials, so the drying plan is documented and defensible for your insurance adjuster.
