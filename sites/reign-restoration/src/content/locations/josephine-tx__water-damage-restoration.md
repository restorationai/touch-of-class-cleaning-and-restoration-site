## Why Josephine Properties See Water Damage Issues

Much of Josephine sits on the same shrink-swell clay that runs through Collin County, and that soil moves with every wet-dry cycle North Texas throws at it. Slab foundations shift slightly over the years, and that movement stresses the PEX and copper lines running under or through the slab, which is a common source of slow leaks that go unnoticed until flooring starts to cup or baseboards darken. Because Josephine has grown quickly with newer subdivisions, a lot of homes share similar plumbing layouts and builder-grade water heaters, so when one unit fails in a neighborhood, we often see the same failure pattern show up next door within a season or two. Heavy spring storms add a second pressure point: fast, hard rain on still-settling lots can overwhelm yard grading before it's fully established, pushing water toward foundations and crawlspaces faster than older, more settled lots nearby.

## Our Water Damage Restoration Process in Josephine

We start with extraction, pulling standing water with truck-mount and portable units so saturated carpet pad and subfloor don't sit any longer than necessary. From there we moisture-map the affected rooms with meters, not guesswork, because a slab home can look dry on the surface while the concrete itself is still holding water that will re-wet flooring if drying stops too early. Air movers and dehumidifiers go in next, sized to the room and the materials involved; a newer home with engineered hardwood over slab dries on a different timeline than one with older carpet and pad, and we adjust equipment placement accordingly. We monitor daily, document moisture readings for the insurance file, and don't pull equipment until the numbers confirm the structure is actually dry, not just dry to the touch.
