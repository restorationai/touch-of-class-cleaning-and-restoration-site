North Texas doesn't ease into severe weather — it arrives fast, and Mesquite sits squarely in the corridor where supercells dropping hail the size of golf balls can roll through with almost no warning. When a storm tears shingles off a roof on a Tuesday afternoon, the structural exposure that follows — wet insulation, compromised decking, water tracking into wall cavities — starts doing damage within hours, not days. Reign Restoration responds to storm damage calls across Mesquite and the surrounding Dallas County communities, working to stop secondary damage before it compounds the original loss.

## Why Mesquite Properties Are Particularly Vulnerable to Storm Damage

Mesquite's housing stock skews heavily toward homes built between the 1960s and 1990s — an era when roof decking was often 3/8-inch plywood rather than today's thicker OSB panels. That older decking delaminates quickly once water penetrates a compromised shingle layer, and what looks like a handful of missing shingles from the street can hide a section of decking that's already soft to the foot. Brick veneer construction — common throughout the city — can also mask water infiltration: water enters at the mortar line, wicks behind the veneer, and saturates wall insulation long before any interior staining appears.

The clay-heavy soils across Dallas County add another layer of risk. After a heavy storm, saturated soil exerts lateral pressure on foundation walls and can channel surface runoff toward slab perimeters rather than away from them. Homes in lower-lying areas of Mesquite that were graded before modern drainage standards can see standing water pooling against the foundation within 30 minutes of a major rain event — a condition that turns a wind-damage call into a combined wind-and-water-intrusion job.

Hail is its own category here. The I-30 corridor through Mesquite is well within the hail belt that insurance adjusters call "Hail Alley," and a single storm can leave hundreds of properties with punctured roof membranes, dented HVAC equipment, and cracked skylights — all of which become water entry points before most homeowners realize the roof was hit.

## Our Storm Damage Restoration Process in Mesquite

Every job starts with a systematic exterior and interior assessment before any restoration work begins. On the exterior, the crew documents shingle loss, flashing separation, fascia damage, and any tree impact points — photographing each area in a format that supports insurance documentation. On the interior, moisture meters and thermal imaging map where water has already traveled, because visible staining rarely tells the full story.

Emergency tarping and board-up happen the same visit when the structure is open to the elements. Wet insulation is removed promptly — saturated fiberglass batts hold moisture against wood framing and accelerate mold colonization; the IICRC S500 standard guides our drying protocols, and we monitor daily until affected assemblies reach target moisture levels. Structural drying typically runs three to five days depending on the depth of penetration and ambient conditions.

Tree impact jobs require an additional step: assessing whether the impact has shifted roof framing or load-bearing members before closing the opening. We coordinate with structural assessors when a tree strike has affected ridge beams or rafters, so the repair scope is accurate before reconstruction begins.

Mesquite sees a meaningful number of homes with original 1970s-era aluminum soffit and fascia systems. It's one of the first things the crew checks on homes from that era — because a small soffit gap during a 60 mph wind event can push several gallons of water into attic insulation before the storm passes.
