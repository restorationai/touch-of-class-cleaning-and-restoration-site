## Why Allen Properties See Repeated Storm Damage

Most of Allen's housing stock was built between roughly 1990 and 2010 — slab-on-grade brick veneer construction that looks solid but has specific vulnerabilities. Brick veneer is a cosmetic layer, not a structural one. When hail or wind-driven debris cracks the mortar or chips the veneer, water finds the gap and migrates behind the brick into the wood framing and OSB sheathing before any interior staining appears. Homeowners near the original town center off Main Street are dealing with an older generation of frame housing that has its own set of issues: original roof decking, aged flashing, and in some cases, no ridge ventilation — meaning moisture that gets in has nowhere to go.

North Texas hail seasons are not a minor inconvenience. The DFW corridor sits inside one of the most active hail corridors in the country, and Allen's ZIP codes 75002 and 75013 see multiple significant hail events most years. Hail that measures 1.5 inches in diameter can puncture standard 3-tab shingles and leave impact fractures in architectural shingles that don't leak immediately — they leak six months later when the next rain hits the same compromised spot.

## Our Storm Damage Restoration Process in Allen

The first thing that happens when we arrive is a full exterior and interior assessment before a single piece of debris is moved. Insurance adjusters need photographic documentation of conditions as they existed at the time of loss — not after a crew has already tarped, hauled, or repositioned materials. We photograph everything systematically, including roof decking, fascia, gutters, window seals, and any visible interior water intrusion, before we begin emergency stabilization.

Emergency tarping and board-up come next, followed by interior moisture mapping using thermal imaging and calibrated moisture meters. Storm damage in Allen rarely stops at the roof — water that enters through a compromised ridge or a cracked soffit travels along ceiling joists and can show up in a bedroom wall twelve feet from the entry point. We trace the moisture path completely rather than treating only the visible wet spot.

Once the structure is stable and dry, structural repairs follow: roof decking replacement, framing repairs, drywall, insulation, and finish work. As an IICRC Certified Firm with technicians holding WRT and FSRT credentials, the drying protocols we follow are documented and defensible — which matters when your adjuster is reviewing the scope of loss.
