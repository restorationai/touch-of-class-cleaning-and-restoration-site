## Why Murphy Properties See Fire Damage Issues

Murphy's housing boom put up a lot of slab-on-grade construction with vaulted ceilings and open-concept kitchens connected directly to living areas. That layout is great for everyday living, but during a fire it lets smoke and heat travel fast, pulling soot into the HVAC trunk lines and distributing it through every room on the system before anyone can shut the unit down. Newer homes also mean more engineered wood, laminate flooring, and synthetic carpet fiber than you'd find in older construction, and those materials produce a denser, more acidic soot that etches into glass, metal fixtures, and appliance finishes within hours if it isn't addressed. Collin County's hot, humid summers compound the problem after the fire is out: water used by firefighters sits in wall cavities and subfloor, and if drying doesn't start quickly, you're dealing with a secondary mold problem on top of the original fire loss.

## Our Fire Damage Restoration Process in Murphy

We start with a damage assessment that separates what's salvageable from what needs to come out, because in open-plan Murphy homes smoke travel often means rooms far from the fire's origin still need cleaning even though they look untouched. Structural drying equipment goes in immediately if firefighting water is present, since Texas humidity gives mold a narrow window to establish itself in wet drywall and insulation. From there we do dry and wet soot removal depending on the material (wood trim gets treated differently than painted drywall or HVAC ductwork), then move into odor control using thermal fogging or hydroxyl generators rather than masking agents, which don't actually break down the smoke compounds trapped in porous surfaces. Reconstruction, when needed, is scoped alongside the cleanup so homeowners aren't juggling two separate contractors for one claim.

## Murphy Insurance Coordination

Most of the subdivisions built in Murphy since the 1990s fall under an HOA, and many of those associations have architectural review requirements for exterior repairs, roofline changes, or siding replacement after fire or smoke damage. We document the loss thoroughly with photos and moisture readings before any demolition starts, which gives homeowners what they need both for the insurance claim and for any HOA submission tied to exterior work. We bill most major carriers directly, so the paperwork side moves alongside the restoration instead of holding it up.

## Local note

One thing we watch for in Murphy specifically: a lot of the newer construction uses foam-core insulation in attics instead of traditional batt insulation, and that material can trap smoke odor and off-gas slowly for weeks if it isn't identified and removed during the initial cleanup. It's an easy thing to miss on a surface walk-through, which is why we check attic insulation type as a standard step on any fire job in this area, not just the ones where the fire started near the roofline.
