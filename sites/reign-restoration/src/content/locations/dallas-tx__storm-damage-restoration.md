Dallas storms don't ease in gradually — a spring supercell can drop baseball-sized hail on Preston Hollow, spin a tornado through Oak Cliff, and flood White Rock Lake's surrounding streets inside the same hour. When that happens, the damage to your roof, siding, windows, and interior isn't just cosmetic. ## Why Dallas Properties See Severe Storm Damage

Dallas sits in one of the most active severe-weather corridors in North America. Hail seasons peak in April and May, and the city averages more hail events per year than almost anywhere in the country — insurance adjusters here call it "hail alley" for a reason. But the physical makeup of Dallas neighborhoods shapes how that damage plays out on the ground.

East Dallas neighborhoods like Lakewood, the M Streets, and Casa Linda are built on flat Blackland clay — expansive soil that swells when saturated and shrinks in drought. Homes here are often 1920s–1940s pier-and-beam construction with original cast-iron drains and wood-framed walls that absorb moisture faster than modern assemblies. A storm that punches through a roof or breaks a window doesn't just wet the surface; it saturates wall cavities that were never designed to be dried with mechanical equipment.

West of the escarpment, Oak Cliff's rolling terrain channels runoff differently. Streets in the Bishop Arts District and surrounding blocks can sheet-flood in under 20 minutes during a fast-moving storm, pushing water under doors and into finished basements before residents have time to react. Meanwhile, Lake Highlands' 1960s slab ranches face a different problem: when hail or wind strips flashing and shingles, water infiltrates the slab perimeter and wicks up through the foundation edge — damage that doesn't show up on the ceiling until days later.

## Our Storm Damage Restoration Process in Dallas

Every storm job starts with a thorough damage assessment before any material is moved or covered. We photograph and document the full scope — roof penetrations, window breaches, interior water intrusion, and any structural compromise — because that documentation is the foundation of your insurance claim. Skipping this step, or letting an unlicensed crew tarp and patch before a proper assessment, is one of the most common reasons Dallas homeowners end up undercompensated.

From there, the process follows a clear sequence:

1. **Emergency stabilization** — tarping exposed roof decking, boarding broken windows and doors, and extracting any standing water before it migrates further into wall assemblies or flooring.
2. **Moisture mapping** — thermal imaging and calibrated moisture meters identify hidden saturation in walls, ceilings, and subfloor cavities that visual inspection misses.
3. **Structural drying** — IICRC-certified drying plans use commercial desiccant and refrigerant dehumidifiers alongside high-velocity air movers, calibrated to the specific material assemblies present (pier-and-beam subfloors dry differently than poured slabs).
4. **Debris and tree removal coordination** — when a downed tree has breached the structure, we coordinate safe removal and shore up the opening before weather can compound the damage.
5. **Reconstruction** — once the structure is dry and stable, we move into repairs: roofing, framing, drywall, flooring, and finish work, all under one contractor relationship.
