## Why Farmersville Properties See Water Damage Issues

Most of Farmersville's housing stock sits on concrete slab foundations, a near-universal choice in this part of North Texas, and slab leaks are one of the most common water intrusion calls we get from the area. Expansive clay soil swells after heavy spring rain and contracts hard during summer drought, and that constant movement stresses the plumbing lines running through or under the slab until a joint finally gives. Add in older galvanized supply lines still present in some of the town's original homes near downtown, and you get a pattern of slow leaks that go undetected until flooring starts to cup or a utility bill spikes. Farmersville's position outside the Dallas-Fort Worth storm sewer network also means heavier rain events can overwhelm drainage around older properties faster than in more developed suburbs, pushing water toward foundations instead of away from them.

## Our Water Damage Restoration Process in Farmersville

Water extraction starts the moment we're on site, using truck-mounted and portable extraction equipment to pull standing water before it migrates into wall cavities. From there, we map moisture with meters and thermal imaging, which matters more here than in homes on pier-and-beam construction, because a slab leak can push water laterally under flooring for feet in every direction before it surfaces anywhere visible. Structural drying equipment, air movers and commercial dehumidifiers, is placed based on that moisture map rather than guesswork, and we track drying progress daily against the IICRC S500 standard until materials read dry, not just damp-looking. For confirmed slab leaks, we coordinate with a licensed plumber for leak location and repair before we close out the drying phase, since re-wetting from an unrepaired leak undoes the mitigation work.
