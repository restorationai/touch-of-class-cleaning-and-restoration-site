## Why Garland Homes and Commercial Properties Often Need More Than a Patch

North Texas clay soil expands when it gets wet and contracts in drought — a cycle that Garland homeowners know well after every summer. That movement stresses slab foundations, which in turn cracks interior tile, buckles hardwood floors, and pulls drywall seams apart. A kitchen or bathroom that looks like a cosmetic problem is sometimes sitting on a foundation that shifted a quarter-inch or more. Before any renovation scope is finalized, we assess the structural baseline so the finished remodel doesn't crack again in two years.

Garland's older neighborhoods also carry a mix of original single-pane windows, knob-and-tube or aluminum wiring in some pre-1970 homes, and galvanized supply lines that corrode from the inside out. A bathroom remodel that opens up walls often surfaces one or more of these issues. Knowing what's typical in this housing stock means we budget and schedule for it rather than treating it as a surprise change order.

The North Texas heat is a scheduling factor too. Interior finish work — paint, flooring adhesives, caulk — performs differently when a house has been sitting at 100°F through August. We sequence work to control temperature and humidity inside the structure, which matters especially for hardwood acclimation and tile setting.

## Our Renovation and General Contracting Process in Garland

Every project starts with a scoped walkthrough, not a ballpark estimate over the phone. For post-damage rebuilds, we pull the adjuster's scope and reconcile it against actual field conditions — insurance line items are written from photos, and photos don't always catch what's behind drywall or under subfloor.

From there, the process moves through permitting, material selection, and a written construction schedule. Garland falls under the City of Garland's Building Inspection Division, which requires permits for structural work, electrical, plumbing, and HVAC changes. We handle the permit applications and schedule inspections so the homeowner isn't managing that process on top of an already stressful situation.

Subcontractors we bring to Garland jobs are vetted and carry their own licensure and insurance. Electrical and plumbing work on permitted jobs is performed by licensed tradespeople — not general laborers. That matters for final inspection sign-off and for your homeowner's insurance coverage going forward.

## Coordinating with Insurance on Post-Damage Rebuilds in Garland

A significant portion of Reign Restoration's Garland work comes directly from water, fire, or mold losses where the remediation is complete and the property needs to be rebuilt to pre-loss condition. In those cases, we work from the insurance carrier's scope of loss and supplement it where the field conditions justify additional line items.

We document everything with photos, moisture readings, and written notes that match the format adjusters expect. If a supplement is warranted — say, the original flooring material is discontinued and a comparable substitute costs more — we provide the documentation to support that conversation with the carrier. Homeowners should not have to negotiate that alone.

## Local Note

Garland's older neighborhoods near downtown, built largely between the 1940s and 1960s, frequently used a thicker lath-and-plaster wall construction that predates standard drywall. When renovation work opens those walls — for a bathroom remodel, a kitchen expansion, or post-water-damage rebuild — the plaster requires different handling than modern sheetrock. It's heavier, it generates significantly more debris, and matching the texture on a partial repair is difficult enough that a full-room skim coat is often the cleaner solution. Crews that don't work in older Dallas County housing stock regularly sometimes underbid the demo and disposal on these jobs. We factor it in from the start.
