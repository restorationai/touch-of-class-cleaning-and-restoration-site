## Why Frisco Homes Create Specific Contents Challenges

Frisco's rapid growth from farmland to one of the fastest-expanding cities in North Texas produced a housing stock that is almost entirely slab-on-grade brick veneer construction, most of it built between 1998 and today. That design pattern has a few consequences that matter when contents are involved in a loss.

First, attic-mounted air handlers are standard in these homes. When a condensate drain clogs or a supply line fails at the air handler, water follows ceiling drywall and insulation down into finished living space — and it moves fast across open floor plans. By the time a homeowner in Phillips Creek Ranch or Hollyhock notices discoloration on a ceiling, the water has often already reached furniture, rugs, and built-in cabinetry below.

Second, Frisco sits on Houston Black clay, one of the most expansive soils in the country. Seasonal swelling and shrinking creates slab movement that can stress PEX and copper supply runs at joints over time. A slow pinhole leak behind a wall can go undetected for weeks, allowing secondary mold colonization — which begins within 24 to 48 hours of moisture exposure — to reach contents stored in adjacent closets or built-in shelving.

Third, Frisco's newer neighborhoods like Hollyhock and Edgestone at Legacy tend to have open-concept layouts with fewer interior walls, which means smoke and soot from even a contained kitchen fire can coat contents across a much larger square footage than in a compartmentalized older home.

## Our Contents Pack-Out and Restoration Process in Frisco

Every contents job begins with a detailed room-by-room inventory before a single item leaves your home. We photograph, barcode, and log each piece — furniture, electronics, clothing, décor, documents — so you always know what we have and where it is. That documentation also feeds directly into your insurance claim file.

From there, the process breaks into three tracks depending on what each item needs:

- **Pack-out and climate-controlled storage**: Items that need to leave the loss environment immediately are packed, transported, and stored in a secure, climate-controlled facility. Frisco's summer heat — routinely above 100°F from June through August — can accelerate off-gassing from smoke-damaged materials and warp wood furniture if items sit in a non-climate-controlled environment, so temperature management during storage is not optional.
- **Contents cleaning and deodorization**: Smoke residue, soot, and water-borne contamination require different cleaning chemistries. Dry soot from a fast-burning fire behaves differently than wet soot from a smoldering fire; protein residue from a kitchen fire requires enzyme-based treatment. Our IICRC FSRT-certified technicians match the cleaning method to the residue type rather than applying a single process to everything.
- **Specialty item handling**: Electronics, artwork, documents, and antiques follow separate protocols. Water-damaged documents, for example, can sometimes be freeze-dried to halt further deterioration — a step that matters for homeowners near Frisco Heritage Museum-caliber heirlooms or business records.

Once your home's structural repairs are complete, we coordinate return delivery and assist with placement so the process closes cleanly.
