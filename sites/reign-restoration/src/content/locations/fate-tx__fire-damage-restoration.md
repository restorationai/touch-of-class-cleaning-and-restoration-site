## Why Fate's Housing Stock Shapes Fire Damage Outcomes

Most of Fate's residential growth has happened in master-planned communities built between roughly 2008 and the present, using engineered lumber, OSB sheathing, and open floor plans. These construction choices are efficient but they create a specific fire-damage pattern: engineered lumber loses structural integrity faster under heat than dimensional lumber, OSB delaminates when wet during suppression, and open floor plans let smoke and soot migrate to every room before firefighters can contain the blaze. That means even a contained kitchen fire can leave smoke odor and fine particulate in bedrooms on the opposite side of the house.

Fate also sits in Rockwall County, where summer temperatures regularly push past 100°F. Heat-soaked attic spaces in the weeks after a fire can slow the off-gassing of smoke compounds from insulation and roof decking, making odor control a longer process than it would be in a cooler climate. Addressing that requires thermal fogging and hydroxyl treatment calibrated to the ambient conditions — not just a single pass with an ozone machine.

## Our Fire and Smoke Restoration Process in Fate

Every fire job starts with a structured assessment before any cleaning begins. The crew walks the property to document structural fire damage, map the soot migration path, and identify materials that can be restored versus those that need to be removed. Photographs and moisture readings are taken at this stage — both for the insurance claim and to establish a baseline for the work ahead.

From there, the process moves in a deliberate sequence:

- **Board-up and tarping** to secure the structure and prevent weather intrusion through compromised rooflines or windows
- **Debris removal** of charred materials that cannot be salvaged
- **Dry cleaning and wet cleaning of surfaces** using chemistry matched to the soot type — protein-based soot from kitchen fires responds differently than petroleum-based soot from synthetic materials
- **Odor elimination** using thermal fogging and, where needed, hydroxyl generators that can run safely in occupied adjacent spaces
- **Structural drying** if suppression water is present, following the IICRC S500 standard
- **Scope documentation** for your adjuster, itemizing affected materials and recommended repairs

Post-fire restoration in Fate's newer construction often requires coordination with the original builder's warranty documentation, since some structural components carry manufacturer specs that affect how repairs are permitted and documented.
