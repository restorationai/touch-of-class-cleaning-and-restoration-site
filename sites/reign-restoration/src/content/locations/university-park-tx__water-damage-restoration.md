## Why University Park Properties See Water Damage Differently

University Park was largely built out by the 1940s, incorporated as its own municipality in 1924, and has been quietly rebuilding ever since. That mix creates real complications when water intrudes. Pier-and-beam homes have open crawl spaces where moisture accumulates under the floor deck and accelerates wood rot and mold colonization — mold can begin establishing colonies within 24 to 48 hours of a water event if conditions are humid and warm, which Dallas-area summers guarantee. Slab homes, on the other hand, concentrate water damage in wall assemblies and flooring rather than beneath the structure, but slab leaks from aging copper supply lines are common in homes built before 1970.

## Our Water Damage Restoration Process in University Park

Every job starts with a moisture mapping inspection. We use thermal imaging cameras and calibrated moisture meters to trace water migration through walls, under flooring, and into ceiling assemblies — because water follows gravity and framing cavities, not the path you'd expect from looking at a wet floor. In University Park's older homes, that often means water has traveled farther than the visible stain suggests.

From there, the process moves through four stages:

1. **Water extraction** — truck-mounted and portable extractors pull standing water from hard and soft surfaces. In pier-and-beam homes, we access the crawl space to extract pooled water before it saturates the subfloor from below.
2. **Structural drying** — high-capacity axial and centrifugal air movers combined with commercial-grade desiccant or refrigerant dehumidifiers create a controlled drying environment. We calibrate equipment placement to the specific wall and floor assemblies present, which differ significantly between a 1940s plaster-and-lath interior and a 2010s drywall build.
3. **Monitoring** — we return daily to record moisture readings and adjust equipment. Drying is not complete until structural materials reach documented equilibrium moisture content, following the IICRC S500 standard.
4. **Controlled demolition if needed** — when wall cavities or subfloor panels cannot dry in place, we perform targeted removal to expose wet framing and insulation. We document every step for your insurance claim.

Reign Restoration holds IICRC certification as a firm and carries IICRC WRT (Water Restoration Technician) credentials, so the drying plan is based on psychrometric science, not guesswork.
