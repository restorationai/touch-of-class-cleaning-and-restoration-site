When a burst pipe soaks through the hardwood floors of a Canyon Creek ranch house — or a kitchen fire leaves smoke residue embedded in the upholstered furniture of a Cottonwood Heights split-level — the damage to your belongings can feel just as devastating as the structural loss. Contents restoration and storage is the process of getting those items out safely, cleaning and restoring what can be saved, and holding everything in a climate-controlled facility while your home is being repaired. Reign Restoration handles that entire chain for Richardson homeowners and property managers, from the first pack-out to final delivery.

## Why Richardson Properties See Elevated Contents Damage

Richardson's housing stock tells the story. Richardson Heights, Canyon Creek, and Cottonwood Heights were built out between the 1950s and early 1980s, and a large share of those homes still sit on original cast-iron or clay drain lines running beneath the slab. When those lines fail — and in a hot, dry summer followed by a wet spring, they fail more often than most homeowners expect — water migrates under finished flooring and wicks upward into baseboards, cabinetry, and the furniture sitting against them. By the time a homeowner notices discoloration or odor, contents have already been exposed for hours.

The Houston Black clay soil threading under Duck Creek and Prairie Creek corridors compounds the problem. That reactive clay expands when saturated and contracts in drought, and the resulting slab movement can crack supply lines inside walls with very little warning. A slow leak behind a kitchen wall can go undetected for weeks, meaning furniture, electronics, artwork, and clothing in adjacent rooms absorb elevated humidity long before visible water appears.

Fire losses carry their own Richardson-specific wrinkle. The older wood-frame construction common in the 75081 and 75080 ZIP codes burns differently than newer builds — smoke travels farther through open attic cavities and settles into contents rooms that were never directly exposed to flame. That means a garage fire can leave a fine, oily soot film on clothing and linens two rooms away.

## Our Contents Pack-Out and Restoration Process

The process begins with a room-by-room inventory before anything is moved. Every item is photographed, catalogued, and assigned a condition rating — this documentation matters both for your insurance claim and for ensuring nothing gets lost or misattributed during restoration.

From there, contents move to a controlled facility where each category gets the appropriate treatment:

- **Soft goods** (clothing, bedding, drapery): ultrasonic or ozone-assisted cleaning to remove smoke particulates and microbial contamination from water exposure
- **Hard goods and furniture**: surface cleaning, deodorization, and where applicable, refinishing or reupholstering coordination
- **Electronics**: inspection and dry-out before any power-on testing; referral to certified electronics restorers for complex items
- **Documents and photographs**: freeze-drying or air-drying protocols to stabilize paper before mold colonization can take hold — mold can begin developing within 24 to 48 hours on wet paper in Texas summer humidity

Items that cannot be immediately restored are held in climate-controlled storage. You receive an itemized list and can request access or updates at any stage.

## Insurance Coordination for Richardson Losses

Most homeowner policies in Texas cover sudden and accidental water or fire losses, and contents are typically a named coverage category. Reign documents every item before it leaves your property — photographs, condition notes, and replacement-value estimates — in a format that aligns with what adjusters need to process a contents claim. That documentation reduces back-and-forth and helps avoid the situation where a homeowner is asked to reconstruct a list of damaged items from memory weeks after the loss.

If your policy includes an ALE (Additional Living Expense) provision, the cost of contents storage during your repair period may be covered. Ask your adjuster specifically about that line item — it is frequently overlooked on residential claims in this area.

## Local Note

Homes built in Richardson Heights and Canyon Creek during the 1960s and 1970s frequently have original hardwood floors over a concrete slab with no vapor barrier between them. When water gets under those floors, it does not just damage the wood — it creates a trapped humid environment directly beneath furniture legs and area rugs. Contents sitting on the floor in those rooms absorb significantly more moisture than the same items would in a newer build with a raised subfloor. During pack-out in these homes, Reign's crew checks the underside of furniture and the backs of lower cabinetry before cataloguing condition, because damage that looks cosmetic on the surface is often more extensive underneath.
