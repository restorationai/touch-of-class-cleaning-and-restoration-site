Caddo Mills is growing fast as Hunt County families move east out of the Dallas metro, and that growth is colliding with a housing stock that ranges from century-old farmhouses near the original town square to new builds going up along FM 36. Renovating in this mix means knowing when a wall is load-bearing original lumber versus 2010s framing, and when a kitchen remodel is really a rewire job in disguise.

## Why Caddo Mills Properties Need a Different Renovation Approach

Hunt County's blackland prairie soil shifts with every wet-dry cycle, and older Caddo Mills homes built before slab-on-grade became standard often show it in cracked tile, sticking doors, and uneven bathroom floors. A remodel that doesn't account for this movement tends to crack again within a year or two. We also see a fair number of properties where a previous owner's DIY addition was never permitted, which surfaces during a kitchen or bathroom remodel when we open a wall and find wiring or plumbing that won't pass a current Hunt County inspection. Part of our scope on these jobs is flagging that early, before it becomes a surprise at final walkthrough.

## Our Renovation and General Contracting Process in Caddo Mills

We start with a walkthrough that separates cosmetic work from structural and code concerns, which matters more here than in newer subdivisions because so much of the older housing stock predates current electrical and plumbing code. From there we pull permits through the appropriate Hunt County or city process depending on the address, sequence trades so a kitchen remodel doesn't sit half-finished while we wait on a sub, and handle the inspections before we close up walls. For post-damage rebuilds, where a remodel is really the second half of a restoration claim, our crews carry the IICRC-trained background from our water, fire, and mold work, so the same team that documented the loss can frame the plan to put the room back together correctly instead of handing it off cold to a separate contractor.

## Caddo Mills Insurance Coordination for Rebuild Work

When a renovation follows a covered loss, fire, water, or storm damage, we document scope and materials in a way that lines up with how most carriers want to see a rebuild estimate, which cuts down on the back-and-forth that slows a project down. We're a licensed general contractor (#MRC2276), so the same entity that handles mitigation can carry the rebuild through to final punch list without a second contract or a second set of insurance paperwork.

## Local note

One thing we watch for in Caddo Mills specifically: a lot of the older lots in and around town were platted before municipal water expanded out from the city core, so some properties still run on well water or septic even with in-town addresses. That changes how we plan a bathroom remodel, since moving a fixture or adding a second bath can mean a septic capacity check before demo ever starts, not after.
