## Why Garland Properties Face Elevated Water Damage Risk

Garland's housing stock spans several decades, and that range matters when water gets into a structure. Neighborhoods developed in the 1960s and 1970s — which make up a significant portion of the city's residential core — were built with galvanized steel supply lines that are now well past their service life. Galvanized pipe corrodes from the inside out, narrowing the bore and developing pinhole leaks that can saturate wall cavities for weeks before a homeowner notices a soft spot in the drywall or a spike on the water bill.

The soil is another factor that rarely gets discussed. Garland sits on expansive clay — the same shrink-swell geology that plagues most of Dallas County. During dry stretches, the clay contracts and creates gaps along foundation walls and slab penetrations. When heavy rain follows a dry spell, water channels directly through those gaps into crawl spaces and slab edges before the soil has time to re-expand and seal. That cycle repeats every season and gradually works water into places that look dry on the surface.

Finally, Garland's flat topography means storm runoff has nowhere fast to go. Low-lying streets and properties near creek drainages can see sheet flooding during heavy convective storms that outpaces the city's storm infrastructure.

## Our Water Damage Restoration Process in Garland

Every job starts with a thorough moisture assessment — not just the visible wet area, but the surrounding materials. Thermal imaging cameras and calibrated moisture meters map how far water has migrated inside wall assemblies, under flooring, and into subfloor sheathing. In Garland's older ranch-style homes, water from a supply-line failure in a hall bathroom can travel along the slab and appear under kitchen cabinets 20 feet away.

Once the scope is mapped, water extraction comes first. Truck-mounted and portable extractors pull standing water and saturated material moisture down to a level where structural drying equipment can take over. We then set a calculated drying system — LGR dehumidifiers, high-velocity air movers, and desiccant units where needed — sized to the affected square footage and material types. Drying targets follow the IICRC S500 standard, and we monitor psychrometric readings daily until every reading confirms the structure has returned to normal moisture levels for North Texas conditions.

Documentation runs in parallel throughout: photos, moisture logs, and equipment placement records that your insurance adjuster can use directly.

## Garland Insurance Coordination

Most standard homeowner policies cover sudden and accidental water damage — a burst pipe, an appliance supply line failure, or wind-driven rain entering through storm damage. What they typically exclude is gradual leakage, so the documentation we produce at the start of a job matters. Clear photographic evidence of the failure point, the affected materials, and the moisture readings establishes that the loss was sudden, not a slow drip that went unaddressed.

Reign Restoration works directly with adjusters, provides itemized scope documents formatted for Xactimate review, and can answer technical questions about drying methodology and equipment logs. We do not steer you toward or away from a claim — that decision is yours — but we make sure the paperwork supports the strongest honest case for coverage.
