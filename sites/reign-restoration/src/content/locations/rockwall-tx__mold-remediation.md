## Why Rockwall Properties See Mold Issues

The same water feature that makes Rockwall one of the most desirable addresses in Kaufman and Rockwall counties also keeps relative humidity elevated for weeks at a stretch. Mold spores need only 24 to 48 hours and a moisture source to begin colonizing porous materials — drywall paper, wood framing, carpet backing, and HVAC insulation are all common targets here.

Rockwall's housing stock adds another layer of complexity. The city grew rapidly through the 1990s and 2000s, meaning a large share of homes use OSB (oriented strand board) sheathing and paper-faced drywall — materials that absorb water quickly and sustain mold growth efficiently once wet. Older neighborhoods closer to the historic downtown square tend to have pier-and-beam foundations with crawl spaces that trap ground moisture, while newer subdivisions near the lake often have slab foundations where slab-edge leaks go undetected for months. Both scenarios create the slow, hidden moisture conditions that mold thrives on.

HVAC systems also play an outsized role. In Rockwall's climate, air conditioners run hard from April through October. When a refrigerant line sweats or a condensate drain clogs — both common issues in high-humidity environments — water can drip into air handler closets and spread mold spores through ductwork to every room in the house.

## Our Mold Remediation Process in Rockwall

Every job starts with a thorough moisture mapping inspection. Using thermal imaging cameras and calibrated moisture meters, the crew identifies not just the visible mold colony but the hidden wet zones feeding it. In Rockwall homes, that often means checking inside exterior wall cavities on the lake-facing side of the structure, under kitchen and bathroom slab penetrations, and inside HVAC air handler closets.

Once the moisture source is confirmed and addressed, the team establishes negative-air containment — sealing off the affected area with 6-mil poly barriers and running HEPA-filtered negative air machines to prevent cross-contamination to clean areas of the home. This step is not optional; without proper containment, remediation activity can aerosolize spores and spread the problem.

Affected porous materials — drywall, insulation, subfloor sections — are removed and bagged for disposal per Texas Department of State Health Services guidelines. Structural members that can be saved are HEPA-vacuumed, treated with an EPA-registered antimicrobial, and dried to confirmed moisture levels before any reconstruction begins. The IICRC AMRT (Applied Microbial Remediation Technician) standard governs the entire workflow, from initial assessment through final clearance.

## Local Note

One pattern the crew sees repeatedly in Rockwall's lakeside and near-lake neighborhoods: homes built in the late 1990s and early 2000s often have a thin layer of stucco or EIFS (exterior insulation and finish system) cladding on the front elevation. When that cladding develops small cracks — common after years of North Texas freeze-thaw cycles — water infiltrates behind the system and sits against the OSB sheathing with nowhere to drain. By the time mold appears on an interior wall, the sheathing behind the EIFS may already be significantly compromised. Reign's inspectors probe these cladding systems as a standard step on any Rockwall job where the exterior finish matches that profile.
