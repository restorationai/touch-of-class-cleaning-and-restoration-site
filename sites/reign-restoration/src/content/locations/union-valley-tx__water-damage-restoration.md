## Why Union Valley Properties See Water Damage Issues

Much of Hunt County sits on Blackland Prairie clay, soil that swells when saturated and shrinks hard during dry stretches. That cycle stresses slab foundations and the plumbing runs underneath them, which is a common source of slow leaks that go undetected until flooring starts to cup or baseboards darken. Rural and semi-rural properties around Union Valley also tend to rely on well systems or private septic, and a failed pressure tank or a backed-up line can put standing water into a crawlspace or utility room with little warning. Add in older homes built before PEX plumbing became standard, and galvanized or early copper lines are more likely to pinhole and fail without notice.

## Our Water Damage Restoration Process in Union Valley

We start by identifying the water category and the materials affected, since that determines how we approach extraction and drying under the IICRC S500 standard. For slab homes, we're checking for water that's migrated under flooring or into wall cavities from the base up; for pier-and-beam and crawlspace construction, we're also assessing floor joists and subfloor for saturation that isn't visible from inside the living space. Extraction comes first with truck-mount or portable pumps depending on access, followed by placement of air movers and dehumidifiers sized to the actual cubic footage of the affected area, not a flat number. We monitor moisture content in framing and flooring daily with meters, not guesswork, and we don't call a space dry until readings match the unaffected baseline in the home.

Most of the water losses we see in this part of Hunt County come from plumbing failures or appliance malfunctions rather than flood events, which generally fall under a standard homeowner's policy rather than separate flood coverage. ## Local note

One thing we watch for specifically in this area: homes on well water that lose a pressure tank often flood a utility closet or garage slowly over days rather than all at once, since the pump keeps cycling and pushing small amounts of water out with each failed cycle. By the time it's noticed, the subfloor or slab underneath has often been wet far longer than the visible puddle suggests, which changes how aggressively we need to dry it.
