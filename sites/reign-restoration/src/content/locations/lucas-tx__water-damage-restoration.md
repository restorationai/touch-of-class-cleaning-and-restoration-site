## Why Lucas Properties See Water Damage Issues

Most homes in Lucas sit on large, minimum-acreage lots, and a meaningful share rely on private well or septic systems rather than full municipal service. That changes how we approach a loss. A supply line failure near a septic drain field needs careful handling so extraction water and surface runoff don't overload the field or push contaminated water back toward the house. Add in North Texas's pattern of long dry stretches followed by heavy spring rain, and you get soil that contracts and swells enough to crack slabs, separate plumbing joints, and open gaps around foundation penetrations where groundwater can intrude after a hard storm.

## Our Water Damage Restoration Process in Lucas

We start with moisture mapping, not guesswork, using meters to find out how far water has traveled under flooring and behind walls before we touch a single piece of equipment. On the larger custom homes typical of Lucas, that often means checking multiple zones: a kitchen or primary bath on one end of the house and a detached garage, guest quarters, or outbuilding on the other, since a single water event can affect structures that aren't directly connected. Extraction comes next, pulling standing water from floors, carpet pad, and any crawlspace access points. Then we set containment and air movers calibrated to the specific materials involved; slab homes on clay soil dry differently than pier-and-beam construction, and we adjust drying time and dehumidification targets accordingly, following IICRC S500 drying standards rather than a fixed timeline. We document moisture readings daily until the structure reads dry, which is also what your insurance carrier will want to see.
