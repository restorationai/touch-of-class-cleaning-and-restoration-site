## Why Wylie Properties See Mold Issues

Collin County sits in a humid subtropical climate zone where summer dew points regularly climb above 70°F. That moisture-laden air doesn't stay outside — it infiltrates attics, crawl spaces, and wall assemblies every time a door opens or a building envelope has even a minor gap. Wylie's housing stock skews heavily toward homes built between 2000 and 2020, and that era of construction relied heavily on OSB (oriented strand board) sheathing and paper-faced drywall — both of which are highly susceptible to mold colonization when moisture content climbs above 19 percent.

Irrigation systems are nearly universal in Wylie's subdivisions, and improperly graded lots can direct sprinkler runoff toward foundation walls rather than away from them. Over time, that repeated wetting of the slab perimeter wicks moisture into lower wall cavities — exactly the kind of slow, hidden saturation that goes unnoticed until a musty odor or visible staining finally prompts a call.

## Our Mold Remediation Process in Wylie

Every job begins with a thorough moisture mapping inspection. Using thermal imaging and calibrated moisture meters, the crew identifies not just the visible mold but the wet building materials feeding it. Skipping this step is the single most common reason mold returns after a surface cleaning — the source stays wet, and the colony regrows.

Once the scope is confirmed, the affected area is sealed off with negative-air containment barriers and HEPA-filtered air scrubbers running continuously. This keeps spores from migrating into clean areas of the home during removal. Contaminated drywall, insulation, and framing are removed to clean margins — not just wiped down — and treated surfaces are HEPA-vacuumed and wiped with an EPA-registered antimicrobial agent. Structural drying follows to bring all remaining materials to acceptable moisture levels before any reconstruction begins. The entire process follows the IICRC S520 Standard for Professional Mold Remediation.
