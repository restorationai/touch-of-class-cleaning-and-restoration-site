## Our Water Damage Restoration Process in Richardson

When we arrive, the first priority is stopping the source — shutting the main, isolating the affected zone, or coordinating with the homeowner on utility shutoff. From there, the process follows a documented sequence calibrated to what we actually find in Richardson homes.

**Moisture mapping** comes before any equipment is placed. We use thermal imaging cameras and penetrating moisture meters to trace how far water has traveled inside wall cavities, under flooring, and into the slab edge. In slab-on-grade homes — which describes the majority of the housing stock in Richardson Heights and Canyon Creek — water migrates laterally under tile and hardwood in ways that aren't visible from the surface.

**Extraction** removes standing water first, then residual moisture from carpet pad, subfloor, and cabinetry. High-efficiency truck-mount and portable extractors handle both categories. In homes with original hardwood over slab, we assess whether the floor can be dried in place or whether cupping has already progressed too far — that decision affects the entire drying timeline.

**Structural drying** uses a calculated combination of commercial-grade LGR dehumidifiers and high-velocity air movers positioned to create a drying chamber around affected materials. Drying targets are set per the IICRC S500 standard and monitored with daily moisture readings until affected materials reach acceptable equilibrium. In Richardson's humid summers, ambient conditions can slow drying significantly — equipment staging and air exchange are adjusted accordingly.

**Documentation** runs parallel to every step. We photograph affected areas, log moisture readings, and produce a scope of work that your insurance adjuster can act on directly.
