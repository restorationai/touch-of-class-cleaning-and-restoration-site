## Why Allen Properties See Water Damage Differently

Allen's housing stock tells a specific story. The vast majority of homes here were built between roughly 1990 and 2010 — a window when slab construction dominated Collin County and builders favored brick veneer over wood siding. That combination creates a particular water damage pattern: moisture that enters through a roof penetration, a failed flashing, or a plumbing failure doesn't drain through a crawl space. It pools on the slab, migrates laterally through flooring adhesive, and climbs wall cavities.

The Rowlett Creek and Cottonwood Creek corridors compound the risk in subdivisions like Twin Creeks and Bethany Lakes. Wide drainage easements behind those 1990s lots manage storm volume, but during heavy North Texas rain events — the kind that drop three inches in two hours — the easements can back up and push water toward rear foundations. Homes along those corridors see a disproportionate share of the crawl-space-adjacent flooding calls we receive in the 75002 and 75013 ZIP codes.

North Texas clay soil adds another layer. Expansive clay shifts with moisture, and repeated wet-dry cycles around a slab perimeter can open hairline cracks at the foundation-to-wall joint — a subtle entry point that goes unnoticed until a heavy rain season reveals itself as a wet utility closet or a damp master bath wall.

## Our Water Damage Restoration Process in Allen

Every job begins with a moisture mapping inspection — thermal imaging and calibrated moisture meters across all affected rooms, not just the visible wet area. In Allen's slab homes, water routinely travels six to ten feet from the source before it shows up on a surface, so we map the full perimeter before any equipment is placed.

From there, the process moves in a defined sequence:

1. **Water extraction** — truck-mounted extractors pull standing water and saturated carpet padding within the first hour on site.
2. **Controlled demolition** — baseboard removal, selective drywall cuts at the flood line, and flooring removal where moisture readings confirm saturation. We photograph and document every material removed for your insurance claim.
3. **Structural drying** — commercial LGR dehumidifiers and high-velocity air movers are positioned to dry the slab surface, wall cavities, and subfloor simultaneously. Drying typically runs three to five days, with daily moisture readings logged per the IICRC S500 standard.
4. **Antimicrobial treatment** — applied to all exposed framing and concrete before any reconstruction begins.
5. **Clearance verification** — final moisture readings confirm all materials are within acceptable ranges before walls close.

We handle direct insurance billing with most major carriers, documenting the loss with photos, moisture logs, and scope reports in the format adjusters expect.
