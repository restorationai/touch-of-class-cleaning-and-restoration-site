## Why Fate Properties See Mold Issues

Fate's building boom means a large share of the housing stock was framed within the last 15 years — which sounds like good news until you consider that modern construction uses OSB sheathing, paper-faced drywall, and engineered lumber. All three materials are significantly more hospitable to mold than the solid lumber and plaster used in older homes. When moisture gets behind vinyl siding or into an attic with inadequate ridge ventilation — a common detail deficiency in high-volume tract construction — the conditions for mold growth arrive quickly.

North Texas summers compound the issue. Fate's humid subtropical climate means outdoor humidity regularly climbs above 70% from May through September. When a home's air conditioning system is undersized, cycling irregularly, or set too high during a vacation, interior relative humidity can creep into the range where surface mold thrives. Attics and crawl spaces are the first places it shows up, but HVAC ducts and wall cavities behind exterior-facing bathrooms are close behind.

## Our Mold Remediation Process in Fate

Every job starts with a thorough visual inspection and moisture mapping. We use thermal imaging cameras and calibrated moisture meters to trace the moisture source — because remediating visible mold without fixing the underlying water intrusion is a short-term fix that fails. Once the source is identified and stopped, we establish negative-air containment using 6-mil poly barriers and HEPA-filtered air scrubbers to prevent spores from migrating to unaffected rooms during the removal process.

Affected porous materials — drywall, insulation, and compromised framing — are removed and bagged per EPA guidelines. Non-porous surfaces are HEPA-vacuumed, then treated with an EPA-registered antimicrobial solution. We run commercial-grade dehumidifiers and air movers until moisture readings in the structural materials return to acceptable baselines, typically below 16% in wood framing. Our technicians hold the IICRC AMRT (Applied Microbial Remediation Technician) credential, which means the process follows a defined standard — not a guess.

When the remediation scope is complete, we provide written documentation of pre- and post-remediation moisture readings and photographic evidence of the affected area before and after. That documentation matters for insurance claims and for any future buyer's due diligence.

## Local Note

One pattern we see repeatedly in Fate's newer subdivisions: builders often route HVAC condensate drain lines into the garage rather than to an exterior exit point. When that drain line clogs — which happens with algae buildup in the humid Texas summers — the overflow pan fills and eventually saturates the drywall behind the air handler. Because the garage is not conditioned space, the moisture goes unnoticed far longer than it would inside the house. If you have an air handler in your garage and haven't had the condensate line flushed recently, it's worth checking before the next cooling season starts.
