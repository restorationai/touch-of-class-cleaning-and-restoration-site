## Why Frisco Properties Take Storm Hits Differently

Frisco's housing stock is almost uniformly two-story brick veneer over wood frame, with attic air handlers sitting directly above finished living space. That combination creates a specific failure chain during severe weather: hail punches through ridge cap and step flashing, water enters the attic, and within hours it's wicking into the insulation batts above the air handler. The handler's condensate pan overflows, and suddenly you have a water intrusion problem on the second floor that started as a roofing problem on the exterior.

The Houston Black clay soil beneath most of Frisco compounds the issue. After a heavy storm event, the ground swells and sheds water laterally rather than absorbing it. Stewart and Panther creeks — which drain west toward Lewisville Lake — back up during sustained rainfall, and properties in lower-lying sections of Frisco Lakes and Newman Village can see sheet flow across slabs that was never anticipated in the original grading plan. That standing water under a door threshold can migrate beneath luxury vinyl plank flooring before the storm has even passed.

## Our Storm Damage Restoration Process in Frisco

The first hour on-site is about stopping the bleeding, not paperwork. We tarp exposed roof decking, board compromised windows, and establish a moisture baseline with thermal imaging and pin-type meters before any material is moved. That documentation matters — it becomes the foundation of your insurance claim.

From there, the process follows a sequence calibrated to Frisco's construction norms:

1. **Exterior stabilization** — emergency tarping of hail-damaged or wind-lifted roofing; board-up of broken glass.
2. **Interior moisture mapping** — thermal camera scans of ceilings and exterior walls to locate hidden saturation, especially above attic air handlers.
3. **Controlled demolition** — removal of saturated drywall, insulation, and flooring only to the extent necessary. Frisco's two-story volume ceilings mean we use lifts, not ladders, to reach upper wall cavities safely.
4. **Structural drying** — commercial desiccant dehumidifiers and high-velocity air movers, deployed to meet IICRC S500 drying standards. Drying logs are recorded daily.
5. **Reconstruction scoping** — detailed line-item estimate formatted for Xactimate, the software most Texas carriers use, so there are no translation delays with your adjuster.

Reign Restoration is an IICRC Certified Firm, and our technicians hold WRT (Water Restoration Technician) and FSRT (Fire and Smoke Restoration Technician) credentials — relevant here because storm events often involve both water intrusion and smoke or electrical damage from lightning strikes.

## Local Note

Here's something that catches homeowners off guard in newer Frisco subdivisions: the brick veneer on most post-2000 construction is a single wythe — one brick thick — with a one-inch air gap behind it. After a severe hail event, that air cavity can trap wind-driven water that entered through cracked mortar joints or weep holes. The water sits in the cavity, invisible from inside the house, and slowly wicks into the OSB sheathing behind it. By the time you see a stain on an interior wall, the sheathing may already be delaminating. Thermal imaging during our initial assessment is specifically designed to catch this pattern before it becomes a mold problem.
