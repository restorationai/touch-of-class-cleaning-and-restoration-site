## Why Greenville Properties Face Elevated Storm Damage Risk

Hunt County averages more than 50 severe thunderstorm events annually, and Greenville's position along US-69 and US-380 puts it directly in the path of storm cells that strengthen as they cross the Blackland Prairie. That clay-heavy soil — the same dark, expansive earth that makes Hunt County farmland productive — creates a secondary problem after heavy rain: it absorbs moisture slowly and holds it against foundation slabs and pier-and-beam crawl spaces, extending the window during which structural wood stays wet.

Greenville's housing stock adds another layer of complexity. The city has a meaningful number of homes built between the 1940s and 1970s, many with original wood-framed roof decking and older felt underlayment that degrades faster under hail impact than modern synthetic materials. A storm that leaves a newer subdivision with cosmetic shingle bruising can leave a mid-century home with actual deck penetrations — meaning water is already inside the attic before the rain stops.

## Our Storm Damage Restoration Process in Greenville

When Reign Restoration arrives at a Greenville property after a storm, the first priority is emergency stabilization — tarping breached roof sections, boarding compromised windows, and containing any active water intrusion before it migrates into wall cavities or subfloor assemblies. This step matters most in the 24–72 hour window when mold colonization risk rises sharply.

From there, the process moves through documented damage assessment, moisture mapping with thermal imaging and pin-type meters, and debris removal. For tree-impact damage — a common call after Greenville's spring storm season — we assess not just the visible crush zone but the surrounding framing for racking damage that isn't always obvious from the exterior. Structural repairs, interior drying, and final reconstruction are sequenced to meet the documentation requirements most insurance carriers request before releasing repair funds. As an IICRC Certified Firm, Reign Restoration dries structural assemblies to the S500 standard, which gives adjusters a defensible drying log.

## Local Note

One pattern that comes up repeatedly on Greenville storm jobs: homes with older metal roofing — a style common on agricultural properties and some mid-century residential buildings in Hunt County — often show no obvious exterior breach after a hail event, but the fasteners have backed out slightly under impact, creating pinhole gaps that only reveal themselves during the next rain. By the time a homeowner notices ceiling staining, the attic insulation has been cycling through wet-dry-wet for weeks. If your property has a standing-seam or corrugated metal roof and took a direct hail hit, a fastener inspection is worth adding to the initial assessment even if the surface looks intact.
