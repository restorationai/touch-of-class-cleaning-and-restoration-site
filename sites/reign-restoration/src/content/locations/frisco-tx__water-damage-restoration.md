## Why Frisco Properties See Water Damage Differently Than Older DFW Cities

Nearly every home in Frisco was built after 1995, which means the building stock is almost uniformly two-story brick veneer over wood framing with attic-mounted air handlers and PEX or copper supply runs. That sounds modern and reliable — until a PEX fitting at a toilet supply or a refrigerator ice-maker line fails behind cabinetry. Because the homes sit on post-tension slabs rather than pier-and-beam foundations, there is no crawl space to inspect. Water that gets under the slab edge or into a wall cavity has nowhere obvious to go, and it stays.

The Houston Black clay soil that runs through the Stewart and Panther Creek drainages west of the city compounds the problem. During a wet spring or after a heavy North Texas thunderstorm, that clay swells and can shift slab sections just enough to stress supply lines or crack tile grout — creating a slow leak that goes unnoticed for weeks. Neighborhoods closer to those creek corridors, including parts of Stonebriar and Plantation Resort, have seen this pattern repeatedly.

Attic air handlers are another Frisco-specific risk. Because finished living space sits directly below the mechanical unit, a clogged condensate drain or a cracked drip pan can saturate insulation and ceiling drywall before the homeowner notices a water stain. By the time it's visible, the structural drying job is significantly larger than it would have been if caught early.

## Our Water Damage Restoration Process in Frisco

Every job starts with a full moisture mapping inspection using thermal imaging and calibrated moisture meters. In Frisco's slab-on-grade homes, we pay particular attention to the perimeter walls, the base of interior columns, and any space adjacent to the attic mechanical unit — areas where water migrates invisibly along the concrete edge.

Once we've mapped the affected zone, water extraction comes first. Standing water is removed with truck-mounted extractors, and then we transition to targeted drying using industrial desiccant and refrigerant dehumidifiers sized to the cubic footage of the affected rooms. Frisco's high summer humidity — regularly above 70% during storm season — means undersized consumer-grade equipment simply can't pull enough moisture from the air to meet the IICRC S500 drying standard. We monitor psychrometric data daily and adjust equipment placement until every affected material reads within normal range.

Where drywall, insulation, or flooring is too saturated to dry in place, we perform controlled demolition — removing only what can't be saved — and document everything photographically for your insurance claim. We work directly with most major carriers and can submit the scope of loss on your behalf.
