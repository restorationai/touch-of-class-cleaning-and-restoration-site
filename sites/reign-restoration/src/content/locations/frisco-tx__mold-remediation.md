Frisco's rapid build-out from open prairie to one of the fastest-growing cities in the country happened almost entirely on slab-on-grade construction — and that foundation choice, combined with North Texas humidity and the expansive Houston Black clay underneath neighborhoods like Stonebriar and Phillips Creek Ranch, creates conditions where mold can take hold faster than most homeowners expect. A slow attic air handler leak or a hairline PEX fitting failure behind drywall doesn't need long to turn into a remediation project. When it does, the response has to be methodical, not just fast.

## Why Frisco Properties See Mold Issues

Frisco sits in a climate band where summer dew points regularly climb into the low 70s — air that feels like a warm wet towel even at 10 p.m. When that outdoor humidity infiltrates an attic space and meets a supply plenum running 55°F air, condensation forms on every surface it touches. Because virtually all of Frisco's housing stock was built after 1995, attic air handlers sitting directly over finished second-floor ceilings are nearly universal. A pan drain that clogs once, or a flex duct joint that separates slightly, can drip onto OSB sheathing and insulation for weeks before the ceiling below shows a stain.

The Houston Black clay soil under much of the city also moves — it swells when wet and shrinks when dry. That seasonal movement stresses slab perimeters, and hairline cracks in brick veneer or stucco can let driven rain migrate into wall cavities. In older sections of Stonebriar and Starwood, those homes are now approaching 25–30 years old, which means original caulking around windows and penetrations has degraded. Mold colonization can begin within 24 to 48 hours of sustained moisture — the clock starts the moment water enters a wall cavity, not when you notice the smell.

## Our Mold Remediation Process in Frisco

Every job starts with a thorough moisture mapping inspection — thermal imaging and pin-type meters to find wet material that isn't visibly discolored yet. In Frisco's two-story volume-ceiling homes, we pay particular attention to the attic deck above finished space and the wall cavities adjacent to upstairs bathrooms, where supply lines and drain stacks run inside exterior walls.

Once the scope is defined, we establish negative-pressure containment using 6-mil poly barriers and HEPA-filtered air scrubbers before any demolition begins. Affected drywall, insulation, and OSB are removed, bagged, and sealed on-site. Structural framing is HEPA-vacuumed and treated with an EPA-registered antimicrobial agent. We dry residual moisture in framing to below 16% MC before reconstruction begins — skipping that step is how mold comes back. The process follows IICRC AMRT protocols, and Reign Restoration holds IICRC AMRT (Mold) certification alongside IICRC Certified Firm status.

For homes in ZIP codes 75034 and 75035 — which cover a large portion of central and east Frisco including the Phillips Creek Ranch and Hollyhock areas — we've seen a pattern of mold behind shower niches in master baths where the original tile backer was standard drywall rather than cement board. If your inspection reveals that condition, we document it for your insurance carrier before any material is touched.

## Frisco Insurance and HOA Coordination

Many Frisco communities — particularly master-planned developments near The Star in Frisco corridor and along Legacy Drive — have active HOAs with exterior repair approval requirements. ## Local Note

Something worth knowing if you're in a Frisco home built between 2000 and 2010: many of those builds used paper-faced fiberglass batt insulation in exterior walls, and the paper facing is itself a cellulose food source for mold. When we open a wall cavity in these homes and find elevated moisture, we almost always find mold growth on the paper facing even when the drywall surface looks clean. Homeowners who've had a previous "mold cleanup" that didn't include insulation removal sometimes discover this the hard way during a home sale inspection.
