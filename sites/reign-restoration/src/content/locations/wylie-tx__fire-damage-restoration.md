## Why Wylie Properties Face Particular Fire Damage Challenges

Wylie has grown fast. Much of the housing stock along the 78 corridor and in subdivisions that pushed east from Sachse through the 2000s and 2010s was built with engineered lumber — LVL beams, I-joists, and oriented strand board — rather than the solid-sawn framing you'd find in older Texas towns. Engineered wood performs well under normal conditions, but it fails faster under fire load than dimensional lumber, which matters for structural assessment after a fire. A home that looks intact from the street may have compromised floor joists or roof trusses that aren't visible until a trained technician probes them.

Wylie also sits in Collin County, where clay-heavy soil causes foundation movement year-round. Pre-existing hairline cracks in brick mortar or drywall — common in any home that's gone through a few Texas freeze-thaw cycles — give smoke a direct path into wall cavities. We scope those penetrations before sealing, because trapping smoke odor behind a fresh coat of paint is one of the most common post-fire restoration mistakes.

## Our Fire Damage Restoration Process in Wylie

Every job starts with a structured assessment before a single piece of debris is moved. We photograph and document every affected room, which builds the evidence file your insurance adjuster will need. From there, the process follows a defined sequence:

**1. Stabilization** — Board-up, roof tarping, and utility isolation to stop secondary damage from weather or a compromised electrical panel.

**2. Debris and char removal** — Burned materials are removed down to clean substrate. We separate salvageable items from unsalvageable ones and inventory both, which matters for contents claims.

**3. Soot and smoke cleaning** — Dry-cleaning sponges for loose soot, then alkaline or acidic cleaning agents matched to the surface type (protein-fire residue from a kitchen fire needs different chemistry than the synthetic-material smoke from an electrical fire). HEPA vacuuming runs throughout.

**4. Odor neutralization** — Thermal fogging and hydroxyl generation reach into HVAC ducts, wall cavities, and attic spaces where surface wiping can't. In Wylie's tightly built newer homes, the HVAC system is often the last place smoke odor hides.

**5. Structural drying** — If firefighting water is present, we integrate moisture mapping and drying per the IICRC S500 standard before reconstruction begins. Skipping this step causes mold within days in a North Texas summer.

**6. Reconstruction** — Framing, drywall, insulation, paint, and finish work handled by our own licensed crew under Texas MHIC license #MRC2276.
