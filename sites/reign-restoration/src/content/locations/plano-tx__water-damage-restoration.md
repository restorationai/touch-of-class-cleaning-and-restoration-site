## Our Water Damage Restoration Process in Plano

Every job starts with thermal imaging and moisture mapping — not a visual walk-through. In Plano's slab construction, water travels horizontally under the concrete before it surfaces, so the wet spot you can see is rarely where the water actually originated. We use penetrating moisture meters on slab edges and non-invasive sensors along baseboards to trace the true migration path before we pull a single piece of flooring.

Once the scope is confirmed, we deploy truck-mounted extraction units for standing water, followed by desiccant or refrigerant dehumidifiers and high-velocity air movers calibrated to the room volume and material type. Structural drying in Plano's brick veneer homes requires attention to the wall cavity between the brick and the interior framing — that air gap can trap humidity and slow drying significantly if equipment placement doesn't account for it. We dry to IICRC S500 standard, logging temperature, relative humidity, and material moisture content at each check until readings confirm the structure is back to pre-loss equilibrium.

For slab leaks specifically, we coordinate with your plumber on access cuts and handle the water mitigation around the repair so you're not managing two separate crews on the same timeline.

## Local Note

One thing that catches homeowners off guard in Plano's 1980s-era slab homes: the original vapor barrier under the concrete is often degraded or absent entirely. When a supply line fails and water saturates the soil beneath the slab, that moisture can continue to drive humidity up through the concrete long after the leak is repaired and the surface appears dry.
