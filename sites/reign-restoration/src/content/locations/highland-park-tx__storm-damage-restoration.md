## Why Highland Park Properties See Distinct Storm Damage Patterns

North Texas severe weather is not subtle. The Dallas–Fort Worth corridor sits in one of the most active hail corridors in the country, and Highland Park's position — a landlocked enclave surrounded by Dallas — means it catches the same supercell tracks that funnel down from Denton County with no geographic buffer. What makes the damage patterns here unusual is the building stock.

Homes platted between 1907 and the 1930s were built on pier-and-beam foundations, some with true basements — rare for this part of Texas. When wind-driven rain breaches a roofline or a falling limb punches through a soffit, water doesn't just pool on a slab; it migrates into subfloor cavities and crawl spaces that can stay wet for weeks if not properly extracted. Plaster walls, common throughout Old Highland Park, wick and hold moisture at a slower release rate than drywall, which means drying timelines are longer and moisture mapping needs to go deeper into the wall assembly before you can confirm the structure is dry.

## Our Storm Damage Restoration Process in Highland Park

The first priority on any storm call is stopping ongoing intrusion. That means emergency tarping of damaged rooflines — critical on slate and clay tile systems where a partial breach will expand rapidly with the next rain event — and boarding compromised windows or doors. Reign Restoration's IICRC-certified team documents everything photographically before touching a surface, which matters enormously for insurance claims on high-value historic properties.

Once the structure is secured, the process moves to systematic moisture mapping using thermal imaging and calibrated pin and pinless meters. On pier-and-beam homes, that inspection extends under the floor deck. Plaster walls require readings at multiple depths because the dense material can show a dry surface while the lath behind it remains saturated. Industrial desiccant dehumidifiers and high-velocity air movers are positioned based on the mapping data, not by guesswork, and readings are logged daily until the structure meets the drying targets set by the IICRC S500 standard.

Tree impact calls — a common outcome when mature oaks and pecans along the Lakeside Drive and Preston Road corridor come down in straight-line wind events — add a structural assessment layer. Reign coordinates with structural engineers when load-bearing members are involved, and debris removal is staged carefully to avoid secondary damage to slate tile or ornamental masonry.
