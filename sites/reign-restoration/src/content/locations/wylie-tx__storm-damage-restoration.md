## Why Wylie Properties See Elevated Storm Damage Risk

Wylie sits in one of the most active severe-weather corridors in the country. The broad, flat terrain of Collin County gives storms room to organize and accelerate, and Wylie's position on the eastern edge of the Dallas–Fort Worth metroplex means it catches the trailing edge of squall lines that have already gathered intensity over the open prairie to the west.

The local housing stock adds another layer of vulnerability. Wylie has grown rapidly over the past two decades, and a large share of the residential inventory consists of brick-veneer homes built in the 1990s through 2010s — construction that looks solid but can hide real weaknesses. Brick veneer is not structural; when hail or wind forces water behind the veneer, it saturates the OSB sheathing underneath without showing obvious exterior damage. Homeowners sometimes don't discover the moisture intrusion until mold begins forming inside wall cavities weeks later. The clay-heavy expansive soils common throughout Collin County also shift seasonally, and storm-driven saturation can accelerate foundation movement that widens gaps around door frames and window sills — new entry points for the next storm.

The area also has a significant number of mature trees — post oaks and cedar elms that predate the newer subdivisions — that become projectiles in high-wind events. A single downed limb can punch through a roof deck, expose attic insulation to rain, and introduce enough moisture to begin mold colonization within 24 to 48 hours if the opening isn't tarped and dried promptly.

## Our Storm Damage Restoration Process in Wylie

Every job starts with a thorough exterior and interior assessment before any materials are removed or dried. We photograph and document the full scope of damage — roof penetrations, compromised soffits, broken windows, water intrusion paths — in a format that aligns with what insurance adjusters need to process a claim efficiently.

Once the scope is clear, the sequence runs like this:

1. **Emergency stabilization** — tarping exposed roof sections, boarding broken windows, and extracting any standing water to stop the damage from spreading.
2. **Moisture mapping** — using thermal imaging cameras and calibrated moisture meters to trace exactly how far water has traveled into wall assemblies, subfloors, and ceiling cavities. In Wylie's brick-veneer homes, this step often reveals saturation behind the veneer that isn't visible from the surface.
3. **Structural drying** — deploying commercial-grade desiccant dehumidifiers and high-velocity air movers, monitored daily against drying logs until materials reach target moisture content per the IICRC S500 standard.
4. **Debris and tree damage cleanup** — coordinating safe removal of downed limbs and any materials that can't be restored.
5. **Reconstruction** — our team handles the rebuild phase directly, so homeowners aren't left managing a separate contractor after the mitigation is done.

## Local Note

One pattern we've seen repeatedly in Wylie's newer subdivisions: homes built with radiant barrier roof decking — common in North Texas construction after roughly 2005 — can trap heat in attic spaces in ways that slow structural drying after a roof breach. The barrier that keeps summer heat out also reduces airflow, which means attic insulation that gets wet after a storm takes longer to dry than it would in an older home with a ventilated ridge-and-soffit system. We account for this by adding targeted attic ventilation during the drying phase rather than relying solely on dehumidifiers positioned at the living-space level.
