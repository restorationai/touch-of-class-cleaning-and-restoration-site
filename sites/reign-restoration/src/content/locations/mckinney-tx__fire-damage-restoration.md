## Why McKinney's Housing Stock Shapes Fire Damage Differently

McKinney sits at a genuine architectural fault line. East of US 75, the blocks surrounding the Old Collin County Courthouse are lined with 1890s–1920s Folk Victorians and Craftsman bungalows built on pier-and-beam foundations over open crawlspaces. Those crawlspaces act like chimneys during a structure fire — heat and smoke travel upward through subfloor gaps, saturating hardwood tongue-and-groove decking and the original balloon-frame walls that were never designed with fire blocking. Older drain lines in these homes predate PVC, meaning heat can warp or crack cast-iron and galvanized fittings, creating a secondary water damage risk the moment firefighters suppress the blaze.

West of US 75, the picture changes completely. Master-planned communities like Stonebridge Ranch and Trinity Falls are built on concrete slab with engineered trusses and OSB sheathing. Slab construction eliminates the crawlspace pathway, but OSB and engineered lumber char and delaminate faster than old-growth dimensional lumber under sustained heat, and the tightly packed truss systems in these homes require careful structural assessment before any restoration work begins. The same fire in two different McKinney ZIP codes — say 75069 versus 75071 — can present an entirely different scope of damage.

## Our Fire Damage Restoration Process in McKinney

Every job starts with a full structural and content assessment before a single cleaning product is opened. We photograph and document every affected surface for insurance purposes, then establish containment to prevent soot from migrating into unaffected areas — a step that matters especially in open-concept floor plans common in newer McKinney builds.

From there, the process follows a deliberate sequence:

- **Emergency board-up and tarping** — securing windows, doors, and roof penetrations against North Texas weather, which can shift from dry heat to driving rain within hours during spring storm season.
- **Structural drying** — firefighting water must be extracted and the structure dried before any reconstruction begins. We use commercial desiccant and refrigerant dehumidifiers calibrated to the ambient humidity levels typical of Collin County summers.
- **Smoke and soot removal** — dry chemical sponges for loose soot, alkaline cleaners for protein-based residues (common in kitchen fires), and thermal fogging or hydroxyl generators to neutralize odor molecules embedded in framing, insulation, and HVAC systems.
- **Content pack-out and cleaning** — salvageable belongings are inventoried, packed, and cleaned at our facility, keeping your items out of the compromised structure during restoration.
- **Reconstruction** — from drywall and insulation to cabinetry and flooring, we carry the job through to a finished, permitted repair.

## Local Note

Homes in the blocks immediately surrounding Historic Downtown McKinney often have original plaster-and-lath interior walls rather than modern drywall. Plaster doesn't absorb smoke odor the same way drywall does — the dense, hard surface holds soot on top rather than pulling it in — but the lath cavities behind the plaster can trap smoke and off-gas for weeks if not addressed. We use injection-based deodorization through existing penetrations rather than demolishing intact plaster, preserving the historic character of the home while still reaching the odor source. It's a detail that matters in a neighborhood where the architecture is part of the property value.
