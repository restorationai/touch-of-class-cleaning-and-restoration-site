## Why Caddo Mills Properties See Water Damage Issues

Caddo Mills sits in the Blackland Prairie region of Hunt County, where the clay-heavy soil expands when wet and contracts hard during dry spells. That cycle puts constant stress on slab foundations, and it's a leading cause of the hairline slab cracks and shifted plumbing joints that turn into slow leaks under flooring. Add in the area's sudden spring and early-summer thunderstorms, which can dump several inches of rain in an hour, and you get two very different water intrusion patterns: the quiet, months-in-the-making slab leak, and the fast-moving storm surge that overwhelms yard drainage and pushes water against foundations and into garages. Newer construction built on slab-on-grade foundations in Caddo Mills' growing subdivisions handles standing water differently than the older pier-and-beam homes scattered through the more established parts of town, where water can pool under the house in the crawlspace and go unnoticed until the subfloor starts to soften.

## Our Water Damage Restoration Process in Caddo Mills

We start with extraction, pulling standing water with truck-mount and portable units depending on how far the affected room sits from a driveway or access point. From there we inspect the building type before deciding on a drying plan. Slab homes need moisture meters and often sub-surface tools to find trapped water in the slab itself, since clay soil swelling can trap moisture against the foundation longer than you'd see in sandier soils. Pier-and-beam and crawlspace homes get a different approach: we check floor joists and insulation for saturation and may need to access the crawlspace directly to set drying equipment underneath the house, not just inside it. Either way, we document moisture readings daily and dry materials to the IICRC S500 standard rather than guessing by feel, because clay-soil moisture can wick back into a slab even after the surface reads dry.
