Murphy sits in one of the fastest-growing corners of Collin County, and that growth shows up in the renovation work we see: young subdivisions built on expansive Blackland Prairie clay next to a handful of older farmhouse-style properties left over from before the city's 1990s building boom. Renovations, remodels and general contracting here have to account for both, whether you're updating a kitchen that was already outdated by the time your HOA finished the neighborhood or rebuilding a section of a house after a storm or water loss.

## Why Murphy Properties Need a Different Remodeling Approach

Most homes in Murphy were built within the last three decades, which means slab foundations, builder-grade finishes, and open floor plans that were standard for the era but rarely match what homeowners want a decade or two later. The region's clay-heavy soil expands and contracts with North Texas's wet-dry cycles, and that movement can crack drywall, shift door frames, and throw off cabinet lines in ways that make a simple kitchen refresh turn into a leveling and framing conversation first. Older properties scattered through the area bring their own issues: original plumbing runs, undersized electrical panels, and finishes that no longer meet current code. Either way, a remodel in Murphy usually starts with an honest look at what's underneath the surface before anyone picks a countertop.

## Our Renovation and Remodeling Process in Murphy

We start with a walkthrough that separates cosmetic wants from structural needs, since those two things get priced and permitted very differently. For kitchen and bathroom remodels, that means checking slab condition, venting, and water line routing before demo begins, particularly in homes with the foundation movement common across Collin County's clay soil. Once scope is set, we pull the required permits through the city, schedule trades in the right order, and keep the homeowner looped in on timeline changes rather than letting a project go quiet for weeks. Post-damage rebuilds follow a similar path but start from a different point: matching existing materials, reconstructing to code, and documenting the work so it lines up with what the insurance carrier approved.

## Murphy Insurance and Permit Coordination

A fair share of our general contracting work in Murphy comes after a water, fire, or storm claim rather than from a homeowner simply wanting an update. In those cases, we coordinate directly with the adjuster on scope and pricing so the rebuild matches the approved estimate, and we pull city permits for any structural, electrical, or plumbing work rather than treating it as optional paperwork. Many Murphy subdivisions also carry HOA architectural guidelines covering exterior changes, roofing materials, and even fencing, so we check those requirements early for any rebuild that touches the exterior or changes a home's footprint, which saves a second round of approvals later.

## Local note

Homes built in Murphy's newer phases, particularly anything finished after the mid-2000s expansion, tend to have PEX plumbing and slab foundations poured to more consistent modern specs, which usually means fewer surprises during a bathroom or kitchen gut. The older pocket of properties near the city's original core is a different story: expect cast iron or galvanized runs in some cases, and foundations that have had more decades to react to the area's clay soil. Knowing which category a property falls into before demo starts changes how we scope the job and quote the timeline.
