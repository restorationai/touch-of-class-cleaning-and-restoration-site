## Why Nevada Properties See Unique Fire Damage Patterns

Nevada sits in a mostly rural pocket of Collin County, where a lot of homes are on larger lots with septic systems, propane tanks, and older electrical panels that weren't built for today's appliance load. Propane-fed water heaters and furnaces are common out here, and when a fire starts near one, the restoration crew has to treat the structure differently than a typical suburban gas-line home, checking for pressure damage and heat-warped framing well beyond the visible burn area. Longer driveways and gravel or caliche access roads also mean fire departments sometimes take a few extra minutes to get trucks in position, which can let smoke saturate further into ceiling cavities and attic insulation than it would in a tighter subdivision.

## Our Fire Damage Restoration Process in Nevada

We start with a walkthrough to document the loss for your insurance file, then board up or tarp any exposed openings before weather or wildlife become a second problem. From there, our crew separates salvageable contents from total losses, removes charred or saturated building materials, and runs HEPA air scrubbers to pull soot particulate out of the air before anyone spends real time inside. Structural fire damage in older Nevada homes, many built with full dimensional lumber and plaster-over-lath in the original sections, often means the framing itself survives better than modern engineered lumber would, but it also holds smoke odor longer and needs deeper thermal fogging or ozone treatment to fully clear. We dry any firefighting water intrusion per the IICRC S500 standard before reconstruction starts, because rebuilding over damp subfloor just invites a second claim.

A lot of the older homes scattered around Nevada still have knob-and-tube or early romex wiring in at least one section of the house, usually an addition or a converted porch.
