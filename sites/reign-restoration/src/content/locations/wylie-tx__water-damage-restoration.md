## Why Wylie Properties Are Vulnerable to Water Damage

The same black clay soils that make Collin County farmland productive create real headaches for homeowners. When saturated, the soil expands and presses against slab foundations — a process that can force groundwater into hairline cracks that weren't there the season before. After a heavy storm, that moisture doesn't evaporate quickly; it lingers under flooring, inside wall cavities, and along the base of exterior walls.

Wylie's building boom over the last two decades also means a significant portion of the housing stock was framed quickly with engineered lumber and OSB sheathing. Both materials absorb water faster than older-growth dimensional lumber and can begin to swell, delaminate, or support microbial growth within 24 to 48 hours of sustained exposure. A slow leak behind a refrigerator water line or a failed wax ring under a toilet — the kind of damage that goes unnoticed for days — can compromise subfloor panels before a homeowner ever smells anything unusual.

Winter months bring a separate risk. When temperatures dip into the low 20s, which happens several times each January and February in this part of Texas, pipes in unconditioned garages, exterior walls with minimal insulation, and irrigation backflow preventers are all candidates for freezing and bursting. The resulting water release can be sudden and high-volume.

## Our Water Damage Restoration Process in Wylie

Every job starts with moisture mapping — not a visual inspection, but a systematic reading of walls, floors, and ceilings using thermal imaging cameras and calibrated moisture meters. This step matters especially in Wylie's newer construction, where water can travel surprising distances through open-web floor trusses before surfacing.

Once the full scope is documented, water extraction begins using truck-mounted and portable extraction units capable of pulling standing water from carpet, pad, hardwood, and tile grout lines. Structural drying follows: high-capacity desiccant or refrigerant dehumidifiers are placed based on the cubic footage of the affected space, and air movers are positioned to create the directional airflow the IICRC S500 standard requires for efficient evaporation. Moisture readings are logged daily so drying progress is tracked against a documented baseline — not estimated.

If materials cannot be dried in place — saturated drywall below the flood cut line, swollen OSB subfloor panels, or waterlogged insulation — controlled demolition removes only what cannot be saved. Scope is kept conservative; unnecessary tear-out adds cost and rebuild time.
