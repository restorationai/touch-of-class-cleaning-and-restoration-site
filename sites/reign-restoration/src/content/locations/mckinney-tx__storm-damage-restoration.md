## Why McKinney Properties Are Especially Vulnerable to Severe Weather

North Texas sits squarely in Tornado Alley's eastern corridor, and Collin County averages some of the highest annual hail-day counts in the state. But the local risk isn't just about frequency — it's about what the storms find when they arrive.

The historic housing stock near the Old Collin County Courthouse square presents a specific challenge: wide, deep porches that catch wind like a sail, original wood-frame windows with single-pane glass, and drain lines predating PVC that can back up when storm volume overwhelms them. A straight-line wind event that barely registers on radar can peel porch fascia, shatter original glazing, and push water through gaps that modern weatherstripping would have sealed.

Further west, Wilson Creek and Honey Creek carry mapped floodplain toward the East Fork of the Trinity. Subdivisions built along those drainages — including parts of Craig Ranch — can see sheet flooding that overtops driveways and enters garages within minutes of a heavy rain event. Slab foundations in those areas don't have the crawlspace exposure of the older homes, but water that enters through garage doors or HVAC penetrations spreads silently under luxury vinyl plank before anyone notices.

Hail is the third variable. McKinney's rapid growth means a wide range of roof ages across the same ZIP code — a 2022 architectural shingle on a new build in Painted Tree handles a 1.5" hailstone very differently than a 15-year-old 3-tab on a resale in Tucker Hill.

## Our Storm Damage Restoration Process in McKinney

Every storm job starts with a documented scope — not a verbal estimate, but a line-item record of every affected surface, because Collin County insurance adjusters and McKinney HOA architectural review boards both require written documentation before repair approvals move forward.

**Step 1 — Emergency stabilization.** Tarping compromised roofs, boarding broken windows, and extracting any standing water before secondary damage compounds the loss. On pier-and-beam homes near the historic district, we check crawlspaces immediately — storm water pooling under the floor system is the fastest path to mold colonization, which can begin within 24–48 hours in a Texas summer.

**Step 2 — Moisture mapping.** We use thermal imaging and calibrated moisture meters to trace water migration through walls, subfloors, and ceiling assemblies. In older plaster-and-lath construction common downtown, moisture readings behave differently than in drywall — plaster holds water longer and releases it slowly, so drying timelines are extended and we adjust our equipment placement accordingly.

**Step 3 — Structural drying and debris removal.** Industrial air movers, desiccant dehumidifiers, and where needed, injectidry systems for wall cavities. Tree limb removal and debris clearing happen in parallel so the structure is accessible for the trades that follow.

**Step 4 — Reconstruction coordination.** Roof repairs, window replacement, siding, and interior finishes — all permitted through the City of McKinney's building department where required. We carry IICRC certification as a firm, and our technicians hold WRT, AMRT, and FSRT credentials, which insurers and adjusters recognize when reviewing the claim file.
