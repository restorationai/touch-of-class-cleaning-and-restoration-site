## Why Allen's Housing Stock Shapes Fire Damage Differently

The vast majority of Allen's residential neighborhoods were built between roughly 1990 and 2010 — a growth window that produced street after street of similar construction: brick veneer over wood framing, open floor plans, and interconnected HVAC ductwork that runs through unconditioned attic space. That design is energy-efficient under normal conditions, but after a fire it becomes a distribution network for smoke and soot. Combustion byproducts travel the duct runs and deposit on coil surfaces, insulation batts, and the interior of every supply register in the house — rooms that saw no flames at all can carry measurable odor and particulate contamination.

North Texas summers also matter here. Allen's heat and humidity accelerate the bonding of smoke residue to porous surfaces. Soot that sits on painted drywall or wood trim for 48 to 72 hours in July heat begins to etch and stain in ways that require more aggressive cleaning or full replacement. Acting quickly isn't just about peace of mind — it directly affects how much material can be restored versus what has to be torn out.

There is also a pocket of older frame housing near Allen's original town center off Main Street where construction methods differ significantly from the post-1990 subdivisions. Those homes may have original wood lath, thicker plaster walls, and older electrical systems — all factors that change both the fire risk profile and the restoration approach.

## Our Fire Damage Restoration Process in Allen

The first hour on-site is about documentation and containment, not cleaning. A technician walks the structure, photographs every affected surface, and uses thermal imaging to locate heat-affected areas that aren't visible to the eye — including inside wall cavities where fire traveled along framing before suppression. That documentation becomes the foundation of your insurance claim file.

Once the scope is established, the process moves through these stages:

- **Emergency board-up and tarping** — securing openings created by firefighting so the structure is protected from weather and unauthorized entry
- **Debris removal** — charred materials are catalogued, bagged, and removed per local disposal requirements
- **Dry ice or soda blasting** on structural framing where surface char is present but the wood is structurally sound
- **HEPA vacuuming and chemical sponge cleaning** of walls, ceilings, and contents before any wet cleaning introduces moisture
- **Thermal fogging and hydroxyl treatment** for odor — smoke odor molecules bond to surfaces and can re-volatilize in heat; treatment has to reach the same cavities the smoke reached
- **Duct cleaning and coil treatment** — standard in Allen homes given the HVAC-distribution issue described above
- **Reconstruction** — from drywall and paint to full structural rebuilding, handled under the same project so you're not managing two separate contractors
