## Why McKinney Properties See Mold Issues

Collin County sits in a humid subtropical climate that rarely lets up. Summer dew points routinely push into the 70s, and when air-conditioned interiors meet that outdoor air at a poorly sealed crawlspace vent or an aging window flashing, condensation follows fast. Mold can begin colonizing a damp surface within 24 to 48 hours — which matters a great deal in a city where Wilson Creek and Honey Creek carry mapped floodplain through residential neighborhoods, and where a heavy storm can push water into finished spaces before a homeowner realizes the yard is saturated.

The historic district compounds the risk. Drain lines in homes built before the 1950s often predate PVC, meaning slow drips from cast-iron or clay fittings can seep into pier-and-beam cavities for weeks without triggering a visible wet spot on the floor above. By the time a musty smell surfaces in a dining room near Chestnut Square Historic Village, the subfloor joists may already carry active growth. Newer construction in Trinity Falls and Painted Tree faces different vulnerabilities: HVAC condensate pans, improperly flashed roof penetrations, and irrigation systems set too close to the foundation are the most common culprits we find on slab homes.

## Our Mold Remediation Process in McKinney

We follow the IICRC S520 Standard for Professional Mold Remediation, which means the process is structured and documented — not improvised. Here is what that looks like on a typical McKinney job:

**Assessment and moisture mapping.** Before any material comes out, we use thermal imaging and pin-type moisture meters to trace the moisture source and map the full extent of contamination. On pier-and-beam homes near the historic square, that often means dropping into the crawlspace with a borescope to check joist faces and insulation batts.

**Containment.** We seal the affected area with 6-mil poly sheeting and establish negative air pressure using HEPA-filtered air scrubbers. This keeps spores from migrating into unaffected rooms during removal — critical in open-floor-plan homes where a kitchen mold problem can quickly cross into a living area.

**Controlled removal.** Porous materials that cannot be cleaned to clearance standards — drywall, insulation, damaged wood — are double-bagged and removed. Structural lumber that can be salvaged is HEPA-vacuumed, treated with an EPA-registered antimicrobial, and dried to target moisture content before encapsulation.

**Clearance verification.** We do not close out a job on visual inspection alone. Air samples or surface swabs go to an independent laboratory, and we provide the written clearance report to you and your insurance adjuster.

## Local Note

Homes in McKinney's historic district — particularly the Craftsman bungalows and Folk Victorians within a few blocks of the McKinney Performing Arts Center — often have original heart pine subfloor decking over open crawlspaces. Heart pine is dense and slow to absorb moisture, which sounds like an advantage, but it also releases moisture slowly during drying. On these jobs we extend our drying validation period and take daily moisture readings rather than pulling equipment on a fixed schedule. Rushing the drying phase on this wood species is one of the most common reasons mold returns after a remediation that appeared successful.
