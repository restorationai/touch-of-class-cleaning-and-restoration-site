## Why Highland Park Homes Present Unique Contents Challenges

Highland Park's 2.2-square-mile footprint is dense with homes built between 1910 and 1940, and that housing stock shapes every contents job we handle here. Plaster walls and ceilings — common throughout Old Highland Park and the Turtle Creek Acreage corridor — absorb smoke odor and water vapor more aggressively than modern drywall. That means soot particles and moisture migrate into soft goods, upholstery, and cabinetry faster and deeper than homeowners expect.

Turtle Creek's floodplain corridor, which runs through Lakeside Park toward Exall Lake, puts a meaningful portion of the neighborhood within reach of storm surge events. When Turtle Creek overtops its banks, ground-floor contents in pier-and-beam homes can be exposed to silty, contaminated floodwater — the kind that requires Category 3 protocols, not a simple wipe-down. Cast-iron drain stacks in these older homes also back up during heavy rainfall, sending sewage into finished basements and lower-level rooms. Contents in those spaces need immediate pack-out to prevent cross-contamination of items that can still be saved.

North Texas summers push attic temperatures well past 130°F, which accelerates off-gassing from smoke-damaged materials and speeds mold colonization on wet contents — IICRC guidance puts mold colonization risk at 24–48 hours after saturation. In a home sealed tight against July heat, that clock runs fast.

## Our Contents Restoration and Storage Process in Highland Park

Every contents job begins with a room-by-room inventory before a single item leaves the property. We photograph, barcode, and log each piece — furniture, artwork, textiles, electronics, kitchenware — so you have a complete chain-of-custody record that holds up with your insurance adjuster.

Pack-out from a Highland Park estate home requires care beyond speed. We use padded crating for artwork and mirrors, acid-free wrapping for documents and photographs, and climate-controlled vehicles for transport. Items go to our secure storage facility where temperature and humidity are actively managed — critical for antique wood furniture that can warp or crack if exposed to the humidity swings common between a North Texas summer and a heated winter interior.

Cleaning methods are matched to the damage type and material:

- **Smoke and soot**: Dry-cleaning sponges for delicate surfaces, ultrasonic cleaning tanks for hard goods and metals, ozone and hydroxyl treatment for odor in sealed chambers
- **Water and flood damage**: Dehumidification drying for wood furniture, hand-washing and freeze-drying for documents and books, antimicrobial treatment for porous materials
- **Mold-affected contents**: IICRC AMRT-guided protocols, HEPA-filtered cleaning environments, and disposal recommendations for items that cannot be safely restored

Once cleaning and drying are complete, items are re-inventoried and returned to the home — or held in storage if reconstruction is still underway.
