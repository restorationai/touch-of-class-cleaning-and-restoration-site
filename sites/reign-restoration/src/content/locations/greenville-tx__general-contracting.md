## Why Greenville Homes Present Unique Renovation Challenges

North Texas clay soil is notoriously expansive — it swells with moisture and contracts during dry spells, and Greenville sits squarely in that zone. Over time, that movement telegraphs into a home's structure: door frames that no longer square up, tile floors that crack along grout lines, and drywall that develops hairline fractures near corners and window headers. Before any cosmetic renovation makes sense, those underlying shifts need to be assessed. Skipping that step means a beautiful new kitchen floor may start cracking within a season.

Older homes in Greenville — particularly those built before 1970 — frequently contain materials that require careful handling during demolition: lead-based paint on trim and window sashes, asbestos-containing floor tiles, and original plaster walls that behave very differently from modern drywall when it comes to moisture, adhesion, and structural attachment. A general contractor working in this market needs to know what they're pulling out before the first pry bar goes in.

Greenville also sits in a climate band that delivers genuine extremes — summer heat that pushes attic temperatures well past 140°F and periodic ice storms that can stress roofing, gutters, and exterior cladding in a single event. Renovation scopes here often need to account for insulation upgrades, attic ventilation corrections, and exterior envelope improvements that wouldn't be as pressing in milder climates.

## Our Renovation and General Contracting Process in Greenville

Every project starts with a thorough walkthrough — not a sales visit, a working assessment. We document existing conditions, identify any hidden damage (especially relevant on post-water or post-fire rebuilds), and flag material concerns before a scope of work is written. From there, the process moves through permitting, material selection, and phased construction. We pull the permits, schedule inspections, and keep the project moving through each phase — framing, mechanical rough-ins, insulation, drywall, finishes — without handing the homeowner a clipboard and telling them to figure it out.

For kitchen and bathroom remodels specifically, we coordinate plumbing and electrical subcontractors as part of the general contract. You don't need to manage three separate trade relationships; that's the point of hiring a licensed GC.

Homes within a few blocks of Greenville's historic downtown corridor — many of them built between the 1910s and 1940s — often have original wood-framed windows, tongue-and-groove subfloor systems, and interior trim profiles that haven't been manufactured in decades.
