A sewage backup is nothing like a clean water leak. Category 3 water, the IICRC's classification for sewage and sewer line backups, carries bacteria, viruses, and parasites that can colonize porous building materials within hours. Carpet, carpet pad, drywall below the waterline, and particleboard cabinetry that have been in contact with sewage are generally not salvageable. Anything else in the affected area has to be cleaned, disinfected, and verified before it's considered safe to use again.

## What sewage cleanup and sanitization actually involves

Sewage cleanup starts with identifying the source: a sewer line blockage, a septic tank overflow, a failed sump pump, or a municipal backup pushing water up through floor drains. Royse City sits on a mix of older line infrastructure and newer subdivisions on clay-heavy soil, and both tree root intrusion into older sewer laterals and soil movement around newer lines are common culprits. Once the source is identified (and, if it's a plumbing or municipal issue, flagged for the appropriate repair), the physical work begins: removing standing sewage, extracting saturated porous materials, and disinfecting every surface the water touched. Equipment includes trash pumps or wet vacs rated for solids, PPE for technicians, EPA-registered antimicrobial and disinfectant products, and air scrubbers to manage airborne contaminants during the work. Most residential sewage jobs run from a single bathroom backup (a day or two of work) to a whole-floor event from a main line backup, which can take several days once you account for demolition, disinfection, and drying.

## Our process

1. **Source and contamination assessment.** We identify where the sewage entered, the category and class of water, and how far it traveled under flooring and behind walls.
2. **PPE staging and containment.** Technicians suit up and the affected area is contained to keep contamination from spreading to unaffected rooms through HVAC or foot traffic.
3. **Raw sewage removal and demolition.** Standing sewage is extracted, and any porous material that absorbed Category 3 water, carpet, pad, baseboards, affected drywall, is removed and bagged for disposal per waste handling guidelines.
4. **Disinfection and antimicrobial application.** Remaining structural surfaces are cleaned and treated with EPA-registered disinfectants, following the IICRC S500 standard for Category 3 water losses.
5. **Drying, monitoring, and sanitization verification.** Structural drying equipment runs until moisture readings confirm the space is dry, and surfaces are checked before anything is reconstructed or the area is cleared for use.

## What separates a good sewage cleanup response from a bad one

The most common mistake is under-scoping the demolition. Sewage wicks upward into drywall and insulation well above the visible waterline, and a crew that only cuts drywall to the waterline leaves contaminated material behind the finished wall. The same applies to subfloor: sewage that reaches under tile or hardwood through seams and grout lines can sit there undetected if the flooring isn't pulled or probed with a moisture meter.

Another gap is treating sewage cleanup like a standard water loss and skipping antimicrobial application altogether, or applying it without the contact time needed for it to work. Adjusters reviewing sewage claims typically look for documented moisture readings before and after drying, photos of the contamination extent before demolition, and a clear waste disposal record for anything removed. Thorough documentation at each step is what supports a clean claim and protects you if a dispute comes up later.
