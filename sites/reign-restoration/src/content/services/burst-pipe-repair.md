## What burst pipe cleanup and repair actually involves

A burst pipe is a two-part problem: the plumbing failure itself and the water damage it leaves behind. Our crews handle both, starting with locating and stopping the source, whether that's a frozen and split supply line, a corroded joint, a slab leak under the foundation, or a water line break at the main. From there it becomes a standard Category 1 or 2 water loss, depending on how long the water sat and what it passed through. Equipment on site typically includes submersible pumps for standing water, truck-mounted extraction for saturated carpet and pad, and low-grain refrigerant dehumidifiers paired with air movers to bring wall cavities and subfloor back to a dry standard. Drying times run anywhere from two to five days depending on the materials involved and how much water got behind baseboards, cabinetry, or flooring before it was caught.

## Our process

1. **Shutoff and source confirmation.** Before any extraction starts, we confirm the water is off at the main or the affected line and identify where the pipe actually failed, since a visible wet spot on drywall is often several feet from the real break.
2. **Water extraction and material triage.** We pull standing water first, then assess which materials can be dried in place (solid wood subfloor, most drywall) versus what needs to come out (saturated insulation, swollen particleboard cabinets, carpet pad that's wicked gray water).
3. **Moisture mapping and containment.** Using moisture meters and thermal imaging, we map how far the water traveled inside walls and under flooring, since burst pipe water tends to migrate well past the visible stain.
4. **Structural drying with daily monitoring.** Air movers and dehumidifiers run until moisture readings in studs, subfloor, and drywall hit a dry standard, which we document in writing for your file and your carrier.
5. **Pipe repair and reconstruction scoping.** Once drying is confirmed, we coordinate the actual pipe repair or re-pipe section with scope documentation for drywall, flooring, or cabinetry that needs to go back.

## What separates a good burst pipe response from a bad one

The most common mistake is treating a burst pipe as just a mop-and-fan job. Surface drywall can read dry within a day while the stud cavity behind it, or the subfloor beneath laminate, stays wet for a week and starts growing mold that nobody sees until a flooring crew pulls it up. A good response uses moisture meters and infrared imaging to find water that migrated sideways through insulation and along top plates, not just where the stain shows. Adjusters specifically look for daily drying logs with moisture readings, photos of the source before repair, and documentation of what was removed versus dried in place. Skipping that paperwork is how homeowners end up arguing with their carrier over whether the full scope was necessary.
