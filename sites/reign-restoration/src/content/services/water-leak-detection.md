**Noticing a water bill that jumped without an obvious reason, a warm patch on the slab, or a musty smell that won't go away even though you can't find a drip?** That combination usually points to a hidden leak, not a visible one, and finding it before it finds its way into your drywall or foundation is the whole point of water leak detection.

A leak you can see is a plumbing call. A leak you can't see, behind a wall, under a slab, inside a ceiling cavity, is a detection problem, and it's the kind of thing that quietly does damage for weeks before anyone notices the warped baseboard or the spike in the water bill.

## What water leak detection actually involves

Leak detection is diagnostic work, not demolition. The goal is to find the exact source and path of water intrusion without tearing out more of the house than necessary. That means acoustic listening equipment that picks up the sound of water moving through pipe under pressure, thermal imaging cameras that reveal temperature differences where moisture is present behind a surface, and moisture meters that confirm what the thermal scan is showing. For leaks suspected in a concrete slab, which is common in North Texas home construction, that often means pressure testing the plumbing system and sometimes tracer gas to isolate which line is compromised. The process is methodical: rule out the obvious, narrow the search area, confirm with instruments, then mark the exact point before anyone opens a wall or cuts into flooring.

## Our process

1. **Symptom review.** We start with what you've noticed, water bill history, visible staining, smells, sounds of running water when fixtures are off, since those details point us toward supply lines, drain lines, or an appliance connection before equipment ever comes out.
2. **Non-invasive diagnostic scan.** Acoustic listening devices and thermal imaging cover the suspect areas first. This is how we find a leak in a wall or under flooring without guessing.
3. **Pressure and tracer gas testing for slab leaks.** When the evidence points to a line running through or under the foundation, we isolate zones of the plumbing system and test under pressure to confirm which section is leaking.
4. **Pinpointing and marking.** Once the leak is narrowed to a specific point, we confirm it with a secondary method before marking the exact location, so any access cut is small and targeted.
5. **Documentation and handoff.** You get a written report with moisture readings, images, and the confirmed location, which goes to your plumber for repair and, if water has already reached framing or drywall, directly into a drying scope.

## What separates a good leak detection response from a bad one

The most common mistake is skipping instrumentation and going straight to demolition, opening multiple sections of drywall or cutting large slab access points on a hunch. That adds cost and repair scope that often wasn't necessary. A good diagnostic process narrows the search before any cutting happens.

The second mistake is stopping at "found the leak" without checking how far the water already traveled. Water moves along framing members, under subfloor, and into wall cavities well beyond the point of origin, so a thorough check includes moisture mapping of the surrounding structure, not just the leak source itself. Insurance adjusters reviewing a claim look for exactly this: dated moisture readings, photos of the affected area, and a clear explanation of how the leak was located, since that documentation is what supports coverage for the resulting damage, not just the plumbing repair.

## What does water leak detection cost?

Costs vary depending on whether the leak is in an accessible wall, under a slab, or tied to an irrigation or exterior line, and every property is different enough that we provide a written scope before any diagnostic or repair work begins. Homeowners insurance typically covers the resulting water damage once a leak is found, such as drying and repair of affected drywall or flooring, but the plumbing repair itself and the detection service are usually treated as a maintenance cost rather than a covered loss. ## Seasonal & regional considerations

Royse City sits on clay-heavy soil that expands and contracts with Texas's swing between summer drought and heavy spring rain, and that movement puts stress on both slab foundations and the plumbing running through them. Many homes in the area are slab-on-grade construction, which means a slab leak doesn't show up as a puddle, it shows up as a warm spot on the floor or a gradual rise in the water bill. Winter freezes are less frequent here than farther north, but the occasional hard freeze can still crack an exposed or poorly insulated line, and that's often when a small existing leak gets noticed for the first time.
