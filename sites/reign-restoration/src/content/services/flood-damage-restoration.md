Flood damage is different from a burst pipe or appliance leak. The water carries silt, runoff, and often sewage or chemical contaminants from outside the home, which changes how it has to be handled from the first hour. A flooded living room can look manageable on the surface while the baseboards, subfloor, and wall cavities stay saturated for days, setting up conditions for mold and structural rot long after the water recedes.

## What flood damage restoration actually involves

Flood damage restoration starts with floodwater extraction, but the real work is in what comes after. Because floodwater is almost always classified as Category 3 (grossly contaminated) under IICRC S500 guidelines, porous materials that absorb it, carpet pad, drywall, insulation, baseboards, often can't be dried in place and have to come out. Crews use truck-mount or portable extraction units to pull standing water first, then move to air movers, low-grain-refrigerant dehumidifiers, and moisture meters to track what's happening inside walls and under flooring, not just what's visible on the surface. A single-room flood might dry out in a few days; a flooded first floor with wet wall cavities and subfloor typically runs closer to a week of monitored drying before materials read dry per the S500 standard.

## Our process

1. **Safety and utility check.** Before anyone steps into standing water, we confirm electrical and gas systems are safe and identify contamination risk (sewage backflow, chemical runoff, structural hazards).
2. **Floodwater extraction.** High-volume pumps and extraction units remove standing water, prioritizing areas where water is actively migrating into framing or finished flooring.
3. **Removal of contaminated porous materials.** Carpet, pad, drywall up to the waterline, and insulation that absorbed Category 3 water are removed and disposed of per local requirements rather than dried in place.
4. **Structural drying with moisture monitoring.** Air movers and dehumidifiers are positioned based on moisture readings, not guesswork, and we track drying progress with daily or near-daily moisture logs.
5. **Antimicrobial treatment and clearance check.** Remaining structural materials are treated to address bacteria and mold risk, and final moisture readings confirm the space is dry before reconstruction begins.

## What separates a good flood response from a bad one

The most common mistake is treating floodwater like a clean-water leak: drying carpet and drywall that should have been removed, or skipping moisture mapping because the surface looks dry. Floodwater that's left to dry "in place" behind baseboards or under flooring creates a mold problem within 24 to 48 hours even when the room appears fine. A thorough response documents moisture readings room by room, photographs contaminated materials before removal, and keeps a written log that supports the insurance claim instead of leaving the adjuster to take your word for it. Adjusters specifically look for this documentation, along with proof that Category 3 water was handled with appropriate PPE and disposal, not just dried.

## What does flood damage restoration cost?

Costs vary with how much water intruded, how long it sat before cleanup started, and how many materials were contaminated versus salvageable. ## Seasonal & regional considerations

Royse City sits on the blackland prairie of Rockwall County, where heavy clay soil swells and contracts with moisture and drains slowly during intense rain events. Spring and early summer bring the region's heaviest storm systems, and newer slab-on-grade neighborhoods alongside older homes with crawlspaces both face flash-flood risk when drainage can't keep up. Quick extraction matters more here than in drier climates, since humidity levels in North Texas summers slow natural drying and give mold a head start if water sits too long.
