Water, fire, and storm damage often leave more behind than the obvious mess. Once a structure has been gutted down to studs or subfloor after extraction and demolition, someone has to put it back together to code, not just make it look finished. That's the gap reconstruction services fill: the framing, drywall, flooring, trim, and permitting work that turns a stripped-out shell back into a livable space, and the part of the job where cutting corners shows up years later as a sagging floor or a failed inspection.

## What reconstruction services actually involve

Post-damage reconstruction picks up where mitigation leaves off. If a burst pipe soaked a kitchen and the cabinets, subfloor, and lower drywall had to come out, reconstruction is the rebuild: new subfloor, insulation where it was removed, drywall hung and finished, cabinets reset, flooring installed, paint matched. On a fire loss, it can mean replacing structural framing members that lost integrity under heat, not just cosmetic surfaces. Scope ranges from a single bathroom to a full structural rebuild after a storm tore off part of a roofline. Timelines vary by scope: a contained single-room rebuild might run one to three weeks, while a structural rebuild involving permits, framing inspections, and multiple trades can take two to three months. The work has to satisfy both the local building department and the insurance carrier's scope of loss, which means documentation at every stage matters as much as the physical rebuild.

## Our process

1. **Scope of work and damage assessment.** Before anything gets rebuilt, we document what was removed during mitigation, what's structurally sound, and what needs replacing. This becomes the written scope that both the homeowner and the insurance adjuster reference.
2. **Permits and code review.** Structural work, electrical, and plumbing typically require permits through the local building department. We pull what's required and build to current code, which sometimes means upgrades beyond what was originally there, especially on older homes.
3. **Framing and rough-in.** Structural members, subfloor, and rough electrical or plumbing go in first. This is the stage where inspections happen before anything gets closed up behind drywall.
4. **Finishing work.** Drywall, texture, paint, trim, flooring, and fixtures bring the space back to a finished state. Matching existing materials, flooring runs, paint sheens, trim profiles, is where a lot of the skill shows.
5. **Final walkthrough and close-out.** We review the completed work against the original scope with the homeowner, and close out any remaining permit inspections.

## What separates a good reconstruction response from a bad one

The most common failure point is rebuilding before the structure is actually ready. Closing up drywall over framing that hasn't been verified dry, or over wiring that wasn't inspected, creates problems that don't surface until months later. A careful rebuild confirms moisture content in framing and subfloor is back to normal range before any new material goes over it, not just that it "looks dry."

The second issue is scope mismatch with the insurance estimate. Adjusters write estimates based on what they can see and standard line items; a rebuild crew that doesn't flag hidden damage, like insulation that got wet beyond the visible waterline, or framing that needs sistering, leaves the homeowner covering that cost out of pocket later. A thorough reconstruction team documents and photographs before closing anything up, and submits supplements to the carrier when the actual damage exceeds the original scope.

Material and finish matching is where corners get cut on a budget job. Flooring that doesn't match the existing run, paint that's close but not exact, trim with a slightly different profile, these are the details that make a repair look like a repair instead of disappearing into the house.

## What does reconstruction cost?

Reconstruction costs depend entirely on scope: how much of the structure needs rebuilding, whether permits and structural work are involved, and the finish level being restored. Every loss is different, which is why we provide a written scope of work with itemized pricing before any rebuild work begins. Homeowners insurance typically covers reconstruction costs tied to a covered peril (a burst pipe, a fire, wind damage) up to the policy's dwelling limits, though cosmetic upgrades beyond the original condition are usually the homeowner's expense.

| Scenario | Typical range |
|---|---|
| Single room, drywall and paint only | $1,500 - $4,000 |
| Bathroom rebuild (subfloor, drywall, fixtures) | $5,000 - $15,000 |
| Kitchen rebuild (cabinets, flooring, drywall) | $10,000 - $35,000 |
| Structural framing repair (localized) | $4,000 - $12,000 |
| Full room addition or major structural rebuild | $25,000 - $75,000+ |
| Whole-house reconstruction after major loss | $75,000 - $250,000+ |

## Seasonal & regional considerations

North Texas clay soil shifts with moisture swings, which means foundations and framing in Royse City and the surrounding area can move more than in regions with stable soil. After a major water loss, that movement can show up as new cracking or door and window misalignment during the rebuild phase, something a reconstruction crew familiar with local soil conditions watches for before closing up walls. Spring storm season also tends to compress demand on permitting and material lead times across the region, which can stretch timelines on larger rebuilds.
