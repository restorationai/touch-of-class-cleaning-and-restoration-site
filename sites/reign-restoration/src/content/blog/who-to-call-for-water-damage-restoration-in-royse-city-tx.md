## Who Should I Call First for Water Damage in Royse City?

For water damage in Royse City, your first call is to a licensed water damage restoration company. If the water source is still flowing, your very first step is to shut it off at the supply valve before you call anyone. Once the water is stopped, call a restoration crew, not a plumber, not your insurance company, and not a general contractor.

Here is why the order matters:

- **Restoration company first.** They stop the spread, extract standing water, and begin structural drying. Every hour of delay allows water to migrate deeper into wall cavities, subfloor, and insulation. In North Texas, where slab-on-grade construction is the norm, water under a slab is a serious problem that compounds fast.
- **Plumber second (if needed).** Once the restoration crew is on-site and the source is confirmed stopped, a plumber can make the permanent pipe repair. Many restoration companies coordinate this handoff for you.
- **Insurance company third.** Call your insurer after mitigation has started, not before. Adjusters want to see that you took reasonable steps to prevent further damage. A restoration company can document the loss scope before anything is touched, which protects your claim.

If there is any electrical hazard (water near a panel, outlets submerged, appliances in standing water), call 911 or your utility company before anyone enters the space.

## What Happens When You Call a Water Damage Company?

When you call a water damage restoration company in Royse City, you will speak with someone who asks a few quick triage questions: What is the water source? Is it still flowing? How many rooms are affected? Is there standing water?

Have this information ready before you dial:

- The source of the water (burst pipe, appliance, roof leak, sewage backup)
- Approximate square footage affected
- Whether the water is still active
- Your address and best contact number
- Your insurance carrier and policy number if you have it handy

After the call, a crew arrives and does a structured assessment. They use a moisture meter and thermal imaging camera to map where water has traveled, including inside walls and under flooring that looks dry on the surface. This documentation is critical for your insurance claim and for building a complete drying scope.

The IICRC S500 standard, the industry benchmark for water damage restoration, classifies losses by water category and moisture class. Category 1 is clean water (a supply line break). Category 2 is gray water (washing machine overflow, dishwasher). Category 3 is black water (sewage, floodwater from outside). The category affects how aggressively materials need to be removed and what PPE the crew wears. A certified technician will tell you which category you are dealing with and what that means for your home.

Once the assessment is complete, extraction begins: truck-mounted or portable extractors pull standing water, then high-velocity air movers and low-grain refrigerant (LGR) dehumidifiers are placed to dry the structure. Drying typically takes three to five days, with daily moisture readings to confirm progress. At the end, a clearance check confirms the structure has returned to normal moisture levels before any rebuild work begins.

## When Is It a Plumber vs. a Restoration Company?

The simplest rule: a plumber fixes the source of the water; a restoration company handles everything the water touched.

Call a plumber when:
- A pipe is still actively leaking and you cannot reach the shutoff valve
- A fixture needs to be replaced or repaired at the source
- You need a camera inspection of drain lines after a sewage backup

Call a restoration company when:
- Water has already spread to floors, walls, or ceilings
- You have standing water in any room
- You can smell musty odor, which signals moisture trapped in materials
- Your insurance company needs a documented scope of damage

In many cases, both calls happen the same day. The restoration crew stabilizes the structure while the plumber queues up the repair. If the source is already stopped (a frozen pipe that thawed and burst, for example), the restoration call is the only urgent one.

## What About Insurance? Who Handles That?

Most standard homeowners policies in Texas cover sudden and accidental water damage, such as a burst pipe or a failed water heater. Gradual leaks, flooding from outside, and sewer backups are typically excluded unless you have a specific rider or separate flood policy through the [National Flood Insurance Program](https://www.fema.gov/flood-insurance).

A restoration company documents the loss from the moment they arrive: photos, moisture readings, equipment logs, and a written scope of work. That documentation is what your adjuster uses to evaluate the claim. Skipping or delaying professional mitigation can result in a denied claim on the grounds that the damage was allowed to worsen.

For a plain-English breakdown of what Texas homeowners policies typically cover, see [Does Homeowners Insurance Cover Water Damage?](/blog/does-homeowners-insurance-cover-water-damage/).

If you are not sure whether your loss is covered, call the restoration company first. They deal with insurance carriers every day and can help you understand what documentation to gather before the adjuster visits.
