## What to Look for When Choosing a Water Damage Restoration Company

The right company for your situation checks four boxes: they respond fast, they hold real certifications, they document for insurance, and they dry to a measurable standard, not just until things "look dry."

**Response speed matters because of the mold clock.** The [EPA notes that mold can begin to grow within 24 to 48 hours](https://www.epa.gov/mold) of a water event. A company that cannot reach you within a few hours of your call is not a water damage company in any meaningful sense, they are a cleanup crew.

**IICRC certification is the industry baseline.** The IICRC S500 standard governs how water damage is classified (Category 1 clean water, Category 2 gray water, Category 3 black water), how drying targets are set, and what documentation is required. A non-certified company may dry surfaces without measuring the moisture content inside the wall cavity, which leads to hidden mold and a denied insurance claim. Ask any company you call whether they hold WRT certification and whether they are an IICRC Certified Firm.

**Insurance documentation is part of the job.** Your adjuster will ask for a moisture map, a drying log, and a written scope of work. A restoration company that cannot produce these is going to cost you money on your claim. Reign Restoration handles this documentation as a standard part of every job.

**Drying to a standard, not to appearance.** Walls can look dry while holding 20 to 30 percent moisture content inside the framing. The IICRC S500 sets specific drying goals measured with a calibrated moisture meter. Ask any company you hire what their target moisture readings are and how they verify them before they pull equipment.
