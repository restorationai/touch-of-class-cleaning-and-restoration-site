A water bill that jumps for no obvious reason, a warm spot on the slab, a musty smell behind a bathroom wall that won't go away no matter how much you clean: these are the quiet signals of a leak that hasn't announced itself yet. By the time water shows up as a stain on the ceiling or a soft spot in the flooring, it has often been moving through framing, slab, or soil for weeks. Water Leak Detection finds the source before it turns into a structural or mold problem, without tearing out walls or floors to go looking for it.

## What Water Leak Detection actually involves

This is diagnostic work, not demolition. Using moisture meters, thermal imaging cameras, and acoustic listening equipment, a technician traces where water is actually moving instead of guessing based on where it surfaced. Thermal imaging picks up temperature differences in slab and wall surfaces that often correspond to a wet area long before it's visible. Acoustic detection listens for the specific sound a pressurized line makes when water is escaping under a slab or behind tile. For a hidden water leak in a wall or a suspected slab leak, this combination usually narrows the source to a specific section of pipe or a specific wall cavity, rather than a vague "somewhere over here." Most leak detection visits take one to a few hours depending on the size of the home and how many fixtures or lines need to be checked.

## Our process

1. **Symptom review** - we start with what you've noticed: a water bill spike, a sound of running water with no fixture on, a warm patch on the floor, or a damp smell in one room.
2. **Non-invasive scanning** - thermal imaging and moisture meters sweep the suspect area to map where moisture is actually present, not just where it's visible.
3. **Acoustic and pressure testing** - for suspected slab or in-wall supply line leaks, acoustic equipment isolates the sound signature of the leak, and in some cases a pressure test on isolated lines confirms which run is losing pressure.
4. **Source confirmation** - we pinpoint the leak to a specific location before any opening is made, so if drywall, flooring, or slab needs to come up, it's a targeted cut, not an exploratory one.
5. **Written findings** - you get a clear explanation of what was found, where, and what repair or further work (plumbing repair, drying, or reconstruction) it calls for.

## What separates a good Water Leak Detection response from a bad one

The most common mistake is skipping the diagnostic step entirely and cutting open walls or slab based on where water is visible, which is frequently not where the leak originates, since water travels along framing, vapor barriers, and slab seams before it surfaces. A rushed job also skips the moisture mapping step that tells you how far the water has already spread, which matters if there's a mold or structural conversation coming later with an adjuster. A good leak detection visit documents moisture readings and thermal images at the time of inspection, which becomes useful evidence if a claim is filed and an adjuster asks how the source and extent of the leak were determined. It also distinguishes clearly between "we found the leak" and "we fixed the leak," since detection and plumbing repair are two different scopes of work even when they happen back to back.

## Seasonal & regional considerations

Auburn's housing stock includes a mix of older homes with original cast iron or galvanized supply lines and newer construction on slab, both of which carry different leak patterns. Older pipe tends to develop pinhole leaks from internal corrosion, often first noticed as a slow drop in water pressure or a damp spot that comes and goes. New England's freeze-thaw winters also stress exposed or poorly insulated plumbing runs, so a leak that started as a hairline crack in colder months can widen and become a bigger hidden water leak by spring. Homes with finished basements are worth a periodic check, since water entering through a foundation crack or a slab leak can travel along the underside of flooring for a long time before it's visible.
