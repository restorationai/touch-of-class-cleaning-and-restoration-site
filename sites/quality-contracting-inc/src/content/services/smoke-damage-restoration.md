Smoke moves through a house long after the flames are out, settling into drywall pores, fabric fibers, and HVAC ductwork within hours. A fire in one room can leave acidic residue coating surfaces two floors away, and the longer that residue sits, the harder it bonds to paint, metal, and grout. ## What smoke damage restoration actually involves

Smoke damage restoration is different from general fire cleanup because it targets residue and odor rather than structural char. Soot particles are acidic and microscopic, which means they travel through air currents and settle on cool surfaces like mirrors, chrome fixtures, and HVAC coils long before anyone notices a visible film. The work typically includes cleaning hard surfaces, treating porous materials like drywall and insulation, running HVAC and duct cleaning, and addressing contents separately from the structure itself. Timeline depends heavily on how long the residue sat before cleanup started and whether the fire was protein-based (a kitchen or grease fire, leaving a thin, hard-to-see but strong-smelling film) or synthetic (burning plastics, furniture, or electronics, leaving thicker, sticky black soot). Equipment ranges from HEPA air scrubbers and thermal foggers to hydroxyl generators for odor neutralization, chosen based on what actually burned.

## Our process

1. **Soot characterization.** We identify whether residue is protein-based, synthetic, or a combination, because each type requires a different cleaning agent and approach. Using the wrong cleaner on protein soot can set the smell permanently into surfaces.
2. **Pre-cleaning and surface treatment.** Dry sponging and vacuuming removes loose soot before any wet cleaning begins, since wiping dry soot with a damp cloth can grind it deeper into porous materials.
3. **Thermal fogging or ozone treatment.** For odor that's penetrated beyond the surface, we use thermal fogging to drive deodorizing agents into the same pathways the smoke traveled, or ozone treatment in unoccupied spaces to break down odor molecules at the source.
4. **HVAC and duct cleaning.** Ductwork recirculates smoke odor through the house every time the system runs, so this step is often what determines whether an odor actually stays gone.
5. **Contents cleaning and sealing.** Salvageable fabrics, electronics, and belongings get cleaned separately, and affected surfaces are sealed with an odor-blocking primer before repainting.

## What separates a good smoke damage response from a bad one

The most common mistake is treating smoke damage like a surface cleaning job and stopping once a room smells better to the nose standing in the doorway. Odor that seems gone at room temperature often returns on a humid day or when the heat kicks on, because residue trapped in subfloor, insulation, or duct lining never got addressed. Masking agents and air fresheners cover odor temporarily without removing the acidic residue causing it, and that residue continues etching into metal and glass the longer it's left untreated. Insurance adjusters evaluating a smoke claim typically look for documentation of affected square footage beyond the visible burn area, since smoke migration often extends well past the room of origin, and for evidence that HVAC systems were inspected and cleaned, not just the living space.
