## What water heater flood cleanup actually involves

A leaking or burst water heater is a clean-water loss at the moment of failure, but it rarely stays that way. Tank corrosion, sediment, and rust particulate contaminate the water as it drains, and if it's been sitting for more than a day or two, it starts tracking toward Category 2 territory. The work involves shutting off the water supply and gas or electric to the unit, extracting standing water from flooring and any pooled areas, pulling baseboards and sometimes lower drywall to check the wall cavity, and setting air movers and dehumidifiers to dry the subfloor, framing, and any insulation that got wet. If the tank was in a finished basement or a closet near living space, carpet pad, laminate flooring, and cabinet toe-kicks are common casualties that need to come out rather than dry in place.

## Our process

1. **Source confirmation and shutoff.** We verify the water heater is isolated (supply valve closed, power or gas off) before any extraction starts, since a slow leak that's still active will undo drying work.
2. **Water extraction and material assessment.** We remove standing water with truck-mount or portable extraction equipment and check flooring, baseboards, and drywall moisture content with a meter rather than guessing by sight or smell.
3. **Selective demolition where needed.** Wet carpet pad, saturated laminate, and swollen baseboard typically come out; hardwood and tile often don't, depending on how long the water sat and what's underneath.
4. **Structural drying and monitoring.** Air movers and dehumidifiers are placed based on the affected square footage, and we track moisture readings over several days until subfloor and framing are back to a dry standard, not just surface-dry.
5. **Documentation for the claim.** Photos, moisture logs, and a written scope of the affected materials are compiled for the homeowner and the insurance carrier.

## What separates a good water heater flood response from a bad one

The most common mistake is drying the visible surface and closing the job without checking what's underneath. A subfloor under vinyl or laminate can read dry at the top while the plywood stays saturated for days, and that's exactly where mold gets a foothold within 24 to 72 hours in a warm, enclosed utility space. Another frequent miss is ignoring the wall cavity behind baseboard, especially in a finished basement, where water wicks up drywall from the bottom and stays trapped behind insulation. Adjusters generally want to see moisture readings taken at multiple points, not a single reading, along with photos of the water heater itself (tank corrosion, fitting failure, or valve condition) since that often determines whether the loss is covered as sudden and accidental. A good response also checks for water that traveled to an adjacent room or down through a floor joist bay to the level below, which is easy to miss if the inspection stops at the utility closet door.

## What does water heater flood cleanup cost?

Costs depend on how much water escaped before it was found, what flooring and wall materials got wet, and whether the water sat long enough to affect subfloor or framing.
