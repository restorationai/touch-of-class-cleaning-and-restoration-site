Between wood smoke residue, barn dust tracked in from backyard paddocks, and the humidity swings of a Worcester County summer, fabric and leather here need a cleaning approach that accounts for all of it, not just a quick surface pass with a rental machine.

## Why Hopkinton Homes See Upholstery Wear and Staining

Hopkinton sits on larger rural and semi-rural lots compared to many MetroWest towns, and a lot of that land supports horses, chickens, and dogs that come and go through mudrooms and family rooms. That means pet dander, hay dust, and fine soil particles settle deep into cushions and armrests long before they're visible. Add in the number of older homes still running wood stoves or fireplaces through a Massachusetts winter, and you get a fine soot film that clings to fabric fibers and dulls the color of upholstery well before it looks obviously dirty. Humidity is the other factor: summers here get muggy enough that natural fibers can hold onto moisture and start to smell musty if a cushion gets damp and isn't dried through completely.

## Our Upholstery Cleaning Process for Hopkinton Homes

We start every job by identifying the fiber and construction, cotton, linen, synthetic blend, or leather, because each one reacts differently to heat, moisture, and cleaning solution. Fabric pieces get a pre-inspection for existing stains, a vacuum pass to lift loose soil and pet hair before any liquid touches the material, then hot water extraction or low-moisture cleaning depending on what the fabric can tolerate. Leather upholstery gets a gentler approach: a pH-balanced cleaner followed by a conditioning step, since Hopkinton's dry winter heat and humid summers both pull moisture out of leather over time and accelerate cracking if it's never conditioned. For homes with wood stoves, we pay extra attention to armrests and headrests where soot and skin oils build up fastest, using a targeted pre-treatment before the main clean so we're not just moving residue around.
