## Why Bellingham Properties See Water Damage Issues

Bellingham sits in Norfolk County on a mix of older New England farmhouse lots and mid-century subdivisions, and both building types fail in predictable ways. Older homes in town often still run on private wells and septic systems, which means a failed pressure tank or a cracked distribution box can push gray water into a crawlspace long before anyone notices a smell. Newer split-levels and ranches built from the 1960s through the 1980s typically have finished or partially finished basements below grade, and those slabs take on groundwater fast during the heavy spring thaw or a slow-moving nor'easter. Add in a New England freeze-thaw cycle that stresses copper and PEX supply lines every winter, and burst pipes above finished ceilings are one of the most common calls we get from this part of the county.

## Our Water Damage Restoration Process in Bellingham

We start by identifying the water category and shutting off the source, whether that's a supply line, a sewage backup, or storm intrusion through a foundation crack. Extraction comes next with truck-mount or portable pumps depending on how much standing water is involved, followed by moisture mapping of the subfloor, framing, and insulation so we know what's actually wet versus what just looks wet. Structural drying equipment goes in based on that mapping, not a flat room count, because a Bellingham basement with exposed concrete dries differently than a finished family room with carpet pad and drywall. We monitor moisture content daily until materials read dry per the IICRC S500 standard before any reconstruction starts.
