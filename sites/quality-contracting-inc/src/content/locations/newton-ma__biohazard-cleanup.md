## Why Newton's Housing Stock Shapes Biohazard Cleanup

Newton's residential neighborhoods are dominated by homes built before World War II — colonials, Victorians, and triple-deckers that predate modern construction standards. That matters for biohazard work in ways that aren't immediately obvious. Older hardwood subfloors, plaster walls, and unfinished basement areas are porous in ways that modern materials are not. Biological contamination can wick into wood grain and plaster substrate well beyond the visible surface, meaning the cleanup boundary is almost always larger than what you can see at first inspection.

Multi-family housing — common throughout Newton's denser village centers — adds a layer of coordination. Shared entryways, common stairwells, and adjacent units can all become secondary concern areas depending on the nature and location of the incident. Proper containment at the outset prevents cross-contamination and protects neighbors who have no knowledge of what occurred.

## Our Biohazard Cleanup Process in Newton

Every job begins with a thorough assessment before any material is disturbed. The affected area is isolated using physical barriers and negative air pressure where appropriate, so that particulates and odor do not migrate to unaffected parts of the structure. All contaminated materials — porous surfaces, textiles, sharps containers, and regulated waste — are handled in compliance with Massachusetts Department of Public Health guidelines for biohazardous waste disposal. Nothing is bagged and left curbside; regulated waste follows a documented disposal chain.

After removal, affected surfaces are treated with hospital-grade EPA-registered disinfectants appropriate to the specific biological agents present. We use ATP testing — a rapid surface-contact method — to verify that contamination levels meet acceptable thresholds before we close out the work area. The goal is not just visual cleanliness but measurable decontamination.

We work in plain, unmarked vehicles when requested. Discretion is a standard part of how we operate, not an upgrade.

Newton's older triple-deckers and converted carriage houses — particularly common in the denser village areas — often have finished basement spaces that were retrofitted well after the original construction.
