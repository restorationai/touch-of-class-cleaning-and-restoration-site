## Why Weston Properties See Water Damage Issues

Weston's housing stock skews older and larger than most of its neighbors. A significant share of homes in town were built before modern moisture barriers and vapor retarders were standard practice, which means wall cavities and subfloor assemblies can absorb and hold water in ways that newer construction simply doesn't. Fieldstone and poured-concrete foundations — both common here — are permeable by nature; a sustained rain event or a rising water table in late March can push moisture through a foundation wall without any single visible crack.

The town's heavily wooded lots compound the issue. Root systems from mature oaks and maples grow toward supply and drain lines, and slow-developing root intrusions into older cast-iron or clay drain lines can back water up into finished basements long before a homeowner notices a gurgling drain. Add Weston's position in a watershed that feeds several local ponds and wetland areas, and it becomes clear why basement water intrusion and crawl space saturation are recurring calls in this town — not isolated incidents.

## Our Water Damage Restoration Process in Weston

Every water damage job starts with a thorough moisture mapping survey using thermal imaging and calibrated moisture meters. In Weston's older homes this step is especially important: plaster over wood lath reads dry at the surface while the lath and the framing behind it can remain saturated for days. Skipping this step means leaving hidden moisture that will feed mold growth within 24 to 48 hours of the initial event.

Once affected materials are identified, standing water is removed with truck-mounted extraction equipment, followed by placement of commercial-grade desiccant and refrigerant dehumidifiers and high-velocity air movers positioned to create a directed drying system — not just airflow in a room. Drying progress is logged daily against psychrometric targets so the structure reaches documented dry standard before any reconstruction begins. If materials like plaster, hardwood flooring, or original millwork can be dried in place rather than demolished, that's the approach — both because it's faster and because matching historic trim and plaster profiles in Weston's older homes is expensive and sometimes impossible.

## Local Note

Weston's older Colonial and Georgian homes frequently have original horsehair plaster on interior walls — a material that behaves very differently from modern drywall during a water loss. Horsehair plaster is dense and slow to release moisture, which means drying timelines run longer than industry averages built around half-inch gypsum board. It also means aggressive drying — too much heat, too fast — can cause the plaster to crack and delaminate from the lath, turning a salvageable wall into a demolition and replaster job. Drying targets and equipment settings need to be dialed back and monitored more carefully in these homes, and that's exactly the kind of material-specific judgment that matters in a town where preserving original finishes is often a priority for the homeowner.
