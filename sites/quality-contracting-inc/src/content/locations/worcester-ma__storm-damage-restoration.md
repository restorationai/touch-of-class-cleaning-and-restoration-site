## Why Worcester Properties See Serious Storm Damage

The geography here works against homeowners in a few specific ways. Worcester sits on a series of ridgelines and valleys that funnel and accelerate wind, so gusts during a severe storm routinely run 10–15 mph higher than what the regional forecast suggests. Neighborhoods on exposed high ground — Grafton Hill, Vernon Hill, Tatnuck — take the brunt of straight-line wind events and the occasional tornado-warned cell that tracks up from Connecticut.

The housing stock compounds the problem. The triple-deckers and two-families built between the 1890s and 1920s that line so many Worcester streets were constructed with flat or low-slope roofs, which pool water when gutters back up with debris. Once a seam opens, water finds the old-growth lumber framing fast. Plaster walls — common in pre-war Worcester homes — don't telegraph moisture the way modern drywall does; a ceiling can look intact while the substructure above it is saturated. In the Canal District, mill-conversion lofts present a different challenge: large-span timber frames and original brick masonry that need careful assessment before any interior work begins.

Low-lying blocks near Quinsigamond Village and the Middle River corridor also see groundwater intrusion during heavy storm events, especially when the city's aging combined sewer system surcharges. That's not just a wet basement — it's a contamination issue that requires a different cleanup protocol than clean rainwater.

## Our Storm Damage Restoration Process in Worcester

The first priority after any storm is stopping active water entry. We start with emergency board-up and roof tarping to close the structure before anything else. From there, the process follows a consistent sequence calibrated to what Worcester's older buildings actually need:

**Damage documentation** — We photograph and measure every affected area before touching anything, producing a detailed loss report formatted for insurance carriers. Worcester homeowners frequently deal with multiple-unit claims when a storm damages a triple-decker; we document each unit separately so each tenant's or owner's claim is clean.

**Debris and tree removal** — Downed trees and limbs are cut, staged, and hauled. We coordinate with utility contacts when a tree has brought down a service drop, but we don't work on live lines — that call goes to Eversource.

**Structural drying and moisture mapping** — We use thermal imaging and pin-type moisture meters to find water that has migrated into wall cavities and subfloors. In Worcester's plaster-wall homes, drying timelines run longer than in newer construction; we set drying goals based on actual readings, not a fixed number of days.

**Structural and finish repairs** — Once materials are dry and stable, we move into reconstruction: framing, roofing, siding, windows, insulation, and interior finishes. We carry the work through to a finished state, so you're not managing a handoff between a mitigation company and a separate GC.
