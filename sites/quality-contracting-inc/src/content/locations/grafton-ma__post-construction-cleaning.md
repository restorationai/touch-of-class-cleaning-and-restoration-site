## Why Grafton's Housing Stock Makes Post-Construction Cleanup More Demanding

Grafton's neighborhoods don't share a single building profile, and that matters when you're planning a construction debris cleaning. Around Grafton Center and the Common, you're often working in homes built between the 1780s and the 1880s — structures with horsehair plaster walls, hand-cut timber framing, and fieldstone foundations. Construction dust in these homes behaves differently than in modern builds: plaster is porous and holds particulate matter longer, original millwork has deep profiles that trap sanding residue, and older window glazing is more vulnerable to abrasive cleaning agents.

Move toward Fisherville and Farnumsville along the Blackstone River corridor and the stock shifts to late-19th and early-20th century mill-village housing — denser, with smaller rooms, low attic clearances, and surfaces that have been painted and repainted for over a hundred years. A renovation in one of these homes often disturbs multiple paint layers, creating a cleanup scenario that requires careful surface-by-surface assessment before any cleaning product touches the wall.

Nearer the Grafton MBTA commuter rail station, newer commuter subdivisions from the 1990s and 2000s present a third profile: open floor plans, engineered hardwood, and large windows that show every smear. The final clean standard buyers expect in these homes is high, and the punch-list inspection that follows construction is unforgiving.

## Our Post-Construction and Specialty Cleaning Process in Grafton

A thorough post-construction cleanup moves in phases, and skipping the sequence is what leads to a "clean" room that looks dusty again 48 hours later.

**Phase 1 — Rough debris removal.** We clear all visible construction waste: drywall offcuts, packaging, tape scraps, and any material the trades left behind. This happens before any fine cleaning begins, because dragging debris across a finished floor creates new damage.

**Phase 2 — Top-down dust elimination.** Construction dust is light and stays airborne. We work ceiling to floor — light fixtures, HVAC registers, window sills, door casings — using HEPA-filtered equipment so the particulate is captured rather than redistributed. In Grafton's older homes, this phase takes longer because the decorative profiles on original trim hold dust in ways that flat modern molding simply doesn't.

**Phase 3 — Surface-specific cleaning.** Windows, glass, tile, cabinetry hardware, and appliances each get the appropriate cleaner and technique. Grout haze on new tile, overspray on glass, and adhesive residue on hardware are all addressed individually.

**Phase 4 — Floor finishing.** Hardwood, tile, carpet, and original wide-plank floors each require a different final treatment. We don't apply a single mop pass and call it done.

**Phase 5 — Final walkthrough.** We go room by room against a checklist before we consider the job complete. If something doesn't meet the standard, we address it before leaving the site.
