## Why Southbridge Town Properties See Roofing Problems

Much of Southbridge Town's residential housing was built during the town's manufacturing peak in the early-to-mid twentieth century, when the optical and textile industries drove dense neighborhood construction. That era of building typically means steeper roof pitches, narrow eaves, and underlayment that has long since passed its useful life, even if the surface shingles still look passable from the street.

The regional climate compounds the issue. Worcester County winters routinely cycle through freeze-thaw patterns that force water into small cracks, expand them, and leave gaps that let the next rain event travel straight to the sheathing. Ice dams are a particular concern on the older homes common throughout town, where attic insulation was installed to standards that no longer hold up. A roof that appears sound in October can be hiding saturated decking by March.

Southbridge Town also receives meaningful snowfall accumulation most winters. Flat or low-slope sections on older capes and colonials, often added as rear extensions during mid-century renovations, are especially vulnerable to ponding when gutters ice over.

## Our Roofing Installation and Replacement Process in Southbridge Town

Every project starts with a full deck inspection, not just a shingle count. On homes with board sheathing rather than plywood, common in pre-1960 construction throughout this part of Worcester County, we check for soft spots, rot, and gaps between boards before any new material goes down. Skipping that step is how a new roof ends up failing in year three.

Once the deck is confirmed sound or repaired, we install a self-adhering ice-and-water shield along the eaves and in valleys, sized to meet Massachusetts building code requirements for this climate zone. Felt or synthetic underlayment goes over the field of the roof, followed by the finish material the homeowner has selected. We walk through shingle weight, color, and warranty options before the job starts, not as an afterthought.

Flashing at chimneys, skylights, and wall intersections gets particular attention on older homes, where original flashing may be lead or galvanized steel that has corroded or pulled away from the masonry. Step flashing and counter-flashing are replaced as a matter of course, not left in place to fail again.

Ridge ventilation is assessed on every job. Homes in this region that trap heat in the attic accelerate shingle aging from the underside, a problem that doesn't show up until a roof is already years past its best performance.

## Local Note

Southbridge Town's older triple-deckers and mill-era two-families often have low-slope rear roofs that were originally covered in built-up tar and gravel, then re-covered once or twice over the decades with rolled roofing or modified bitumen. When we strip one of these roofs, it's common to find two or three layers of material, each hiding the condition of the one below.
