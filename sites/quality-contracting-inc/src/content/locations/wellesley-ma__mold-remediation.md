## Why Wellesley Properties See Mold Issues

Wellesley sits in a part of eastern Massachusetts where the soil transitions between glacial till and dense clay — drainage that works reasonably well in a dry summer can back up against a foundation the moment the ground saturates in late fall or early spring. That moisture pressure finds its way into basements and crawl spaces, and from there it migrates into wall cavities and subfloor assemblies.

The town's older neighborhoods are filled with homes built between the 1920s and 1950s, many of which have stone or brick foundations without modern waterproofing membranes. These foundations wick moisture differently than poured concrete, and the plaster-and-lath interior walls common in homes of that era absorb and hold humidity in ways that modern drywall does not. Once relative humidity inside a wall assembly climbs above roughly 60 percent and stays there, mold colonization is a matter of time, not chance.

High-canopy lots — common throughout Wellesley's residential areas — also reduce direct sun exposure on north-facing roof sections and exterior walls, slowing the natural drying that would otherwise limit surface mold on wood sheathing and trim.

## Our Mold Remediation Process in Wellesley

Every remediation starts with a thorough visual inspection and moisture mapping. We use thermal imaging and calibrated moisture meters to locate saturation behind finished surfaces before any demolition begins — this matters especially in older Wellesley homes where disturbing plaster unnecessarily creates more work and more dust.

Once the affected area is identified, we establish negative-air containment using poly sheeting and HEPA-filtered air scrubbers. This keeps mold spores from migrating to unaffected rooms during removal. Contaminated materials — whether drywall, plaster, wood framing, or insulation — are bagged and removed per Massachusetts Department of Environmental Protection guidance on mold-containing debris.

After physical removal, affected structural surfaces are treated and dried to documented moisture targets. We do not close up walls until readings confirm the assembly has reached equilibrium with the surrounding environment. Rushing that step is the most common reason mold returns.

Mold claims in Massachusetts can be complicated by how the originating water loss is categorized. ## Local Note

Wellesley's older homes frequently have balloon-frame construction, where wall cavities run continuously from the basement sill plate to the roof rafters without the fire blocking that platform-frame homes built after the mid-20th century typically include. In a mold scenario, this means spores and moisture can travel vertically through an entire wall assembly from a single source point in the basement. It is not unusual to find mold activity on second-floor framing that traces back to a damp first-floor rim joist — a connection that is easy to miss if you are only inspecting where the visible staining appears.
