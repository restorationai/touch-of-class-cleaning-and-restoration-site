## Why Natick Roofs Wear the Way They Do

Middlesex County's climate pattern means Natick properties accumulate ice dams more readily than homeowners in milder climates expect. When heat escapes through an under-insulated attic, it melts snow at the roof deck while the eaves stay cold. The meltwater refreezes at the overhang, backs up under shingles, and can push water into wall cavities and ceiling assemblies before a single drop shows up inside the house. This is not a drainage problem you solve with a better gutter, it starts at the roof system itself, with proper ice-and-water shield installation at the eaves and rakes, adequate ventilation, and the right shingle profile for a pitched New England roof.

Natick's housing stock adds another layer of complexity. The town grew substantially in the postwar decades, and many neighborhoods carry a mix of mid-century Cape Cods and split-levels alongside older Colonials. Those mid-century builds often used lower roof pitches that shed snow less efficiently than steeper Victorian-era profiles, and their original decking may be board sheathing rather than plywood, something that affects how new shingles are fastened and whether a full deck replacement makes more sense than an overlay.

## Local Note

Natick's older Colonials and Cape Cods frequently have low-slope sections at dormers and shed additions that were originally covered with rolled roofing or modified bitumen rather than shingles. When those sections are replaced as part of a larger re-roof, the transition flashing between the low-slope membrane and the adjacent shingle field is the detail most likely to fail if it is not done deliberately. We treat those transitions as their own scope item, not an afterthought, because that seam is where the next ice dam will find its opening if the flashing is not lapped and sealed correctly.

If your home has a rear addition or a garage connection with a low-pitch roof section, mention it when you call. It changes the material estimate and the installation sequence, and it is better to scope it correctly from the start than to discover it on the day of installation.
