## Why Natick Properties See Storm Damage Differently

Natick's housing stock tells the story of several different eras of construction, and each era has its own vulnerabilities. Older homes — particularly the Colonial and Cape Cod styles built before mid-century — often have original wood-framed roofing systems with board sheathing rather than modern plywood or OSB. When a nor'easter drives rain horizontally at 50-plus mph, that sheathing can absorb water at the joints long before a visible leak appears inside. By the time a homeowner notices a ceiling stain, the framing above it may already be saturated.

Natick also has significant tree canopy. The mature oaks and maples that make neighborhoods attractive in summer become projectiles in a severe windstorm. Tree impact damage is among the most complicated storm losses to scope correctly — a limb through a roof opens the structure to rain intrusion across a much wider area than the visible puncture, and the structural loading from a downed tree can compromise rafters, ridge boards, and even load-bearing walls in ways that aren't obvious from street level.

Finally, Natick's proximity to Lake Cochituate and the Charles River watershed means that soil saturation during heavy rain events can be rapid. When the ground is already at capacity from a prior storm, runoff has nowhere to go — and that water finds its way into basement walls, crawl spaces, and slab edges.

## Our Storm Damage Restoration Process in Natick

The first priority after any storm loss is stopping the exposure. That means emergency tarping and board-up to prevent additional rain intrusion before permanent repairs begin. Once the structure is protected, the real assessment starts: moisture mapping with thermal imaging and pin-type meters to find water that traveled beyond the obvious damage zone, structural inspection to identify compromised framing, and documentation of all affected materials for insurance purposes.

Debris removal comes next — downed limbs, displaced roofing material, broken glass. From there, the scope of repairs is built out: roofing, siding, windows, gutters, interior drywall, insulation, and any structural repairs that the storm load caused. Quality Contracting, Inc. handles the full sequence rather than handing off between trades, which matters when insurance timelines are running and a partially repaired structure is still vulnerable to the next weather system.

Throughout the process, we document with photographs and written scope notes that align with how insurance carriers evaluate storm claims — itemized by damage type and location within the structure, not as a single lump estimate.

## Natick Insurance Coordination

Storm damage claims in Massachusetts are generally covered under standard homeowners' policies as a named peril, but the documentation requirements matter. Carriers want to see the pre-repair condition of every damaged component — which means photographs and written scope before any debris is cleared or temporary repairs are made. We work within that sequence deliberately: document first, stabilize second, repair third. We provide the documentation package directly to your adjuster and can communicate with the carrier on scope disputes, particularly on older homes where like-for-like replacement may involve materials that are no longer standard.
