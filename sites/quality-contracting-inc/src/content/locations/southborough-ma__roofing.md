## Why Southborough Properties See Roofing Deterioration Faster Than Expected

The climate here does most of the damage. Worcester County winters push repeated freeze-thaw cycles through late February and into March, and ice dams are a genuine structural concern on the older homes that make up a significant portion of Southborough's housing stock. When meltwater backs up under shingles and refreezes at the eave line, it forces moisture into the roof deck and, eventually, into the attic and ceiling below. Asphalt shingles that might last 25 years in a milder climate can show granule loss, curling, and cracking well before that mark here.

Southborough also sits in a region where heavy, wet spring snowfall is common. That kind of load is different from dry powder: it packs dense, sits longer, and adds significant weight to roof structures that were engineered to a different era's load assumptions. On cape-style homes with lower-pitched rooflines, that combination of ice dam risk and snow load makes the quality of the roofing system especially consequential.

## Our Roofing Installation and Replacement Process in Southborough

Every replacement project starts with a full inspection of the existing roof deck once the old material is stripped. That step matters here because many Southborough homes have had shingles layered over previous roofing, and the deck condition underneath is rarely what it appears from the surface. Any soft spots, rotted sheathing, or compromised fascia are addressed before a single new shingle goes down.

From there, the process follows a deliberate sequence: ice-and-water shield installed along the eave line and in valleys, synthetic underlayment across the full deck, and then the finish roofing material of your choice. Proper ice-and-water shield installation is not optional in this climate; it is the primary defense against the ice dam damage that central Massachusetts winters reliably produce. Flashing around chimneys, skylights, and roof penetrations is replaced rather than reused, because reusing old flashing is one of the most common reasons a new roof leaks within a few years.

We pull the required permits through the Southborough Building Department before work begins. Permit requirements for roofing replacement are enforced locally, and working without one creates complications at resale and can affect homeowner's insurance claims.

## Local Note: What Southborough's Older Housing Stock Means for Your Roof Deck

Many of the homes in Southborough's established residential areas were built with board sheathing rather than plywood or OSB decking. Spaced board sheathing, common in homes from the mid-twentieth century and earlier, behaves differently under modern roofing systems. It can dry out and shrink over decades, leaving gaps that create uneven nailing surfaces and, in some cases, spots where ice dam moisture has worked its way in and softened individual boards. When we strip a roof on an older Southborough home and find board sheathing, we assess each section carefully before deciding what can stay and what needs to be replaced with new plywood. Homeowners sometimes ask why the deck work adds time to the project: this is why. Skipping that assessment and nailing down new shingles over compromised boards is how a new roof develops problems in year two.
