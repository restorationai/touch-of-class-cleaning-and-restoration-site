## Why Newton Properties See Sewage Backup Issues

Newton is a city of distinct villages, and the plumbing infrastructure beneath them reflects their age. Homes in areas like Newton Centre and Waban — many built between the 1890s and 1940s — frequently have original clay tile laterals connecting to the municipal sewer main. Clay tile is functional when intact, but root intrusion from the mature trees that line Newton's residential streets is a persistent problem. A single large root mass can partially obstruct a lateral for years, causing slow drains, until a heavy rain event or a surge from a blocked main pushes sewage back through the lowest fixture in the house.

Newton also sits in a part of Middlesex County where the water table rises meaningfully during spring snowmelt and after sustained rainfall. Basement floor drains and sump pits in low-lying sections of the city can receive backpressure from a saturated sewer system, particularly in neighborhoods where combined sewer overflows have historically been a concern. That means a sewage backup in Newton isn't always the result of something wrong inside the house — it can be a system-level event that affects multiple properties on the same block.

## Our Sewage Cleanup and Sanitization Process in Newton

Sewage is classified as Category 3 water — the most contaminated type — regardless of whether it originates from a municipal sewer line or a private septic system. The cleanup protocol reflects that.

When we arrive at a Newton property, the first step is containment: sealing off the affected area to prevent cross-contamination into living spaces. Porous materials that have absorbed sewage — drywall, insulation, carpet, subfloor sheathing — are removed and properly disposed of. There is no reliable way to sanitize saturated porous materials after Category 3 exposure, and attempting to dry them in place creates ongoing microbial risk.

After extraction and demo, all affected surfaces are treated with EPA-registered disinfectants. We run air scrubbers with HEPA filtration to address airborne particulates during the process. Structural drying follows, and we monitor moisture readings in framing and concrete until they reach acceptable levels. In Newton's older homes, that drying phase often takes longer than it would in newer construction — plaster and masonry hold moisture differently than modern drywall and OSB, and rushing that phase leads to mold colonization within days.
