## Why Westborough Properties Are Vulnerable After Sudden Damage

Westborough's housing stock spans a wide range of construction eras. Older colonials and capes built before modern vapor barriers and synthetic sheathing rely heavily on wood-board wall systems and older roof decking that absorbs moisture quickly once exposed. A single night of rain against an unprotected opening can saturate wall cavities that would take weeks to dry properly. Newer construction in town tends to use engineered lumber and OSB sheathing — materials that swell and delaminate faster than traditional plywood when they get wet, meaning even a short exposure window matters.

The town also sits in a climate band that produces ice dams regularly in winter. A fire or impact event that strips roofing material in January or February doesn't just leave the structure exposed to rain — it leaves it exposed to freeze-thaw cycling that can force water under remaining shingles and into wall assemblies far from the original damage point. Tarping in these conditions has to account for ice and standing water already present on the roof deck, not just the opening itself.

## Our Board-Up and Tarping Process in Westborough

Every job starts with a rapid exterior assessment — not just the obvious breach, but the surrounding structure. A fire that blows out a first-floor window often vents heat through the eaves; we check the soffit line and roof edge before committing to a board-up plan. For window and door openings, we use exterior-grade plywood cut and fastened to the structural framing, not just the window casing, so the closure holds against wind pressure rather than pulling out at the first gust.

Roof tarping follows a similar logic. We secure tarps with weighted battens and anchor lines tied to ridge or structural points rather than just draping material over the opening. On steeper-pitched roofs — common on older New England homes throughout the area — we use non-penetrating ballast systems where the roof material condition makes fastening inadvisable. The goal is a closure that stays in place through the next storm, not just overnight.

We document every opening, every measurement, and every fastening point with photographs before and after. That documentation matters for your insurance claim and for any contractor doing permanent repairs afterward.

## Local Note: Ice and Existing Moisture Before You Tarp

One thing that matters specifically in Westborough's winters: when a loss event happens during or after a snow or ice event, the roof deck beneath a tarp may already have standing water or ice present before we arrive. Trapping that moisture under a tarp without addressing it first can accelerate damage rather than limit it. Our crew checks the deck condition before securing any tarp, and where water is pooling, we clear it and allow brief drainage before the tarp goes down. It's a small step that makes a real difference in whether the tarp protects the structure or inadvertently holds moisture against it.
