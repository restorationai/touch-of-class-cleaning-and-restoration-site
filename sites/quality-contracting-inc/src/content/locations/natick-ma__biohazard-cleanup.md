## Why Natick Properties Present Unique Biohazard Considerations

Natick's housing stock spans a wide range of construction eras. Older homes — particularly those built before the 1970s — often have porous subfloor materials, plaster-and-lath wall assemblies, and unfinished basement spaces where biological contamination can penetrate deeply and quickly. Unlike modern drywall, which can sometimes be removed in discrete panels, older plaster systems may require more careful assessment to determine how far contamination has traveled beneath the surface.

The town's climate adds another layer of complexity. Middlesex County experiences humid summers and cold, wet winters. In warmer months, biological material degrades faster and odor-causing compounds spread more readily through HVAC systems and into adjacent rooms. In winter, frozen ground and sealed building envelopes can concentrate odors indoors. Timing and containment matter enormously, and both are shaped by the season in which a loss occurs.

## Our Biohazard Cleanup Process in Natick

Every biohazard situation is different, but the remediation framework is consistent. The crew arrives in unmarked vehicles to protect the privacy of everyone involved. The first step is a thorough assessment — identifying all affected surfaces, materials, and airflow pathways before any work begins. Containment barriers are established to prevent cross-contamination to unaffected areas of the home.

Affected porous materials — flooring, subflooring, insulation, soft goods — are carefully removed, bagged, and transported for proper disposal in accordance with Massachusetts Department of Public Health guidelines for regulated medical and biohazardous waste. Hard surfaces are treated with EPA-registered disinfectants appropriate for the specific biological agents present. Air scrubbers with HEPA filtration run throughout the process to address airborne particulates. Once remediation is complete, the space is tested and documented before containment barriers come down.

For properties in Natick with finished basements or multi-unit configurations — common in the denser residential areas near the town center — crew members coordinate closely with property owners or managers to ensure that adjacent units or shared mechanical spaces are assessed as part of the scope.
