## Why Millbury Properties See Duct Buildup Faster Than You'd Expect

Millbury's housing stock spans a wide range: nineteenth-century mill-worker cottages near the river with original or early-replacement ductwork, mid-century ranches in West Millbury, and newer construction around Route 146 and The Shoppes at Blackstone Valley. Each category has its own duct problem.

Older homes near the Blackstone River corridor tend to have galvanized steel ducts with joints that were sealed with cloth tape rather than mastic — tape that dries, cracks, and lets conditioned air (and whatever's in it) escape into wall cavities. When spring runoff raises the water table and stone foundations seep, the relative humidity inside those homes spikes. Humid air moving through dusty ducts is a reliable recipe for mold colonization on duct liner surfaces, and mold can establish a foothold in as little as 48 to 72 hours after a sustained moisture event.

Lake homes on Singletary face a different version of the same problem: high ambient humidity from the water, combined with the start-stop use patterns of seasonal or part-time occupancy, means ducts can sit stagnant for months at a time. Stagnant air in an unoccupied home allows dust, pollen, and spores to settle deep into duct runs where a standard filter change won't reach.

Newer retail and commercial builds near Route 146 typically have larger-diameter commercial ductwork, but high foot traffic and frequent door cycling pull in particulates at a rate that residential systems aren't designed for.

## Our Air Duct Cleaning Process in Millbury

We use a negative-pressure source removal method — the same approach recommended by the National Air Duct Cleaners Association (NADCA) and consistent with EPA guidance on duct cleaning. Here's what that looks like on a Millbury job:

**Access and inspection first.** Before any equipment goes in, we inspect accessible duct sections with a camera or inspection mirror to document what's actually present — dust and debris, biological growth, deteriorated insulation, or pest intrusion. In older Bramanville homes, we frequently find collapsed flex duct sections or disconnected joints that need attention before cleaning makes sense.

**Negative pressure containment.** A truck-mounted or portable high-efficiency vacuum unit connects to the main trunk line and holds the entire duct system under negative pressure. This keeps dislodged debris from blowing back into living spaces — important in occupied homes where residents are present during the work.

**Mechanical agitation.** Rotary brushes and compressed-air whips work through each supply and return branch, loosening debris from duct walls and driving it toward the vacuum. In homes with older galvanized ducts, we use softer brush heads to avoid disturbing deteriorating joints.

**Register and grille cleaning.** Every supply and return grille comes off, gets cleaned, and goes back. We also clean the blower compartment, evaporator coil housing (exterior surfaces), and accessible plenum areas.

**Dryer vent cleaning.** Many Millbury homeowners add dryer vent service at the same visit. Lint accumulation in dryer vents is a leading cause of residential fires, and the longer vent runs common in two-story mill-era homes create more opportunity for blockage.

**Optional sanitization.** If inspection reveals biological growth or persistent odor, we can apply an EPA-registered antimicrobial treatment to duct interior surfaces after cleaning. We'll tell you plainly whether we think it's warranted — it's not a default add-on.
