## Why Hudson Properties See Mold Issues

Hudson sits on the Assabet River in Middlesex County, and that river valley brings the kind of groundwater and humidity patterns that keep basements damp long after a storm has passed. A lot of the town's housing stock was built around its mill-era boom, which means fieldstone and early poured-concrete foundations that were never designed with a vapor barrier in mind. Add New England's humid summer stretches and poorly ventilated crawlspaces, and you get the classic Hudson pattern: musty basements, damp sill plates, and mold creeping up the back of finished walls in homes that otherwise look dry from the inside.

We also see it in the mill buildings that have been converted into residential lofts and mixed-use space downtown. Brick-and-beam construction holds moisture differently than stick-built homes, and old masonry can wick groundwater for months before a tenant notices a smell or a stain. Property managers in those buildings are often the first to call because the condition shows up in a shared stairwell or mechanical room before anyone's individual unit.

## Our Mold Remediation Process

We start with a visual and moisture assessment, not a guess. That means a moisture meter on the suspect wall or subfloor, a look at the HVAC and any recent plumbing history, and a straight answer about whether what you're looking at needs full remediation or just a cleaning and a fix to the moisture source. If containment is warranted, we build negative-air containment around the work area using plastic sheeting and an air scrubber, following the same general approach laid out in the IICRC S520 mold remediation standard, so spores don't travel into the rest of the house or building while we're working.

From there it's removal of affected materials that can't be salvaged, HEPA vacuuming and antimicrobial treatment of surfaces that can, and drying the space down with air movers and dehumidifiers until moisture readings come back to a normal range for the season. We also address the source, whether that's a sump pump that isn't keeping up, a grading issue sending water toward the foundation, or a slow leak around an old cast-iron waste line, because remediation without fixing the source just means a repeat visit in a year.

## Local note

One thing that catches homeowners off guard in Hudson: fieldstone foundations common in the town's older housing stock don't hold a consistent moisture reading the way poured concrete does. A spot can test dry on the surface while the stone and mortar behind it stay saturated, which is part of why we check multiple points across a foundation wall rather than relying on a single reading before deciding a space is actually dry.
