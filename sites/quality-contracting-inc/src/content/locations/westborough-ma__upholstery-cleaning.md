## Why Westborough Upholstery Sees Extra Wear

Many homes in this part of central Massachusetts were built before central air conditioning was standard, which means living rooms rely on window units or ductless splits that cycle humidity unevenly through the season. Fabric upholstery in those rooms can hold onto moisture longer than it would in a house with whole-home HVAC, and that moisture is what lets odor and light mildew take hold in cushion foam. Leather pieces in the same homes face the opposite problem: dry winter heat pulls oils out of the hide, leaving it stiff and prone to cracking at the seams if it isn't conditioned regularly. We see both patterns often enough in Worcester County that we calibrate our approach to the piece and the room, not a one-size method.

## Our Upholstery Cleaning Process in Westborough

We start with a fabric and fiber check before any water or cleaning agent touches the piece. Cotton and linen blends common in older New England furniture react differently under heat than synthetic microfiber, so the wrong setting can set a stain instead of lifting it. For fabric sofas and sectionals, we use low-moisture steam extraction that agitates the fibers, pulls out embedded soil, and rinses cleaning solution back out rather than leaving it to dry in the cushions. For leather, we clean with a pH-balanced solution suited to the finish, then follow with a conditioning treatment so the hide doesn't dry out faster once we've lifted the surface oils and grime. Drying time in humid months runs longer than a manufacturer's spec sheet suggests, so we set up air movement and check cushions before we consider the job finished, not just the visible fabric surface.

One detail that surprises homeowners here: town water in this part of Worcester County runs on the harder side, which means tap water used in DIY carpet-and-upholstery rental machines can leave a mineral film on fabric as it dries, especially on lighter-colored sofas.
