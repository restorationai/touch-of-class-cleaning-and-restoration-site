## Our Carpet Cleaning Process in Oxford

Every job starts with a dry inspection: we walk the affected rooms, identify high-traffic lanes, spot potential staining agents (pet urine, iron-rich well water deposits, tracked-in road treatment chemicals common along Route 12 in winter), and note fiber type. Wool and wool-blend carpets found in some of Oxford's older village homes require lower-pH solutions and gentler agitation than the synthetic cut-pile common in newer subdivisions — we treat them differently.

From there, the process moves through four stages:

1. **Pre-treatment and dwell time** — A targeted pre-spray loosens bonded soils and begins breaking down stain compounds before any heat is applied.
2. **Hot-water extraction** — Truck-mounted equipment delivers water at controlled temperature and pressure, then recovers it along with suspended soils in a single pass. The recovery rate matters as much as the injection pressure; under-extracted carpets stay wet too long and invite mildew.
3. **Spot treatment** — Stubborn stains — rust from iron-laden well water, tannin from tracked-in leaf debris, pet urine salts — get targeted treatment with the appropriate enzyme or reducing agent after the main extraction pass.
4. **Grooming and dry-time management** — Pile is groomed to promote even drying, and we advise on airflow. In Oxford's humid summer months, we recommend running central air or fans for at least four hours post-cleaning; in winter, baseboard heat alone is rarely sufficient.
