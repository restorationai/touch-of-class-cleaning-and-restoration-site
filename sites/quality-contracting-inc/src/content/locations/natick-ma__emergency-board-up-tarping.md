## Why Natick Properties Face Distinct Board-Up and Tarping Needs

Natick's housing stock tells a lot of the story. A significant share of the town's residential neighborhoods were built between the 1930s and the 1960s, when balloon-frame and platform-frame construction was standard. Those older wall cavities are more open than modern construction, which means a broken window or a fire-damaged exterior wall doesn't just expose one room — wind-driven rain can travel laterally through wall framing and reach areas far from the original opening.

The climate compounds this. MetroWest Massachusetts gets genuine freeze-thaw cycling from late October through April. A roof breach left unprotected overnight in February doesn't just let in rain; it lets in air cold enough to freeze any standing water in the attic or upper-floor framing, expanding it and widening the damage. Ice dams — a perennial problem on older Natick roofs with inadequate insulation — can lift shingles and create openings that look minor from the ground but are actively channeling meltwater into the structure. Tarping those areas correctly, weighted and sealed at the ridge rather than just draped, is the difference between containing the loss and watching it grow.

## Our Board-Up and Tarping Process in Natick

Every board-up or tarping job starts with a quick exterior and interior assessment — not a full damage estimate, but enough to understand what's open, what's at risk of secondary exposure, and what the structure can safely support for attachment points.

For window and door board-ups, we use exterior-grade plywood cut to fit the opening, secured with structural screws rather than nails, so the panel holds against wind load without damaging the surrounding frame further. On older Natick homes with wood siding or original window casings, we take extra care with fastener placement to avoid splitting material that's already weathered.

Roof tarping on pitched New England roofs requires more than throwing a blue poly tarp over the damage. We anchor tarps at the ridge using cap boards — horizontal lumber that distributes tension across the tarp rather than concentrating stress at grommets, which tear out in wind. The tarp runs over the ridge and down both sides when possible, so water sheds away from the opening rather than pooling at its edges. We weight and secure the lower edges to prevent uplift.

Fire-damaged openings get an additional step: we check for smoldering material near the board-up area before sealing, since enclosing an area with residual heat can create conditions that reignite. The board-up is staged to allow any remaining heat to dissipate while still blocking weather and unauthorized entry.

## Working with Insurance After a Natick Loss

Emergency board-up and tarping is almost universally covered under homeowners' policies as a mitigation expense — it's the action that prevents a covered loss from getting worse. Most carriers want documentation: photographs of the opening before and after, a written scope of what was installed, and sometimes a signed authorization from the policyholder.

Quality Contracting provides that documentation as a standard part of every job. We photograph the damage, record materials used, and can communicate directly with your adjuster's office. Natick properties covered under older policies with actual-cash-value language may see depreciation applied to the underlying damage, but the mitigation work itself is typically reimbursed at full cost — your adjuster can confirm the specifics of your coverage.

## Local Note

Natick's older neighborhoods include a number of homes with slate or clay tile roofing — materials that were popular in the mid-twentieth century and are still found on properties near the town center and on some of the larger lots along the Route 135 corridor. Tarping over slate or tile requires a different approach than tarping over asphalt shingles: the tiles are brittle and uneven, so walking the roof or placing boards directly on the surface can cause additional breakage. We use ridge-anchored systems that minimize foot traffic on the field of the roof and avoid direct contact with the tile wherever possible. It's a slower setup, but it avoids turning a tarp job into a tile replacement job.
