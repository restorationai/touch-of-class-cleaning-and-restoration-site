## Why Grafton Homes See Duct Buildup Faster Than You'd Expect

Grafton's housing stock spans roughly three centuries and three distinct building patterns, and each one creates its own duct-cleaning challenge.

Around Grafton Center and the Common, homes built before 1900 were often retrofitted with forced-air systems long after original construction. Those installations frequently used non-standard duct dimensions, sharp bends, and uninsulated runs through unheated crawl spaces — conditions that encourage condensation, dust adhesion, and, over time, microbial growth. The fieldstone foundations common in this part of town also allow ground moisture to migrate upward, raising relative humidity at the air handler and in the lower duct runs.

In Fisherville and Farnumsville, the mill-village housing stock along the Blackstone River tends to be densely packed and older, with ductwork that may not have been professionally serviced in years. River-adjacent properties also see higher ambient humidity and seasonal flooding risk, both of which can introduce moisture into duct systems through return-air grilles located near grade.

Closer to the Grafton MBTA commuter rail station, newer subdivisions built in the 2000s and 2010s present a different issue: builder-grade flex duct that sags, kinks, or separates at connections over time, creating dead zones where debris collects and airflow drops. Residents who moved in from Boston or Providence expecting city air quality sometimes notice the difference in air circulation within the first heating season.

## Our Duct Cleaning Process in Grafton

Every job starts with a visual inspection — we pull grilles, photograph the interior of accessible trunk lines, and note any signs of moisture, pest intrusion, or duct damage before a single brush turns. This matters especially in older Grafton homes where we occasionally find disconnected duct sections or original asbestos-wrapped fittings that require a different handling protocol before cleaning can proceed.

For standard cleaning, we use truck-mounted negative-pressure equipment combined with rotary brush agitation on the supply and return sides. The negative-pressure setup keeps loosened debris from migrating into living spaces — a detail that matters in homes where furniture and finished surfaces are close to supply registers. After mechanical cleaning, we offer an EPA-registered antimicrobial treatment for duct interiors when moisture or biological growth is confirmed during inspection; we apply it only where the inspection justifies it, not as a default upsell.

Dryer vent cleaning is available as an add-on or standalone service. In Grafton's older homes, dryer vents frequently run long horizontal distances through exterior walls before exhausting — sometimes with undocumented bends added during renovations. We use a flexible rod system with a camera attachment to confirm the vent is clear and properly exhausting before we close out the job.
