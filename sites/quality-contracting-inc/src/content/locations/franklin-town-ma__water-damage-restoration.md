## Why Franklin Town Properties See Water Damage Issues

Much of the housing stock in this part of Massachusetts was built before modern vapor barrier standards, which means basements and crawlspaces were never designed to handle sustained groundwater pressure. Add in the freeze-thaw cycle that defines a typical New England winter, and you get pipes that crack from expansion, foundations that develop hairline cracks over the years, and sump pumps that get overworked during spring thaw and summer downpours. Homes with finished basements are especially vulnerable because insulation and drywall trap moisture against framing long after the visible water is gone, setting up conditions for microbial growth within 24 to 48 hours if drying is delayed.

## Our Water Damage Restoration Process in Franklin Town

We start every call with extraction, pulling standing water with truck-mount or portable units depending on how much square footage is affected and whether the water reached finished living space or stayed confined to a basement or utility area. From there we map moisture with meters and thermal imaging, because surface-dry drywall or subfloor can still be holding water in the layers underneath, particularly in older homes where plaster and lath behave differently than modern drywall. Structural drying follows, using air movers and dehumidification sized to the room's humidity load, not a flat equipment count, and we track readings daily until materials hit dry standard rather than guessing at a timeline. For homes built with full basements, which is common across this area, we pay close attention to sill plates and framing where water tends to wick upward long after the floor looks dry.

A lot of Franklin Town basements were finished out gradually over decades rather than built that way originally, which means the drywall, insulation, and flooring underneath weren't always installed with a moisture barrier between them and the slab.
