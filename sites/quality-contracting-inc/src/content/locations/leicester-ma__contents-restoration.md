## Why Leicester Properties See Unique Contents Damage Patterns

The housing stock along Route 9 and throughout Leicester's villages tells a story of layered construction eras. Mill-era homes in Rochdale and Cherry Valley frequently have plaster-and-lath walls, wide-plank wood floors, and older cabinetry that absorbs moisture and smoke odor differently than modern drywall construction. When water intrudes — whether from a frozen supply line, a failed sump pump, or a roof breach after a heavy ice storm — it wicks into furniture joints, saturates upholstery stuffing, and penetrates wood grain in ways that require slower, more deliberate drying than a newer home might.

Properties ringing Stiles Reservoir and Lake Sargent add another layer of complexity. Many of those homes sit on private well and septic systems, and seasonal use means a slow leak can go undetected for weeks before anyone opens the door. By then, contents have been sitting in elevated humidity long enough for mold to begin colonizing fabric and paper within 48 to 72 hours of the initial moisture event. That timeline matters enormously when deciding which items can be saved and which cannot.

Smoke and fire losses carry their own Leicester-specific wrinkle. Older homes with plaster ceilings and original woodwork often have gaps and cavities where smoke odor embeds deeply — and the contents inside absorb that same odor. A piece of furniture that looks visually intact after a kitchen fire may still carry volatile organic compounds that make it unlivable without professional cleaning.

## Our Contents Pack-Out and Storage Process in Leicester

The first step when we arrive is a room-by-room contents inventory. Every item is photographed, logged by category, and assessed for restorability before anything is moved. This documentation matters for your insurance claim and also protects against disputes about pre-loss condition.

Items that can be restored are carefully packed and transported to our climate-controlled storage facility. There, belongings restoration work begins: ultrasonic cleaning for hard goods, ozone and hydroxyl treatment for smoke-saturated soft goods, controlled drying for water-damaged furniture, and HEPA-filtered cleaning for electronics and documents. Delicate items — framed artwork, antiques, family heirlooms common in Leicester's older homes — are handled separately with appropriate padding and environmental controls.

Storage continues in a secure, monitored environment until your property is ready for move-back. At that point, we coordinate delivery and placement so the return of your belongings aligns with the completion of structural repairs, not before.

One pattern we've seen repeatedly in Leicester's mill-village homes: original wide-plank pine floors and antique furniture built from the same regional wood species absorb smoke odor into the grain at a slower rate than upholstery, but they also release it more slowly during treatment.
