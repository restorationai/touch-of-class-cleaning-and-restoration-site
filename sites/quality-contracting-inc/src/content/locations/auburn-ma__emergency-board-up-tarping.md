## Why Auburn Properties Are Particularly Vulnerable

A significant portion of Auburn's residential housing stock was built between the 1940s and 1970s — cape-style and split-level homes that are common throughout the town's older neighborhoods. These structures tend to have shallower roof pitches and aging asphalt shingles that are more susceptible to wind uplift than modern architectural shingles. When a branch comes down or a ridge cap blows off, the decking underneath can absorb water within minutes, not hours.

Auburn also sits in a frost-heave zone. Freeze-thaw cycles through the winter months stress masonry foundations, window frames, and door surrounds — meaning a window that was slightly loose in October can fail outright during a January cold snap, leaving an opening that needs immediate boarding. ## Our Emergency Board-Up and Tarping Process in Auburn

When you call Quality Contracting, Inc. at (508) 756-8800, the first thing we do is triage the opening over the phone — size, location on the structure, whether there's active weather moving through — so we arrive with the right materials loaded and don't waste time making a second trip.

On site, the process follows a consistent sequence:

1. **Hazard assessment** — We walk the perimeter before touching anything. Broken glass, compromised structural members, and live electrical near water all get identified before boarding begins.
2. **Measurement and material prep** — We cut plywood to fit openings precisely rather than nailing oversized sheets that leave gaps. For fire-damaged openings, we use heavier 3/4-inch CDX plywood and secure it with structural screws, not just nails, because fire-weakened framing needs more distributed load.
3. **Roof tarping** — Heavy-duty polyethylene tarps (minimum 6-mil, typically 10-mil for storm damage) are run over the ridge and secured with weighted cap boards screwed into the decking — not just bungee-corded to the gutters, which fail in the next wind event.
4. **Documentation** — We photograph every opening before and after boarding, with measurements noted. This documentation package goes directly to your insurance adjuster and is formatted to match what most carriers request for emergency mitigation claims.

## Local Note

One thing worth knowing about older cape-style homes common in Auburn: the roof-to-wall junction on a cape is a low-slope transition point where tarping requires extra attention. Standard flat-run tarps tend to pool water right at that break rather than shedding it, which can push moisture under the tarp and into the wall cavity. We use a two-tarp overlap technique at that transition — running a secondary strip tarp under the main field tarp at the knee wall line — to redirect water away from the most vulnerable joint. It's a small detail that makes a meaningful difference in how well the tarp performs through a week of New England rain while your insurance claim is being processed.
