Wellesley's mix of large colonial revivals, Victorians, and mid-century ranches means that when a pipe bursts behind a plaster wall or a kitchen fire leaves smoke residue on antique furniture, the belongings inside are often as irreplaceable as the structure itself. Contents restoration and storage is the part of recovery most homeowners don't think about until they're standing in a smoke-damaged living room wondering what can actually be saved — and what the process looks like from pack-out to return.

## Why Wellesley Homes Present Unique Contents Challenges

A significant portion of Wellesley's housing stock predates World War II. Older homes in this area tend to carry more porous, absorbent materials — plaster ceilings, hardwood floors finished with oil-based products, wool rugs, upholstered pieces with horsehair or cotton batting. These materials hold odor and moisture differently than modern synthetics. Smoke from a kitchen fire doesn't just coat surfaces; it penetrates the fibers of upholstered chairs and settles into the grain of unfinished wood. Water from a burst supply line doesn't evaporate from a wool rug the way it does from a nylon carpet pad.

Wellesley's winters also matter here. Freeze-thaw cycles stress older plumbing, and when a pipe fails in January or February, the response window before secondary damage — mold colonization can begin within 24 to 48 hours of a water intrusion — is short. Getting contents out of the affected space quickly is often the difference between cleaning a piece and replacing it.

## Our Contents Pack-Out and Restoration Process

Every contents job begins with a detailed room-by-room inventory before anything is moved. Each item is photographed, catalogued, and assessed for the appropriate cleaning method — ultrasonic cleaning for hard goods and small valuables, ozone or hydroxyl treatment for soft goods with embedded odor, dry-cleaning processes for textiles and window treatments. Items that can be restored on-site are handled there; items requiring deeper treatment are carefully packed and transported to a climate-controlled storage facility.

Storage is not a holding pen. It's a controlled environment where items are protected from temperature swings and humidity while the structural work at your property is completed. When your home is ready, contents are returned in the same catalogued order they left — nothing arrives back in a generic box labeled "miscellaneous."

For furniture restoration after fire or water damage, the cleaning sequence matters. Soot is acidic, and delay causes permanent etching on finished wood and metal hardware. Pieces are cleaned in priority order based on material vulnerability, not convenience.

## Working with Insurance on Contents Claims in Wellesley

Contents losses are frequently underdocumented in insurance claims. Carriers require itemized inventories with pre-loss values, and homeowners rarely have that documentation ready when they need it. The pack-out inventory Quality Contracting produces serves as the foundation for your adjuster's review — photographs, item descriptions, and condition notes are all captured before anything leaves the property.

## Local Note

Older Wellesley homes with original horsehair plaster walls present a specific challenge during contents pack-out: demolition or drying work on those walls releases fine particulate that settles on everything in adjacent rooms — including furniture and soft goods that weren't directly affected by the original loss. It's worth doing a secondary wipe-down of items in neighboring rooms even when they appear clean, because that fine dust is abrasive and can dull finished surfaces over time if it's left in place. It's a detail that matters in a town where a lot of the furniture is as old as the house.
