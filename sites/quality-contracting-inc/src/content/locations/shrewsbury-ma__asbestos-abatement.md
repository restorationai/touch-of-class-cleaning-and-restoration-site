## Our Asbestos Abatement Process in Shrewsbury

Every project begins with a thorough inspection and bulk sampling before any abatement work is scheduled. Samples are sent to an accredited laboratory, and results typically return within a few business days. Once the presence of asbestos-containing material is confirmed, we develop a written abatement plan that complies with Massachusetts Department of Environmental Protection (MassDEP) regulations and EPA guidelines — both of which impose specific notification, containment, and disposal requirements for licensed abatement contractors working in the state.

During abatement, the work area is sealed with heavy-gauge polyethylene sheeting and maintained under negative air pressure using HEPA-filtered air scrubbers. Workers wear full personal protective equipment, and all removed material is wetted to suppress fiber release, double-bagged in labeled disposal bags, and transported to a licensed disposal facility. Air clearance testing is conducted after removal and before containment is broken — not as an optional add-on, but as a required step under Massachusetts regulations. You receive documentation of the clearance result for your records, which is especially important if you're selling the property or pulling a building permit.

One pattern that comes up repeatedly in Shrewsbury's older neighborhoods — particularly in homes that have changed hands multiple times since the 1970s — is layered flooring. In Massachusetts, if any layer is suspected to contain asbestos, the entire assembly needs to be assessed before demolition continues.
