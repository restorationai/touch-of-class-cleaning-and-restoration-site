## Our Duct Cleaning Process, Calibrated to Shrewsbury Conditions

We begin every job with a camera inspection of the main trunk lines and branch runs before any equipment goes in. In older Shrewsbury homes, this step catches problems that change the cleaning approach: collapsed flex sections, disconnected joints behind finished ceilings, or evidence of past rodent activity in the ductwork. Skipping the inspection and going straight to brushing can push debris deeper or miss a bigger issue entirely.

Once we understand what we're working with, we use negative-air pressure — a high-CFM vacuum collection unit connected to the main trunk — while rotary brush agitation works through each branch run from the register back toward the air handler. Every supply and return register is cleaned individually. The air handler cabinet, blower wheel, and evaporator coil housing are wiped down as part of the same visit, because cleaning ducts while leaving a contaminated blower wheel just recirculates the problem.

For homes near the Lake Quinsigamond shoreline where humidity-driven mold growth is a documented concern, we can apply an EPA-registered antimicrobial treatment to interior duct surfaces after mechanical cleaning — this is an add-on, not a substitute for the physical cleaning itself.

Dryer vent cleaning is frequently requested alongside duct work, and for good reason: the short, often-kinked dryer vent runs common in Shrewsbury's cape-style homes accumulate lint quickly and represent a real fire risk.
