## Why Worcester Properties See Elevated Board-Up and Tarping Needs

The city's housing stock tells the story. The dense rows of flat-roofed triple-deckers built between the 1890s and 1920s — common in Main South, Vernon Hill, and Burncoat — were constructed before modern waterproofing membranes existed. A flat roof that loses even a small section of roofing material after wind damage can funnel hundreds of gallons into the floors below within a single rainstorm. Ice dams are a compounding problem: Worcester's steep hills and hard freezes create ideal conditions for ice to back up under aging shingles, and when that ice releases in a late-winter thaw, the resulting water intrusion can blow out soffit panels and leave roof decking exposed.

Fire damage is the other major driver. Worcester sees a meaningful number of residential structure fires each year, and after firefighters clear a scene, the building is rarely secure. Broken windows, compromised door frames, and sections of roof opened by ventilation cuts all need to be addressed before the next weather event — and before opportunistic entry becomes a secondary problem. The Canal District's converted mill buildings present their own challenge: large industrial window openings and older masonry that doesn't accommodate standard plywood sizing without custom framing.

## Our Emergency Board-Up and Tarping Process in Worcester

Every job starts with a rapid site assessment. We walk the perimeter and interior (where safe entry is possible) to catalog every opening — windows, doors, roof penetrations, and any structural gaps left by fire department operations. Photographs are taken before a single piece of material goes up, because that documentation is what your insurance adjuster will use to establish the pre-mitigation condition of the property.

For window and door openings, we use exterior-grade plywood cut and fastened to the structural framing, not just the window casing. On Worcester's older wood-frame homes, the casing itself is often soft or fire-damaged, so anchoring to solid framing is the only approach that holds through a New England windstorm. For roof damage, heavy-duty polyethylene tarps are secured with weighted battens and fastened at the eaves — not just draped and held with sandbags, which fail in the kind of sustained wind gusts Worcester gets off the hills in winter.

Larger roof losses may require plywood decking laid over the damaged area before the tarp goes on, particularly on flat-roof structures where standing water would otherwise pool and add structural load. We stage materials efficiently to minimize the footprint on the property and to keep adjacent units in multi-family buildings accessible.

## Local Note: What Worcester's Flat Roofs Demand That Pitched Roofs Don't

Contractors who work primarily in suburban markets sometimes underestimate how differently a flat-roof triple-decker behaves after storm or fire damage. On a pitched roof, water sheds to the eaves; on a flat roof in ZIP code 01610 or 01603, any tarp installation that doesn't account for positive drainage will create a pond. We pitch tarps slightly using furring strips under the leading edge so water runs to the gutter line rather than pooling at center-span. It's a small adjustment, but on a building with three occupied floors below, it's the difference between a tarp that holds through a March rain and one that collapses under the weight of standing water at 2 a.m.

Most homeowners and landlords in Worcester carry property insurance that covers emergency protective measures — board-up and tarping are typically considered part of the covered loss, not a separate out-of-pocket expense.
