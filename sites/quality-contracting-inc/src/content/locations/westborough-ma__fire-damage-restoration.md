## Why Westborough Properties Are Vulnerable to Fire Spread and Smoke Penetration

Westborough's residential neighborhoods include a significant share of homes built in the mid-twentieth century, many with balloon-frame or platform-frame construction. In balloon framing — common in houses built before the 1960s — wall cavities run continuously from the foundation sill to the roof line, giving smoke and superheated gases an unobstructed vertical channel. A fire that appears contained to a first-floor kitchen can push smoke into attic insulation and second-floor wall cavities within minutes.

The town also sits in a part of central Worcester County where winter heating season is long and wood-burning fireplaces and pellet stoves are common secondary heat sources. Creosote buildup, chimney liner failures, and improper hearth clearances are recurring ignition points in this climate. That means fire damage here often starts at a chimney chase and radiates outward through the roof structure — a pattern that requires careful decking and rafter inspection before any interior restoration begins.

## Our Fire Damage Restoration Process in Westborough

Every fire loss is different, but the sequence of work follows a disciplined order that prevents secondary damage from compounding the original loss.

**Stabilization first.** Before any cleaning begins, the structure needs to be secured — boarding windows and doors, tarping compromised roof sections, and shutting off utilities if the fire department has not already done so. Westborough's winters mean an unsecured structure can sustain freeze damage within hours of a cold-weather fire.

**Scope and documentation.** Every affected surface, system, and material is catalogued with photographs and moisture readings. This documentation is what your insurance adjuster will use to evaluate the claim, and it is what keeps the scope of work from being disputed later.

**Soot and smoke removal.** Dry soot from fast-burning fires brushes off differently than the oily, wet soot left by smoldering synthetic materials. The cleaning method — dry chemical sponges, HEPA vacuuming, alkaline cleaners — is matched to the residue type, not applied uniformly across every surface.

**Odor neutralization.** Thermal fogging and hydroxyl generation address smoke odor at the molecular level, reaching into porous materials that surface cleaning cannot touch. In homes with plaster walls — still common in Westborough's pre-1960 housing stock — odor can persist inside the plaster matrix itself and may require encapsulation or selective demolition.

**Structural repair and rebuild.** Once the structure is clean and dry, framing, sheathing, insulation, drywall, and finishes are restored to pre-loss condition.
