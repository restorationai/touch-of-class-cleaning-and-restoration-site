## Why Wellesley Properties See Board-Up and Tarping Needs

The town's tree canopy is one of the defining features of neighborhoods like Cliff Estates and the areas surrounding Wellesley College — and it is also one of the most consistent sources of structural damage during storm events. Mature oaks and elms with root systems constrained by decades of landscaping are prone to partial failure: a limb heavy enough to punch through a slate or asphalt roof, shatter a double-hung window, or cave in a porch roof. Because so many Wellesley homes were built between roughly 1890 and 1950, original-growth framing members are common — dense and durable, but often without modern hurricane strapping, which means roof sections can lift or shift under wind loads that newer construction handles without issue.

Fire damage board-up is a separate but equally urgent need. Older wood-frame construction in Wellesley burns fast and leaves large, irregular openings that standard plywood sizing does not cover cleanly. Smoke and water from suppression efforts also weaken surrounding framing, so the board-up crew needs to assess structural integrity at every opening before anchoring panels — a step that takes longer on a century-old balloon-frame home than on a 1990s platform-frame one.

## Our Emergency Board-Up and Tarping Process in Wellesley

When we arrive on site, the first priority is a rapid perimeter walk to identify every compromised opening — not just the obvious ones. A broken window board-up that misses a cracked basement light well or a displaced soffit panel leaves the property exposed. We photograph each opening before and after securing it, which creates a documented record your insurance adjuster can use without a second site visit.

For roof tarping, we use reinforced polyethylene tarps weighted and secured with wood battens screwed into undamaged decking — not sandbags dropped over the ridge, which shift and fail in wind. On Wellesley's older slate and clay-tile roofs, we take extra care not to crack intact field tiles during access, because replacement materials for pre-war roofing can be difficult to source and expensive to match. Where a roof section has partially collapsed, we do not walk it; we work from ladders and ridge access points to keep the tarp installation safe and the remaining structure undisturbed.

Board-up panels are cut to fit each opening, shimmed plumb, and fastened into structural framing rather than just finish trim. On historic or architecturally detailed exteriors — common throughout Wellesley's older residential corridors — we use fastener patterns that minimize damage to original millwork so restoration work starts from a cleaner baseline.

## Local Note

Wellesley's older homes frequently have slate roofs, and slate is brittle in cold weather — a detail that matters during winter storm response. When temperatures drop below freezing, foot traffic on a damaged slate field can fracture intact tiles well beyond the original impact zone, turning a contained repair into a full-section replacement. Our crew approaches winter roof tarping on slate and tile surfaces from the eaves and ridge rather than walking the field directly, using standoff ladders and extension tools to position and secure the tarp without adding new breakage. It takes a few extra minutes; it saves a significant amount in avoided tile replacement.
