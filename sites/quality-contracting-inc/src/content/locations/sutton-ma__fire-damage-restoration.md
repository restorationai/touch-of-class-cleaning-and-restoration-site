## Why Sutton Properties Face Distinct Fire Damage Challenges

Sutton's housing mix is unusually varied for a town its size. The mill village neighborhoods of Manchaug and Wilkinsonville contain worker cottages and converted industrial buildings from the late 1800s and early 1900s — structures with balloon-frame construction, horsehair plaster, and narrow stud cavities that allow smoke and heat to travel vertically through walls with very little resistance. A kitchen fire in one of these homes can push smoke into the attic and down into the basement in the same event.

Lake homes on Singletary and Manchaug Pond often feature open floor plans and cathedral ceilings that look beautiful until a fire occurs — those large air volumes carry soot farther and deposit it on surfaces that are difficult to reach without scaffolding or lifts. Add to that the fact that most of Sutton runs on private wells and septic rather than municipal utilities, and post-fire water management becomes more complicated: water used in suppression has nowhere to go except into the ground or into the structure itself.

## Our Fire Damage Restoration Process in Sutton

The first priority after the fire department clears a scene is stabilization. That means boarding windows and doors, tarping any compromised roof sections, and assessing whether the structure is safe to enter. In Sutton's older homes, that structural assessment matters more than in newer builds — balloon framing and post-and-beam construction respond to heat stress differently than modern platform framing, and what looks intact from the outside may have compromised load-bearing members inside.

Once the structure is secure, the work moves in a deliberate sequence:

- **Debris and char removal** — burned materials are documented, photographed for the insurance claim, and removed. Structural lumber is tested for integrity before any decision is made to replace or sister it.
- **Soot and smoke residue cleaning** — dry sponging for loose soot, chemical sponging for oily residues, and HEPA vacuuming throughout. Smoke residue in plaster walls requires different chemistry than residue on drywall.
- **Odor neutralization** — thermal fogging and hydroxyl treatment reach into wall cavities and subflooring where spraying alone cannot. In homes with granite ledge foundations — common throughout Sutton — we pay close attention to the basement, where smoke can pool and odor can linger for months if not treated at the source.
- **Structural drying** — suppression water soaks into subfloors, wall cavities, and insulation. We use calibrated drying equipment and monitor moisture readings daily until materials reach acceptable levels.
- **Reconstruction coordination** — once the remediation phase is complete, we coordinate the rebuild, managing scope, permits, and inspections through the Sutton Building Department.
