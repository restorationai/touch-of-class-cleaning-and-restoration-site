## Our Air Duct Cleaning Process in Leicester

Every job starts with a camera inspection of the main trunk lines and as many branch runs as access allows. This step matters more in Leicester's older homes than in newer construction because we frequently find disconnected flex duct, collapsed inner liners, or debris dams that a standard cleaning would push further into the system rather than remove. We document what we find before any equipment goes to work.

Cleaning is performed with truck-mounted negative-air equipment — a continuous vacuum that keeps the system under negative pressure throughout the process, so loosened debris travels out of the home rather than redistributing through supply registers. Technicians work each branch individually with rotary brush agitation, then address the air handler cabinet, blower wheel, and evaporator coil housing. On homes where dryer vent cleaning is needed — a separate but frequently overlooked fire risk — we handle that in the same visit so the exterior wall penetration is inspected and cleared at the same time.

If sanitization is requested, we apply an EPA-registered antimicrobial agent to interior duct surfaces after cleaning is complete. We don't recommend sanitization on every job — it's most appropriate where visible biological growth or persistent musty odor is present after mechanical cleaning.
