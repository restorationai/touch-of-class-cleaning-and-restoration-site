## Why Wellesley Roofs Wear the Way They Do

The town sits in a climate band that delivers genuine freeze-thaw cycles every winter. Water works into small cracks, freezes, expands, and widens those cracks before the next thaw. On older homes with original or single-layer roofing, this process accelerates granule loss and eventually compromises the deck beneath. Ice dams are a particular concern on houses with low-slope sections or dormers, meltwater backs up behind the dam, finds the smallest gap in flashing or underlayment, and migrates into the attic or wall cavity before a homeowner notices anything inside.

Wellesley's tree cover, which gives the town much of its character, also means gutters fill fast and roof surfaces stay damp longer than they would on an open lot. That sustained moisture is exactly the condition that allows moss and algae to colonize asphalt shingles, shortening their effective lifespan and voiding some manufacturer warranties if left untreated.

## Our Roofing Installation and Replacement Process in Wellesley

A replacement project here starts with a thorough inspection of the deck, not just the surface layer. In homes built before the 1970s, it's common to find skip sheathing or board decking beneath the existing shingles rather than plywood. That substrate affects how new materials are fastened and whether a solid decking layer needs to be added before installation begins.

Once the deck condition is confirmed, old material is stripped completely. Full tear-off lets the crew inspect every inch of the substrate and address any soft spots, rot, or compromised flashing before new underlayment goes down. In a climate like Wellesley's, a self-adhering ice-and-water shield along the eaves and in valleys isn't optional, it's the layer that keeps a bad winter from becoming a structural problem.

New shingles are installed with attention to the manufacturer's wind-resistance specifications, which matter in a region that sees nor'easters with sustained gusts capable of lifting improperly fastened material. Ridge ventilation is evaluated and upgraded if needed, because a poorly ventilated attic accelerates shingle aging from the underside regardless of how good the surface product is.

Wellesley has a number of neighborhoods where homes were built with wide, decorative overhangs and minimal attic ventilation, a design choice that looked elegant in the 1950s and 1960s but creates a persistent ice dam problem today.
