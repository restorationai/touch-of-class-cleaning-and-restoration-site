## Why Framingham Properties Are Particularly Vulnerable to Storm Damage

Framingham's housing stock tells a story of several different eras, and each era comes with its own storm-damage profile. Neighborhoods developed in the postwar decades — the 1950s through the early 1970s — often feature large canopy trees that have had sixty-plus years to grow root systems close to foundations and branch canopies over rooflines. When a severe thunderstorm or nor'easter brings sustained gusts, those trees become the primary hazard: limbs and full trunks can puncture roofs, collapse gutters, and drive debris through windows in seconds.

The region's clay-heavy glacial soils don't drain quickly, which matters after a storm. Once a roof is breached or a basement window well floods, water finds its way in faster than it finds its way out. Framingham also sits in a watershed with several ponds and the Sudbury River corridor nearby, meaning that prolonged rain events raise the water table quickly — a factor that turns what looks like a minor roof leak into a wet crawlspace or basement within hours.

Older homes with original wood-framed roofs and aging asphalt shingles are especially susceptible to wind uplift. A shingle that's been through fifteen New England winters may look serviceable until a 60-mph gust tests the adhesive strip — and by then, the underlayment and sheathing are already exposed.

## Our Storm Damage Restoration Process in Framingham

The first priority after any storm loss is stopping the weather from continuing to enter the structure. That means emergency tarping of breached roof sections, boarding compromised windows, and wrapping exposed wall cavities — temporary measures that protect the building while a full scope of damage is documented.

Once the structure is stabilized, the assessment goes deeper. Moisture readings taken at the surface of walls and ceilings often understate the actual saturation, especially in homes with plaster-over-lath construction common in Framingham's older neighborhoods. Thermal imaging helps locate water that has migrated behind finished surfaces before it has a chance to promote mold growth — which in Massachusetts's humid summers can begin colonizing within 24 to 48 hours of a moisture intrusion.

From there, the restoration scope typically includes structural drying with commercial-grade dehumidifiers and air movers, debris removal, damaged material extraction, and coordinated reconstruction — roofing, siding, framing, insulation, and interior finishes as the scope requires. Every step is documented with photographs and moisture logs, which become the foundation of the insurance claim.

## Framingham Insurance Coordination

Storm damage claims in Massachusetts are generally covered under the dwelling portion of a standard homeowner's policy, but the documentation requirements matter. Carriers want to see a clear separation between pre-existing wear and storm-caused damage — and that distinction is often contested on older roofs. Our team photographs and measures every affected area, notes the storm date against visible damage patterns, and provides a written scope that aligns with the line-item format most carriers use for claim review.

If your policy includes a separate wind or hurricane deductible — which some Massachusetts policies have introduced in recent years — we'll walk you through how that affects your out-of-pocket exposure before work begins.
