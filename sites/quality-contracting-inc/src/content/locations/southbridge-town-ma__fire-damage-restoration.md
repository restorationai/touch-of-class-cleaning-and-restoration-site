## Why Southbridge Town's Building Stock Shapes Fire Damage Restoration

A significant portion of Southbridge Town's residential housing dates to the late 1800s and early 1900s, built to house workers for the optical and textile industries that once anchored the local economy. Those homes share a few traits that complicate post-fire restoration in ways that newer construction does not.

First, original plaster-and-lath walls are porous in a way that modern drywall is not. Smoke particulates and volatile organic compounds from combustion penetrate plaster deeply, which means surface cleaning alone will not eliminate odor. Effective fire and smoke restoration in these homes requires HEPA vacuuming of cavities, thermal fogging or hydroxyl treatment for odor, and in some cases selective demolition of plaster that has absorbed too much char residue to be salvaged.

Second, older homes in Southbridge Town frequently have knob-and-tube or early-generation aluminum wiring that a fire can damage in ways that are not visible without opening walls. Before any reconstruction begins, a licensed electrician needs to assess the full circuit path, not just the panels.

Third, original wood framing, while often old-growth and dense, chars differently than engineered lumber. Structural assessments after a fire in a pre-1950 Southbridge Town home require close attention to mortise-and-tenon joinery and load-bearing members that may look intact on the surface but have lost meaningful structural capacity.

## Our Fire Damage Restoration Process in Southbridge Town

Every fire loss is different, but the sequence of work follows a disciplined order that prevents secondary damage and keeps the insurance documentation clean.

**Stabilization and board-up.** Once the scene is released, the first priority is securing the structure against weather and unauthorized entry. Southbridge Town winters are unforgiving, and an open roof or broken window after a fire can introduce water damage that compounds the original loss significantly.

**Scope and documentation.** We photograph and document every affected surface, cavity, and system before any material is removed. This documentation becomes the backbone of the insurance claim and the reconstruction permit application.

**Debris removal and cleaning.** Char, ash, and smoke-saturated materials are removed and disposed of properly. Salvageable structural members are cleaned with appropriate chemical agents matched to the material type.

**Odor elimination.** Smoke odor in a Southbridge Town home with plaster walls requires more than surface treatment. We use thermal fogging and hydroxyl generation to address odor molecules that have penetrated deeply into porous substrates.

**Reconstruction.** Framing, insulation, drywall, and finish work are completed to current Massachusetts building code, with all required permits pulled through the Southbridge Town building department.
