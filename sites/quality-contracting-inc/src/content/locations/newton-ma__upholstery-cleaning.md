Newton's older Colonials, Victorians, and post-war Capes hold a lot of upholstered furniture that has never seen a professional cleaning, and New England's swing from humid summers to bone-dry winter heating takes a visible toll on both fabric and leather. A sofa that looks fine under a lamp often hides ground-in soil along the seat seams, pet dander worked deep into the weave, or a leather finish that's starting to crack from months of forced-air heat. Upholstery cleaning in Newton isn't just about stains, it's about slowing that wear down before it becomes permanent.

## Why Newton Homes See Upholstery Wear Differently

A large share of Newton's housing stock predates central air conditioning, which means window units and radiator heat are still doing a lot of the climate work in older rooms. That combination dries out leather upholstery faster than in newer construction and pulls moisture out of natural fibers like wool and cotton blends, making them brittle at the fold lines first. Homes closer to the Charles River corridor also run a bit more humid in summer, which gives mildew odor more of a foothold in cushions that don't get much airflow. We adjust our approach based on which of these two patterns we're dealing with rather than treating every piece the same way.

## Our Upholstery Cleaning Process

We start with a fiber and dye test on an inconspicuous area, because a lot of the older upholstered pieces we see in Newton are reupholstered antiques or higher-end furniture where the original fabric composition isn't obvious. From there, dry, loose soil gets removed with a vacuum pass using an upholstery attachment, not just a quick wipe, since embedded grit is what actually cuts fibers over time. Fabric pieces are then cleaned with hot water extraction or a low-moisture method depending on the fiber, while leather gets a pH-balanced cleaner followed by a conditioner to replace the oils that heating systems strip out. Drying time varies by fabric density, but we set fans and, when needed, a dehumidifier so cushions aren't sitting damp overnight.

## Local note

One thing we watch for in Newton specifically: a lot of the wool and wool-blend upholstery in older homes here was never meant to get fully wet-cleaned, and hot water extraction on the wrong blend can cause shrinkage or puckering at the seams. We test fiber content before committing to a method rather than assuming a sofa is a standard poly-blend just because it looks like one, which matters more in a city with this much inherited and reupholstered furniture than it would in a neighborhood full of newer, mass-produced pieces.
