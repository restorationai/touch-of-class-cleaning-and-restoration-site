## Why Northborough Properties See Water Damage Issues

The housing stock in Northborough spans a wide range of construction eras. Many homes along the older residential streets near the town center were built with balloon-frame construction and original cast-iron or galvanized steel plumbing that has been patching itself together for decades. Those pipes do not announce their failure gradually. They go, and when they do, water travels vertically through wall cavities from the attic to the crawl space before it ever pools on a floor you can see.

Northborough's clay-dominant soils also resist drainage. During heavy rain events or rapid snowmelt, hydrostatic pressure builds against foundation walls and pushes water through poured concrete or block foundations at the cove joint, the seam where the floor meets the wall. Finished basements that look completely dry in summer can take on an inch or more of water in a single March storm. The town's position in Worcester County means it sees the same nor'easter systems that hit the coast, but with less wind and more sustained precipitation, which means slower ground saturation and longer drainage windows.

## Our Water Damage Restoration Process in Northborough

When we arrive at a Northborough property, the first step is a thorough moisture assessment before any equipment is placed. We use thermal imaging alongside pin and pinless moisture meters to map exactly where water has traveled, not just where it is visible. In homes with plaster-and-lath construction, which is common in Northborough's older neighborhoods near the town center, the lath can hold moisture long after the plaster surface reads dry to the touch. Skipping this step leads to drying plans that miss saturated cavities and create conditions for mold growth within days.

Once we understand the full scope of the loss, we extract standing water using truck-mounted and portable extraction units, then establish a drying system calibrated to the specific materials and square footage involved. Structural drying in a Northborough colonial with thick plaster walls requires more time and more precisely placed drying equipment than a newer home with standard drywall. We monitor moisture readings daily and adjust equipment placement until readings return to pre-loss baselines. Nothing gets closed up until the numbers confirm it.

Where materials cannot be saved, we remove only what is necessary. Selective demolition, rather than wholesale tear-out, keeps reconstruction costs lower and gives your insurance adjuster a clear picture of what actually needed to go.

## Local Note

Northborough's older homes near the town center frequently have original horsehair plaster over wood lath, a combination that absorbs water at a different rate than modern drywall and releases it more slowly during the drying phase. A room that reaches acceptable surface moisture readings in two days with drywall can take four or five days with plaster-and-lath because the lath holds water in the wood fibers and releases it gradually as the air in the space dries out. Rushing the drying timeline on these homes and closing up walls too early is one of the most common reasons secondary mold problems appear weeks after a restoration is declared complete.
