## Why Millbury Properties See Sewage Backup More Often Than Newer Suburbs

Millbury's identity as a nineteenth-century mill town on the Blackstone River is part of what makes it a genuinely interesting place to live — and part of what makes sewage incidents more likely in certain pockets of town. Bramanville and the neighborhoods closest to the river corridor sit on soils that shift and saturate during spring snowmelt and heavy rain. ## Our Sewage Cleanup and Sanitization Process in Millbury

Sewage is classified as Category 3 water — the most contaminated type — which means the cleanup protocol is more involved than a standard water loss. The process begins with containment: plastic sheeting and negative air pressure keep pathogens from migrating into living areas while the affected zone is assessed. Every porous material that has had direct contact with raw sewage — drywall, insulation, carpet, wood subfloor — is evaluated for removal. In Millbury's older homes, that often includes original tongue-and-groove pine subfloor, which holds moisture and bacteria in its grain and typically cannot be effectively sanitized in place.

Once contaminated material is bagged and removed according to Massachusetts Department of Environmental Protection guidelines, the structural surfaces that remain are treated with EPA-registered antimicrobial agents applied by fogging and direct surface application. We then run commercial desiccant or refrigerant dehumidifiers and air movers calibrated to the specific square footage and material composition of the space. Moisture readings are logged at each visit so drying progress is documented — a record that matters both for your peace of mind and for any insurance claim you file.

## Local Note: Stone Foundations and Sewage — a Millbury-Specific Complication

Here's something that comes up repeatedly in Millbury's older housing stock: fieldstone and granite block foundations are not monolithic surfaces. The mortar joints between stones — especially in homes built before 1930 — are often soft, partially missing, or have been repointed with incompatible materials over the decades. When sewage contacts those joints, liquid and organic matter work into the voids and are extremely difficult to reach with surface-applied disinfectant alone. In these situations, we use a combination of pressurized antimicrobial injection into accessible voids and HEPA-filtered air scrubbing to address what you can't see on the surface. Skipping that step leaves contamination behind even when the visible surfaces look clean.
