## Why Fiskdale Properties See Roofing Problems Early

The Sturbridge area sits at an elevation where winter storms arrive with more ice load than communities closer to the coast. Ice dams are a recurring problem here: when heat escapes through an under-insulated attic, it melts snow at the roof deck while the eaves stay frozen. Water backs up under shingles, saturates the decking, and eventually finds the ceiling below. Homes in this part of Worcester County also tend to run toward older construction, with roof pitches and framing profiles designed for cedar shakes or early three-tab asphalt, not always the heavier architectural shingles common today. That mismatch matters when you are selecting replacement materials, because the load rating and fastening pattern need to match what the rafters were built to carry.

The tree canopy throughout Fiskdale is dense, which is part of what makes the area attractive, but it also means moss and lichen colonize north-facing roof sections faster than homeowners expect. Moss holds moisture against the surface long after rain stops, accelerating shingle deterioration and eventually compromising the underlayment beneath.

## Our Roofing Installation and Replacement Process in Fiskdale

Every project starts with a thorough inspection of the existing deck. Before any new material goes on, the crew checks for soft decking, damaged or missing drip edge, and the condition of existing flashing around chimneys, skylights, and any penetrations. In older Fiskdale homes, it is common to find two or even three layers of shingles already in place. Massachusetts building code limits the number of roofing layers allowed before a full tear-off is required, and adding weight to a deck that is already carrying multiple layers is a structural concern we take seriously.

Once the deck is confirmed sound or repaired, installation follows a sequenced approach: ice-and-water shield along the eaves and valleys, synthetic underlayment across the field, then starter course and shingles installed to the manufacturer's specifications for wind resistance. In a climate with the wind exposure Fiskdale sees during nor'easters, proper nailing pattern and starter-strip adhesion are not optional steps.

Flashing details get particular attention here. The intersection of a chimney or dormer with a sloped roof is where most leaks originate, and step flashing installed correctly is the difference between a roof that lasts and one that returns you to the contractor in three years.
