Crawl space encapsulation — sealing the crawl space with a continuous vapor barrier and conditioning the air inside it — is one of the most effective ways to stop that moisture cycle before it rots sills, buckles flooring, or creates the damp conditions where mold takes hold.

## Why Leicester Properties Are Particularly Vulnerable

The housing stock along Route 9 and through villages like Cherry Valley and Rochdale tells the story. Many of these homes were built in the 19th and early 20th centuries to house mill workers, and their crawl spaces were designed with ventilation strategies that made sense before building science caught up with the realities of cold, wet New England winters. Open vents pull in humid summer air that condenses on cold surfaces below the subfloor. In winter, those same vents let frigid air freeze pipes and drive heating costs up. Homes on private wells and septic — common throughout the 01524 zip code — face an additional wrinkle: the ground around those systems stays wetter longer, and that moisture doesn't stay put. It wicks under foundations and into crawl spaces through block and stone walls that were never intended to be waterproof.

## What the Encapsulation Process Looks Like in Leicester

Every crawl space is different, but the sequence is consistent. The first step is a thorough inspection — removing old, fallen fiberglass batt insulation (a near-universal finding in pre-1970 homes here), checking for active water intrusion, and identifying any existing mold or wood decay that needs to be addressed before sealing anything in. Encapsulating over a problem doesn't solve it; it hides it.

Once the crawl space is clean and dry, a heavy-mil polyethylene liner — typically 12 to 20 mil — goes down across the entire dirt floor and up the foundation walls, sealed at seams with moisture-resistant tape and fastened to the wall above the frost line. Vents are sealed from the inside. If the crawl space is large or the humidity load is significant, a dedicated crawl space dehumidifier is installed and wired to a drain or condensate pump so it runs automatically without needing to be emptied by hand. The result is a conditioned space that stays within a controlled humidity range year-round, regardless of what the weather is doing outside.

In older homes with stone or rubble foundations — not uncommon in Rochdale and Cherry Valley — we pay close attention to wall penetrations and the sill plate connection, where gaps are often wider and less predictable than in poured-concrete construction. Those areas get extra attention with spray foam or rigid foam board before the liner goes up.
