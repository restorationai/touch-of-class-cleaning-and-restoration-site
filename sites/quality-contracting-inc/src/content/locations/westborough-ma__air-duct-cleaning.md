That cycle is particularly rough on forced-air systems, where condensation inside supply and return runs creates exactly the conditions that allow dust, mold spores, and allergens to accumulate season after season. ## Why Westborough Homes See Duct Buildup Faster Than You'd Expect

Westborough's housing stock spans a wide range of eras, from mid-century colonials and split-levels built during the town's post-war growth to newer construction near the I-495 corridor. The older homes — many of them built before modern vapor barriers were standard — tend to have ductwork running through unconditioned spaces like unfinished basements and crawlspaces. In those zones, temperature differentials cause condensation on the interior duct walls, and that moisture becomes a surface for dust and biological particulates to cling to.

Newer construction has its own wrinkle: tighter building envelopes trap indoor air more effectively, which means whatever is circulating through the ducts — pet dander, construction dust from a recent renovation, pollen carried in from outside — stays in circulation longer. Either way, the ductwork ends up doing more work than most homeowners realize.

Westborough also sits in a region with significant tree cover and seasonal pollen loads. Oak and birch pollen counts in Central Massachusetts can run high from April through June, and that particulate finds its way into return air intakes, especially in homes with older or loosely fitted grille covers.

## What the Cleaning Process Actually Involves

A thorough duct cleaning isn't a single pass with a shop vac. The process starts with a visual inspection — often with a camera inserted into the main trunk lines — to document what's present before any equipment runs. That baseline matters both for understanding the scope of the job and for confirming results afterward.

From there, the main trunk lines and branch runs are placed under negative pressure using a truck-mounted or portable HEPA-filtered vacuum system. Technicians work each register and grille systematically, using rotary brushes and compressed air tools to dislodge debris from duct walls and drive it toward the collection point rather than back into the living space. Supply and return sides are worked separately.

If sanitization is warranted — typically when there's visible biological growth or a strong musty odor coming from the vents — an EPA-registered antimicrobial agent can be applied to interior duct surfaces after the mechanical cleaning is complete. This step is optional and should be discussed before the job starts, not assumed.

Dryer vent cleaning follows a similar mechanical process and is worth scheduling at the same time. Lint accumulation in dryer exhaust runs is one of the more common fire hazards in residential properties, and the venting in older Westborough homes sometimes runs longer horizontal distances than current best practices recommend, which accelerates buildup.
