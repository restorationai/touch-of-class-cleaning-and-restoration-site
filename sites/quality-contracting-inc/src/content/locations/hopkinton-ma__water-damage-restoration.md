## Why Hopkinton Properties See Water Damage Issues

Hopkinton's development history creates a layered set of risk factors that don't show up in generic water damage guides. The town grew rapidly through the 1980s and 1990s, meaning a large share of its residential stock sits on slab-on-grade or shallow basement foundations that were built before modern waterproofing membranes became standard practice. Those foundations can wick groundwater through hairline cracks that are invisible until a wet spring or a heavy nor'easter raises the water table.

The town also straddles several small ponds and tributaries feeding into the Sudbury and Assabet systems. Properties near these low-lying corridors are particularly vulnerable during the freeze-thaw cycles that define a Massachusetts late winter — ice dams on rooflines push meltwater under shingles, while frost-heaved exterior grading channels that same water toward foundation walls instead of away from them. Interior supply lines in older construction are often copper with soldered joints that fatigue over decades; a joint that holds through summer can fail when temperatures swing 40 degrees overnight in February.

## Our Water Damage Restoration Process in Hopkinton

Every water loss is different, but the sequence of decisions that protects a Hopkinton home follows a consistent logic. The first step on-site is moisture mapping — using thermal imaging and calibrated moisture meters to find water that has migrated behind walls, under flooring, or into subfloor assemblies before it shows any visible sign. Water behind plaster or beneath hardwood can read dry at the surface while the substrate remains saturated; skipping this step leads to mold colonization that typically begins within 24 to 48 hours of a loss event.

Once the full extent of the intrusion is documented, standing water is extracted using truck-mounted and portable extraction units, followed by the placement of industrial air movers and desiccant or refrigerant dehumidifiers calibrated to the square footage and construction type of the affected space. Readings are logged daily so drying progress can be tracked against target moisture levels — not estimated by feel. Affected materials that cannot be dried in place (saturated insulation, compromised drywall below the flood line) are removed and documented before drying equipment is set.

## Local Note

Hopkinton's older Colonial and Cape-style homes — particularly those built in the 1960s and 1970s in established neighborhoods closer to the town center — frequently have plaster-over-lath wall systems rather than modern drywall. Plaster absorbs water more slowly than drywall, which can make a loss look less severe in the first hours. The tradeoff is that plaster also releases moisture slowly during drying, and the wood lath behind it can hold saturation long after the plaster face reads acceptable on a surface meter. We account for this by using penetrating probes rather than relying on surface readings alone, and we extend drying timelines accordingly rather than pulling equipment prematurely.
