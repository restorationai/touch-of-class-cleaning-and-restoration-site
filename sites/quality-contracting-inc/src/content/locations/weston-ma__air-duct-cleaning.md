## Why Weston Properties See Duct Buildup Faster Than You'd Expect

Weston sits in a climate band that gives HVAC systems a genuine workout — humid summers that push air conditioning hard, and cold winters that keep furnaces running for months at a stretch. That continuous cycling pulls air through the duct system constantly, and whatever is in that air — dust, pet dander, insulation fibers, pollen from the surrounding woodlands — accumulates on duct walls and inside return-air boxes.

The housing stock adds another layer. A large share of Weston's residential properties date from the mid-twentieth century or earlier, and many were retrofitted with ductwork that wasn't designed with cleanability in mind. Older flex duct and rectangular sheet-metal runs in unconditioned attic or basement spaces tend to collect moisture condensation during seasonal transitions, creating conditions where dust accumulates and, over time, organic growth can take hold. Homes that have had renovation work — kitchen expansions, finished basements, dormers added to Cape-style houses — often have patchwork duct systems where older and newer sections meet, and those joints are prime spots for debris buildup.

## Our Duct Cleaning Process, Calibrated to Weston Homes

Before any equipment goes in, the process starts with a visual inspection of accessible duct runs, registers, and the air handler itself. In Weston's older homes this step matters more than usual — it's common to find insulation fragments inside ducts from past renovation work, or to discover that a section of ductwork was sealed off and forgotten during a remodel. Knowing what's in the system before cleaning begins determines the right approach.

The cleaning itself uses negative-pressure equipment — a high-powered vacuum attached to the main trunk line — combined with rotary brush agitation on the individual branch runs. This combination dislodges debris from duct walls and pulls it into a contained collection unit rather than redistributing it through the house. Supply and return sides are cleaned separately. Registers and grilles are removed, cleaned, and reinstalled. If the inspection turns up biological growth or heavy contamination, an EPA-registered sanitizing agent can be applied to duct interiors after mechanical cleaning is complete.

Dryer vent cleaning is often added at the same visit. Weston's older homes frequently have longer-than-ideal dryer vent runs — sometimes routed through interior walls or across finished basement ceilings — and lint accumulation in those runs is a genuine fire risk that most homeowners don't think about until there's a problem.
