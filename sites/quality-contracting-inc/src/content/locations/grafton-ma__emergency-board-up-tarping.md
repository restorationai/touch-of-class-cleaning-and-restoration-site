## Why Grafton's Housing Stock Makes Board-Up and Tarping Especially Time-Sensitive

Grafton's built environment is unusually varied for a town its size, and that variety directly shapes how quickly an open structure becomes a serious problem. Around Grafton Center and the Common, you'll find homes dating to the 1700s and 1800s — many with fieldstone foundations, wide-plank pine floors, and original plaster-and-lath walls. These materials are far more absorbent than modern construction. Nearer the Grafton MBTA commuter rail station, newer subdivisions built in the 1990s and 2000s present their own challenges: engineered lumber roof trusses and OSB sheathing absorb moisture quickly once exposed, and insurance carriers increasingly scrutinize delay between the loss event and the date of first mitigation.

## Our Board-Up and Tarping Process in Grafton

When we arrive on-site, the first step is a rapid exterior assessment — identifying every breach point before touching anything. On older Grafton homes, that means checking not just the obvious damage but also adjacent sections of the structure: a blow to a Victorian-era cornice can loosen decorative trim several feet away, creating secondary entry points that aren't immediately visible.

For window and door board-up, we use exterior-grade plywood cut to fit and secured with structural screws into sound framing — not stapled sheeting that pulls free in the next wind event. On historic properties near the Common, we take care to avoid fastening into original decorative millwork where solid structural members are accessible nearby.

Roof tarping follows a specific sequence: we secure the tarp's leading edge at the ridge or the highest intact sheathing point, run it down and over the eave, and weight or fasten the perimeter so it won't lift under wind load. For larger or more complex roof profiles — common on the older multi-story homes in Grafton Center — we use cap-and-wrap techniques that cover the ridge line and prevent wind from getting underneath.

Documentation runs throughout the process. Every breach point is photographed before and after securing, with measurements recorded. This photo log is formatted to meet standard insurance carrier requirements and is available to your adjuster promptly.
