## Our Water Damage Restoration Process in Grafton

When we arrive at a Grafton property, the first step is a thorough moisture assessment — not just the visible standing water, but the hidden saturation in wall cavities, subfloor assemblies, and insulation bays. We use thermal imaging and calibrated moisture meters to map the full extent of the loss before any equipment is placed.

Water extraction comes next. High-capacity truck-mounted and portable extractors pull standing water from floors and pull trapped water from carpet padding and subfloor materials. In older homes near Grafton Common with wide-plank hardwood floors, we pay particular attention to the gaps between boards — water channels down through those seams quickly and saturates the subfloor before it's visible on the surface.

Structural drying follows extraction. We calculate the drying system based on the affected square footage, material types, and ambient conditions — a fieldstone basement in January holds moisture differently than a finished family room in July. Refrigerant and desiccant dehumidifiers, along with high-velocity air movers, are positioned to create directed airflow through affected assemblies. We monitor moisture readings daily and adjust equipment until materials reach documented dry standard, typically over three to five days depending on severity.

Throughout the process, we photograph and document conditions for insurance purposes, which matters in Worcester County where carrier adjusters often require detailed moisture logs before approving structural repairs.

## Local Note: Fieldstone Foundations and Hidden Moisture

One thing that catches homeowners off guard in older Grafton Center and North Grafton homes is how long a fieldstone foundation wall holds moisture after a flood event appears to be resolved. Unlike poured concrete, fieldstone and mortar are highly porous, and the stone itself can store significant water that releases slowly into the basement air over one to two weeks. We've seen cases where a homeowner ran their own dehumidifier for a week, believed the space was dry, and then discovered active mold growth on wood framing because the masonry was still off-gassing moisture. In those homes, we extend the monitoring period and often apply targeted drying to the foundation wall itself — not just the floor and framing — before closing out the job.
