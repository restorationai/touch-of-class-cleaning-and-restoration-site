## Why Hopkinton Roofs Wear Differently Than You Might Expect

Hopkinton's position along the I-495 corridor puts it squarely in a microclimate that collects more snow accumulation than coastal towns to the east. Roofs here carry heavier snow loads for longer stretches of the season, and the thaw-refreeze pattern that follows a January warm spell is exactly what drives ice dams along the eaves. Once an ice dam forms, meltwater backs up under shingles and works its way into the sheathing before you see a single stain on the ceiling.

The housing stock adds another layer of complexity. Hopkinton grew steadily through the 1980s and 1990s, and a significant portion of the single-family homes in town were built during that period with 20- to 25-year architectural shingles that are now at or past their rated service life. Many of those roofs also have low-slope sections over additions or attached garages where standard shingle installation is not the right answer without additional underlayment protection. Getting the material specification right matters more on a mixed-pitch roof than on a simple gable.

## Our Roofing Installation and Replacement Process in Hopkinton

Every project starts with a roof inspection that goes beyond a visual scan from the ground. We check decking condition, ventilation balance, and flashing integrity at every penetration point before we quote a replacement. Skipping that step leads to installing new shingles over a substrate that will fail in a few years.

Once we have a clear picture of what the roof needs, we walk you through material options that make sense for Hopkinton's climate. Impact-rated shingles have become a practical choice here given the hail and ice exposure the area sees, and proper ridge and soffit ventilation is non-negotiable on a replacement in this climate zone. Poor attic ventilation is one of the most common reasons roofs in this part of Massachusetts age prematurely.

During installation, we strip the existing material down to the deck, inspect every sheet of plywood or OSB for soft spots or delamination, and replace what needs replacing before a single new shingle goes down. The job is not done until the ridge cap is seated, the gutters are cleared of debris from the tear-off, and the yard is walked for fasteners.
