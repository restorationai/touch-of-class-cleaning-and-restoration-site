## Why Newton Properties See Water Damage Differently

Much of Newton was built in the late 19th and early 20th centuries, when plaster-and-lath wall systems were standard. Unlike modern drywall, plaster is dense and slow to release moisture — a surface moisture reading can look acceptable within a day or two while the wooden lath behind it stays saturated for a week or more. That hidden saturation is where secondary mold colonization begins, typically within 48 to 72 hours of initial wetting.

Newton's topography compounds the risk. The city sits across a series of rolling hills and low-lying corridors, and neighborhoods closer to the Charles River watershed — including Auburndale and Newton Lower Falls — can see groundwater intrusion during prolonged rain events that wouldn't affect higher-elevation parts of town. Clay-heavy soils common throughout much of Middlesex County shed water rather than absorbing it, which means heavy rainfall has nowhere to go except toward foundations. Sump pump failures and window-well flooding are among the most common calls from Newton homeowners after a multi-day storm system.

## Our Water Damage Restoration Process in Newton

Every water damage job begins with a thorough assessment before any equipment is placed. Moisture meters and thermal imaging cameras map where water has traveled — not just where it's visible. In Newton's older homes, that often means tracing water movement through plaster ceilings from an upstairs bathroom, or finding saturation in a finished basement that entered through a crack in a parged fieldstone wall rather than through a floor drain.

Once the scope is documented, water removal begins. Standing water is extracted first using truck-mounted or portable extraction units, depending on access. Structural drying follows — commercial-grade desiccant or refrigerant dehumidifiers and high-velocity air movers are positioned to create a controlled drying environment. Drying logs are recorded daily so the process is defensible to your insurance adjuster and so we know precisely when materials have reached acceptable moisture levels rather than guessing.

Demolition is kept to the minimum necessary. In a home with original plaster, removing a wall to dry it faster isn't always the right call — it depends on moisture readings, the extent of saturation, and what's behind it. We make that call based on data, not convenience.
