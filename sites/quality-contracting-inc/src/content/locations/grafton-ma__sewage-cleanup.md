## Why Grafton Properties See More Sewage Backup Issues

Grafton's geography and housing stock create a set of conditions that make sewer and septic problems more likely — and more complicated to resolve — than in newer suburban communities.

The mill villages of Fisherville and Farnumsville were built along the Blackstone River and Quinsigamond River corridors, where the water table sits close to the surface. Shallow water tables mean that during heavy rainfall or spring snowmelt, groundwater infiltrates aging clay and cast-iron lateral sewer lines, overwhelming capacity and pushing sewage back through floor drains and basement toilets. This phenomenon — called inflow and infiltration — is especially pronounced in low-lying areas near the river valleys.

Around Grafton Center and the Common, many homes predate modern municipal sewer connections entirely and rely on private septic systems. Septic tanks in this part of Worcester County are often undersized by today's standards, and the dense, glacially deposited soils in central Massachusetts drain slowly. ## Our Sewage Cleanup and Sanitization Process in Grafton

Sewage is classified as Category 3 water — the most contaminated type — and every step of the cleanup process reflects that. We don't treat a sewage backup the same way we treat a burst supply line.

**Containment first.** Before extraction begins, we establish containment barriers to prevent cross-contamination from spreading to unaffected areas of the home. In Grafton's older split-level and cape-style homes, sewage that enters a basement can migrate toward finished living space through gaps in subfloor framing — containment stops that spread.

**Extraction and removal.** Standing sewage is extracted using truck-mounted equipment. Porous materials — carpet, pad, lower sections of drywall or plaster, insulation — that have direct contact with Category 3 water are removed and bagged for disposal per Massachusetts Department of Environmental Protection guidelines. We do not attempt to dry and salvage sewage-saturated porous materials.

**Disinfection and deodorization.** After bulk removal, all affected hard surfaces are treated with EPA-registered disinfectants rated for sewage pathogens. This includes concrete block walls, floor joists, subflooring, and any HVAC components that may have drawn contaminated air.

**Structural drying.** Commercial desiccant dehumidifiers and air movers run until moisture readings in walls and framing return to baseline. In Grafton's older homes, plaster walls release moisture more slowly than modern drywall — drying cycles often run longer than a homeowner expects, and we monitor daily rather than pulling equipment on a fixed schedule.

One thing that catches homeowners off guard in the older sections of Grafton Center and around the Common: fieldstone foundations are not monolithic.
