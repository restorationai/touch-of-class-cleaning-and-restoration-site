## Why Framingham Homes See Duct Buildup Faster Than You'd Think

Massachusetts sits squarely in a climate zone that swings hard between seasons. Framingham winters pull dry air through heating systems for months at a stretch, loosening fine particulates that then settle inside ductwork. Come spring and summer, humidity rises quickly — Framingham's inland position means it doesn't get the coastal breeze that moderates conditions closer to the shore. That moisture can interact with the dust layer already coating duct walls, creating conditions where allergens and biological growth take hold.

The housing stock adds another layer. A significant portion of Framingham's residential neighborhoods were built between the 1950s and 1980s, when duct design prioritized airflow volume over filterability. Return-air ducts in those homes often run through unconditioned spaces — crawlspaces, unfinished basements, wall cavities — where they pick up whatever is in the surrounding air. Fiberglass duct liner from that era also traps particulates in a way that smooth metal duct does not, making periodic cleaning more than just a comfort measure.

## What the Cleaning Process Actually Involves

Air duct cleaning is not a vacuum-and-go service. The process starts with an inspection — either a camera run through accessible trunk lines or a visual check at each register — to document what's present before any equipment touches the system. That baseline matters, especially in older Framingham homes where previous owners may have done DIY modifications to the duct layout.

The main cleaning uses negative-pressure equipment: a high-powered vacuum collection unit connects to the main trunk, and the system is put under negative pressure so that dislodged debris travels toward the collection point rather than back into living spaces. Technicians work register by register, using compressed air tools and rotary brushes to break material loose from duct walls. Supply and return sides are cleaned separately, and the air handler cabinet — blower wheel, evaporator coil housing, and drain pan area — is addressed as part of the same visit when accessible.

If sanitization is requested, an EPA-registered antimicrobial agent is applied after cleaning. This is not a substitute for physical debris removal; it's a follow-on step for situations where biological growth has been confirmed or suspected.

Dryer vent cleaning follows a similar negative-pressure approach and is worth adding to the same appointment. Framingham's older homes sometimes have dryer vents that were routed through long horizontal runs — sometimes with multiple elbows — before exiting the building. Those configurations trap lint at every bend, and lint accumulation in a vent is a fire risk that compounds quietly over time.
