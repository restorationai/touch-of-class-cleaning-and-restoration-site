## Our Board-Up and Tarping Process in Millbury

Every job starts with a rapid site assessment. Before a single sheet of plywood goes up, we walk the perimeter and document every breach — broken windows, compromised door frames, roof punctures, and any structural instability that would affect how we stage materials and personnel. That documentation matters: insurers want a clear record of conditions at the time of service, and we photograph everything before we touch it.

For window and door board-up, we use minimum ¾-inch CDX plywood, cut and fitted to the opening rather than rough-nailed over the exterior. On the older homes common near Millbury Center, we pay close attention to original trim profiles and sill conditions — forcing a board against rotted or fragile wood can widen the damage rather than contain it. Fasteners are placed to hold against wind load without destroying historic millwork unnecessarily.

Roof tarping follows a similar discipline. We use reinforced polyethylene tarps rated for extended outdoor exposure, weighted and secured with battens and rope rather than simply draped and stapled. On the steep-pitched rooflines typical of Millbury's older residential stock, proper anchor points matter — a tarp that peels back in the next storm is worse than no tarp at all because it creates a false sense of security while water continues to enter.
