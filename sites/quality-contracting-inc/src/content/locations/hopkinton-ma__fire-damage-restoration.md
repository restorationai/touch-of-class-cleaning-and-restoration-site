## Why Hopkinton Homes Face Particular Challenges After a Fire

Hopkinton's housing stock spans several decades and construction styles. Older homes — many built before modern fire-retardant materials were standard — often feature balloon-frame construction, where wall cavities run continuously from the basement to the attic. In a fire, that open cavity acts like a chimney flue: smoke and heat travel vertically through walls with little resistance, depositing soot in spaces that look undamaged from the outside. You may walk into a room that shows no visible char and still smell smoke weeks later because the residue has migrated into the framing.

The town's climate adds another layer. Central Massachusetts winters are cold and long, and homes are sealed tightly for energy efficiency — which means smoke odor has nowhere to escape after a fire is extinguished. Volatile organic compounds from burned synthetic materials bind to insulation, subflooring, and HVAC ductwork. Without systematic deodorization and duct cleaning, those compounds recirculate every time the heating system runs.

## Our Fire Damage Restoration Process in Hopkinton

The first step on any fire-damaged property is a thorough assessment — not just of what burned, but of where smoke traveled. Thermal imaging and air sampling help identify hidden soot deposits behind walls and above ceilings before any demolition begins. That matters in Hopkinton's older homes, where removing the wrong section of plaster or original woodwork prematurely can increase both cost and repair time.

From there, the process moves through board-up and structural stabilization if needed, controlled demolition of materials that cannot be cleaned, dry-ice or chemical sponge cleaning of salvageable surfaces, HEPA vacuuming, and hydroxyl or ozone deodorization calibrated to the size and seal of the space. Contents that can be restored — furniture, documents, textiles — are inventoried and treated separately. The goal at every stage is to preserve what can be saved and document what cannot, because that documentation drives your insurance claim.

## Local Note

Hopkinton's older colonial and cape-style homes frequently have knob-and-tube or early Romex wiring that was never fully replaced — a common pattern in towns settled well before mid-century electrical codes. After a fire, electricians and restoration crews sometimes discover that heat traveled along these older wiring runs into wall cavities that showed no external damage.
