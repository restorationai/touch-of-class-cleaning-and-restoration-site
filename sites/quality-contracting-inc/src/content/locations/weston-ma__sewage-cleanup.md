## Why Weston Properties See Sewage Backup Issues

Weston's mix of older estates, mid-century colonials, and newer construction on former farmland creates a patchwork of plumbing ages and septic configurations. Homes built before the town expanded its municipal sewer lines — particularly those on larger lots toward the rural western edges — depend on septic tanks and leach fields that can become saturated during the heavy spring snowmelt and fall rain cycles that hit this part of eastern Massachusetts reliably every year. When the soil around a leach field is already holding water from a nor'easter or a prolonged wet stretch, a tank that's overdue for pumping can push effluent back through floor drains, toilets, and utility sinks before a homeowner realizes what's happening.

Even properties connected to municipal sewer lines aren't immune. Aging lateral lines that run under mature tree canopy — and Weston has no shortage of century-old oaks and maples — are vulnerable to root intrusion that narrows the pipe and eventually causes a full blockage. A sewer line backup of that kind floods a finished basement just as fast as any septic failure.

## Our Sewage Cleanup and Sanitization Process in Weston

Sewage cleanup is not the same as water damage cleanup. Category 3 contaminated water — which is what raw sewage is classified as — requires full containment, proper PPE, and disposal protocols that go beyond pumping and drying.

The process begins with containment: isolating the affected area to prevent cross-contamination to clean portions of the home. Standing sewage is extracted using truck-mounted equipment rated for contaminated water. Porous materials — drywall, insulation, carpet, wood subfloor — that have absorbed sewage cannot be dried and saved; they are removed and bagged for disposal per Massachusetts Department of Environmental Protection guidelines for contaminated solid waste.

Once the structure is cleared down to cleanable surfaces, we apply EPA-registered hospital-grade disinfectants to every affected surface, including framing, concrete, and HVAC components if the system was running during the event. Odor is addressed at the source through hydroxyl or ozone treatment rather than masking agents. Structural drying follows using commercial dehumidifiers and air movers, with moisture readings logged throughout to document dry-standard compliance.
