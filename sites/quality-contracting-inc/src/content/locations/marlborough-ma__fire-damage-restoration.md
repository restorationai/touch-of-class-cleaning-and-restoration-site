## Why Marlborough Properties See Distinct Fire Damage Challenges

Marlborough sits in central Worcester County, and its housing inventory reflects that history. A significant portion of the city's residential stock dates from the 1940s through the 1970s, with some neighborhoods containing homes built even earlier around the former shoe manufacturing era. Older homes in this range frequently have plaster-and-lath wall assemblies, original knob-and-tube or early Romex wiring, and wood-framed balloon construction, all of which change the way fire spreads and smoke infiltrates.

Balloon framing, common in pre-1950 construction, has continuous stud cavities running from the foundation to the roof line. When a fire starts on one floor, smoke and heat can travel vertically inside the walls to floors that appear visually unaffected. This means a kitchen fire can leave detectable smoke odor and residue inside second-floor wall cavities even when the upstairs rooms look clean. A restoration approach calibrated for modern platform-framed homes will miss that.

Marlborough also experiences genuine New England winters, with freeze-thaw cycles that stress older roofing and chimney masonry. Chimney fires are not uncommon in homes that rely on older fireplaces or wood stoves during cold months, and a chimney fire that escapes into the attic framing can be deceptively extensive by the time it is discovered.

## Our Fire Damage Restoration Process in Marlborough

The first step after a fire is a thorough assessment, not just of what burned, but of where smoke traveled. In Marlborough homes with plaster walls and older framing, that means checking wall cavities, attic spaces, and HVAC returns before any cleaning begins. Smoke that gets sealed behind new drywall or paint will off-gas for years.

Once the scope is established, the process moves through several distinct phases:

- **Structural stabilization**: Board-up and temporary weatherproofing to protect the structure from the elements while work is underway.
- **Debris and char removal**: Burned materials are removed carefully, with attention to what can be salvaged versus what must go. In older Marlborough homes, original hardwood flooring and solid-wood millwork are worth evaluating before they are discarded.
- **Smoke and soot cleaning**: Dry-chemical sponges, HEPA vacuuming, and thermal fogging address different types of residue. Wet smoke from smoldering fires leaves a sticky, pungent deposit that requires different chemistry than the dry soot from a fast, hot fire.
- **Odor neutralization**: Ozone treatment or hydroxyl generation is used in enclosed spaces after surface cleaning is complete.
- **Reconstruction**: Framing, insulation, drywall, painting, and finish work are handled in-house, so the project does not stall waiting for a separate contractor to pick up where cleanup left off.
