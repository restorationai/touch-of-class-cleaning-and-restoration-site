## Why Natick Properties See Duct Issues

A significant portion of Natick's housing stock dates from the postwar building boom of the 1950s and 1960s, when builders used galvanized steel ductwork that has now been in service for six or seven decades. That metal develops surface corrosion and small gaps at the seams over time, which lets conditioned air — and whatever is inside the ducts — migrate into wall cavities. Homes built closer to the town center, some of which predate World War II, may have had ductwork added during later renovations, sometimes in configurations that create hard-to-clean dead zones where particulate settles.

Natick also borders the Cochituate State Park wetlands and sits near the upper Charles River watershed. Properties on the lower-lying streets that drain toward these areas can pull in higher ambient humidity through the return-air side of an HVAC system, especially in basements with crawl-space intakes. Over a heating season, that moisture-laden air deposits a fine layer of organic material on duct walls — exactly the kind of surface where mold spores find a foothold.

## Our Air Duct Cleaning Process in Natick

The work starts with a camera inspection of the main trunk lines and branch runs before any equipment is connected. That step matters in older Natick homes because it reveals whether a section of ductwork has a gap, a collapsed flex connector, or a previous pest intrusion — conditions that change the cleaning approach entirely. Running a high-powered vacuum through a compromised section without sealing it first just redistributes debris into living spaces.

Once the system is mapped, we use negative-pressure containment: a truck-mounted or high-capacity portable vacuum unit is connected to the main trunk, drawing the system to negative pressure while rotary brush agitation works through each branch run from the registers back toward the air handler. Supply and return sides are cleaned separately. If the system includes a humidifier bypass, UV light assembly, or an older electrostatic filter housing — all common add-ons in Natick homes that have been updated over the years — those components are cleaned or inspected as part of the same visit. Dryer vent cleaning can be added at the same appointment, which is worth doing if the dryer is on an interior wall with a long horizontal run, a layout common in the split-level and raised-ranch homes throughout town.

## Local Note

One pattern worth knowing about Natick specifically: a number of homes built in the 1970s and early 1980s were fitted with electric resistance heating as a primary system and later had central air added as a retrofit. That retrofit often used undersized return ducts — sometimes a single 14-inch return for an entire floor — because the original construction never anticipated a forced-air system. Undersized returns create higher velocity airflow that pulls more particulate into the duct system and deposits it faster. If your home has this configuration, duct cleaning intervals of every three to four years are more realistic than the often-cited five-year guideline.
