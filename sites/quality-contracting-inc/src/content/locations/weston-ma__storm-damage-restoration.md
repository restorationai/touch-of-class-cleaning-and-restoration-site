When a nor'easter or a fast-moving summer thunderstorm rolls through Middlesex County, that canopy becomes the primary hazard — branches the diameter of a fence post can punch through ridge boards, tear gutters from fascia, and drive rainwater into wall cavities before the storm has even passed. ## Why Weston Properties See Significant Storm Damage

Weston's housing stock skews older and larger than most of its neighbors. Many of the town's colonial and cape-style homes were built in the postwar decades, with roof framing and sheathing that predates modern engineered lumber standards. That construction era means roof decks are often board-sheathed rather than plywood-paneled — a detail that matters after a tree strike, because individual boards can crack and separate without the visible sagging that signals obvious failure on a plywood deck. Water finds those gaps fast.

The town's topography compounds the issue. Weston's terrain rolls through a series of ridges and low-lying areas, and properties in the lower elevations can accumulate runoff from neighboring lots during heavy rain events. When storm drains on local roads back up — a pattern common during the intense, short-duration downpours that have become more frequent in recent summers — that water has nowhere to go except toward foundation walls and basement entries. Homes on larger parcels with long, tree-lined driveways also face a secondary problem: downed limbs blocking access can delay assessment and allow water to migrate further into the structure while the property sits unattended.

## Our Storm Damage Restoration Process in Weston

The first priority after any storm breach is stopping active water entry. That means emergency tarping or board-up of any roof or wall opening before a full damage assessment begins — not after. Once the structure is secured, the process shifts to documentation: photographs, moisture readings at the wall and floor assembly level, and a written scope that separates structural repair from water mitigation. Insurance carriers handling claims in Massachusetts require that separation clearly, and doing it right at the front end prevents disputes later.

For homes with board-sheathed roofs, the assessment goes deeper than a surface inspection. We probe the sheathing, check the rafter tails, and look at the soffit and fascia condition, because water that enters at a breach point often travels horizontally along the top plate before it shows up on a ceiling. Drying equipment — dehumidifiers and air movers — gets placed based on actual moisture readings, not square footage estimates. We pull readings at multiple depths until the structural members reach acceptable levels before any repair work closes the assembly.

## Weston Insurance Coordination

Storm damage claims in Massachusetts are generally covered under the dwelling portion of a homeowner's policy, but the line between wind damage and flood damage matters enormously for coverage. A tree that punches a hole in a roof and allows rain to enter is typically a wind claim — covered. Groundwater that enters through a foundation wall during the same storm is typically not covered under a standard policy. We document both mechanisms separately so your adjuster can apply the right coverage to each component without conflating them. For Weston properties with older roofs, carriers will sometimes raise the question of pre-existing wear; thorough photo documentation at the time of loss is the best protection against that argument.

Getting that coordination started early keeps the structural repair from stalling while the tree question gets sorted out.
