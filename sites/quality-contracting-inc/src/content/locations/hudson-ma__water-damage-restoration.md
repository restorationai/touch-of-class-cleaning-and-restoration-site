## Why Hudson Properties See Water Damage Issues

Hudson grew up as a shoe and textile mill town along the Assabet River, and that history shows up in the building stock today. A lot of the housing near the downtown core and along the older residential streets was built before modern plumbing codes, which means galvanized and cast iron supply lines that corrode from the inside and fail without warning. Basements in these older homes were often finished or semi-finished decades after construction, so water that gets in behind baseboards or under flooring can sit against original lath and plaster longer than it would in a newer build. Add in a water table that runs high near the river corridor, and homes on the lower-lying streets deal with seasonal seepage on top of the usual pipe and appliance failures.

## Our Water Damage Restoration Process in Hudson

We start by identifying the source and shutting it down, then move into extraction with truck-mounted or portable pumps depending on how much water is involved and where it's pooled. In Hudson's older homes, that extraction step is usually faster than the drying step that follows, because plaster and old subfloor hold moisture and release it slowly. We set moisture meters into the walls and subfloor rather than just reading the surface, because surface-dry plaster over a saturated lath base is one of the most common ways a water job gets re-opened a few weeks later as a mold problem. Air movers and dehumidifiers get placed and monitored daily until readings confirm the structure is dry per the IICRC S500 standard, not just until it looks dry.
