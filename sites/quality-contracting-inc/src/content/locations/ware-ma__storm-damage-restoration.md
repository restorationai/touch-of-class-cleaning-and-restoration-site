## Why Ware Properties See Storm Damage Issues

The combination of hilly terrain and an aging tree canopy is the biggest driver of storm calls we see from this part of Hampshire County. Heavy, wet snow and ice storms load branches that have grown over rooflines for generations, and when they come down they tend to take gutters, fascia, and sometimes a section of roof deck with them. Older homes near the river bottom are also more prone to basement and crawlspace water intrusion during fast-rising storm events, since many were built before modern perimeter drainage and sump systems were standard.

## Our Storm Damage Restoration Process in Ware

The first visit is about stopping further damage, not starting the full repair. That usually means tarping an open roof section, boarding a broken window or door, and extracting standing water before it works into subflooring or plaster walls common in Ware's older housing stock. Once the property is stable, we document every affected material with photos and moisture readings, assess structural elements like roof decking and load-bearing walls, and build a repair plan that accounts for what's salvageable versus what needs to come out. Drying runs on equipment placement and monitoring rather than a fixed calendar, since older lath-and-plaster walls hold moisture longer than drywall and will show a false dry reading at the surface if you pull equipment too soon.

## Local note

One thing that catches new crews off guard in Ware: several of the older mill buildings and converted multi-family properties near the river have flat or low-slope roof sections tucked behind a pitched front facade, a common retrofit from decades of additions. Those flat sections pond water fast in a heavy storm and are often the first place a leak starts, even when the visible pitched roof out front looks untouched. We check those hidden sections first on any pre-1950s property, because that's usually where the real damage is.
