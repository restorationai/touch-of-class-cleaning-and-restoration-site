## Why Milford Properties See Sewage Backup Issues

Milford's housing stock spans early 1900s triple-deckers and mill-era homes near the town center to newer construction further out, and the sewer infrastructure underneath reflects that age gap. Cast iron waste lines common in the older homes corrode and narrow over decades, and tree roots from mature street trees frequently work into joints and cracks, creating the kind of partial clog that turns into a full backup during a heavy rain event. Properties outside the town center that rely on private septic systems face a different problem: saturated leach fields during wet New England springs and falls can push effluent back up through the house's lowest drains instead of draining out. Either way, once sewage enters a living space it carries bacteria, viruses, and parasites that standard household cleaning will not fully address, which is why extraction and sanitization have to happen together, not as separate steps days apart.

## Our Sewage Cleanup and Sanitization Process in Milford

We start by identifying the source, whether that is a municipal line backup affecting multiple fixtures at once or a single failed septic connection, because the containment strategy differs for each. Standing sewage and contaminated water are extracted first, followed by removal of any porous materials that absorbed contamination, carpet padding, drywall below the flood line, and insulation in a crawl space are common losses. Surfaces that can be saved are cleaned and treated with EPA-registered antimicrobial products, and the space is dried and monitored following IICRC S500 drying guidelines until moisture readings confirm the structure is stable. Throughout the job we document conditions with photos and moisture readings, which matters in Milford because many homes here carry separate sewer backup riders on their homeowners policies that require that kind of evidence.
