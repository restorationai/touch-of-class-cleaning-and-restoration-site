## Why Sturbridge Furniture Needs Different Care

Sturbridge's housing stock skews older than a lot of central Massachusetts towns, thanks partly to the historic district built up around Old Sturbridge Village and the farmhouses and colonial-era homes scattered along the back roads leading out from the village. Many of these homes still have their original wool or horsehair-stuffed upholstery, or family heirloom pieces reupholstered decades ago in fabrics that don't tolerate aggressive cleaning chemicals. At the same time, Sturbridge sits close enough to its lakes that summer humidity works its way into cushions and frames, and that moisture holds onto odors longer than it would in a drier climate. Add in the wood-burning stoves and fireplaces common in the town's older homes, and you get upholstery carrying a mix of smoke residue and trapped humidity that a standard surface clean won't touch.

## Our Upholstery Cleaning Process in Sturbridge

We start by identifying the fiber, whether that's a synthetic blend common in newer furniture or a natural fiber more typical in Sturbridge's older homes, since the wrong cleaning method can set a stain permanently into wool or silk blends. From there we vacuum thoroughly to lift embedded grit, spot-treat any protein or oil-based stains, and use low-moisture steam extraction on most fabric pieces so cushions aren't left saturated in a humid house for days. Leather gets a separate conditioning step after cleaning, since leather that dries out too fast in a heated Sturbridge living room during winter will crack at the seams within a season or two. We finish with a drying pass using air movers so nothing stays damp long enough to pick up mildew smell, which is a common complaint we hear on lake-adjacent properties.
