## Why Wellesley Homes See Distinct Upholstery Wear

Wellesley's housing stock leans older, colonial and Victorian-era homes with formal living rooms that see less daily traffic, paired with newer additions and finished basements that see a lot more. New England's humid summers and dry, forced-air winters also swing the moisture content of natural fibers like wool and cotton blends, which is part of why we check fiber content and test for colorfastness before applying any cleaning solution, not just on delicate or antique pieces.

## Our Upholstery Cleaning Process

We start with a dry vacuum to lift surface debris and grit, since grit left in place acts like sandpaper during agitation and can dull or fray fibers. Next we identify the fabric or leather type and run a colorfastness spot test in an inconspicuous area, because Wellesley's older homes frequently have reupholstered or slipcovered pieces where the outer fabric and original frame padding are different ages and react differently to moisture. From there we apply a pretreatment suited to the soil type, hand-agitate stubborn spots, and hot-water extract for fabric pieces or use a specialized leather cleaning and conditioning process for leather. We finish with grooming to restore the nap and set up fans to speed drying, since a sofa that stays damp too long in a closed-up formal room can develop a musty smell before anyone notices.

## Fabric and Leather Considerations for Wellesley Homes

Leather furniture shows up often in Wellesley's home offices and dens, and it needs a completely different approach than fabric. Aniline and semi-aniline leathers absorb cleaning products the way fabric does and can stain or darken if the wrong solution is used, while protected, pigmented leather tolerates cleaning better but still needs conditioning afterward to prevent cracking, especially in homes with dry winter heat. For fabric pieces, we pay close attention to natural fibers like silk blends and wool, which are more common in Wellesley's higher-end furnishings and require lower moisture, gentler agitation, and longer drying windows than synthetic microfiber or polyester blends.
