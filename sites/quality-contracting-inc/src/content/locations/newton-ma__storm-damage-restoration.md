## Why Newton Properties See Significant Storm Damage

Newton's housing stock skews older than most Greater Boston suburbs. A large share of single-family homes were built between the 1890s and the 1950s, which means balloon-frame construction, true-dimension lumber, and plaster-over-lath walls — all of which behave differently under storm stress than modern platform-framed houses with OSB sheathing. When wind-driven rain finds a breach in a slate or cedar-shake roof, it doesn't just wet the top layer; it wicks laterally through the lath cavity and can travel several feet from the original entry point before showing up as a stain on a ceiling.

The city's tree canopy is a genuine asset — and a genuine liability during high-wind events. Newton's mature hardwoods are beautiful until a storm-weakened limb drops onto a sunroom or a full root ball heaves out of saturated soil and takes a fence, a gas meter, or a corner of a foundation with it. Post-storm roof punctures and crushed gutters are the most common calls; structural damage from full tree falls is less frequent but far more involved.

Newton also sits at a moderate elevation with varied micro-drainage. Neighborhoods built on flatter terrain can see standing water in basements and crawlspaces within hours of a heavy storm, especially where older clay drain tile has partially collapsed or where downspouts discharge too close to the foundation.

## Our Storm Damage Restoration Process in Newton

Every job starts with a thorough damage assessment before any materials are moved or discarded. That documentation matters — for your insurance adjuster, for any required building permits, and for understanding the full scope of what the storm actually did versus what was already a maintenance issue.

Once the scope is clear, the sequence typically runs:

**Emergency stabilization** — tarping breached roofs, boarding broken windows and doors, and securing any structural elements that pose an immediate hazard. In Newton's older homes, this step sometimes requires working around original architectural details — decorative cornices, historic window trim, masonry chimneys — that need protection rather than just speed.

**Water extraction and drying** — any storm-driven water inside the building needs to come out before structural repairs begin. Plaster walls can read dry at the surface while the underlying lath stays saturated for days; we use moisture meters and thermal imaging to map the actual wet zone, not just the visible stain.

**Debris removal and structural repair** — tree limbs, damaged roofing materials, and compromised framing are removed and the structure is rebuilt to current code. Newton falls under Massachusetts State Building Code, and any permitted repair work goes through the Newton Inspectional Services Department.

**Interior restoration** — once the envelope is weathertight and dried to standard, interior finishes are restored. Matching historic plaster profiles, period trim profiles, or original hardwood flooring is a detail that matters in Newton's older neighborhoods, and we account for it in the scope rather than treating every interior the same.

## Local Note

Newton's older neighborhoods contain a notable number of homes with original slate roofing, and slate behaves very differently from asphalt shingles after storm damage. A few cracked or displaced slates do not mean the roof needs full replacement — slate roofs routinely last a century or more when the individual damaged pieces are replaced and the copper or lead flashings are maintained. Calling for a full tear-off on a repairable slate roof is both unnecessary and a loss of a durable, historically appropriate material.
