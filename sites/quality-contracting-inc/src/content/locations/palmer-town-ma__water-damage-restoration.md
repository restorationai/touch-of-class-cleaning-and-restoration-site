## Why Palmer Town Properties See Water Damage Issues

Palmer Town sits in a part of Massachusetts that gets hit hard by both winter freeze-thaw cycles and heavy spring rain. Older homes in town, many built with fieldstone or poured-concrete foundations from decades past, tend to develop hairline cracks as the ground shifts through repeated freezing and thawing. Those cracks become entry points once snowmelt or a hard rain saturates the soil around the foundation. Add in galvanized or early-generation copper plumbing that's still common in houses built before the 1970s, and you get a town with more than its share of slow leaks behind walls and sudden pipe failures during cold snaps. Basements with low clearance and limited sump capacity compound the problem: water that would drain harmlessly in a newer build can pool for hours before anyone notices.

## Our Water Damage Restoration Process in Palmer Town

The first step on any call is confirming the water source and shutting it off or containing it, because extraction on an active leak is wasted effort. From there, we extract standing water with truck-mounted or portable pumps depending on how accessible the affected area is, then move into moisture mapping. Older plaster-and-lath walls, which still show up in a fair number of Palmer Town's pre-1960 houses, hold moisture differently than drywall. Surface readings can look dry within a day while the lath behind it stays wet for a week or more, so we track readings daily rather than pulling equipment early based on a single pass. Structural drying follows the general moisture-mapping approach laid out in the IICRC S500 standard, with air movers and dehumidifiers placed and adjusted based on actual readings rather than a fixed schedule.

## Local note

A detail that catches a lot of homeowners off guard: Palmer Town's older housing stock often has additions built at different times, which means subfloor materials can change room to room in the same house, plywood in a 1980s addition, old-growth pine board in the original structure. That matters for drying, because each material absorbs and releases moisture at a different rate. We check subfloor type room by room instead of assuming the whole house dries on the same timeline, which keeps us from pulling equipment too early in the newer section while the original part of the house is still holding moisture.
