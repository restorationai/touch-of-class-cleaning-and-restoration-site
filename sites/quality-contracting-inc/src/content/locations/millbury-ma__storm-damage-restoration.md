## Why Millbury Properties See Disproportionate Storm Damage

Millbury's housing stock tells two very different stories. In the older neighborhoods closer to the Blackstone River and the historic mill sites, you'll find homes built in the late 1800s and early 1900s — balloon-frame construction, original clapboard siding, and rooflines that were designed for snow loads but not for the lateral wind pressure a strong derecho or the remnants of a coastal hurricane can generate. Shingles fail at the ridge, fascia boards pull away, and once the roof deck is exposed, water reaches interior framing within minutes.

On the Route 146 corridor near The Shoppes at Blackstone Valley, the picture shifts to newer commercial and mixed-use construction — but flat or low-slope roofing systems on retail buildings are especially vulnerable to ponding when storm drains back up. Lake Singletary properties add a third layer: shoreline homes that face open fetch across the water, meaning wind-driven rain hits siding and window seals at angles that standard weatherstripping was never rated for.

Spring storms compound the problem because the ground in this part of Worcester County is already saturated from snowmelt running into the Blackstone watershed. A storm that drops two inches of rain in an hour has nowhere to go — it moves laterally into crawl spaces and through those stone foundation walls.

## Our Storm Damage Restoration Process in Millbury

The first step on any storm job is a structured exterior and interior assessment before a single piece of debris moves. We document the damage photographically in a format that insurance adjusters can read directly — roof penetrations, water intrusion points, structural deflection in wall framing, and any immediate hazards like compromised load-bearing members or downed utility connections.

Once the scope is clear, work proceeds in a logical sequence: emergency tarping or board-up to stop active water entry, controlled debris removal (including tree limbs on the roof, which require careful rigging to avoid secondary damage to the structure below), water extraction from affected interior spaces, and then drying with commercial-grade desiccant and refrigerant dehumidifiers calibrated to the actual square footage and material types involved. Structural repairs — sheathing, framing, roofing, siding — follow after moisture readings confirm the building envelope is stable.

For homes in the 01527 ZIP code with original plaster ceilings, we adjust drying timelines accordingly. Plaster releases absorbed moisture more slowly than modern drywall, and pulling equipment too early leads to secondary mold growth inside wall cavities — a problem that costs significantly more to address than extending the initial dry-out by 24 to 48 hours.

## Local Note

Homes in the Bramanville neighborhood near the old mill sites often have interior brick chimney chases that were repointed decades ago with a hard Portland cement mortar — a common repair practice that's now understood to accelerate freeze-thaw spalling in the original softer brick. If you're seeing water damage in a location that doesn't seem to correspond to any obvious roof penetration, the chimney is worth investigating before assuming the roof is the source.
