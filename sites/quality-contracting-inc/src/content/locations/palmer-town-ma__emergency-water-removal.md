## Why Palmer Town Properties See Water Damage

Much of Palmer's housing stock dates back to the town's railroad and mill era, with fieldstone or mortared-rubble foundations that were never designed to be fully watertight. Those foundations weep and seep under hydrostatic pressure in a way poured concrete doesn't, especially on lots closer to the river corridors where the water table sits higher for much of the year. Add in a New England winter freeze-thaw cycle that stresses old galvanized and copper supply lines, and you get two distinct water-loss seasons: pipe bursts and frozen supply line failures in the coldest months, and basement seepage or sump failure during spring thaw and heavy rain events. Both scenarios start the same way, a homeowner finds water where it shouldn't be, and both call for the same urgency in getting it out.

## Our Water Removal Process in Palmer Town

The first step is always assessment: how much water, how long it's been sitting, and what it's touched. In a Palmer basement with a stone foundation, we're also checking whether the water is still actively coming in through the foundation itself, which changes the extraction plan. Standing water comes out first with truck-mounted or portable extraction equipment, then we move to the materials that have absorbed water but aren't visibly wet, subfloor, framing, insulation behind walls. Moisture mapping with meters tells us what's actually saturated rather than guessing from what looks dry on the surface. Drying follows the IICRC S500 standard for structural drying, with air movers and dehumidification sized to the space and the load of water involved, not a one-size-fits-all setup.

## Palmer Town Insurance Coordination

One thing worth knowing before you call your carrier: a homeowners policy typically covers a sudden pipe burst or appliance failure, but water that enters from river overflow or rising groundwater is usually a flood event, which falls under a separate flood policy if you carry one. For properties closer to the Quaboag, Ware, or Chicopee corridors, it's worth confirming with your agent which category applies before the loss happens, because the claims process and documentation differ. We photograph and document the loss either way and can walk you through which category your situation likely falls under, though the carrier makes the final determination.

## Local note

Older Palmer basements with fieldstone or mortared-rubble walls hold moisture in the mortar joints themselves, not just on the surface, so a wall that reads dry to the touch can still be releasing moisture into the air for days after extraction. We factor that into how long we leave dehumidification equipment running rather than pulling it the moment surfaces look dry, because pulling equipment too early on these foundations is one of the more common reasons secondary mold shows up a week or two later.
