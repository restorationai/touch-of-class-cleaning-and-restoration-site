## Why Bellingham Properties See Odor Removal Issues

Bellingham sits in Norfolk County near the Rhode Island line, in a part of the Blackstone Valley where summer humidity runs high and many homes still rely on basements and crawlspaces that were never fully sealed against moisture. That combination is a problem for odor. Damp framing and subfloor hold onto smoke particles, mildew compounds, and pet dander far longer than dry materials would, and a musty basement smell can work its way up through first-floor flooring over a season or two without anyone finding a visible leak. Older housing stock around town, including capes and colonials built before modern vapor barriers were standard, tends to have more of these hidden moisture pockets than newer construction near the Interstate 495 corridor. Heating oil is still common in the area too, and a puffback or inefficient burner can leave a sooty, acrid smell that spreads through ductwork and settles on soft surfaces throughout the house.

## Our Odor Removal and Deodorization Process

We start by identifying the source, not just the symptom. A smoke smell traced back to a chimney liner issue gets treated differently than the same smell coming from scorched material inside a wall cavity, so we inspect before we treat. Once we know what we're dealing with, the work generally follows a sequence: remove or clean contaminated porous materials that can't be deodorized, treat remaining surfaces to break down odor-causing compounds at the source, then run air treatment to clear residual particles from the structure itself. For protein or smoke residue that's bonded to surfaces, thermal fogging can reach into cracks and cavities that a surface wipe-down misses, mimicking how the original smoke penetrated the space. For whole-room or whole-house situations, ozone treatment or hydroxyl deodonization clears airborne and surface-bound odor molecules, with hydroxyl generators preferred in occupied homes since they don't require the same evacuation precautions ozone does.

## Equipment & Methods We Use

Which method we use depends on the material and the odor source, not a one-size-fits-all cycle. Thermal fogging works well on smoke residue clinging to drywall, trim, and framing because the fog particles behave similarly to the smoke that caused the problem, settling into the same gaps and seams. Ozone generators are effective for stubborn, deeply set odors in unoccupied spaces, like a vacant rental between tenants or a home mid-renovation, but we schedule people and pets out during treatment and ventilate fully before anyone returns. Hydroxyl generators produce a similar oxidizing effect without the off-gassing restrictions, which makes them the better fit for a lived-in Bellingham home where a family can't easily relocate for a few days. In basements and crawlspaces where moisture is part of the odor story, we'll also address humidity control, since deodorizing a damp space without correcting the moisture just means the smell returns.
