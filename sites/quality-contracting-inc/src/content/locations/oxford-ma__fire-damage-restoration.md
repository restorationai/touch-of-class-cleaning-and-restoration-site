## Our Fire Damage Restoration Process in Oxford

The first step on any fire job is a thorough assessment — not just of the burn area, but of everywhere smoke traveled. In a balloon-framed home near North Oxford, that often means opening wall cavities to inspect framing that looks fine from the outside but has absorbed years of smoke in a matter of hours. We document everything photographically before touching a surface, both for your insurance adjuster and to establish a clear baseline for the scope of work.

Once the assessment is complete, the process moves through controlled demolition of unsalvageable materials, HEPA-filtered dry and wet cleaning of smoke-affected surfaces, thermal fogging or hydroxyl treatment for odor neutralization, and structural drying if suppression water is present. Soot chemistry varies by the type of fire — a protein fire from a kitchen stove leaves a nearly invisible, extremely pungent residue that behaves differently than the wet, oily soot from a structural fire — and the cleaning agents and techniques have to match. We seal char-affected framing with encapsulants rated for fire restoration before any reconstruction work closes those surfaces back up.

## Local Note

In the older two-family homes clustered near Oxford Center and along the French River, plaster walls are often applied over wood lath rather than metal mesh, and that combination holds smoke odor significantly longer than modern assemblies. We have found that thermal fogging alone is not enough in these structures — the odor compounds have penetrated the plaster body itself, not just the surface.
