## Our Board-Up and Tarping Process in Sutton

The first call starts a documentation chain. Before a single sheet of plywood goes up, we photograph every opening, measure every gap, and note the existing condition of surrounding materials. This matters for your insurance adjuster — a well-documented board-up file shortens the claims cycle significantly.

For fire-damaged structures, we assess whether the opening is load-bearing adjacent before anchoring. Older Sutton homes — particularly the two- and three-family mill worker housing in Wilkinsonville — sometimes have interior walls that carried structural load, and a fire can leave a window opening in a compromised wall section. We treat those differently than a clean storm breach in a modern stick-frame house.

Roof tarping on Sutton properties gets anchored with a cap-and-batten system rather than simple tie-downs. The open terrain along the Route 146 corridor generates sustained wind that will lift a tarp secured only at the edges within a single storm cycle. We run 2x4 battens across the tarp surface and fasten into the decking to hold through follow-on weather.

## Local Note

Sutton's granite ledge — the same formation exposed at Purgatory Chasm — runs close to the surface across much of town, and many older foundations were poured directly against it. After a fire or significant roof breach, water intrusion doesn't always travel the path you'd expect: it can sheet along ledge and emerge in a basement corner far from the roof opening above. When we tarp a Sutton property, we pay attention to slope and drainage direction on the roof plane, positioning tarp overlaps so runoff directs away from the foundation rather than toward it. It's a small detail, but on a ledge-heavy lot it prevents a secondary water claim on top of the original loss.
