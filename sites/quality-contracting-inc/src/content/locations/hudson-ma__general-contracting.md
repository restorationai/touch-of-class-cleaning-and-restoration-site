## Renovations, Remodels and General Contracting in Hudson, MA

Hudson's housing stock runs the gamut from converted mill worker cottages near the Assabet River to newer construction off Route 62, and that mix changes what a renovation actually involves. A kitchen gut job in a century-old frame house means dealing with balloon framing and knob-and-tube remnants before a single cabinet goes in; the same project in a 1990s build is mostly cosmetic. Quality Contracting, Inc. plans around that difference from the first walkthrough, whether the job is a straightforward remodel or a rebuild following fire or water damage.

### Why Hudson Homes Need a Tailored Approach

Hudson grew up as a shoe and textile mill town, and a good share of its residential blocks were built to house that workforce, meaning smaller footprints, shared wall lines, and additions that were bolted on over decades without always matching the original structure. When we open a wall in one of these homes, we're often finding settled framing, undersized electrical panels, and plumbing runs that were patched rather than replaced. That changes the scope conversation early: a bathroom remodel in an older Hudson home frequently uncovers knob-and-tube wiring or galvanized supply lines that need addressing before the finish work can proceed, and we flag that during estimating rather than mid-demo.

### Our Renovation and GC Process in Hudson

We start with a walkthrough that separates cosmetic scope from structural and systems work, because those two categories price and schedule differently. For post-damage rebuilds, that means coordinating directly with the restoration side of a claim, drying and demo first, then framing, rough-in, and finish, so the homeowner isn't managing two separate contractors with two separate timelines. For a planned kitchen or bathroom remodel, we pull permits through the town, schedule inspections around Hudson's typical turnaround, and sequence trades so plumbing and electrical rough-ins pass inspection before drywall closes anything up. On older homes we budget time for surprises in the walls; on newer builds we move faster because the structure behaves the way the plans say it should.

### Hudson Insurance and Permitting Coordination

A post-damage rebuild in Hudson usually starts with an insurance scope that covers drying, demolition, and basic reconstruction, but it rarely covers upgrades the homeowner wants while the walls are already open, things like relocating a kitchen island or adding a bathroom vanity run. We separate the insurance-covered scope from the owner-paid upgrade scope in writing before work starts, so there's no confusion when the carrier's check and the final invoice don't match line for line. Massachusetts building code requires permits for most structural, electrical, and plumbing work regardless of whether the project started as a rebuild or a planned remodel, and we handle that filing as part of the job rather than leaving it for the homeowner to sort out.

## Local note

Homes close to the Assabet River corridor in Hudson often sit on shallower foundations or have basements that have taken on water at some point, even if there's no visible damage now. When we're remodeling a lower level or finishing a basement in that part of town, we check for old moisture staining on sill plates and joists before we frame anything new over it, because closing up a damp structural member behind fresh drywall is the kind of mistake that costs far more to fix later than it would have cost to catch upfront.
