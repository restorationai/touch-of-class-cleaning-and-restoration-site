## Our Carpet Cleaning Process in Worcester

Every job starts with a fiber and soil assessment. Not every carpet in a Worcester home responds the same way: a wool area rug in a Quinsigamond Village triple-decker needs a different pH balance than a synthetic Berber in a newer Webster Square condo. Getting that wrong causes shrinkage, color bleed, or residue buildup that re-attracts soil within weeks.

From there, the process moves through four concrete steps:

1. **Pre-treatment and agitation** — A targeted pre-spray breaks down oil-based soils and the salt residue common in Worcester entryways. A counter-rotating brush machine works the solution into the pile, lifting compacted fibers that extraction alone won't reach.
2. **Hot water extraction** — Truck-mounted or high-capacity portable equipment flushes the loosened soil and pre-treatment solution out of the carpet. Water temperature and dwell time are calibrated to the fiber type identified in the assessment.
3. **Stain treatment** — Specific spots (pet urine, grease, tannin stains from coffee or wine) get targeted enzyme or oxidizing treatments before the final rinse pass.
4. **Controlled drying** — In Worcester's humid summers, ambient air alone won't dry a carpet in a reasonable window. High-velocity air movers accelerate drying and reduce the risk of mildew odor developing in the padding underneath.

For rental units between tenants — a common request in Worcester's dense rental market — we document pre- and post-cleaning conditions with photographs, which landlords can attach to security deposit accounting.

## Local Note: Salt and Re-Soiling in Worcester Winters

Something that comes up repeatedly in Worcester jobs — especially in first-floor units of triple-deckers with shared entryways — is accelerated re-soiling after cleaning. The culprit is usually residual road salt in the carpet backing that wasn't fully flushed during a previous cleaning. Salt crystals are invisible once dry, but they attract moisture from the air and act like a magnet for new soil. On Worcester jobs during the winter and early spring, a second rinse pass with a slightly acidic rinse agent neutralizes the alkaline salt residue and dramatically extends how long the carpet stays clean.
