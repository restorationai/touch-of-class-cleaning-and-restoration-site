## Why Wellesley Properties Are Particularly Vulnerable to Storm Damage

## Our Storm Damage Restoration Process in Wellesley

The first priority after any storm loss is stopping the water. That means emergency tarping of breached roof sections, boarding of broken windows or doors, and a rapid interior assessment to identify where water has already migrated. Water travels fast inside wall assemblies — it follows insulation batts, runs along top plates, and pools above ceilings without showing itself until the drywall is already saturated.

Once the structure is stabilized, the scope of damage gets documented thoroughly — photographs, moisture readings at wall and floor assemblies, and a written damage summary formatted for insurance submission. Structural repairs follow: framing, sheathing, roofing, siding, and interior finishes are addressed in sequence so that no repair traps moisture inside the building envelope.

For tree-impact damage specifically, the process includes assessing whether roof decking and rafters have been racked or cracked, not just punctured. A limb that hits at an angle can transfer lateral load to ridge and hip framing in ways that aren't visible from a ladder inspection alone.

Wellesley's older homes with plaster-and-lath interior walls present a specific drying challenge after storm-driven water intrusion. Unlike modern drywall, plaster can read near-dry at the surface on a moisture meter while the lath and the wood framing behind it remain significantly elevated in moisture content.
