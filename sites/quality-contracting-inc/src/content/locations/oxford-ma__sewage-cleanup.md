## Why Oxford Properties See Sewage Backup Issues

Oxford's geography does a lot of the explaining. The French River runs the length of town, and the Hodges Village Dam and Buffumville Lake flood-control infrastructure exist precisely because this valley has always collected water. Low-lying lots near the river — particularly in North Oxford and along the Route 12 corridor — sit above soils that stay saturated through much of the spring and after heavy rain events. When the water table rises, it pressurizes aging lateral sewer lines and overwhelms septic drain fields that were sized for drier conditions.

The housing stock adds another layer. Oxford's mix of postwar capes, village two-families, and older ranch-style homes often has original cast-iron or clay tile sewer laterals that have been in the ground for 60 to 70 years. Root intrusion, joint separation, and corrosion make these pipes prone to blockage and backflow. Newer subdivisions off the I-395 and Route 12 corridor have PVC lines in better shape, but they're not immune to lift-station failures or municipal main surcharges during high-flow storm events.

## Our Sewage Cleanup and Sanitization Process in Oxford

Raw sewage removal isn't a shop-vac job. The process starts with containment — sealing off the affected area to prevent cross-contamination to living spaces — followed by extraction of standing waste using truck-mounted equipment capable of handling solids. Once the bulk material is removed, every surface that contacted Category 3 water gets treated: concrete floors, wall cavities, floor joists, subfloor sheathing, and any porous material that can't be adequately sanitized gets bagged and removed per Massachusetts solid waste guidelines.

Sanitization follows extraction. We apply EPA-registered antimicrobial agents to all affected surfaces, including areas that appear dry but tested positive for moisture with thermal imaging. Structural drying with commercial dehumidifiers and air movers runs until readings confirm the assembly has returned to normal moisture levels for the region. In Oxford's climate — cold, wet springs and humid summers — that drying phase can take longer than it would in a drier part of the country, and we monitor daily rather than guessing at a fixed timeline.

One thing that catches homeowners off guard in the older neighborhoods around Oxford Center: many of the postwar capes and two-families in the 01540 ZIP code were built with concrete block basement walls rather than poured concrete.
