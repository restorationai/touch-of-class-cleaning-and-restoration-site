**Upholstery cleaning in Franklin Town** tends to get pushed to the bottom of the to-do list until a spill sets in or a musty smell settles into the cushions. In a New England town that swings from humid summers to damp, heating-season winters, fabric and leather furniture absorb more moisture and airborne dust than most homeowners realize, and that buildup shows up as dulled color, flattened pile, and odors that regular vacuuming never quite reaches.

## Why Franklin Town Homes See Upholstery Wear and Odor Issues

A lot of the housing stock around Franklin Town includes homes built from the mid-20th century onward, many with finished basements, sunrooms, or family rooms that see heavier use and less climate control than the main living areas. Upholstered furniture placed in these spaces picks up humidity swings each season: summer mugginess lets fabric fibers hold onto moisture and odor-causing bacteria, while dry winter heat can make leather stiffen and crack at the seams if it isn't conditioned. Add in pets, kids, and the everyday traffic of a busy household, and sofas and chairs end up doing more work than the fabric was designed for.

## Our Upholstery Cleaning Process in Franklin Town

We start every job with a fabric and fill inspection, because a synthetic blend, a natural fiber weave, and a leather hide all respond differently to heat, moisture, and cleaning agents. From there:

- We vacuum thoroughly to lift surface dust and debris before any liquid touches the piece.
- We test cleaning solutions on a hidden area first, especially important on older or antique furniture that may have been reupholstered with materials that weren't designed for modern steam methods.
- We use low-moisture steam extraction on most fabric pieces to avoid oversaturating cushions, which in a humid Massachusetts summer can otherwise take days to fully dry and invite mildew if left damp.
- Leather upholstery gets a separate cleaning and conditioning step rather than steam, since heat and excess water can damage the hide's finish.
- We finish with a grooming pass to restore fiber direction and appearance, and we set fans or air movers when the room's humidity calls for faster drying.

## Equipment & Methods We Use for Upholstery

For sofa cleaning, couch cleaning, and fabric upholstery cleaning, we rely on hot water extraction equipment calibrated to lower pressure and moisture output than what's used on carpet, since furniture cushions and frames can't handle the same volume of water without risking damage to padding or wood joints underneath. For leather upholstery cleaning, we use pH-balanced cleaners followed by a conditioner matched to the hide type, aniline, semi-aniline, or pigmented, because using the wrong product on the wrong leather can strip its protective finish.

## Local note

One thing we watch for in Franklin Town living rooms: furniture positioned near baseboard heaters or radiators often has one side of the fabric drier and more brittle than the rest from years of heat exposure, which changes how the material reacts to steam cleaning. We adjust technique and drying time on that side rather than treating the whole piece the same way.
