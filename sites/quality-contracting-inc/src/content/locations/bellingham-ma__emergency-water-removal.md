## Why Bellingham Properties See Water Damage

New England's freeze-thaw cycle is hard on buried water lines and foundation walls. A hard freeze followed by a quick thaw, common in this part of Massachusetts from late fall through early spring, can crack aging pipe joints or push groundwater against foundation walls that have settled over fifty or sixty years. Heavy spring snowmelt and summer downpours add pressure from the outside, which is why a lot of the calls we get in Bellingham start the same way: water showing up along a basement wall or around a floor drain with no obvious leak above it. Homes with finished basements, a common upgrade in this area's ranch and split-level housing, tend to hide the problem longer because carpet and drywall mask moisture until it's already traveled.

## Our Water Extraction Process in Bellingham

The first step is pulling standing water with truck-mounted or portable extraction equipment, since submersible pumps alone leave too much behind in carpet pad and subfloor. From there we moisture-map the affected area with meters to find out how far the water traveled under flooring and behind baseboards, because in a lot of Bellingham's older construction the visible wet spot is smaller than the actual damage. Drying follows the IICRC S500 standard for structural drying: air movers to break the surface tension on wet materials, dehumidification to pull the moisture out of the air before it re-settles, and daily monitoring until readings confirm the structure is dry, not just dry to the touch. Fieldstone foundations and dirt-floor crawlspaces take longer to dry than modern poured concrete, since stone and packed earth hold moisture and release it slowly.
