## Why Marlborough Furniture Needs a Different Approach

Marlborough sits in the MetroWest corridor, and its housing stock reflects a few distinct eras: shoe-manufacturing-era homes near the older core of the city, mid-century ranches and colonials from the suburban growth years, and newer construction and condo developments built out along the Route 20 and I-495 corridor as the tech and biotech office parks expanded. Each era brought different furniture habits with it. Older homes often still have original wool or wool-blend upholstered pieces passed down through a family, which react differently to moisture and heat than the polyester blends common in furniture bought in the last two decades. Newer condos and townhomes tend to have more synthetic and bonded leather pieces, which need lower moisture and different pH-balanced solutions to avoid delamination.

New England's seasonal swing adds another layer. Humid summers push moisture into fabric fibers and can trap odors, especially in finished basements and family rooms that don't get much airflow. Winter brings dry forced-air heat that pulls natural oils out of leather, leading to cracking if it isn't conditioned after cleaning. Homes that still use a wood stove or fireplace for supplemental heat, which is common in Marlborough's older neighborhoods, often show a fine soot film on upholstery near the hearth that regular vacuuming doesn't touch.

## Our Upholstery Cleaning Process

We start by identifying the fabric type and testing colorfastness in an inconspicuous spot before any solution touches the visible surface, since a wool blend and a microfiber sectional call for different chemistry and heat settings. Pretreatment targets specific soiling, whether that's pet dander worked into a family room couch, mineral buildup from a well-water spill in homes on private wells outside the municipal water system, or general body-oil buildup on high-use seating. Hot water extraction is our standard method for fabric upholstery, pulling embedded soil and moisture out with a low-moisture approach that speeds drying. Leather pieces get a separate, gentler cleaning and conditioning step rather than the steam process used on fabric. We finish with grooming to lift the nap on fabric pieces and a light conditioning pass on leather so it doesn't dry stiff.
