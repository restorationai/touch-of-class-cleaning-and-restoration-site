## Why Grafton's Housing Stock Shapes Every Renovation Decision

Age and origin matter more in Grafton than in most Central Massachusetts towns. Homes around Grafton Center and the Common were often built before modern insulation, vapor barriers, or load-path engineering were standard practice. That means a kitchen remodel that looks straightforward on paper can open up to balloon-frame walls, knob-and-tube wiring tucked behind plaster, and sill plates sitting directly on fieldstone with no sill seal between them. In the mill villages, you're often dealing with structures that were built fast and cheap to house workers — low ceiling heights, minimal subfloor depth, and shared walls that complicate structural modifications.

The commuter subdivisions near the Grafton MBTA station present a different challenge: homes built quickly during the late 1990s and early 2000s construction boom that are now hitting the age where builder-grade finishes fail simultaneously. These homes look newer but often need more coordination than they appear to — multiple subcontractors, permit pulls with the Grafton building department, and sequencing that keeps the house livable throughout.

## Local Note: What Grafton's Fieldstone Foundations Mean for Renovation Scope

One thing that surprises homeowners in the older sections of Grafton Center and around the Common: fieldstone foundations aren't just a cosmetic quirk — they actively affect what you can do above them.
