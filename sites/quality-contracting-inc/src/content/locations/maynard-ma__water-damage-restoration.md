## Why Maynard Properties See Water Damage Issues

Maynard grew up as a mill town, and a lot of its housing stock reflects that: older capes, colonials, and multi-family buildings with foundations that predate modern waterproofing standards. Combine that with a New England freeze-thaw cycle and the spring runoff that follows a hard winter, and you get basements that take on water even when there's no obvious storm event. Homes closer to the Assabet River corridor deal with a higher water table for part of the year, which puts extra pressure on foundation walls and sump systems that were never designed for today's storm intensity. Add in aging municipal stormwater infrastructure common to small Middlesex County towns, and a heavy rain event can back up faster than a homeowner expects.

## Our Water Damage Restoration Process in Maynard

The first visit is about finding out where the water actually is, not just where it's visible. In older Maynard homes with fieldstone or brick foundations, moisture travels upward through masonry before it ever shows as a stain, so we check wall cavities and baseboards higher than we would in a home built on poured concrete. Extraction comes first, pulling standing water out of basements, crawlspaces, and flooring before it has time to wick further into wood framing. From there we set up structural drying equipment calibrated to the materials in the house, plaster and old-growth lumber dry differently than drywall and dimensional lumber, and we track moisture content daily until readings return to normal rather than guessing based on a fixed number of days. Sewage or contaminated water intrusion gets handled separately, following IICRC S500 drying standards for category and class determination before any rebuild work starts.

## Local note

Many of Maynard's older homes near the river sit on fieldstone or brick foundations rather than poured concrete, and those materials hold and release moisture on a much slower timeline. A basement wall can read dry on a surface moisture meter while the masonry behind it is still saturated, which is why we take readings at multiple depths and don't call a job finished based on one pass with a moisture meter.
