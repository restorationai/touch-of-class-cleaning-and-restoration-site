## Why Newton's Building Stock Complicates Fire Damage

Much of Newton was built during a period when balloon-frame construction was standard — a method where wall cavities run continuously from the foundation sill to the roof rafters with no fire blocking in between. In a modern platform-frame house, fire tends to stay contained to one floor. In balloon-frame construction, a kitchen fire can push smoke and heat vertically through interior walls into attic spaces before the fire itself ever reaches those areas. That means visible char on the first floor often understates the smoke migration above it.

Older plaster walls compound the problem. Unlike drywall, which can be cut out and replaced in sections, plaster over wood lath is structural in character — removing it carelessly can destabilize adjacent surfaces. Soot particles also penetrate plaster more deeply than they do modern gypsum board, which means surface cleaning alone rarely eliminates odor. Thermal fogging and hydroxyl treatment are typically necessary to reach the compounds that have migrated into the substrate.

## The Restoration Process, Calibrated to Newton Homes

Every fire loss starts with a scope — a room-by-room assessment of what burned, what was smoke-affected, and what sustained secondary water damage from suppression. In Newton's older homes, that scope almost always extends further than it first appears. Smoke travels through gaps in plaster, around pipe penetrations, and along the top plates of interior walls.

Once the scope is documented, the sequence generally runs: structural stabilization and board-up if needed, contents pack-out and inventory, demolition of non-salvageable materials, dry-ice or soda blasting of framing where char is present, HEPA vacuuming and chemical sponge cleaning of smoke-affected surfaces, odor neutralization, and finally reconstruction. Reconstruction in Newton often involves matching historic trim profiles, sourcing period-appropriate materials, or navigating the city's building permit process — all of which add time that a homeowner should plan for.

Insurance carriers typically require detailed photo documentation and a line-item estimate before authorizing work. Quality Contracting coordinates that documentation from the first day on-site so the claim moves in parallel with the restoration, not after it.
