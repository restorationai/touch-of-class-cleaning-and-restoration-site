## Why Westborough Properties See Roofing Problems

Central Worcester County gets meaningful snowfall most winters, and Westborough's position along Route 9 and I-495 puts it squarely in the band that accumulates ice at eaves before the rest of the roof sheds its load. That temperature differential, warm attic air meeting cold eave overhangs, is the primary engine behind ice dam formation. Once water backs up under shingles and into the underlayment, the damage compounds quickly: rotted sheathing, stained ceilings, and insulation that loses its R-value when it gets wet.

The town's housing stock adds another layer of complexity. Westborough has a substantial number of homes built in the postwar decades when roof pitches were lower and ventilation details were minimal by today's standards. Low-slope roofs trap heat in summer and ice in winter, accelerating shingle granule loss. On the other end of the spectrum, the newer construction near the commuter rail corridor often features steeper pitches and architectural shingles that still need proper ice-and-water shield installation to perform as warranted.

## Our Roofing Installation and Replacement Process in Westborough

Every project starts with a thorough inspection of the decking, not just the shingles. Shingles can look worn on the surface while the plywood or board sheathing underneath is still sound, or they can look acceptable while hiding soft spots from years of slow moisture intrusion. We probe and document the deck condition before any material is ordered.

Once the scope is clear, we pull the required permit from Westborough's Building Department. Skipping the permit might save a day on scheduling, but it creates real problems at resale and can void manufacturer warranties. After tear-off, we install ice-and-water shield along the eaves and in all valleys, a step that is not optional in Massachusetts given the climate. Synthetic underlayment goes over the field of the roof before any finish material is applied.

For most Westborough homes, architectural asphalt shingles are the practical choice: they carry longer manufacturer warranties than three-tab, shed snow more readily due to their dimensional profile, and match the character of the neighborhood. Where a homeowner prefers metal roofing for longevity or cedar shake for aesthetics, we work with those materials as well. The final step before cleanup is a ridge vent or box vent inspection to confirm the attic is ventilating properly, because a new roof over a poorly ventilated attic will age prematurely regardless of shingle quality.
