## Why Weston Properties See Mold Issues

The geology beneath Weston matters more than most homeowners realize. The town sits on glacial till and outwash deposits that drain unevenly — water moves quickly through sandy pockets but pools against clay lenses, and that inconsistency puts lateral pressure on foundation walls in ways that newer construction in flatter, sandier towns doesn't experience. Fieldstone and poured-concrete foundations common in pre-1970 Weston homes weren't built with modern waterproofing membranes, so seasonal groundwater migration into basements and crawl spaces is a recurring pattern rather than a one-time event.

Weston's tree canopy — one of the densest of any town its size in eastern Massachusetts — also keeps roof surfaces and gutters shaded and damp well into spring. Gutters that stay wet through April feed water into fascia boards and soffit cavities, and from there it's a short path to attic sheathing. Attic mold in Weston is frequently a ventilation and moisture story, not just a leak story.

## Our Mold Remediation Process in Weston

Every project begins with a thorough moisture mapping of the affected area. We use thermal imaging and calibrated moisture meters to locate saturation behind surfaces that read dry to the touch — plaster walls, in particular, can hold moisture deep in the substrate while the painted face feels normal. That hidden saturation is what allows mold to persist after a surface cleaning that looks complete.

Once we understand the moisture map, we establish containment. Negative-air pressure barriers isolate the work zone so that disturbed spores don't migrate to unaffected rooms — critical in occupied homes and especially important in the larger, open-plan layouts common in Weston's newer estates. HEPA-filtered air scrubbers run continuously throughout the project.

Affected porous materials — drywall, insulation, wood trim — are removed and bagged for disposal. Structural framing and masonry that can be retained are treated with EPA-registered antimicrobial agents and dried to documented moisture levels before any encapsulation or reconstruction begins. We don't close a wall until the numbers confirm it's ready.
