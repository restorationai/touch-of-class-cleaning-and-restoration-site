## Why Millbury's Housing Stock Complicates Fire Damage

Millbury isn't a uniform suburb. The neighborhoods closest to the Blackstone River — including Bramanville and the older blocks around Millbury Center — contain homes built in the mill-era construction style common throughout Worcester County: balloon-frame walls, plaster-and-lath interiors, and stone or brick foundations that were never designed with modern fire-stop blocking in mind. In a balloon-frame house, fire and smoke travel vertically through continuous wall cavities from the basement sill plate to the roof ridge without interruption. That means smoke contamination in these homes regularly extends two or three floors above the room of origin, and structural assessment has to account for char that may have traveled inside wall cavities before the fire department arrived.

The newer retail corridor around The Shoppes at Blackstone Valley and the Route 146 interchange brought a wave of residential development alongside it — vinyl-sided colonials and townhomes with engineered lumber framing. These structures respond differently to fire heat: LVL beams and OSB sheathing can lose structural integrity faster than dimensional lumber, which changes how we sequence the structural assessment before any restoration work begins.

## Our Fire Damage Restoration Process in Millbury

Every job starts with a full scope walk — not an estimate, a scope. We photograph and document every affected surface, cavity, and system before anything is touched, because insurance carriers require it and because it protects you if supplemental damage is found later. From there, the process moves in a specific order:

**Emergency board-up and tarping** comes first if the structure is open to weather. Millbury winters are hard on exposed framing; a fire in late fall or winter that leaves a roof opening can introduce moisture damage within days, compounding the loss.

**Structural drying and stabilization** follows if firefighting water is present — hose lines leave significant moisture in flooring, subfloor, and wall cavities, and that water has to be addressed before any rebuild can begin.

**Soot and smoke removal** is the most labor-intensive phase. We use dry chemical sponges, HEPA vacuuming, and thermal fogging or hydroxyl generation depending on the extent of odor penetration. In plaster-walled homes common to Millbury Center, smoke odor can absorb into the plaster substrate itself, requiring encapsulation primers in addition to surface cleaning.

**Structural repairs and reconstruction** close out the project — framing, insulation, drywall, painting, and finish work handled by the same crew, so you're not coordinating between a remediation company and a separate contractor.
