## Why Palmer Homes See Upholstery Wear Differently

Palmer's seasonal swing matters more than people expect. Summers along the Quaboag River valley run humid, and that humidity gets pulled into fabric fibers, feeding odor-causing bacteria and dust mites in upholstery long before anyone notices a visible stain. Come winter, many of the town's older houses and triple-deckers rely on forced-air or woodstove heat that dries the air out fast, which is exactly when leather and vinyl upholstery starts to crack and fade at the seams. We see both extremes on the same piece of furniture over a single year, which is part of why a once- or twice-a-year cleaning schedule tends to outperform occasional spot treatment.

## Our Upholstery Cleaning Process

We start by identifying the fabric or hide, because a microfiber sectional, a cotton-blend armchair, and a leather sofa each need a different approach. Fabric upholstery typically gets a pre-treatment for visible soiling, followed by hot water extraction with a low-moisture steam method that lifts embedded dirt without oversaturating the cushions or the frame underneath, since older furniture frames in Palmer's older homes are more likely to be wood that can warp if it stays wet too long. Leather pieces get cleaned with a pH-balanced conditioner-safe process rather than steam, protecting the finish instead of stripping it. We finish with a controlled drying pass so cushions aren't sitting damp in a humid room for days, which is a real risk during Palmer's muggier stretches.
