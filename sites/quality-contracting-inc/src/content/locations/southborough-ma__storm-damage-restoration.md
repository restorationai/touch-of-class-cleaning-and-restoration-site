## Why Southborough Properties Are Vulnerable to Storm Damage

Southborough's housing stock skews older — a significant share of the town's single-family homes were built in the mid-twentieth century or earlier, with roof framing, window flashing details, and chimney construction that reflect the standards of their era rather than today's. That matters during a storm because aged asphalt shingles lose granule coverage gradually, and a single severe hail event can push a roof that was "functional" into active leakage without looking dramatically different from the street.

The town's topography adds another layer of exposure. Southborough occupies rolling terrain in Worcester County, and properties on elevated lots or near open fields regularly see higher sustained wind speeds than the regional average suggests. Mature trees — a defining feature of many residential streets — are a genuine dual-edged asset: beautiful in calm weather, a significant structural hazard when root systems are saturated from spring snowmelt or a prolonged rain event and a gust arrives at the wrong angle.

Finally, Southborough's proximity to the Sudbury River watershed means that storm events don't always stay above ground. Heavy rainfall can overwhelm older storm drain infrastructure and push water toward basement walls and window wells in low-lying areas, turning what looks like a wind event into a combined wind-and-water loss.

## Our Storm Damage Restoration Process in Southborough

Every storm loss is different, but the sequence of decisions matters. The first priority on any Southborough job is stopping active water entry — that means emergency tarping of breached rooflines, boarding compromised windows or doors, and assessing whether standing water inside the structure needs to be extracted before any drying equipment is placed.

Once the structure is stabilized, the documentation phase begins. We photograph and measure all affected areas, identify materials that can be dried in place versus materials that need to be removed, and produce a written scope that your insurance adjuster can work from directly. Older homes with plaster walls and original wood-framed roofs require more careful moisture mapping than newer construction — a surface reading can show dry while the underlying framing holds saturation for days.

Drying equipment — industrial dehumidifiers and air movers calibrated to the affected square footage — runs until daily moisture readings confirm the structure has returned to pre-loss levels. We don't pull equipment on a fixed schedule; we pull it when the numbers say the job is done.

Southborough's older Colonial and Cape-style homes frequently have unventilated or minimally ventilated attic spaces — a construction detail common in Worcester County homes built before modern energy codes required balanced intake and exhaust.
