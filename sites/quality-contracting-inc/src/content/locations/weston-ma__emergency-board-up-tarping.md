## Why Weston Properties Are Particularly Vulnerable After a Loss

Weston's tree canopy is one of its defining features, but those mature oaks and maples become projectiles in a nor'easter or a fast-moving summer thunderstorm. Homes along the town's winding, wooded roads frequently sit on large lots where a single tree can take out a section of roofline, a dormer, or a bay window before anyone on the street notices. The town's housing stock skews older — many properties date to the mid-20th century or earlier, with original single-pane windows and wood-framed construction that offers little resistance once a structural opening forms.

Fire losses compound the problem. Post-fire, a home's roof deck and window frames are often structurally compromised in ways that aren't visible from the street. Leaving those openings unsealed invites rain intrusion, accelerates smoke odor migration into unaffected areas, and can create safety hazards for adjusters and contractors who need to access the property later.

## Our Board-Up and Tarping Process in Weston

Every board-up and tarping job starts with a rapid assessment of all breach points — not just the obvious one. On a Weston Colonial with dormers, that means checking the roofline at multiple elevations, inspecting the attic hatch from below if safe, and confirming that any broken windows on secondary facades are accounted for. A single missed opening can funnel wind-driven rain into a wall cavity for days before interior damage becomes visible.

For roof tarping, we use reinforced polyethylene tarps secured with weighted lumber battens rather than nails driven into the existing deck — a method that protects the underlying structure and keeps the tarp stable in the kind of sustained winds that follow a nor'easter. For window and door board-up, we cut OSB panels to fit each opening precisely, fasten them with structural screws into the surrounding framing, and seal the perimeter to reduce wind infiltration. On historic or architecturally detailed homes — common in Weston — we take care around original trim and molding to avoid damage that would complicate later restoration.

## Weston Insurance Coordination for Board-Up and Tarping

Board-up and tarping costs are typically covered under the dwelling protection portion of a homeowner's policy as emergency mitigation — meaning the work is meant to prevent further loss, which most carriers treat favorably. Documentation is critical: insurers want photographs of each opening before and after boarding, a written scope describing materials and dimensions, and a clear timeline. Quality Contracting produces that documentation as a standard part of every job, not as an afterthought, so your adjuster has what they need to process the claim without back-and-forth.

Weston homeowners with older, high-value properties should be aware that some carriers require a licensed contractor — not just any handyman — to perform emergency mitigation for the costs to be reimbursable. Confirming contractor credentials with your insurer before work begins is worth the five-minute call.
