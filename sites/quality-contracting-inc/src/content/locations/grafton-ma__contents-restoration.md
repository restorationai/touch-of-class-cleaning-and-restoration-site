## Why Grafton Properties See Contents Damage Differently

Grafton's three main village centers each carry their own architectural fingerprint, and that fingerprint shapes how contents get damaged and how they need to be treated. Around Grafton Center and the Common, homes built in the 18th and 19th centuries often have fieldstone foundations, horsehair plaster walls, and original wide-plank flooring. When water intrudes — whether from a failed sump, a frozen pipe, or a roof breach — it wicks laterally through those older materials and reaches furniture, rugs, and stored items faster than it would in a modern framed home.

Down in Fisherville and Farnumsville, the mill-village housing stock along the Blackstone River corridor tends to sit on low-lying lots. Seasonal flooding and high groundwater are recurring realities here, and contents on lower floors — finished basements, ground-level living spaces — are routinely the first casualties. Upholstered furniture, wood cabinetry, and paper records absorb that moisture within hours. Without a structured pack-out and controlled drying environment, mold colonization can begin in as little as 24 to 48 hours.

Newer commuter subdivisions near the Grafton MBTA station (ZIP 01519) face a different set of risks: open floor plans with large furniture pieces, finished lower levels, and attached garages that can channel fire and smoke through a home quickly. Soot from synthetic materials — foam cushions, laminate cabinetry, engineered flooring — is chemically different from wood-smoke residue and requires different cleaning chemistry to neutralize.

## Our Contents Restoration and Storage Process in Grafton

The first step after any loss is a documented pack-out. Every item removed from a Grafton home is photographed, inventoried, and assigned a condition rating before it leaves the property. This matters both for insurance documentation and for your own peace of mind — you know exactly what was taken, where it is, and what its pre-treatment condition was.

From there, contents move to a climate-controlled storage and processing facility where each category of item is treated according to its material and contamination type:

- **Soft goods** (upholstery, clothing, drapery): ozone treatment and ultrasonic or immersion cleaning depending on soiling level
- **Hard goods** (wood furniture, cabinetry, decorative items): hand-cleaning with appropriate solvents, followed by deodorization
- **Electronics**: dry-out and evaluation before any cleaning attempt — moisture and soot inside a device require different handling than surface contamination
- **Documents and photographs**: freeze-drying or air-drying protocols to halt further degradation

Once items are cleaned, deodorized, and confirmed dry, they are stored in a secure, climate-controlled environment until your home is ready to receive them. Re-delivery is coordinated around your reconstruction timeline so nothing sits in a damp or unfinished space.
