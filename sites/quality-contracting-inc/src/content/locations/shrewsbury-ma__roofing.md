## Why Shrewsbury Roofs Wear Out Faster Than You'd Expect

Worcester County winters are not gentle, and Shrewsbury's position on the higher ground east of the city means it often picks up more accumulation than surrounding towns. Ice dam formation is the most common culprit behind premature roof failure here. When heat escapes through an under-insulated attic, snow melts at the peak, runs toward the cold eaves, and refreezes. That ice wedges under shingles, forces water into the sheathing, and eventually finds its way into the soffit or the top-floor ceiling. The problem is compounded on homes built in the 1960s and 1970s, which make up a large share of Shrewsbury's housing stock. Those roofs were typically designed with minimal attic ventilation by today's standards, and decades of thermal cycling have left many of them with sagging decking or brittle felt underlayment that no longer sheds water reliably.

Spring brings its own stress. The soil in this part of central Massachusetts retains moisture well into April, and the temperature swings between March nights and April afternoons cause flashing around chimneys and dormers to expand and contract repeatedly. That movement is where leaks usually start, even on roofs that look intact from the street.

## Our Roofing Installation and Replacement Process in Shrewsbury

Every project starts with a thorough inspection of the decking, not just the surface material. On older Shrewsbury homes, we frequently find that the visible shingles are the last thing that needs attention. Rotted or delaminated OSB sheathing beneath them is what drives the real cost and timeline, and we identify it before a single shingle is removed so there are no surprises mid-project.

Once the scope is confirmed, we strip the existing roofing down to bare decking, replace any compromised sections, and install a high-quality synthetic underlayment before the new shingles go on. For homes in Shrewsbury with steeper pitches or complex valley systems, we pay particular attention to the ice-and-water shield application, running it well past the interior wall line to account for the severe ice dam conditions common here. Ridge ventilation is assessed on every job, because a new roof over a poorly ventilated attic will fail ahead of schedule regardless of the shingle quality.

We coordinate directly with the town of Shrewsbury's building department for permit pulls when required, so the project stays code-compliant and your resale or insurance position is protected.

## Local Note

Shrewsbury has a meaningful number of homes built during the postwar subdivision boom of the late 1940s and 1950s, and many of them were roofed with skip sheathing rather than solid decking, a technique that left gaps between boards to allow cedar shakes to breathe. When those homes were later re-roofed with asphalt shingles, the skip sheathing was often left in place and simply covered over. If your home is from that era, there is a reasonable chance the decking under your current shingles is not a solid surface. We check for this specifically on older Shrewsbury properties, because installing over skip sheathing without filling the gaps or overlaying solid plywood creates soft spots and shortens the life of the new roof significantly.
