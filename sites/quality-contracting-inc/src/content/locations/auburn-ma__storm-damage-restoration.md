## Why Auburn Properties See Serious Storm Damage

Auburn's position along Route 20 and the I-290 corridor places it in a micro-climate zone where cold air funneling down from the Wachusett plateau meets warmer, moisture-laden air moving north from Connecticut. The result is a disproportionate share of damaging wind events, heavy wet snow, and ice storms compared to communities closer to the coast. Homes along the Stoneville and Pakachoag Hill sections of town tend to sit on elevated, exposed lots that catch the full force of northwest winds — the same winds that routinely exceed 60 mph during strong nor'easters.

The housing stock adds another layer of complexity. A large portion of Auburn's single-family homes were built between the 1940s and 1970s, which means roof decks are often 1×6 skip-sheathing or board-and-batten rather than modern OSB panels. That older sheathing can split and delaminate when a limb strikes it, leaving a ragged opening that's harder to tarp cleanly than a puncture through modern plywood. Older homes in the 01501 ZIP code also frequently have unvented attic cavities — once water gets in, it has nowhere to evaporate, and structural framing can stay wet for weeks without intervention.

## Our Storm Damage Restoration Process in Auburn

Every storm job starts with a systematic exterior assessment before a single interior wall gets opened. We photograph and document the full scope — missing or lifted shingles, soffit and fascia damage, compromised flashing, window and door frame racking — because that documentation is what your insurance adjuster needs to approve a complete claim rather than a piecemeal one.

Once the exterior is stabilized with commercial-grade tarps or plywood boarding, we move inside to identify hidden moisture. Storm events that look like roof-only damage often drive water laterally through wall cavities, especially in older Auburn homes where the exterior sheathing has gaps at corner boards and window trim. We use thermal imaging cameras and calibrated moisture meters to map the wet zone before any drywall comes down, so we're removing only what's actually damaged — not guessing.

Drying follows the IICRC S500 framework: industrial air movers and desiccant or refrigerant dehumidifiers run in a closed system until every structural reading hits the target moisture content for that material. In Auburn's humid summers, ambient outdoor air can work against drying if equipment isn't sized correctly — we calculate the psychrometric load for each job rather than placing a standard number of machines and hoping.

Reconstruction — framing repairs, roof deck replacement, insulation, drywall, and finish work — is handled by the same company. You don't hand off to a second contractor mid-job.
