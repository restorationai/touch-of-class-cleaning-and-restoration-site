## Why Millbury Properties See Mold Issues

Millbury's history as a nineteenth-century mill town left a legacy of building stock that's charming but structurally challenging. Homes near the Blackstone River and the old canal system frequently have rubble-stone or brick foundations with no modern waterproofing membrane — water doesn't flood in dramatically, it seeps in slowly, keeping crawl spaces and lower-level framing perpetually damp through March, April, and into May. That chronic low-level moisture is exactly the environment where *Cladosporium*, *Penicillium*, and *Stachybotrys* (commonly called black mold) colonize wood joists and subfloor sheathing without triggering a visible leak that would otherwise prompt a call.

Lake Singletary adds another layer of risk. Homes on the lake's edge often have below-grade mechanical rooms or finished lower levels that sit close to the water table. After a wet season, the humidity differential between the lake-side exterior and a conditioned interior can drive condensation into wall cavities — a slow process that goes unnoticed until a musty odor becomes impossible to ignore.

Millbury Center's denser residential blocks, many built before 1950, frequently have balloon-frame construction. In that framing style, wall cavities run continuously from the basement to the attic with no fire blocking — which means mold that starts in a damp basement wall can migrate upward through those open cavities over time, making the affected area larger than it first appears.

## Our Mold Remediation Process in Millbury

Every job starts with a thorough inspection — not just the area you can see, but the spaces behind it. In Millbury's older homes, that means probing subfloor assemblies, checking rim joists (a notorious cold spot for condensation in uninsulated foundations), and using a moisture meter to map the full extent of elevated readings before any containment goes up.

Once the scope is clear, we establish negative-air containment using 6-mil poly barriers and HEPA-filtered air scrubbers. This keeps disturbed mold spores from migrating to unaffected rooms — critical in a balloon-frame home where open wall cavities can act as a highway. Affected porous materials (drywall, insulation, deteriorated wood) are removed and bagged following EPA guidelines. Structural wood that can be preserved is HEPA-vacuumed, wire-brushed, and treated with an EPA-registered antimicrobial. We then address the moisture source — because remediation without fixing the underlying water intrusion is a temporary fix at best.

Drying equipment (commercial dehumidifiers and air movers) runs until moisture readings return to normal baselines for the material type. In Millbury's stone-foundation basements, that process often takes longer than it would in a poured-concrete modern home, because rubble stone holds and releases moisture more slowly.
