## Why Grafton Properties See Mold Issues

Grafton sits in a part of central Massachusetts where the frost line runs deep and snowmelt lingers well into April. That prolonged wet season puts sustained hydrostatic pressure on foundations — especially the rubble and fieldstone construction common in older homes near Grafton Center and Grafton Common. Water doesn't need a dramatic flood event to create a mold problem; a hairline crack in a foundation wall, a poorly graded yard, or a condensation-prone crawl space is often enough.

The mill villages of Fisherville and Farnumsville tell a different story. Homes built along the Blackstone River corridor in the 19th century were constructed quickly and cheaply to house mill workers, often with minimal subfloor ventilation and wood framing that has absorbed decades of seasonal humidity. In these properties, mold isn't always visible at first — it grows inside wall cavities and beneath original pine flooring before it ever appears on a surface. By the time a musty odor becomes noticeable, the colony is typically well established.

Newer commuter subdivisions near the Grafton MBTA station present a third pattern: tightly built homes with high-efficiency envelopes that trap interior moisture. Inadequate bathroom exhaust, improperly sealed crawl spaces, and HVAC condensate issues are common culprits in these developments.

## Our Mold Remediation Process in Grafton

Every project begins with a thorough visual inspection and moisture mapping. We use thermal imaging and calibrated moisture meters to locate hidden saturation in walls, subfloors, and ceiling assemblies before any demolition begins — because cutting into a wall without knowing the moisture boundary is how contamination spreads.

Once the affected area is defined, we establish negative air pressure containment using 6-mil poly barriers and HEPA-filtered air scrubbers. This step is non-negotiable in occupied homes, and it's especially important in older Grafton properties where open floor plans and original ductwork can carry spores from a basement to upper floors in minutes.

Contaminated porous materials — drywall, insulation, compromised framing — are removed, bagged, and disposed of according to Massachusetts Department of Environmental Protection guidelines. Remaining structural surfaces are HEPA-vacuumed and treated with an EPA-registered antimicrobial agent. We do not consider a job complete until post-remediation moisture readings confirm the area is dry and clearance conditions are met.

## Local Note

Homes with fieldstone or brick foundations in the Grafton Center ZIP code 01519 often have what contractors call a "false dry" problem in late spring. The interior surfaces of the foundation wall feel dry to the touch after a warm stretch, but moisture readings taken two inches into the mortar joints still show elevated levels. If remediation begins before that subsurface moisture has fully dissipated, mold can return within a season.
