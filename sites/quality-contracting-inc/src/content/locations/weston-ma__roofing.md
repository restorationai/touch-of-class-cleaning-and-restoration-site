## Why Weston Roofs Age the Way They Do

Weston sits in a climate band that delivers genuine punishment across all four seasons. Winters bring ice dam conditions that are particularly aggressive on roofs with lower pitches or inadequate attic insulation, a common combination in the ranch-style homes built throughout town in the 1950s and 1960s. When ice dams form at the eave line, meltwater backs up under shingles and works into the decking and framing before any visible leak appears inside the house. By the time a homeowner notices a water stain on a ceiling, the damage is often well behind the wall.

Summer brings its own stress. Extended stretches of heat and humidity accelerate shingle granule loss, and the dense tree cover that shades Weston's lots keeps roof surfaces damp longer after rain, which accelerates algae streaking and shortens shingle lifespan compared to more open suburban lots. A roof that might last 25 years in a sunnier, drier location can realistically lose five or more years off that estimate here.

## What Roof Replacement Actually Involves in Weston

A roofing project in Weston follows a sequence that doesn't change regardless of the home's age or style: full tear-off of existing layers, a thorough deck inspection before anything new goes down, replacement of any soft or delaminated sheathing, and installation of a high-quality synthetic underlayment before the final roofing material goes on.

For Weston's older colonial and cape-style homes, the deck inspection step is especially important. Homes built before the 1970s often have skip-sheathing or board decking rather than plywood panels, and that original material may have decades of moisture cycling behind it. Identifying compromised decking before the new roof goes on prevents the kind of structural problem that surfaces years later as a sagging ridge or a soft spot underfoot in the attic.

Material selection matters here too. Architectural shingles with a higher impact rating perform better under the branch and debris load that Weston lots generate. For homes where aesthetics are a priority, premium shingles that replicate the look of cedar shake or slate are worth discussing, particularly on larger properties where curb appeal is part of the home's value proposition.
