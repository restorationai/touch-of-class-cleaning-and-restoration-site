## Why Leicester Properties Are Particularly Vulnerable to Water Damage

The village neighborhoods of Cherry Valley and Rochdale grew up around 19th-century mill operations, which means a significant share of the housing stock predates modern plumbing standards, vapor barriers, and pressure-treated framing. Fieldstone foundations — common on older properties throughout the 01524 ZIP code — are porous by nature. They wick groundwater during spring snowmelt and again after heavy summer rain, often depositing moisture inside walls and under floors where it sits undetected for days.

Properties ringing Stiles Reservoir and Lake Sargent add another layer of complexity: many are on private wells and septic systems rather than municipal water, which means a supply-line failure or sump pump outage doesn't come with a utility shutoff call as a backstop. Homeowners are often the first — and only — line of defense until a restoration crew arrives. The combination of older construction, high-elevation freeze exposure, and private utility dependence makes Leicester one of the more demanding environments in central Massachusetts for water damage work.

## Our Water Damage Restoration Process in Leicester

Every job starts with a thorough moisture survey using thermal imaging and calibrated meters — not a visual walkthrough. In homes with plaster-and-lath walls, which are common in Leicester Center and the older sections of Cherry Valley, moisture readings can look deceptively low at the surface while the underlying wood framing holds significantly more water. We map the full extent of saturation before any drying equipment goes in.

Extraction comes next. Standing water is removed with truck-mounted and portable extraction units, and then the slower work begins: placing desiccant dehumidifiers and high-velocity air movers in a calculated pattern to pull moisture out of structural cavities without over-drying finishes or warping hardwood floors. In Leicester's cooler shoulder seasons — late September through November and again in March — outdoor air is often too cold and humid to assist with drying, so we rely entirely on equipment rather than ventilation. Drying logs are recorded daily so there's a documented timeline if an insurance adjuster needs to verify the scope.

Once structural readings reach dry standard, we assess what materials can be saved and what needs to come out. We communicate that scope clearly before any demolition begins.

Most homeowner policies cover sudden and accidental water discharge — a burst pipe, a failed washing machine supply line, an ice-dam backup — but the documentation burden falls on the policyholder to establish cause, timeline, and scope.
