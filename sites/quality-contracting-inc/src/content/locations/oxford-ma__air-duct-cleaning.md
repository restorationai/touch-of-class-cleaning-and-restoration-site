## Why Oxford Properties See Duct Issues

Oxford sits in a low-lying river valley where the Hodges Village and Buffumville flood-control dams were built specifically because this landscape floods. That hydrological history matters for indoor air quality. Even homes that have never taken on visible water can experience elevated indoor humidity during wet seasons — and ductwork is where that moisture quietly collects. Fiberglass duct liner absorbs ambient humidity, and once organic debris settles in, you have the ingredients for microbial growth inside the air distribution system.

The postwar capes common throughout Oxford Center and Texas Village were built before modern vapor barriers and tight building envelopes were standard. Many were retrofitted with forced-air systems in the 1970s and 1980s, which means the ductwork itself is now 40 to 50 years old — old enough to have accumulated significant debris and, in some cases, to have developed gaps at joints where conditioned air (and whatever is in it) leaks into wall cavities. Newer subdivisions along the I-395 and Route 12 corridor have their own issue: construction dust from nearby development finds its way into systems during the build phase and is rarely fully cleared before the first occupants move in.

## Our Air Duct Cleaning Process in Oxford

The process starts with a camera inspection of the main trunk lines and branch runs before any equipment is connected. This step matters more in older Oxford homes because it tells us what we're dealing with — whether it's standard dust accumulation, deteriorating duct liner, or evidence of past moisture intrusion — before we commit to a cleaning method.

Once we've assessed the system, we connect a high-powered negative-pressure vacuum to the main return trunk and work through the supply and return registers with rotary brush agitation, dislodging debris and pulling it toward the collection unit rather than into the living space. For homes with flex duct — common in 1980s and 1990s retrofits throughout Oxford — we adjust brush pressure carefully to avoid tearing the inner liner, which would create a bigger problem than the one we came to fix.

If the inspection revealed any biological growth or persistent odor, we apply an EPA-registered duct sanitizer after mechanical cleaning. This is an optional step, not a default upsell, and we'll show you the inspection footage so you can make an informed decision.

Dryer vent cleaning is often added at the same visit, particularly in the two-family homes common in Oxford Center where a single vent run may serve two units and hasn't been cleared in years. A clogged dryer vent is a fire risk, and the combination service saves a separate scheduling call.

One pattern we've noticed in Oxford's older river-valley homes: the return air chases in postwar capes were often built using the stud bays between wall framing rather than dedicated sheet metal ductwork. When we find them, we flag them for the homeowner because no amount of duct cleaning will fully address air quality if the return system is pulling air through an unsealed wall cavity.
