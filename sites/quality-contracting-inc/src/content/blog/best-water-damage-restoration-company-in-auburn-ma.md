## What Should You Look for in a Water Damage Restoration Company?

The right company for a water loss does four things well: they respond before the damage spreads, they dry to a documented standard, they handle insurance documentation, and they identify secondary damage before it becomes a bigger problem.

**Response time matters because water doesn't wait.** The [EPA's guidance on mold](https://www.epa.gov/mold) notes that mold can begin to develop within 24 to 48 hours of a moisture event. Every hour of standing water increases the likelihood of secondary mold growth, structural damage to subfloor and framing, and a more complicated insurance claim.

**IICRC certification matters because it sets the drying standard.** A company drying to the IICRC S500 standard uses moisture meters, thermal imaging, and psychrometric calculations to confirm that materials have returned to acceptable moisture content before equipment is pulled. A company that just runs fans for a few days and calls it done is leaving you with hidden moisture that will cause problems later.

**Documentation matters because your adjuster will ask for it.** A well-documented water loss includes: initial moisture readings by material and location, daily drying logs, equipment placement records, and a final moisture verification. If your restoration company can't produce these, your claim may face delays or disputes.

**Full-service capability matters because water losses rarely stop at one system.** A basement flood that touches finished drywall, insulation, framing, and contents is not a one-trade job. A company that handles extraction, drying, mold remediation, sewage sanitization, and contents restoration under one roof keeps your project moving without handoff gaps.

## What Happens During a Water Damage Restoration Job?

A properly run water damage restoration follows a defined sequence. Here is what that looks like from the first call to final sign-off:

1. **Emergency contact and assessment.** You call, describe the situation, and the crew is dispatched. On arrival, they identify the water source (if still active), classify the water category (Cat 1 clean water, Cat 2 gray water, Cat 3 black water), and assess affected materials.
2. **Water extraction.** Truck-mounted or portable extractors remove standing water. This step is done before drying begins.
3. **Moisture mapping.** Technicians use moisture meters and sometimes thermal imaging cameras to identify all wet materials, including hidden moisture behind walls and under flooring.
4. **Structural drying setup.** LGR (low-grain refrigerant) dehumidifiers and high-velocity air movers are positioned to move air across wet surfaces and pull moisture out of the structure. Equipment placement follows psychrometric calculations, not guesswork.
5. **Daily monitoring.** Technicians return each day to check moisture readings, adjust equipment, and log progress. Drying typically takes 3 to 5 days for a standard residential loss, longer for Category 2 or 3 events or heavily saturated materials.
6. **Mold assessment.** If the loss sat for more than 24 to 48 hours before mitigation started, or if visible mold is found, a mold assessment and remediation plan is added to the scope.
7. **Final moisture verification and documentation.** When all materials reach target moisture content, equipment is removed and a final report is generated for your insurance file.
8. **Reconstruction referral or in-house repair.** Damaged drywall, flooring, and trim are repaired or replaced.
