For water damage restoration in Auburn, call Quality Contracting, Inc. at **(508) 756-8800**. They handle the full scope: emergency water extraction, structural drying, mold prevention, and insurance documentation, all under one contractor. One Call Does It All.

But "first" depends on the situation:

- **Water still flowing from a pipe or appliance?** Call a licensed plumber to stop the source before anyone starts extraction. A restoration crew can't dry what's still getting wet.
- **Electrical panel submerged or sparking?** Call 911 and your utility (Eversource at 800-592-2000) before entering the space. Water and live circuits are a life-safety issue, not a restoration issue.

Once the scene is safe and the source is controlled, the restoration call is the one that drives everything else.

## When Is It a Plumber vs. a Restoration Company?

A plumber fixes the source of the water. A restoration company handles everything the water touched after it escaped. These are two different scopes of work, and you often need both.

If your washing machine supply hose failed, a plumber replaces the hose. The restoration crew extracts the water from your laundry room, dries the subfloor and adjacent walls, and documents the damage for your insurance adjuster. If the subfloor or drywall needs to come out and be rebuilt, a full-service restoration contractor handles that too, so you're not coordinating a separate GC.

For slow leaks behind walls, the line is less clear. If you're seeing staining but no active drip, start with a restoration company. They carry moisture meters and thermal cameras that can locate hidden moisture without opening walls unnecessarily.

## What Happens When You Call for Water Damage Restoration?

When you call a restoration company for water damage, the first conversation takes about five minutes and covers four things: the source of the water, how long it's been sitting, what materials are affected (carpet, hardwood, drywall, concrete), and whether the property is occupied.

That information determines the crew size, the equipment load, and the urgency level. Here's what the visit looks like:

1. **Moisture mapping.** The crew walks the affected area with a pin-type moisture meter and a thermal camera. They're looking for where the water traveled, not just where it's visible. Water in a Worcester County colonial can wick 10-15 feet from the source through subfloor and wall cavities before you see it.
2. **Category and class assessment.** Water is classified by contamination level: Category 1 (clean supply line), Category 2 (gray water, dishwasher or washing machine), or Category 3 (sewage or floodwater). The category determines PPE requirements and whether porous materials can be dried in place or must be removed. Class describes how much moisture is present and how hard it will be to dry.
3. **Extraction.** Truck-mounted or portable extractors pull standing water. For carpet, a weighted extraction tool pulls water from the pad. For hardwood, the approach depends on species and construction.
4. **Drying setup.** LGR (low-grain refrigerant) dehumidifiers and high-velocity air movers go in. The crew calculates the equipment count based on the cubic footage and the class of loss, per the [IICRC S500 Standard for Professional Water Damage Restoration](https://www.iicrc.org).
5. **Daily monitoring.** A technician checks moisture readings each day and adjusts equipment until the structure reaches dry standard. This typically runs 3-5 days for a Category 1 loss with no structural removal.
6. **Documentation for insurance.** Moisture logs, photos, equipment placement records, and a written scope go to your adjuster. This is the paperwork that protects your claim.

## What Should I Have Ready When I Call?

Having a few things ready speeds up the first call and helps the crew prepare the right equipment load.

- **The water source** (supply line, roof, appliance, sewer backup, groundwater)
- **How long it's been sitting** (minutes, hours, overnight)
- **Approximate square footage affected** (even a rough guess helps)
- **Whether the power is off** to the affected area
- **Your insurance carrier and policy number** (not required to start, but useful)

You do not need to have filed a claim before calling a restoration company. In fact, most adjusters prefer that mitigation starts immediately. Waiting for claim approval to begin drying is one of the most common mistakes homeowners make, and it can result in denied coverage for secondary damage (mold, structural deterioration) that occurred during the delay. The [EPA's guidance on mold and moisture](https://www.epa.gov/mold) recommends beginning drying within 24-48 hours to prevent mold growth.

For more on what homeowners policies typically cover, see [Does Homeowners Insurance Cover Water Damage? A Plain-English Guide](/blog/does-homeowners-insurance-cover-water-damage/).

## What to Do Right Now While You Wait for the Crew

While you're waiting, a few actions reduce damage without creating new hazards.

- **Stop the source** if you haven't already. Shut the main water supply valve if you can't isolate the specific line.
- **Move valuables and electronics** off wet floors if it's safe to do so. Lift furniture legs onto aluminum foil or plastic to prevent staining.
- **Do not use a household vacuum or shop vac** on sewage-contaminated water. Category 3 water requires containment, not just extraction.
- **Leave wet drywall and flooring in place.** Tearing out materials before documentation can complicate your insurance claim. Let the crew assess first.
- **Document with your phone.** Video and photos of the damage before any cleanup protect your claim.
