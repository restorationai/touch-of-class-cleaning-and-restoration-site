## Restoration emergencies common in Shrewsbury

Shrewsbury sits just across Lake Quinsigamond from Worcester, and that geography shapes the kind of damage local properties see. The lakefront homes along the Lake Quinsigamond shoreline — many with walk-out basements and daylight lower levels — sit close to a water table that rises fast during a hard New England rain or a rapid snowmelt in March. A basement that stayed dry all winter can take on several inches of water in a single storm event.

The housing stock adds another layer of complexity. The postwar capes and ranches that fill neighborhoods like Edgemere and the White City area were built in the 1950s and 1960s, often with minimal vapor barriers, older cast-iron or galvanized supply lines, and insulation that absorbs moisture rather than repelling it. When those pipes fail — and cold snaps in central Massachusetts push them toward failure every January and February — water finds its way into wall cavities and subfloor assemblies quickly. Mold can begin colonizing wet cellulose materials within 24 to 48 hours, which is why the response window matters.

Shrewsbury also runs its own municipal electric utility, SELCO, rather than relying on Eversource. After a storm causes power interruptions, restoration crews need to coordinate utility shutoffs and reconnects through SELCO rather than the regional provider — a detail that affects job sequencing and drying timelines if equipment needs to be staged before power is restored.

The Route 9 commercial corridor brings its own set of challenges: retail and restaurant spaces with large flat or low-slope roofs that pond water, older mixed-use buildings with shared walls, and tenant turnover that sometimes leaves deferred maintenance hidden until a leak becomes a major loss.

## Services we provide in Shrewsbury

**Water damage mitigation and drying** — Water damage in Shrewsbury often starts below grade. We extract standing water, map moisture with thermal imaging and pin meters, and set drying equipment calibrated to the specific materials in your home — whether that's the concrete block foundation of a 1950s ranch or the engineered lumber subfloor of a newer subdivision off Dean Park.

**Fire and smoke restoration** — Smoke residue from a kitchen fire or a chimney event doesn't stay in one room. It migrates through HVAC systems and settles into attic insulation. We assess the full travel path of smoke through the structure, clean or remove affected materials, and address odor at the source rather than masking it.

**Mold remediation** — Lakefront properties and older homes with chronic moisture issues are common mold candidates in Shrewsbury. We contain affected areas, remove compromised materials, and treat surfaces following established remediation protocols — then address the underlying moisture source so the problem doesn't return.

**Reconstruction** — Once mitigation is complete, we handle the rebuild: framing, drywall, flooring, painting, and finish carpentry. Working with a single contractor through both phases keeps the project on a consistent timeline and simplifies the insurance documentation.

Most of Shrewsbury's older stock sits on poured concrete or block foundations with full basements, and the pre-war houses closer to the Town Center often have fieldstone walls and plaster over wood or rock lath.
