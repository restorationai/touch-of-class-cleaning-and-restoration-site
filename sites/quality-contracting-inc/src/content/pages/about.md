Quality Contracting, Inc. has been serving homeowners and property managers in central Massachusetts from its base in Auburn — a town that sits almost exactly at the geographic center of the state, which makes it a natural hub for responding to losses across Worcester County and beyond. The company was built around restoration work because that's where the need is most acute: a burst pipe at 2 a.m., a kitchen fire, a basement that's been quietly growing mold behind the drywall for months. These aren't projects people plan for, and the contractor they call needs to know exactly what they're doing.

## What we do

On any given week, the work looks like this: a crew is pulling water-damaged flooring in a Cape-style home in Shrewsbury while someone in the office is on the phone with an adjuster at a major carrier, walking through a scope of loss line by line. Another team is setting up commercial-grade drying equipment in a Worcester triple-decker where a supply line failed over the weekend. Restoration work is part skilled trades, part documentation, part logistics — and the documentation piece matters more than most homeowners realize. Insurance carriers require specific moisture readings, drying logs, and photo records before they'll approve a claim. That's work that happens behind the scenes, but it's often the difference between a smooth claim and a disputed one.

The calls come from people who are stressed, sometimes displaced from their homes, and trying to make fast decisions about contractors they've never heard of. The job starts with showing up, explaining what's actually happening to the structure, and giving a clear picture of what comes next — not a vague estimate and a handshake.

## Our certifications and licensure

Restoration isn't a loosely regulated trade, and the certifications that matter in this industry exist for specific reasons. The IICRC standards — set by the Institute of Inspection, Cleaning and Restoration Certification — govern how water damage, fire damage, and mold remediation work is supposed to be performed. Those standards (the S500 for water, the S520 for mold) define drying targets, containment protocols, and documentation requirements. Working to those standards protects both the homeowner and the integrity of the repair.

For any home built before 1978, lead paint is a real consideration during demolition and reconstruction. Federal law requires contractors disturbing painted surfaces in pre-1978 housing to follow EPA Renovation, Repair and Painting (RRP) protocols. Central Massachusetts has a significant stock of older housing — triple-deckers, Colonials, mill-era worker homes — so this comes up regularly.

## Where we work

Auburn sits at the junction of I-290 and the Mass Pike, which means the team can reach Worcester in minutes and run the Pike or Route 9 straight east into MetroWest. Regular service areas include Worcester, Shrewsbury, Westborough, Southborough, Hopkinton, Framingham, Natick, Wellesley, Needham, Newton, and Weston, with crews responding across a 50 mile radius of Auburn. The region's older housing stock, cold winters, and freeze-thaw cycles create predictable patterns: ice dams in January and February, sump pump failures in the spring thaw, humidity-driven mold issues in older basements through the summer. This is the area the company knows.
