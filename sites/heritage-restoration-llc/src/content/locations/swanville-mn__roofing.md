## Why Swanville Properties See Accelerated Roofing Wear

Swanville sits in a part of Morrison County where temperature swings between January lows and March melt cycles can be extreme within a single week. That repeated contraction and expansion works on asphalt shingles the same way it works on pavement: granules loosen, tabs curl, and the self-sealing strips that hold shingles together in wind lose their grip earlier than the product warranty might suggest.

The housing stock in and around Swanville skews older, which means many homes were built with roof pitches and ventilation designs that made sense for the materials available at the time but create problems today. Low-slope sections over additions or attached garages are especially prone to standing water when gutters ice over. Attic ventilation that was adequate for wood shingles often falls short for modern architectural asphalt, trapping heat and moisture that shortens the life of a new roof from the inside out before the exterior shows any sign of trouble.

## Our Roofing Installation and Replacement Process in Swanville

Every project starts with a roof inspection that goes beyond counting missing shingles. The decking underneath is the foundation of any new installation, and in homes that have seen several Minnesota winters, soft spots, delaminated plywood, or deteriorated skip sheathing are common findings that need to be addressed before new materials go down.

Once the scope is clear, material selection is matched to the structure. For most Swanville residential roofs, high-wind-rated architectural shingles with a Class 4 impact rating offer the best balance of longevity and insurance premium benefit. Ice and water shield is installed along all eaves and in valleys, not just at the minimum code-required width, because the conditions here demand it. Ridge ventilation is evaluated and upgraded where needed so the new roof breathes correctly from day one.

Tear-off, decking repair, underlayment, flashing, and final installation are sequenced to keep the structure weather-tight at every stage. We do not leave bare decking exposed overnight when rain or snow is in the forecast.

## Local Note

In the rural stretches of Morrison County around Swanville, a significant number of homes were built with board-and-batten or tongue-and-groove roof decking rather than plywood or OSB. That older solid-board sheathing is actually quite durable when it is dry, but when it has absorbed moisture over years of ice dam cycles it can be deceptively solid-looking on the surface while being punky underneath. Probing the decking during tear-off rather than relying on a visual inspection alone is standard practice on these jobs, because the cost of replacing a few compromised boards during installation is a fraction of what it costs to address a decking failure after a new roof is already down.
