Foley sits in the heart of Benton County, where freeze-thaw cycles hit hard and the region's clay-heavy soils slow drainage after heavy rain or snowmelt. When water finds its way into a Foley home, through a burst pipe during a January cold snap, a sump pump that couldn't keep up with spring runoff, or a washing machine supply line that let go overnight, it moves fast and hides in places that look dry at the surface. Heritage Restoration LLC responds to water damage calls in Foley and the surrounding Benton County area, handling everything from initial water extraction through structural drying and documentation for your insurance claim.

## Why Foley Properties See Water Damage Issues

Benton County's soil composition is a real factor here. Clay-dominant soil doesn't absorb and release water the way sandy or loamy ground does, during spring thaw or a heavy summer storm, water tables rise quickly and lateral pressure against basement walls increases. Foley's housing stock includes a meaningful share of homes built in the mid-twentieth century, when basement waterproofing standards were minimal and drain tile systems were either absent or have since aged past their useful life. That combination, saturated ground pressing against older block or poured-concrete foundations, is one of the most common pathways for water intrusion in this area.

Winter is its own category of risk. Pipes running through exterior walls or unheated crawl spaces are vulnerable once temperatures drop below freezing for several consecutive days, which is routine in central Minnesota. A pipe that freezes and then thaws can release a significant volume of water in a short time, and in older construction the water often travels behind plaster or into wall cavities before it reaches the floor where someone notices it.

## Our Water Damage Restoration Process in Foley

The first step on any water loss is a thorough moisture assessment, not just of the visible wet area, but of the surrounding materials. Moisture meters and thermal imaging reveal where water has migrated beyond the obvious damage zone. In homes with plaster walls, which are common in older Foley construction, the surface can read close to dry while the lath behind it holds significant moisture. Missing that means the drying plan is wrong from the start.

Once the scope is mapped, standing water is extracted using truck-mounted or portable extraction equipment, depending on access. Affected materials are evaluated: what can be dried in place, what needs to be removed to allow airflow behind it, and what is already beyond saving. Structural drying follows using commercial-grade air movers and dehumidifiers calibrated to the volume and material types in the space. Our team holds IICRC certifications in Water Restoration (WRT) and Applied Structural Drying (ASD), so the drying plan follows the IICRC S500 standard rather than guesswork. Moisture readings are logged daily until all affected assemblies reach acceptable levels.

For homes built before 1978, and there are many in Foley, any work that disturbs painted surfaces requires care around lead-containing materials. Heritage Restoration LLC is EPA Lead-Safe Certified, which matters when water damage forces the removal of older trim, plaster, or painted drywall.

## Foley Insurance Coordination

Most standard homeowner policies cover sudden and accidental water losses, a burst pipe, an appliance failure, an ice dam that forces water under shingles. What they typically don't cover is gradual seepage or long-term neglect, which is why documentation at the time of loss matters. Heritage Restoration LLC photographs affected areas systematically, records moisture readings, and produces the scope of work in a format that insurance adjusters recognize. We work with major carriers and can communicate directly with your adjuster to keep the claim moving.

## Local Note

One thing worth knowing about older homes in the Foley area: many were built with full-depth basements that have floor drains connected to the municipal sewer system rather than to a dedicated sump pit. During high-flow events, those floor drains can back up rather than drain, meaning water from a sewer surcharge enters the basement through the very opening that's supposed to remove it. That's a different category of loss than a clean-water pipe break, and it changes both the extraction approach and how the claim is typically classified by your insurer. If you're not sure what type of water event you're dealing with, describe what you saw when you called, it helps us bring the right equipment and set the right expectations before we arrive.
