When a heating system pushes too hard on a January night, or a kitchen fire moves into wall cavities lined with decades-old insulation, the damage spreads faster and deeper than it looks from the doorway. ## Why Foley Homes Are Particularly Vulnerable to Fire Damage

Much of Foley's housing was built during the mid-twentieth century, when balloon-frame construction was standard. In that framing style, wall cavities run continuously from the basement to the attic without fire blocking between floors. A fire that starts in a lower-level utility room can travel vertically through those open cavities and reach the attic long before flames are visible on the upper floor. By the time firefighters arrive and suppress the fire, smoke and heat have often traveled well beyond the room of origin.

Benton County's climate adds another layer of complexity. Homes here are tightly weatherized against Minnesota winters, which means smoke odor compounds get trapped in insulation, ductwork, and soft furnishings rather than dissipating. That sealed-up construction that keeps heating bills manageable in February is the same reason smoke smell can linger for months if restoration isn't handled correctly from the start.

## Our Fire Damage Restoration Process in Foley

Every fire loss is different, but the sequence of work follows a disciplined order regardless of the size of the damage.

The first step is a thorough structural assessment. We identify which materials are salvageable and which have been compromised by heat, char, or smoke penetration. In older Foley homes, this often means probing wall cavities and checking attic decking for hidden char that isn't visible from finished surfaces.

Once the scope is clear, we move into controlled demolition of unsalvageable materials, followed by HEPA vacuuming and dry-sponge wiping of all smoke-residue surfaces. Soot chemistry varies by what burned: synthetic materials leave an oily, smearing residue that requires different cleaning chemistry than the dry, powdery soot from natural wood. We match the cleaning method to the residue type rather than applying a single approach to everything.

Odor neutralization comes next. Thermal fogging and hydroxyl generation are the primary tools, and in a tightly built Minnesota home, we run equipment long enough to reach the air pockets inside wall assemblies, not just the open living space. Ductwork is inspected and cleaned separately, because a forced-air system that ran during or after the fire will have distributed smoke particles throughout every room.

The final phase is reconstruction, which we handle under Minnesota contractor license #BC807677, so you work with one company from emergency response through finished repairs.
