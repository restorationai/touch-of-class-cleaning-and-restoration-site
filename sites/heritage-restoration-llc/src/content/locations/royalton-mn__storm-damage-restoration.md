## Why Royalton Properties See Repeated Storm Damage

Morrison County's position in central Minnesota puts Royalton directly in the path of storm tracks that travel northeast from the Dakotas. The area sees a full range of severe weather: straight-line winds that exceed tornado-level force without rotating, hail events that strip granules from asphalt shingles in minutes, and heavy wet snow in late spring or early fall that snaps tree limbs onto roofs that were never designed to bear that kind of point load.

The housing stock here matters, too. Royalton has a mix of older wood-frame homes and newer construction, and the older builds often have roof decking that has cycled through decades of freeze-thaw. When a storm peels back a section of roofing, that aged decking can absorb standing water in hours, long before a standard roofing contractor would schedule an estimate. Wind-driven rain can reach wall cavities through gaps that look minor from the outside but channel water down to the subfloor.

## Our Storm Damage Restoration Process in Royalton

The first priority after any storm event is stopping ongoing exposure. That means emergency tarping or board-up before any documentation or drying work begins, an open roof in a Minnesota summer storm can take on several inches of rain in an afternoon.

Once the structure is stabilized, the process moves to a full moisture survey. We use thermal imaging and calibrated moisture meters to map water intrusion behind wall cladding, under flooring, and in attic assemblies, areas where surface readings can look acceptable while the structural framing is still saturated. Drying equipment is placed based on that data, not on a standard room count.

Tree impact damage gets a separate structural assessment. A fallen limb that punches through a roof deck can transfer load to rafters and ceiling joists in ways that aren't visible from below. We document that damage in detail because carriers and building officials in Morrison County both want to see the scope before work proceeds.

All water-affected materials are dried to IICRC S500 standard before any reconstruction begins. Closing up wet framing is one of the most common causes of post-storm mold problems in Minnesota homes, and it's avoidable.

## Insurance Documentation for Royalton Storm Claims

Storm claims in Minnesota are handled differently than water or fire claims in one important way: the cause of loss is almost always visible and datable, which works in the homeowner's favor. We photograph and document damage before any emergency work begins, and we retain that documentation through the life of the claim.

We work directly with most major carriers and can provide the scope of work, moisture readings, and photo documentation that adjusters need to process a claim. If a supplement is needed, because hidden damage is found during drying that wasn't visible at the initial inspection, we document that separately so the carrier has a clear record.

For Morrison County properties, permit requirements for storm repair work can vary depending on the scope. Structural repairs and any work that affects the building envelope typically require a permit through the county; we factor that into the project timeline.

## Local Note

One pattern worth knowing in Royalton and the surrounding Morrison County townships: older homes with full-dimension lumber framing, common in builds from the mid-20th century and earlier, hold moisture in the framing members longer than modern dimensional lumber because the wood is denser. Thermal imaging sometimes shows a structure reading dry at the surface while the heavier framing is still releasing moisture. We extend drying cycles on those properties and re-verify with moisture meters before any reconstruction, because closing up that framing prematurely is exactly how a storm claim becomes a mold claim six months later.
