Swanville sits in Morrison County where the flat, open terrain of central Minnesota gives thunderstorms almost nothing to slow them down. When a severe line of weather rolls through, the combination of high straight-line winds, hail, and saturated soils can leave roofs stripped, siding buckled, and water pushing through every compromised seam before the storm has even passed. Heritage Restoration LLC responds to that kind of damage with IICRC-certified crews trained in structural drying, smoke and fire assessment, and mold prevention so that one bad storm does not turn into a months-long repair project.

## Why Swanville Properties See Significant Storm Damage

Central Minnesota's storm season runs longer than most homeowners expect. From late April through September, Morrison County is regularly in the path of squall lines that drop hail large enough to puncture asphalt shingles and leave dents in aluminum fascia. What makes Swanville specifically vulnerable is the region's clay-heavy soil: once it is saturated after a heavy rain event, surface water has nowhere to go quickly. That means water pools against foundations, seeps through basement walls, and finds its way into crawl spaces well after the storm has passed. Homes built in the mid-twentieth century, which make up a large share of the residential stock in this part of Morrison County, often have older window flashing and roof-to-wall transitions that were not designed to handle the wind-driven rain that accompanies modern severe weather events. A storm that looks manageable from the outside can be hiding active moisture intrusion behind siding and inside wall cavities.

Tree damage is another pattern worth noting. The mature oaks and elms common throughout this part of the county are beautiful in calm weather and genuinely dangerous when winds exceed 60 mph. A downed limb or uprooted tree can breach a roof deck in seconds, and once the roof is open, interior damage accelerates fast.

## Our Storm Damage Restoration Process in Swanville

The first priority after any storm is stopping the damage from growing. That means emergency tarping of compromised roof sections, boarding broken windows, and extracting any standing water before it has time to wick into framing and insulation. Heritage Restoration uses moisture meters and thermal imaging cameras to map the full extent of water intrusion, not just the areas that look wet from the surface. Water behind plaster or inside wall cavities can read dry at the surface while the underlying structure stays saturated.

Once the immediate threat is contained, the process moves to structural drying using commercial-grade desiccant and refrigerant dehumidifiers alongside high-velocity air movers. Drying logs are documented throughout so that your insurance adjuster has a clear record of conditions, equipment deployed, and drying progress. After the structure meets drying targets, the scope shifts to repair: roofing, siding, windows, interior finishes, and any framing that was compromised by impact or prolonged moisture exposure.

Because Heritage Restoration holds EPA Lead-Safe certification, homes built before 1978 in the Swanville area can be addressed without triggering a separate contractor search when lead-containing materials are disturbed during storm repair work.

## Local Note

One thing that catches homeowners off guard in this part of Morrison County is how quickly mold colonization can begin after storm-related water intrusion. The warm, humid conditions that follow a summer thunderstorm, combined with the insulation and framing materials common in homes from the 1950s through 1970s, create conditions where mold can establish within 24 to 48 hours of a moisture event. That timeline is not unique to Swanville, but the older housing stock here means there is often less vapor barrier between the exterior and the interior framing, which accelerates how fast moisture reaches wood surfaces. Calling for an assessment the same day as the storm, rather than waiting to see whether things dry on their own, is the single most effective thing a homeowner can do to keep a storm claim from becoming a mold remediation project on top of everything else.
