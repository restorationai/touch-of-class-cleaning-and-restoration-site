## Why Royalton Homes Present Unique Renovation Challenges

Morrison County's climate is not gentle on residential structures. Royalton's position in Central Minnesota means homes cycle through significant freeze-thaw stress every year, frost can penetrate several feet into the ground, and that movement works on foundations, framing, and exterior finishes over time. Many homes in the area were built in eras when insulation standards were minimal, and older construction often used materials, knob-and-tube wiring, galvanized plumbing, original single-pane windows, that complicate modern renovation work.

Because Heritage Restoration is EPA Lead-Safe Certified, we handle pre-renovation testing and containment protocols on older homes where lead-based paint is a real possibility, not a remote one. Skipping that step in a home built before 1978 is not just a code issue, it is a health issue for the family living there during and after the work.

Royalton's rural-residential character also means many properties have private wells and septic systems. Any addition, bathroom remodel, or utility upgrade needs to account for those systems from the planning stage, not as an afterthought when the walls are already open.

## Our Renovation and General Contracting Process in Royalton

Every project starts with a thorough walkthrough, not a quick visual scan, but a systematic look at what the structure is actually doing. In Central Minnesota homes, that means checking rim joists for moisture intrusion, looking at how previous owners patched and improvised, and identifying where the building envelope is losing efficiency.

From there, we build a scope of work that is honest about sequencing. In a kitchen remodel, for example, we do not start cabinet installation before confirming the subfloor is level and dry, a common problem in homes that have had any history of appliance leaks or basement moisture. Bathroom remodels in older Royalton homes frequently reveal tile set over original linoleum over original subfloor, and each layer has to be evaluated before the new work goes in.

For post-damage rebuilds, homes that have gone through water damage, fire, or mold remediation, we coordinate directly with the restoration side of the process. Heritage Restoration holds IICRC certifications in water damage restoration, structural drying, fire and smoke restoration, and mold remediation, which means the rebuild phase is informed by people who understand what the structure went through, not just what it looks like now.

## Local Note

One pattern that comes up repeatedly in Royalton-area homes is the combination of a poured concrete or block basement with minimal exterior waterproofing from the original build era. When homeowners pursue a basement finishing project or a main-floor remodel that opens up the floor system, it is not unusual to find evidence of past moisture intrusion that was never properly addressed, just painted over or covered with flooring.
