## Why Elmdale Properties Are Particularly Vulnerable After a Fire

Much of the residential building stock in rural Morrison County predates modern fire-stop requirements. Older balloon-frame construction, common in farmhouses and in-town homes built before the 1970s, allows smoke and heat to travel vertically through wall cavities from basement to attic without interruption. That means a fire that appears contained to one room can leave soot deposits and odor throughout an entire structure. Elmdale's climate adds another layer of difficulty: temperatures that drop well below freezing in winter mean that water used to suppress a fire can refreeze inside wall assemblies and beneath flooring, masking moisture that will cause structural damage and mold growth if not extracted and dried properly.

Wood-burning stoves and older chimney systems are also more common in this part of Minnesota than in metro areas, and chimney fires or firebox failures produce a particularly dense, oily smoke that bonds to painted surfaces, insulation, and porous materials far more stubbornly than smoke from a kitchen fire.

## Our Fire and Smoke Restoration Process in Elmdale

Every fire loss is different, but the sequence matters. The first priority after the structure is cleared as safe is containment: sealing off unaffected areas so soot and odor do not migrate further during the cleanup process. From there, the work moves in a defined order.

**Assessment and documentation** come first. Every affected surface is catalogued, photographed, and tested for smoke penetration depth. This documentation is what your insurance adjuster needs to process a claim accurately.

**Structural drying** follows if suppression water is present. Using IICRC ASD methods, moisture readings are taken throughout the structure, including inside wall cavities and beneath subfloor layers, not just at the surface where materials may read dry while staying saturated underneath.

**Soot and smoke removal** is then performed using dry chemical sponges on porous surfaces, HEPA vacuuming on insulation and framing, and appropriate cleaning agents matched to the surface type. Oily smoke residue from wood or chimney fires requires different chemistry than the lighter ash from a fast-burning fire.

**Odor elimination** is the final and often most underestimated step. Thermal fogging or hydroxyl treatment reaches into the same voids smoke originally traveled through, neutralizing odor at the molecular level rather than masking it.
