## Why Sobieski Properties Are Particularly Vulnerable to Storm Damage

Morrison County's mix of agricultural land and wooded residential lots means that most homes in the Sobieski area sit among mature trees, and those trees become projectiles in a serious storm. A 70-mph wind gust does not need a tornado to send a limb through a roof deck. Once the roof is breached, the clock starts immediately: Minnesota summer humidity accelerates moisture migration into attic insulation, ceiling joists, and wall cavities within hours.

The housing stock around Sobieski leans older, with many homes built in the post-war decades using construction methods that predate modern weather-resistant sheathing and housewrap. Lap siding and older window glazing are less forgiving when wind-driven rain arrives horizontally. Crawl spaces and full basements are common in this part of Morrison County, and both can collect water quickly when gutters are overwhelmed or foundation grading is compromised by storm runoff. That combination of older envelope details and low-lying rural lots makes thorough moisture assessment essential after any significant weather event, not just the obvious structural repairs.

## Our Storm Damage Restoration Process in Sobieski

Every job starts with a full exterior and interior inspection before any debris is moved. Photographs and moisture readings taken in the first hour become the foundation of the insurance claim, and rushing past this step costs homeowners money later. After documentation, the priority is stopping active water intrusion: emergency tarping of roof breaches, boarding compromised windows or doors, and clearing debris that is redirecting water toward the structure.

Once the building envelope is stabilized, we assess what moisture has already entered. Thermal imaging helps locate water that has migrated behind plaster or into wall cavities without showing visible staining yet. Structural drying follows the IICRC S500 and S520 standards, using calibrated desiccant or refrigerant dehumidifiers and high-velocity air movers positioned to dry assemblies from the inside out. Affected materials are categorized: what can be dried in place, what needs controlled demolition to allow airflow, and what is a total loss. Throughout the process, readings are logged daily so the insurance adjuster has a clear, timestamped record of drying progress.

Heritage Restoration holds IICRC certifications in Water Restoration, Structural Drying, Fire and Smoke Restoration, and Mold Remediation, along with EPA Lead-Safe certification, which matters when storm damage involves pre-1978 construction and disturbed painted surfaces.

## Local Note

One pattern that shows up in rural Morrison County properties after major storms: older homes with full basements and block foundation walls sometimes develop lateral seepage at the mortar joints when saturated soil pressure increases after heavy rain. The storm damage is obvious above grade, but the basement moisture goes unnoticed for days because the homeowner is focused on the roof and siding. By the time a musty odor develops, mold colonization is already underway on stored materials and wood framing. A thorough post-storm inspection in this part of Minnesota should always include the basement, even when the visible damage is entirely on the upper structure.
