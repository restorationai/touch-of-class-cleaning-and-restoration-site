## Why Flensburg Homes Reward Careful Pre-Renovation Assessment

Stearns County's housing stock skews older, and homes in and around Flensburg frequently carry construction details that show up as surprises during demo: knob-and-tube wiring tucked inside walls slated for insulation upgrades, plaster-and-lath ceilings above a planned open-concept kitchen, and balloon-frame wall cavities that complicate both air sealing and any structural modification. None of these are deal-breakers, but each one changes the scope, the schedule, and the permit set.

The climate adds its own layer. Freeze-thaw cycles in this part of Minnesota are aggressive, and slab edges, foundation walls, and any exterior-adjacent framing can show frost heave damage that only becomes visible once interior finishes come down. A remodel that skips a structural look at the rim joist or band board before closing walls back up is a remodel that may need to be reopened in a few years.

## Local Note

One pattern that comes up repeatedly in Stearns County farmhouses and older rural homes is the use of true-dimension lumber, meaning the framing members are actually the full size stamped on them rather than the nominal dimensions used in modern construction. A true 2x6 is meaningfully thicker than a modern 2x6, which affects how new windows and doors are framed into existing openings, how insulation batts fit, and how any new framing ties into old. It is a small detail that a contractor unfamiliar with the regional housing stock can get wrong in ways that show up as air leaks, finish gaps, or failed inspections. We account for it at the measuring stage, not after materials are cut.
