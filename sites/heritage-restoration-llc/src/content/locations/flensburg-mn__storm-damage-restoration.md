## Why Flensburg Properties See Storm Damage Differently

Flensburg is a small, tight-knit community where a significant portion of the housing stock reflects construction from the mid-twentieth century or earlier. Homes built in that era often feature wood-framed roofs with board sheathing rather than modern OSB panels, and that older sheathing can delaminate or split when hail impacts accumulate across a single storm event. Outbuildings, pole barns, and detached garages are common on the larger lots typical of this part of Stearns County, and those structures frequently take the brunt of wind damage because they lack the same structural bracing as the main residence.

The region's clay-heavy soils also matter after a storm. When heavy rain follows wind damage, water that would normally drain away instead pools against foundations and crawl space vents because the ground is already saturated from spring snowmelt or a prior rain event. That pooling accelerates moisture intrusion into rim joists and subfloors, turning what looks like a roofing claim into a structural drying job as well.

## Our Storm Damage Restoration Process in Flensburg

The first priority after any severe weather event is stopping additional water from entering the structure. That means emergency tarping of breached roof sections, boarding compromised windows, and clearing debris that is blocking drainage paths around the foundation. Once the envelope is secured, the assessment phase begins: moisture mapping with thermal imaging and calibrated meters to find water that has already migrated behind walls or under flooring before it becomes visible.

From there, the work follows the IICRC S500 and S520 standards that govern structural drying and mold prevention. Drying equipment is placed based on the actual moisture readings, not a square-footage estimate, and readings are logged daily so the drying curve is documented for the insurance file. After materials reach target moisture levels, damaged structural components, roofing, siding, or interior finishes are repaired or replaced. Heritage Restoration holds IICRC certifications in Water Damage Restoration, Structural Drying, and Applied Microbial Remediation, which matters when a storm loss crosses into mold territory because of a delayed discovery.

One pattern that shows up repeatedly in rural central Minnesota properties is that attic insulation, particularly the blown-in cellulose common in homes updated during the 1970s and 1980s energy-efficiency push, absorbs and holds storm-driven moisture long after the roof breach is repaired.
