## Why Foley Properties See Water Damage Issues

Central Minnesota's climate is hard on homes in ways that aren't always obvious until something fails. Foley experiences some of the most dramatic temperature swings in the state, subzero stretches in January followed by rapid warm-ups in March and April. That pattern does two things to residential properties: it stresses supply lines and exterior hose bibs that weren't fully drained before winter, and it accelerates snowmelt faster than the ground can absorb it, sending water toward foundations and window wells.

Benton County's soil composition, a mix of glacial till and clay-heavy subsoil in many areas, doesn't drain quickly. When saturated, that soil holds hydrostatic pressure against basement walls and floor slabs for days after a rain or melt event. Homes built on slab foundations or with older poured-concrete basements are especially vulnerable to seepage along cold joints and floor cracks. Foley's housing stock includes a meaningful number of homes built in the mid-twentieth century, many of which have original plumbing that has reached or exceeded its expected service life.

## Our Water Cleanup Process in Foley

Every water cleanup job starts with moisture mapping, not a visual scan, but calibrated readings using thermal imaging cameras and penetrating moisture meters to find water that has migrated behind drywall, under flooring, or into subfloor assemblies. Water follows the path of least resistance, and in older Foley homes with plaster-and-lath interior walls, that path can carry moisture surprisingly far from the visible wet spot.

Once we know where the water is, extraction comes first. Standing water is removed with truck-mounted or portable extraction units before any drying equipment is placed. After extraction, we set a calculated drying system, commercial-grade desiccant or refrigerant dehumidifiers paired with high-velocity air movers, sized to the affected area and adjusted based on daily moisture readings. We document psychrometric data throughout the drying process, which matters when your insurance carrier asks for proof that the structure reached dry standard before repairs began.

Materials that cannot be dried in place, saturated insulation, compromised drywall, buckled flooring, are removed and documented before disposal. As an EPA Lead-Safe Certified firm, we follow required protocols when disturbing painted surfaces in homes built before 1978, which is relevant for a number of Foley's older residential properties.

## Foley Insurance Coordination

Most homeowner policies cover sudden and accidental water losses, a burst pipe, a failed water heater, an appliance supply line that let go. Gradual leaks and flood events from rising surface water are typically handled differently, and it's worth knowing which category your loss falls into before you call your carrier. Heritage Restoration documents the loss thoroughly: photos of affected materials, moisture readings at each measurement point, and a written scope of work that aligns with how insurance adjusters evaluate claims. We work with most major carriers and can communicate directly with your adjuster to keep the claim moving.

## Local Note

In Benton County homes with full basements, common throughout the Foley area, sump pump failures during spring melt are one of the most frequent sources of water damage we see in this region. What catches homeowners off guard is that the pump itself often works fine; the problem is a float switch stuck in the off position or a discharge line that froze at the exterior wall and backed water up into the crock. If you're dealing with a flooded basement after a melt event, check whether the discharge line is clear before assuming the pump has failed, but call for extraction either way, because water sitting on a concrete floor wicks into wall framing and insulation faster than most people expect.
