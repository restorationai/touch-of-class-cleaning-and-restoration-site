## Why Harding Properties See Roofing Problems

Morrison County sits in a climate band that delivers some of the state's heaviest snow loads alongside sharp temperature swings. A roof that handles a single hard winter reasonably well can degrade quickly once the underlayment or decking takes on moisture. Older homes in the Harding area, many built during the mid-twentieth century agricultural expansion of the region, often have lower-slope designs that were practical for the era but are less forgiving when ice dams form along the eaves. Once water backs up behind an ice dam and finds a seam, it moves into the attic before a homeowner notices anything inside the living space. Granule-stripped asphalt shingles lose their UV and moisture resistance, accelerating the cycle. In a climate where a roof may face both 90-degree July heat and minus-20-degree January wind chill, the margin for a compromised system is narrow.

## Our Roofing Installation and Replacement Process in Harding

Every project starts with a thorough inspection of the existing deck, not just the visible shingle layer. Decking that has taken on moisture from repeated ice dam intrusion can look structurally sound until it is walked on. We probe suspect areas, check ridge and soffit ventilation (a common weakness in older central Minnesota homes), and document everything before materials are ordered.

For full replacements, we strip to the deck, replace any compromised sheathing, and install a high-quality ice-and-water shield along all eaves and valleys before synthetic underlayment goes down across the field. In Morrison County's climate, that eave protection is not optional. We then install the shingle system of your choice, flash all penetrations and transitions properly, and ensure ridge ventilation is balanced with soffit intake so the attic does not trap the heat that drives ice dam formation in the first place.

Material selection is calibrated to local conditions. Architectural shingles rated for high wind and carrying a class-4 impact resistance designation hold up better against the hail events that move through central Minnesota in late spring and early summer. We walk through options with you before anything is ordered.

## Local Note

One thing that comes up on older Harding-area homes is the combination of a low-slope main roof with a steeper addition built decades later. The transition between the two roof planes is often where flashing was done minimally the first time and where ice dam water finds its easiest path inside. When we see that configuration, we treat the transition valley as a priority, sometimes adding a secondary ice-and-water layer that extends further up the slope than standard practice would require. It is a small material cost relative to the damage a slow leak at that junction can cause over a single winter.
