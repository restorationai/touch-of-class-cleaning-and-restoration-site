## Why Albany Properties See Water Damage Issues

Stearns County's clay-heavy soils drain slowly, which means groundwater pressure against basement walls builds steadily during spring thaw and after sustained rainfall. Homes built in the mid-twentieth century, which make up a significant share of Albany's residential stock, often have poured concrete or block foundations without modern waterproofing membranes. That combination creates conditions where water doesn't just seep in at one crack, it migrates along the full perimeter of the footing.

Albany also sits in a region where temperatures swing hard between seasons. Pipes routed through exterior walls or unheated crawl spaces are vulnerable to freezing in January and February, and a burst pipe inside a wall cavity can discharge dozens of gallons before the pressure drop is even noticed. The freeze-thaw cycle also stresses mortar joints in older block foundations, opening new pathways for water intrusion each spring.

## Our Emergency Water Cleanup Process in Albany

The first step on any water loss is containment and assessment. Technicians use moisture meters and thermal imaging to map how far water has traveled, including into wall cavities and under flooring where it isn't visible at the surface. Standing water is extracted using truck-mounted and portable extraction units before any drying equipment is placed.

Once extraction is complete, the drying phase begins. Industrial air movers and refrigerant or desiccant dehumidifiers are positioned according to a drying plan calibrated to the structure's layout and the materials involved. Readings are logged at each visit so the drying curve is documented, not estimated. Affected materials that cannot be dried in place, such as saturated insulation or buckled subfloor panels, are removed and disposed of properly before the space is closed.

Because Heritage Restoration holds IICRC certifications in Water Restoration Technician and Structural Drying, the process follows the IICRC S500 standard throughout, which matters when your insurance carrier reviews the claim documentation.

## Local Note

One thing worth knowing about Albany-area homes built between the 1950s and 1970s: many have original tongue-and-groove wood subfloor panels rather than the plywood or OSB used in newer construction. Tongue-and-groove subfloor absorbs water more evenly across the panel surface, which means it can look and feel only slightly soft while holding significant moisture in the wood fibers. Standard surface moisture readings can underestimate the actual saturation level. Technicians working in these homes use pin-type meters that measure deeper into the wood rather than relying on surface readings alone, which changes the drying timeline and the equipment layout.
