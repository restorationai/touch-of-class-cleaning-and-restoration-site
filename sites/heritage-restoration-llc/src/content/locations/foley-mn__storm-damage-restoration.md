When a storm tears through and leaves your property open to the sky, the clock starts immediately: water follows every gap, insulation loses its value the moment it gets wet, and wood framing that stays damp for more than a day or two begins a process that doesn't stop on its own.

## Why Foley Properties Are Vulnerable to Storm Damage

Benton County's mix of older farmstead-style homes and mid-century residential construction means a significant share of Foley's housing stock was built before modern wind-load standards became routine. Roofs framed with dimensional lumber rather than engineered trusses can rack under sustained lateral wind pressure, and the wide roof overhangs common on ranch-style homes from the 1960s and 1970s act as sails during straight-line wind events. Soffit and fascia failures on these homes often go unnoticed until water has already tracked behind the exterior sheathing.

The region's clay-heavy soils compound the problem. After a heavy storm, saturated ground can't absorb additional runoff quickly, which pushes water toward foundations and into crawl spaces. Homes with block or poured-concrete basements, common throughout this part of central Minnesota, can take on water through wall cracks that weren't an issue before the storm changed the grade drainage around the foundation.

## Our Storm Damage Restoration Process in Foley

The first priority after any storm loss is stopping the exposure. That means emergency tarping of compromised roof sections, boarding windows or doors that are broken or missing, and identifying every water-entry point before focusing on what's already wet inside. Skipping that step and jumping straight to interior drying is one of the most common mistakes on storm jobs, you can run equipment for days and still lose the race if the building envelope isn't sealed.

Once the structure is protected, we move to a systematic damage assessment: moisture mapping with calibrated meters, thermal imaging to locate water that has migrated behind walls or under flooring, and documentation of structural damage for the insurance claim. From there, drying equipment, commercial dehumidifiers, air movers, and desiccant units when ambient humidity is high, is positioned based on the moisture map rather than guesswork. Structural drying follows the IICRC S500 standard, which sets the psychrometric targets that tell us when materials are genuinely dry rather than just surface-dry.

Debris removal, tree-limb extraction from roofs and structures, and coordination with roofing and siding subcontractors follow once the interior is stable.

## Foley Insurance Coordination

Most homeowner policies in Minnesota cover sudden storm damage to the structure, but the documentation burden falls on the policyholder to prove the loss was storm-related and not pre-existing deterioration.
