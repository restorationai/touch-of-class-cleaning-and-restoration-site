## Why Foley Properties See Roofing Wear Earlier Than Expected

Benton County's climate is the primary driver. Foley averages enough annual snowfall to push significant snow loads onto residential roofs, and the temperature swings between January lows and early spring thaws create repeated expansion-and-contraction stress on shingles, underlayment, and the decking beneath. When that cycle runs for several consecutive winters, granule loss accelerates, seams open, and water finds its way under the surface layer before the homeowner notices anything from the ground.

The housing stock in Foley skews toward single-family homes built in the mid-to-late twentieth century, many with standard 4:12 to 6:12 pitch roofs that are functional but not steep enough to shed heavy snow quickly. Flat or low-slope sections above attached garages and rear additions are especially vulnerable to ice damming, where meltwater backs up behind a frozen edge and forces its way under shingles. If the attic insulation and ventilation are not balanced correctly, the problem compounds year after year.

## Our Roofing Installation and Replacement Process in Foley

Every project starts with a thorough inspection of the existing roof system, not just the shingle surface but the decking, ridge ventilation, soffit intake, and flashing at every penetration and valley. In Foley's climate, decking condition matters enormously. Repeated moisture intrusion from ice dams often leaves OSB or plywood decking soft in localized areas, and replacing shingles over compromised decking is a short-term fix that fails quickly.

Once the scope is clear, the process moves through these stages:

- **Tear-off and decking inspection**: Full removal of existing layers, followed by a board-by-board assessment. Any soft, delaminated, or water-stained sections are replaced before new material goes down.
- **Underlayment and ice-and-water shield**: In Minnesota, code requires ice-and-water shield at the eaves and in valleys. For Foley homes with low-slope sections or known ice-dam history, we extend the shield coverage beyond the minimum to provide a genuine buffer.
- **Shingle installation**: We work with architectural shingles rated for the wind and temperature ranges common to central Minnesota, selecting products with appropriate Class 4 impact ratings where hail exposure is a consideration.
- **Flashing and ventilation**: Chimney, pipe boot, and step flashing are replaced or resealed as part of every full replacement. Ridge vent and soffit balance are verified before the job closes out.
- **Cleanup and final walkthrough**: Magnetic sweeps for fasteners, haul-away of all debris, and a documented walkthrough with the homeowner before we leave the site.

## Local Note

One pattern that comes up specifically in Foley and the surrounding Benton County townships: homes with original cedar shake or older three-tab shingles sometimes have a second layer of shingles installed over the first, which was a common cost-saving approach in past decades. Minnesota's residential building code limits the number of roofing layers before a full tear-off is required, and many Foley homes are already at that limit. If your home had a re-roof done without a tear-off, a new installation will require stripping both layers, which affects both the project timeline and the total cost.
