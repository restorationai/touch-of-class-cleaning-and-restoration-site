Central Minnesota's storm season hits Morrison County hard, and Upsala sits squarely in the path of the severe weather systems that roll across the open farmland from the west. When a fast-moving line of thunderstorms drops hail the size of marbles, tears shingles off older wood-framed homes, or sends a mature oak through a garage roof, the damage compounds quickly. Standing water finds every gap in a compromised structure, and what starts as a roof puncture can become a mold problem within days if the interior isn't dried out properly.

## Why Upsala Properties See Storm Damage Differently Than Larger Towns

Upsala is a small community surrounded by agricultural land, and that setting creates specific storm vulnerability. The flat, open terrain west of town gives thunderstorm winds almost nothing to slow them down before they reach residential properties. Older homes in the area, many built with dimensional lumber framing and wood-sheathed roofs, can sustain structural damage that modern engineered lumber handles differently. When a storm peels back roofing material, the underlying sheathing and insulation absorb moisture fast, especially in the humid aftermath of a midsummer storm system.

The region also sees significant hail exposure. Morrison County sits within a corridor where late-spring and early-summer convective storms regularly produce hail events large enough to crack asphalt shingles, dent metal flashing, and break skylights. That kind of surface damage is easy to underestimate from the ground, but water entry through hairline cracks in shingles can saturate wall cavities before a homeowner notices any interior staining.

## Our Storm Damage Restoration Process in Upsala

When we arrive at a property, the first priority is stopping ongoing water intrusion. That means tarping or boarding any breaches in the roof or exterior before we assess what's already wet inside. We use moisture meters and thermal imaging to map where water has traveled behind walls and under flooring, because surface readings alone miss the pockets of saturation that drive mold growth.

Once the building envelope is secured, we extract standing water, set industrial air movers and dehumidifiers calibrated to the structure's size and the ambient humidity conditions, and monitor drying progress daily. Structural drying follows the IICRC S500 and S520 standards, which means we're not pulling equipment based on how things look or feel but on what the instruments actually read. Damaged materials that can't be dried in place, such as saturated insulation or compromised OSB sheathing, are removed and documented for your insurance claim before disposal.

For tree impact damage, we coordinate debris removal and temporary structural support where needed, then assess whether load-bearing elements have been compromised before any finish work begins.
