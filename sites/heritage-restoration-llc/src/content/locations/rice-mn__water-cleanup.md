## Why Rice Properties See Water Damage Differently Than Urban Markets

Morrison County's freeze-thaw cycle is one of the more punishing in central Minnesota. Temperatures routinely drop well below zero from December through February, and the ground stays frozen long enough that when snowmelt arrives in March and April, it has nowhere to go quickly. Homes in Rice that sit on low-lying lots near the Mississippi floodplain are especially vulnerable to that seasonal surge. Crawl spaces and basements that stayed dry all winter can take on water in a matter of days once the frost line breaks.

The housing stock in Rice also matters. Many homes in the area were built in the mid-twentieth century with concrete block foundations rather than poured concrete. Block foundations are more porous by nature, and once water finds a mortar joint, it wicks inward steadily. That kind of slow intrusion is easy to miss until flooring adhesive starts to bubble or a musty smell develops in a finished basement room. By then, moisture has usually been present long enough to begin affecting wall framing and insulation.

## Our Emergency Water Cleanup Process in Rice

The first step on any water loss is containment and documentation. We photograph affected areas before anything is moved, which creates the record your insurance adjuster needs. Then we extract standing water using truck-mounted and portable extraction units, reaching water trapped under flooring and in wall cavities that a shop vac cannot touch.

After extraction, we place commercial-grade desiccant and refrigerant dehumidifiers alongside high-velocity air movers calibrated to the room volume. Drying is monitored with moisture meters and thermal imaging cameras at each visit so we can confirm that readings are trending toward the IICRC S500 standard rather than guessing by feel. In Rice homes with older block foundations, we pay particular attention to the base of walls where block meets slab, because that junction holds moisture longer than the wall surface above it suggests.

If building materials cannot be dried in place, we document them, remove them carefully, and store the records so your adjuster can review scope before any reconstruction begins.
