## Why Long Prairie Properties Face Accelerated Roofing Wear

Central Minnesota's climate creates a specific set of problems for roofing that milder regions simply don't see. In Long Prairie, the combination of significant annual snowfall, prolonged sub-zero temperatures, and the rapid melt cycles that come with spring means roofing materials are constantly expanding and contracting. Asphalt shingles become brittle in extreme cold and can crack or lift at the seams. Flashing around chimneys, skylights, and pipe boots is especially vulnerable, water infiltrates those joints, freezes, and widens the gap a little more each season.

Ice dams are a particular concern for homes with lower-slope roof sections or inadequate attic insulation. When heat escapes through the roof deck, snow melts and refreezes at the eave line, backing water up under shingles where it has no business being. By the time that moisture shows up as a ceiling stain inside, the decking beneath may already be soft.

Long Prairie's housing stock includes a meaningful share of older homes built during the mid-20th century agricultural boom in Todd County. Many of those roofs have seen one or two previous layers of shingles added over the original, which adds weight and masks underlying deck damage. A full tear-off and inspection of the sheathing is often the only way to know what you're actually working with.

## Our Roofing Installation and Replacement Process in Long Prairie

Every project starts with a thorough inspection of the existing roof system, not just the shingles but the decking, ventilation, flashing, and soffit and fascia condition. In Long Prairie's climate, proper attic ventilation isn't optional, it's what prevents the ice dam cycle from repeating on a brand-new roof.

Once the scope is clear, we walk you through material options suited to central Minnesota conditions. Architectural shingles rated for high-wind and impact resistance perform significantly better here than standard three-tab products. For homes where ice damming has been a recurring issue, we discuss ice-and-water shield underlayment coverage extending well past the eave line, which Minnesota's residential code recommends for this climate zone.

Tear-off, deck inspection and repair, underlayment, drip edge, and shingle installation follow a sequenced process. Flashing is replaced, not reused, because reusing old flashing on a new roof is a shortcut that costs more in the long run. Before we leave the site, we do a ground-level and close-up inspection and run a magnet through the yard for fasteners.

One thing that catches homeowners off guard in Long Prairie and the surrounding Todd County area: roofs on homes with unheated attached garages or enclosed porches often develop ice dam problems specifically at the transition point between the heated and unheated spaces.
