## Why Sobieski Properties See Roofing Deterioration Faster Than You Might Expect

Morrison County's climate is not gentle on roofing materials. The region averages well over 50 inches of snow in a heavy winter, and the temperature swings between January lows and spring thaws create ice-dam conditions on any roof with marginal attic ventilation. When ice dams form along the eaves, meltwater backs up under the first course of shingles and works toward the decking. Over several seasons, that moisture cycles through the wood, and what starts as staining can become rot in the sheathing.

Sobieski's housing stock includes a significant share of older homes built with lower-slope roof sections, sometimes over additions or attached garages, where standard three-tab shingles were installed decades ago. Those low-slope areas are particularly vulnerable because water drains slowly and ice has more time to accumulate. Replacing those sections with a product rated for the actual pitch, and pairing it with proper ice-and-water shield underlayment extended well past the eave line, is not an upgrade, it is a code-appropriate repair for this climate.

## Our Roofing Installation and Replacement Process in Sobieski

Every project starts with a full deck inspection before a single bundle of shingles is ordered. We remove the existing material down to the sheathing, assess every sheet for soft spots, delamination, or prior water intrusion, and replace damaged sections before anything goes back on top. Covering compromised decking with new shingles is one of the most common shortcuts in residential roofing, and it guarantees a callback within a few years.

For central Minnesota installations, we use a self-adhering ice-and-water barrier across the entire eave overhang and in all valleys, not just the minimum required at the drip edge. Synthetic underlayment goes over the remaining field. Starter strips are fastened at the correct exposure for the wind zone, and ridge ventilation is matched to the soffit intake so the attic can actually breathe year-round. Proper attic airflow is one of the most overlooked factors in roof longevity in this region, without it, heat buildup in summer degrades shingles from the underside, and moisture in winter has nowhere to escape.

We work with asphalt architectural shingles rated for high-wind and impact resistance, which are appropriate for Morrison County's storm exposure. If you have a metal roofing system or are considering one, we can discuss that option as well during the estimate.

## Local Note

One pattern worth knowing in this part of Morrison County: older homes with steep gable roofs sometimes have original wood-plank sheathing rather than plywood or OSB. Plank sheathing is not a problem in itself, but it does affect how the new roofing system gets fastened and whether additional blocking is needed at the eaves. If your home was built before the mid-1970s, there is a reasonable chance the deck is plank rather than sheet goods, and a contractor who does not check before ordering materials may run into surprises mid-job.
