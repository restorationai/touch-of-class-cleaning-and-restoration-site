## Why St. Joseph Properties See Water Damage Issues

The housing stock in St. Joseph ranges from newer construction near the College of Saint Benedict to older single-family homes closer to the original downtown corridor, and each presents a different water damage profile. Newer builds often rely on sump systems that can fail during a prolonged power outage, while older homes may have original cast-iron or galvanized supply lines that corrode from the inside out over decades. You rarely see the failure coming until water is already behind the drywall.

Stearns County's clay-heavy soils compound the problem. Clay does not drain quickly, so saturated ground during snowmelt or a heavy rain event pushes hydrostatic pressure against foundation walls and floor slabs. Homes with block foundations are especially vulnerable to seepage at the mortar joints, and that moisture often goes undetected until a musty odor or visible staining signals that water has been migrating for weeks.

## Our Water Damage Restoration Process in St. Joseph

Every job starts with moisture mapping. Before any equipment goes in, technicians use thermal imaging cameras and calibrated moisture meters to trace exactly where water has traveled, including inside wall assemblies and under flooring where surface readings can be misleading. Standing water is extracted first using truck-mounted extraction units, then the focus shifts to the materials that absorbed it.

Structural drying in a Minnesota climate requires attention to outdoor conditions. When outdoor temperatures are low, simply opening windows is not a viable drying strategy. Instead, refrigerant or desiccant dehumidifiers run continuously alongside high-velocity air movers, and readings are logged daily against the IICRC S500 standard to confirm that materials are drying at a rate that prevents secondary mold colonization. Mold can begin colonizing porous materials within 24 to 48 hours of a moisture event, which is why the drying timeline is not negotiable.

For homes with older plaster walls, which appear in several of St. Joseph's established residential blocks, the drying process requires a longer window. Plaster holds moisture at a different rate than paper-faced drywall, and surface readings can show acceptable numbers while the underlying lath remains saturated. Technicians account for this by placing monitoring points at multiple depths and extending the drying cycle accordingly rather than closing out a job prematurely.
