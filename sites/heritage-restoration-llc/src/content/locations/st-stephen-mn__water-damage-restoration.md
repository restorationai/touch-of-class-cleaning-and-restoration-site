## Why St. Stephen Properties Are Vulnerable to Water Damage

Central Minnesota winters are not gentle. Temperatures in Stearns County regularly swing from well below zero to above freezing within a single week in late winter, and that cycling is exactly what causes pipes in exterior walls and unheated utility spaces to crack. Older homes in the St. Stephen area, many built with minimal wall insulation by today's standards, are especially susceptible. When those pipes fail, water travels fast along wall cavities and subfloor assemblies before it ever becomes visible at the surface.

Spring is its own challenge. Snowpack across the Stearns County landscape can be substantial, and when it melts quickly, sump pumps that sat idle all winter get overwhelmed. Sump basins that weren't serviced before the season, or homes where the discharge line froze during a cold snap, are common sources of basement flooding calls every March and April. The slow-draining clay soil around St. Stephen compounds the problem: groundwater pressure builds against foundation walls rather than dissipating into the ground.

## Our Water Damage Restoration Process in St. Stephen

Every water damage job starts with moisture mapping, not assumptions. Technicians use thermal imaging cameras and calibrated moisture meters to trace exactly where water has traveled, including inside wall cavities and beneath flooring where surface readings can be misleading. A floor that feels dry to the touch can still have saturated subfloor sheathing underneath.

Once the scope is documented, water extraction comes first. Standing water is removed with truck-mounted and portable extraction units before any drying equipment is placed. After extraction, the drying phase begins using commercial-grade desiccant or refrigerant dehumidifiers and high-velocity air movers positioned to create the airflow patterns the IICRC S500 standard calls for. Moisture readings are logged daily so the drying curve is tracked, not guessed. When materials reach target moisture content, the equipment comes out and the documentation is packaged for your insurance carrier.

For homes with older construction, the process often requires more patience. Plaster and lath, which appear in a number of Stearns County homes built before the 1960s, releases moisture more slowly than modern drywall. Rushing the drying phase in those homes leads to mold colonization behind intact surfaces weeks later.
