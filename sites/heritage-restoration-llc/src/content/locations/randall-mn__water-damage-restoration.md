## Why Randall Properties See Water Damage Issues

Morrison County's climate is not gentle on structures. Winters regularly push well below zero, which means supply lines running through exterior walls or uninsulated crawl spaces are genuine freeze-and-burst risks every season. When a pipe lets go in January or February, the water often travels farther than homeowners expect before anyone notices, soaking wall cavities, subfloor assemblies, and insulation that looks fine from the outside.

Spring is the other high-risk window. The Minnesota River basin and its tributaries influence drainage patterns across this part of the state, and flat or low-lying lots in the Randall area can hold groundwater close to the foundation long after the snow is gone. A basement that stayed dry all winter may take on water in April simply because the water table is temporarily elevated and a small crack in the block wall becomes a slow seep. Left unaddressed for more than 24 to 48 hours, that kind of moisture creates the conditions mold needs to establish itself.

Older homes in and around Randall also tend to have plumbing that has been repaired and re-routed over decades, sometimes with dissimilar materials joined together. Those connections are often the first to fail under temperature stress.

## Our Water Damage Restoration Process in Randall

The first priority on any water damage call is stopping ongoing moisture movement. Technicians identify the source, confirm it is controlled, and then use moisture meters and thermal imaging to map how far water has migrated through walls, floors, and ceilings. What reads dry at the surface is not always dry an inch deeper, and skipping that mapping step is how hidden mold problems develop weeks later.

Once the scope is documented, standing water is removed with truck-mounted or portable extraction equipment. Structural drying follows using commercial-grade air movers and refrigerant or desiccant dehumidifiers sized to the affected area. Drying is monitored against the IICRC S500 standard, with daily moisture readings logged until materials reach acceptable equilibrium levels. In Randall homes with older wood-framed floors or plaster-and-lath wall systems, that drying phase takes longer than it would in newer construction because dense or layered materials release moisture more slowly.

Materials that cannot be dried in place, such as wet insulation or saturated carpet padding, are removed and documented before disposal. That documentation matters for your insurance claim.
