## Why Avon Properties See Water Damage Issues

Much of the residential construction around Avon and the broader Stearns County corridor was built to handle cold winters, but that same construction style creates specific vulnerabilities. Homes with block-wall basements are common in this region, and block walls wick moisture differently than poured concrete. A slow seepage event can saturate the interior face of a block wall while the surface still feels dry to the touch. By the time standing water appears on the floor, the wall cavity may already be holding significant moisture.

The area also sits close to several lakes and low-lying agricultural land, which affects drainage patterns during heavy rain events. When storm runoff overwhelms local drainage infrastructure, water can back up through floor drains or window wells in homes that have never flooded before. That kind of intrusion carries contaminants from soil and outdoor runoff, which changes how the cleanup has to be handled compared to a clean water pipe break.

## Our Emergency Water Cleanup Process in Avon

When we arrive at a property in Avon, the first step is always to identify and stop the source if it is still active, whether that is a broken supply line, a failed sump, or ongoing infiltration. From there, the process follows a structured sequence:

**Moisture mapping:** We use thermal imaging and calibrated moisture meters to find water that has migrated behind walls, under flooring, and into subfloor assemblies. Water behind plaster or older paneling can read dry at the surface while the substrate stays saturated.

**Standing water removal:** Truck-mounted and portable extraction units pull standing water from the affected area. Carpet, pad, and saturated materials that cannot be effectively dried in place are documented and removed.

**Structural drying:** Commercial-grade desiccant and refrigerant dehumidifiers, paired with high-velocity air movers, are positioned according to the IICRC S500 standard for water damage restoration. Daily moisture readings track drying progress and guide equipment adjustments.

**Antimicrobial treatment:** Once materials are dry, affected surfaces are treated to reduce microbial growth risk, particularly important given the extended damp conditions that central Minnesota winters and springs create.

## Local Note

Homes in the Avon area that were built in the 1970s and 1980s often have fiberglass batt insulation in basement rim joist cavities. When water intrudes along the foundation, that insulation absorbs moisture and holds it against the wood framing for weeks, even after the visible water is gone. Standard surface drying equipment does not reach it effectively. We remove and replace saturated rim joist insulation as part of the drying scope rather than leaving a hidden moisture reservoir that can feed mold growth through the following season. It is a detail that matters more in homes of this era and construction type than in newer builds with spray foam rim joists.
