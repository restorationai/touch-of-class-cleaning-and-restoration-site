## Why St. Joseph Properties See Roofing Deterioration Faster Than Owners Expect

Stearns County's climate is not gentle on roofing materials. Average annual snowfall in the region regularly exceeds 40 inches, and the real damage often comes not from the accumulation itself but from the melt-refreeze cycle at the eave line. Ice dams form when heat escaping through the roof deck melts snow that then refreezes at the cold overhang. Water backs up under shingles, saturates the decking, and eventually finds its way into wall cavities before a homeowner notices a single interior stain.

St. Joseph's housing stock adds another layer of complexity. The community has a mix of older farmhouse-style construction and more recent residential development, and the two behave very differently under a Minnesota winter. Older homes frequently have lower roof pitches and minimal attic ventilation by today's standards, which accelerates ice dam formation. Newer builds sometimes have complex rooflines with multiple valleys and penetrations, each of which is a potential leak point if flashing is not installed correctly the first time.

Hail events in central Minnesota, while less frequent than in the southern part of the state, do occur and often go undetected on asphalt shingles until granule loss becomes visible in the gutters. By that point, the UV protection on the shingle surface is already compromised.

## Our Roofing Installation and Replacement Process in St. Joseph

Every project starts with a thorough inspection of the existing system: shingles, underlayment, decking, flashing at all penetrations and transitions, ridge ventilation, and soffit intake. We document what we find before any material is removed, which matters both for your own records and for any insurance claim you may be filing.

Decking condition is assessed once the old material is stripped. In central Minnesota's climate, it is not unusual to find sections of OSB or plywood decking that have absorbed moisture over multiple seasons and need replacement before new shingles go down. Skipping that step is how a new roof develops problems within a few years.

Underlayment selection is calibrated to the roof pitch and exposure. Low-slope sections get a self-adhering ice-and-water barrier extended well past the interior wall line, not just at the eave edge, to account for the ice dam risk specific to this region. High-traffic valleys and all flashing transitions are sealed with compatible materials rather than relying on caulk alone.

We work with asphalt architectural shingles, impact-resistant shingles rated for hail zones, and metal roofing options for clients who want a longer service life. Material selection is a conversation, not a default.
