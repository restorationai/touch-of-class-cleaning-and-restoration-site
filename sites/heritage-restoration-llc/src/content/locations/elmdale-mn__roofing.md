## Why Elmdale Properties See Roofing Problems

Central Minnesota's climate creates a specific and repeating pattern of roofing stress. Temperatures routinely swing from well below zero in January to humid, storm-prone summers, and that range puts every component of a roofing system through its paces. Asphalt shingles contract in the cold and expand in the heat, and after enough cycles the granule loss and cracking that follow are not cosmetic issues but entry points for water.

Ice damming is the most common culprit behind the roof calls we take in this region. When heat escapes through an under-insulated attic, it melts snow at the roof's peak, and that meltwater refreezes at the colder eaves. The ice backs up under shingles and forces water into the decking and eventually into interior framing. Addressing ice dam damage without also correcting attic ventilation and insulation is a short-term fix at best.

Hail is the other major driver. Storms that move through the Morrison County area in late spring and summer can drop hail large enough to fracture shingles and dent metal flashing in a single pass. Because the damage is not always visible from the ground, many homeowners do not discover it until a subsequent rainstorm reveals the leak inside.

## Our Roofing Installation and Replacement Process in Elmdale

Every project starts with a thorough inspection of the existing system, not just the shingles but the decking, flashing, ridge vents, soffits, and fascia. In older Minnesota homes, it is common to find multiple layers of shingles that have been re-roofed over the decades. Most local codes and manufacturer warranties require stripping to bare decking before a new installation, and that is the standard we follow.

Once the old material is removed, the decking is inspected for rot, delamination, or structural soft spots. Any compromised sections are replaced before underlayment goes down. We use ice-and-water shield along the eaves and in valleys, a detail that matters significantly in a climate where ice dams are a seasonal reality rather than a rare event. The rest of the roof receives synthetic underlayment before shingles are installed.

Flashing details around chimneys, skylights, and pipe penetrations get particular attention. These are the points where most long-term leaks originate, and proper step and counter-flashing installation is not something that can be rushed. Ridge cap and ventilation are the final pieces, and we size ridge and soffit venting to match the attic square footage so the system actually moves air rather than just checking a box.
