## Why Swanville Properties Present Unique Renovation Challenges

Morrison County's climate is not gentle on buildings. Ground frost can push footings and crack slabs in ways that don't fully reveal themselves until a wall is opened during a remodel. Older homes in the Swanville area frequently have knob-and-tube or early aluminum wiring that must be addressed before new cabinetry or drywall goes in. Plumbing in homes built before the 1970s often runs copper or galvanized steel that has narrowed from decades of mineral buildup, which means a bathroom remodel can surface a plumbing replacement conversation that wasn't on the original scope.

Insulation is another consistent finding. Minnesota energy code has tightened considerably over the past two decades, and homes that predate those updates often have wall cavities with R-values far below current standards. Opening walls for a remodel is the right moment to bring insulation up to code, and skipping that step can create moisture problems in the finished space when warm interior air meets a cold exterior wall through the winter.

## Our Renovation and General Contracting Process in Swanville

Every project starts with a thorough walkthrough, not a quick estimate. We look at what the structure is telling us before we talk about finishes. That means checking subfloor condition before committing to flooring, probing for moisture behind tile before a bathroom demo begins, and confirming that load-bearing elements are accounted for in any layout change.

From there, the process moves in a sequence that protects the homeowner's timeline and budget:

- **Scope documentation**: written scope with line-item detail so there are no surprises mid-project.
- **Permit coordination**: we pull the required permits through Morrison County and manage inspections so the homeowner doesn't have to track that process.
- **Sequenced trades**: framing, mechanical, electrical, and insulation are coordinated so inspections happen before walls close.
- **Finish work and punch list**: final walkthrough with the homeowner before we consider the project complete.

For post-damage rebuilds, our IICRC certifications in water, fire, smoke, and structural drying mean we understand what the structure went through before the rebuild phase begins. That context matters when deciding which materials can stay and which need to go.

## Local Note

One thing that comes up consistently in Swanville-area homes built in the mid-20th century is the combination of a poured concrete basement and wood-framed walls that sit directly on the slab without a pressure-treated bottom plate. When moisture wicks up through the concrete, that framing absorbs it silently for years. By the time a homeowner decides to finish the basement or remodel the space, the bottom foot of the stud wall is often compromised. We check for this before any basement renovation scope is finalized, because discovering it mid-demo changes both the timeline and the materials budget in ways that are easier to plan for than to react to.
