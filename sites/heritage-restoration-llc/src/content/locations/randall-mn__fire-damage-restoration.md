## Why Randall Properties Face Distinct Fire Damage Challenges

Morrison County housing stock includes a significant share of older wood-frame construction, and Randall is no exception. Homes built before modern electrical codes were adopted often have knob-and-tube or early aluminum wiring that can arc inside wall cavities, starting fires that smolder behind plaster or board sheathing before breaking into open flame. That hidden ignition pattern means the visible char on a wall surface may represent only a fraction of the actual heat exposure. Smoke travels through wall chases, attic spaces, and ductwork, depositing oily residue in rooms that appear untouched.

Minnesota's cold winters add another layer. After firefighters extinguish a blaze, water from suppression soaks into subfloors and wall cavities. In sub-freezing temperatures, that moisture can freeze before it is extracted, expanding inside framing members and causing secondary structural damage that has nothing to do with the fire itself. A restoration plan in Randall has to account for both the fire damage and the water damage that follows, often simultaneously.

## Our Fire Damage Restoration Process in Randall

The first step after a fire is a thorough assessment: we document every affected surface with photos and moisture readings before touching anything. This documentation matters for your insurance claim and establishes a baseline for the scope of work.

From there, the process moves through several stages:

- **Board-up and weatherization**: Broken windows and compromised rooflines need immediate protection, especially heading into a Morrison County winter. We secure openings to prevent further weather intrusion.
- **Smoke and soot removal**: Dry soot from fast-burning fires and wet, oily soot from slow smoldering fires require different cleaning chemistry. We identify the smoke type before selecting cleaning agents.
- **Odor neutralization**: Thermal fogging and hydroxyl treatment penetrate the same pathways smoke used, breaking down odor compounds rather than masking them.
- **Structural drying**: Water from suppression is extracted and dried using commercial dehumidifiers and air movers, following IICRC S500 drying protocols. In cold conditions, we monitor ambient temperature closely to keep drying equipment operating efficiently.
- **Structural assessment and repair**: Charred framing, compromised sheathing, and heat-damaged insulation are documented, removed, and replaced. Because Heritage Restoration LLC holds a general contractor license (#BC807677), we can carry the project from emergency stabilization through finished reconstruction.
