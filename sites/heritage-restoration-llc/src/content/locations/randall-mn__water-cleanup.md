## Why Randall Properties See Water Damage Issues

Morrison County's climate creates a specific pattern of water intrusion events. Deep frost penetration through the winter months puts pressure on buried supply lines and foundation walls. When temperatures swing upward in late February or March, that frozen ground thaws unevenly, and the water has to go somewhere. Crawl spaces and basements in this part of central Minnesota are particularly vulnerable during those transition weeks.

The housing stock in and around Randall leans older, with many homes built in construction eras when vapor barriers were minimal or absent and basement walls were poured with thinner concrete sections. Older plumbing in these homes, particularly galvanized supply lines, is more prone to pinhole failures and joint separation during freeze-thaw stress. A slow drip behind a wall can read completely dry at the surface while the framing behind it stays saturated for weeks.

Summer storm events add a second exposure window. Morrison County sees heavy convective rain in June and July, and low-lying properties near the Long Prairie River corridor can receive surface water faster than the soil absorbs it. Sump pump failures during these events are one of the most common calls we receive from the Randall area.

## Our Emergency Water Cleanup Process in Randall

When we arrive at a Randall property, the first step is containment and assessment, not just extraction. Standing water gets removed with truck-mounted or portable extraction equipment, but the more important work is mapping moisture with thermal imaging and calibrated moisture meters. Water visible on the floor is rarely the full extent of the problem. It migrates under baseboards, into wall cavities, and beneath flooring systems in ways that are invisible until the right equipment is applied.

After extraction, we establish a drying system calibrated to the structure. Commercial-grade dehumidifiers and air movers are positioned based on the moisture readings, not placed at random. We monitor and log readings across the drying period so there is a documented record of the structure returning to acceptable moisture levels. This documentation matters for insurance claims and for confirming the job is actually finished, not just surface-dry.

Where older construction materials are involved, such as plaster, board sheathing, or older engineered flooring, we adjust drying timelines accordingly. These materials release moisture more slowly than modern drywall and OSB, and rushing the process leads to callbacks.

## Randall Insurance Coordination

Most standard homeowner policies cover sudden and accidental water discharge, which includes burst pipes, appliance failures, and certain storm-related intrusions. What they typically do not cover is long-term seepage or maintenance-related failures, so documenting the timeline and cause of loss accurately matters from the first hour.

We photograph affected materials before any extraction begins, log moisture readings throughout the drying process, and organize the documentation in a format that aligns with how adjusters review claims. We work with most major carriers and can communicate directly with your adjuster to keep the claim moving. We do not inflate scopes, and we do not understate damage to close a job faster.

## Local Note

In older Randall-area homes with full basements, the rim joist area where the floor framing meets the foundation wall is one of the most consistently overlooked moisture pathways. During a water intrusion event, water wicks into that framing from both directions, and because the rim joist cavity is often insulated with older fiberglass batts, the moisture hides behind the insulation rather than showing on the surface. We pull and inspect that area as a standard part of assessment on any basement water loss in this region, because leaving saturated rim joist framing in place is one of the most reliable ways to grow mold inside a wall you cannot see.

If your Randall home has had water in the basement before and you noticed a musty smell the following summer, there is a reasonable chance that area was not fully dried the first time. It is worth a moisture check before assuming the problem resolved itself.
