## Why Harding Properties Face Particular Fire Damage Challenges

Morrison County's housing stock includes a significant share of older homes built when balloon-frame construction was standard. In that framing style, wall cavities run continuously from the basement to the attic with no fire blocking between floors. When a fire starts in a lower level, smoke and heat travel upward through those open cavities before the flames ever reach the second floor. That means visible char may stop at the kitchen, while smoke residue and odor have already penetrated insulation, subflooring, and attic sheathing above.

Harding's rural setting also means many properties rely on LP gas or fuel oil rather than natural gas. A tank or line failure during a fire adds a secondary hazard that affects how crews approach initial assessment and where they position equipment during the early stages of cleanup. Heritage Restoration coordinates with local utility contacts and follows established safety protocols before any restoration work begins inside the structure.

## Our Fire Damage Restoration Process in Harding

Every fire loss is different, but the sequence of work follows a consistent logic shaped by what smoke and heat actually do to a building.

**Structural assessment and board-up.** Before cleaning starts, the structure needs to be secured. We assess load-bearing elements, identify areas of compromised framing, and board or tarp openings to prevent weather intrusion, which matters especially in a Minnesota winter when an open roof or broken window can introduce moisture damage on top of fire damage.

**Soot and smoke residue removal.** Soot chemistry varies by what burned. Protein-based smoke from a kitchen fire leaves a thin, almost invisible film that carries a persistent odor. Synthetic materials produce wet, smearing soot that bonds aggressively to painted surfaces. We identify the residue type and match the cleaning method accordingly, using dry chemical sponges, alkaline cleaners, or thermal fogging based on what the surfaces require.

**Odor neutralization.** Smoke odor is not a surface problem. Molecules penetrate porous materials including wood framing, drywall, and insulation. We use hydroxyl generation and thermal fogging to reach odor sources that surface wiping cannot address.

**Structural drying if water is present.** Firefighting water adds a second loss layer. We deploy moisture meters and thermal imaging to map saturation, then run commercial drying equipment calibrated to the structure's materials and the ambient conditions outside.

**Documentation for insurance.** We photograph and document affected materials, scope of work, and equipment placement throughout the project to support your insurance claim.

## Local Note

In older Morrison County homes built with wood lath and plaster walls, fire and smoke cleanup requires a different approach than work in modern drywall construction. Plaster is dense and relatively non-porous at the surface, but the wood lath behind it absorbs smoke odor deeply. Wiping the plaster face may look complete while the lath is still off-gassing. On Harding properties with this construction, we test behind wall surfaces before closing out odor treatment, because a home that smells clean on day one can develop noticeable odor again within weeks if the lath was not addressed.
