## Why Swanville Properties See Distinct Fire Damage Challenges

Much of the residential building stock in and around Swanville was constructed during an era when wood framing was heavier, insulation cavities were smaller, and interior finishes leaned toward plaster and wood paneling rather than today's drywall. Those older materials hold smoke odor differently. Plaster, in particular, is porous enough to absorb soot particles and volatile organic compounds deep into its surface, meaning a fire that looks contained to one room can leave odor problems throughout the house if the structure isn't assessed and treated systematically.

Morrison County's cold winters add another layer of complexity. When a fire occurs in January or February and exterior temperatures are well below freezing, the suppression water from firefighting operations can freeze inside wall cavities before it ever gets a chance to drain. That trapped moisture, combined with smoke-saturated framing, creates conditions where secondary mold colonization can begin within days. Addressing both the fire and smoke damage and the moisture left behind is not optional in this climate; it's part of the same job.

Wood-burning stoves and older heating systems are also more common in rural Morrison County than in metro areas, and chimney-related fires or heating appliance failures are a recurring cause of structural fire damage in this region. The resulting soot from incomplete combustion, sometimes called wet or oily soot, is among the most difficult residue types to clean because it smears rather than brushes away and bonds to surfaces aggressively.

## Our Fire Damage Restoration Process in Swanville

Every fire loss is different, but the sequence of work follows a consistent logic. The first step on-site is a full structural and content assessment: what burned, what was smoke-affected, what was wet from suppression, and what is structurally sound enough to restore versus what needs to come out. We document all of this with photographs and written scope notes, which becomes the foundation of the insurance claim.

Demolition of unsalvageable materials comes next, followed by HEPA vacuuming of all soot-affected surfaces before any wet cleaning begins. Wet-cleaning soot before dry removal is one of the most common mistakes in fire cleanup, and it drives staining deeper into porous materials. After surface cleaning, we apply appropriate sealers to framing and subfloor where odor has penetrated the wood grain, then treat the air with thermal fogging or hydroxyl generation to address airborne odor molecules that surface cleaning alone cannot reach.

Structural drying runs concurrently with smoke remediation when suppression water is present. Our IICRC ASD (Applied Structural Drying) certification means we monitor moisture readings in walls, floors, and ceilings throughout the drying phase rather than estimating by feel or appearance.

## Local Note

Older homes in rural Morrison County, including many in the Swanville area, were built with balloon-frame construction rather than the platform framing used after roughly the mid-20th century. In a balloon-frame structure, wall cavities run continuously from the foundation to the roof line without fire-blocking between floors. That means smoke and heat from a fire on the first floor can travel vertically through wall cavities and affect the second floor or attic even when the visible fire damage appears limited to one level. During our initial assessment, we probe these cavities specifically in older homes because the scope of smoke penetration is almost always larger than the burn pattern on the walls suggests.
