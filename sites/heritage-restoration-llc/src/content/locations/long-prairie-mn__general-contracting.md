Long Prairie winters are not gentle to buildings. The freeze-thaw cycle that hammers Todd County from November through March works its way into foundations, shifts door frames, and leaves older homes with gaps, settled floors, and moisture damage that compounds over years. When a renovation or rebuild is on your list, whether you are updating a kitchen that has not changed since the 1970s, remodeling a bathroom after a slow pipe leak, or rebuilding a structure after fire or water damage, the work needs to account for what Minnesota's climate has already done to the bones of the building. Heritage Restoration LLC brings licensed general contracting (license #BC807677) and EPA Lead-Safe certified crews from Little Falls to Long Prairie to handle exactly that kind of layered project.

## Why Long Prairie Homes Present Unique Renovation Challenges

Todd County's housing stock leans older. A significant share of Long Prairie's residential properties were built before 1980, and many date to the mid-twentieth century or earlier. That era of construction means a few things that matter before a single wall comes down.

First, lead paint is a realistic concern in any home built before 1978. Disturbing painted surfaces during a kitchen or bathroom remodel without proper containment and testing is not just a health risk, it is a regulatory one. Heritage Restoration's EPA Lead-Safe certification means the crew follows federal RRP protocols for containment, cleanup, and disposal, which protects your family and keeps the project compliant.

Second, older framing dimensions do not match modern lumber standards. A wall framed with true two-by-fours from the 1950s is actually two inches by four inches, which creates fitment issues when installing modern cabinetry, windows, or insulation batts. Crews that work primarily in newer construction sometimes miss this until they are mid-project. It is the kind of detail that adds cost and delay when it is a surprise, and it should not be.

Third, Minnesota's climate means insulation upgrades are almost always worth folding into a remodel. If walls are already open for a kitchen renovation, adding modern insulation to exterior walls is far cheaper than doing it as a standalone project later.

## Our Renovation and General Contracting Process in Long Prairie

Every project starts with a thorough walkthrough, not a quick visual estimate. For post-damage rebuilds especially, what is visible at the surface rarely tells the full story. Water behind plaster can read dry at the surface while the underlying structure stays saturated. Smoke residue works into wall cavities and HVAC systems in ways that affect finish work months later if not addressed first.

From there, the process follows a clear sequence:

- **Scope and documentation**: We photograph existing conditions, document any pre-existing damage relevant to insurance, and produce a written scope of work before any demolition begins.
- **Permitting**: Long Prairie building permits run through the City of Long Prairie and, depending on the scope, may involve Todd County review. We handle permit applications and schedule inspections so the project stays on the right side of the local building department.
- **Demolition and rough work**: Framing, rough plumbing, rough electrical coordination, and any structural repairs happen in sequence so inspections can be staged correctly.
- **Finish work**: Drywall, flooring, cabinetry, tile, trim, and paint are scheduled to minimize idle time between trades.
- **Final walkthrough**: We walk the completed space with you before closing out the project.

## Local Note

Long Prairie sits in a region where many homes were built on crawl spaces rather than full basements, particularly in neighborhoods developed through the 1950s and 1960s. Crawl space construction in central Minnesota is vulnerable to ground moisture migration, especially in spring when snowmelt raises the water table. Before a bathroom or laundry room remodel on the ground floor, it is worth inspecting the crawl space for vapor barrier condition and any signs of prior moisture intrusion. Finding that problem before subfloor work begins saves significant rework later. It is a step that gets skipped when a contractor is not familiar with how central Minnesota soil and drainage behave through the seasons.
