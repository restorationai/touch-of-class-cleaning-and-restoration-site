## Our Storm Damage Restoration Process in Baxter

The first priority after any storm event is stopping ongoing water intrusion. That means emergency tarping and board-up before any interior work begins, a step that also matters for insurance documentation, because carriers want to see that reasonable mitigation happened promptly. Once the structure is secured, the process moves to a systematic damage assessment: roof, exterior envelope, attic, and then interior surfaces and structural members.

Moisture mapping with thermal imaging and calibrated moisture meters tells us what the eye can't see. Water that enters through a compromised soffit or a cracked ridge cap doesn't stay at the point of entry, it tracks along roof sheathing, wicks into insulation, and can reach ceiling drywall two or three rooms away from the actual breach. Drying is conducted using commercial-grade desiccant and refrigerant dehumidifiers alongside high-velocity air movers, following IICRC structural drying protocols. Readings are logged daily so the drying curve is documented for both the homeowner and the insurance adjuster.

Tree damage cleanup is handled in coordination with debris removal before interior restoration begins. Structural repairs, from replacing damaged rafters and sheathing to re-roofing and siding replacement, are completed under Minnesota contractor license #BC807677.

Storm damage claims in Minnesota are subject to specific documentation requirements that differ from water or fire claims. One pattern that comes up repeatedly in the Brainerd Lakes area: homes with decorative pine and cedar siding, common in cabins and lake-adjacent residential builds throughout Crow Wing County, absorb storm-driven rain differently than vinyl or fiber cement.
