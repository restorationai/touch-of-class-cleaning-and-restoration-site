## Why Harding Properties See Water Damage Issues

Morrison County sits in a climate zone where freeze-thaw cycles can occur multiple times in a single late-autumn or early-spring week. That repeated expansion and contraction stresses supply lines, especially in older homes where galvanized or early copper plumbing has already seen decades of mineral buildup. When a line finally gives, water doesn't stay in one place: it follows framing cavities downward, saturating insulation, soaking rim joists, and pooling beneath flooring where it can begin promoting mold colonization within 24 to 48 hours of the initial event.

Beyond frozen pipes, the region's spring snowmelt is a recurring source of water intrusion. Low-lying properties and those with older drainage tile around their foundations can see groundwater work its way through block or poured-concrete walls during a heavy melt. Sump pump failures during this window are common, and the resulting standing water in a basement can wick into finished wall assemblies quickly if extraction doesn't start promptly.

## Our Water Damage Restoration Process in Harding

The first step on any water loss is containment and assessment. A technician uses moisture meters and thermal imaging to map where water has traveled beyond the visible wet area, because water behind plaster or under vinyl plank flooring can read dry at the surface while the substrate beneath stays saturated. That hidden moisture is what drives long-term structural damage and mold growth if it isn't addressed.

Once the scope is mapped, water removal begins using truck-mounted or portable extraction equipment suited to the floor type and volume of standing water. From there, the drying phase follows the IICRC S500 standard: calibrated air movers and refrigerant or desiccant dehumidifiers are positioned to create a directed airflow pattern through affected cavities. Readings are logged daily so drying progress is documented and the equipment configuration can be adjusted if a section isn't tracking toward the target moisture levels.

Materials that cannot be dried in place, such as saturated insulation or swollen particleboard subfloor panels, are removed to allow the structural framing behind them to dry completely before any reconstruction begins. This step matters more in older construction where framing lumber is denser and releases moisture more slowly than modern engineered lumber.
