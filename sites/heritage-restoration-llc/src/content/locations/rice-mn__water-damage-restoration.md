## Why Rice Properties Are Vulnerable to Water Damage

Morrison County's soil profile includes stretches of clay-heavy ground that drains slowly. After a heavy rain or a rapid thaw, that clay holds moisture against foundation walls for days rather than hours, creating sustained hydrostatic pressure. Homes built on slab foundations, which are common in newer Rice-area construction, can show moisture intrusion through hairline cracks that are nearly invisible until flooring starts to buckle or a musty odor appears.

Older homes in the area often have unfinished or partially finished basements with block foundation walls. Block construction is especially susceptible because each mortar joint is a potential entry point, and water that wicks through block can travel laterally inside the wall cavity before it ever shows on the interior face. By the time a homeowner notices a wet spot, the moisture has typically spread well beyond the visible stain.

Rice also sits in a climate zone where frozen pipes are a genuine winter threat. A single night where temperatures drop sharply and a heating system hiccups is enough for supply lines in exterior walls or unheated crawl spaces to freeze and split. The burst often goes undetected until the system thaws and water begins flowing freely.

## Our Water Damage Restoration Process in Rice

Every job starts with moisture mapping. Technicians use thermal imaging cameras and calibrated moisture meters to locate water that has migrated behind walls, under subfloors, and into insulation cavities. Visible water is only part of the picture, and skipping this step leads to hidden pockets of moisture that feed mold growth weeks after the surface appears dry.

Once the scope is documented, water extraction begins using truck-mounted and portable extraction units capable of pulling standing water from carpet, hardwood, and concrete surfaces. Structural drying follows, using commercial-grade low-grain refrigerant dehumidifiers and high-velocity air movers positioned according to the IICRC S500 standard for water damage restoration. Drying targets are set based on the material type and baseline readings, not on a fixed number of days.

Moisture readings are logged at each visit so the drying curve is documented throughout the process. That documentation matters for insurance claims and also confirms that materials have reached acceptable moisture content before any reconstruction begins. Affected materials that cannot be dried in place, such as saturated insulation or swollen OSB sheathing, are removed and disposed of properly.

## Local Note: Crawl Spaces and the Rice-Area Frost Line

One thing that comes up specifically in this part of Morrison County is the interaction between frost depth and crawl space moisture. The frost line in central Minnesota runs deep, and homes with shallow crawl spaces can experience ground moisture that is essentially trapped between frozen soil below and a cold subfloor above for weeks at a time. That sustained damp environment accelerates wood decay and creates conditions where mold can establish itself even without a visible leak. If a water loss in Rice involves any crawl space component, the drying plan has to account for that enclosed, cold environment rather than treating it like an above-grade room. Airflow and dehumidification strategies differ, and drying times are typically longer.
