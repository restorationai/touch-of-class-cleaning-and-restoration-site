## What to Look for When Choosing a Water Damage Company in Little Falls

Choosing the right company when water is actively damaging your home comes down to four things.

**Certification.** IICRC WRT and ASD credentials mean the technician knows how to classify the water source, set measurable drying goals, and document when the structure is actually dry. Without those credentials, drying is guesswork. The [EPA's guidance on mold and moisture](https://www.epa.gov/mold) is clear that materials not dried within 24 to 48 hours are at risk for mold growth. Certified drying is not optional.

**Licensing.** Minnesota requires contractors performing restoration work to be licensed. Ask for the license number and verify it. Heritage Restoration LLC's license is BC807677.

**Documentation.** Your insurance claim depends on a written scope, moisture readings, and a drying log. If a company cannot produce those documents, your adjuster may deny or reduce the claim. Ask before you hire.

**Local accountability.** A locally owned company has its reputation tied to your community. If something goes wrong, you are not calling a national 1-800 number. You are calling the owner.

## What Happens During Water Damage Restoration in Little Falls

A properly executed water damage job in central Minnesota follows a defined sequence. Understanding it helps you evaluate whether the company you hired is doing it right.

1. **Emergency extraction.** Standing water is removed first using truck-mounted or portable extraction equipment. The faster this happens, the less secondary damage occurs.
2. **Moisture mapping.** Technicians use thermal imaging cameras and moisture meters to find water that has migrated into walls, subfloors, and ceiling cavities. Water travels further than it looks.
3. **Categorization.** The water source is classified: Category 1 (clean, like a supply line), Category 2 (gray water, like a washing machine overflow), or Category 3 (black water, like sewage or floodwater). Category determines PPE requirements, what materials can be saved, and how the job is documented.
4. **Drying system setup.** LGR (low-grain refrigerant) dehumidifiers and high-velocity air movers are placed to create airflow across wet materials. The number and placement of units is calculated, not guessed.
5. **Daily monitoring.** Moisture readings are taken each day. Drying is not complete until readings reach the IICRC S500 standard for the material type.
6. **Clearance documentation.** A final moisture report is produced. This is what your insurance company and your own records need.
7. **Reconstruction.** If materials were removed (drywall, flooring, insulation), reconstruction brings the space back to pre-loss condition.
