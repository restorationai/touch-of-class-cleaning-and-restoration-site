## What to Look for When Choosing a Water Damage Restoration Company in Chicago

The right restoration company does three things well: they stop the damage from spreading, they dry the structure correctly, and they document everything your insurance company needs. Here is what to check before you hire anyone.

**Licensing and insurance.** Illinois does not have a single statewide restoration contractor license, but any company working in Chicago should carry general liability insurance and workers' compensation. Ask for certificates before work begins. Dry Bros is licensed and insured.

**IICRC certification.** The [IICRC S500 Standard for Professional Water Damage Restoration](https://www.iicrc.org) is the industry benchmark for how water damage jobs should be scoped, dried, and documented. A company that follows S500 protocols will use moisture mapping, set drying goals, and verify the structure is dry before they leave. Ask whether the crew is IICRC-trained.

**Insurance documentation.** If you are filing a claim, your restoration company's scope of work is the foundation of your adjuster's decision. A good company provides a written scope before work begins, photos of all affected areas, moisture readings at intake and at job completion, and a drying log. Our post on [does homeowners insurance cover water damage](/blog/does-homeowners-insurance-cover-water-damage/) explains what documentation typically protects a claim.

**Local presence.** A locally owned company has a direct stake in its reputation in the Chicago market. National franchises vary significantly by location. When you call a local company, you typically reach someone who can give you a straight answer about scheduling and scope.

**Response time.** Water damage follows a predictable timeline: Category 1 clean water (a burst supply line) becomes a Category 2 situation (microbial growth risk) within 24-48 hours if not dried. The faster extraction begins, the lower the total damage. Ask any company you're considering how quickly they can be on-site.

## How Water Damage Restoration Actually Works

Water damage restoration is not just drying things out. It is a structured process governed by the [IICRC S500 standard](https://www.iicrc.org), and skipping steps leads to hidden moisture, structural damage, and mold growth.

Here is what a properly run job looks like:

1. **Emergency extraction.** Truck-mounted or portable extractors remove standing water. This is the fastest phase.
2. **Moisture mapping.** Technicians use calibrated moisture meters and thermal imaging to find water that has wicked into walls, subfloors, and ceiling cavities. You cannot dry what you haven't found.
3. **Drying equipment placement.** Commercial LGR (low-grain refrigerant) dehumidifiers and high-velocity air movers are positioned to create an airflow pattern that pulls moisture out of materials. The number and placement of units is calculated by the affected square footage and material types.
4. **Daily monitoring.** Readings are taken each day. Drying goals (specific moisture content targets per material type) are set at the start and verified at the end.
5. **Documentation.** A complete drying log, moisture readings at intake and completion, and photos of all affected areas are compiled for the insurance claim.
6. **Mold check.** If the loss was slow or undetected, or if drying took longer than expected, a mold assessment is warranted before reconstruction begins. Our post on [signs of hidden mold](/blog/signs-of-hidden-mold/) covers what to watch for.

A company that skips moisture mapping or pulls equipment early to close the job faster is a company that will leave moisture in your walls. That moisture becomes mold within weeks.
