The biggest cost drivers are how much water intruded, what materials got wet, and how long the water sat before extraction started.

## How Much Does Water Damage Restoration Cost in IL?

Most Illinois homeowners pay between $1,300 and $5,000 for a standard clean-water loss affecting one or two rooms, and $7,000 to $20,000 or more when sewage backup, flooding, or extensive structural drying is involved. The final number depends on the water's source, how many square feet got wet, and whether drywall, flooring, or framing need to be removed and rebuilt.

Those ranges hold across the Chicago metro, from lakefront condos in Evanston to older bungalows on the South Side to newer construction in Naperville. Housing age matters here: a lot of Chicago-area housing stock has plaster walls, older subfloors, or finished basements below grade, and all three add labor time compared to a newer build with open-stud construction.

## What Drives the Cost of Water Damage Restoration?

The water's category and how long it sat are the two factors that move the price the most, followed by square footage and the materials affected. A same-day clean-water response costs far less than a three-day-old Category 3 loss.

**Category and class of water.** The [IICRC](https://iicrc.org) classifies water losses by contamination level: Category 1 is clean water (a supply line break), Category 2 is gray water (a washing machine overflow), and Category 3 is grossly contaminated water (sewage backup, floodwater). Higher categories require more demolition, more disposal, and more disinfection, which raises cost.

**Response time.** Drywall, carpet pad, and subfloor start absorbing moisture within hours. A loss caught and mitigated the same day usually needs less material removal than one sitting untreated for two or three days, when mold growth becomes a real risk.

**Square footage and materials.** Hardwood floors, plaster walls, and finished basements cost more to dry and repair than carpet over concrete. A one-room bathroom leak and a whole-basement flood from a Chicago combined-sewer backup during a heavy storm are not the same job, even though both start as "water damage."

**Access and layout.** A walkout basement dries faster and costs less to service than a below-grade basement with limited exterior access, which is common in older Cicero and Berwyn housing stock.

## Will Insurance Cover the Cost?

Most standard homeowners insurance policies cover sudden, accidental water damage, like a burst pipe or a failed water heater, but exclude gradual leaks and, in most cases, exclude surface flooding unless you carry separate flood coverage. Sewer and sump pump backup usually requires a specific endorsement.

That distinction matters a lot in Illinois. Chicago and many of its older suburbs run combined sewer systems that can back up into basements during heavy rain, and that kind of loss is often excluded from a standard policy unless you added a sewer backup rider. The [Federal Emergency Management Agency](https://www.fema.gov) administers the National Flood Insurance Program for homeowners in flood-prone areas who want separate flood coverage.

Deductibles on covered water losses typically run $500 to $2,500. What actually protects your claim is documentation: photos of the standing water, moisture readings at the start of the job, and an itemized scope of work. ## What Does the Restoration Process Actually Involve?

Water damage restoration in Illinois generally moves through four phases: inspection, extraction, drying, and reconstruction, each of which affects the final invoice. Understanding the phases makes the cost breakdown make more sense.

1. **Inspection and moisture mapping.** A technician uses a moisture meter and often a thermal camera to identify how far the water traveled, including behind walls and under flooring, not just what's visible.
2. **Water extraction.** Standing water gets pumped or vacuumed out using truck-mounted or portable extraction equipment. This step usually happens within hours of the call to limit how much material needs replacing later.
3. **Structural drying.** Air movers and LGR dehumidifiers run for typically three to five days, with daily moisture checks to confirm materials are drying to the IICRC S500 standard rather than just "looking dry."
4. **Reconstruction.** Anything that couldn't be dried, like saturated drywall, insulation, or carpet pad, gets removed and replaced. This is usually the largest line item on a bigger loss.
