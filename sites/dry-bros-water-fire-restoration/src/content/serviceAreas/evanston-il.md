## Restoration emergencies common in Evanston

Evanston sits on the western shore of Lake Michigan, and that geography shapes the kind of damage property owners deal with. Lake-effect snow accumulates fast and heavy, and when it melts against foundations that have been in the ground since the early 1900s, water finds every crack. Spring thaw combined with heavy rain regularly overwhelms the city's combined sewer system, pushing water back through floor drains into finished basements. Summer thunderstorms roll in off the lake with little warning, and the clay-heavy soil that underlies much of the North Shore doesn't absorb water quickly, it sheds it toward foundations instead. The result is a predictable seasonal rhythm of water intrusion, mold growth, and the occasional fire loss from electrical systems in aging housing stock.

## Services we provide in Evanston

### Water damage restoration

Water damage in Evanston frequently starts below grade. Basements in the city's older two-flats, single-family homes, and courtyard apartment buildings are often finished living space, which means a sewer backup or foundation seep can saturate drywall, subfloor, and personal property before anyone notices the smell. Dry Bros extracts standing water, maps moisture with thermal imaging and moisture meters, and sets drying equipment calibrated to the IICRC S500 standard, because what reads dry at the surface can stay saturated inside plaster or beneath hardwood for days.

### Fire and smoke damage restoration

Kitchen fires, electrical fires in knob-and-tube wiring, and chimney fires are all common in Evanston's older housing stock. Smoke residue travels far beyond the room of origin, settling into HVAC ducts, wood framing, and plaster. Dry Bros documents the full extent of smoke migration, removes charred material, and neutralizes odor at the source rather than masking it, an important distinction when an insurance adjuster is reviewing the scope.

### Mold remediation

Evanston's lake-adjacent humidity and the frequency of basement water intrusion create conditions where mold can begin colonizing porous materials within 24 to 48 hours of a moisture event. Remediation involves containing the affected area, removing compromised material, and treating surfaces, followed by post-remediation verification to confirm clearance. Dry Bros does not offer medical advice on health effects, but does follow established industry containment and removal protocols.

### Reconstruction and build-back

Once mitigation is complete, damaged framing, drywall, flooring, and finishes need to be rebuilt to code. Dry Bros coordinates the build-back phase so property owners are not managing two separate contractors while also dealing with an insurance claim.

## Building stock, site conditions, and permits in Evanston

Most of Evanston's residential housing was built between the 1880s and the 1950s, with a significant share of brick two-flats, Craftsman bungalows, and larger frame homes on full basements. Plaster-on-lath walls are common in this era, and they behave differently from modern drywall when wet, the plaster face can appear intact while the wood lath and framing behind it stay saturated. Galvanized steel supply lines and cast-iron drain stacks are typical in pre-war construction; both corrode over time and can fail without warning. Homes built before 1980 should be assumed to contain asbestos-containing materials in floor tile, pipe insulation, or textured ceilings, and lead-based paint in pre-1978 structures, both require specific handling protocols before any demolition work begins. Evanston's soil is predominantly clay-heavy glacial till that drains slowly and exerts lateral pressure on basement walls. The city's Community Development Department handles building permits for structural repair and reconstruction; most scope-of-work projects beyond cosmetic repair will require a permit, and restoration contractors should plan for that step in the project timeline.
