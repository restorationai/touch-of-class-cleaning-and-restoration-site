Skokie sits on the flat, clay-heavy glacial lakebed that underlies much of the North Shore, and that geology matters the moment moisture finds its way into a home. Clay soils drain slowly, basement walls stay damp longer after heavy rain, and the region's freeze-thaw cycles push water through foundation cracks that can go unnoticed for an entire winter. By the time a musty smell surfaces in a finished basement or a discolored patch appears on drywall, mold has often been colonizing behind the surface for weeks, sometimes longer.

## Why Skokie Properties See Mold Issues

Skokie's housing stock skews heavily toward mid-century construction, ranch homes, brick bungalows, and two-flats built largely between the 1940s and 1970s. Many of these homes have original basement waterproofing that has aged past its effective life, and crawl spaces or slab-on-grade sections that were never designed for the moisture loads that come with today's more intense storm events. When a sump pump fails during a spring storm or a slow plumbing leak goes undetected under a kitchen floor, the conditions for mold growth arrive fast. At 70°F and above 60% relative humidity, conditions common inside Skokie homes from late spring through early fall, certain mold species can begin colonizing porous materials within 24 to 48 hours of a moisture event.

Older homes in Skokie also frequently feature plaster-and-lath interior walls rather than modern drywall. Plaster is more resistant to surface mold than paper-faced drywall, but the wood lath behind it is highly susceptible once moisture migrates inward. A surface reading of "dry" on a moisture meter does not mean the lath is clear, and that distinction shapes how remediation is scoped here.

## Our Mold Remediation Process in Skokie

Every job starts with a thorough moisture and mold assessment, not just the visible growth, but the source driving it. Treating mold without addressing the underlying moisture pathway guarantees recurrence. Once the scope is established, the affected area is isolated with physical containment barriers and negative air pressure to prevent spores from migrating to unaffected rooms during removal.

Contaminated porous materials, drywall, insulation, wood framing, are removed, bagged, and disposed of per Illinois EPA guidelines. Non-porous surfaces are cleaned using EPA-registered antimicrobial agents. HEPA air scrubbers run throughout the project and continue after physical removal to capture airborne spores. Structural drying follows if residual moisture remains in framing or subfloor assemblies. The job is not complete until post-remediation air and surface testing confirms spore counts have returned to normal background levels, a step that protects both the occupants and the integrity of any insurance documentation.

Mold claims in Illinois can be complicated by policy language distinguishing "sudden and accidental" water damage from long-term seepage. When mold follows a covered event, a burst pipe, an appliance failure, storm-driven water intrusion, documentation of the moisture source is critical to a successful claim. Dry Bros photographs and documents affected materials systematically, producing the kind of written scope and photo record that carriers and their adjusters need to process a claim. ## Local Note

Skokie's mid-century brick homes often have a construction detail worth knowing: the original mortar between courses can become porous over decades, allowing water to wick slowly into the interior wythe of the wall. This kind of moisture intrusion rarely shows up as a visible wet spot, it reads as elevated humidity in a finished room, and the mold it feeds grows on the back side of interior finishes. If you are seeing recurring mold on an exterior-facing wall without an obvious roof or plumbing leak, the brick assembly itself may be the moisture pathway. That diagnosis changes both the remediation scope and the permanent fix.
