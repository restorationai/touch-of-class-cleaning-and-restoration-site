## Why Des Plaines Properties Face Elevated Board-Up and Tarping Needs

The housing stock in Des Plaines tells the story. A significant portion of the city's residential neighborhoods were built in the postwar boom of the late 1940s through the 1960s, which means wood-framed construction with older roof decking, single-pane windows, and in many cases original masonry chimneys. When a fire or severe windstorm compromises that envelope, the structure is immediately vulnerable in ways that newer construction is not, aged sheathing absorbs rain quickly, older mortar joints wick moisture, and original window frames offer almost no resistance once the glass is gone.

Des Plaines also sits in a drainage basin that sees standing water after heavy rain events. A roof opened by storm damage doesn't just let in wind, it lets in water that can migrate into wall cavities within hours. Board-up and tarping isn't just about security; in this climate, it's about stopping a water damage clock that starts the moment the structure is breached.

## Our Emergency Board-Up and Tarping Process in Des Plaines

Every job starts with a rapid exterior assessment, we walk the perimeter before touching anything, because in fire-damaged structures, compromised soffits and fascia boards can make a ladder placement dangerous. We photograph every breach point for your insurance documentation before materials go up.

For window and door openings, we use structural-grade plywood secured to the framing, not just the trim. On older Des Plaines homes where the trim stock is original wood and may be soft or deteriorated, we anchor into the rough framing to ensure the board-up holds through wind gusts rather than pulling free in the next storm. For roof breaches, we deploy heavy-duty polyethylene tarps weighted and fastened at the ridge and secured at the eaves, not draped and hoped for. On steeper-pitched roofs common to the area's Cape Cods and bungalows, we use additional mechanical fastening at the field of the tarp to prevent wind uplift.

Every opening is logged by location and dimension. That documentation goes directly to your adjuster.

## Local Note

Des Plaines homes built in the 1950s and early 1960s frequently have balloon-frame construction rather than the platform framing that became standard later. In a balloon-frame structure, wall cavities run continuously from the foundation to the roof, which matters for board-up work because a breach at the roofline can allow smoke, water, and outside air to travel down through the wall cavity to the basement level. When we're boarding up a fire-damaged balloon-frame home, we note the construction type in our documentation and flag it for the restoration team, because the remediation scope may extend further than the visible damage suggests.
