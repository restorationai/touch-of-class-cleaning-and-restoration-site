Des Plaines sits in a meteorological corridor where Lake Michigan's moisture collides with fast-moving fronts pushing in from the southwest, a combination that produces some of the most unpredictable severe weather in Cook County. Hail that strips shingles, straight-line winds that drop mature oaks across rooflines, and flash flooding that backs up through window wells and basement drains are not rare events here; they're a recurring feature of life along the Des Plaines River valley. When a storm tears through, Dry Bros Water & Fire Restoration is ready to assess the damage, stop secondary losses, and get your property back to pre-storm condition.

## Why Des Plaines Properties Are Particularly Vulnerable to Storm Damage

The housing stock in Des Plaines spans several distinct eras, and each era carries its own storm-damage profile. Homes built in the postwar boom of the late 1940s and 1950s, a large share of the city's residential neighborhoods, typically feature original wood-frame construction with aging roof decking that has absorbed decades of freeze-thaw cycles. When high winds or hail strike, that decking can fail faster than it would on newer OSB sheathing, and water intrusion follows almost immediately.

The city's relatively flat topography and the proximity of the Des Plaines River create drainage challenges that compound storm damage. During heavy rainfall events, surface water has limited elevation change to work with. Sump systems get overwhelmed, window wells fill faster than drains can clear them, and water finds its way into crawl spaces and finished basements through foundation cracks that were previously dormant. What looks like a roof problem from the outside often has a simultaneous water-intrusion component below grade.

Older clay tile sewer laterals, common throughout much of Des Plaines, are also prone to root intrusion and collapse, meaning a heavy storm can simultaneously overwhelm the municipal system and trigger a sewage backup inside the home. That's a biohazard situation layered on top of a structural one, and it requires a crew equipped to handle both.

## Our Storm Damage Restoration Process in Des Plaines

Every storm damage job starts with a thorough inspection, roof, attic, exterior walls, and below-grade spaces, because storm damage rarely confines itself to the obvious point of entry. Water follows framing cavities, insulation channels, and HVAC chases in ways that a surface look won't reveal. We use moisture meters and thermal imaging to map where water has traveled before we touch a single material.

Once the scope is documented, the process moves in a deliberate sequence:

- **Emergency tarping and board-up** to stop active weather intrusion at the roof or wall breach
- **Water extraction** from any affected interior spaces, including crawl spaces and basements
- **Structural drying** using industrial air movers and dehumidifiers, calibrated to the specific material assemblies in the home
- **Debris removal**, fallen tree limbs, damaged roofing material, compromised insulation
- **Damage documentation** with photographs and written scope for your insurance carrier
- **Reconstruction coordination** once the structure is dry and stable

For homes with plaster walls, still common in Des Plaines's older neighborhoods, drying timelines run longer than they do with modern drywall. Plaster holds moisture deep in the substrate and releases it slowly, so we monitor daily readings and adjust equipment rather than pulling it prematurely.

## Insurance Coordination for Des Plaines Storm Claims

Storm damage claims in Illinois are governed by standard homeowners policy language, but the documentation requirements can vary significantly by carrier. We photograph every affected surface, generate itemized scope reports, and communicate directly with adjusters to make sure nothing is missed or undervalued. Roof damage in particular is frequently underdocumented on initial inspection, carriers may note visible shingle loss but miss the underlying decking damage or compromised flashing that will cause leaks for years if not addressed.

If your policy includes additional living expense coverage and the damage makes your home temporarily uninhabitable, we can help you understand what that process looks like and what documentation supports the claim.

## Local Note

Des Plaines homes built near the river corridor, particularly those on lower-elevation lots west of River Road, often have basement floor drains that tie directly into the combined sewer system. During a major storm event, when the municipal system surcharges, those floor drains can become the path of least resistance for sewage-contaminated water to enter the basement. Homeowners who see water coming up through a floor drain after a storm should treat it as a potential sewage backup, not just stormwater, until it's been tested.
