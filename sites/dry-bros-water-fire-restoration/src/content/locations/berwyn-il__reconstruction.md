## Why Berwyn's Building Stock Shapes Every Reconstruction Project

The vast majority of Berwyn's housing was built before modern platform framing became standard. That means load-bearing masonry walls, balloon-frame interior partitions, and plaster-on-lath finishes that behave very differently from contemporary drywall construction when they're damaged by fire, water, or smoke.

Fire damage in a balloon-frame wall cavity travels vertically with almost no natural fire stop, a localized kitchen fire can char framing two stories above the point of origin before suppression crews arrive. Reconstruction in those structures requires careful inspection of the full stud bay, not just the visible burn zone, before any finish work begins. Similarly, water damage in plaster-and-lath assemblies can look surface-dry within days while the underlying lath and masonry remain saturated for weeks, which means any rebuild timeline has to account for extended drying verification before new finishes go in.

Berwyn also sits on the kind of clay-heavy glacial till common throughout the inner-ring suburbs west of Chicago. That soil holds moisture, which contributes to foundation movement over time, and a foundation that has shifted even slightly can complicate structural reconstruction by throwing door and window openings out of square in ways that aren't obvious until framing is already underway.

## Our Reconstruction Process in Berwyn

Every reconstruction engagement starts with a scope document, not a sales conversation. Before a single board is replaced, the affected area is assessed for structural integrity, hidden moisture, and any materials that require special handling, lead paint and asbestos-containing materials are common in Berwyn homes of this era and must be addressed before reconstruction work proceeds.

From there, the process moves in a defined sequence:

- **Structural stabilization**, temporary shoring or boarding where load-bearing elements have been compromised
- **Selective demolition**, removing only what cannot be saved, with documentation for insurance purposes
- **Rough-in trades coordination**, framing, electrical, plumbing, and HVAC rough-ins sequenced to meet Cook County inspection requirements
- **Inspections**, permit pulls and required inspections before walls are closed
- **Finish work**, drywall or plaster match, millwork, painting, and flooring to bring the space back to pre-loss condition

Because Berwyn's permit office processes residential and commercial reconstruction permits through Cook County's building department framework, timeline expectations need to account for inspection scheduling, something we factor into every project plan from the start.
