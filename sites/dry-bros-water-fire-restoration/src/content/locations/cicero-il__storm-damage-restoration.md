That combination, sudden intensity, older housing stock, and flat Cook County drainage, means storm damage here rarely looks like a single clean problem. A downed tree punches through a roof, rain follows immediately into the attic, and within hours you have a structural breach, soaked insulation, and the early conditions for mold growth all running at once.

## Why Cicero Properties Are Especially Vulnerable to Storm Damage

Most of Cicero's residential blocks were built between the 1910s and 1950s, when construction standards, roofing materials, and drainage infrastructure were designed for a different era. Brick two-flats and bungalows dominate the housing stock, and while masonry holds up well under many conditions, the mortar joints on older facades can fail under repeated freeze-thaw cycles, leaving hairline gaps that a hard rain exploits immediately. Flat or low-slope roofs, common on the commercial corridors and on many of the area's two-stories, are particularly unforgiving when storm debris clogs internal drains or when wind peels back aging membrane.

The soil across this part of Cook County is predominantly clay-heavy, which means it saturates quickly and sheds water laterally rather than absorbing it. After a significant storm, that water has to go somewhere, and in a densely built neighborhood with little permeable surface, it often finds its way into basement window wells, foundation cracks, and the gaps around older utility penetrations.

## Our Storm Damage Restoration Process in Cicero

When we arrive at a storm-damaged property, the first priority is a full exterior and interior assessment before any debris is moved. Tarping an active roof breach, boarding compromised windows, and stabilizing any structural elements that could shift further, these steps happen before cleanup begins, because unsecured damage compounds fast.

Once the property is stabilized, we document everything: photographs, moisture readings, and a written scope of loss that your insurance adjuster can work from directly. Water intrusion from storm events is often multi-path, it enters through the roof, travels along rafters, pools in wall cavities, and surfaces somewhere unexpected on the floor below. We use thermal imaging and calibrated moisture meters to trace those paths rather than relying on what's visible at the surface.

Debris removal, structural drying, and material assessment follow in sequence. Where storm water has contacted insulation, drywall, or flooring, we test and document before deciding what can be dried in place and what needs to come out. That distinction matters for your claim and for the long-term health of the structure.
