Des Plaines sits at the confluence of the Des Plaines River and Salt Creek, and that geography matters the moment a sewer line backs up in your basement. The city's combined sewer infrastructure, which carries both stormwater and sanitary waste in a single pipe, means that a hard rain can overwhelm the system and push raw sewage backward into homes across the city. When that happens, what's on your floor isn't just water. It's Category 3 contamination: bacteria, viruses, and pathogens that require professional removal and sanitization, not a mop and a bottle of bleach. Dry Bros Water & Fire Restoration responds to exactly these situations, handling sewage backup cleanup and full sanitization so the space is safe to occupy again.

## Why Des Plaines Properties See Sewage Backup Issues

The combined sewer system that serves much of Des Plaines is a known pressure point during heavy rainfall events. When stormwater volume spikes faster than the system can route it to the Metropolitan Water Reclamation District's treatment infrastructure, the path of least resistance is often back through floor drains, toilets, and utility sink connections in lower-level spaces. Basements in the older residential corridors near the downtown Metra station and along Oakton Street tend to sit low relative to grade, making them especially vulnerable during the spring thaw and the intense summer thunderstorms the Chicago metro sees regularly.

Homes built before the 1970s, and Des Plaines has a substantial number of them, frequently have clay tile lateral sewer lines connecting the house to the municipal main. Clay tile is durable until it isn't: tree root intrusion, ground settlement, and decades of use cause cracks and offsets that let groundwater in and create the blockages that produce backups. A blockage in the lateral is a property-owner responsibility, not the city's, which means the cleanup and the pipe repair both land on the homeowner.

## Our Sewage Cleanup and Sanitization Process in Des Plaines

Sewage cleanup follows a strict sequence because cutting corners at any step leaves contamination behind that isn't visible to the eye.

**Containment first.** Affected areas are isolated to prevent cross-contamination to living spaces. If the backup reached finished drywall or wood framing, those materials are assessed for removal, porous materials that have absorbed Category 3 water cannot be dried and kept; they have to come out.

**Extraction and solid waste removal.** Standing sewage is extracted using truck-mounted equipment. Solid waste and contaminated debris are bagged, labeled, and disposed of in compliance with Illinois EPA guidelines for biohazardous waste, not hauled to a standard landfill.

**Structural drying.** Once the bulk contamination is removed, the drying phase begins. In Des Plaines winters, cold ambient temperatures slow evaporation significantly; we account for that with heated drying equipment rather than relying on ambient air movement alone.

**Antimicrobial treatment and sanitization.** Every surface that contacted sewage, concrete floors, block walls, floor joists, subfloor, is treated with EPA-registered antimicrobial agents. This step addresses pathogens that extraction alone doesn't eliminate.

**Documentation.** We photograph and document each phase, which matters when you're filing a claim with your homeowner's insurance or a sewer backup rider.

## Insurance Coordination for Des Plaines Sewage Claims

Standard homeowner's insurance policies in Illinois often exclude sewer backup damage unless the homeowner has added a sewer backup endorsement, a separate rider that many Des Plaines residents carry precisely because the combined sewer risk is well known locally. If you have that coverage, we document the loss thoroughly: photos of affected materials before removal, moisture readings, waste extraction logs, and a written scope of work. That documentation goes to your adjuster directly, which reduces back-and-forth and keeps the claim moving. If you're not sure whether your policy includes sewer backup coverage, your carrier's declarations page will show it as a line item.
