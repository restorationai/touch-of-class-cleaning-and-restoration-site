## Why Des Plaines Properties Are Vulnerable After a Fire

The housing stock throughout Des Plaines tells a story in wood and plaster. A significant share of single-family homes in the city were built between the 1940s and 1970s, many using balloon-frame construction, a method where wall studs run continuously from foundation to roofline without the fire-blocking that platform-frame homes built after the mid-1970s include. In a balloon-frame home, smoke and heat travel vertically through wall cavities with very little resistance, meaning a kitchen fire can deposit soot inside second-floor walls and attic framing long before the visible damage suggests it.

Cook County winters compound the problem. When temperatures drop well below freezing, firefighters pump large volumes of water to suppress a blaze, and that water soaks into subflooring, insulation, and wall assemblies almost immediately. By the time the fire is out, a Des Plaines property often has two overlapping damage events: the fire and smoke, and the water used to fight it. Both need to be addressed in sequence, and the order matters, drying before soot removal can set odor permanently into porous materials.

## Our Fire Damage Restoration Process in Des Plaines

The first step on any fire loss is a thorough structural and content assessment. Smoke behaves differently depending on what burned, synthetic materials like carpet and upholstery produce a wet, oily soot that smears if wiped incorrectly, while wood and paper fires leave a dry, powdery residue that responds to dry chemical sponges. Getting that distinction right before touching a surface is the difference between cleaning and making the damage worse.

From there, the process moves through controlled demolition of unsalvageable materials, structural drying of any water-saturated assemblies, HEPA-filtered air scrubbing to pull airborne particulates, and surface-by-surface soot removal using methods matched to the material. Odor neutralization follows, thermal fogging and hydroxyl generation are both used depending on whether the structure is occupied or vacant. Reconstruction is scoped after the structure is clean and dry, not before, because hidden damage often surfaces during the cleaning phase.

Throughout every stage, Dry Bros documents materials removed, methods used, and readings taken. That documentation is what your insurance adjuster needs to process a claim without delays.
