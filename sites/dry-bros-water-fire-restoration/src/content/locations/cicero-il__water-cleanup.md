Cicero sits on some of the flattest, most clay-dense ground in Cook County, and that geology does not forgive a slow response to water damage. When a pipe bursts or a sump pump fails here, water spreads fast across basement slabs, wicks into the mortar joints of brick foundations, and stalls, because there is nowhere for it to drain on its own. Dry Bros Water & Fire Restoration responds to water cleanup calls across Cicero, moving quickly to stop that spread before it turns a manageable loss into a structural one.

## Why Cicero Properties See Water Damage Differently

Cicero's housing stock tells a story in its bones. The vast majority of homes and two-flats here were built between the 1910s and the 1950s, which means you're dealing with balloon-frame construction, plaster-and-lath walls, and cast-iron or galvanized steel plumbing that has been quietly corroding for decades. When a supply line finally lets go inside a wall cavity, water doesn't travel in a straight line, it channels down through the framing, pools behind plaster, and can read completely dry at the surface while the wood lath behind it stays saturated.

Cicero also sits in a low-lying corridor where storm sewer capacity gets tested hard during the intense summer thunderstorms that roll off Lake Michigan. Basement flooding from sewer backup is common here, and that type of water carries a different contamination profile than a clean supply-line break, it requires a different extraction and disinfection protocol from the first step.

## Our Water Cleanup Process in Cicero

When we arrive, the first priority is stopping the water source if it hasn't been stopped already, then we assess what category of water we're dealing with. Clean water from a burst supply line, gray water from an appliance, and sewage-contaminated water each require a different approach to safe removal and disinfection.

Extraction comes next. Standing water gets pulled with truck-mounted and portable extraction units. Then we use thermal imaging and calibrated moisture meters to map where water has migrated inside walls, under flooring, and into subfloor assemblies, because in Cicero's older homes, the visible damage is rarely the full picture. Drying equipment, commercial air movers and desiccant or refrigerant dehumidifiers, gets placed based on that moisture map, not guesswork. We monitor readings daily and adjust the drying configuration until structural materials reach their target moisture content.

If flooring or drywall has absorbed enough water that it cannot be dried in place without trapping moisture, we document it, photograph it, and remove what needs to come out before closing anything back up.

## Insurance Coordination for Cicero Water Losses

Most standard homeowner policies in Illinois cover sudden and accidental water damage, a burst pipe, an appliance failure, an ice-dam overflow, but they typically exclude gradual leaks and sewer backup unless you've added a rider. We document the loss thoroughly from the moment we arrive: photographs, moisture readings, affected material inventories, and a written scope of work. That documentation is what your adjuster needs to process the claim, and we provide it in the format carriers expect.

If your policy does include sewer backup coverage, we can work directly with your insurance company on the claim so you're not managing that conversation while also dealing with a wet basement.

## Local Note

In Cicero's older two-flats and bungalows, the original hardwood floors were often installed directly over board subfloor with no vapor barrier between them, a common construction practice through the mid-20th century. When the subfloor gets wet, the hardwood above it can cup or buckle within 24 to 48 hours, but the subfloor itself may stay elevated in moisture content long after the surface looks fine. We've seen cases where surface readings suggested the floor was drying well while the subfloor assembly underneath was still holding enough moisture to support mold growth. That's why we don't pull equipment based on how the floor looks, we pull it based on what the meters say at depth.
