## Why Skokie's Housing Stock Makes Smoke Damage Harder to Clear

Skokie is largely a postwar suburb, and that construction era comes with specific restoration challenges. Homes built before 1970 often have plaster-and-lath walls rather than drywall. Plaster is denser and less porous on the surface, which sounds like an advantage, but smoke oils and protein residue from cooking fires can penetrate hairline cracks in aged plaster and sit inside the wall cavity where standard surface cleaning never reaches them. The odor returns weeks later, especially when summer humidity pushes moisture back through the wall.

Forced-air heating systems, nearly universal in Skokie's single-family homes, are a secondary distribution network for smoke particles. Soot that enters the return-air plenum during a fire gets redistributed every time the furnace cycles. Duct cleaning is not optional on these jobs, it's part of the restoration, not an add-on.

Older brick exteriors common throughout Skokie also absorb smoke differently than vinyl siding. Brick is alkaline and reacts with the acidic compounds in smoke residue, which can cause permanent discoloration if the wrong cleaning chemistry is used. Matching the cleaning agent to the substrate is something that gets worked out on-site, not assumed from a phone call.

## Our Smoke Damage Restoration Process in Skokie

Every smoke job starts with a structured assessment, not a visual scan, but a systematic room-by-room evaluation that documents affected materials, smoke migration paths, and odor concentration zones. We photograph and log everything before any cleaning begins, which matters when you're working with an insurance adjuster.

From there, the process typically moves through these stages:

- **Containment and ventilation**: Negative air pressure keeps cross-contamination from spreading soot to unaffected areas while fresh-air exchange begins pulling smoke odor out of the structure.
- **Dry soot removal**: Loose soot is vacuumed with HEPA-filtered equipment before any wet cleaning. Wiping dry soot with a wet cloth smears it deeper into surfaces, a mistake that creates more work and more cost.
- **Surface cleaning**: Walls, ceilings, cabinetry, and hard surfaces are cleaned with chemistry matched to the material, alkaline cleaners for protein smoke, dry sponges for certain painted surfaces, appropriate agents for brick and masonry.
- **Duct and HVAC cleaning**: Supply and return ducts are cleaned and the system is inspected before it's run again.
- **Thermal fogging or hydroxyl treatment**: Odor molecules that have penetrated porous materials, insulation, subflooring, upholstery, are addressed with fogging or hydroxyl generators, not just surface sprays.
- **Final air quality check**: Before the job closes, we verify that odor levels have returned to baseline.

## Skokie Insurance Coordination for Smoke Damage Claims

Smoke damage claims have a reputation for being contested, partly because the damage isn't always visible to an adjuster walking through a property. Odor is real damage, but it requires documentation, air sampling data, affected-material inventories, and photo logs that show migration paths, to hold up in a claim. We produce that documentation as a standard part of the job, not as a special request.

## Local Note

Skokie's older brick homes frequently have unlined or partially lined chimneys, a common feature in mid-century construction that was never updated. When a chimney fire or fireplace malfunction is the source of smoke damage, the chimney itself can be a secondary reservoir of soot and creosote that continues off-gassing into the living space long after the visible fire is out. On jobs where the chimney is involved, we flag this early and coordinate with a chimney specialist before sealing up the structure, because enclosing a contaminated flue traps the problem rather than solving it.
