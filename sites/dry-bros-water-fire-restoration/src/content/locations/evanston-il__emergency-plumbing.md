Evanston's winters are unforgiving, and the city's aging housing stock, much of it built before World War II, means that when temperatures plunge off Lake Michigan, pipes in uninsulated exterior walls and crawl spaces don't just freeze: they split. A burst pipe in an older Evanston home can dump dozens of gallons into plaster ceilings and hardwood floors before a homeowner even realizes something is wrong. Dry Bros Water & Fire Restoration responds to plumbing emergencies throughout Evanston, stopping the water fast and starting the drying process before secondary damage compounds the loss.

## Why Evanston Properties See Emergency Plumbing Issues

Evanston sits on the western shore of Lake Michigan, and that geography matters when temperatures drop. Wind chill off the lake accelerates pipe freezing in ways that inland Chicago neighborhoods don't experience to the same degree. Homes in the city's older residential corridors, many built in the 1910s through 1940s, commonly have galvanized steel supply lines that have narrowed with decades of mineral buildup. When water freezes inside an already-restricted pipe, the pressure spike is severe, and the pipe fails at its weakest joint rather than at a predictable location.

Slab construction is less common here than in newer suburban developments; most Evanston single-family homes and two-flats sit on full basements or crawl spaces with exposed plumbing runs. That's actually useful, it means a burst pipe is often accessible without opening finished walls, but it also means a slow leak can saturate a basement's rim joist and floor framing for days before it's noticed. Older clay-tile sewer laterals, common in the city's pre-war blocks, can also back up and push water into basement drains during heavy rain events, compounding what looks like a simple plumbing call.

## Our Emergency Plumbing Process in Evanston

When a call comes in, the first priority is source control, stopping the water before anything else. We walk callers through locating the main shutoff, which in many Evanston homes is in the basement utility room or near the water meter pit at the street. If the shutoff is seized or inaccessible, a licensed plumber on our team handles it on arrival.

Once the water is off, we assess the extent of the damage using moisture meters and thermal imaging cameras. In Evanston's older plaster-and-lath construction, surface readings can be misleading, the plaster face dries faster than the wood lath behind it, and the framing cavity can stay saturated long after the wall feels dry to the touch. We document moisture readings at multiple depths before any drying equipment is placed, which matters both for effective restoration and for insurance documentation.

From there, extraction equipment removes standing water, and commercial-grade drying systems are positioned based on the moisture map, not just pointed at the wet spot. We coordinate with a licensed plumber to complete the pipe repair, and we handle the restoration side: drying, structural assessment, and any needed reconstruction.

## Evanston Insurance Coordination

Most standard homeowner policies cover sudden and accidental water damage from a burst pipe, but the language matters. Policies typically exclude damage from a slow leak that went undetected, which is why our moisture documentation from the first visit is important. We photograph affected materials, log moisture readings with timestamps, and provide a written scope of loss that your adjuster can work from directly. We bill major carriers and can communicate with your adjuster throughout the claim so you're not translating between the restoration side and the insurance side on your own.

If your property is part of a condo association or multi-unit building, common in Evanston's denser residential areas, we're accustomed to navigating the question of unit owner versus association responsibility, and we document the loss in a way that keeps both parties' claims clean.

In Evanston's older two-flat and three-flat buildings, the main water shutoff often serves all units from a single valve in the basement, and that basement may belong to a different unit than the one with the leak.
