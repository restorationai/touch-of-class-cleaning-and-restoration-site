## Why Cicero Properties Hold Odors Longer

Cook County's humid continental climate means Cicero sees genuine extremes, hot, muggy summers and cold winters with freeze-thaw cycles that stress older masonry and plumbing. That seasonal moisture load matters for odor work: when brick and mortar absorb water repeatedly over decades, they also absorb whatever is dissolved in that water. Smoke residue from a chimney fire, mold metabolites from a slow basement leak, or pet odor that has soaked into original hardwood subfloors all behave differently in pre-war construction than in a 2010 build with OSB and spray foam.

The flat, dense urban layout of Cicero also means homes sit close together, and HVAC systems, many of them updated piecemeal over the years, can pull air from shared walls or attic cavities. An odor that seems to be coming from one room often has a secondary source behind a plaster wall or under a floor that was never fully dried after a past water event.

## Our Odor Removal and Deodorization Process in Cicero

Effective odor elimination isn't a single step, it's a sequence, and skipping any stage means the smell comes back.

**Source identification first.** Before any equipment goes on, the affected materials are located and documented. Odor-causing compounds have to be removed or neutralized at their origin, not just masked at the air level.

**Thermal fogging** penetrates porous surfaces, plaster, wood trim, original hardwood, by converting a deodorizing solution into a fine fog that follows the same pathways smoke or moisture used to get in. It's especially effective in Cicero's older homes where the wall cavities are irregular and difficult to reach mechanically.

**Hydroxyl deodorization** runs continuously in occupied or semi-occupied spaces. Hydroxyl generators produce the same oxidizing radicals that sunlight creates outdoors, breaking down odor molecules at the molecular level without requiring the space to be vacated for extended periods, useful when a tenant or homeowner can't fully clear out.

**Ozone treatment** is reserved for unoccupied spaces where a high-concentration oxidizing cycle can be run safely. It's aggressive and effective for heavy smoke odor, but it requires proper clearance time before re-occupancy.

After treatment, air quality is assessed to confirm odor compounds have been reduced to acceptable levels before the job is closed.

## Equipment & Methods We Use for Odor Removal

Not every odor problem calls for the same tool. Smoke odor from a structure fire carries different chemical compounds than the microbial off-gassing from a flooded basement or the protein residue from a sewage backup. The deodorization method is matched to the odor type:

- **Thermal fogging**, best for smoke, fire, and protein odors in porous materials
- **Hydroxyl generation**, broad-spectrum, safe for occupied spaces, effective for mold and general organic odors
- **Ozone treatment**, high-intensity, unoccupied spaces only, used for severe smoke saturation
- **Encapsulation sealers**, applied to framing or subfloor when the odor source is structural and cannot be removed

In Cicero's vintage housing stock, the combination of thermal fogging and hydroxyl generation is frequently the right pairing, the fog penetrates the plaster and original millwork, while the hydroxyl unit handles residual airborne compounds over the following hours.

## Local Note

Cicero's brick bungalows often have unfinished basements with poured concrete floors and block walls that were never sealed. When a sump fails or a drain backs up, the concrete absorbs water and the odor compounds that come with it. Surface drying looks complete within a day or two, but the block walls can off-gas musty odors for weeks afterward if they aren't treated directly. Thermal fogging into the block cavities, followed by an encapsulant on the exposed face, addresses what a dehumidifier alone cannot. If you're noticing a smell that returns every time the basement gets humid, the source is almost certainly in the block or the slab, not in the air.
