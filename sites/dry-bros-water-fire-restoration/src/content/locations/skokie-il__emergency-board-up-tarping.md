## Why Skokie Properties Are Especially Vulnerable After a Loss

Skokie's residential fabric is dense with mid-century construction, brick bungalows, two-flats, and ranch-style homes built largely between the 1940s and 1970s. That era of building often means aging wood-frame roof decks, single-pane windows, and in some cases original mortar joints that have softened over decades. When fire or storm damage punches through that envelope, the structure loses its integrity quickly. A broken window in a brick two-flat isn't just a security risk, cold air and moisture can begin warping interior woodwork and plaster within a single overnight freeze.

The city's flat-to-gently-rolling topography also means standing water has fewer natural escape routes after heavy rain. If a storm tears roofing material loose before the rain stops, water enters the attic and travels fast through insulation and ceiling assemblies. Skokie sits in a zone where spring and fall storms regularly produce wind gusts capable of lifting poorly secured tarps, which is why the anchoring method matters as much as the tarp itself.

## Our Board-Up and Tarping Process in Skokie

Every job starts with a site assessment before any material goes up. We document the damage with photographs, both for your records and to support your insurance claim, then determine the right approach for the structure in front of us.

For roof tarping, we use heavy-duty polyethylene tarps rated for extended outdoor exposure, secured with weighted battens and mechanical fasteners appropriate to the roof deck material. On older homes with original wood decking, we take care not to introduce fastener patterns that could create new leak points once the tarp comes down.

For window and door board-ups following fire or impact damage, we cut plywood to fit the opening, use construction-grade screws rather than nails (which back out in temperature swings), and seal edges where weather exposure is a concern. On ground-floor openings, we add a second layer where security is the primary risk. Every opening gets documented before and after.

We coordinate directly with your insurance adjuster and can provide the photo documentation and scope notes they typically request for board-up line items.

## Skokie Insurance Coordination for Board-Up Work

Most standard homeowners policies cover emergency board-up and tarping as part of the loss mitigation benefit, meaning the cost of securing your property is typically reimbursable, not an out-of-pocket add-on. The key is documentation: carriers want to see the pre-board condition, the scope of the opening, and the materials used. We photograph every step and can provide a written scope that aligns with the format most adjusters expect.

If your property is managed or subject to a condo association, Skokie's multi-family stock sometimes involves shared exterior walls or common-area rooflines. In those cases, we can flag the affected boundary clearly in our documentation so your adjuster and the association's insurer can sort coverage without ambiguity.

## Local Note

Skokie's older brick two-flats and courtyard buildings, common throughout the city's interior residential blocks, often have parapet walls along the roofline. After fire damage, those parapets can look structurally sound from the street while the mortar and brick ties behind the facade have been compromised by heat. Before we set up a ladder or walk a roof on a fire-damaged building, we assess parapet stability first. It's a step that matters more in Skokie's mid-century building stock than it does in newer construction, and it's one reason we don't treat every board-up job as a simple punch-and-go.
