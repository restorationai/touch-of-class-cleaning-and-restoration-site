The brick two-flats and bungalows that define so much of Cicero's housing stock were built before modern waterproofing standards existed, which means a flooded basement here isn't just an inconvenience. ## Why Cicero Properties See Basement Flooding So Often

Cicero's drainage challenges are baked into its geography. The municipality sits on a largely impermeable clay substrate that doesn't absorb stormwater the way sandy or loamy soils do. During a heavy rain event, the kind the Chicago metro sees repeatedly from spring through early fall, runoff has nowhere to go quickly. Combined sewer systems in older Cook County suburbs like Cicero can surcharge under that load, pushing sewage-contaminated water back through floor drains and utility sink connections into finished and unfinished basements alike.

The housing stock compounds the problem. A large share of Cicero's residential properties were constructed between the 1910s and 1950s. Many have poured concrete or concrete block foundation walls with no interior drainage tile and no sump pit. Window wells on street-facing garden units can act as collection basins during heavy rain. Lateral sewer lines running from these older homes to the municipal main are frequently made of clay tile, a material that cracks, offsets, and allows groundwater infiltration as it ages.

## Our Basement Flooding Cleanup Process in Cicero

The first step on any flooded basement call is assessing the water category. A burst supply line produces clean water; a backed-up floor drain almost always carries Category 3 contaminated water, which changes how the crew handles every material it contacts. In Cicero's older bungalows, that distinction matters immediately, sewage-contaminated water that has soaked into a wood subfloor or a concrete block wall requires a different extraction protocol and a stricter containment approach than a clean-water pipe leak.

Once the water source is confirmed and controlled, standing water is extracted using truck-mounted or portable extraction units powerful enough to pull water from carpet padding, subfloor seams, and the porous face of concrete block. After bulk extraction, the real work begins: placing industrial desiccant dehumidifiers and high-velocity air movers in a calculated pattern to drive moisture out of the structural assembly, not just the surface. Moisture readings are logged at each visit so the drying curve is documented, not guessed.

Materials that cannot be dried in place, saturated drywall below the flood line, soaked fiberglass batt insulation, wet carpet, are removed and disposed of properly. Antimicrobial treatment is applied to affected framing and concrete surfaces before any reconstruction begins.

## Local Note

Cicero's concrete block foundation walls are common in homes built through the mid-twentieth century, and they behave differently from poured concrete under moisture stress. Block walls are hollow-core assemblies, water can enter the cores and pool inside the wall cavity without ever appearing on the interior face. A surface moisture reading on a block wall can look acceptable while the cores are still holding standing water. Crews working in Cicero need to probe the mortar joints and, when necessary, drill weep holes at the base of the wall to drain the cores before drying can begin in earnest. Skipping that step is one of the most common reasons a basement drying job in an older Cicero home takes longer than expected or produces a mold callback weeks later.
