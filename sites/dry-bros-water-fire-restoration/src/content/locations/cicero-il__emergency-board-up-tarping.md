## Why Cicero Properties Face Particular Board-Up and Tarping Demands

Cicero's housing stock is overwhelmingly pre-1960, with a significant portion built before World War II. That era of construction means balloon-frame walls, single-wythe brick facades, and low-pitch rooflines, details that matter when a fire or storm opens a structure. Balloon framing allows fire and smoke to travel vertically through wall cavities faster than platform framing does, which means fire damage board-up here often needs to address upper-floor openings that were affected even when the fire started on the ground level. The shallow roof pitch common on Cicero bungalows also sheds debris differently than steeper slopes: after a storm tears away shingles or a chimney cap, water pools toward the center rather than running off, and a flat-laid tarp needs to be tensioned and weighted carefully to avoid that same pooling problem.

The proximity of neighboring structures adds another layer. In blocks where two-flats share a party wall or sit within a few feet of each other, broken window board-up has to account for sightlines and access that wouldn't be an issue on a suburban lot. Scaffolding or ladders sometimes have to be staged in the alley rather than the front, and the board material itself needs to be cut to fit masonry openings that rarely conform to standard lumber dimensions.

## Our Emergency Board-Up and Tarping Process in Cicero

When we arrive at a Cicero property, the first step is a perimeter walk to identify every compromised opening, not just the obvious breach. Fire damage frequently blows out windows on floors above the origin point, and storm damage to a roof can leave soffit sections loose that aren't visible from street level. We document each opening with photographs before any material goes up, which creates a record useful for the insurance claim.

For roof tarping, we use heavy-duty polyethylene tarps secured with wood battens screwed into the decking, not just weighted at the edges. On Cicero's low-pitch bungalow roofs, edge weighting alone fails in the first strong wind event. Battens distribute the load and keep the tarp from lifting even when gusts come through the corridor between close-set buildings.

For broken window board-up and door openings, we cut plywood to fit the masonry rough opening, use construction adhesive and structural screws into the surrounding frame or masonry anchors where there's no frame, and brace larger openings from the interior. On ground-floor openings in a vacant or fire-damaged structure, we add a second layer and interior bracing as a deterrent to unauthorized entry.

## Local Note

Cicero's older brick bungalows frequently have unreinforced masonry facades, and after a fire or structural event, the brick above a window or door opening can be compromised even when it looks intact from the street. When we board up those openings, we check whether the lintel, often a steel angle iron in homes of this era, has shifted or shows signs of heat damage before we anchor into the surrounding masonry. Attaching board-up material to a compromised lintel can pull loose under wind load, which defeats the purpose entirely. It's a step that takes an extra few minutes but matters a great deal on Cicero's vintage building stock.
