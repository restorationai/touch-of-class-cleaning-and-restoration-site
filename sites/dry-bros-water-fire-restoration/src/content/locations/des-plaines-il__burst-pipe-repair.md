## Why Des Plaines Properties See Burst Pipe Issues

The Chicago metro's freeze-thaw cycle is a known driver of burst pipes, but Des Plaines has a few local factors that make the risk more pronounced. Much of the city's residential stock was built between the 1940s and 1970s, when copper and galvanized steel supply lines were standard. Galvanized pipe corrodes from the inside out over decades, thinning the wall thickness until a hard freeze, or even a sharp pressure spike from the city main, causes a failure. Homes near the Des Plaines River corridor also sit on clay-heavy soil that shifts seasonally, putting lateral stress on underground supply lines and the connections where they enter the foundation.

Older neighborhoods with original plumbing are particularly vulnerable. A pipe that has held for 50 winters can still fail when January temperatures drop sharply overnight and the furnace cycles off for even a few hours. Attic water lines, garage supply runs, and pipes tucked into exterior bump-outs are the most common failure points we see in this part of Cook County.

## Our Burst Pipe Cleanup and Repair Process in Des Plaines

The first step after shutting off the water is understanding where it went, not just where it's visible. We use moisture meters and thermal imaging to map water migration through wall cavities, under flooring, and into insulation. Standing water is extracted with truck-mounted equipment, and structural drying begins with calibrated air movers and dehumidifiers sized to the affected area, not a one-size-fits-all setup.

Once the environment is stable, the pipe repair happens, whether that's a straightforward copper splice, a section replacement on corroded galvanized line, or a more involved repair where the break occurred inside a finished wall. We document the damaged materials photographically before any removal, which matters later when your insurance adjuster reviews the claim. Drying is monitored daily with moisture readings logged at each check, so the structure is confirmed dry before any rebuild work begins.

## Local Note

Homes in Des Plaines built before the 1960s frequently have plaster-and-lath interior walls rather than drywall. This matters during burst pipe cleanup because plaster absorbs water more slowly than drywall but releases it more slowly too, a wall cavity can read close to dry at the surface while the lath and framing behind it remain saturated for days. We adjust drying timelines and equipment placement accordingly, and we don't rely on surface readings alone to call a structure dry. Pulling a wall open prematurely to check is sometimes the right call; so is leaving monitoring equipment in place longer than a standard drywall job would require.
