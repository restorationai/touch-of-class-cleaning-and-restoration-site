When a fast-moving line of thunderstorms rolls off Lake Michigan, the kind that drops two inches of rain in forty minutes and snaps mature oak limbs like kindling, the damage lands hard on rooftops, siding, and basements all at once. ## Why Skokie Properties See Outsized Storm Damage

The same geography that makes Skokie attractive, flat terrain, mature tree canopy, dense residential blocks, creates real vulnerability when severe weather hits. Flat lots shed water slowly. When a storm drops rain faster than the ground can absorb it, water backs up against foundations, seeps through window wells, and finds every gap in aging brick mortar.

Skokie's housing stock skews heavily toward mid-century construction, brick bungalows, ranch homes, and two-flats built between the 1940s and 1970s. Roofs on these homes often have lower pitches and original or once-replaced decking that can delaminate quickly when a tree limb punches through or wind-driven rain gets under lifted shingles. Once the roof envelope is breached, water moves fast through plaster ceilings and down interior walls before it's visible at floor level.

The mature elms and oaks that line many Skokie residential streets are an asset in summer, and a liability in a derecho. A single downed limb can breach a roof, sever a gutter system, and block a driveway simultaneously, complicating both emergency access and the repair sequence.

## Our Storm Damage Restoration Process in Skokie

The first priority after a storm is stopping ongoing damage. That means emergency tarping of any breached roof sections and boarding windows or doors that are no longer weather-tight. We document conditions with photos before anything is moved, that documentation matters when your adjuster reviews the claim.

Once the structure is stabilized, we assess the full moisture picture. A roof breach in Skokie's brick-and-plaster homes is rarely contained to the obvious wet spot. Water travels along framing, collects inside wall cavities, and can sit behind plaster for days before it registers on a surface reading. We use thermal imaging and moisture meters to trace the actual migration path, not just the visible stain.

Drying equipment, commercial air movers and dehumidifiers calibrated to the affected area, is placed based on that assessment, not a standard layout. Monitoring continues until structural readings confirm the materials have returned to acceptable moisture levels. Debris removal, structural repairs, and finish work follow in sequence, coordinated with your insurance timeline.

## Skokie Insurance Coordination

Most storm damage claims in Cook County go through standard homeowners policies, but the documentation requirements can vary by carrier. We photograph affected materials before extraction, itemize removed debris, and provide moisture logs that support the scope of damage your adjuster needs to see. If your policy includes additional living expense coverage and the home is uninhabitable while work proceeds, we can help you understand what to document on your end, though coverage decisions belong to your carrier, not us.

If your property falls under an HOA or condo association, check whether the association's master policy covers any portion of exterior damage before filing individually. In Skokie's older two-flat and condo conversions, the boundary between unit-owner and association responsibility for roof and exterior repairs isn't always obvious and is worth confirming before work begins.

## Local Note

Skokie's brick bungalows were built with plaster walls over wood lath, a construction method that behaves very differently from modern drywall when water gets inside. Plaster itself can feel dry at the surface within a day or two of a storm, but the wood lath behind it retains moisture significantly longer and is prone to mold colonization if drying isn't extended past the point where surface readings look acceptable. On these homes, we run drying equipment longer than the surface readings alone would suggest and verify with readings taken at the lath depth before signing off.

If your Skokie home was built before 1960 and took storm water through the ceiling or an exterior wall, plan for a longer drying window than a neighbor with a newer build might need, it's the nature of the construction, not a complication we created.
