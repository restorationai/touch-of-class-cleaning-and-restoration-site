## Why Naperville Properties Face Particular Exposure After a Loss

Naperville's housing stock is unusually diverse for a Chicago suburb. The older sections of the city, neighborhoods platted in the late 1800s and early 1900s near the downtown core, include balloon-frame construction where wall cavities run floor-to-roof without fire blocking. When a fire breaches one of those cavities, the structural envelope can be compromised in ways that aren't obvious from the street, and a board-up that doesn't account for hidden voids leaves the interior exposed. Newer subdivisions built through the 1990s and 2000s tend toward engineered lumber trusses, which can lose load-bearing capacity faster under fire exposure than dimensional lumber, meaning roof tarping needs to go on quickly, before additional truss members fail.

Naperville also sits in DuPage County's storm corridor, where hail events capable of puncturing roofing membranes and breaking skylights occur several times each decade. After a significant hail or wind event, many properties in the same neighborhood need tarping simultaneously, which makes calling early, before the backlog builds, genuinely important.

## Our Emergency Board-Up and Tarping Process in Naperville

When a call comes in, the first step is a rapid site assessment to identify every breach: fire-damaged roof sections, broken windows, compromised doors, and any structural openings that aren't immediately visible. We photograph everything before materials go up, because that documentation becomes part of the insurance claim file.

For window and door board-ups, we use plywood cut and secured to the structural framing, not just the window casing, so the barrier holds against wind load and resists forced entry. On roof sections, heavy-duty polyethylene tarps are anchored with lumber battens screwed into sound decking, not just draped and weighted. A tarp that lifts in a 40 mph gust is no protection at all, and Naperville's open prairie geography means wind loads here are real.

If the structure has sustained fire damage, we assess whether the area is safe to work before crews go up. Charred rafters and weakened trusses require a different approach than a clean puncture from a fallen branch.
