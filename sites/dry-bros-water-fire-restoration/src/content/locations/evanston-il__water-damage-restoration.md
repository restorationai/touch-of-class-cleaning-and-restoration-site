Evanston sits on a narrow strip of land between Lake Michigan and the North Shore's clay-heavy soils, a combination that makes water damage a recurring reality rather than a rare event. When a sump pump fails during a spring thaw, a water main cracks under an aging parkway, or a finished basement floods after a heavy rain pushes groundwater through foundation walls, the damage spreads fast. Dry Bros Water & Fire Restoration responds to water emergencies throughout Evanston, handling everything from initial water extraction to full structural drying so the problem doesn't follow you into the next season.

## Why Evanston Properties See Water Damage So Often

Evanston's housing stock is older than most people outside the North Shore realize. A significant share of the city's residential buildings date to the early twentieth century, with brick exteriors, stone foundations, and plaster-over-lath interior walls that behave very differently from modern construction when water gets inside. Brick is porous. Stone foundations, common in the blocks closest to the lakefront, allow lateral moisture migration that a poured-concrete basement would resist. And plaster walls, while durable when dry, absorb water slowly and release it even more slowly, which means a leak that looks contained at the surface can be saturating the lath and framing behind it for days before anyone notices.

Beyond the building stock, Evanston's geography creates pressure from two directions. Lake Michigan storm surges and heavy rainfall events overwhelm the combined sewer system that still serves parts of the city, pushing water back through floor drains. At the same time, the clay soils that underlie most of Evanston's residential neighborhoods drain poorly, so groundwater rises quickly after sustained rain and finds any gap in a foundation wall or footing.

## Our Water Damage Restoration Process in Evanston

When we arrive, the first priority is stopping active water intrusion and understanding how far moisture has already traveled. In Evanston's older homes, that means using thermal imaging cameras and calibrated moisture meters to check behind plaster walls and under hardwood floors, surfaces that can read dry to the touch while the substrate underneath is still saturated.

Once we have a clear picture of the affected area, we begin water extraction using truck-mounted and portable extraction units, followed by strategic placement of commercial-grade air movers and refrigerant or desiccant dehumidifiers. Drying timelines in Evanston's plaster-wall homes run longer than in newer construction, that's not a shortcut we take, it's a physics reality we plan around from the start. We monitor moisture readings daily and adjust equipment placement until every affected material reaches its target drying threshold, documented per the IICRC S500 standard.

If flooring, drywall, or structural materials are too saturated to dry in place, we remove and dispose of them before drying the framing behind, a step that prevents mold from colonizing the cavity while the visible surface looks fine.

## Evanston Insurance Coordination

Most standard homeowner policies cover sudden and accidental water damage, a burst pipe, an appliance failure, an ice dam that forces water under roofing. What they typically exclude is long-term seepage or gradual leaks, which is why the documentation we create at the start of every job matters so much. We photograph affected materials, record moisture readings, and produce a written scope of loss that gives your adjuster the information they need to process the claim accurately. We work with most major carriers and can communicate directly with your adjuster to keep the claim moving.

For Evanston properties in condo associations or multi-unit buildings, common along the Dempster and Davis Street corridors, we're accustomed to coordinating with building managers and HOA boards alongside individual unit owners, since water damage in a shared building rarely affects just one party.

## Local Note

In Evanston's older brick two-flats and courtyard buildings, water that enters through a failed parapet or roof flashing often travels inside the wall cavity for several floors before it shows up as a stain on a first-floor ceiling. By the time a tenant notices, the brick wythe and the wood framing behind it may have been wet for weeks. When we're called to these buildings, we always check above the visible damage, not just the room where the stain appeared, because the source and the symptom are frequently separated by an entire story.
