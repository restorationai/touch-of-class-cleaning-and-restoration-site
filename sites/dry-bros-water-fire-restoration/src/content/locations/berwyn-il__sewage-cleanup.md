## Why Berwyn Homes Are Particularly Vulnerable to Sewer Backups

Berwyn sits on relatively flat, clay-heavy terrain, the kind of soil that drains slowly and puts sustained pressure on combined sewer systems during and after heavy rainfall. The Metropolitan Water Reclamation District of Greater Chicago manages the regional system, and during significant storm events, combined sewers can surcharge, meaning stormwater and sanitary waste share the same pipe and have nowhere to go but back up through the lowest fixture in a building.

The housing stock amplifies the risk. Homes built before World War II typically have clay tile or cast iron lateral lines running from the house to the city main, materials that have had decades to crack, offset, or become infiltrated by tree roots from the mature street trees common throughout Berwyn's residential blocks. A partial blockage that goes unnoticed can become a full backup the first time a washing machine and a toilet flush simultaneously during a wet spring afternoon. Basement bathrooms and laundry tubs, which are standard in Berwyn bungalows, are almost always the first point of overflow.

## Our Sewage Cleanup and Sanitization Process in Berwyn

Raw sewage is classified as Category 3 water, the most contaminated category, which means the cleanup protocol is substantially more involved than a clean water pipe burst. The process starts with containment: affected areas are isolated to prevent cross-contamination to living spaces above the basement level, which matters especially in Berwyn's two-flat and coach-house properties where a lower unit's backup can threaten an occupied upper unit.

Once contained, standing sewage is extracted using truck-mounted or portable extraction equipment capable of handling solid-laden water. Affected porous materials, drywall, insulation, carpet, and in many Berwyn basements, wood framing that sits close to the slab, are assessed and typically removed rather than dried in place, because no drying protocol reliably decontaminates material that has absorbed Category 3 water. Hard surfaces, concrete floors, and block walls are then treated with EPA-registered antimicrobial agents appropriate for sewage contamination. Structural drying with commercial dehumidifiers and air movers follows, and moisture readings are logged throughout to confirm the space reaches safe levels before any reconstruction begins.

## Local Note: Berwyn Basements and Block Wall Absorption

One thing that comes up repeatedly in Berwyn basement cleanups is the behavior of concrete masonry unit (CMU) block walls, the standard foundation material in most of the city's pre-war bungalows. Block walls are porous by nature, and when sewage water sits against them even briefly, the contaminated water wicks into the block itself. Surface cleaning alone does not resolve this. Effective sanitization requires treating the block face and, where accessible, the interior cavity, then confirming with moisture meters that the wall has returned to an acceptable baseline before sealing or painting over it. Homeowners who have had a previous backup "cleaned up" by a general contractor sometimes call back months later with persistent odor, often traced to block walls that were wiped down but never properly dried and treated.
