Berwyn's housing stock tells a story in wood, plaster, and brick, most of the city's bungalows and two-flats were built between the 1910s and 1950s, and the contents inside them often carry the same history. When a burst pipe soaks a living room or a kitchen fire leaves smoke residue embedded in upholstered furniture and family heirlooms, the belongings inside those homes deserve the same careful attention as the structure itself. Contents restoration and storage is the part of disaster recovery most homeowners don't think about until they're standing in a damaged room wondering what can be saved, and Dry Bros Water & Fire Restoration handles exactly that.

## Why Berwyn Homes Create Unique Contents Challenges

Older construction in Berwyn means older contents problems. Plaster walls and original hardwood floors are common, and when water or fire damage occurs, smoke and soot don't stay in one room, they migrate through gaps in original plaster, settle into the fibers of wool rugs and upholstered pieces, and coat the surfaces of furniture that may have been in the same family for generations. The dense, block-by-block development that characterizes Berwyn's residential neighborhoods also means homes sit close together, which can complicate staging and pack-out logistics when a crew needs to move contents out of a damaged property.

Berwyn's climate adds another layer. The Chicago metro's freeze-thaw cycle puts stress on older plumbing every winter, and when a pipe finally fails, often inside a wall or under a first-floor slab, the water doesn't just wet the floor. It wicks into the base of furniture, saturates area rugs, and reaches shelving and stored items in lower cabinets before anyone realizes there's a problem. By the time the damage is visible, contents may already have absorbed enough moisture to support mold growth within 24 to 48 hours.

## Our Contents Restoration and Storage Process in Berwyn

The process starts with a room-by-room inventory before anything is moved. Every item is photographed, catalogued, and assessed, salvageable, restorable, or unsalvageable, so there's a clear record for both the homeowner and the insurance carrier. This documentation step matters especially in older Berwyn homes where antique furniture, vintage textiles, and irreplaceable personal items are common.

Pack-out comes next. Items are carefully wrapped, boxed, and transported to a secure, climate-controlled storage facility while the structure is being dried, cleaned, or rebuilt. For furniture and soft goods affected by smoke or soot, ultrasonic cleaning, ozone treatment, and thermal fogging are among the methods used depending on the material and the type of contamination. Delicate items, oil paintings, wood veneers, upholstered antiques, are handled with methods matched to the material, not a one-size approach.

Once the home is ready, contents are returned, unwrapped, and placed. The goal is to hand the homeowner back their belongings in the same condition they were before the loss, or as close to it as the damage allows.

## Berwyn Insurance Coordination for Contents Claims

Contents losses are often the most contested part of a property claim. Carriers want itemized documentation, replacement cost values, and clear evidence of what was damaged versus what was pre-existing. The inventory and photo documentation Dry Bros creates during pack-out is designed to support that process directly, it gives adjusters what they need without requiring the homeowner to reconstruct a list from memory after a traumatic event.

If your carrier has questions about the scope of contents restoration work, the documentation travels with the claim. Homeowners should also be aware that most standard homeowners policies in Illinois cover contents restoration as part of a covered peril loss, but the burden of proof for individual items falls on the policyholder, which is exactly why a thorough pack-out inventory matters from day one.

## Local Note

Berwyn's bungalow belt, the rows of brick and frame bungalows that define much of the city's residential character, often have finished basements that families use as living space, home offices, or storage for everything from holiday decorations to furniture passed down through generations. When basement flooding occurs, those lower levels can contain some of the most sentimental and hardest-to-replace items in the home. Because basements in this area frequently have older floor drains that back up rather than drain during heavy rain events, water can enter faster than expected and reach items on low shelving before the homeowner is even aware of the problem. If you store anything of value in a Berwyn basement, keeping items on shelving at least 12 inches off the floor is a simple precaution that can make the difference between a restorable item and a total loss.
