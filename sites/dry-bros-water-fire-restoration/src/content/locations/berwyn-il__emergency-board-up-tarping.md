## Why Berwyn's Building Stock Makes Board-Up and Tarping Especially Critical

The same masonry construction that makes Berwyn's bungalows charming creates specific vulnerabilities after a loss event. Brick facades hold heat long after a fire is extinguished, and that residual heat can warp window frames and compromise the mortar around openings, meaning a window that looks intact may no longer seat a standard board without shimming. Flat and low-slope roofs are common on Berwyn's two-flats and coach houses, and they're the first to show stress after hail or high-wind events. Unlike a pitched roof where a tarp can be draped and weighted, a flat roof requires the tarp to be mechanically fastened and lapped at seams to prevent pooling, which adds steps that a crew unfamiliar with this housing type might skip.

Berwyn also sits in a climate corridor where late-spring severe weather, the kind that produces straight-line winds and large hail, can follow a house fire by only hours. Leaving a fire-damaged roof open overnight in that environment isn't a calculated risk; it's a near-certain path to secondary water damage inside walls and ceilings that were otherwise unaffected by the original fire.

## Our Emergency Board-Up and Tarping Process in Berwyn

When we arrive at a Berwyn property, the first step is a perimeter walk to identify every breach, not just the obvious ones. Fire damage in particular creates openings that aren't visible from the street: burned-through eave vents, failed skylight glazing, heat-cracked masonry around chimney chases. We document each opening with photographs before any material goes up, which matters later when your insurance adjuster is reconstructing the scope of loss.

For window and door board-up, we use exterior-grade plywood cut to fit and secured with structural screws rather than nails, nails back out in temperature swings, and Berwyn sees significant swings between seasons. For roof tarping, we use reinforced poly tarps rated for wind load, fastened with lumber battens screwed into the decking rather than simply weighted at the edges. On Berwyn's older homes, we check the condition of the roof decking before fastening, original 1920s skip sheathing is common under asphalt overlays, and it requires a different anchoring approach than modern OSB.

Every opening gets logged, every material used gets documented, and we provide a written scope to the property owner before we leave the site.
