The clay-heavy soil beneath Cicero's street grid doesn't absorb standing water quickly, which means moisture lingers under slabs and inside wall cavities long after the visible puddle is gone. ## Why Cicero Properties See Water Damage Differently Than Newer Suburbs

Cicero's housing stock is overwhelmingly pre-1960, with a large share of two-flats, bungalows, and courtyard apartment buildings constructed between the 1910s and 1940s. Those buildings were built on shallow foundations with minimal waterproofing by modern standards, and their plumbing, often a mix of original cast iron drain lines and copper or galvanized supply pipes added during mid-century updates, is at the age where failures happen without warning.

The town's combined sewer infrastructure compounds the problem. During heavy rain events, the same pipes that carry sanitary waste also carry stormwater runoff. When those systems surcharge, sewage can back up through floor drains in basements, a category of water loss that carries contamination concerns beyond a clean-water pipe break. The distinction matters because it changes how crews approach extraction, what personal protective equipment is required, and how affected materials are handled and documented.

Older plaster-and-lath walls, common throughout Cicero's bungalow belt, behave differently than modern drywall under moisture. The plaster surface can feel dry to the touch within a day or two while the wood lath behind it stays saturated for a week or more. Moisture meters that read only surface resistance will miss this, which is why thorough drying in Cicero's older homes requires both penetrating and non-penetrating sensors, and why drying timelines here often run longer than in newer construction.

## The Water Damage Restoration Process in Cicero

When a crew arrives at a Cicero property, the first priority is stopping the source if it hasn't been stopped already, shutting a supply valve, calling the water department for a main issue, or coordinating with the building owner if it's a multi-unit property. From there, the process follows a consistent sequence calibrated to what's actually wet, not just what's visibly damaged.

**Moisture mapping** comes before any equipment goes in. Thermal imaging and moisture meters identify where water has traveled behind walls, under flooring, and into subfloor assemblies. In Cicero's two-flats and courtyard buildings, water from an upper-unit leak routinely tracks along ceiling joists and appears in a completely different room on the floor below, sometimes two units away from the source.

**Water extraction** removes standing water and pulls moisture from carpet, padding, and hard flooring using truck-mounted or portable extraction equipment. The goal is to reduce the water load before drying equipment is placed, which shortens total dry time and reduces the risk of secondary mold growth, a real concern given that mold colonization can begin on wet organic materials within 24 to 48 hours under the right temperature conditions.

**Structural drying** uses a calculated combination of air movers and dehumidifiers sized to the affected area. Crews return on subsequent days to take readings, adjust equipment placement, and document drying progress. Work is not closed out until moisture readings return to normal dry standards for the material type, not just when the space looks dry.
