## Why Des Plaines Properties See Contents Damage Differently

The housing stock across Des Plaines spans several decades and construction styles. Older ranch homes and two-flats built in the postwar era often have plaster-and-lath interior walls and original hardwood floors, materials that hold moisture and smoke residue differently than modern drywall and engineered flooring. Furniture pushed against those walls during a loss event can absorb contamination from two directions at once: the direct source and the wall surface releasing stored moisture.

Des Plaines also sits within a high-frequency storm corridor. When a sump pump fails during a summer thunderstorm or a roof is breached by ice dams in January, the contents of finished basements, electronics, stored clothing, family heirlooms, holiday items, are typically the first casualties. The longer those items stay in a wet, enclosed space, the harder and more expensive recovery becomes.

## Our Contents Restoration and Storage Process in Des Plaines

The first step is a structured pack-out: every item is photographed, catalogued, and assigned a condition code before it leaves the property. This documentation matters for insurance claims and gives you a clear record of what was removed and its pre-loss state.

From there, items move to a controlled facility where cleaning and restoration methods are matched to the material. Ultrasonic cleaning works well for hard surfaces, ceramics, and metal fixtures with embedded soot. Ozone and hydroxyl treatment addresses smoke odor in soft goods. Freeze-drying is available for documents and photographs that have been saturated. Furniture with structural water damage is assessed individually, some pieces can be dried, refinished, and returned; others need a documented total-loss determination for the adjuster.

Contents that are restorable but cannot return to the home during active remediation go into climate-controlled storage. Items stay inventoried and accessible throughout the process, so you are never guessing what was taken or where it is.

## Local Note

Des Plaines has a notable concentration of older split-level and raised-ranch homes built in the 1960s and 1970s where the lower level sits partially below grade. These spaces often have drop ceilings with acoustic tile directly above finished living areas, and those tiles act like sponges during any overhead leak or flood event. When contents from these lower levels are packed out, the ceiling tile material itself is frequently a contamination source that has been dripping onto furniture and stored items for hours before anyone noticed. If your home has this configuration, mention it when you call, it changes the sequence of the pack-out and the cleaning protocol for items that were stored directly underneath.
