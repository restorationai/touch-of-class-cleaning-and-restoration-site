Evanston's mix of century-old greystones, Victorian frame houses, and mid-rise lakefront buildings means that when a fire breaks out or a storm tears through, the structural exposure isn't just a cosmetic problem, it's an immediate security and weather risk in a city where Lake Michigan can push hard rain and wind off the water with very little warning. Emergency board-up and tarping stops that exposure from becoming a second loss, sealing openings before the next weather system moves in and before opportunistic entry becomes a real concern.

## Why Evanston Properties Face Distinct Board-Up and Tarping Challenges

Evanston's housing stock skews old. A large share of the city's residential buildings predate World War II, and many of those homes were built with balloon-frame construction, a method where wall cavities run continuously from foundation to roofline. When fire or impact damages an exterior wall or window in a balloon-frame house, the opening isn't just a hole in one room; it's a pathway that connects directly to the attic and every floor in between. Standard plywood sizing and fastening patterns used in newer construction don't always translate cleanly to these older structures, where window openings are irregular and exterior trim is often ornate and fragile.

The lakefront climate compounds the urgency. Evanston sits at the edge of Lake Michigan, and the city experiences pressure-driven rain events that can drive water horizontally into an unsecured opening within minutes of a storm arriving. Roof damage that might stay dry overnight in an inland suburb can be soaked through by morning in a neighborhood close to the lake. Tarping needs to account for wind uplift, not just vertical rainfall, which means ballast, edge sealing, and anchor points matter more here than in calmer microclimates.

## Our Board-Up and Tarping Process, Calibrated for Evanston Conditions

The process starts with a rapid assessment of the structural opening, its size, the material around it, and what's immediately behind it. For fire-damaged properties, that means checking whether the surrounding framing is structurally sound enough to accept fasteners before any board is cut. For storm or impact damage, it means identifying secondary openings that aren't always obvious from the street, including soffit breaches and damaged dormer flashing that can let water in even after the primary opening is covered.

For window and door openings, we cut plywood to fit and secure it with structural screws into sound framing, not just into damaged casing. For roof openings, heavy-duty polyethylene tarps are anchored with lumber battens and secured at the ridge and eaves to resist wind uplift, a critical detail on Evanston's steeper-pitched roofs, where a loose tarp edge can catch wind and peel back the entire cover. We document every opening photographically before and after boarding, which matters when the insurance claim is filed.

## Insurance and HOA Coordination in Evanston

Evanston has a significant share of condominiums and multi-unit buildings, particularly in the areas closer to the CTA Purple Line stops and along Chicago Avenue, where older apartment buildings have been converted to condo associations over the decades. Board-up work on a condo unit often involves both the unit owner's policy and the association's master policy, and the scope of what each covers, interior versus exterior, common elements versus individual unit, varies by association documents. We photograph and document the work in a format that supports both claim tracks, so neither the homeowner nor the association is left without the evidence they need.

For single-family properties, most standard homeowners' policies cover emergency protective measures as part of the covered loss. We provide itemized documentation of materials and labor that your adjuster can work from directly.

## Local Note

Evanston's older greystones and brick two-flats often have limestone or terra cotta decorative elements around window openings that are irreplaceable and easy to crack if a crew drives fasteners without checking the substrate first. On these properties, we use standoff framing techniques that anchor into the masonry jamb rather than the decorative surround, a small adjustment that prevents secondary damage to architectural details that are genuinely difficult and expensive to restore. It's the kind of thing that matters in a city where the building stock has real character worth protecting.
