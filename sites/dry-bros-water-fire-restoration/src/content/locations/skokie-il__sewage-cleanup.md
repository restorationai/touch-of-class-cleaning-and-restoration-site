Skokie sits on some of the flattest, most clay-dense ground in Cook County, and that geology matters the moment a sewer line backs up into your basement. Clay soil drains slowly, so when the Metropolitan Water Reclamation District's combined sewer system surges during a heavy rain event, the pressure has nowhere to go but backward through floor drains and laundry tubs. Dry Bros Water & Fire Restoration responds to sewage backup calls throughout Skokie, handling raw sewage removal and full sanitization so the space is safe to occupy again.

## Why Skokie Properties See Sewage Backup Problems

Skokie's housing stock skews heavily toward mid-century construction, brick ranches and two-flats built between the late 1940s and the 1970s. Many of those homes have original cast-iron or clay-tile lateral lines that are now 50 to 70 years old. Cast iron corrodes from the inside out; clay tile joints shift as tree roots and frost cycles work on them. Either condition narrows the pipe's effective diameter over time, and a single heavy storm can push the system past its limit.

The village also sits within the combined sewer service area, meaning stormwater and sanitary sewage share the same underground infrastructure. When that system surcharges, which it does during the intense summer thunderstorms the Chicago region sees regularly, sewage doesn't just back up into one home. Whole blocks can be affected at the same time, which means restoration crews are in high demand exactly when you need one most.

Older homes in Skokie frequently have floor drains in the utility room that connect directly to the sanitary lateral with no backwater valve installed. If your home predates the village's more recent requirements around backwater valve installation, that open drain is the path of least resistance when the main backs up.

## Our Sewage Cleanup and Sanitization Process in Skokie

Raw sewage is classified as Category 3 water, the most contaminated classification in the industry, which means the removal and sanitization process follows a strict sequence, not a shortcut.

**Containment first.** We isolate the affected area to prevent cross-contamination from spreading to finished living spaces. In Skokie's typical finished basement layout, that often means sealing the stairwell and establishing a decontamination zone at the perimeter.

**Extraction and solid waste removal.** Standing sewage and saturated materials are removed using truck-mounted extraction equipment. Porous materials, carpet, pad, drywall below the flood line, insulation, are bagged and removed per Cook County waste handling requirements for contaminated materials.

**Cleaning and disinfection.** Hard surfaces are cleaned, then treated with EPA-registered disinfectants effective against the bacteria and pathogens present in raw sewage. This step is not optional and cannot be shortened, contact time with the disinfectant matters as much as the product itself.

**Structural drying.** Once contaminated materials are out and surfaces are disinfected, we set drying equipment calibrated to the actual conditions in the space. Skokie basements tend to run humid in summer, which affects drying calculations, ambient moisture in the air slows evaporation, so equipment placement and airflow matter more than they would in an arid climate.

**Clearance check.** Before we close out, moisture readings confirm the structural assembly is dry. Residual moisture left behind in a basement slab or wall cavity is how mold colonizes within days of a sewage event.

## Skokie Insurance Coordination

Sewer backup coverage is a separate endorsement on most homeowner policies, it is not automatically included in a standard HO-3. If you added the endorsement, we document the loss with photographs, moisture readings, and a written scope of work formatted for insurance review. We work with most major carriers and can communicate directly with your adjuster to keep the claim moving. If you are not sure whether your policy includes sewer backup coverage, your declarations page will list it as a rider or endorsement; it is worth checking before you assume you are paying out of pocket.

## Local Note

Skokie's village code has encouraged backwater valve installation for years, and some older homes have had them retrofitted, but the valve only protects you if it has been serviced. A backwater valve with a stuck or corroded flapper provides almost no protection and can actually trap sewage in the lateral during a surcharge event. If your home had a sewage backup despite having a backwater valve, the valve itself should be inspected by a licensed plumber as part of the recovery process. We flag this routinely when we see it during cleanup, it is the kind of detail that prevents the next backup, not just addresses the current one.
