Des Plaines sits in a part of northeastern Illinois where lake-effect moisture, clay-heavy soil, and a housing stock that stretches back to the postwar boom create conditions where odors don't just linger, they embed. Smoke from a kitchen fire soaks into the plaster and lath of a 1950s bungalow differently than it does in a newer drywall build. Flood-driven mildew works its way into basement block walls that have been absorbing Cook County groundwater for decades. When a smell won't leave no matter how many windows you open or candles you burn, the source is almost always structural, and that's where professional deodorization starts.

## Why Des Plaines Properties Hold Odors Longer

The older residential neighborhoods throughout Des Plaines, many built between the late 1940s and early 1970s, feature construction materials that are exceptionally porous by today's standards. Original plaster walls, wood-framed cavities with minimal vapor barriers, and unfinished basement walls made of concrete block all act like sponges. Smoke particles and microbial off-gassing from water damage don't just coat the surface; they migrate into the substrate.

Cook County's climate compounds this. Humid summers slow the natural evaporation that might otherwise help dissipate odor molecules, while winter heating cycles cause those same molecules to volatilize and recirculate through ductwork. If your HVAC system was running during a fire or a sewage backup, the odor has almost certainly traveled well beyond the room where the loss occurred.

## Our Odor Removal and Deodorization Process in Des Plaines

Effective deodorization isn't a single treatment, it's a sequence calibrated to the odor source, the affected materials, and the building's construction.

**Assessment and source removal** come first. No deodorization method works if charred material, saturated insulation, or contaminated debris is still present. We identify and remove the primary odor source before any equipment is deployed.

**Thermal fogging** is particularly effective in older Des Plaines homes because the fogging agent penetrates the same porous pathways the odor molecules used to get in, reaching inside wall cavities, subfloor assemblies, and the gaps around older window frames that smoke loves to exploit.

**Hydroxyl deodorization** is our preferred method when occupants or pets need to return to the property sooner. Hydroxyl generators produce oxidizing radicals that break down odor-causing compounds at the molecular level without requiring the space to be vacated for extended periods.

**Ozone treatment**, when conditions call for it, is deployed in unoccupied spaces, it's one of the most aggressive tools available for heavy smoke odor, but it requires proper airing-out time before re-occupancy, something we coordinate carefully with homeowners.

**Duct and HVAC deodorization** closes the loop. In homes where forced-air systems circulated smoke or mold spores, treating the living space without addressing the ductwork guarantees the odor returns with the next heating cycle.
