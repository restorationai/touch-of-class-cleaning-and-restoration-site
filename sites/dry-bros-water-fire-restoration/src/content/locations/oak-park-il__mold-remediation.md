## Why Oak Park Properties See Mold Issues

Cook County's clay-heavy soil drains slowly. After a heavy rain, that water doesn't disappear, it sits against foundation walls, seeps through mortar joints, and pools in crawl spaces beneath homes built long before waterproofing membranes were standard. Oak Park's grid of mature tree canopy is beautiful, but root systems that have grown for decades can compromise drain tile and redirect surface water toward foundations rather than away from them.

The village's housing stock skews heavily toward pre-1940 construction. Balloon-frame walls, where studs run continuously from the foundation sill to the roof, create uninterrupted vertical channels for water to travel. A roof leak or an ice dam along a gutter line doesn't stay in the attic; it can wick downward through wall cavities and show up as mold behind first-floor baseboards weeks later, long after the original water source has dried. Plaster-on-lath walls compound the problem: the surface can feel dry to the touch while the wood lath behind it remains saturated and actively supporting mold growth.

## Our Mold Remediation Process in Oak Park

Remediation here starts with containment, not cleaning. Before any disturbed material becomes airborne, the affected area is sealed with poly sheeting and placed under negative air pressure using HEPA-filtered air scrubbers. This matters especially in Oak Park's attached and semi-detached homes, where a shared wall with a neighbor's unit means spores can travel through gaps around plumbing penetrations or electrical chases if containment is skipped.

Once containment is established, affected materials, drywall, insulation, wood framing, are removed to the extent necessary to reach clean, unaffected substrate. In plaster-wall homes, this step requires more precision: plaster is dense and often salvageable if the lath behind it is structurally sound and the moisture reading has returned to baseline. We use moisture meters and thermal imaging to map the actual boundary of the affected zone rather than guessing by visual inspection alone.

After removal, surfaces are treated with an EPA-registered antimicrobial agent and allowed to dry fully before any reconstruction begins. Final air quality is verified before the containment barrier comes down.

## Local Note

Oak Park homes built before 1978 frequently contain lead paint, and many also contain asbestos in floor tile, pipe insulation, or textured ceiling coatings. When mold remediation requires disturbing those materials, cutting into a plaster ceiling, removing vinyl floor tile to reach a wet subfloor, the scope of work changes. Abatement of lead or asbestos is regulated separately from mold remediation under Illinois EPA guidelines, and the two scopes cannot simply be combined into one crew's work without proper handling protocols. When we scope a job in an older Oak Park home and suspect regulated materials may be present, we flag it before work begins so the homeowner isn't caught off guard mid-project.
