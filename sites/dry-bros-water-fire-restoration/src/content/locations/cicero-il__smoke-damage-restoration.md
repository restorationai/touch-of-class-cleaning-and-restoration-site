## Why Cicero's Housing Stock Makes Smoke Damage Harder to Clear

The same brick-and-plaster construction that gives Cicero's bungalow belt its durability creates a specific challenge after a fire. Plaster walls, common in homes built before the postwar drywall era, are porous in a way that modern wallboard simply isn't. Smoke particles and the volatile organic compounds (VOCs) that carry that persistent burnt smell absorb into plaster and lath at a molecular level. Surface wiping alone won't remove them. The odor will return, especially in summer when heat drives those compounds back out of the walls.

Cicero also sits in a climate zone that swings from humid, hot summers to frigid winters with low indoor humidity. That thermal cycling causes older building materials to expand and contract, which can reactivate embedded smoke odors months after a fire, a frustrating experience for homeowners who thought the problem was resolved. Addressing smoke damage here means treating the structure, not just the surfaces.

## Our Smoke Damage Restoration Process in Cicero

Every smoke damage job starts with a structured assessment, identifying the fire's origin point, the type of materials that burned (synthetic materials like carpet and vinyl produce a different, stickier soot than wood or paper), and how far smoke traveled through the home's duct system and wall cavities.

From there, the work follows a deliberate sequence:

- **Dry soot removal** before any wet cleaning, wiping soot while it's wet drives it deeper into porous surfaces.
- **HEPA vacuuming** of all affected surfaces, including inside HVAC registers, which act as smoke distribution highways in forced-air systems common to Cicero's older homes.
- **Chemical sponge and alkaline cleaning** of walls, ceilings, and trim, with specific products matched to the soot type.
- **Thermal fogging or hydroxyl generation** to neutralize odor molecules embedded in materials that can't be physically cleaned, inside wall cavities, under flooring, in attic spaces.
- **Encapsulation or selective demolition** of materials too saturated with smoke residue to restore, followed by odor-sealing primer before any repainting.

In Cicero's older two-flat and three-flat buildings, smoke commonly travels between units through shared wall cavities and stairwells, a factor that affects the scope of work and matters for insurance documentation.
