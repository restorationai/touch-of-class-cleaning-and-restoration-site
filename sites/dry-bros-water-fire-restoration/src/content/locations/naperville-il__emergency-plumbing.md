## Why Naperville Properties See Plumbing Emergencies

Naperville sits in a climate band where hard freezes arrive fast and linger. Homes built in the 1970s and 1980s, a substantial portion of the city's west-side residential stock, were often plumbed with copper that has decades of thermal cycling behind it. Solder joints weaken over time, and a single night below 10°F can be the event that finally causes a failure that has been building for years.

Newer construction in master-planned communities closer to Route 59 brings a different risk profile. PEX supply lines are more freeze-resistant, but pressure-balancing failures, improperly insulated garage walls, and shared utility chases in attached townhomes can still produce fast, high-volume leaks. In attached units especially, a burst pipe in one home can push water into a neighboring unit's walls before either homeowner knows something is wrong.

The DuPage County soil profile, heavy clay in many areas, means drainage away from foundations is slower than homeowners expect. When a pipe failure coincides with saturated ground after a spring thaw, water finds the path of least resistance into crawl spaces and slab edges.
