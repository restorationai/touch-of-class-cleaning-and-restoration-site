## Why Evanston Properties See Basement Flooding

The soil profile under most of Evanston is glacial lake clay, dense, low-permeability material that doesn't drain well. When rainfall saturates the ground faster than it can move laterally, hydrostatic pressure builds against foundation walls and floor slabs. Older homes with block or rubble-stone foundations are particularly vulnerable because the mortar joints have had decades to soften and crack, creating pathways for water that weren't there when the house was built.

Evanston's combined sewer system, which carries both stormwater and sanitary flow in the same pipe, can also back up during intense rain events. When the main is overwhelmed, water can reverse through floor drains and laundry connections in the basement. That type of intrusion carries contamination concerns that go beyond simple water removal and require a different cleanup protocol than a clean-water pipe burst.

The city's housing stock is another factor. A large share of Evanston's residential buildings date from the late 1800s through the 1940s, which means full masonry basements, older drain tile systems that may have collapsed or silted over, and in some cases original clay sewer laterals that are past their service life. These conditions don't cause flooding on their own, but they raise the risk profile significantly when a storm or plumbing failure occurs.

## Our Basement Flooding Cleanup Process in Evanston

The first step on any flooded basement call is assessing the water source and contamination category before equipment goes in. A clean-water supply-line break is handled differently from a sewer backup or groundwater intrusion, and the category determines what personal protective equipment the crew uses, how materials are handled, and what documentation goes to your insurance carrier.

Once the source is identified and stopped or contained, standing water is extracted using truck-mounted or portable extraction units. In finished basements, that often means pulling carpet and pad immediately, saturated carpet acts as a reservoir that slows structural drying significantly. Drywall that has wicked moisture above the flood line is typically cut and removed rather than dried in place, because leaving wet drywall behind creates conditions where mold can colonize within days.

After extraction, high-capacity air movers and dehumidifiers are positioned to dry the structural assembly, concrete slab, framing, subfloor, and any remaining wall cavities. Moisture readings are taken at each visit and logged so there's a documented drying record, which matters when the claim goes to your adjuster.

## Local Note: Clay Soil and Hidden Saturation

One thing that catches Evanston homeowners off guard is how long the ground stays saturated after a significant rain event. Because the clay soil drains so slowly, hydrostatic pressure against the foundation can persist for days after the storm passes, meaning a basement that appears to have stopped taking on water may still be at risk of additional seepage while the surrounding soil is still holding moisture. If your sump pump is running constantly two days after a storm, that's not a malfunction; it's the clay releasing water gradually. We account for this when setting up drying equipment, because pulling equipment too early in this soil environment often leads to secondary moisture problems.
