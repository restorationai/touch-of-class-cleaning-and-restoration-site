## Why Oak Park Properties Hold Odors Longer

The same construction quality that makes Oak Park homes architecturally significant makes odor remediation more demanding. Plaster-and-lath walls, standard in homes built before the 1950s, are porous in a way modern drywall is not. Smoke particulates and microbial off-gases penetrate the plaster face, travel through the lath cavity, and settle into the structural framing behind it. You can repaint the surface and still smell the fire three months later.

Oak Park's climate compounds this. Cook County winters are long and cold, meaning homes stay buttoned up for months, windows sealed, ventilation minimal. A single odor event (a cooking fire, a sump pump failure, a slow gas leak near a water heater) can saturate interior air and soft furnishings before anyone opens a window. Summer humidity swings in the opposite direction, feeding mold growth in the crawl spaces and unfinished basements common in the area's older housing stock. Both extremes make passive ventilation an unreliable deodorization strategy.

## Our Odor Removal and Deodorization Process in Oak Park

Effective odor removal is a sequence, not a single treatment. We begin with a thorough source assessment, locating the origin of the odor rather than masking it. In Oak Park's older homes, that often means checking inside wall cavities, beneath original hardwood floors, and in the mechanical spaces where cast-iron drain lines or aging HVAC ductwork can harbor years of biological buildup.

Once the source is identified and any contaminated material is removed or treated, we select the appropriate deodorization technology for the structure:

- **Thermal fogging** penetrates porous surfaces, plaster, wood trim, upholstered furniture, by converting deodorizing solution into a fine fog that follows the same pathways smoke traveled. It's particularly effective in the kind of compartmentalized floor plans common in Oak Park's two-flats and Prairie-style homes.
- **Hydroxyl deodorization** uses UV-generated hydroxyl radicals to break odor molecules apart at the molecular level. Unlike ozone, hydroxyl generators are safe to run in occupied or partially occupied spaces, which matters when a tenant is still on-site or contents can't be relocated.
- **Ozone treatment** is reserved for unoccupied spaces with severe smoke or biological odor. It's aggressive and fast, but the space must be vacated and properly ventilated before re-entry, a step we manage and document.
- **HEPA air scrubbing** runs throughout the process, capturing particulates that carry odor before they resettle on cleaned surfaces.

The right method, or combination of methods, depends on the odor type, the building materials involved, and how long the odor source was active before remediation began.

## Local Note: What Older Oak Park Homes Do to Odor Timelines

One thing that surprises homeowners in Oak Park's historic districts: odor can return days after an initial treatment if the underlying material wasn't fully addressed. This is especially common in homes with original horsehair plaster, which can hold moisture and organic compounds deep in its matrix. A surface-level fogging treatment may neutralize the ambient smell, only for the odor to re-emerge as the plaster warms and off-gasses again. We account for this by scheduling a follow-up assessment after the initial treatment, not because the first pass failed, but because the building's age demands a two-stage confirmation that the source is truly resolved, not just temporarily suppressed.
