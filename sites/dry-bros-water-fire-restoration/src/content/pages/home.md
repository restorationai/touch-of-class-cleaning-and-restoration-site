When water soaks through your subfloor, smoke odor settles into every surface, or a storm tears through your roof, the damage compounds fast. Dry Bros Water & Fire Restoration handles water damage, fire and smoke damage, and storm damage restoration for homeowners and property managers across Chicago and the surrounding area, getting the right crew and equipment on-site before a manageable problem becomes a structural one.

## Services we handle

**Water damage restoration**, Whether it's a burst pipe in a vintage Chicago two-flat or a basement that took on water during a heavy rain event, standing water needs to come out quickly. Moisture that lingers behind drywall or under hardwood can stay elevated long after the surface feels dry.

**Fire and smoke damage restoration**, Smoke residue is acidic and keeps working after the flames are out, etching glass, corroding metal, and embedding itself in porous materials. Effective restoration means addressing both the visible char and the invisible odor that clings to framing, insulation, and HVAC systems.

**Storm damage restoration**, Chicago's freeze-thaw cycles, lake-effect weather, and severe spring storms create a predictable set of problems: ice dams, wind-driven rain through compromised rooflines, and water intrusion through foundations. Prompt board-up and tarping limits secondary damage while a full restoration plan comes together.

## Why homeowners and property managers call us

Restoration is not the same as remodeling. The goal is to stop active damage, dry or clean affected materials to measurable standards, and document everything so your insurance claim reflects the actual scope of the loss. That documentation piece matters more than most homeowners realize until they're sitting across from an adjuster.

Dry Bros works directly with insurance carriers, photographing affected materials, preparing detailed scope reports, and billing most major carriers directly so you're not carrying the full cost out of pocket while waiting for reimbursement. You focus on your family or your tenants; the paperwork side stays on our end.

Chicago's older housing stock adds complexity that generic contractors often underestimate. Homes built before the mid-20th century frequently have plaster-and-lath walls, balloon-frame construction, and cast-iron or galvanized plumbing, all of which behave differently during a water or fire loss than modern materials do. Knowing what's behind the walls before equipment gets placed is part of doing this correctly.
