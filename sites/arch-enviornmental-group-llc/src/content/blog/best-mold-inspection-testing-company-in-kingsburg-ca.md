## What Should a Mold Inspection in Kingsburg Actually Include?

A professional mold inspection includes more than a visual walk-through. A thorough inspection covers visual assessment of all accessible areas, moisture mapping with a calibrated pin or pinless meter, air sampling with spore trap cassettes (at least one outdoor baseline plus one per suspect area), and surface sampling where visible growth is present. The [EPA's guidance on mold](https://www.epa.gov/mold) recommends that any visible mold larger than 10 square feet be assessed by a qualified professional before remediation begins.

The report you receive should identify the mold species or genera detected (Cladosporium and Penicillium/Aspergillus are common background molds; Stachybotrys and Chaetomium indicate chronic moisture and are more concerning), the spore concentration compared to the outdoor baseline, the moisture source driving growth, and a written scope of what needs to happen next. That last piece matters: a good inspection report gives a remediator a clear starting point and gives your insurance adjuster the documentation they need.

In Kingsburg's climate, the most common moisture sources driving indoor mold are roof leaks after winter storms, HVAC condensation in attic spaces during the summer cooling season, and crawl space humidity from the Kings River floodplain soils. An inspector who knows the region knows where to look first.

## How to Choose: Questions to Ask Before You Book

Before you schedule any mold inspection in Kingsburg, ask these questions:

1. **What certifications does the inspector hold?** A Certified Industrial Hygienist (CIH), Certified Mold Inspector (CMI), or a firm with a NIOSH 582 Microscopist on staff can analyze samples at a higher level of precision than a general inspector with a weekend course.
2. **Do they perform the sampling and the analysis, or do they outsource to a third-party lab?** Outsourcing is common and fine, but ask which lab and what the turnaround is. For real estate transactions with a closing deadline, turnaround matters.
3. **Will the report include species identification and spore counts?** A report that only says "mold present" is not useful for insurance or remediation scoping.
4. **Do they have a conflict of interest?** A company that both inspects and remediates has a financial incentive to find problems. A dedicated inspection firm with no remediation arm gives you an unbiased assessment.
5. **Are they licensed and insured in California?** California does not currently license mold inspectors at the state level, but the firm should carry general liability and errors and omissions coverage.
