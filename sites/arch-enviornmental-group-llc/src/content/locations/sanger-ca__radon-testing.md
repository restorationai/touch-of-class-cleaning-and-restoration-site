Homes here, many of them built during the agricultural boom decades of the mid-twentieth century, often sit on foundations that were never designed with soil-gas barriers in mind. ## Why Sanger Properties See Radon Concerns

Radon forms naturally when uranium in soil and rock decays. In the San Joaquin Valley, the alluvial fan deposits that underlie much of Fresno County create variable radon potential, pockets of higher-uranium parent material can produce elevated soil-gas concentrations that migrate upward through foundation cracks, utility penetrations, and sump openings. Sanger's older housing stock compounds the risk: homes built before the 1980s predate modern vapor retarder requirements, meaning there's often little between the soil and your living space except an aging concrete slab or open-vent crawl space.

California does not currently mandate radon disclosure in real estate transactions the way some other states do, which means many Sanger homeowners have simply never tested. The EPA recommends action at or above 4 pCi/L, a threshold that can be reached in Central Valley homes without any visible sign of a problem. Radon is colorless and odorless; the only way to know your level is to measure it.

## Our Radon Testing Process in Sanger

Arch Environmental Group uses continuous radon monitoring equipment, electronic devices that log radon concentrations hour by hour over a 48-hour minimum test period. Unlike passive charcoal canisters that return a single averaged number, continuous monitors produce a time-resolved graph that can reveal whether elevated readings correlate with specific conditions: slab heating cycles during hot Valley afternoons, overnight temperature inversions that suppress ventilation, or HVAC cycles that change interior pressure.

For the test to be valid, closed-house conditions must be maintained, windows and exterior doors kept closed except for normal entry and exit, for at least 12 hours before the monitor is placed and throughout the test. We walk through your home before placement to identify the lowest livable level (typically a bedroom or family room on the ground floor or basement), confirm the monitor location meets EPA protocol, and schedule pickup and report delivery on a timeline that works around your schedule.

Once results are in hand, we provide a written report with the hourly data, the average concentration, and a plain-language explanation of what the number means relative to EPA guidelines. If levels are elevated, we can discuss what mitigation options typically look like, though mitigation itself is a separate scope performed by licensed mitigators.

## Equipment & Methods We Use for Radon

The continuous monitors we deploy are calibrated electronic devices, not the mail-in kits sold at hardware stores. Calibration records matter: a monitor that hasn't been verified against a known standard can read high or low by a meaningful margin, which is consequential when the action level is 4 pCi/L and your result comes back at 3.8 or 4.3. Our equipment is maintained and calibrated per manufacturer specifications, and calibration documentation is available on request.

## Local Note

Sanger's summer heat creates a condition worth knowing about: as daytime temperatures push into the triple digits and homes seal up with air conditioning running, interior air pressure dynamics shift. Tightly closed, mechanically cooled homes can develop slight negative pressure relative to the soil beneath them, which draws soil gas, including radon, upward more aggressively than in a naturally ventilated house. This means a summer test in a well-sealed Sanger home may reflect a somewhat different exposure pattern than a winter test when windows are cracked. We account for this when reviewing your results and can note seasonal context in the written report.
