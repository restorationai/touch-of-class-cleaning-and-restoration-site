## Why Porterville Properties Carry Distinct Environmental Due Diligence Considerations

Tulare County's agricultural economy has shaped land use in and around Porterville for well over a century. Orchards, packing sheds, fuel storage for farm equipment, pesticide mixing areas, and light industrial operations have occupied parcels that today look like straightforward commercial real estate. That layered history is exactly what a Phase 1 Environmental Site Assessment is designed to uncover: historical aerial photographs, Sanborn fire insurance maps, regulatory database searches, and a thorough site reconnaissance can surface recognized environmental conditions that a visual walkthrough alone would miss.

The region's soil profile adds another layer of complexity. The valley's fine-grained alluvial soils can retain petroleum hydrocarbons and agricultural chemicals at depth while appearing unremarkable at the surface. When a Phase 1 identifies a potential concern, a Phase 2 Environmental Site Assessment, involving soil borings, groundwater sampling, and laboratory analysis, is often the next step before a lender or investor will proceed. Understanding which parcels warrant that follow-on investigation, and which do not, is where experienced assessment work saves clients time and money.

## Our Environmental Site Assessment Process in Porterville

A Phase 1 ESA follows the ASTM E1527-21 standard and produces a report that satisfies the All Appropriate Inquiries rule under CERCLA, protecting buyers from inherited liability when the process is done correctly. The work involves four core components: records review, site reconnaissance, interviews with owners and occupants, and a written report with findings and conclusions.

For Porterville properties, the records review pays particular attention to agricultural chemical storage, underground storage tank registrations with the State Water Resources Control Board, and any prior regulatory action through the Tulare County Environmental Health Division. The site reconnaissance includes a physical walkthrough of the parcel and adjoining properties, looking for staining, stressed vegetation, vent pipes, fill areas, or other indicators that something may have been stored or disposed of on-site.

If a Phase 2 is warranted, soil and groundwater samples are collected at locations informed by the Phase 1 findings, analyzed by a certified laboratory, and compared against applicable California Regional Water Quality Control Board screening levels. The resulting report gives buyers, sellers, and lenders a defensible picture of what is present, at what concentrations, and what, if anything, it means for the intended use of the property.
