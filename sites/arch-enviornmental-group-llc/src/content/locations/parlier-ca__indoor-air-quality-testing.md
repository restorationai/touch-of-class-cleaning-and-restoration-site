## Why Parlier Properties See Air Quality Issues

The San Joaquin Valley already carries some of the highest ambient particulate matter concentrations in the country, and Parlier's position among active agricultural fields means that pesticide drift, fungal spores from disturbed soil, and fine dust from harvest operations can work their way into homes through gaps around windows, doors, and older duct systems. Many homes in the area were built during the 1950s through 1970s using construction materials and ventilation standards that predate modern indoor air quality awareness. Attic insulation from that era may contain vermiculite or fibrous materials; older floor tiles and pipe insulation can harbor asbestos; and lead-based paint on interior trim is common in homes built before 1978. Any one of these factors can elevate the risk profile of a home's air, and in Parlier's climate, where windows stay closed for air conditioning through the long summer, contaminants that enter or off-gas have nowhere to go.

## Our Indoor Air Quality Testing Process in Parlier

A thorough air quality inspection is not a single swab or a consumer-grade monitor left on a countertop. The process begins with a visual walkthrough to identify potential source areas: water staining that may signal mold colonization behind walls, deteriorating insulation, HVAC components that have not been serviced in years, or building materials that warrant closer examination. From there, air samples are collected using calibrated pumps and cassettes at multiple locations inside the home and at least one outdoor reference point, because understanding what is elevated indoors requires knowing the baseline outside. Bulk samples of suspect materials may also be collected if the visual assessment warrants it. Samples are submitted to an accredited laboratory, and results are reviewed by our team, which holds certifications as a Certified Asbestos Consultant, Lead Inspector Assessor, and NIOSH 582 Microscopist. That combination of credentials matters: it means the person interpreting your results is trained to identify asbestos fiber types under microscopy, not just flag a number on a printout.

## Equipment and Methods We Use for Air Quality

For mold and fungal spore assessment, we use spore trap cassettes analyzed by phase contrast or polarized light microscopy, depending on what the sample warrants. One pattern that comes up repeatedly in San Joaquin Valley agricultural communities is the presence of Valley Fever spores, caused by the Coccidioides fungus, which lives in the dry, alkaline soil common throughout Fresno County. While standard indoor air quality testing panels do not routinely screen for Coccidioides (that is a medical and public health matter), homeowners in Parlier who are concerned about fungal exposure should mention it when scheduling so the inspection scope can be discussed appropriately.
