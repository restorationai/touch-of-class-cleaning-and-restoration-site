## Why Hanford Properties See Elevated Mold Risk

The Central Valley's climate is often described as hot and dry, but that framing misses the nuance that matters for mold. Hanford regularly experiences dense tule fog from late fall through early spring, and that fog deposits surface moisture on rooflines, attic vents, and exterior walls night after night. When that moisture finds a path inside, through aging caulk, a slow roof penetration, or a crawl space vent that doesn't seal properly, it doesn't take long for spore counts to climb. The IICRC S520 standard notes that mold can begin colonizing a wet cellulosic surface within 24 to 48 hours under the right temperature and humidity conditions, and Hanford's mild winters keep interior temperatures in exactly that range for months at a time.

Housing stock in the area also plays a role. Many homes in and around Hanford were built during the postwar agricultural expansion of the 1950s through 1970s, when vapor barriers in crawl spaces were inconsistent and attic insulation was often installed without adequate ventilation baffles. Those construction details age poorly in a valley climate, and they're worth inspecting closely.

## Our Mold Inspection and Testing Process

A mold assessment is not a visual scan with a flashlight. The process begins with a structured walk-through to identify moisture pathways, plumbing penetrations, HVAC condensate lines, roof-to-wall intersections, and crawl space conditions. Moisture readings are taken at suspect surfaces using calibrated instruments, and any area showing elevated readings or visible growth is documented photographically before sampling begins.

Air sampling for mold is conducted using calibrated spore trap cassettes placed at breathing height in affected rooms and in at least one outdoor control location. The outdoor sample establishes a baseline for what's naturally present in Hanford's ambient air, which in agricultural areas can include elevated counts of certain outdoor mold genera during harvest season. That context matters when interpreting lab results. Bulk or swab samples may be collected from visible growth to identify the specific genus and species present. All samples are submitted to an accredited third-party laboratory, and results are typically returned within a few business days. The final written report details findings, sampling data, and recommended next steps in plain language.

## Local Note

One detail that comes up repeatedly in the Hanford area: homes with evaporative coolers, swamp coolers, rather than refrigerated air conditioning. Evaporative cooling works by pushing humid air through the home, which is efficient in a dry heat but means the interior humidity rises with the cooler's operation. In a home that already has a slow moisture intrusion behind a wall or in a closet adjacent to the cooler's supply duct, that added humidity can accelerate mold colonization significantly. If your home uses evaporative cooling and you're investigating a mold concern, that system's duct routing and pad housing are worth including in the inspection scope.
