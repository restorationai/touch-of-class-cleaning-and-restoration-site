## Why Minkler Properties See Radon Concerns

Radon is a colorless, odorless gas produced when uranium in soil and rock breaks down. The Sierra Nevada foothills have long been associated with granitic and metamorphic parent material, both of which tend to produce higher uranium concentrations than the flat alluvial soils deeper in the San Joaquin Valley. Homes in Minkler and the broader Fresno County foothill corridor often sit on cut-and-fill lots or have crawl spaces that open directly to disturbed soil, giving radon a relatively short path into living areas. Seasonal pressure differences, common in foothill climates where nighttime temperatures drop sharply even in summer, can pull soil gases inward through foundation cracks and utility penetrations. The result is that radon levels in house can fluctuate significantly between seasons, which is one reason a short-term test alone does not always capture the full picture.

## Equipment and Methods We Use for Radon

Electronic continuous monitors are our primary tool for radon test for home work in this region. Unlike passive charcoal canisters that are mailed to a lab and return a single number, continuous monitors record concentration data at set intervals throughout the test window. This matters in foothill settings because a home that reads at 3.8 pCi/L on average may be spiking above 6 pCi/L during overnight hours when the building depressurizes. Knowing the pattern, not just the average, helps a mitigation contractor design a sub-slab depressurization system that actually addresses the source rather than one sized for a theoretical average.

All equipment is calibrated on a regular maintenance schedule. We document device placement with photographs and record foundation conditions, window and door status, and HVAC operation during the test period, all of which are required for a defensible radon inspection result.
