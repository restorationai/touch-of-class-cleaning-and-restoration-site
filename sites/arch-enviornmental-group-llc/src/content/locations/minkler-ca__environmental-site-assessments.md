## Why Minkler Properties Warrant Close Environmental Scrutiny

The San Joaquin Valley's agricultural legacy creates a specific set of environmental concerns that differ from urban or suburban markets. Minkler and the surrounding Fresno County foothill corridor have historically supported citrus groves, stone fruit orchards, and row crops, all of which relied on organochlorine pesticides, fumigants, and nitrogen-heavy fertilizers applied over many decades. These compounds do not simply disappear. Chlorinated solvents from equipment maintenance, petroleum hydrocarbons from fuel storage, and legacy pesticide residues can persist in the shallow soils and perched groundwater that characterize this part of the valley.

Rural parcels in this region also frequently contain aging above-ground and underground storage tanks associated with irrigation pump stations and farm equipment. Tanks installed before modern secondary-containment requirements are a common recognized environmental condition (REC) that surfaces during Phase 1 review. Any lender underwriting a transaction on Fresno County agricultural or transitional land will expect that history to be documented.

## Our Environmental Site Assessment Process in Minkler

A Phase 1 ESA follows the ASTM E1527-21 standard, which defines the scope of inquiry any environmental professional must complete. For Minkler properties, that process begins with a thorough review of historical aerial photographs, Sanborn maps where applicable, regulatory agency databases, and county records that document prior uses of the subject parcel and adjoining land. Agricultural areas like this one often show use transitions, orchard to residential, farmstead to light commercial, that compress multiple risk profiles onto a single site.

Site reconnaissance in Minkler means walking the property with attention to staining patterns, stressed vegetation, vent pipes, fill ports, and the kind of subtle surface evidence that points to subsurface conditions. The written ESA report documents findings, identifies RECs, and, when warranted, recommends Phase 2 investigation.

When a Phase 2 ESA is needed, soil borings and groundwater sampling are collected and submitted to a California-certified laboratory. Results are interpreted against applicable regulatory screening levels, giving buyers, sellers, and lenders a clear picture of whether conditions require remediation or regulatory reporting before closing.

## Local Note

One pattern that comes up repeatedly on Fresno County foothill parcels near the Kings River agricultural zone: older farmsteads often have a secondary structure, a pump house, a packing shed, or a converted storage building, that does not appear on current assessor maps but shows clearly on historical aerials from the 1960s and 1970s. These structures are worth locating before fieldwork begins, because they frequently sit over the original equipment maintenance area and represent the highest-probability location for petroleum or solvent impacts. Reviewing multiple vintages of aerial photography before the site visit, rather than relying solely on current parcel data, consistently improves the quality of the reconnaissance.
