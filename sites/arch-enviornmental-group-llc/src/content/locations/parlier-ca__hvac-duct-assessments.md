Parlier sits in the heart of the San Joaquin Valley, where summer temperatures routinely push past 105°F and agricultural dust from surrounding fields finds its way into every gap in a building's envelope. That combination puts unusual stress on HVAC systems and ductwork: units run hard for months on end, and the particulate load in the air means ducts accumulate contaminants faster than they would in a coastal or mountain climate. If you have noticed reduced airflow, a musty smell when the system kicks on, or visible discoloration around supply registers, an HVAC and duct system assessment is the right first step before those conditions become a larger air quality problem.

## Why Parlier Properties Face Elevated Duct Contamination Risk

The Central Valley's agricultural setting shapes indoor air quality in ways that are easy to underestimate. Harvest season brings elevated particulate matter, pesticide drift, and mold spores from field crops into the local air supply. Residential and commercial HVAC filters are not always rated to intercept fine agricultural particulates, and what the filter misses settles inside the ductwork itself.

Parlier's housing stock includes a significant share of older construction built during the postwar growth of Fresno County's farm communities. Many of these homes were fitted with ductwork that runs through unconditioned attic spaces, where summer attic temperatures can exceed 150°F. That heat cycling degrades duct seals, creates gaps at joints, and allows insulation fibers, dust, and biological material to enter the supply air stream. Evaporative coolers, still common in the area as a lower-cost alternative to central air, introduce outdoor air with minimal filtration and can deposit moisture inside duct cavities, creating conditions where mold colonization is possible within 24 to 48 hours of a humidity event.

## Our HVAC and Duct System Assessment Process in Parlier

A thorough assessment begins before any equipment is deployed. We review the age and configuration of the system, the type of ductwork (sheet metal, flex duct, or duct board), and any prior service records the property owner can provide. In Parlier's older residential properties, flex duct installed decades ago is a common finding, and that material degrades in ways that rigid metal does not.

From there, the process moves through several concrete stages:

- **Visual inspection** of accessible ductwork, air handlers, and return plenums for visible debris, biological growth, or physical damage.
- **Sampling and testing** where indicated, including surface swabs or tape lifts for mold identification, and air sampling to establish whether duct contamination is affecting occupied spaces. As a certified asbestos consultant and lead inspector/assessor, Arch Environmental Group also evaluates older duct insulation and mastic materials for regulated substances that require special handling before any remediation work begins.
- **Airflow and pressure diagnostics** to identify leakage points that are pulling unconditioned attic or crawl space air into the supply stream.
- **Written assessment report** documenting findings, photographs, and laboratory results, formatted to support insurance claims or contractor bids if remediation is warranted.

Because the assessment is independent of any remediation work, the findings are objective. You receive a clear picture of what is in your ducts and what, if anything, needs to happen next.

## Equipment and Methods Used for HVAC Assessment

Accurate duct assessments depend on calibrated sampling tools and proper chain-of-custody procedures for any laboratory samples collected. We use NIOSH-recognized sampling protocols, consistent with the training held by our NIOSH 582-certified microscopist, to ensure that fiber and particulate counts are defensible if results are used in an insurance or legal context.

For biological contamination, surface and air samples are submitted to accredited third-party laboratories. We do not interpret results in ways that overstate or understate risk; the laboratory data speaks for itself, and the written report explains what it means in plain terms. Where asbestos-containing duct insulation or transite components are suspected in older Parlier properties, those materials are sampled and analyzed separately under the protocols governing our asbestos consultant certification.
