## Why Squaw Valley Properties Face Distinct Clearance Challenges

The Sierra Nevada foothills climate is not forgiving to building envelopes. Properties in this part of Fresno County cycle through cold, wet winters and dry, hot summers, and that thermal cycling stresses rooflines, wall assemblies, and crawl spaces in ways that accelerate moisture intrusion. Older mountain cabins and rural residential structures common in the Squaw Valley area often have pier-and-beam or partial crawl space foundations, where groundwater and condensation can accumulate without obvious interior signs. When remediation is performed in these spaces, standard visual inspection alone is not enough. Spore counts can remain elevated in a crawl space even after visible mold has been removed, and only air sampling or surface tape-lift analysis can confirm the space has returned to an acceptable baseline.

Asbestos-containing materials are also a real concern in structures built before the 1980s, which includes a meaningful portion of the rural residential stock in this part of Fresno County. After asbestos abatement, a proper clearance air test using phase contrast microscopy, performed by a qualified analyst, is required before the space can be reoccupied or reconstructed. ## Our Post-Remediation Verification Process

Clearance testing is not a single action. It is a structured sequence calibrated to what was remediated and what the space is used for.

For mold clearance, the process typically begins with a visual inspection of the remediated area to confirm containment has been removed and surfaces appear clean. Air samples are then collected inside the remediated zone and compared against outdoor control samples taken at the same time. Spore species and concentrations are analyzed, and the results are compared against pre-remediation baseline data when available. A clearance letter is issued only when indoor counts are consistent with or lower than outdoor ambient levels and no remediation-target species are elevated.

For asbestos clearance, aggressive air sampling is conducted after final cleaning, using calibrated pumps and cassettes that meet NIOSH 7400 or NIOSH 7402 analytical methods. Fiber concentrations must fall below the applicable action level before the space is cleared. Because our principal holds both a Certified Asbestos Consultant designation and NIOSH 582 microscopist credentials, the analytical chain from sample collection to final report stays within one accountable firm.

All reports are formatted for submission to insurance carriers, property buyers, county environmental offices, or legal proceedings, whichever your situation requires.
