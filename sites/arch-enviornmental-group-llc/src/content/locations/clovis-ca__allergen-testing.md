## Why Clovis Properties See Elevated Indoor Allergen Loads

The San Joaquin Valley's climate is one of the most allergen-intensive in California. Clovis experiences hot, dry summers with sustained wind events that lift dust from surrounding agricultural fields and carry it into residential neighborhoods. That same dust carries mold spores, pollen fragments, and fine soil particles that settle into carpet fibers, upholstered furniture, and HVAC ductwork.

Clovis has also seen significant residential growth over the past two decades, with large tracts of newer single-family homes built on land that was previously row-crop farmland. Disturbed agricultural soil can introduce elevated concentrations of dust mite allergens and mold spores into new construction before landscaping is established. Older homes closer to the historic downtown core, meanwhile, often have original ductwork that has accumulated decades of particulate buildup, a hidden reservoir that recirculates allergens every time the system cycles on.

Pet dander is another consistent finding in Clovis homes. The region's suburban density means many households keep indoor pets year-round, and dander from cats and dogs binds to fabric surfaces and becomes airborne with foot traffic or air movement. A dust sample allergen analysis can quantify exactly how much cat or dog allergen is present per gram of settled dust, a number that matters when a family member's symptoms aren't improving despite standard cleaning routines.

## Our Allergen Testing Process in Clovis

Allergen testing begins with a structured site assessment. A certified consultant walks the property to identify likely accumulation zones, HVAC returns, bedroom carpeting, upholstered furniture, and any area with visible dust buildup or reported symptom concentration. This walkthrough shapes where samples are collected, so the results reflect the actual exposure pattern in your home rather than a random grab sample.

Dust samples are collected using standardized collection methods that allow results to be compared against established health-based benchmarks. Samples go to an accredited laboratory for analysis covering common indoor allergens: dust mite allergens (Der p 1 and Der f 1), cat allergen (Fel d 1), dog allergen (Can f 1), and cockroach allergen (Bla g 2), among others depending on the scope requested. Results typically return within a few business days and are presented in a written report that identifies which allergens are present, at what concentrations, and what those levels mean relative to published guidance.

Where air sampling is appropriate, particularly in homes with forced-air HVAC systems common throughout Clovis, we can include airborne particle counts alongside settled dust analysis to give a more complete picture of what occupants are actually inhaling.
