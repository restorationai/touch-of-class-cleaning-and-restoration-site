## Why Dinuba Properties Face Distinct Water Quality Concerns

The eastern San Joaquin Valley's agricultural intensity is unmatched in California, and Dinuba's surrounding raisin and citrus operations have shaped the groundwater profile over generations. Nitrate contamination is a documented, ongoing concern in Tulare County's shallow aquifers, the same aquifers that feed many private wells on properties at the rural edges of the city. High nitrate levels are colorless and odorless, which means a well that looks and tastes fine can still carry concentrations that pose real health risks, particularly for infants and pregnant women.

Beyond nitrates, the region's native soils and rock formations contribute naturally occurring arsenic and uranium to groundwater at levels that occasionally exceed federal drinking water standards. Older homes in Dinuba, particularly those built before the 1980s, may also have lead solder in interior plumbing or, in some cases, original lead service lines connecting the meter to the house. A water quality test is the only way to know what's actually in the water reaching your tap, because these contaminants leave no visible or sensory trace.

## Our Water Quality Testing Process in Dinuba

Testing begins with a site assessment, understanding whether the property is on a private well, a mutual water system, or municipal supply, and identifying any obvious risk factors like proximity to agricultural fields, the age of interior plumbing, or recent changes in water taste or clarity. ## Local Note

One thing worth knowing if you're on a private well anywhere near the agricultural blocks east and south of Dinuba: well depth matters enormously for nitrate risk. Shallow wells, those drilled before modern depth requirements, draw from the same near-surface aquifer that receives irrigation return water and is most vulnerable to fertilizer-derived nitrate loading. Deeper wells tap confined aquifers that are generally better protected, but they carry their own geogenic concerns, including arsenic. If you don't know your well's depth or construction date, that information is often on file with the Tulare County Environmental Health Division, and it's a useful starting point before interpreting any test results.

If your property was built before 1978 and you're concerned about lead, the risk isn't only from the service line, interior plumbing soldered before 1986 can leach lead into standing water, particularly first-draw water that's been sitting overnight. Running the tap for 30 seconds before drinking is a reasonable interim step, but it's not a substitute for knowing your actual lead levels.
