If you've noticed persistent sneezing, itchy eyes, or unexplained respiratory irritation inside your home, the source may not be obvious without professional allergen testing. ## Why Orange Cove Properties Face Distinct Allergen Pressures

The Central Valley's climate is the defining factor here. Orange Cove experiences hot, dry summers and mild, foggy winters, a cycle that concentrates airborne particulates indoors. During harvest season, field dust and organic debris migrate into homes through window gaps, door seals, and HVAC intakes. That same dust carries mold spores, insect fragments, and plant proteins that settle into carpet fibers and upholstered surfaces.

Many homes in and around Orange Cove were built in the mid-20th century, when construction standards allowed for less airtight envelopes. Older crawl spaces and attic spaces in these homes often accumulate decades of settled dust, rodent dander, and decomposing organic material, all of which can become suspended in indoor air when HVAC systems cycle on. Pet dander from dogs and cats compounds the problem in homes with animals, because dander particles are small enough to remain airborne for hours and embed deeply into soft surfaces.

Dust mite populations also thrive in the Valley's winter fog season, when indoor humidity rises and bedding, rugs, and upholstered furniture hold enough moisture to support mite colonies. Without testing, there is no reliable way to know whether dust mites, pet dander, mold proteins, or field-origin particulates are the primary driver of symptoms.

## Our Allergen Testing Process in Orange Cove

Allergen testing is not a single swab and a result. The process begins with a structured walkthrough of the property to identify likely accumulation zones: HVAC filter housings, return-air registers, bedroom flooring, and any crawl space access points. In older Orange Cove homes with original ductwork, we pay particular attention to duct interiors, where decades of settled material can be disturbed every time the system runs.

Dust samples are collected using standardized methods, typically wipe or vacuum cassette sampling depending on the surface type. Samples are then submitted to an accredited laboratory for analysis against a panel of common indoor allergens, including dust mite allergen (Der p1 and Der f1), cat dander (Fel d1), dog dander (Can f1), cockroach allergen (Bla g2), and mouse urinary protein (Mus m1). Results are returned with quantified allergen levels per unit area, giving you actual numbers rather than a pass/fail judgment.

The written report identifies which allergens are present, at what concentrations, and in which areas of the home. That specificity matters because remediation strategies differ: dust mite reduction focuses on encasements and humidity control, while pet dander requires different surface cleaning protocols.
