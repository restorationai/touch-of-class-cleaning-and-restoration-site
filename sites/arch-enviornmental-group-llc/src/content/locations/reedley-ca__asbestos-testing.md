## Why Reedley Properties Are Likely to Contain Asbestos

Construction practices in the San Joaquin Valley through the 1970s leaned heavily on materials we now know to be hazardous. Popcorn ceilings, floor tiles, pipe insulation, roofing felts, drywall joint compound, and textured wall coatings all commonly contained asbestos during this era. Reedley's older residential neighborhoods, many of which feature homes built between the 1940s and early 1980s, are squarely in that window.

The valley's climate adds another layer of concern. Prolonged heat causes building materials to expand and contract repeatedly over decades, which can degrade the binders holding asbestos fibers in place. A popcorn ceiling that looks intact may have developed microscopic fractures that make it friable, meaning fibers can become airborne with minimal disturbance. This is especially relevant before any remodel that involves scraping, sanding, cutting, or demolition.

Agricultural and light-industrial properties in the Reedley area often have older outbuildings, equipment sheds, or processing structures that were built without the regulatory oversight applied to residential construction. These structures can contain asbestos in roofing panels, insulation wrap, and flooring materials that are rarely inspected until a sale or renovation forces the issue.

## Our Asbestos Testing and Inspection Process in Reedley

A proper asbestos survey in Reedley begins with a thorough visual assessment of the property, identifying suspect materials by location, condition, and estimated age. As a Certified Asbestos Consultant, the inspector documents every material that could plausibly contain asbestos before any samples are collected.

Sampling follows a systematic protocol. Suspect materials are carefully collected using methods designed to minimize fiber disturbance, sealed, and sent to an accredited laboratory for analysis. The lab results, typically returned within a few business days, with rush options available, identify whether asbestos is present and at what concentration. The final report maps out exactly which materials tested positive, their condition, and the recommended course of action: abatement before work proceeds, encapsulation, or ongoing monitoring if the material is in good condition and will not be disturbed.

For homeowners planning to sell, refinance, or pull a permit for renovation work in Reedley, that written report is often required documentation. Fresno County and the San Joaquin Valley Air Pollution Control District have notification and demolition requirements tied to asbestos-containing materials, a certified inspection report is the foundation of that compliance process.

## Local Note

One thing that comes up specifically in Reedley's older agricultural and residential properties: many structures built in the postwar decades used asbestos-cement corrugated roofing panels on outbuildings and covered equipment areas.
