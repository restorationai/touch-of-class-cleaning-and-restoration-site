## Why Lemoore Properties See HVAC and Duct Contamination Issues

The Central Valley's climate creates a specific set of conditions that accelerate duct system problems. Lemoore's hot, dry summers mean air conditioning runs for months at a stretch, and the temperature swings between day and night can cause ductwork joints to expand and contract repeatedly. Over time, that movement creates gaps where unconditioned attic air, insulation fibers, and settled dust enter the supply stream.

The region also has a significant agricultural footprint. Pesticide drift, mold spores from field crops, and soil particulates from plowing season are not just outdoor concerns. When HVAC systems pull in air through return vents, those contaminants can accumulate on coil surfaces and inside duct liners. Homes built during Lemoore's growth periods in the 1970s and 1980s often have original ductwork with interior duct board lining that, once it begins to degrade or absorb moisture from a coil leak, becomes an environment where mold colonization can take hold without any visible sign from the living space.

Naval Air Station Lemoore's proximity also means a portion of the local housing stock includes base-adjacent residential construction built to federal contractor specifications from earlier decades, some of which used duct materials and installation methods that would not meet current standards.

## Our HVAC and Duct System Assessment Process in Lemoore

An assessment starts before any equipment is deployed. The technician reviews the system layout, the age and type of ductwork, and any history of water intrusion or condensation issues near the air handler. In Lemoore's climate, coil condensate pans are a frequent starting point because they can overflow during peak cooling season and introduce moisture directly into the plenum.

From there, the process includes:

- **Visual inspection** of accessible duct sections, registers, and the air handler cabinet for visible growth, debris accumulation, or liner deterioration
- **Particle and spore sampling** from supply and return air streams using calibrated equipment, with samples sent to an accredited laboratory
- **Surface sampling** inside duct sections where visual findings suggest biological activity
- **Moisture mapping** around the coil and plenum to identify any ongoing or historical water intrusion

Because Arch Environmental Group holds certifications as a Certified Asbestos Consultant, Lead Inspector Assessor, and NIOSH 582 Microscopist, the team is equipped to identify not just mold but also legacy materials sometimes found in older duct insulation, including fibrous materials that can become airborne when duct sections are disturbed during cleaning or renovation.

## Local Note

One pattern that comes up specifically in Lemoore's older residential neighborhoods is duct systems that were extended or modified when room additions were built in the 1980s and 1990s.
