## Why Visalia Properties See Air Quality Issues

The San Joaquin Valley is one of the most air-challenged regions in the country, and that outdoor burden doesn't stay outside. Valley fever, caused by *Coccidioides* spores in disturbed soil, is endemic to this part of California, and construction activity, wind events, and agricultural tilling can push those spores into the air around homes and businesses. Inside, HVAC systems that run almost continuously through a long, hot summer become efficient collectors of particulate matter, mold fragments, and allergens.

Visalia's housing stock spans a wide range of construction eras. Older homes built before the 1980s may contain asbestos-containing materials in floor tiles, duct insulation, or popcorn ceilings, materials that remain stable until disturbed by a renovation or a pest intrusion. Homes from that same era may also have lead-based paint in deteriorating condition. Neither hazard is visible to the naked eye, which is exactly why air sampling and materials testing matter before any remodel or after any water intrusion event.

Cooler, damper winters, combined with the region's clay-heavy soils that drain slowly, mean that crawl spaces and slab foundations in Visalia can accumulate moisture seasonally. That moisture, trapped under a home through the wet months, creates conditions where mold can colonize well before it's visible on any interior surface.

## Our Indoor Air Quality Testing Process in Visalia

A thorough air quality inspection here starts with a visual assessment of the property, HVAC registers, attic access points, crawl space conditions, and any areas with past water damage or recent renovation work. From there, air samples are collected using calibrated pumps and cassettes following NIOSH-recognized protocols. Depending on what the initial walkthrough suggests, sampling may target airborne mold spores, asbestos fibers, particulate matter, or volatile organic compounds.

Samples are submitted to an accredited third-party laboratory. Results typically return within a few business days, at which point a written report is prepared that explains what was found, what the findings mean for the occupants, and, if remediation is warranted, what the appropriate scope of work looks like. The report is written to be useful to you, your contractor, your physician, or your insurance adjuster, depending on why the testing was initiated.

## Local Note

One thing that comes up repeatedly in the Central Valley is the relationship between evaporative coolers, "swamp coolers", and indoor air quality. Many Visalia homes, particularly those built in the 1960s through the 1980s, still rely on evaporative cooling rather than refrigerated air. Swamp coolers work by drawing outside air through water-saturated pads, which means they also pull in whatever is in that outside air: dust, pollen, mold spores, and during wildfire season, fine particulate matter. Unlike a sealed HVAC system with a replaceable filter, an evaporative cooler provides almost no filtration. Homeowners who rely on one and have unexplained allergy symptoms or musty odors indoors should specifically mention it when scheduling a test, it changes which sampling locations matter most.
