## Why Easton Properties See Elevated Allergen Loads

The Central Valley's climate is one of the most allergen-active in California. Summers are long, hot, and dry, which means homes run evaporative coolers or recirculate air through ducts that may not have been cleaned in years. Dust mite populations thrive in bedding and upholstered furniture even in arid climates, because indoor humidity from cooking, bathing, and breathing is enough to sustain them. Pet dander, which is not fur but microscopic skin flakes, becomes airborne easily in low-humidity air and stays suspended longer than it would in a coastal environment.

Agricultural activity around Easton also matters. Field dust, pollen from surrounding crops, and particulates stirred up during harvest seasons enter homes through gaps around windows and doors, through HVAC intakes, and on clothing and footwear. Older homes in the area, many built with construction methods and materials common to mid-century California, often have less airtight envelopes than newer builds, which means outdoor allergen loads translate more directly into indoor air quality problems.

## Our Allergen Testing Process in Easton

Allergen testing is not a single swab and a result. The process begins with a structured walkthrough of the property to identify the most likely reservoirs: carpeted bedrooms, upholstered furniture, HVAC registers, and areas where pets spend the most time. Dust samples are collected using standardized methods, including settled dust collection from surfaces and filter media, depending on what the assessment calls for.

Samples are analyzed by a certified laboratory for common allergen categories including dust mite allergen (Der p 1 and Der f 1), cat allergen (Fel d 1), dog allergen (Can f 1), cockroach allergen, and mouse urinary protein where applicable. The report you receive identifies allergen concentrations by room or zone, not just a single whole-home average, so you know where the problem is densest and where remediation efforts will have the most impact. ## Local Note

One thing worth knowing about valley homes in this part of Fresno County: evaporative coolers, sometimes called swamp coolers, are common in properties built before central air conditioning became standard. These systems pull outside air directly into the home without the filtration that a modern forced-air system provides. During high-pollen periods or after nearby field activity, an evaporative cooler can actively draw allergen-laden outdoor air into living spaces. If your home uses this type of cooling, the allergen testing process should account for it, because the intake and distribution points are often the highest-concentration zones in the home and are frequently overlooked in a standard assessment.
