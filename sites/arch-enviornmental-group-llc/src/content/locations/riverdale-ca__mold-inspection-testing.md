## Why Riverdale Properties Are Prone to Mold Growth

The San Joaquin Valley's climate is not what most people picture when they think of mold country, but the conditions here are deceptively favorable. Daytime temperatures spike in summer, then drop sharply overnight, and that thermal cycling causes condensation to form inside wall cavities, around single-pane windows common in older ranch-style homes, and along cold-water supply lines. When that condensation has no path to evaporate, it feeds mold colonies that can colonize porous materials within 24 to 48 hours of sustained moisture exposure.

Riverdale's agricultural setting adds another layer. Irrigation runoff and high water tables in parts of Fresno County mean that slab foundations and low-clearance crawl spaces can wick ground moisture upward, especially in homes built before modern vapor barrier standards became common. Properties along irrigation canal corridors are particularly worth monitoring after the spring irrigation season begins.

## Our Mold Inspection and Testing Process

A mold inspection is not a visual sweep of obvious staining. The process begins with a thorough walkthrough to identify moisture sources, areas of elevated humidity, and materials showing discoloration or deterioration. Arch Environmental Group uses calibrated moisture meters and thermal imaging to locate hidden wet zones behind drywall, under flooring, and inside ceiling assemblies before any sampling begins.

Air sampling for mold involves drawing a measured volume of air through a spore trap cassette, which is then submitted to an accredited laboratory for analysis. The lab report identifies the genera of mold present and compares indoor spore counts to an outdoor baseline sample taken at the same time. That comparison is essential: some mold spore types are naturally present in San Joaquin Valley air at elevated levels due to agricultural dust and organic matter, and a competent assessment accounts for that regional baseline rather than treating every outdoor-origin spore as a sign of an indoor problem.

Surface sampling, using tape lifts or swabs, is added when a specific area of suspected growth needs to be characterized. Together, the air and surface data give you a defensible, laboratory-backed report rather than a verbal opinion.

## Local Note

Homes in the Riverdale area that were built during the postwar agricultural expansion of the 1950s and 1960s frequently have original cellulose-based insulation in attic spaces. That material absorbs and retains moisture far more readily than modern fiberglass or spray foam, and it provides an ideal substrate for mold growth that is invisible from below. If your home dates to that era and you have not had the attic inspected, it is one of the first places a thorough mold assessment should include, particularly after any roof leak or after an unusually wet winter in the Central Valley.
