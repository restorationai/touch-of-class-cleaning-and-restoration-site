## Why Selma Properties Are Prone to Mold Growth

The San Joaquin Valley's agricultural irrigation infrastructure keeps the regional water table relatively high in many areas, and older homes in Selma, particularly those built in the mid-20th century with slab-on-grade or shallow crawl space foundations, often lack the vapor barriers and subfloor ventilation that modern construction requires. When tule fog rolls through from November into March, exterior humidity can stay above 80% for days at a stretch, and that moisture finds its way into any gap in the building envelope.

Crawl spaces are a particular concern. Without adequate cross-ventilation, condensation accumulates on floor joists and subfloor sheathing. Mold can begin colonizing wood surfaces within 24 to 48 hours of sustained moisture contact, and because the damage is hidden below living areas, it often goes undetected until a real estate transaction or an HVAC inspection brings it to light.

Selma's older residential stock also includes homes with original single-pane windows and minimal wall insulation. Cold interior surfaces during foggy winters create condensation points along exterior walls, especially in kitchens and bathrooms where indoor humidity is already elevated.

## Our Mold Inspection and Testing Process in Selma

A thorough mold assessment is not a visual walk-through with a flashlight. The inspection process starts with a structured moisture survey using calibrated meters to map elevated readings behind walls, under flooring, and in ceiling assemblies, areas where surface conditions can look completely normal while the substrate stays saturated.

From there, air sampling for mold spore counts gives us a baseline comparison between indoor and outdoor air. If indoor spore concentrations are significantly elevated, or if the species profile indoors differs from what's present outside, that's meaningful data, not a guess. Arch Environmental Group's team includes a NIOSH 582 Microscopist, which means spore samples are analyzed under laboratory conditions with the rigor that standard requires, not simply sent to a third-party lab with no chain-of-custody oversight.

Surface sampling, tape lifts or swabs from visually suspect areas, identifies the specific genera present, which matters when a property owner or their physician wants to understand what they've been exposed to. All findings are compiled into a written assessment report that documents sample locations, laboratory results, and the scope of any affected materials. That report is what an insurance adjuster, a remediation contractor, or a prospective buyer's attorney will rely on.

## Local Note

One pattern worth knowing in Selma and the surrounding Fresno County communities: homes built in the 1950s and 1960s sometimes used a plaster-and-lath wall system rather than drywall. Plaster walls absorb and release moisture much more slowly than modern gypsum board, which means a slow plumbing leak behind a plaster wall can sustain mold growth for months without producing the visible staining or bubbling that drywall shows quickly. Moisture meter readings on plaster can also be misleading, the dense substrate can register as drier than it actually is at the lath layer. A thorough inspection in a home of that era accounts for this and uses multiple measurement methods rather than relying on a single reading.
