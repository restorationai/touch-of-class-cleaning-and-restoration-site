## Why Selma Properties Are Prone to Mold Growth

The Central Valley's tule fog season is more than an inconvenience on Highway 99, it drives persistent moisture into the building envelope of older homes that weren't designed with today's vapor barriers and tight insulation standards. Selma's housing stock includes a significant number of homes built in the mid-20th century, many with original wood-framed crawl spaces, single-pane windows, and aging plumbing that sweats or seeps. When a slow leak behind a kitchen wall or a dripping supply line under a bathroom vanity goes undetected for even a week or two, mold colonies can establish themselves before the surface shows any visible sign.

The region's agricultural irrigation infrastructure also contributes. Properties near irrigation canals or low-lying fields can experience elevated ambient humidity and soil moisture that migrates upward through concrete slab foundations, a pathway that's easy to overlook but shows up clearly in a thorough inspection.

## Our Mold Inspection and Testing Process in Selma

A mold inspection is not a visual walkthrough with a flashlight. The process begins with a systematic assessment of the building envelope, attic, crawl space, wall cavities where accessible, HVAC returns, and any area with a history of water intrusion. Moisture mapping with calibrated meters identifies zones that read elevated even when surfaces appear dry, which is common in Selma homes where plaster or stucco exteriors can trap moisture long after a leak is repaired.

When sampling is warranted, air samples are collected using calibrated pumps and cassettes and sent to an accredited laboratory for analysis. Surface samples, tape lifts or swabs, may be collected from visible growth to identify the species present. The certified team at Arch Environmental Group holds credentials including NIOSH 582 Microscopist certification, which means spore identification is evaluated with the same analytical rigor used in industrial hygiene settings, not just a general "mold present" checkbox. Results come back with spore counts by genus, giving you and your contractor or physician a factual baseline rather than a vague concern.

A written report documents findings, sample locations, laboratory results, and, where applicable, observations about conditions that are likely to sustain mold growth if not corrected. That report is what an insurance adjuster, a real estate agent, or a remediation contractor needs to take the next step.
