Radon, a naturally occurring radioactive gas that forms as uranium in the soil decays, has no smell and no color, and it accumulates quietly in homes with limited sub-slab ventilation. ## Why Riverdale Properties Face Radon Considerations

The San Joaquin Valley's underlying geology includes granitic and sedimentary formations that can carry trace uranium deposits. As those minerals break down over time, radon migrates upward through the soil and into structures through foundation cracks, utility penetrations, and the natural pressure differential between the ground and a conditioned living space. Homes built on slab foundations, which are common throughout Fresno County's rural communities including Riverdale, offer radon fewer barriers than homes with crawl spaces that allow for some natural air exchange beneath the structure.

California does not mandate radon testing for residential real estate transactions the way some other states do, which means many Riverdale homeowners have simply never had a test performed. The EPA's action level of 4 picocuries per liter (pCi/L) is the threshold at which mitigation is recommended nationally, and California's own guidance aligns with that benchmark. Without a baseline measurement, there is no way to know where a given home stands.

## Our Radon Testing Process in Riverdale

Arch Environmental Group uses continuous radon monitoring equipment rather than passive charcoal canisters for residential testing. A continuous monitor records radon concentrations in real time, hour by hour, over the course of the test period. That data produces a detailed graph of how levels fluctuate with changes in weather, barometric pressure, and HVAC operation, which gives a more complete picture than a single averaged result from a passive device.

For a standard short-term test, the monitor is placed in the lowest livable area of the home, typically a first-floor bedroom, finished basement, or ground-level living space. Closed-house conditions are maintained during the test period, meaning windows and exterior doors remain closed except for normal entry and exit. Once the test is complete, the data is downloaded, analyzed, and provided in a written report that includes the average result, the hourly readings, and a plain-language explanation of what the numbers mean for the occupants.

## Equipment and Methods We Use for Radon

Passive charcoal canisters are inexpensive and widely used, but they produce a single averaged number with no visibility into how conditions changed during the test. Continuous electronic monitors capture fluctuations that matter in San Joaquin Valley homes, where seasonal temperature swings and the operation of evaporative coolers in summer can meaningfully affect how radon moves through a structure. An evaporative cooler, which is common in this climate as an alternative to refrigerated air, introduces outside air and can temporarily dilute indoor radon levels, potentially causing a passive test to underreport actual exposure. Continuous monitoring captures those dynamics and provides a more defensible result for real estate transactions or personal health decisions.
