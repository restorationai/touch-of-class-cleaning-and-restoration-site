## Why Minkler Properties Carry a Higher Lead Paint Risk

The San Joaquin Valley's agricultural towns developed quickly during the mid-twentieth century, and residential construction in communities like Minkler often relied on materials that are now understood to be hazardous. Homes built before 1978 are the primary concern, since lead-based paint was federally banned for residential use that year. In Fresno County's rural corridor, a significant share of the existing housing stock predates that cutoff, and many of those structures have seen minimal renovation since original construction.

The Valley's climate compounds the problem. Summer temperatures routinely climb well above 100 degrees Fahrenheit, and the thermal cycling between scorching days and cooler nights stresses painted surfaces year after year. Wood trim, window frames, exterior siding, and porch components are especially vulnerable. When paint chalks, peels, or powders under those conditions, lead particles can migrate into soil around the foundation and into interior dust, creating exposure pathways for children and adults alike.

## Our Lead Paint Testing and Inspection Process

A lead inspection identifies whether lead-based paint is present on any surface in a structure. A risk assessment goes further, evaluating the condition of those surfaces and the likelihood that they are creating actual exposure. Arch Environmental Group holds a Lead Inspector Assessor certification, which means the same professional who collects the data can also interpret risk and document findings in a format that satisfies California's regulatory requirements.

For most Minkler properties, the process begins with a visual survey of all painted surfaces, followed by XRF (X-ray fluorescence) testing. XRF is a non-destructive method: the analyzer is held against a surface and delivers an immediate reading of lead concentration without chipping or sampling the paint. Where XRF readings are inconclusive, paint chip samples are collected and sent to an accredited laboratory for confirmation. The final report documents every tested component, the lead concentration found, and the condition of the surface, giving you a clear picture of what exists and what, if anything, requires action.

## What to Expect After the Inspection Report

A lead inspection report is not a work order. It is a documented baseline. If lead-based paint is found in good condition and undisturbed, it may not require immediate action, but it must be disclosed in any real estate transaction and managed carefully during any future renovation. California's renovation, repair, and painting rules require contractors working on pre-1978 housing to follow specific lead-safe work practices, and your inspection report gives those contractors the information they need to plan the job correctly.

If the report identifies deteriorated lead paint or conditions that pose an active risk, the assessor's findings will outline the scope of what needs to be addressed. Arch Environmental Group does not perform the remediation itself, which means the assessment is entirely objective: the findings reflect what is there, not what would generate additional work.
