## Why Centerville Homes Are Prone to Elevated Indoor Allergen Loads

The San Joaquin Valley's climate is a significant factor. Long, hot summers with low humidity cause dust to become airborne easily, while the region's agricultural activity introduces mold spores, pollen proteins, and fine organic particles that settle inside homes whenever windows are opened or HVAC systems pull in outside air. Centerville's housing stock includes a mix of mid-century construction and newer tract homes, and older builds often have duct systems that haven't been inspected in years. Accumulated debris inside ductwork becomes a reservoir for dust mite allergens and pet dander, redistributed every time the system cycles on.

Crawl spaces are another common concern in this part of Fresno County. Homes with vented crawl spaces can draw in soil-level dust and mold particulates, especially after the wet season when moisture lingers beneath the subfloor. That combination of biological and particulate allergens can be difficult to distinguish by symptoms alone, which is exactly why surface and air sampling gives you a clearer picture than guesswork.

## Our Allergen Testing Process in Centerville

Allergen testing isn't a single swab and a result. The process begins with a walkthrough to identify the rooms and surfaces most likely to harbor concentrated allergen loads: bedrooms with wall-to-wall carpet, living areas with upholstered furniture, and any space with pets or a history of water intrusion. From there, sampling typically involves a combination of dust collection from settled surfaces and, where indicated, air cassette sampling to capture airborne particles.

Samples are submitted to an accredited laboratory for analysis. Results identify specific allergen categories, including dust mite proteins (Der p 1 and Der f 1), cat and dog dander proteins, cockroach allergens, and rodent urinary proteins where applicable. The written report translates lab data into actionable findings so you understand which areas of your home carry the highest burden and what remediation steps, if any, are warranted. Our team holds NIOSH 582 microscopist credentials, which means the analytical side of particulate and fiber identification is handled at a professional standard.

Dust sample collection for allergen analysis follows established protocols using measured surface area wipes, which allow the lab to report allergen concentrations per unit area rather than just presence or absence. ## Local Note

One pattern that comes up in San Joaquin Valley homes is the effect of evaporative coolers, which are common in Centerville and throughout Fresno County as an alternative to central air conditioning. Unlike sealed refrigerant systems, swamp coolers work by drawing outside air across a wet pad, which means they also pull in whatever is in the outdoor air: agricultural dust, pollen, and mold spores included. Homes that rely on evaporative cooling often show higher settled allergen loads in rooms closest to the cooler intake, a detail worth flagging during the walkthrough so sampling reflects where the burden is actually concentrated.
