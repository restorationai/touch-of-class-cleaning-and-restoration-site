## Why Fowler Homes See Elevated Indoor Allergen Loads

The San Joaquin Valley's climate creates a near-perfect environment for allergen accumulation indoors. Dry, windy conditions from spring through fall pull fine agricultural dust and pollen through window gaps, door sweeps, and attic vents. Once inside, those particles don't simply settle, they bind to carpet fibers, accumulate in HVAC filter media, and colonize the surface of mattresses and upholstered furniture.

Fowler's housing stock includes a significant number of homes built in the mid-twentieth century, many with original ductwork that was never designed to filter fine particulates. Older forced-air systems can circulate dust mite allergen (Der p 1 and Der f 1), pet dander proteins, and mold spores throughout a home every time the blower cycles. Homes that have been re-roofed or had additions built without full air-sealing often have attic bypass points that funnel outdoor air, and whatever it's carrying, directly into living spaces.

Seasonal field burning and harvest operations in the broader Fresno County area also spike outdoor particulate levels at predictable times of year, which means indoor allergen loads can shift dramatically between spring and late fall. A test conducted in one season may tell a different story than one done six months later, which is worth keeping in mind when scheduling.

## What Allergen Testing Involves

Allergen testing is not the same as a general air quality screen. The process targets specific protein-based allergens, dust mite waste particles, cat and dog dander (Fel d 1, Can f 1), cockroach allergen (Bla g 2), and rodent urinary proteins, among others, using dust samples collected from settled surfaces, HVAC returns, and soft furnishings.

The collection method matters. A wipe or vacuum sample from the right location captures allergen reservoirs that a midair sample would miss entirely, because most allergen mass is bound to settled dust rather than suspended in the breathing zone. Arch Environmental Group's team, credentialed as a Certified Asbestos Consultant and Lead Inspector/Assessor with NIOSH 582 Microscopist training, applies the same methodical sampling discipline to allergen work, defined sample areas, documented chain of custody, and analysis through an accredited laboratory.

Results come back as quantified concentrations (typically micrograms of allergen per gram of dust), which can be compared against published health-based thresholds. That gives you and your physician or allergist something concrete to act on, rather than a vague recommendation to "clean more often."

## Local Note

One pattern worth knowing in Fowler and the surrounding Fresno County agricultural corridor: evaporative coolers (swamp coolers) are common in older homes as a lower-cost alternative to refrigerated air. They work by pulling outside air through water-saturated pads, which is efficient for cooling but also means they actively introduce outdoor dust, pollen, and mold spores into the indoor air stream. Homes running evaporative coolers during peak pollen season can accumulate allergen loads significantly faster than comparable homes on refrigerated systems. If your home uses a swamp cooler, that context shapes where samples should be collected and how results should be interpreted.
