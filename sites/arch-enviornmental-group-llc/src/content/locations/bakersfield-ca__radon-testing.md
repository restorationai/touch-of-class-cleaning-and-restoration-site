## Why Bakersfield Properties Are Worth Testing

The San Joaquin Valley floor is underlain by sedimentary formations that vary in uranium content across Kern County. While California as a whole tends to have lower average radon readings than states in the EPA's high-risk Zone 1, that statewide average obscures meaningful variation at the local level. Slab-on-grade construction, common throughout Bakersfield's postwar and contemporary subdivisions, can still allow radon to enter through slab cracks, construction joints, and utility penetrations. Homes with raised foundations and crawl spaces present a different entry pathway: soil gas accumulates beneath the floor deck and diffuses upward.

Bakersfield's climate adds a layer of complexity. During the region's hot summers, residents keep windows closed and HVAC systems running continuously, reducing natural air exchange. Lower ventilation rates mean radon that enters has fewer pathways to escape, which can push indoor concentrations higher than the same home would show in a more naturally ventilated climate. Winter temperature inversions, a well-documented phenomenon in the valley, can also suppress outdoor air movement and affect how soil gases behave near the foundation.

## Our Radon Testing Process in Bakersfield

Arch Environmental Group uses continuous electronic radon monitors rather than passive charcoal canisters for most residential and commercial tests. Continuous monitors log radon concentrations hour by hour throughout the test period, which typically runs 48 hours under closed-building conditions as specified by EPA and state guidance. That hourly data record is more informative than a single averaged number: it shows whether concentrations spike at night when soil moisture changes, or whether a particular part of the day consistently reads higher, which can point toward a specific entry point.

Before placing monitors, a certified consultant walks the property to note foundation type, visible cracks in slabs or stem walls, sump pit locations, and HVAC configuration. In Bakersfield's older neighborhoods near downtown, where homes may date to the 1940s and 1950s, original concrete work is sometimes porous or has settled unevenly, creating preferential pathways for soil gas. That walkthrough shapes where monitors are placed and how results are interpreted.

After the test period, the consultant retrieves the equipment, downloads the data, and produces a written report with the average radon concentration in picocuries per liter (pCi/L), the EPA action level for context, and a clear recommendation on whether mitigation is warranted.
