Hanford sits in the heart of Kings County, where the San Joaquin Valley's agricultural legacy shapes more than the landscape, it shapes what ends up in the water. Decades of intensive farming, aging irrigation infrastructure, and a groundwater basin that has absorbed decades of runoff mean that tap water and well water alike can carry contaminants that a standard visual check will never reveal. Whether your home draws from a private well or a municipal line, knowing exactly what you're drinking matters here in ways that differ from coastal California communities.

## Why Hanford Properties Face Distinct Water Quality Concerns

The Central Valley's geology does a lot of the work that contaminants need done for them. Hanford's underlying soils are fine-grained alluvial deposits that hold water well but also hold what's dissolved in it. Nitrates from fertilizer applications have historically elevated in Kings County groundwater, and arsenic occurs naturally in some deeper aquifer zones across the region, neither of which has a taste, color, or smell that tips you off at the faucet.

Older homes in Hanford, particularly those built before the late 1970s, may also carry lead risk from solder joints and service line materials that were standard at the time. That risk doesn't disappear when the water leaves the municipal system in good condition, it can be introduced at the point where aging plumbing meets your household supply. Arch Environmental Group's lead inspector and assessor credentials are specifically relevant here: we can evaluate both the water and the plumbing context together rather than treating them as separate problems.

Private well owners in the rural and semi-rural parcels around Hanford carry the full burden of their own water safety. Unlike municipal customers who receive periodic utility testing, well owners have no automatic safety net. A well that tested clean five years ago may not reflect what's in the aquifer today, especially after drought cycles that concentrate minerals and after wet years that flush surface contaminants downward.

## Our Water Quality Testing Process in Hanford

Testing starts with a site assessment, not a kit dropped in the mail. We collect samples under controlled conditions, flushed lines, first-draw samples where lead risk is the concern, and field blanks to rule out contamination during collection. The distinction matters because improper sampling technique is one of the most common reasons water test results come back misleading.

Samples go to an accredited laboratory. Results typically return within a few business days depending on the analyte panel selected. We don't hand you a printout and walk away: the report review includes a plain-language explanation of what each result means relative to EPA maximum contaminant levels and, where applicable, California's often stricter state standards under the California Safe Drinking Water Act.

For homes where lead is a concern, we can pair water sampling with a physical inspection of plumbing materials, a step that helps distinguish whether an elevated result is coming from the service line, interior solder joints, or a fixture. That integrated approach is more useful than a water result alone when you're deciding whether to install a filter, replace a line, or pursue a different fix.
