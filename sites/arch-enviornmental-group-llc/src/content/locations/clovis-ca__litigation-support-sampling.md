Clovis sits at the eastern edge of the San Joaquin Valley, where decades of agricultural irrigation, expansive clay soils, and a rapid residential building boom have created conditions that show up repeatedly in environmental disputes, mold behind stucco, asbestos in mid-century tract homes, and moisture intrusion that neither party can agree on. When a landlord-tenant disagreement, a real estate transaction, or a construction defect claim reaches the point where documentation matters more than opinion, Arch Environmental Group LLC provides the forensic sampling and litigation support that turns environmental conditions into defensible evidence.

## Why Clovis Properties Generate Environmental Disputes

Fresno County's growth pressure pushed development into Clovis aggressively through the 1970s, 1980s, and again in the 2000s. That layered building history means the housing stock is uneven: older ranch-style homes built before asbestos restrictions sit within blocks of newer stucco subdivisions that have their own moisture management challenges. The valley's hot, dry summers followed by cold, foggy winters create condensation cycles that are easy to misread, a landlord may point to low outdoor humidity while a tenant's bedroom wall is harboring active mold growth behind vapor-impermeable paint. In habitability disputes, that kind of surface-versus-substrate disagreement is exactly where forensic sampling resolves what visual inspection cannot.

Clovis also has a significant proportion of slab-on-grade construction, common throughout the Central Valley. Slab foundations concentrate moisture intrusion at specific penetration points, plumbing sleeves, HVAC pads, expansion joints, and those localized failures can produce hidden mold colonization that begins within 48 to 72 hours of a water event. In litigation, the timeline of that colonization is often contested. Chain-of-custody sampling, collected and documented to evidentiary standards, is the only way to establish when conditions became actionable.

## Our Forensic Sampling and Litigation Support Process

Forensic environmental work is methodologically different from a standard inspection. Every sample collected for litigation purposes is handled with documented chain of custody from collection through laboratory analysis. Arch Environmental Group's certified team, holding credentials as a Certified Asbestos Consultant, Lead Inspector Assessor, and NIOSH 582 Microscopist, collects air, bulk, swab, and tape-lift samples using protocols that will hold up to opposing expert scrutiny.

For mold disputes, that typically means spore trap air samples at multiple locations with an outdoor control, surface samples from visually suspect areas, and in contested cases, bulk material samples to establish whether growth is active or legacy. For asbestos and lead claims, common in Clovis properties built before 1980, sampling follows EPA and AHERA protocols, with laboratory results from accredited third-party labs. The written report is structured for legal use: findings stated with appropriate qualifiers, methodology cited, and conclusions that an expert witness can defend on cross-examination.

## Local Note

One pattern that comes up in Clovis habitability cases involves the valley's tule fog season, roughly November through February. Prolonged fog keeps outdoor relative humidity elevated for weeks at a time, and in homes with inadequate attic ventilation, a common finding in 1980s and 1990s Clovis tract construction, that ambient moisture loads the attic assembly and can migrate into ceiling cavities. Attorneys and adjusters sometimes assume mold in a ceiling is evidence of a roof leak; forensic sampling that distinguishes condensation-driven growth from liquid intrusion can change the entire theory of a case.
