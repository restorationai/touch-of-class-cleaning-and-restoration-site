## Why Reedley Properties Are Particularly Vulnerable After Flooding

Reedley sits in a stretch of the valley where agriculture, aging residential stock, and intense seasonal weather converge in ways that raise the stakes for post-flood mold risk. Many homes in the area were built in the mid-20th century, when vapor barriers were minimal or absent entirely, and wall cavities were framed with old-growth lumber that absorbs and holds moisture differently than modern engineered wood. That lumber doesn't fail quickly, but it stays wet longer, giving mold a sustained foothold before surface materials show any visible sign of a problem.

The region's climate compounds this. After a flood or plumbing failure, Reedley's dry summer heat can pull moisture from the surface of drywall or plaster quickly, creating a false impression that materials have dried out. Beneath that dry surface, subfloor sheathing, wall framing, and insulation batts can remain saturated for weeks. Without thermal imaging and calibrated moisture meters reading deep into the assembly, that hidden saturation goes undetected, and undetected moisture is where mold thrives.

Irrigation infrastructure throughout Fresno County also means that soil around residential foundations stays seasonally saturated in ways that aren't always obvious from street level. Slab foundations and raised foundations alike can wick ground moisture upward after a flood event, particularly when perimeter drainage has been compromised by debris or soil displacement.

## Our Post-Flood Mold Assessment Process in Reedley

A meaningful mold assessment after flooding is not a visual walkthrough with a flashlight. The process Arch Environmental Group uses starts with moisture mapping, a systematic pass through every affected room using non-invasive moisture meters and, where warranted, thermal imaging to identify temperature differentials that indicate wet assemblies behind finished surfaces.

Once moisture boundaries are mapped, air sampling captures what is actually suspended in the indoor environment. Spore trap cassettes are collected at multiple locations, typically inside affected rooms, in adjacent unaffected spaces for comparison, and outdoors to establish a baseline. Those samples are analyzed by a NIOSH 582-trained microscopist, which means the person reading your results has laboratory-level competency in identifying and quantifying fungal spores, not just running a test kit.

Surface sampling and bulk material sampling are added where visual evidence or elevated moisture readings suggest active colonization. The final deliverable is a written assessment report that documents findings, identifies affected materials, and provides a scope of work that a remediation contractor can use, or that your insurance adjuster can use to evaluate your claim.

## Local Note

One pattern worth knowing in Reedley and the surrounding Fresno County agricultural communities: homes near active orchards and vineyards sometimes show elevated outdoor mold spore counts during harvest and post-harvest periods, when decomposing organic material in adjacent fields contributes to ambient fungal loads.
