## Why Dinuba's Housing Stock Makes Lead Testing a Practical Priority

Tulare County's San Joaquin Valley towns, Dinuba included, grew steadily through the early and mid-twentieth century, when lead-based paint was the industry standard for interior trim, exterior siding, window sashes, and porch components. Homes built before 1940 are especially likely to carry multiple generations of paint, with original lead-containing layers buried beneath decades of repaints. That layering is exactly why surface appearance tells you nothing: a freshly painted wall can still test positive for lead substrate beneath it.

The valley's climate adds a compounding factor. Hot, dry summers followed by cool, damp winters cause wood framing and trim to expand and contract repeatedly. Over time, that cycling creates fine cracks and paint chalking, particularly on south- and west-facing exterior surfaces that absorb the most sun. Chalking and flaking paint on older homes is one of the primary pathways for lead dust exposure, especially for children playing near foundations or in yards where paint chips have settled into soil.

Dinuba's mix of single-family bungalows, farmworker-era cottages, and older multi-unit rentals means lead risk isn't confined to one neighborhood type. Rental properties in particular carry disclosure and remediation obligations under California Health and Safety Code, and landlords who skip a proper inspection before renovation work can face significant liability.

## Our Lead Paint Testing and Inspection Process in Dinuba

A lead inspection documents whether lead-based paint is present anywhere on a property. A lead risk assessment goes a step further, it identifies deteriorated paint, lead-contaminated dust, and soil conditions that pose an immediate exposure risk, then recommends corrective action. Depending on your situation (pre-sale disclosure, pre-renovation compliance, or a concern about a child's health), you may need one or both.

The process on-site typically works like this:

- **XRF scanning**: A handheld XRF analyzer reads lead concentration in paint layers non-destructively, component by component, walls, trim, doors, windows, cabinets, exterior siding. Results are immediate and do not require lab turnaround.
- **Wipe sampling**: Where XRF readings are inconclusive or where dust accumulation is a concern (windowsills, floors near friction surfaces), we collect wipe samples and submit them to an accredited laboratory.
- **Soil sampling**: For risk assessments, bare soil near building drip lines and play areas is sampled and analyzed for lead content, a step that matters in older Dinuba neighborhoods where exterior paint has been weathering for decades.
- **Written report**: California regulations require a written report from a certified lead inspector or assessor. Our report documents every component tested, every reading, and any recommended actions, formatted to satisfy lender, escrow, and contractor requirements.

All work is performed under the Certified Lead Inspector-Assessor credential held by Arch Environmental Group LLC.
