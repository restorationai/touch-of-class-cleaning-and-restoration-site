## Why Sanger Properties See Elevated Indoor Allergen Loads

The San Joaquin Valley's agricultural calendar shapes indoor air quality in ways that are easy to overlook. During harvest and tillage seasons, fine particulate matter, including allergenic proteins from crops, soil fungi, and decomposing plant material, migrates through window gaps, HVAC returns, and door seals into living spaces. Once inside, those particles settle into carpet fibers, upholstered furniture, and bedding, where they accumulate alongside more familiar allergens like dust mite waste and pet dander.

Sanger's climate adds another layer. Hot, dry summers desiccate dust mite populations temporarily, but the cooler, more humid months of late fall and winter create ideal breeding conditions, roughly 70–80°F and above 50% relative humidity, right inside the average home's mattress or area rug. Homes with evaporative coolers, which are common in this part of Fresno County, can introduce outdoor air with minimal filtration, pulling seasonal allergens directly into living areas.

Older wood-frame construction, which makes up a significant share of Sanger's housing stock, tends to have gaps around window frames and subflooring that allow allergen-laden dust to accumulate in wall cavities and crawl spaces. Those reservoirs are invisible to the eye but detectable through targeted sampling.

## Our Allergen Testing Process in Sanger

Allergen testing is not a single swab of a countertop. A meaningful assessment involves collecting dust samples from the locations where allergen exposure actually happens, sleeping areas, living room flooring, and HVAC filter surfaces, and sending those samples to an accredited laboratory for quantitative analysis.

The process typically works like this:

1. **Site walkthrough**, We assess the home's layout, HVAC type, pet history, and any recent water intrusion or renovation activity that could affect allergen distribution.
2. **Dust collection**, Samples are gathered using standardized vacuum collection methods from mattresses, carpeted floors, and upholstered surfaces. The quantity of dust collected per square foot matters; the lab needs enough material for accurate quantification.
3. **Laboratory analysis**, Samples are analyzed for common allergen groups: dust mite allergens (Der p 1, Der f 1), cat dander (Fel d 1), dog dander (Can f 1), cockroach allergens (Bla g 2), and mouse urinary protein (Mus m 1) where relevant.
4. **Written report**, Results are returned in a written report showing measured allergen levels against established health-based thresholds, so you and your physician have concrete data rather than subjective impressions.

## Equipment and Methods We Use for Allergen Testing

Dust sample allergen analysis depends heavily on how the sample is collected. We use high-volume vacuum collection with standardized nozzle attachments and collection filters sized to capture the fine particulate fraction where allergen proteins concentrate. Sample weights are recorded to allow the lab to normalize results per gram of dust, a step that makes results comparable across rooms and across visits if you retest after remediation or HVAC upgrades.

For clients concerned about airborne allergen levels rather than settled dust, air sampling with calibrated pumps and collection cassettes is also available. This method captures what is actively circulating at breathing height, which can be informative when symptoms are most pronounced during specific activities like vacuuming or making a bed.
