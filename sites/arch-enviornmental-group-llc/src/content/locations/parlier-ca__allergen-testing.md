## Why Parlier Homes Accumulate Allergens at Higher Rates

The Central Valley's climate does most of the work here. Parlier experiences long dry summers with low humidity and persistent wind, which keeps airborne particulates in circulation and drives them indoors through window gaps, door frames, and HVAC returns. Dust mite populations, by contrast, peak in the brief humid stretches of late fall and early spring when overnight moisture raises indoor relative humidity without residents noticing.

Agricultural activity around the city adds a layer that urban testing labs do not always account for. Pollen from row crops, orchard trees, and roadside grasses enters homes on clothing, through open windows during harvest season, and via evaporative coolers, a common fixture in Parlier households, that draw unfiltered outdoor air directly into living spaces. Pet dander from dogs and cats accumulates in carpets and upholstered furniture year-round, but the combination with crop dust and tracked-in soil makes it harder for residents to isolate which allergen is actually responsible for their symptoms.

Older housing in Parlier, much of it built in the mid-twentieth century with minimal insulation and single-pane windows, tends to have more air infiltration than newer construction, which means outdoor allergens move freely between the exterior environment and interior living areas.

## Our Allergen Testing Process in Parlier

Allergen testing is a sampling and laboratory analysis service, not a visual inspection. The process begins with a structured walkthrough of the home to identify the rooms and surfaces most likely to harbor concentrated allergen reservoirs: bedroom carpets, upholstered furniture, HVAC filter housings, and areas near evaporative cooler intakes.

Dust samples are collected using standardized wipe or vacuum methods from settled surfaces. These samples are then submitted to an accredited laboratory for analysis. Depending on what the household is concerned about, panels can screen for dust mite allergen (Der p 1 and Der f 1 proteins), cat and dog dander (Fel d 1 and Can f 1), cockroach allergen (Bla g 2), and mouse urinary proteins, among others.

Results come back as quantified allergen levels, not just positive or negative findings. That distinction matters because a low-level detection in a bedroom is a very different situation from a high-concentration finding in a child's sleeping area. The written report identifies where concentrations are highest and what remediation or environmental controls are likely to reduce exposure.

Given the prevalence of evaporative coolers in this part of Fresno County, we pay particular attention to the pads and interior housing of those units, which can accumulate both biological growth and allergen-laden dust that gets redistributed every time the system runs.

## Local Note

Evaporative coolers are standard equipment in Parlier and throughout this part of the Central Valley, and they create an allergen pathway that does not exist in air-conditioned homes. The water-saturated pads pull outdoor air through a wet medium, which is efficient for cooling but also captures and concentrates pollen, dust, and biological particulates inside the pad itself. When the cooler runs, that material gets pushed into the home. If your household runs an evaporative cooler from spring through early fall, sampling the cooler pad and the nearest interior surfaces is a step we include specifically because of how common this equipment is in the area.
