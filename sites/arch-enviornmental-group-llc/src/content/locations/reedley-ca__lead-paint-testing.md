## Why Reedley's Housing Stock Makes Lead Testing a Practical Priority

Reedley developed rapidly during the early-to-mid 1900s as the San Joaquin Valley's packing and agricultural economy expanded. Many of the craftsman bungalows, farmhouses, and mid-century tract homes that line the older residential streets near downtown were built during the decades when lead-based paint was the industry standard, used on interior trim, exterior siding, window sashes, and porch railings precisely because of its durability in the Valley's intense summer heat.

That same heat is worth noting. The Central Valley's cycle of scorching summers and cool, damp winters causes paint to expand, contract, and eventually chalk or peel, a process that accelerates on south- and west-facing surfaces that receive direct afternoon sun. On pre-1978 homes, peeling paint isn't just a cosmetic issue; it's a potential lead exposure pathway, particularly in homes with children or in properties being prepared for renovation.

Fresno County's pre-renovation notification and abatement requirements also apply here. Any contractor disturbing more than a threshold area of painted surface in a pre-1978 residence is subject to EPA Renovation, Repair, and Painting (RRP) rules. A lead inspection report from a certified professional gives your contractor the documentation they need to proceed correctly, or to know that additional precautions are required.

## Our Lead Paint Testing and Inspection Process in Reedley

Arch Environmental Group uses XRF (X-ray fluorescence) analysis as the primary testing method. XRF is non-destructive, the analyzer is held against the painted surface and delivers an immediate reading of lead concentration without chipping or sampling the paint. This matters in older Reedley homes where original trim and built-in cabinetry are often worth preserving; there's no damage to the surface being tested.

A full inspection covers every painted component in the structure: walls, ceilings, doors, door frames, window sills, baseboards, exterior siding, and outbuildings if present. Each component is logged by location and substrate, and the final report maps the results room by room so your contractor, real estate agent, or lender can read it without interpretation.

For situations where a full inspection isn't required, a targeted renovation scope, a landlord compliance check, or a specific area of concern, a lead risk assessment focuses on the condition of painted surfaces and the likelihood of exposure rather than cataloging every component. We'll discuss which approach fits your situation before scheduling.

Arch Environmental Group holds a Certified Lead Inspector/Assessor credential, which is the qualification required under California and federal standards to issue legally recognized lead inspection reports.

## Local Note

One pattern worth knowing in Reedley: many of the older homes near the downtown commercial district were built with multiple layers of paint applied over decades, sometimes five or six distinct layers on original wood trim. XRF reads through all of them simultaneously, which is one reason it's the preferred method over chip sampling in these situations. Chip sampling from a multi-layer surface can return a result that reflects only the topmost layers, potentially missing lead in a lower stratum that would be disturbed during sanding or stripping. In a city where the housing stock skews older and original woodwork is common, that distinction matters.
