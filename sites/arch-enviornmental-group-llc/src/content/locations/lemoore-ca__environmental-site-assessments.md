## Why Lemoore Properties Warrant Careful Environmental Due Diligence

Kings County agriculture has relied on pesticides, fumigants, and fertilizers for generations. Parcels that were farmed, or that border actively farmed land, carry a realistic potential for residual soil and groundwater impacts that a Phase I Environmental Site Assessment is specifically designed to surface. Beyond agriculture, the presence of NAS Lemoore introduces a secondary consideration: properties near the installation may fall within areas where historical fuel handling, solvent use, or other military-industrial activity has influenced the surrounding environment. A thorough review of regulatory databases, historical aerial photographs, and Sanborn fire insurance maps is essential for any commercial or industrial parcel in this corridor.

The region's soils also matter for Phase II work. The expansive, low-permeability clays common across the San Joaquin Valley floor can trap contaminants near the surface rather than allowing them to migrate quickly downward, which affects both the sampling strategy and the interpretation of results. Conversely, sand lenses within those clay profiles can create preferential pathways that move a plume laterally in ways that a coarse grid of borings will miss entirely.

## Our Environmental Site Assessment Process in Lemoore

A Phase I ESA follows the ASTM E1527-21 standard and consists of four core components: records review, site reconnaissance, interviews, and the written report. For Lemoore properties, the records review pays particular attention to the California GeoTracker and EnviroStar databases, USDA soil survey data for Kings County, and historical aerial imagery that documents land use back to the mid-twentieth century, the period when organochlorine pesticide applications were at their peak in this part of the valley.

Site reconnaissance in an agricultural-edge community like Lemoore means looking carefully at drainage patterns, the condition of any on-site storage tanks or chemical handling areas, and the relationship of the subject property to neighboring operations. If the Phase I identifies Recognized Environmental Conditions, a Phase II ESA moves the work into the field: soil borings, groundwater sampling, and laboratory analysis calibrated to the specific contaminants of concern identified in the Phase I report. ## Equipment and Methods for Site Assessments in Lemoore

Phase I work is documentation-driven, but Phase II fieldwork in the San Joaquin Valley clay environment typically calls for hollow-stem auger drilling rather than direct-push technology when borings need to reach depth through the dense near-surface clays.
