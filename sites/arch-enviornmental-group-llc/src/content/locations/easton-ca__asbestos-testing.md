## Why Easton-Area Properties Carry a Higher Asbestos Risk

The San Joaquin Valley building boom of the 1950s through the 1970s coincided with peak asbestos use in American construction. Tract homes and agricultural outbuildings in this region were often built quickly and economically, which meant liberal use of asbestos-containing materials in everything from roofing felt to joint compound. The dry, hot summers here can cause older materials to become brittle and friable over time, meaning fibers are more likely to become airborne when disturbed. That matters especially during the kind of remodeling projects common in Easton: kitchen gut-outs, bathroom retiles, popcorn ceiling removal, and HVAC replacements.

The Valley's temperature swings also accelerate the degradation of older insulation materials. Pipe wrap and boiler insulation in homes built before the mid-1980s can look intact but crumble at the touch. A visual inspection alone will not tell you whether a material is hazardous. Only laboratory-confirmed bulk sampling can.

## Our Asbestos Testing and Inspection Process

Arch Environmental Group's certified asbestos consultant conducts a systematic survey of the property, identifying all suspect materials and their condition before a single sample is collected. Suspect materials are categorized by type, friability, and accessibility, following the inspection protocols that California's Division of Occupational Safety and Health (Cal/OSHA) and the EPA both reference for pre-renovation surveys.

Bulk samples are collected using controlled technique to minimize fiber release during sampling. Each sample is sealed, labeled, and submitted to an accredited laboratory for polarized light microscopy (PLM) analysis, the method required for regulatory compliance in California. Results are compiled into a written report that identifies each material, its asbestos content by percentage, its location in the structure, and a recommended course of action. That report is the document your contractor, your county building department, and your insurance carrier will ask for.

## What the Inspection Report Means for Your Project

A completed asbestos survey gives you documented, defensible information before demolition or renovation begins. In California, contractors performing regulated demolition work are required to have a pre-demolition asbestos survey on file. Without one, a contractor who disturbs asbestos-containing material can face significant Cal/OSHA penalties, and the liability can flow back to the property owner.

For real estate transactions, a clean asbestos report can remove a material contingency and accelerate closing. For landlords, it documents the condition of the property and supports a maintenance record. For homeowners simply replacing a popcorn ceiling or pulling up old vinyl tile, it is the difference between a safe project and an unknowing exposure.
