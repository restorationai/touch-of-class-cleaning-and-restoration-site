## Why Dinuba Properties Warrant a Closer Look at Radon

Radon forms from the natural decay of uranium in soil and rock. The San Joaquin Valley's underlying geology, including alluvial fan deposits and granitic basement rock that extends through Tulare County, can produce measurable radon concentrations depending on local soil composition, moisture content, and how a structure is built. Homes with slab-on-grade foundations, which are common in Dinuba's newer subdivisions, can still accumulate radon if the slab has unsealed penetrations. Older homes with raised wood-floor construction and open crawlspaces present a different pathway: soil gas enters the crawlspace and migrates into living areas through gaps in the subfloor.

California does not currently mandate radon testing at the point of sale, which means many Dinuba properties have never been evaluated. That regulatory gap puts the responsibility squarely on homeowners and buyers to initiate testing on their own.

## Our Radon Testing Process in Dinuba

A radon inspection is not a single-number snapshot, it's a measurement protocol designed to produce a result you can act on. Our process in Dinuba follows established EPA and state guidance:

**Initial assessment.** Before placing any device, we walk the structure to identify foundation type, visible slab cracks, crawlspace access points, and any prior mitigation systems. This step matters because a home with an existing sub-slab depressurization pipe that was never connected to a fan may read differently than an untouched property.

**Device placement.** We use calibrated continuous radon monitors for short-term testing. These electronic devices log radon concentrations at regular intervals throughout the test period, producing an hour-by-hour record rather than a single averaged result. Closed-house conditions are required during the measurement window.

**Data review and reporting.** After the test period, we retrieve the monitor, download the data, and produce a written report showing average concentration in picocuries per liter (pCi/L), the EPA action level for comparison, and our findings. If levels are at or above 4 pCi/L, the report will note that mitigation should be evaluated by a qualified contractor.

## Equipment and Methods We Use for Radon

Continuous radon monitors offer a significant advantage over passive charcoal canisters in real-world testing situations. Because they log data over time, they can reveal whether a spike occurred during a specific period, a useful data point if a window was opened or HVAC operation changed during the test. Our team holds certifications including NIOSH 582 Microscopist credentials, reflecting a broader commitment to laboratory-grade environmental measurement standards. Radon testing is a distinct discipline, but the underlying discipline of precise environmental sampling runs through all of our work.

For longer-term baseline measurements, we can discuss alpha-track detector options, which capture a 90-day or longer average and are sometimes preferred by buyers who want a more seasonally representative picture of a home's radon behavior.

## Local Note

One pattern worth knowing in Dinuba and the surrounding Tulare County communities: homes built during the postwar agricultural expansion of the 1950s and 1960s often used concrete block or poured-concrete perimeter walls with minimal sealing at the mudsill, the horizontal lumber that connects the framing to the foundation. Over decades, that joint can open up as the concrete cures and the wood settles, creating a direct pathway for soil gas to enter wall cavities. If you're testing an older Dinuba home and the result comes back elevated, that mudsill gap is one of the first places a mitigation contractor will look.
