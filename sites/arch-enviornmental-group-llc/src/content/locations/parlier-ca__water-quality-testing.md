## Why Parlier Properties Face Distinct Water Quality Concerns

The San Joaquin Valley has one of the most agriculturally productive landscapes in the country, and Parlier is surrounded by it. Fertilizer applications, pesticide use, and decades of irrigation have left a measurable legacy in the region's groundwater. Nitrate contamination is a documented concern across Fresno County's smaller communities, and private wells are particularly vulnerable because they sit outside the monitoring obligations that apply to public water systems.

Beyond agricultural inputs, the Valley's geology contributes naturally occurring arsenic and uranium to groundwater in some areas. These are not contamination events in the dramatic sense; they are slow, background conditions that accumulate quietly. A single water test is the only way to know whether your well or your home's plumbing is delivering water that meets health-based standards.

Older homes in Parlier may also carry a different risk: lead-bearing solder and brass fittings were standard in residential plumbing installed before 1986. Even if the source water is clean, lead can leach from the plumbing itself, especially when water sits in pipes overnight or during periods of low use.

## Equipment and Methods We Use for Water Testing

Field instruments allow us to capture readings for parameters that change rapidly after a sample is collected, including temperature, dissolved oxygen, and certain volatile compounds. For regulated contaminants, laboratory analysis using EPA-approved methods is the standard, and it is the only result that carries weight with a lender, a health department, or a real estate transaction.

For lead specifically, first-draw sampling, where water is collected after sitting in the pipes for a defined period, is the protocol that captures worst-case exposure. We follow that procedure rather than a flush sample, which can underreport lead from interior plumbing.
