## Why Hanford Properties Generate Environmental Disputes

The Central Valley's climate is unforgiving in ways that accelerate moisture and air quality problems. Hanford experiences triple-digit summer temperatures followed by cold, foggy winters, and that thermal cycling stresses building envelopes in ways that are easy to overlook until a dispute surfaces. Older residential construction throughout Kings County frequently relied on slab-on-grade foundations with minimal vapor barriers, which means ground moisture can migrate upward through concrete and feed hidden mold colonies long before a tenant notices a musty smell or a discolored wall.

The region's agricultural economy also means many properties, including converted farmworker housing and older commercial buildings, were built to standards that predate current California Title 24 requirements. Habitability disputes in this context often hinge on whether a condition existed before or after a tenancy began, and that question cannot be answered without scientifically collected, chain-of-custody samples taken at the right locations and the right time.

## Our Forensic Sampling and Litigation Support Process

Forensic environmental work is fundamentally different from a routine inspection. Every decision, from where a bulk sample is collected to how a swab is labeled, is made with the assumption that opposing counsel will scrutinize the methodology. Our process is built around that standard.

We begin with a site assessment that documents the physical conditions relevant to the dispute: moisture readings, visible staining, HVAC pathways, and construction features that could explain or contradict a party's claims. Sampling is then conducted using protocols appropriate to the contaminant in question, whether that is mold spore trap analysis, bulk material collection for asbestos or lead, or surface swabs for microbial growth. All samples travel under documented chain of custody to an accredited laboratory, and the resulting data is organized into a report written to be understood by a judge or jury, not just an industrial hygienist.

Where the case requires it, our certified asbestos consultant and lead inspector assessor credentials allow us to offer testimony on the significance of findings, the adequacy of prior remediation, or the likely source and timeline of contamination.

## Local Note

One pattern worth knowing in Kings County litigation: properties built during the postwar agricultural expansion of the 1950s and 1960s often used lightweight stucco cladding over wood framing without a continuous weather-resistive barrier. When those walls are sampled during a moisture or mold dispute, surface readings can look acceptable while the wood sheathing behind the stucco is actively decaying. Experienced forensic sampling in this building type requires invasive sampling at the wall cavity, not just surface swabs, and that distinction has changed the direction of more than one habitability case in the Central Valley.
