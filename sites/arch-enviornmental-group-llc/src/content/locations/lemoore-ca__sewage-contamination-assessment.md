## Why Lemoore Properties Face Distinct Sewage Contamination Risks

The valley floor around Lemoore has a high water table in certain areas, and the region's clay-heavy soils don't drain quickly. When a main line backs up or a septic system fails, that Category 3 water doesn't just sit, it wicks laterally through slab-on-grade foundations and into wall cavities faster than it would in areas with sandier soil profiles. Many homes in the Lemoore area were built during the mid-20th century expansion tied to Naval Air Station Lemoore and the surrounding agricultural economy. That era of construction often means cast-iron or Orangeburg drain lines that have reached or exceeded their service life, making backup events more common and the contamination more complex to assess.

Agricultural runoff patterns in Kings County also mean that outdoor sewage events can interact with soil that already carries elevated biological load from nearby land use. That context matters when a licensed assessor is interpreting bacterial contamination testing results and advising on remediation scope.

## Our Sewage Contamination Assessment Process in Lemoore

The assessment begins with a visual and instrument-based survey of the affected area to establish the contamination boundary. This is not a walk-through with a clipboard. It involves moisture mapping with calibrated meters, surface sampling for bacterial indicators, and documentation of all affected materials with photographs and written findings that meet insurance carrier standards.

For slab-on-grade homes common throughout Lemoore, we pay close attention to the perimeter where flooring meets the wall base, since Category 3 water migrates under vinyl plank, laminate, and tile before it becomes visible at the surface. Air quality sampling may be included when the backup has been present long enough for secondary biological growth to begin, which in Lemoore's summer heat can happen within 24 to 48 hours of the initial event.

The final deliverable is a written assessment report that identifies affected zones, classifies contamination level, and outlines the remediation scope a qualified contractor will need to follow. That report is the document your insurance adjuster, your remediation contractor, and potentially your local building department will reference.
