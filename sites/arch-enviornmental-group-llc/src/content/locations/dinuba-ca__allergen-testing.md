## Why Dinuba Properties See Elevated Indoor Allergen Loads

The San Joaquin Valley's agricultural landscape is one of the most productive in the world, and Dinuba is surrounded by it, vineyards, citrus groves, and row crops that generate pollen, mold spores, and fine particulate matter across multiple seasons. Unlike coastal California cities where marine air provides some dilution, the valley's geography tends to trap airborne particles close to the ground, especially during temperature inversions in fall and winter.

Inside homes, that outdoor load compounds with common indoor sources. Dust mites thrive in the warm, occasionally humid conditions that Dinuba sees during late summer and early fall. Pet dander from dogs and cats accumulates in carpet fibers, upholstered furniture, and HVAC ductwork. Older housing stock, particularly homes built before the 1980s, often has less airtight construction, which means more outdoor allergens infiltrating through gaps around windows, doors, and crawlspace vents. Evaporative coolers, which are common in the valley because of the dry climate, draw unfiltered outdoor air directly into living spaces, bypassing the filtration a central air system would provide.

## Our Allergen Testing Process in Dinuba

Allergen testing is not a single-method service. The right sampling approach depends on what you suspect, how the home is used, and where symptoms tend to flare. Our process typically involves:

**Surface dust sampling**, a measured wipe or vacuum collection from floors, upholstered furniture, and bedding that captures settled allergen reservoirs. Dust mite allergen (Der p 1, Der f 1) and pet dander proteins (Fel d 1 for cats, Can f 1 for dogs) are quantified against established health-based thresholds.

**Air sampling**, impaction or cassette-based collection that captures what's actively circulating at breathing height. This is particularly useful when symptoms are worst during certain activities or times of day.

**HVAC and duct screening**, in homes with forced-air systems or evaporative coolers, we collect from supply registers and return air pathways, where allergen concentrations often tell a different story than the main living areas.

Samples are analyzed by an accredited laboratory. You receive a written report with measured concentrations, comparison to published guidance levels, and a plain-language summary of what the numbers mean for the people living in the home.

## Local Note

One detail that comes up repeatedly in valley homes with evaporative coolers: the water reservoir and distribution pads inside these units can harbor mold and bacteria, and when the cooler runs, it aerosolizes whatever has colonized those pads directly into the home. Allergen testing that focuses only on dust mite and pet dander can miss this entirely. When we test a Dinuba home that relies on a swamp cooler as the primary cooling system, we include a discussion of the unit's condition and recommend sampling near supply registers, because what the cooler is pushing into the living space matters as much as what's already settled on the floor.
