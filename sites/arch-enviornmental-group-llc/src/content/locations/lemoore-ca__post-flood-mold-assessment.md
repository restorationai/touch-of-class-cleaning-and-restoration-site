## Why Lemoore Properties Face Elevated Mold Risk After Flooding

The agricultural flatlands surrounding Lemoore are irrigated intensively, and the water table in parts of Kings County sits closer to the surface than most homeowners realize. When a flood event saturates a slab-on-grade foundation, the dominant construction type in Lemoore's residential neighborhoods, moisture wicks upward through the concrete and into wall framing and flooring systems. Unlike a crawl-space home where you can visually inspect the understructure, slab homes hide saturation behind drywall and under vinyl plank or tile. Surface readings can look dry while the bottom plate and lower stud cavities stay wet for weeks.

Lemoore's housing stock includes a significant number of homes built during the 1960s through 1980s, many of them constructed with paper-faced drywall and fiberglass batt insulation, both highly susceptible to mold colonization once they absorb water. Older homes in the city core may also contain materials that require additional evaluation before any remediation work begins, including asbestos-containing floor tile and textured ceiling coatings that were common in that era.

## Our Post-Flood Mold Assessment Process in Lemoore

A thorough assessment starts before any samples are collected. The first step is moisture mapping: using calibrated pin-type and non-invasive radio-frequency meters to build a room-by-room picture of where water traveled and where it stopped. In Lemoore homes, that often means checking the full perimeter of slab edges, interior closets adjacent to exterior walls, and any below-grade mechanical spaces.

Once the moisture map is complete, we conduct a visual inspection for visible fungal growth, staining, and odor patterns. Air sampling follows, using spore-trap cassettes pulled at a measured flow rate and analyzed by an accredited laboratory. We collect both indoor samples and an outdoor control sample, a step that matters in the San Joaquin Valley because ambient outdoor spore counts here vary significantly by season and agricultural activity, and a valid comparison baseline is essential for interpreting results accurately.

All samples are analyzed by a NIOSH 582-trained microscopist, which means the person reading your results is credentialed specifically in airborne particle identification, not just running a general environmental screen. The written report identifies species present, quantifies concentrations, flags any readings that exceed outdoor baseline levels, and gives a clear scope for remediation if one is warranted.
