## Why Ivanhoe Properties Face Distinct Clearance Challenges

The housing stock in Ivanhoe and the surrounding Tulare County corridor includes a significant share of mid-century construction, much of it built during the agricultural expansion of the 1950s and 1960s. Homes from that era frequently contain materials, floor tile mastics, pipe insulation, textured ceiling coatings, that were installed before asbestos restrictions took effect. When a water loss or mold event triggers remediation in one of these properties, asbestos clearance testing is often required alongside air quality verification, not just a visual inspection.

The area's clay-heavy soils also affect crawl spaces and slab foundations differently than sandy soils further north. Clay retains moisture longer after a plumbing leak or roof intrusion, which means sub-slab and under-floor cavities can stay damp well after surface materials appear dry. Post-remediation verification in these spaces requires moisture mapping in addition to air sampling to confirm conditions won't support renewed microbial growth.

## Our Clearance Testing and Post-Remediation Verification Process

Clearance testing is an independent confirmation step, not a formality performed by the same crew that did the remediation work. The process begins with a visual assessment of the remediated area to confirm containment barriers have been removed properly and that no visible residue remains. From there, air samples are collected using calibrated pumps and cassettes following established industrial hygiene protocols.

For mold clearance, spore trap samples are analyzed by an accredited laboratory and compared against outdoor control samples taken the same day. For asbestos clearance, phase contrast microscopy or transmission electron microscopy is used depending on the scope of the prior abatement. As a Certified Asbestos Consultant and NIOSH 582 Microscopist, Arch Environmental Group LLC can perform both the field sampling and the microscopic analysis in-house, which shortens the turnaround on your remediation clearance letter.

When results meet the applicable clearance criteria, a written verification report is issued. That document is what your contractor, insurer, or county health office needs before reconstruction work begins.
