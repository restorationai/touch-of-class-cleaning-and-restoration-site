Easton sits in the heart of California's San Joaquin Valley, where decades of agricultural growth brought a mix of older farmhouses, mid-century worker housing, and more recent residential development. In communities like this, built across several construction eras, lead-based paint turns up in places that catch homeowners off guard: window sashes painted shut, porch trim that chalks in the summer heat, and interior walls that look perfectly sound until a renovation crew cuts into them. If you are planning a remodel, selling a home, or responding to a landlord compliance requirement, a professional lead paint testing and inspection is the right first step before any work begins.

## Why Easton Properties Carry a Higher Lead Paint Risk

Fresno County's agricultural communities, including Easton, developed rapidly during the post-World War II era and again in the 1960s and 1970s. Housing built before 1978, when the federal government banned residential use of lead-based paint, is common throughout the area. In the Central Valley's climate, with intense summer heat and dry winters, painted surfaces expand and contract repeatedly across the seasons. That thermal cycling accelerates paint chalking and micro-cracking, which releases lead dust at a rate that newer construction simply does not produce.

Older wood-framed homes in the area frequently have multiple paint layers applied over decades, meaning that even surfaces that appear stable can contain lead at depth. Friction surfaces such as door frames, window channels, and cabinet hardware are the most likely sources of ongoing lead dust exposure, and they are also the surfaces most likely to be disturbed during routine maintenance.

## Our Lead Paint Testing and Inspection Process in Easton

A thorough inspection begins with a visual assessment of all painted surfaces, followed by XRF (X-ray fluorescence) testing, which reads lead concentration in paint layers without requiring destructive sampling. XRF is fast, non-invasive, and produces component-by-component results that a renovation contractor can act on immediately.

Where XRF results are inconclusive or where sampling is required for regulatory compliance, we collect paint chip samples and submit them to an accredited laboratory. For properties where occupant exposure is the concern rather than renovation planning, a lead risk assessment adds dust wipe sampling and soil sampling to the scope, identifying actual exposure pathways rather than just paint condition.

Arch Environmental Group LLC holds certification as a Lead Inspector Assessor, which means the inspection report we produce carries the regulatory standing required for HUD compliance, California Department of Public Health disclosure requirements, and pre-renovation documentation under the EPA's RRP (Renovation, Repair, and Painting) rule.

## What the Inspection Report Covers

The written report produced after a lead inspection identifies every tested component, the lead concentration found at each, and whether that concentration meets or exceeds the federal hazard threshold. For renovation projects, the report tells your contractor exactly which surfaces require lead-safe work practices under California's RRP regulations and which are clear to proceed without special precautions.

For property sales, the report satisfies the seller disclosure requirement for pre-1978 housing and gives buyers documented information rather than a checkbox disclosure. For landlords responding to a tenant complaint or a public health referral, the risk assessment format adds the exposure pathway analysis that regulatory agencies typically require.
