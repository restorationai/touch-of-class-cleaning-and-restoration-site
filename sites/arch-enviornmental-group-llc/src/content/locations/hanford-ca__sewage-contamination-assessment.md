## Why Hanford Properties Face Elevated Sewage Contamination Risk

The San Joaquin Valley's expansive clay soils shift seasonally, and in Hanford that movement puts steady lateral pressure on older sewer laterals. Many residential properties in the city were built during the postwar boom of the 1950s through 1970s, when cast-iron and Orangeburg pipe were the standard for drain lines running from the house to the city main. Both materials degrade over time: Orangeburg softens and collapses, cast iron corrodes from the inside out. When a lateral fails or the city main surges during a heavy rain event, Category 3 water, the industry classification for grossly contaminated wastewater, can push back through floor drains, toilets, and sink traps.

Hanford's climate adds another layer of complexity. Summer temperatures regularly exceed 100°F, and a sewage-affected crawl space or subfloor cavity in those conditions becomes a rapid incubator for bacterial growth. Assessment work conducted in the warmer months requires faster sample turnaround and careful documentation of ambient conditions at the time of testing, because temperature and humidity readings at the time of sampling are part of the evidentiary record for insurance carriers.

## Our Sewage Contamination Assessment Process in Hanford

Assessment begins with a structured walkthrough to map the contamination boundary, the line between confirmed contact and unaffected materials. That boundary is not always obvious. Wastewater wicks through grout lines, travels under vinyl plank flooring, and saturates the bottom plates of framed walls without visible staining at the surface. Moisture meters and thermal imaging help establish where the water traveled; surface and bulk sampling then confirm whether bacterial contamination followed the same path.

Samples are submitted to an accredited laboratory for analysis targeting fecal coliform indicators and other pathogens associated with Category 3 water events. Results are compiled into a written assessment report that identifies affected materials by location, documents pre-remediation conditions with photographs, and provides a scope of work for remediation contractors to follow. After remediation is complete, clearance sampling verifies that bacterial counts have returned to acceptable levels before the report is closed out. That two-phase structure, pre-remediation assessment followed by post-remediation clearance, is what insurance carriers and public health guidance both require before a property is considered restored.

## Local Note

One pattern worth knowing in Hanford: a number of the city's older residential blocks were built on slab-on-grade foundations with the drain lines running through the slab itself. When a slab-penetrating drain fails and sewage migrates beneath the concrete, it can travel several feet in either direction before surfacing, or it may not surface at all, remaining trapped under the slab. Assessment in these situations requires sampling at multiple points along the suspected migration path, not just at the point of visible backup. If your property was built before 1975 and sits on a slab, mention that when you call, so we can plan the assessment scope accordingly.
