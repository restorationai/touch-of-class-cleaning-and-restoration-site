That cycle of drying and re-wetting is exactly the kind of environment where dust mites, pet dander, and mold-adjacent allergens accumulate quietly inside homes before anyone notices a pattern of sneezing, congestion, or skin irritation. ## Why Squaw Valley Homes Accumulate Allergens Differently

The Sierra foothills climate is not like the Central Valley floor. Squaw Valley properties experience a longer cool-and-damp shoulder season than communities at lower elevations, and many homes here rely on forced-air heating systems that sit idle through the summer, collecting dust and dander in ductwork before cycling that material back into living spaces when temperatures drop in the fall. Homes with wood-burning stoves or pellet inserts, common in this area, add combustion particulates to the mix that can carry and concentrate biological allergens.

Crawl-space construction is prevalent in the foothills, and the ground moisture that comes with snowmelt and spring rain creates a reservoir for dust mite habitat directly beneath living areas. Carpet over a damp subfloor is one of the most efficient allergen traps there is. If your home has older carpet and a vented crawl space, allergen loads in the lowest rooms can run significantly higher than the rooms above.

## Our Allergen Testing Process in Squaw Valley

Allergen testing is not a single swab and a yes-or-no answer. The process starts with a structured walkthrough to identify likely accumulation zones: upholstered furniture, mattresses, area rugs, HVAC registers, and any areas with visible dust accumulation or past moisture history. From those zones, we collect dust samples using validated collection methods and send them to an accredited laboratory for quantified analysis.

The lab results identify specific allergen types and concentrations, including dust mite allergen (Der p 1 and Der f 1), cat and dog dander (Fel d 1, Can f 1), and cockroach allergen where relevant. Quantified results matter because they tell you not just whether an allergen is present but whether it is present at levels associated with sensitization risk. That distinction shapes what remediation, if any, makes sense.

Because Arch Environmental Group holds a Certified Asbestos Consultant and Lead Inspector Assessor credential, our site assessors are trained to recognize building material conditions that can complicate allergen accumulation, such as deteriorating insulation or aged HVAC components that may warrant separate evaluation alongside the allergen screen.

## Local Note

One pattern worth knowing about Squaw Valley and the surrounding foothills: homes that were built or expanded in the 1970s and 1980s often used fiberglass batt insulation in floor systems over crawl spaces, and that insulation can sag or detach over decades, leaving gaps where cold, moist air from the crawl space communicates directly with the subfloor above. That airflow path carries whatever is in the crawl space, including dust mite habitat and rodent dander, straight into the rooms where people sleep and spend time. If your home was built in that era and you have unexplained allergy symptoms concentrated in ground-floor rooms, the crawl space is worth including in any allergen assessment.
