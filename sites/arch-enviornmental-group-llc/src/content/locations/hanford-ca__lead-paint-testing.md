## Why Hanford Properties See Lead Paint Concerns

Kings County's housing inventory skews older than many California coastal markets. Hanford's core residential areas developed substantially between the 1920s and the 1960s, a period when lead carbonate and lead chromate pigments were standard ingredients in both interior and exterior paints. Those layers don't disappear with time. They get painted over, and over again, until a renovation, a sanding project, or simple deterioration disturbs them.

The Central Valley climate adds a layer of complexity. Hanford's hot, dry summers and cool, occasionally damp winters cause wood substrates to expand and contract repeatedly across the year. That cycling stresses paint films, producing the chalking, flaking, and alligatoring that creates lead dust and paint chip hazards, particularly on window sills, door frames, and exterior siding where movement is greatest. A visual inspection alone cannot tell you whether that peeling paint contains lead at concentrations above the federal hazard threshold. Only testing can.

## Our Lead Paint Testing and Inspection Process in Hanford

The inspection begins with a systematic walk-through of the structure, documenting painted surfaces by room, component type, and visible condition. From there, XRF (X-ray fluorescence) analysis is the primary tool: a calibrated analyzer reads lead concentration in milligrams per square centimeter through the full paint layer stack without requiring the surface to be disturbed or destroyed. Results are immediate, and the data is logged by component so you receive a room-by-room report rather than a single pass/fail number.

Where XRF readings fall in an inconclusive range, or where a surface geometry limits the analyzer's contact, paint chip samples are collected and submitted to an accredited laboratory for confirmation. The final report identifies which components contain lead-based paint, which present an immediate hazard based on condition, and which are intact and manageable in place. That distinction matters for renovation planning: not every lead-containing surface requires abatement before work begins, but every one requires a documented plan.

Arch Environmental Group holds certification as a Lead Inspector/Assessor, which means the inspection report we produce satisfies California's requirements for pre-renovation disclosure and contractor notification under the EPA's Renovation, Repair, and Painting rule.
