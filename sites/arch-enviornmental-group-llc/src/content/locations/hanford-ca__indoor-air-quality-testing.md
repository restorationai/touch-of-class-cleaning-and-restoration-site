## Why Hanford Properties See Air Quality Issues

Kings County's agricultural economy is one of Hanford's defining features, and it shapes indoor air quality in ways most residents don't immediately connect. Fumigants, fertilizer particulates, and field-burn smoke can infiltrate homes through attic vents, crawl space gaps, and aging weatherstripping, especially in neighborhoods where homes were built in the 1950s through 1970s, when construction standards for envelope tightness were far more relaxed than today's Title 24 requirements.

The Valley's climate compounds the problem in a second way: the combination of hot, dry summers and cool, damp winters creates conditions where mold can establish itself in crawl spaces and wall cavities even in a region most people think of as arid. Overnight humidity in winter months, paired with poorly insulated slab foundations common in mid-century Hanford construction, can produce enough condensation to sustain mold colonies that never fully dry out between seasons. Meanwhile, older homes in Hanford may still contain asbestos-containing materials in floor tiles, pipe insulation, or textured ceilings, materials that release fibers into the air when disturbed during remodels or routine HVAC maintenance.

## Our Indoor Air Quality Testing Process in Hanford

Air quality testing is not a single sample and a report, it's a systematic process calibrated to the specific contaminants most likely in a given building and region. For Hanford properties, that typically means evaluating for mold spores and mycotoxins, particulate matter (including PM2.5 from outdoor agricultural sources that have migrated indoors), volatile organic compounds (VOCs) from building materials and cleaning products, and, where the construction era warrants it, asbestos fiber counts.

The process begins with a visual assessment of the HVAC system, attic, crawl space, and any areas with visible staining or moisture history. From there, air samples are collected using calibrated pumps and cassettes, some designed for mold spore analysis, others for asbestos fibers under NIOSH 582 methodology. Bulk material samples may be collected where suspect asbestos-containing materials are identified. All samples go to an accredited laboratory, and the resulting report identifies what was found, at what concentration, and what remediation steps, if any, are warranted. As a Certified Asbestos Consultant and Lead Inspector Assessor, Arch Environmental Group can assess the full range of legacy building materials that are still common in the Valley's older housing stock.

## Local Note

One pattern worth knowing in Hanford's older residential areas: homes built on concrete slabs in the 1960s and early 1970s often used vinyl asbestos tile (VAT) as the primary flooring material, then had new flooring installed directly on top rather than removed. That layering is common enough in this part of the Valley that any remodel project, even something as routine as replacing carpet, should be preceded by a material assessment before any cutting, grinding, or adhesive removal begins.
