## Why Riverdale Properties See Elevated VOC and Formaldehyde Concerns

The Central Valley's climate does something that cooler coastal regions don't: it bakes the interiors of buildings. Composite wood products, vinyl flooring, spray foam insulation, and paint all release VOCs at higher rates as temperature rises. A home that passed a builder's inspection in March can have measurably different air chemistry by July, when indoor temperatures climb and windows stay shut against the outdoor heat and agricultural dust common to Fresno County.

Riverdale's agricultural surroundings also mean that many properties, residential and commercial alike, sit near fields where pesticide applications occur seasonally. While outdoor pesticide drift is a separate concern, it adds to the broader picture of indoor air quality that residents here are increasingly aware of. Homes with aging HVAC systems that pull in outside air without adequate filtration can compound the problem.

New construction and renovation activity in the area has also brought in a wave of engineered lumber, laminate flooring, and prefabricated cabinetry, many of which use urea-formaldehyde resins as binders. These materials are legal and widely used, but they do off-gas, and in a tightly sealed Central Valley home, that off-gassing has nowhere to go.

## Equipment and Methods We Use for VOC Testing

Formaldehyde testing typically uses passive diffusion badges or active sorbent tube sampling, depending on the exposure duration and the sensitivity required. VOC testing uses Summa canisters or thermal desorption tubes that capture a broad spectrum of compounds for laboratory analysis. We do not rely on handheld photoionization detectors alone for final results, since those instruments screen for total VOCs but cannot identify individual compounds or meet the documentation threshold needed for insurance claims, legal disputes, or pre-purchase inspections.

For new construction or post-renovation testing, we can structure the sampling protocol to align with LEED or WELL building standard methodologies, which is increasingly relevant for commercial tenants and property managers who need documentation for occupancy decisions.
