## Our Clearance Testing and Post-Remediation Verification Process in Parlier

Clearance testing is not a formality, it is a structured, methodology-driven confirmation that remediation met its objectives. The process typically follows these stages:

**Visual inspection first.** Before any air or surface samples are collected, a thorough visual assessment confirms that containment has been removed, affected materials are gone, and the work area is visually clean. In the Valley's dusty environment, this step matters more than it might in a coastal city, agricultural particulates can mask or complicate sample readings if the workspace is not properly prepared.

**Air sampling and surface sampling.** Depending on the scope of work, clearance may involve spore trap air samples, tape lift or swab surface samples for mold, or phase contrast microscopy (PCM) and transmission electron microscopy (TEM) for asbestos fiber analysis. Arch Environmental Group's certified asbestos consultant and NIOSH 582 microscopist credentials mean fiber identification is handled with laboratory-grade precision, not estimated in the field.

**Laboratory analysis and reporting.** Samples go to an accredited laboratory. Results are interpreted against clearance criteria, and a written post-remediation verification report documents the findings. That report is what your insurance adjuster, property manager, or future buyer will ask to see.

**Clearance letter issuance.** When results confirm the space meets clearance standards, a formal remediation clearance letter is issued. This is the document that closes out the project and protects all parties.
