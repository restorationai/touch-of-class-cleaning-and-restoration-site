## Why Fowler Properties See Duct Contamination Issues

The San Joaquin Valley's climate is one of the most demanding in California for HVAC equipment. Fowler's summers are long and intensely hot, which means air conditioning systems run nearly continuously from May through September. That sustained runtime pulls enormous volumes of outdoor air across filters and into duct systems, air that, in this part of the valley, carries fine agricultural dust, pollen, and during harvest season, crop residue particulates that standard filters are not always rated to capture.

Winter brings a different problem. Valley fog, the thick tule fog that settles across Fresno County from December through February, drives indoor humidity up in homes that are otherwise tightly closed against the cold. Older construction in Fowler, including wood-frame homes built during the mid-twentieth century agricultural boom, often lacks the vapor management details that modern builds include. When warm, moist air contacts the cooler interior surfaces of ductwork, condensation can form, and that moisture is exactly what mold colonies need to establish themselves inside a system that then circulates air throughout every room.

## Our HVAC and Duct System Assessment Process in Fowler

An assessment is not the same as a cleaning estimate. Our process begins with a visual and instrumental inspection of the air handler, return and supply plenums, and accessible duct runs, looking for visible microbial growth, debris accumulation, liner deterioration, and signs of past moisture intrusion. Where conditions suggest contamination, we collect air and surface samples using methods consistent with NIOSH and AIHA guidelines, then submit them to an accredited third-party laboratory.

In Fowler's agricultural setting, we pay particular attention to return-air pathways. Homes and commercial buildings near field operations sometimes have duct penetrations or poorly sealed register boots that allow soil-level particulates to bypass the filter entirely. We document what we find with photographs and written observations, and the final report identifies whether remediation is warranted, what scope would address the problem, and, critically, what a post-remediation verification test should confirm before the system is returned to normal use. Because Arch Environmental Group is the assessment firm, not the remediation contractor, our findings are not shaped by an interest in selling you a cleaning job.

## Local Note

One pattern worth knowing in Fowler and the surrounding valley communities: evaporative coolers, swamp coolers, were the dominant cooling technology in many homes built before central air became standard, and some properties still use them either as primary cooling or alongside a refrigerated system. Evaporative coolers introduce significant outdoor humidity directly into the living space and into any connected ductwork. When a homeowner later adds or upgrades to a refrigerated air system without fully decommissioning or isolating the old evaporative unit, the interaction between the two systems can create persistent moisture conditions inside the ducts that neither system alone would produce. If your home has had both systems at any point, that history is worth mentioning when you schedule an assessment.
