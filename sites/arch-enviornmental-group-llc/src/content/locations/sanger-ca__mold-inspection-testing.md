## Why Sanger Properties Are Prone to Mold Growth

The Central Valley's agricultural economy shapes Sanger's built environment in ways that matter for mold risk. Many homes in the area were constructed during mid-century growth periods, when vapor barriers were minimal or absent entirely and crawl spaces were left unencapsulated. Irrigation canals and orchard drainage nearby keep the water table relatively accessible, and slab foundations in older neighborhoods can wick ground moisture upward through concrete that was never sealed. Add in the region's periodic tule fog season, when condensation settles on every surface for weeks at a stretch, and you have a recipe for chronic, low-level moisture intrusion that mold spores are well-adapted to exploit.

HVAC systems in Sanger homes also deserve attention. Units that cycle hard through the summer to combat valley heat tend to accumulate condensation in ducts and air handlers. When those systems aren't maintained, mold colonization inside ductwork can distribute spores throughout an entire house with every cooling cycle, making air sampling for mold an especially important part of any thorough assessment here.

## Our Mold Inspection and Testing Process in Sanger

A mold assessment from Arch Environmental Group starts with a visual walkthrough that goes beyond what's visible at eye level. We check crawl spaces, attic decking, the interior of HVAC air handlers, and any area with a history of plumbing leaks or roof intrusion. ## Local Note: Valley Construction and Hidden Moisture

One pattern worth knowing about in Sanger and the surrounding Fresno County communities: homes built in the 1950s through 1970s frequently used stucco exteriors over wood lath, and the weep screed at the base of those walls was often installed too close to grade, or backfilled over entirely during later landscaping work. When irrigation water or rainwater pools against the foundation, it can travel behind the stucco layer and saturate the lath and sheathing for months before any interior sign appears. If you're buying or selling a property in Sanger and the stucco grade line looks buried, that's worth flagging for the inspection, it's a common source of the kind of slow, hidden moisture that mold thrives on.
