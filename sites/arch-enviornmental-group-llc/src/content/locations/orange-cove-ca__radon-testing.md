The gas forms naturally as uranium in underlying rock and soil decays, and it enters homes silently through foundation cracks, crawl space vents, and gaps around utility penetrations. ## Why Orange Cove Properties See Radon Concerns

The Central Valley's eastern edge, where Orange Cove is situated in Fresno County, sits closer to Sierra Nevada granite than most valley floor communities. Granitic rock and the decomposed granite soils derived from it carry naturally elevated uranium concentrations. As that uranium breaks down, radon gas migrates upward through the soil column. In a well-ventilated open field that migration is harmless, but beneath a slab foundation or inside a sealed crawl space, the gas accumulates.

Orange Cove's housing stock adds another layer of consideration. Many homes here were built in the mid-twentieth century, when foundation sealing standards were minimal and crawl space vapor barriers were rarely installed. Older construction with unsealed block foundations, open sump pits, or original concrete slabs that have developed hairline cracks over decades gives radon more entry points than a newer home with a sealed slab and modern weatherproofing. The region's hot, dry summers also mean homes are closed up for air conditioning much of the year, limiting natural dilution.

## Our Radon Testing Process in Orange Cove

A radon inspection begins with a site assessment: we look at foundation type, crawl space conditions, and any existing ventilation before placing test devices. For most residential properties in Orange Cove, we use continuous radon monitoring equipment that logs readings hour by hour over a minimum 48-hour closed-house period. Continuous monitors give a time-resolved record rather than a single averaged number, which matters when a home's ventilation habits or HVAC cycling might otherwise skew a passive charcoal canister result.

After the monitoring period, we retrieve the equipment, download the data log, and produce a written report showing average radon concentration in picocuries per liter (pCi/L) alongside the hour-by-hour trend. The EPA action level is 4 pCi/L. If results exceed that threshold, the report includes a clear explanation of mitigation options so you can have an informed conversation with a licensed radon mitigator before any work begins. We do not perform mitigation ourselves, which means our testing results carry no financial interest in the outcome.

Arch Environmental Group uses calibrated continuous electronic monitors rather than relying solely on passive charcoal canisters. Identifying that pathway early shapes both the test placement and any subsequent mitigation design.

One pattern worth knowing in this part of Fresno County: homes built on the valley's eastern agricultural fringe, including many in and around Orange Cove, often have original concrete block stem walls rather than poured concrete foundations.
