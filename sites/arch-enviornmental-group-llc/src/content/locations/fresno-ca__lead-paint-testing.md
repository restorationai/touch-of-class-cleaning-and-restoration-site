## Why Fresno's Housing Stock Makes Lead Testing Essential

Fresno grew rapidly in the post-World War II decades, and large swaths of the city's residential neighborhoods, particularly those developed between the 1930s and the early 1970s, contain housing that predates the federal ban on residential lead paint. Bungalows and ranch-style homes from that era frequently have multiple layers of paint on original wood windows and door casings, which is exactly where lead concentrations tend to be highest. Older multi-family buildings near downtown and along established corridors also carry elevated risk, particularly in units that have seen repeated repainting without full stripping.

The Central Valley climate adds a layer of complexity. Fresno's intense summer heat, routinely exceeding 100°F, causes wood substrates to expand and contract dramatically through the seasons. That thermal cycling accelerates paint chalking and micro-cracking, which can release lead dust even without any renovation activity. Homeowners who notice peeling paint on south- and west-facing exterior surfaces after a hot summer should treat that as a prompt to test, not just repaint.

## Our Lead Paint Testing and Inspection Process in Fresno

A lead inspection answers one question: is lead-based paint present, and where? A lead risk assessment goes further, evaluating whether existing lead paint poses an immediate hazard based on its condition and location. Depending on what you need, a pre-sale disclosure, a pre-renovation clearance, or a full risk picture for a rental property, the scope of the inspection is calibrated accordingly.

On-site, the process typically begins with a room-by-room XRF survey. The XRF analyzer reads lead concentration in milligrams per square centimeter through the paint layers without destructive sampling, which means painted surfaces stay intact during the inspection. Where XRF readings are inconclusive, sometimes the case on irregular substrates like stucco, which is common on Fresno exteriors, paint chip samples are collected and submitted to an accredited laboratory for confirmation. Results are compiled into a written report that identifies every tested component, its lead content, and its condition, giving contractors, owners, and insurers a clear record.

## Local Note: Stucco Exteriors and XRF Readings in the Central Valley

One thing that comes up regularly on Fresno properties is the prevalence of stucco exteriors, it's the dominant finish on homes built from the 1940s through the 1980s across the Central Valley. Stucco's density and mineral composition can scatter XRF readings in ways that require additional paint chip sampling to confirm. An inspector unfamiliar with this pattern might log an inconclusive result and move on; a certified lead inspector assessor knows to collect a chip sample at those locations and send it to the lab rather than leaving the question open. That distinction matters when you're trying to satisfy a lender, a contractor, or a California Department of Public Health requirement before renovation work starts.

## Before You Renovate: California RRP and What It Means for Fresno Homeowners

California follows the EPA's Renovation, Repair, and Painting (RRP) Rule, which requires contractors working on pre-1978 housing to use lead-safe work practices. Before significant work begins, window replacement, siding removal, interior gut renovations, having a lead inspection report in hand protects you and your contractor. It documents the baseline condition of the property, satisfies disclosure requirements in real estate transactions, and can prevent costly stop-work orders if a lead issue surfaces mid-project. For landlords managing older rental stock in Fresno, a current risk assessment is also a practical step toward demonstrating due diligence under California's landlord habitability standards.
