## Why Clovis Properties Are Prone to Mold Growth

The Central Valley's climate does most of the work. Clovis experiences a wide swing between dry, scorching summers and cool, fog-heavy winters, and it's during the transitional months of late fall and early spring that moisture intrusion tends to peak. Irrigation-heavy landscaping common throughout the area can push groundwater closer to slab foundations, and homes built on expansive clay soils may experience subtle foundation movement that opens small gaps in stucco or drywall over time.

Older housing stock in established Clovis neighborhoods, particularly homes built in the 1970s and 1980s before modern vapor barrier standards were common, often lacks the moisture management details that newer construction includes. Attic spaces in these homes can accumulate condensation when warm interior air meets a cold roof deck during fog season, and that condensation feeds mold colonies that may go undetected for months. HVAC systems that run hard through the summer also create condensation in ductwork, and a slow drip inside a duct chase is rarely visible until the damage is significant.

## Our Mold Inspection and Testing Process in Clovis

A mold inspection is not a visual scan followed by a guess. The process begins with a thorough walkthrough to identify moisture sources, plumbing penetrations, window frames, roof-to-wall transitions, and HVAC components, using moisture meters and thermal imaging where conditions warrant. Surface readings alone can be misleading; a wall can feel dry to the touch while the framing behind it holds elevated moisture that sustains active mold growth.

When sampling is warranted, air spore testing captures what's actually circulating in the breathing zone of the space. Tape-lift or swab samples can identify surface colonies and help determine species. All samples are analyzed by an accredited laboratory, and the written report details spore counts, species identified, and a comparison against outdoor baseline samples taken the same day, context that matters when you're deciding whether remediation is necessary or whether the levels fall within normal range for the Central Valley's outdoor environment.
