## Why Clovis Properties Are Particularly Vulnerable to Hidden Mold

The Central Valley's climate is the defining factor. Clovis experiences hot, dry summers followed by cool, humid winters where overnight condensation on poorly insulated surfaces is common. Stucco-clad tract homes built during the city's rapid growth phases, many of them constructed with wood-frame cavities that are difficult to inspect visually, can harbor moisture for extended periods without obvious exterior signs. Attic spaces in these homes often have minimal cross-ventilation, and when HVAC systems cycle on and off through long summer days, the temperature swings inside wall assemblies create repeated condensation events.

Irrigation is another factor worth noting. Clovis sits adjacent to some of the most intensively irrigated agricultural land in California, and residential landscaping here tends to run heavy. Overwatered foundation plantings, drip systems left too close to slab edges, and graded lots that don't drain away from the structure efficiently are recurring contributors to elevated moisture at the building envelope, exactly the conditions that support mold growth in crawl spaces and lower wall cavities.

## What Mold Inspection and Testing Actually Involves

A mold assessment is not a visual walkthrough. Our process begins with a structured inspection of the areas most likely to hold moisture, bathrooms, kitchens, laundry rooms, attic decking, and any space that has experienced a past water intrusion. We use moisture meters and thermal imaging to identify elevated readings behind surfaces that appear dry.

Where conditions warrant, we collect air samples using calibrated pumps that draw a measured volume of air through a spore trap cassette. Those cassettes go to an accredited third-party laboratory for analysis. Surface samples, tape lifts or swabs, are collected when visible growth is present and species identification is needed. The resulting laboratory report identifies genera and spore counts, giving you and any remediation contractor a documented baseline rather than a guess. ## Local Note: Stucco Exteriors and the Fog Season

One pattern that comes up repeatedly in Central Valley inspections is moisture intrusion at stucco weep screeds during tule fog season. Stucco is the dominant exterior finish across Clovis residential construction, and when the weep screed at the base of the wall system is partially blocked by soil or mulch, a common condition in established landscaping, fog-season humidity can wick into the wall assembly without any discrete leak event. Homeowners often attribute musty odors in living areas to the HVAC system when the actual source is the lower wall cavity. Air sampling in those rooms, combined with moisture readings at the base of exterior walls, is often what distinguishes a filtration problem from an active mold condition.
