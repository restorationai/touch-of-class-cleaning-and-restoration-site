## Why Orange Cove Properties See Elevated VOC and Formaldehyde Concerns

The Central Valley's agricultural economy shapes the air in Orange Cove in ways that differ from coastal California cities. Fumigants, herbicides, and soil treatments applied to surrounding orchards and vineyards don't stay outside. Homes with older weatherstripping, single-pane windows, or crawlspace vents oriented toward field rows can pull ambient chemical compounds indoors, layering them on top of whatever VOCs are already off-gassing from interior materials.

At the same time, the region's extreme summer heat creates a second pressure. Composite wood products, adhesives, vinyl flooring, and spray foam insulation all release formaldehyde and other VOCs at higher rates as temperatures rise. A home that tested within acceptable limits in March may read significantly higher in July, especially if it was recently renovated or furnished. Homes built or remodeled in the past decade often used engineered wood products that carry formaldehyde-based binders, and in a climate like Orange Cove's, those products off-gas more aggressively than they would in a cooler region.
