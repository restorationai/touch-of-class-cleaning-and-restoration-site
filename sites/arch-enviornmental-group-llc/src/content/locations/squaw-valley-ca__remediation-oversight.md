When a remediation project goes wrong in this environment, the consequences compound quickly. ## Why Squaw Valley Properties Present Distinct Remediation Challenges

The Sierra Nevada climate is not forgiving to structures. Seasonal snowpack and spring runoff push moisture into crawl spaces, foundation walls, and subfloor assemblies. Cabins and lodges built in earlier construction eras commonly contain vermiculite insulation, asphalt floor tile, and pipe wrap that predate modern hazardous material regulations. When a remediation contractor begins work without a properly written scope of work and an independent set of eyes monitoring the project, disturbed asbestos-containing material or inadequately contained mold can migrate into unaffected areas of the structure.

At elevation, temperature swings between night and day can also affect containment integrity. Negative air pressure barriers that hold steady at valley temperatures may flex or fail when nighttime temperatures drop sharply, a detail that matters on multi-day abatement projects where the crew is not on-site around the clock.

## What Remediation Oversight and Project Management Looks Like on a Squaw Valley Project

Independent oversight means Arch Environmental Group LLC is not the contractor doing the physical abatement work. That separation is the point. Our role is to:

- Review and approve the remediation protocol before work begins, confirming that the scope matches the hazard assessment findings
- Conduct site visits during active abatement to verify containment setup, personal protective equipment compliance, and waste handling procedures
- Collect or coordinate third-party air and surface samples at defined project milestones
- Review contractor documentation, including daily logs, waste manifests, and photo records, for completeness
- Issue a written project closeout report that satisfies insurer requirements and, where applicable, regulatory agency submittals

For mold projects, the drying and remediation sequence is verified against IICRC S520 standards. For asbestos and lead abatement, work is evaluated against Cal/OSHA Title 8 requirements and California Department of Public Health guidelines. Our team holds a Certified Asbestos Consultant credential, Lead Inspector Assessor certification, and NIOSH 582 Microscopist qualification, which means the sampling and analysis side of oversight is handled by the same team reviewing the project, not subcontracted to a lab with no site context.

A third-party project manager changes that dynamic. We review contractor bids against the documented hazard conditions and flag line items that are missing, inflated, or inconsistent with the site assessment before a contract is signed.

Our closeout reports are written to satisfy that requirement directly, reducing back-and-forth between the adjuster, the contractor, and the property owner.

## Local Note

Properties in the Squaw Valley area that were built as seasonal cabins and later converted to year-round occupancy frequently have insulation and vapor barrier assemblies that were never designed for continuous habitation. When mold or moisture damage is discovered in these structures, the remediation scope often needs to account for building envelope deficiencies that a contractor focused on surface cleaning will miss entirely. Oversight that includes a review of the original construction intent, not just the visible damage, catches these gaps before the project is closed out and the problem returns the following winter.
