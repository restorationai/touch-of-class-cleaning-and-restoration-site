## Why Easton Properties Deserve a Closer Look at Radon

The San Joaquin Valley floor is underlain by alluvial deposits and compacted clay soils that, depending on local variation, can trap and channel soil gases differently than the sandy or fractured-rock soils found elsewhere. Radon is a naturally occurring radioactive gas produced by uranium decay in soil and rock. It doesn't need a dramatic geological formation to be present at elevated concentrations indoors. In Easton and the surrounding unincorporated Fresno County area, homes built during the mid-twentieth century often feature crawl spaces, older concrete block foundations, or poured slabs with minor cracking that can serve as entry points for soil gas. Gaps around utility penetrations, deteriorating vapor barriers in crawl spaces, and pressure differentials created by HVAC systems all contribute to radon levels in a house rising above what outdoor air would suggest.

California does not currently mandate radon testing at the point of sale the way some other states do, which means many Easton homeowners have simply never had a test performed. The EPA action level of 4 picocuries per liter (pCi/L) is the threshold most commonly used to guide mitigation decisions, but the agency also notes that levels between 2 and 4 pCi/L carry meaningful long-term risk. A radon inspection gives you actual data rather than a regional average.

## Our Radon Testing Process in Easton

Arch Environmental Group uses continuous radon monitoring equipment rather than passive charcoal canisters alone.
