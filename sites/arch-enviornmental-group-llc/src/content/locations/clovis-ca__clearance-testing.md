## Why Clovis Properties Face Distinct Clearance Testing Challenges

The San Joaquin Valley's climate creates a specific set of conditions that affect how contaminants behave inside buildings. During the long dry season, homes are sealed tight against heat, which concentrates airborne particulates, mold spores, asbestos fibers, and lead dust, faster than in more temperate climates. When remediation work disturbs those materials, the verification step has to account for how tightly the structure was holding air before the project began.

Clovis also has a broad mix of housing stock. Older ranch-style homes built in the 1960s and 1970s, common throughout the city's established west-side neighborhoods, frequently contain asbestos in floor tile, duct insulation, and popcorn ceilings. Homes built during Clovis's rapid growth in the 1980s and 1990s often have HVAC systems that were sized for the era's building standards, meaning duct cross-contamination during a mold or asbestos abatement project is a real risk that clearance sampling needs to address directly. Newer construction in the city's master-planned communities can present its own challenges: tightly built envelopes trap moisture when a plumbing failure goes undetected, giving mold a fast path to colonization before the occupants notice anything.

## Our Clearance Testing and Post-Remediation Verification Process in Clovis

Clearance testing is not a formality, it is the documented proof that a remediation project met the standard it was contracted to meet. The process varies depending on the contaminant involved, and each type has its own analytical method and pass/fail threshold.

**Mold clearance air testing** uses spore trap cassettes or impactor samples collected inside the work area and compared against outdoor control samples. Because Clovis's outdoor spore counts shift significantly with the agricultural calendar, harvest season can spike ambient fungal levels, we time indoor sampling carefully and document outdoor conditions at the moment of collection. That context is part of the report, not an afterthought.

**Asbestos clearance testing** follows phase contrast microscopy (PCM) or transmission electron microscopy (TEM) protocols depending on the project scope. Our principal holds a NIOSH 582 Microscopist credential, which means the fiber analysis is performed by someone trained specifically in that methodology, not sent to a third-party lab with no connection to the field conditions.

**Post-remediation verification for lead** involves wipe sampling of surfaces and, where applicable, clearance of dust in adjacent areas that may have received fallout during abatement. Our Lead Inspector/Assessor credential covers both the sampling protocol and the interpretation of results against California and federal action levels.

Every clearance report we issue documents the sampling locations, the analytical results, the comparison benchmarks, and a plain-language summary suitable for submission to an insurance carrier, a property management company, or a future buyer's inspector.
