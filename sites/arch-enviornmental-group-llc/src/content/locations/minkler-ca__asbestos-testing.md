## Why Minkler Properties Are Worth a Closer Look

The San Joaquin Valley's intense summer heat and dry winters create a particular maintenance cycle for older structures. Roofing materials, duct insulation, and exterior siding on homes built before the mid-1980s were frequently manufactured with asbestos fibers, and decades of thermal expansion and contraction in this climate can cause those materials to become brittle and friable over time. Agricultural properties in this part of Fresno County often include secondary structures, pump houses, and equipment sheds that received even less scrutiny than the main residence. Those outbuildings are frequently overlooked during a standard home inspection but can contain asbestos-containing pipe wrap, corrugated roofing panels, or spray-applied insulation that predates modern material standards.

Popcorn ceilings applied before the late 1980s are one of the most common asbestos-containing materials found in residential properties throughout this region. Because Minkler's housing stock skews older and many properties have changed hands without formal disclosure documentation, buyers and sellers alike benefit from having a certified asbestos consultant collect samples and analyze them before any surface is disturbed.

## Our Asbestos Testing and Inspection Process

A thorough asbestos survey starts with a visual assessment of the building's age, construction type, and condition of suspect materials. Arch Environmental Group's certified asbestos consultant identifies materials that are likely to contain asbestos based on their location, appearance, and the construction era of the structure. Samples are collected using strict containment protocols to prevent fiber release during the collection process, then submitted to an accredited laboratory for polarized light microscopy analysis, the method required under California regulations for bulk material testing.

For Fresno County properties, the results feed directly into documentation that satisfies the requirements of the San Joaquin Valley Air Pollution Control District, which governs asbestos-related demolition and renovation notifications in this region. Knowing which materials are positive, which are negative, and which require further point-count analysis gives contractors and property owners a legally defensible baseline before a single wall is opened or a tile is pried up.
