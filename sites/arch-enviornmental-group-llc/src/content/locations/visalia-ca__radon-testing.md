Radon is invisible, odorless, and produced naturally as uranium in California's soils breaks down, and Visalia's position in the southern San Joaquin Valley, where alluvial soils and older residential construction create conditions worth paying attention to, makes radon testing a reasonable step for any property owner here. Whether you're buying a home, preparing to sell, or simply want to know what your family is breathing, a professional radon test gives you a number you can act on rather than a guess.

## Why Visalia Properties Are Worth Testing

The San Joaquin Valley's geology is dominated by deep alluvial deposits, sediments carried down from the Sierra Nevada over millions of years. These soils can contain naturally occurring uranium and radium, and as those elements decay, radon gas migrates upward through the soil and into structures through foundation cracks, utility penetrations, and slab joints. Visalia's housing stock spans a wide range of construction eras, from mid-century ranch homes and post-war bungalows to newer subdivisions built on slab foundations. Older homes often lack the sub-slab ventilation or vapor barriers that newer construction incorporates, giving radon a more direct path into living spaces.

California does not have a statewide radon mapping program as detailed as some other states, which means many homeowners here operate under the assumption that radon is a "Midwest problem." That assumption isn't supported by soil science. The EPA recommends testing every home regardless of region, and the agency's action level of 4 picocuries per liter (pCi/L) applies equally in Visalia as it does in Denver or Pittsburgh.

## Our Radon Testing Process in Visalia

Arch Environmental Group uses calibrated continuous radon monitors (CRMs) rather than passive charcoal canisters for most residential and commercial tests. A CRM records radon concentrations hour by hour over the test period, producing a data log that shows how levels fluctuate with changes in barometric pressure, HVAC cycling, and occupant behavior, information a single charcoal canister result cannot provide.

The process follows EPA and AARST-NRPP closed-building protocols:

- **Closed-building conditions** are maintained for at least 12 hours before and throughout the test period.
- The monitor is placed in the **lowest livable level** of the home, a finished basement if one exists, or the ground-floor living area on slab-on-grade construction, which is common throughout Visalia.
- The monitor runs for a minimum of 48 hours under short-term test protocols, or up to 90 days for long-term tests when a more representative seasonal average is needed.
- Results are delivered as a written report with the hourly data log, the average pCi/L reading, and a plain-language explanation of what the number means and what options exist if mitigation is warranted.

## Local Note

One pattern worth knowing in the Visalia area: slab-on-grade construction, extremely common in the tracts built throughout Tulare County from the 1960s through the 1990s, tends to show more variable radon readings than homes with crawl spaces, because slab integrity changes over decades. Hairline cracks from the region's periodic seismic activity and soil settlement can open new pathways for soil gas. If your home was built before 1990 and hasn't been tested since any significant remodeling or addition work, the original slab may no longer be the same barrier it was at construction. A continuous monitor placed over 48 hours will capture that variability in a way a single-point test cannot.
