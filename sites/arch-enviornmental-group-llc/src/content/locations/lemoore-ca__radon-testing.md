Lemoore sits in the southern San Joaquin Valley, where the same flat, clay-heavy soils that make Kings County farmland productive can also trap soil gases beneath slab foundations and crawl spaces. Radon, a colorless and odorless radioactive gas that forms naturally as uranium in the soil decays, has no obvious warning signs inside a home. A radon test is the only way to know whether levels in your Lemoore property are within a safe range, and it is a step that many local homeowners skip until a real estate transaction forces the question.

## Why Lemoore Properties Warrant a Closer Look at Radon

The San Joaquin Valley's geology is not uniform, and Kings County sits over alluvial deposits that vary considerably in mineral composition from one parcel to the next. Clay-dominant soils can limit how freely soil gas moves laterally, which sometimes concentrates gases beneath a structure rather than dispersing them. Homes built on concrete slabs, which are common throughout Lemoore's residential neighborhoods given the flat terrain and mild frost risk, can still accumulate radon through hairline cracks, utility penetrations, and gaps around plumbing sleeves.

Lemoore also has a significant share of housing built during the postwar and mid-century periods, when construction standards around sub-slab ventilation and vapor barriers were far less rigorous than they are today. Older homes with original concrete work are more likely to have the kinds of small structural gaps that allow soil gas entry. Newer construction is not automatically safe either: tightly built, energy-efficient homes hold indoor air longer, which means any radon entering the structure has more time to accumulate before it is diluted.

## Our Radon Testing Process in Lemoore

Arch Environmental Group uses continuous radon monitoring equipment rather than passive charcoal canisters for most residential and commercial tests. A continuous monitor records radon concentrations hour by hour throughout the test period, which typically runs 48 hours under closed-house conditions. That hourly data matters because it can reveal spikes tied to weather pressure changes or HVAC cycling, patterns that a single averaged reading from a passive device would obscure.

Before placing monitors, we assess the structure: foundation type, HVAC layout, any existing sub-slab ventilation, and areas of the home most likely to show elevated readings, such as basements, slab-on-grade living spaces, and rooms directly above crawl spaces. After the test period, we retrieve the monitor, analyze the data, and provide a written report that includes the average result, the hourly trend, and a plain-language explanation of what the numbers mean relative to the EPA action level of 4 picocuries per liter. If levels are elevated, we explain mitigation options clearly so you can make an informed decision about next steps.
