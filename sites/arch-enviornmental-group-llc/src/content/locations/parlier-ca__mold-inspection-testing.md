## Why Parlier Properties See Elevated Mold Risk

The San Joaquin Valley's agricultural cycle shapes indoor air quality in ways that most coastal inspectors do not account for. During the irrigation months, ambient humidity in the Parlier area rises significantly, and that moisture finds its way into homes through foundation vents, window gaps, and aging weatherstripping. Many residences in Parlier were built during the mid-twentieth century housing expansions that followed agricultural labor demand in Fresno County. Homes from that era commonly feature pier-and-beam or shallow slab foundations, single-pane windows, and minimal vapor barriers in the crawl space, all of which allow ground moisture to migrate upward.

Evaporative coolers, which remain common in this part of the valley because of their low operating cost, introduce outdoor air directly into the living space. When outdoor humidity climbs in July and August, so does indoor relative humidity, and sustained indoor humidity above 60 percent is enough to support mold colonization on drywall paper, insulation batts, and wood framing within days of a water intrusion event.

## Our Mold Inspection and Testing Process in Parlier

A mold inspection begins with a visual walkthrough of the property, including areas that are often overlooked: the space beneath evaporative cooler pads, the interior of window frames, and any room that shares a wall with an irrigation-adjacent exterior. Moisture readings are taken with calibrated meters at multiple points, and any anomaly is documented before sampling begins.

Air sampling for mold uses spore trap cassettes placed at breathing height in affected rooms and at least one outdoor control sample. The outdoor control is especially important in the San Joaquin Valley, where ambient outdoor spore counts from agricultural dust and field crops can be elevated. Comparing indoor and outdoor counts gives a clear picture of whether the indoor environment is genuinely amplified or simply reflecting valley-wide conditions. Surface swab or tape-lift samples are collected from any visible growth to identify genus and species. All samples are analyzed by an accredited laboratory, and results are returned in a written report that documents findings, sample locations, and what the data means for the occupants.

Arch Environmental Group's team holds certifications including NIOSH 582 Microscopist training, which is specific to the analysis of airborne particulates and fibers, a credential that matters when interpreting spore trap data accurately.

## Local Note

One pattern that comes up in Parlier and the surrounding Fresno County agricultural communities is mold growth concentrated near the ceiling line of rooms served by evaporative coolers. When the cooler's water distribution pads deteriorate or the float valve sticks, water can overflow into the ductwork or drip down the exterior wall cavity. Because the cooler sits on the roof, the moisture path is vertical and often hidden behind drywall for its entire length. A standard visual inspection that focuses only on bathrooms and under sinks will miss this entirely. Checking the cooler unit, the roof penetration, and the ceiling directly below the unit is a specific step that matters in valley homes and is part of every inspection Arch Environmental Group performs in this area.
