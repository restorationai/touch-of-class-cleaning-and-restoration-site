If your ducts haven't been assessed since the last major remodel or tenant turnover, what's circulating through the vents may have little to do with the air you think you're breathing. ## Why Clovis Properties Face Distinct HVAC and Duct Contamination Risks

The San Joaquin Valley's climate creates conditions that stress ductwork in ways that aren't common in coastal California. Prolonged heat causes flexible duct liners to expand and contract repeatedly over a season, opening small gaps at joints and connections where conditioned air, and whatever is suspended in it, can escape into wall cavities or pull in attic air loaded with fiberglass particles, rodent dander, or accumulated dust.

Clovis also sits in a region where wildfire smoke events have become a seasonal reality. Fine smoke particulate is small enough to pass through standard residential filters, and it can deposit a thin, oily film on duct walls and coil surfaces that doesn't vacuum away. That residue holds onto subsequent dust and biological material, building up a layer that degrades air quality long after the smoke advisory has lifted.

Many homes in the Clovis area were built during the rapid residential expansion of the 1980s and 1990s. Duct systems from that era were often installed with materials and sealing practices that don't hold up well over 30-plus years of Valley summers. Fiberglass duct board, common in tract construction of that period, can delaminate on the interior surface, shedding fibers into the airstream and creating a rough texture that traps biological growth when moisture is present.

## Our HVAC and Duct System Assessment Process in Clovis

An assessment begins before any equipment goes into the duct. We start with a visual inspection of accessible components, the air handler, return air plenum, supply and return registers, and any exposed flex duct in the attic or crawlspace. Attic conditions in Clovis are worth particular attention: an attic that reaches 150°F in July and holds humidity from a poorly sealed bath exhaust fan is a reliable environment for mold colonization on duct surfaces.

Once the visual pass is complete, we use borescope cameras to inspect the interior of ducts that can't be opened without cutting. This step frequently reveals conditions that aren't apparent from the register side, standing debris, moisture staining, visible microbial growth, or pest intrusion. Where contamination is suspected, we collect samples using tape lifts, swabs, or bulk material collection and submit them to an accredited laboratory for analysis.

Because Arch Environmental Group holds NIOSH 582 Microscopist certification, we can provide on-site analysis of fiber samples, relevant when the concern involves asbestos-containing duct insulation or fiberglass fiber release, in addition to coordinating full laboratory reporting for biological contaminants. The written assessment report documents findings by zone, photographs every area of concern, and provides a clear scope for any remediation contractor who needs to act on the findings.

## Equipment and Methods We Use for HVAC Assessment

The tools we bring to a Clovis assessment are calibrated to the specific questions the property owner needs answered. Particle counters measure airborne particulate at the register and compare it to ambient room air, a meaningful delta points to in-duct contamination rather than general household dust. Moisture meters and thermal imaging cameras identify areas where condensation or a slow leak has created conditions favorable to mold growth on duct surfaces or in the air handler cabinet.

For properties where occupant health complaints are the driving concern, we can pair the duct assessment with an indoor air quality sampling protocol that captures spore counts, VOCs, or specific fiber types depending on what the history of the building suggests. The combination gives a more complete picture than duct inspection alone.

## Local Note

One pattern worth knowing in Clovis: many homes built during the 1990s tract expansions were equipped with oversized HVAC systems relative to the actual load of the house. An oversized unit short-cycles, it cools the air quickly but doesn't run long enough to pull adequate humidity out of it. In the Valley's dry summers that seems harmless, but during the region's foggy winter months and the brief but humid spring, short-cycling leaves moisture in the duct system long enough to support mold growth on duct liner surfaces. If a home has a history of musty odors that appear seasonally, system sizing is part of the conversation during an assessment.
