## Why Squaw Valley Properties Are Particularly Vulnerable to Mold

The Sierra Nevada snowpack is the defining moisture event for this region. Properties at this elevation routinely experience ground saturation from March through June as snow melts into soil that is still partially frozen and cannot absorb water quickly. That water finds the path of least resistance, which is often a crawl space vent, a foundation crack, or a poorly flashed roof-to-wall junction.

Cabin-style and A-frame construction common in mountain communities compounds the risk. Steep roof pitches shed snow effectively, but the deep roof overhangs that shade walls year-round keep siding and sheathing damp far longer than comparable structures at lower elevations. Wood-framed walls with minimal vapor management, combined with the relatively short dry season at altitude, mean that moisture can persist inside wall cavities through multiple seasons before any visible surface sign appears. By the time a stain shows on interior drywall, mold colonization behind it may already be well established.

## Our Mold Inspection and Testing Process in Squaw Valley

A thorough inspection here starts before any sampling equipment comes out of the case. The inspector walks the exterior first, looking at grade slope relative to the foundation, the condition of crawl space venting, and whether snow-retention features on the roof have caused ice-dam patterns on fascia boards. In mountain properties, those exterior clues often point directly to where interior moisture is hiding.

Inside, we use calibrated moisture meters and thermal imaging to map elevated readings across floors, walls, and ceilings without opening surfaces unnecessarily. When readings or visible growth warrant sampling, we collect air samples using calibrated pumps and spore-trap cassettes, following AIHA-recognized protocols. Bulk or swab samples are added when we need species identification on a visible colony. All samples go to an accredited third-party laboratory, and you receive a written report that documents sample locations, spore counts, species identified, and how indoor air compares to outdoor baseline samples taken the same day.

Our team holds a Certified Asbestos Consultant and Lead Inspector Assessor credential alongside NIOSH 582 Microscopist certification, which means we can read and interpret fungal spore slides in-house and understand how older building materials in mountain cabins interact with moisture over time.
