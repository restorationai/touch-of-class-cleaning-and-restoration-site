## Why Clovis Properties Warrant a Closer Look at Lead Paint

Fresno County's building boom accelerated through the 1950s, 1960s, and into the 1970s, and Clovis absorbed a significant share of that residential construction. Homes built before 1978, the federal cutoff year for lead-based paint in residential use, are common throughout older Clovis corridors, particularly in neighborhoods that predate the city's post-2000 expansion toward the foothills. The San Joaquin Valley's dry summers and temperature swings between seasons cause painted wood trim, window sashes, and exterior siding to expand and contract repeatedly, which accelerates paint chalking and micro-cracking. That weathering process is exactly how intact lead paint becomes a fine dust or chip hazard, especially on friction surfaces like windows and doors that get daily wear.

Owners of pre-1978 homes in Clovis who are planning kitchen remodels, window replacements, or any work that disturbs painted surfaces are required under the EPA's Renovation, Repair and Painting (RRP) Rule to have a certified inspector confirm the presence or absence of lead before work begins, or to treat all painted surfaces as lead-containing. Testing first is almost always the more cost-effective path.

## Our Lead Paint Testing and Inspection Process in Clovis

Arch Environmental Group's lead inspector/assessor conducts systematic inspections using XRF (X-ray fluorescence) technology, a non-destructive method that reads lead concentration in paint layers without requiring paint removal. The device produces results on-site, component by component, so you leave the inspection knowing exactly which surfaces are positive, which are negative, and what the measured levels are.

For properties where a full risk assessment is appropriate, often rental units, properties involved in a real estate transaction, or homes where a child under six lives, the process goes further. A risk assessment identifies not just the presence of lead paint but the condition of painted surfaces, the likelihood of exposure, and the recommended response: interim controls, encapsulation, or full abatement. The written report meets California Department of Public Health standards and is the document your contractor, lender, or property manager will need.

When paint chip sampling is warranted alongside or instead of XRF, for surfaces where instrument access is limited, or where a laboratory confirmation is required, samples are collected and submitted to an accredited laboratory. Turnaround time and lab selection are discussed with you before collection begins.

## Local Note

One thing that comes up repeatedly in the Central Valley's older residential stock: exterior paint on wood-framed homes in this climate tends to chalk heavily on south- and west-facing elevations due to intense UV exposure. That chalking can make a surface look like it's in poor condition when the underlying paint layer is still adherent, and vice versa, a freshly painted surface may be covering lead-positive layers that an XRF instrument will still detect through the topcoat. In Clovis, where many mid-century ranch homes have had multiple repaint cycles over the decades, XRF's ability to read through paint layers without disturbing them is particularly valuable.
