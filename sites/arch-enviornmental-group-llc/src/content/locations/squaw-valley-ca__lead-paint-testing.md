## Why Squaw Valley Properties Are at Elevated Lead Risk

Fresno County's mountain communities developed rapidly during the mid-20th century to support recreational demand, and construction timelines were fast. That means a significant portion of the housing stock in and around Squaw Valley dates to before 1978, the year the federal government banned lead-based paint in residential buildings. In mountain climates, the problem compounds: cold winters, heavy snowpack, and seasonal humidity swings cause wood siding, window trim, and porch substrates to expand and contract repeatedly. Paint that looks intact from the street can be chalking, chipping, or peeling on the north-facing sides of a structure where sun exposure is minimal and moisture lingers. Those are exactly the conditions that generate lead dust at concentrations worth measuring.

Older cabins in the area frequently feature original single-pane windows with painted frames, tongue-and-groove interior paneling painted over multiple times, and exterior trim that has been repainted in place rather than stripped. Each paint layer applied before 1978 is a potential source.

## Our Lead Paint Testing and Inspection Process in Squaw Valley

Arch Environmental Group LLC holds a Lead Inspector Assessor certification, which means the inspection is conducted by a credentialed professional, not a general contractor with a store-bought kit. The process begins with a systematic visual assessment of the property, identifying all painted surfaces by substrate type, location, and visible condition. That assessment drives the sampling strategy.

For most residential properties, XRF (X-ray fluorescence) testing is the most efficient method. An XRF analyzer reads lead concentration in paint layers without damaging the surface, producing results on-site rather than days later in a lab. Where XRF readings fall in an inconclusive range or where surface geometry makes direct XRF contact difficult, paint chip samples are collected and submitted to an accredited laboratory for confirmation.

A full lead risk assessment goes further than a basic inspection: it includes dust wipe sampling from floors, window sills, and window wells, and soil sampling from bare soil near the building's drip line. The final report identifies lead-containing materials by location, condition, and hazard classification, and it satisfies the documentation requirements that California contractors and property owners need before renovation work begins under the EPA's Renovation, Repair, and Painting (RRP) rule.
