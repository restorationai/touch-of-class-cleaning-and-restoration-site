## Why Lemoore Properties See Air Quality Issues

The San Joaquin Valley already carries one of the highest concentrations of agricultural particulates and ozone precursors in California, a regional reality that affects what settles inside homes when windows are opened or HVAC systems pull in outside air. In Lemoore specifically, the flat terrain and limited tree canopy mean wind-blown dust from surrounding farmland moves directly into residential areas with little natural filtration.

Many homes in Lemoore were built during the mid-20th century expansion tied to NAS Lemoore's growth, and older construction often means older HVAC ductwork, original insulation materials, and building envelopes that weren't sealed to modern standards. Gaps around duct connections, degraded vapor barriers in crawl spaces, and aging window seals all create pathways for outdoor particulates and moisture to enter the living space. When moisture does get in, through a slow roof leak, a sweating evaporative cooler, or a crawl space that floods during a wet winter, the warm temperatures that define a Lemoore summer can push mold colonization timelines from weeks down to days.

## Our Indoor Air Quality Testing Process in Lemoore

Air quality testing isn't a single sample collected at the front door. A thorough inspection works through the home systematically, starting with a visual assessment of areas most likely to harbor biological growth or particulate accumulation: attic insulation, crawl space vapor barriers, return air plenums, and any rooms with a history of water intrusion.

From there, air samples are collected using calibrated pumps drawing measured volumes of air through collection media appropriate for the contaminant in question. Mold spore trap samples capture what's actively circulating. Bulk or surface samples identify what's present at a specific location. For homes where asbestos-containing materials are suspected, a real consideration in construction from the 1960s through the 1980s, bulk material sampling and analysis follows a separate chain of custody protocol. Our team holds certifications as a Certified Asbestos Consultant and Lead Inspector/Assessor, with NIOSH 582 Microscopist credentialing for fiber identification, so the laboratory analysis behind your report reflects that level of technical specificity.

Results are returned with a written report that explains what was found, at what concentrations, and what those concentrations mean relative to established reference ranges, not just a number without context.
