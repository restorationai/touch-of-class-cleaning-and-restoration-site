## Why Easton Properties See Air Quality Issues

The San Joaquin Valley has some of the most challenging outdoor air conditions in the United States, and that reality doesn't stop at your front door. Easton's agricultural surroundings mean that particulate matter, pesticide drift, and seasonal crop-burning residue can infiltrate building envelopes, especially in older homes with single-pane windows, weathered door seals, or crawl spaces that weren't designed with modern infiltration control in mind.

Beyond what comes in from outside, the Valley's extreme summer heat drives heavy air conditioning use for months at a stretch. HVAC systems that run hard accumulate dust, mold spores, and microbial growth in ductwork and on coil surfaces. When those systems cycle on, they distribute whatever has built up directly into occupied spaces. Add the region's periodic "tule fog" season in winter, dense, damp air that settles low and can elevate indoor humidity in poorly sealed structures, and you have a year-round cycle of conditions that stress indoor air quality in ways that don't apply to coastal California or the Bay Area.

## Our Indoor Air Quality Testing Process in Easton

A thorough air quality assessment isn't a single swab or a quick visual check. The process begins with a walkthrough of the property to identify likely source areas: HVAC components, crawl space vents, attic access points, areas with prior water intrusion, and any visible staining or discoloration on walls and ceilings.

From there, air samples are collected using calibrated pumps and cassettes following protocols consistent with NIOSH methodology, the same framework used in occupational health investigations. Surface samples and bulk material samples may be collected where visible growth or settled dust warrants a closer look. Samples are sent to an accredited laboratory, and results are interpreted by a credentialed professional. Arch Environmental Group LLC holds certifications as a Certified Asbestos Consultant, Lead Inspector/Assessor, and NIOSH 582 Microscopist, which means the person reading your results understands the full range of fiber, particulate, and biological contaminants, not just one category.

You receive a written report that explains what was found, what the concentrations mean in plain language, and what remedial steps, if any, are warranted. There's no pressure to purchase follow-on services from us, the report stands on its own.

## Local Note

One thing worth knowing about properties in this part of Fresno County: many of the ranch-style and agricultural worker homes built in the mid-20th century in and around Easton use evaporative (swamp) coolers rather than refrigerated air conditioning. Evaporative coolers work by pulling outside air through water-saturated pads, which is effective for cooling but also introduces significant moisture into the indoor environment. In a dry summer that's manageable, but during the Valley's periodic humid spells or when a cooler pad develops mold growth, those units can become a direct pathway for elevated spore counts inside the home. If your property uses an evaporative cooler, that's one of the first things we'll evaluate during an air quality inspection, and it's a detail that matters here in a way it simply doesn't in most of coastal California.
