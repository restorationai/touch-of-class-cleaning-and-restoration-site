## Why Hanford Homes and Buildings Face Elevated Off-Gassing Risk

Formaldehyde and other volatile organic compounds do not behave the same way in every climate. In the San Joaquin Valley heat, off-gassing accelerates. Composite wood products, laminate flooring, adhesives, and foam insulation all release VOCs faster at high temperatures, which means a Hanford home in July can have meaningfully higher indoor chemical concentrations than the same home would in a milder coastal city.

Kings County also has a significant stock of homes built during the suburban expansion of the 1970s through 1990s, a period when urea-formaldehyde resins were used extensively in pressed-wood cabinetry, subflooring, and wall paneling. Those materials can continue releasing formaldehyde for years, and older HVAC systems with limited fresh-air exchange trap the compounds indoors rather than diluting them. New construction is not exempt either: freshly installed flooring, cabinetry, and spray foam in homes built or remodeled recently can off-gas heavily during the first months of occupancy, especially through a Hanford summer.

## Our VOC and Formaldehyde Testing Process in Hanford

Testing begins with a walkthrough to identify likely source materials and any rooms where occupants have reported symptoms or odors. For formaldehyde specifically, we use methods sensitive enough to detect concentrations well below the levels that trigger regulatory concern, giving you a clear picture before symptoms become serious. In Hanford, we pay particular attention to HVAC intake placement and whether windows are typically kept closed during summer months.
