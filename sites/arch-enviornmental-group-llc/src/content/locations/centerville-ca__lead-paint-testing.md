## Why Centerville Properties Carry Lead Paint Risk

The San Joaquin Valley's growth pattern means Centerville has housing stock spanning multiple decades, with older neighborhoods carrying the highest concentration of pre-1978 construction. In California's Central Valley, many homes from the 1940s through the early 1970s were built with lead-based paint on interior woodwork, exterior siding, window sashes, and porch railings, surfaces that weather hard under the valley's intense summer heat and dry winds. That thermal cycling causes paint to expand and contract repeatedly, accelerating chalking and chipping. What looks like ordinary peeling paint on an older Centerville home can be a lead hazard that becomes airborne the moment a renovation crew starts cutting or sanding.

California also enforces the EPA's Renovation, Repair, and Painting (RRP) Rule, which requires contractors working on pre-1978 housing to follow lead-safe work practices. If you're a property owner or landlord in Centerville planning any work that disturbs painted surfaces, a lead inspection before the job starts protects your tenants, your contractor, and your liability exposure.

## Our Lead Paint Testing and Inspection Process in Centerville

Arch Environmental Group uses XRF (X-ray fluorescence) technology for non-destructive lead paint testing. An XRF analyzer reads lead concentration in milligrams per square centimeter directly through the paint layers without chipping or sampling the surface. This matters in older Centerville homes where multiple paint generations may be layered over original lead-based coats, XRF reads through all of them and gives a result in seconds per component.

The inspection process follows a component-by-component protocol: walls, ceilings, trim, doors, windows, exterior surfaces, and any painted metal or masonry are each logged individually. Where XRF results fall in an inconclusive range, paint chip samples are collected and submitted to an accredited laboratory for confirmation. After the inspection, you receive a written report identifying every tested component, its lead concentration, its condition, and whether it meets the threshold for a regulated lead hazard. That report is what your contractor, your insurance carrier, and California's CDPH recognize as documentation of the property's lead status.

As a Certified Lead Inspector/Assessor, Arch Environmental Group is qualified under California's licensing framework to perform both lead inspections and lead risk assessments, two distinct scopes that matter depending on whether you need a full property baseline or a targeted evaluation of specific hazards.

## Equipment and Methods We Use for Lead Testing

XRF testing is the industry standard for a reason: it's fast, it's non-destructive, and it produces defensible data. The instrument we use is calibrated to NIST standards and operated according to HUD and EPA protocols. For properties where surface conditions make XRF readings unreliable (heavily textured surfaces, certain composite materials), paint chip sampling fills the gap. Chip samples are sent to a CDPH-accredited laboratory, and results are incorporated into the final inspection report.

For renovation projects specifically, a lead test before renovation gives your contractor the component-level data they need to scope lead-safe work practices accurately, rather than treating the entire structure as presumed positive, which drives up project costs unnecessarily.
