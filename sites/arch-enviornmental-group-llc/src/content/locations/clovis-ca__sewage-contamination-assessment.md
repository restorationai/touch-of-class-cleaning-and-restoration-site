## Why Clovis Properties Face Distinct Sewage Contamination Risks

The San Joaquin Valley's agricultural and development history has shaped Clovis's infrastructure in ways that matter during a sewage event. Older sections of Clovis, particularly neighborhoods developed before the city's rapid expansion of the 1990s and 2000s, may have aging clay or cast-iron lateral lines that are more prone to root intrusion and stress fractures. When those laterals fail, sewage doesn't just pool in a bathroom; it can wick into subfloor assemblies, travel under slab-on-grade foundations, and reach wall cavities before the odor becomes obvious at the surface.

Clovis also sits in a region with expansive clay soils. Seasonal swelling and shrinking puts lateral sewer lines under cyclical stress, particularly after the dry summers and wet winters the Valley experiences. That soil movement is a recurring mechanical stressor that shows up in sewage backup patterns across the area, not a rare event, but a predictable one for properties on older streets.

Beyond the infrastructure, the valley heat matters for assessment timing. Bacterial contamination testing that might allow a 48-hour sample incubation window in a cooler climate needs to account for the fact that affected materials in an unconditioned Clovis garage or crawl space during July are essentially in an incubator.

## Our Sewage Contamination Assessment Process in Clovis

A thorough sewage contamination assessment is a documentation exercise as much as a testing exercise. The goal is to establish a defensible, third-party record of what was contaminated, to what extent, and whether remediation has returned the space to an acceptable baseline.

The process typically moves through these stages:

**Initial site evaluation.** We identify the source and the migration path, where the sewage entered, how far it traveled horizontally and vertically, and what building materials it contacted. In slab-on-grade construction common throughout newer Clovis developments, this often means checking for moisture intrusion beneath flooring using non-destructive moisture meters before any demolition is considered.

**Sample collection.** Surface swab samples and, where indicated, air samples are collected from affected and control areas. Sampling protocols follow established industrial hygiene standards for Category 3 water events. Chain of custody documentation accompanies every sample to an accredited laboratory.

**Laboratory analysis.** Results identify the specific bacterial and pathogen load present. This matters for remediation scoping, a light surface contamination on sealed concrete is a different remediation problem than saturation in OSB subfloor.

**Post-remediation verification.** After a contractor has completed cleanup, we return to collect clearance samples. Remediation is not complete until testing confirms contamination has been reduced to acceptable levels. This step protects homeowners and satisfies most insurance carriers.

## Clovis Insurance Coordination for Sewage Events

Sewage backup claims in California are often subject to policy-specific riders, standard homeowner's policies frequently exclude sewer backup unless the homeowner purchased an endorsement. That coverage question is between you and your carrier, but what we provide is the documentation that makes any claim defensible: timestamped photographs, moisture mapping, laboratory chain-of-custody reports, and a written assessment that describes conditions in the language adjusters and restoration contractors need.

We work with property owners, their public adjusters, and restoration contractors throughout Fresno County. Our role is assessment and documentation, independent of the remediation contractor, which is increasingly what carriers and HOAs require before authorizing remediation scope.

## Local Note

Clovis has seen significant tract-home construction since the 1990s, and many of those homes use engineered wood subfloor (OSB) rather than traditional plywood or solid wood. OSB is notably less forgiving in sewage events, it begins to swell, delaminate, and lose structural integrity faster than plywood when exposed to Category 3 water, and it can read as "dry" on a surface moisture meter while remaining saturated in its core layers. During assessment in these homes, we use both resistance-type and capacitance-type moisture instruments, and we probe at multiple depths before concluding a subfloor assembly is unaffected. Skipping that step is how contamination gets missed and shows up again six months later as a mold problem.
