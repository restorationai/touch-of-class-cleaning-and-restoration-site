## Our Lead Paint Testing and Inspection Process in Sanger

The inspection begins with a visual assessment of all painted surfaces, interior and exterior, looking for deteriorated paint, friction surfaces like windows and doors, and impact surfaces like baseboards and stair risers. From there, XRF (X-ray fluorescence) analysis allows us to test painted surfaces non-destructively, reading lead concentration in milligrams per square centimeter without breaking the paint film. XRF is the preferred method for comprehensive surveys because it covers a large number of components quickly and does not damage surfaces that may turn out to be lead-free.

Where XRF readings fall in an inconclusive range, or where a client specifically needs laboratory confirmation, paint chip samples are collected and submitted to an accredited laboratory for atomic absorption analysis. The final report identifies every tested component, its lead concentration, the condition of the paint, and, critically, whether the surface poses a hazard under current EPA standards. That report is what your contractor, your escrow officer, or your local building department will want to see.
