## Why Minkler Properties See Elevated Indoor Allergen Loads

The San Joaquin Valley is one of the most agriculturally active air basins in the country, and Minkler is no exception. Citrus and stone-fruit operations in the surrounding Fresno County foothills generate pollen and organic dust that infiltrates homes during harvest and pruning seasons. When that outdoor load meets the relative humidity that builds up inside during the valley's tule fog months, the interior environment becomes hospitable to dust mites in ways that a dry-climate home simply would not be.

Older rural construction in this part of Fresno County also tends to rely on crawl spaces and pier-and-beam foundations rather than sealed slabs. Crawl spaces in foothill communities like Minkler act as a reservoir for dust, animal dander from wildlife and pets, and occasionally mold spores that migrate upward through subfloor gaps into living areas. Standard HVAC filters were not designed to catch the fine particulate fraction that carries the proteins responsible for allergic reactions, so recirculated air can keep allergen levels elevated even in a clean-looking home.

## Our Allergen Testing Process in Minkler

Allergen testing is not a single swab of a surface. The process Arch Environmental Group follows is methodical because allergen distribution inside a home is uneven, and a single sample from the wrong location will miss the problem entirely.

We begin with a walkthrough to identify the rooms and surfaces most likely to concentrate allergens: upholstered furniture, area rugs, HVAC return vents, and any crawl space access points. Dust samples are collected using standardized wipe or vacuum methods depending on the surface type, then submitted to an accredited laboratory for analysis. The panel typically screens for dust mite allergen (Der p 1 and Der f 1), cat and dog dander proteins, cockroach allergen, and mouse urinary proteins, covering the most clinically significant triggers.

In Minkler homes with active crawl spaces, we often recommend a separate sample from the subfloor area, because allergen concentrations below the living space can differ dramatically from what is measured in the rooms above. Results come back with quantified levels, not just positive or negative readings, so you and your physician or allergist have actionable data rather than a vague flag.

## Equipment and Methods We Use for Allergen Testing

The accuracy of allergen testing depends heavily on collection technique. We use NIST-traceable dust collection methods and submit samples to laboratories that report results in micrograms per gram of dust, the unit that clinical thresholds are based on. This matters because a result that says "dust mite detected" tells you nothing about whether the level is below the sensitization threshold or ten times above it.

For air sampling in situations where a homeowner suspects airborne allergen exposure, we can deploy calibrated air pumps with appropriate filter cassettes. Our team holds NIOSH 582 microscopist credentials, which means we are trained in the microscopic identification of particulate matter, a skill that complements laboratory immunoassay results when the sample picture is complex.
