## Why Centerville Properties See Radon Concerns

The San Joaquin Valley floor is not uniform. Centerville-area soils range from sandy loam near older agricultural parcels to heavier clay-influenced ground closer to the foothills. Both soil types can harbor elevated radium concentrations, and the rate at which radon gas migrates into a structure depends heavily on foundation type, soil permeability, and how tightly the building envelope is sealed. Homes built in the mid-twentieth century, which make up a significant portion of Fresno County's residential stock, often have concrete slabs poured without the vapor barriers and sub-slab ventilation that newer construction requires. That gap in building practice is one of the primary reasons radon levels in older valley homes can read higher than owners expect.

California does not mandate radon testing at the point of sale the way some other states do, so many Centerville homeowners have simply never had a test done. The EPA action level of 4 picocuries per liter is the threshold most professionals use to recommend mitigation, but even levels between 2 and 4 pCi/L carry measurable long-term risk with sustained exposure.

## Our Radon Testing Process in Centerville

Arch Environmental Group uses continuous electronic radon monitors rather than passive charcoal canisters for most residential engagements. Continuous monitors log radon concentrations hour by hour over a minimum 48-hour test period, producing a data record that shows fluctuation patterns, not just an average. That matters in the valley because diurnal pressure changes, common in inland California climates with wide day-to-night temperature swings, can cause radon entry rates to spike during cooler overnight hours. A single-point snapshot can miss those peaks entirely.

The monitor is placed in the lowest livable level of the home, typically a bedroom, living area, or finished bonus room at ground level. Closed-house conditions are maintained throughout the test period. After retrieval, results are reviewed against EPA and California Department of Public Health guidance, and a written report is provided that you can share with a real estate agent, lender, or mitigation contractor.
