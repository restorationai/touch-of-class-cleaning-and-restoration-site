## Why Clovis Properties See Elevated VOC and Formaldehyde Concerns

The San Joaquin Valley's climate plays a direct role in indoor air chemistry. When outdoor temperatures climb through June, July, and August, interior surfaces, engineered hardwood, laminate flooring, composite cabinetry, spray foam insulation, adhesives, and paint, release VOCs at a faster rate than they would in a cooler climate. Homes that are tightly sealed for energy efficiency trap those compounds indoors rather than allowing them to dissipate. Clovis has seen significant residential development in recent years, and many of those homes are built with materials that carry formaldehyde-based resins in the substrate. New construction air quality testing is particularly relevant here because off-gassing from pressed wood products and adhesives can remain elevated for months after a certificate of occupancy is issued, sometimes well past the point when a family has already settled in.

Older homes in established Clovis neighborhoods present a different set of variables. Renovation work, new flooring over existing subfloor, fresh paint, replacement cabinetry, introduces the same off-gassing sources into a structure that may have limited ventilation compared to newer builds designed around mechanical fresh-air systems.

## Our VOC and Formaldehyde Testing Process in Clovis

Testing begins with a walkthrough to identify likely source materials and note any recent renovation activity, new furnishings, or HVAC changes that could affect readings. Sampling is conducted using calibrated passive diffusion badges or active air pumps depending on the compounds targeted and the duration of sampling required. For formaldehyde specifically, NIOSH-validated methods are used to distinguish it from the broader VOC spectrum, which matters when you're trying to determine whether a pressed-wood product, a paint, or a cleaning product is the primary contributor.

Samples are submitted to an accredited laboratory, and results are interpreted against established reference values, including guidelines from the California Air Resources Board, which sets some of the most stringent indoor air quality benchmarks in the country. California's regulatory environment means Clovis homeowners and property managers have access to meaningful comparison thresholds, and the lab report you receive will be contextualized against those standards, not just raw numbers.
