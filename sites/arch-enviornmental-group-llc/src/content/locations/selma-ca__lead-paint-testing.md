If you own or are renovating an older home in Selma, the question isn't whether lead-based paint was ever used on the property; it's where it is, how much, and whether it poses a risk to anyone living or working there. Arch Environmental Group LLC brings certified lead inspection and risk assessment services directly to Selma, giving property owners the documentation they need before a contractor lifts a single brush or pry bar.

## Why Selma's Housing Stock Makes Lead Testing Essential

The Central Valley's growth pattern means Selma has a substantial share of homes built between the 1940s and 1970s, an era when lead-based paint was standard on interior trim, exterior siding, window sashes, and porch floors. The dry, hot summers and cold valley-fog winters create a cycle of thermal expansion and contraction that gradually causes paint to crack, chalk, and flake. When that happens in a pre-1978 home, deteriorating lead paint shifts from a latent concern to an active exposure risk, particularly for children and pregnant women.

Agricultural communities like Selma also tend to have a higher proportion of rental housing that has changed hands multiple times without a formal lead inspection ever being completed. Landlords preparing to renovate, sell, or lease to families with young children face specific obligations under California and federal law, and a certified inspection report is the clearest way to satisfy those obligations and protect tenants.

## Our Lead Paint Testing and Inspection Process in Selma

Every engagement begins with a visual assessment of the property, identifying painted surfaces by age, condition, and substrate. From there, we use XRF (X-ray fluorescence) analysis, a non-destructive technology that reads lead concentrations in paint layers without requiring samples to be cut from walls or trim. XRF delivers results on-site and in real time, which means we can map the entire property in a single visit rather than sending samples to a lab and waiting days for answers.

Where XRF readings fall in an inconclusive range, which can happen with certain multi-layer paint systems common on older Central Valley bungalows, we collect paint chip samples for laboratory confirmation. The final deliverable is a written inspection report that identifies the location, condition, and lead concentration of every tested component. That report is formatted to meet California Department of Public Health requirements and can be submitted directly to your contractor, your real estate agent, or your local building department.

For properties where a more detailed evaluation is needed, rental units with deteriorating paint, homes where children have already been identified with elevated blood lead levels, or buildings undergoing major renovation, we also conduct lead risk assessments. A risk assessment goes beyond identifying where lead paint exists; it evaluates exposure pathways, including dust, soil, and bare surfaces, and produces specific recommendations for interim controls or abatement.

## Equipment and Methods We Use for Lead Testing

The XRF analyzer we use is a calibrated, portable instrument that meets the performance standards set by the U.S. Department of Housing and Urban Development for lead-based paint inspections. It reads through multiple paint layers simultaneously, which matters in older homes where six or eight coats of paint may sit on a single door frame. The instrument generates a time-stamped data file for every reading, which becomes part of the permanent inspection record.

For dust wipe sampling, relevant when a renovation has already begun or when a property has a history of paint disturbance, we follow EPA-approved collection and laboratory protocols. Results from accredited laboratories are incorporated into the final report alongside the XRF data.

## Local Note

One pattern worth knowing in Selma and the surrounding Fresno County communities: many homes built in the postwar era used a painted stucco exterior finish that was common across the Central Valley. Stucco holds lead paint differently than wood siding, the paint bonds tightly to the substrate and may not show visible deterioration even when the lead concentration is high. XRF testing is especially valuable on stucco surfaces because visual inspection alone can underestimate the risk. If your property has the smooth or dash-finish stucco typical of mid-century San Joaquin Valley construction, make sure your inspector tests those exterior walls directly rather than relying on a surface condition assessment.
