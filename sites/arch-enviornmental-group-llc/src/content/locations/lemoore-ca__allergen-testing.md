## Why Lemoore Properties See Elevated Indoor Allergen Loads

The San Joaquin Valley's agricultural calendar is a significant driver of what ends up inside Lemoore homes. Cotton, alfalfa, and grain fields surrounding the city shed pollen and fine particulate matter across multiple seasons, and that material works its way through window seals, door gaps, and HVAC returns. Homes with evaporative coolers, which are common in this part of Kings County because they are cost-effective in dry heat, pull outdoor air directly inside without the filtration a central air system provides. That means pollen counts outdoors translate more directly into indoor air quality than they would in a fully sealed, mechanically cooled home.

Beyond field dust, Lemoore's housing stock includes a meaningful share of homes built in the 1960s and 1970s, when construction standards around vapor barriers and duct sealing were less rigorous. Older ductwork accumulates settled dust over decades, and that reservoir becomes a delivery system for dust mite allergens, mold fragments, and pet dander every time the system cycles. Proximity to Naval Air Station Lemoore also means a rotating population of renters and buyers, so homes frequently change occupants, and with them, the pet history of the property changes too. ## Our Allergen Testing Process in Lemoore

Allergen testing is not a single swab and a result. The process starts with a walkthrough to identify the rooms and surfaces most likely to harbor concentrated allergen loads: bedrooms where dust mites colonize mattresses and pillows, living areas with carpet or upholstered furniture, and any space with a history of pet occupation. Arch Environmental Group collects dust samples from settled surfaces using standardized collection methods, and those samples go to an accredited laboratory for analysis.

The lab report identifies specific allergens by type and quantifies them against established threshold levels. Dust mite allergen (Der p1 and Der f1 proteins), cat and dog dander (Fel d1, Can f1), and cockroach allergen (Bla g2) are among the most clinically relevant markers tested. Results typically return within a few business days. Once you have the report, you understand which allergens are present at actionable concentrations and where remediation or behavioral changes are most likely to reduce exposure. That specificity is what separates allergen testing from guessing.
