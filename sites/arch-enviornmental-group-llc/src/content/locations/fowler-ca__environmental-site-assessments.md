## Why Fowler Properties Warrant a Close Look at Environmental History

Fowler's economy was built on agriculture, and agriculture in the Central Valley has historically relied on pesticides, fumigants, herbicides, and petroleum-based equipment fuels, all of which can migrate into soil and groundwater over time. Properties that once housed fruit-packing operations, cold storage facilities, fertilizer distributors, or farm-supply businesses carry a different risk profile than a suburban office park. Even parcels that appear vacant may have served as informal storage or waste disposal sites during earlier decades when regulatory oversight was limited.

The valley's clay-heavy soils complicate the picture further. Clay layers can trap contaminants near the surface rather than allowing them to disperse, which means a relatively small historical release can produce a concentrated plume that persists for years. Conversely, sandy lenses within those clay profiles can channel contaminants laterally in ways that aren't obvious from surface conditions alone. A Phase I ESA that accounts for this regional geology, not just a checklist walkthrough, is what distinguishes useful due diligence from a document that simply checks a lender's box.

## Our Environmental Site Assessment Process in Fowler

A Phase I Environmental Site Assessment follows the ASTM E1527-21 standard and involves four core components: records review, site reconnaissance, interviews, and a written report. In Fowler and the broader Fresno County area, the records review phase often surfaces agricultural chemical use records, underground storage tank (UST) registrations, and historical aerial photography that reveals land-use changes invisible at street level today. We pull state and federal regulatory databases, review Sanborn fire insurance maps where available, and examine historical topographic maps to reconstruct what stood on a parcel before the current improvements.

The site reconnaissance in Fowler's commercial and light-industrial zones frequently involves identifying recognized environmental conditions (RECs) tied to on-site fuel storage, hydraulic equipment, and legacy irrigation infrastructure. Our certified consultant documents staining patterns, vent pipes, fill ports, and any evidence of dumping or burial, the physical clues that databases alone can't capture.

When the Phase I identifies RECs that warrant further investigation, a Phase II ESA moves the work from research to field sampling. Soil borings, groundwater monitoring wells, and laboratory analysis of collected samples produce the data needed to confirm or rule out actual contamination. In Fresno County, Phase II work may also intersect with the Regional Water Quality Control Board's oversight programs depending on the contaminants and concentrations involved.

## Local Note

One thing that catches buyers off guard in Fowler and similar Central Valley agricultural communities: former pesticide-mixing and chemical-storage areas are not always reflected in current county parcel records or visible in satellite imagery. Many of these areas were simply graded over during property improvements in the 1980s and 1990s. Historical aerial photographs from the 1950s through 1970s, available through USGS and university archives, often tell a completely different story about how a parcel was used. Pulling and reviewing those aerials is a standard part of our Phase I process, and in the Fowler area it has consistently been one of the most revealing steps in the records review.
