For homeowners here, that outdoor burden doesn't stay outside, it migrates through window screens, HVAC returns, and door gaps, settling into carpets and upholstery where dust mites thrive in the warm, occasionally humid indoor air. ## Why Selma Homes Accumulate Allergens Differently

The San Joaquin Valley's agricultural economy is one of the most productive in the world, and that productivity comes with a byproduct: year-round particulate. Grape harvests, stone fruit orchards, and row crops surrounding Selma release pollen, mold spores, and fine organic dust across multiple seasons, not just spring. Unlike coastal California cities that get a marine-layer reset, Selma's dry summers and still-air inversions allow allergen particles to concentrate rather than disperse.

Inside older Selma homes, many built during the postwar growth of the 1950s through 1970s, ductwork is often undersized, poorly sealed, or lined with decades of settled dust. That dust is a habitat, not just a nuisance. Dust mite colonies establish wherever relative humidity stays above roughly 50%, and even in a dry climate like Selma's, kitchen steam, bathroom moisture, and evaporative coolers can create localized humid zones that sustain mite populations year-round. Pet dander compounds the problem: in a single-story ranch-style home with limited air exchange, dander shed by cats or dogs redistributes continuously through the HVAC system.

## Our Allergen Testing Process in Selma

Allergen testing is a sampling and laboratory process, not a visual inspection. The work begins with a structured walkthrough of the home to identify the rooms and surfaces most likely to harbor concentrated allergen loads, typically bedrooms, living areas with upholstered furniture, and any room served by a return-air vent. From those locations, we collect dust samples using standardized wipe or vacuum-cassette methods designed to capture settled particulate rather than airborne particles alone.

Samples are submitted to an accredited laboratory for immunoassay analysis. The panel typically screens for dust mite allergen (Der p 1 and Der f 1 are the two most clinically significant proteins), cat dander (Fel d 1), dog dander (Can f 1), and cockroach allergen (Bla g 2), the four most common triggers in residential settings. Results come back as quantified concentrations, not just positive/negative readings, which means you and your physician can see whether levels are below, at, or above the thresholds associated with sensitization risk.

Arch Environmental Group's certified team handles the collection with the same chain-of-custody documentation used in regulatory sampling work. That matters if results are ever needed for a landlord-tenant dispute, a real estate disclosure, or a physician's referral record.

## Local Note: Evaporative Coolers and Dust Mite Risk

One thing worth knowing if you live in Selma and rely on a swamp cooler rather than central air conditioning: evaporative coolers work by pulling outdoor air across a wet pad and pushing humidified air into the home. In a dry Valley summer that feels refreshing, but it also raises indoor relative humidity in the rooms closest to the unit, sometimes enough to sustain dust mite populations in bedding and carpet even when the rest of the house is dry. Homes that run evaporative coolers through June, July, and August often show elevated mite allergen readings in the master bedroom or main living area despite the region's overall arid climate. If your home uses a swamp cooler and someone in the household has unexplained allergy symptoms, that's a specific sampling location worth prioritizing.
