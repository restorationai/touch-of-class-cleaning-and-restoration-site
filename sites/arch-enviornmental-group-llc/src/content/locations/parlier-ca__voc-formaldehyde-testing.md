## Why Parlier Homes Are Particularly Prone to Off-Gassing Concerns

The San Joaquin Valley's climate is a significant factor in indoor air quality that does not get enough attention. During the long, hot summers, residents in Parlier and throughout Fresno County run central air conditioning almost continuously, which recirculates interior air rather than replacing it. When flooring adhesives, laminate cabinets, spray foam insulation, or composite wood panels are off-gassing formaldehyde or other VOCs, that recirculated air concentrates the compounds rather than diluting them.

Parlier also has a housing stock that spans several decades, including homes built during periods when pressed-wood products and urea-formaldehyde insulation were standard materials. Older homes that have been renovated with new cabinetry, flooring, or paint can suddenly introduce fresh off-gassing sources into a building envelope that was not designed for modern ventilation standards. New construction in the area is not exempt either: freshly installed flooring, cabinetry, and interior finishes can off-gas at elevated rates for months, especially when a home is closed up during the summer heat.

## Our VOC and Formaldehyde Testing Process in Parlier

Testing begins with a walkthrough to identify potential source materials and note any conditions, such as recent renovation, new furniture, or visible moisture, that could affect results. Sampling is conducted using calibrated air sampling equipment that captures both total VOC levels and formaldehyde specifically, since formaldehyde behaves differently from other organic compounds and requires its own measurement method.

Samples are collected at breathing-zone height in the rooms where occupants spend the most time, typically bedrooms and main living areas. We follow established industrial hygiene protocols for sample duration and handling to ensure the results hold up whether you are presenting them to a landlord, a contractor, a physician, or a real estate attorney. A written report documents measured concentrations, compares them to reference guidelines, and identifies likely source materials where the data supports that conclusion.

For new construction or post-renovation testing in Parlier, we can also conduct a closed-building test, where the home is kept closed for a defined period before sampling, to simulate worst-case occupancy conditions. This approach is particularly relevant in the Valley's climate, where homes are kept sealed for long stretches.

## Local Note: Valley Heat and the Closed-Home Effect

One thing that becomes clear when testing homes in the Parlier area is how dramatically the Valley's summer heat changes the off-gassing math. Formaldehyde emission rates from composite wood products and adhesives increase with temperature, so a cabinet that off-gasses at a moderate rate in a 70°F home can release significantly more in a home that reaches 85°F or higher indoors before the air conditioning catches up in the morning. If you are testing a home after a period of high heat and the house has been closed, the results will reflect those elevated conditions. That is actually useful information, because it reflects what occupants are breathing during the hottest months, not a best-case scenario.
