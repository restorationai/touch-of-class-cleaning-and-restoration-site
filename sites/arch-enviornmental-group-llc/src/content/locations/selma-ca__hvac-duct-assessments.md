Selma sits in the heart of the San Joaquin Valley, where summer temperatures routinely push past 100°F and the region's agricultural dust, fine particulate matter carried on dry Delta winds, finds its way into every gap in a building envelope. That combination puts HVAC systems and ductwork under unusual stress: coils run nearly year-round, dust loads accumulate faster than in coastal climates, and the moisture swings between dry summer heat and the Valley's damp, tule-fog winters create conditions where biological growth inside duct systems is more than a theoretical concern. If you've noticed musty air when the system kicks on, or if your home or commercial property hasn't had a duct assessment in several years, the San Joaquin environment is a compelling reason to take a closer look.

## Why Selma Properties See HVAC and Duct Contamination Issues

The Central Valley's climate is genuinely different from what most HVAC guidelines are written around. Tule fog, the dense, ground-level fog that settles across Fresno County from late fall through early spring, raises indoor humidity in poorly sealed structures even when outdoor temperatures are cold. When that moisture infiltrates ductwork insulation or collects near evaporator coils, it creates the kind of sustained dampness that supports mold colonization. Mold can begin establishing a foothold on duct liner material within 24 to 48 hours of a moisture event; once it's embedded in fiberglass duct board, surface cleaning alone won't resolve the problem.

Selma's housing stock adds another layer of complexity. Many homes in the area were built during the postwar agricultural boom, when duct systems were often installed with minimal sealing and routed through unconditioned attic spaces that reach extreme temperatures in summer. Older flex duct can degrade, develop micro-tears, and allow attic air, laden with insulation fibers, rodent dander, and accumulated dust, to be pulled into the living space. Agricultural properties on the city's edges may also have shop or barn HVAC systems that were never designed for the particulate loads they're handling.

## Our HVAC and Duct System Assessment Process in Selma

An assessment begins before any equipment goes into the ductwork. The process starts with a visual inspection of accessible system components, the air handler, return air plenum, supply and return registers, and any visible duct runs in attic or crawl space areas. In the San Joaquin Valley, attic inspections during summer require specific protocols because attic temperatures can exceed 150°F by midday; scheduling and sequencing matter here in ways they simply don't in milder climates.

Once the visual phase is complete, bulk sampling or swab sampling of suspect areas, discoloration on duct liner, debris accumulation near registers, visible growth near coil pans, is collected and submitted to an accredited laboratory for analysis. As a Certified Asbestos Consultant and Lead Inspector/Assessor with NIOSH 582 Microscopist credentials, Arch Environmental Group brings laboratory-grade analytical discipline to the sampling process: chain of custody is documented, sample locations are photographed, and results are interpreted in the context of what was actually observed in the system, not just reported as a number.

The written assessment report identifies what was found, where it was found, and what remediation scope, if any, is warranted. That documentation is usable by your HVAC contractor, your insurance carrier, or a remediation firm.

## Equipment and Methods We Use for HVAC Assessment

Duct contamination assessments in a Valley climate call for tools calibrated to the conditions. Borescope cameras allow inspection of duct interiors without destructive access, useful in finished ceilings where cutting is not practical. Particle counters and air sampling pumps capture airborne particulate data at the register level, which can reveal whether a duct system is actively releasing material into occupied space even when nothing is visible at the surface. Moisture meters and thermal imaging help locate areas where condensation or infiltration has occurred inside insulated duct sections, problems that are invisible from the outside but show up clearly as temperature anomalies.

For properties where asbestos-containing duct insulation or duct tape is a possibility, a real concern in structures built before the mid-1980s, the team's asbestos consulting credentials mean that material identification happens as part of the same site visit, not as a separate engagement.

## Local Note

One pattern worth knowing in Selma and the surrounding Fresno County communities: evaporative coolers, swamp coolers, were the standard cooling solution in many homes built before central air became economical in the Valley. Properties that later had central HVAC added sometimes retained the old evaporative cooler ducting or used it as part of the new system layout. That hybrid ductwork can have significant gaps, mismatched materials, and areas that were never designed to handle the static pressure of a forced-air system. If your home has gone through that kind of retrofit, the duct system deserves a closer look than a standard assessment would give a purpose-built installation.
