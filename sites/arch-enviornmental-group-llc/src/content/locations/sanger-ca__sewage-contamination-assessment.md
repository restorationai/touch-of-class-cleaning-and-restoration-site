## Why Sanger Properties Face Elevated Sewage Contamination Risk

The Central Valley's clay-heavy soils shift seasonally, expanding with winter moisture and contracting through the long dry summers. That cycle stresses sewer laterals, particularly in properties built before modern flexible-joint pipe standards were common. Older homes in Sanger often have original clay or Orangeburg pipe running from the house to the city main, and those lines are prone to root intrusion, offset joints, and collapse. When a lateral fails, sewage doesn't just back up through floor drains, it can migrate laterally through subfloor cavities and wall chases before anyone sees visible evidence at the surface.

Sanger's agricultural setting also means that some properties on the city's edges are served by private septic systems rather than the municipal sewer network. A failing septic system introduces a different contamination profile than a municipal backup, and the assessment protocol, including soil sampling around the tank and drain field, differs accordingly. Knowing which system serves a given address is the first step before any sample collection begins.

## Our Sewage Contamination Assessment Process in Sanger

A sewage contamination assessment is a structured investigation, not a visual inspection. The process begins with a site walkthrough to identify all areas of suspected exposure, visible intrusion points, affected flooring materials, wall cavities, and HVAC pathways that may have pulled contaminated air through the structure. In Sanger's older housing stock, that walkthrough pays particular attention to pier-and-beam foundations and crawl spaces, where Category 3 water can pool out of sight.

Surface and bulk samples are collected from affected materials using chain-of-custody protocols and submitted to an accredited laboratory. Depending on the scope, sampling may include swab cultures for coliform bacteria, ATP surface testing for rapid preliminary readings, and air sampling where sewage gases are a concern. Written results document the contamination boundary, which materials are affected, which are clear, and what the laboratory findings show. That documentation is what a remediation contractor needs to scope the work correctly, and what an insurance adjuster needs to authorize coverage.
