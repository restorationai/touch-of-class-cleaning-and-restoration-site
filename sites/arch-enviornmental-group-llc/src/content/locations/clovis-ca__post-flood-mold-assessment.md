Clovis sits in the southern San Joaquin Valley, where summer heat regularly pushes past 100°F and the region's clay-heavy soils drain slowly after heavy rain events, a combination that creates ideal conditions for mold to take hold well before the water on your floor is even dry. If your home has experienced flooding from a broken pipe, a roof breach during a winter storm, or irrigation runoff, a post-flood mold assessment is not a precaution you can defer. Mold colonies can begin establishing within 24 to 48 hours on wet drywall, insulation, and subflooring, and in Clovis's climate, that window closes fast.

## Why Clovis Properties Face Elevated Mold Risk After Flooding

The San Joaquin Valley's climate creates a particular challenge: flood events often arrive in winter or early spring when outdoor humidity is high, but properties then sit through weeks of warm weather before repairs are completed. That warm, damp interior environment, especially in homes with minimal crawl space ventilation or slab-on-grade construction common throughout Clovis, accelerates mold colonization in ways that aren't always visible at the surface.

Clovis has grown rapidly over the past few decades, and its housing stock reflects several distinct construction eras. Older neighborhoods near the historic Old Town Clovis corridor tend to feature wood-frame construction with plaster or early drywall that absorbs and retains moisture differently than newer materials. Newer master-planned communities built on the city's expanding eastern edges often use engineered lumber and OSB sheathing, which can swell and delaminate quickly when saturated, and both materials support mold growth once moisture content climbs above roughly 19 percent. Neither era is immune; the risk profile is just different.

## Our Post-Flood Mold Assessment Process in Clovis

A thorough post-flood mold assessment is not a visual walkthrough. The process begins with moisture mapping, using calibrated pin and pinless meters to document moisture levels across affected walls, ceilings, and floors, including areas that appear dry at the surface. Water behind plaster or inside wall cavities can read dry to the touch while the structural framing behind it stays saturated for weeks.

Following moisture mapping, air sampling is collected from affected rooms and compared against an outdoor baseline. This comparison is essential: mold spore counts that look alarming in isolation may fall within normal range once measured against what's already present in Clovis's outdoor air during a given season. Tape-lift or swab samples may be collected from visible growth to identify species and inform any remediation scope. All samples are analyzed by an accredited laboratory, and results are compiled into a written assessment report, the document your insurance carrier, contractor, or HOA will need before remediation work begins.

Arch Environmental Group's certified team holds credentials as a Certified Asbestos Consultant and Lead Inspector Assessor, with NIOSH 582 microscopist certification, meaning the same inspection visit can flag co-occurring hazards like asbestos-containing materials or lead paint that are sometimes disturbed during flood damage repairs in older Clovis homes.
