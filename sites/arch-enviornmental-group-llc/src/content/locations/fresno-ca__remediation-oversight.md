## Why Fresno's Building Stock and Climate Create Oversight Challenges

Fresno grew rapidly through several distinct construction eras, and that layered history shows up in the walls, ceilings, and mechanical systems of buildings throughout the city. Commercial and multi-family structures built before the mid-1980s frequently contain asbestos-containing materials in floor tile, pipe insulation, roofing felts, and textured coatings, materials that can be disturbed during even routine tenant improvements if no one has conducted a proper survey first. Older residential neighborhoods contain homes where lead-based paint is a near-certainty on any pre-1978 surface.

The climate adds a separate layer of complexity. Fresno's hot, dry summers suppress surface moisture, which can mask elevated moisture readings inside wall cavities and under slab flooring, conditions where mold can establish before it becomes visible. When winter rains arrive, flat commercial roofs and aging stucco exteriors that have been slowly degrading through summer heat cycles are suddenly under water-intrusion pressure. That combination of deferred moisture damage and legacy building materials is exactly the scenario where a remediation contractor's scope of work needs an independent set of eyes.

## Our Remediation Oversight and Project Management Process

Effective third-party oversight starts before the remediation crew mobilizes. We review the existing survey data, assess whether the proposed scope of work addresses the full extent of contamination, and identify any gaps in the contractor's protocol that could result in incomplete abatement or a failed clearance. Our certified asbestos consultant and lead inspector/assessor credentials mean we can evaluate both the hazardous materials component and the microbial remediation plan within a single engagement, a meaningful efficiency on mixed-hazard projects.

During active remediation, we conduct periodic site visits to verify that containment barriers are properly constructed, negative air pressure is being maintained, and workers are following the written protocol. We document conditions with photographs and field notes that become part of the project record. At project closeout, we coordinate or conduct the clearance sampling required to confirm that abatement is complete before containment is broken and the space is returned to service. That clearance documentation is what a property owner needs when an insurer, a future buyer, or a regulatory agency asks whether the work was done correctly.

## Local Note

One pattern we see consistently in Fresno's older commercial inventory, particularly in buildings constructed during the postwar boom years that make up a significant portion of the city's light industrial and retail stock, is that asbestos-containing floor tile was often installed over original concrete slab in multiple layers. When a contractor's scope of work only accounts for the visible top layer, the underlying tile and mastic remain, and clearance sampling will reflect that. Catching this in the protocol review phase, before abatement begins, is far less expensive than a failed clearance and a change order. It's the kind of detail that shows up in a thorough pre-project review and gets missed when oversight isn't part of the project structure.
