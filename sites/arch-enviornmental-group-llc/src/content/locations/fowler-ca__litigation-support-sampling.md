## Why Fowler Properties Generate Environmental Disputes

The San Joaquin Valley's agricultural economy shaped Fowler's housing stock in ways that matter for environmental litigation. A significant portion of the city's residential properties were built in the mid-twentieth century, when asbestos-containing materials were standard in floor tiles, pipe insulation, and roofing felts, and when lead-based paint was still widely applied. When a landlord performs unpermitted renovations on an older Fowler rental, or when a buyer discovers post-close that a crawl space has been harboring moisture damage for years, the legal question almost always requires forensic-grade sampling, not a quick visual inspection.

Fowler's position in the valley also means that irrigation infrastructure, high water tables in certain parcels, and clay-heavy soils can drive moisture into slab foundations and subfloor assemblies in ways that are easy to miss on a surface inspection. Mold colonization behind baseboards or inside wall cavities can be well-established before any visible staining appears. In habitability disputes and insurance coverage disagreements, that hidden damage is exactly what forensic sampling is designed to document.

## Our Forensic Sampling and Litigation Support Process

Forensic environmental work is fundamentally different from a standard pre-remediation assessment. Every decision, where to sample, how many samples, which laboratory, how to document chain of custody, is made with the understanding that the data may be presented in a deposition, a mediation, or a courtroom. Our process in Fowler begins with a thorough site inspection and a review of any prior reports, repair records, or correspondence relevant to the dispute.

Sampling locations are selected to answer the specific legal question at issue, not simply to confirm a problem exists. For a mold dispute, that may mean air samples in occupied rooms alongside bulk or swab samples from suspect materials, with a control sample from outside the building for comparison. For an asbestos or lead claim, it means sampling the materials actually alleged to have been disturbed. As a Certified Asbestos Consultant and Lead Inspector/Assessor with NIOSH 582 microscopist credentials, the firm can analyze fiber samples and prepare reports that meet the evidentiary standards attorneys require. All samples are submitted to accredited third-party laboratories, and the resulting data is presented in a written report structured for legal use, clear methodology, defensible conclusions, and no overreach beyond what the data supports.

## Local Note

One pattern worth knowing in Fowler and the surrounding Fresno County communities: older agricultural worker housing, some of it converted or expanded informally over decades, often has layered construction history that complicates sampling strategy. A wall that looks like 1970s drywall on the surface may have original lath-and-plaster underneath, with insulation materials added at different eras. In litigation involving these properties, a single bulk sample from the visible layer can miss asbestos-containing material in the substrate. Thorough forensic sampling in these structures means probing the full assembly, not just the outermost finish, and documenting each layer separately so the report accurately reflects what was actually present and potentially disturbed.
