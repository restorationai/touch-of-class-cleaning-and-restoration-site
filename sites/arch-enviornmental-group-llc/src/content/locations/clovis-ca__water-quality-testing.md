Whether your household draws from a private well on the east side of town or from municipal supply lines that move through older distribution infrastructure, the contaminants worth testing for here, nitrates from surrounding farmland, arsenic from native geology, and bacteria that can enter through cracked well casings, are specific to this place. ## Why Clovis Properties Face Distinct Water Quality Concerns

The San Joaquin Valley floor is one of the most intensively farmed regions in the United States, and that agricultural intensity leaves a chemical fingerprint in local groundwater. Nitrate contamination, a byproduct of fertilizer application and animal waste, is a documented concern in Fresno County's groundwater basins, which underlie much of the Clovis area. Private well owners are especially exposed, because unlike municipal customers, they have no treatment plant between their aquifer and their tap.

Beyond agriculture, the geology itself matters. Naturally occurring arsenic concentrations in Valley groundwater have been a subject of state monitoring for years. Homes on private wells in the unincorporated fringe east of Clovis, where lot sizes grow and city water service thins out, can pull from aquifers where arsenic levels warrant periodic testing even if results have been clean before. Conditions shift as neighboring land use changes and as drought years concentrate dissolved solids in shallower wells.

Municipal customers aren't off the hook either. Older service lines and interior plumbing in homes built before the mid-1980s may contain lead solder or lead-bearing fixtures. Fresno County's housing stock includes a meaningful share of homes from that era, and lead does not announce itself, water can look, smell, and taste perfectly normal while carrying levels that exceed EPA action thresholds.

## Our Water Quality Testing Process in Clovis

Testing begins with a conversation about the property: well or municipal supply, age of the home, any recent changes to plumbing, and what's prompting the concern. That context shapes which panel of tests makes sense. A family with a newborn in a pre-1986 home needs a lead screen. A rural property owner who noticed a change in taste after last winter's heavy rains needs a coliform bacteria test alongside a nitrate panel.

Sampling is done following chain-of-custody protocols that preserve the integrity of results for regulatory or real-estate purposes. Samples are shipped to an accredited laboratory; we do not run in-house analysis and then hand you a number, you receive a certified lab report you can present to a lender, a county health department, or a prospective buyer. Our team holds credentials as a Lead Inspector/Assessor, which means lead-in-water findings can be contextualized within a broader lead-risk assessment of the property if that's needed.

Turnaround on most standard panels runs several business days from sample receipt at the lab. If results flag a concern, we walk through what the numbers mean in plain language, not just whether a value exceeds a standard, but what the likely source is and what remediation or treatment options are worth exploring.

One thing worth knowing if you're on a private well in the eastern Clovis area: the transition zone between city water service and unincorporated county parcels means some properties that carry a Clovis mailing address are actually served by private wells or small mutual water companies rather than the City of Clovis utility.
