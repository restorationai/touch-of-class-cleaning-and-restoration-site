## Why Centerville Properties Face Distinct Water Quality Challenges

The San Joaquin Valley's agricultural legacy is productive and also complicated. Decades of fertilizer application, pesticide use, and irrigation return flows have left nitrates and other agricultural byproducts in the region's shallow aquifers. Private well owners in rural Centerville are particularly exposed because their water supply sits outside the monitoring requirements that apply to public water systems. A well that tested clean years ago is not necessarily clean today. Groundwater levels shift with drought cycles, and as the water table drops, wells can begin drawing from different strata with different contaminant profiles.

Older homes in the area may also carry lead risk from the inside out. Plumbing installed before the late 1980s often used lead solder at copper joints, and galvanized steel pipes common in mid-century construction can leach iron, manganese, and other metals as they corrode. The water leaving a treatment plant or a clean well can pick up contaminants on its way to your glass if the pipes are old enough.

## Our Water Quality Testing Process in Centerville

Testing starts with understanding what you're trying to rule out. For a private well, that typically means a baseline panel covering coliform bacteria, nitrates, pH, hardness, and arsenic. If the property is near agricultural fields or an older fuel storage site, we expand the panel to screen for pesticides, volatile organic compounds, or other site-specific concerns. For homes on a community system where lead is the primary worry, we collect first-draw samples from kitchen and bathroom fixtures following EPA-recommended stagnation protocols.

Samples are handled under strict chain-of-custody procedures and sent to a certified laboratory. Results typically return within a few business days, and we walk you through what the numbers mean in plain terms. A result above the EPA maximum contaminant level for nitrates reads differently for a household with an infant than it does for a household of adults, and we explain that context rather than just handing you a spreadsheet.

Arch Environmental Group holds certifications as a Lead Inspector Assessor, which means lead-in-water testing is part of a broader lead risk assessment when the situation calls for it. If we're already evaluating paint or dust in an older home, adding a water sample to the scope is a natural extension, not a separate mobilization.

## Local Note

One thing worth knowing about private wells in this part of the San Joaquin Valley: arsenic occurs naturally in some of the deeper aquifers here, not just as a result of industrial contamination. California's maximum contaminant level for arsenic in public water is 10 parts per billion, but private wells are not subject to that standard by default. If your well was drilled to reach deeper water during a drought year, it may be drawing from a zone that was never tested for naturally occurring arsenic. This is not a scare tactic; it is a documented regional pattern, and a one-time test gives you a baseline that is worth having on file.
