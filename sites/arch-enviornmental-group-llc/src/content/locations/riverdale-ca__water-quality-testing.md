## Why Riverdale Properties Face Distinct Water Quality Concerns

Fresno County's San Joaquin Valley floor has some of the most intensively farmed land in the country. That agricultural intensity matters for water quality in ways that show up on lab reports. Nitrates from fertilizer application can migrate into shallow groundwater, and private wells that have not been tested in several years may carry concentrations that exceed safe thresholds for infants and pregnant women. Pesticide residues, including certain fumigants historically used on orchards and vineyards in this corridor, have been detected in Valley groundwater at various points over the decades.

Beyond agricultural inputs, the clay-dominant soils common to this part of Fresno County affect how quickly surface contaminants move downward and how well a well casing maintains its seal over time. Older well casings, particularly those installed before modern grouting standards became routine in California, can allow surface water intrusion during heavy rain events or spring snowmelt runoff from the Sierra Nevada foothills to the east. That intrusion pathway is one of the more common reasons a well that tested clean five years ago may test differently today.

## Lead and Older Plumbing in Riverdale Homes

Lead in drinking water is rarely a source problem in this part of California. It is almost always a plumbing problem. Homes built before 1986 may contain lead solder at copper pipe joints, and fixtures manufactured before that era can leach lead even when the supply water itself tests clean at the meter. If your home was built during or before the mid-1980s, a first-draw lead sample taken from the kitchen tap after water has sat in the pipes overnight is the most informative test you can run. ## Local Note

One thing worth knowing about this part of Fresno County: irrigation district activity can create temporary pressure fluctuations in the local groundwater table during peak growing season, typically late spring through early fall. Property owners with shallow wells have occasionally noticed a change in water clarity or taste during those periods, which is sometimes dismissed as a seasonal quirk. It is worth documenting and testing rather than assuming it will resolve on its own, because a seasonal pattern can indicate a recurring intrusion pathway that gets worse over time rather than better.
