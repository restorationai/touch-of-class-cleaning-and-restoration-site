## Why Fresno Properties Are Prone to Mold Growth

The San Joaquin Valley's climate does not behave like coastal California. Fresno sees summer temperatures that routinely climb above 100°F, which drives homeowners to run air conditioning for months at a time. That sustained HVAC use creates condensation in ductwork, around air handlers, and along poorly insulated walls, especially in homes built in the postwar tract-housing boom of the 1950s through 1970s, when vapor barriers and ventilation standards were far less rigorous than they are today.

When the season shifts and Tule fog settles in from late November through February, exterior-facing walls in older stucco homes absorb ambient moisture that has nowhere to drain. Crawl spaces under slab-on-grade homes in lower-lying parts of the city can hold ground moisture year-round, particularly in areas where the water table is influenced by the Kings River basin and surrounding agricultural irrigation. Mold can colonize a damp surface in as little as 24 to 48 hours, and it often does its worst work inside wall cavities and subfloor assemblies, places that look and feel dry at the surface.

## Our Mold Inspection and Testing Process in Fresno

A mold inspection is not the same as a mold test, and conflating the two leads homeowners to spend money in the wrong order. The inspection comes first: a trained assessor walks the property systematically, using moisture meters and thermal imaging to identify elevated readings behind surfaces, in crawl spaces, and around mechanical systems. Visual evidence, staining, efflorescence, deteriorating drywall paper, is documented with photographs before any samples are collected.

Sampling follows the inspection findings. Air samples capture spore counts in affected rooms and compare them against an outdoor baseline; surface samples (tape-lift or swab) identify the genus of mold present on a specific material. All samples are analyzed by an accredited laboratory. The resulting report identifies what species are present, at what concentrations, and which building materials are implicated, information a remediation contractor needs to scope the work correctly and that an insurance adjuster needs to process a claim.

That distinction matters when a report needs to hold up to insurer or legal scrutiny.

## Local Note: Stucco and Concealed Moisture in Fresno's Older Neighborhoods

One pattern that comes up repeatedly in Fresno's mid-century housing stock is moisture intrusion behind stucco cladding that reads completely dry on the interior surface. Traditional three-coat stucco is a slow moisture reservoir, it absorbs water during fog season or after irrigation overspray, then releases it inward over days or weeks. A homeowner running the AC in a sealed house creates a vapor pressure differential that pulls that moisture toward the cooler interior wall assembly. The result is mold growth inside the wall cavity that produces no visible staining and no obvious wet spot, but does produce elevated airborne spore counts and, eventually, a persistent musty odor. Thermal imaging during an inspection is often the only non-destructive way to locate these pockets before they become a full remediation project.
