Selma sits in the heart of the San Joaquin Valley, where summer heat regularly pushes past 100°F and the Kings River basin feeds a high water table that can complicate drainage after even a moderate flood event. When floodwater enters a home or commercial building here, whether from a broken irrigation line, a storm surge off Highway 99, or a backed-up municipal lateral, the combination of warm ambient temperatures and residual moisture creates near-ideal conditions for mold colonization within 24 to 48 hours. A post-flood mold assessment is not a precaution you schedule when it's convenient; it's a time-sensitive step that determines whether your building is safe to reoccupy and what remediation, if any, is actually required.

## Why Selma Properties Are Particularly Vulnerable After a Flood

The San Joaquin Valley's agricultural economy shaped Selma's residential development across several distinct eras, and that history matters when you're tracking moisture after a flood. Older homes in Selma, many built during the mid-20th century boom tied to the raisin and stone-fruit industries, often feature stucco exteriors over wood framing, concrete slab foundations, and original plaster or early drywall interiors. Stucco reads dry at the surface almost immediately after water intrusion, but the wood framing and wall cavities behind it can stay saturated for weeks. Standard visual inspection misses this entirely.

Selma's clay-heavy soils, common throughout Fresno County's valley floor, drain slowly after heavy rainfall or irrigation overflow. That slow drainage keeps soil moisture elevated against foundation walls and under slab edges, feeding humidity into crawl spaces and lower-level rooms long after the visible water is gone. Without systematic moisture mapping, that hidden reservoir can sustain mold growth for months before a homeowner notices discoloration or odor.

## Our Post-Flood Mold Assessment Process in Selma

Arch Environmental Group's assessment process is built around documentation and measurement, not guesswork. Every engagement begins with a thorough visual inspection of all affected areas, walls, ceilings, subfloor, HVAC returns, and any concealed cavities accessible without destructive opening. From there, the process moves through four concrete phases:

**Moisture Mapping:** Calibrated moisture meters and thermal imaging cameras identify wet zones that aren't visible to the eye. In Selma's older stucco-and-frame construction, this step is especially important because moisture migrates laterally through wall assemblies in ways that differ from modern OSB-sheathed framing.

**Air Sampling:** Spore trap or cassette samples are collected from affected rooms and from an outdoor control location. The comparison between indoor and outdoor spore counts, analyzed by a NIOSH 582-certified microscopist, tells you whether mold amplification is actually occurring inside the building, not just whether mold spores are present (they always are, at some level).

**Surface and Bulk Sampling:** Where visible growth is present or strongly suspected, swab or tape-lift samples identify the genus and species involved. This matters for remediation scoping: Cladosporium found on a window frame is a different conversation than Stachybotrys identified in a flooded wall cavity.

**Written Assessment Report:** Every assessment concludes with a written report documenting findings, sample results, moisture readings, and, where remediation is warranted, a scope of work that a licensed contractor can bid from. That report is also the document your insurance adjuster will need.
