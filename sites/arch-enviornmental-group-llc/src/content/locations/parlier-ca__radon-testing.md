Parlier sits in the heart of the San Joaquin Valley, where the same clay-heavy soils that make the surrounding farmland so productive also trap and channel soil gases in ways that matter inside your home. Radon, a colorless and odorless radioactive gas produced by the natural decay of uranium in soil and rock, can accumulate beneath slab foundations and crawl spaces without any visible sign. Because Parlier's residential neighborhoods include a mix of older agricultural-era housing and more recent tract construction, radon entry points and accumulation patterns vary considerably from one property to the next. A professional radon test is the only way to know what the air in your home actually contains.

## Why Parlier Properties See Radon Concerns

The San Joaquin Valley's geology is the starting point for understanding radon risk in Fresno County. The valley floor is underlain by alluvial deposits, including layers of decomposed granite washed down from the Sierra Nevada, and granite is one of the more uranium-bearing rock types. As uranium breaks down, it produces radium, and radium produces radon gas. That gas migrates upward through soil and enters structures through foundation cracks, utility penetrations, and the gap where a slab meets a stem wall.

Parlier's agricultural history also means a portion of the housing stock was built during an era when foundation sealing and sub-slab ventilation were not standard practice. Homes built before the 1980s rarely included the passive radon-resistant features that newer construction incorporates under current California building guidance. If your home was built before that period, or if it has a crawl space rather than a full slab, radon testing is a particularly important step.

Seasonal patterns add another layer. During the cooler months, homes in the Central Valley are closed up tightly, reducing the natural dilution that open windows provide in summer. Radon that seeps in during a well-sealed winter has fewer pathways out, which can push indoor concentrations higher than a warm-season test would show.

## Our Radon Testing Process in Parlier

Arch Environmental Group uses continuous electronic radon monitors rather than passive charcoal canisters for most residential tests. Continuous monitors record radon concentration at regular intervals throughout the test period, typically 48 hours for a short-term screening test, and produce a report that shows how levels fluctuate over time rather than just a single averaged number. That hourly data is useful in Parlier homes where HVAC cycling, window use, or slab construction type can cause meaningful swings in readings.

The monitor is placed in the lowest livable level of the home, which in most Parlier residences is the ground floor or a finished room adjacent to the slab. Doors and windows are kept closed under closed-house conditions for the duration of the test, a protocol established by the EPA and consistent with AARST-ANSI standards. At the end of the test period, the data is downloaded and a written report is generated that includes the average radon concentration in picocuries per liter, the hourly readings, and a clear statement of whether the result is above or below the EPA action level of 4 pCi/L.
