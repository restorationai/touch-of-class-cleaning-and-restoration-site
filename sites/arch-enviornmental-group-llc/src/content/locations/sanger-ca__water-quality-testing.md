If your home or property relies on a well, or if you've noticed a change in taste, odor, or clarity in your water, a professional water quality test is the clearest way to know what you're actually drinking. ## Why Sanger Properties Face Distinct Water Quality Concerns

The Central Valley's agricultural economy is one of the most productive in the world, and Sanger is surrounded by it, citrus groves, vineyards, and row crops stretch in every direction. That productivity comes with a water-quality tradeoff. Nitrates from fertilizer application, pesticide residues, and coliform bacteria from livestock operations can migrate into shallow groundwater over time, particularly after heavy rain events when surface water moves quickly through sandy loam soils into the underlying aquifer.

Sanger's older residential areas include homes built in the mid-twentieth century, when lead solder was standard in copper plumbing and lead-based paint was common. In those homes, the municipal supply may test clean at the meter, but water sitting in interior pipes can pick up lead before it reaches the faucet, a risk that's easy to miss without point-of-use sampling. Fresno County has also documented elevated arsenic levels in certain groundwater zones across the eastern valley floor, making arsenic screening a reasonable addition to any comprehensive well test in this area.

## Our Water Quality Testing Process in Sanger

Testing begins with a site assessment, understanding the source (municipal connection or private well), the age of the plumbing, and any known concerns in the immediate area. From there, samples are collected following chain-of-custody protocols and sent to an accredited laboratory for analysis. Depending on what you need to know, a panel can cover:

- **Bacteria and coliform**, the baseline for any well or post-flood situation
- **Nitrates and nitrites**, especially relevant for properties near agricultural land
- **Lead and copper**, critical for homes with pre-1986 plumbing
- **Arsenic, chromium, and other heavy metals**, warranted by local groundwater history
- **pH, hardness, and total dissolved solids**, useful context for filtration decisions

Results come back from the lab with a written interpretation, so you're not left reading a table of numbers without context. If a contaminant exceeds a regulatory threshold, the report explains what that means and what remediation or filtration options are typically used to address it.

## Local Note

One thing worth knowing about Sanger's private well stock: many wells in the area were drilled during the mid-twentieth century agricultural expansion and have casings that predate modern grouting standards. An older, shallow casing can allow surface water, and whatever it carries from nearby fields, to bypass the natural filtration of deeper soil layers and enter the well more directly. If your well casing is more than 40 or 50 years old and you've never had the water tested, a bacteria panel combined with a nitrate screen is a reasonable starting point before investing in any treatment system.
