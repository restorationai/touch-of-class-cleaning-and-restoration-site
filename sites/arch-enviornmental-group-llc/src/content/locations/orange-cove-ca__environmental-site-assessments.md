Orange Cove sits in the southeastern corner of Fresno County, where the foothills of the Sierra Nevada meet the eastern edge of the San Joaquin Valley floor. That geography matters when a buyer, lender, or developer needs an environmental site assessment: agricultural chemical use in the surrounding citrus and stone-fruit orchards, historic irrigation practices, and proximity to rural fuel storage create a due-diligence picture that is meaningfully different from a generic Central Valley transaction. Arch Environmental Group LLC brings certified environmental consulting to Orange Cove and the surrounding Fresno County foothill corridor, helping buyers and sellers understand what is in the ground before a deal closes.

## Why Orange Cove Properties Warrant Close Environmental Due Diligence

The land around Orange Cove has been intensively farmed for more than a century. Pesticide and fumigant applications, underground irrigation infrastructure, and the small fuel tanks common to farm equipment yards all create potential recognized environmental conditions (RECs) that a Phase I ESA is designed to identify. Older commercial parcels along the town's main corridors may also have operated as service stations, farm-supply stores, or equipment repair shops at some point in their history, uses that routinely involved petroleum products and solvents. A title search alone will not surface those histories; a records review, site reconnaissance, and interviews with knowledgeable parties, the three pillars of a Phase I conducted to ASTM E1527-21 standards, are what reveal them.

Fresno County's regulatory environment adds another layer. Agricultural parcels that are being converted to commercial or residential use may require coordination with the Fresno County Department of Public Health and the Central Valley Regional Water Quality Control Board, depending on what the Phase I uncovers. Understanding those agency relationships before you are under contract saves time and negotiating leverage.

## Our Environmental Site Assessment Process in Orange Cove

A Phase I ESA begins with a thorough review of federal and state environmental databases, historical aerial photographs, Sanborn fire insurance maps where available, and county assessor records. For Orange Cove parcels, historical aerials are especially useful because they can document orchard spray programs, former fuel storage locations, and changes in land use that predate current ownership by decades.

The site reconnaissance visit follows. The environmental professional walks the property, photographs structures, drainage patterns, staining, stressed vegetation, and any above- or below-ground storage tanks. In the foothill climate around Orange Cove, seasonal drainage channels that carry runoff from upslope agricultural areas deserve particular attention, because contaminants can migrate downhill and accumulate in low-lying areas of a parcel even when the source is off-site.

If the Phase I identifies RECs that cannot be resolved through records alone, a Phase II ESA moves to soil and groundwater sampling. Arch Environmental Group holds the certifications to carry that work forward, including asbestos consulting and lead inspector-assessor credentials that become relevant when older structures are part of the property being assessed.

The final ESA report documents findings, conclusions, and any recommended next steps in a format that satisfies lender requirements, SBA guidelines, and most commercial real estate purchase agreements.

## Local Note

One thing that surprises buyers new to the Orange Cove area: many parcels here carry senior water rights tied to the Consolidated Irrigation District or smaller mutual water companies, and those water delivery laterals sometimes run through or beneath commercial lots in ways that are not reflected on standard utility maps. During a site reconnaissance, we note visible irrigation infrastructure and flag it in the report, because buried laterals and their associated access easements can affect where soil borings are placed in a Phase II and how a future owner plans site improvements. It is a detail that rarely comes up in a downtown Fresno transaction but is routine in the foothill citrus belt.
