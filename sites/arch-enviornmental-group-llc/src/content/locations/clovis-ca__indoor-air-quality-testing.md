## Why Clovis Properties See Air Quality Challenges

The San Joaquin Valley is one of the most pollutant-burdened airsheds in the country, and that outdoor reality doesn't stay outside. When HVAC systems pull in air to cool a home through a Clovis summer, they also pull in fine particulate matter, ozone-related byproducts, and agricultural aerosols that accumulate in duct systems and settle into building materials. Homes built during Clovis's rapid growth periods, particularly the tract developments that expanded the city's footprint through the 1990s and 2000s, often feature tightly sealed construction that improves energy efficiency but reduces natural air exchange. Without adequate ventilation, volatile organic compounds (VOCs) from flooring adhesives, cabinetry, and interior finishes concentrate indoors rather than dissipating.

Older housing stock in the more established parts of Clovis presents a different set of concerns. Homes built before the 1980s may contain building materials with asbestos-containing compounds in floor tiles, insulation, or textured ceiling coatings, materials that remain stable until disturbed by renovation or mechanical damage. Lead-based paint in pre-1978 construction is another factor, particularly in properties that have changed hands multiple times and seen piecemeal remodeling over the decades.

## Our Indoor Air Quality Testing Process in Clovis

Testing begins with a walkthrough assessment of the property, noting HVAC configuration, visible moisture indicators, renovation history, and any occupant-reported symptoms such as persistent headaches, respiratory irritation, or unusual odors. That context shapes the sampling plan, because a home with a recent bathroom remodel raises different questions than one where occupants are reacting to something they cannot identify.

Air samples are collected using calibrated pumps and media appropriate to the contaminant in question: spore trap cassettes for mold and biological particulates, sorbent tubes for VOC analysis, and phase-contrast or transmission electron microscopy (TEM) preparation for asbestos fiber identification. Arch Environmental Group's team includes a NIOSH 582-certified microscopist, which means asbestos fiber analysis is evaluated by a credentialed specialist, not simply forwarded to a third-party lab with no chain of custody oversight. Bulk material samples from suspect building components are collected following established protocols and submitted to accredited laboratories. Results are compiled into a written report that identifies what was found, at what concentrations, and what those levels mean relative to established reference guidelines.

## Equipment and Methods We Use for Air Quality

The equipment used for indoor air quality testing matters as much as the credentials of the person operating it. Calibrated air sampling pumps with verified flow rates ensure that sample volumes are accurate, a detail that directly affects whether laboratory results are defensible in an insurance or legal context. For mold assessment, both air sampling and surface sampling (tape lifts or swabs) are often used together, because air counts alone can miss settled reservoirs of growth that aren't actively releasing spores at the moment of testing. Thermal imaging and moisture meters are used during the walkthrough phase to identify areas of elevated moisture that may not yet show visible mold but represent conditions where biological growth is likely.
