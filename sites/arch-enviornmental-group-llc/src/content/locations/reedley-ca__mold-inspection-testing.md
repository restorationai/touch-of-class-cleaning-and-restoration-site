## Why Reedley Properties See Elevated Mold Risk

The San Joaquin Valley's climate is often described as semi-arid, but that label understates how wet Reedley's microenvironment can get. Drip irrigation and flood irrigation in the surrounding orchards and vineyards raise ambient humidity across the growing season, and that moisture doesn't stop at property lines. It seeps into crawl spaces under older ranch-style homes, condenses on poorly insulated ductwork, and collects in wall cavities where vapor barriers were never installed or have since degraded.

Reedley's residential housing stock includes a significant number of homes built in the mid-twentieth century, when building practices didn't prioritize vapor control the way modern codes do. Those older homes often have original single-pane windows, minimal attic ventilation, and wood-framed walls that absorb and hold moisture. When a slow plumbing leak or a roof flashing failure goes unnoticed for even a week or two, mold colonization can begin, and in the warm interior temperatures common here in summer, it can spread faster than in cooler climates.

## Our Mold Inspection and Testing Process in Reedley

A mold inspection is not a visual sweep. The process starts with a structured walk-through to identify moisture sources, checking under sinks, around water heaters, along the roofline, and in any crawl space or attic accessible on the property. Moisture readings are taken at suspect surfaces using calibrated meters, because mold often hides in areas that look dry at the surface while the substrate behind them remains saturated.

When conditions warrant sampling, and in Reedley's agricultural-humidity environment, they frequently do, we collect air samples using a calibrated pump and spore trap cassettes. Those samples are sent to an accredited laboratory for analysis. The lab report identifies the genera and relative concentrations of mold spores present indoors compared to an outdoor baseline sample taken the same day. That comparison is what tells you whether elevated mold levels are coming from inside the building or are simply a reflection of the regional outdoor environment.

Surface samples, including tape lifts or swabs, may be collected from visible growth or stained areas to confirm the species present. The resulting written report documents findings, sampling locations, and, where applicable, recommendations for remediation scope. Because our team holds a NIOSH 582 Microscopist certification alongside our Certified Asbestos Consultant and Lead Inspector Assessor credentials, we bring laboratory-level analytical competency to the assessment rather than relying solely on third-party interpretation.

## Local Note: Irrigation Season and Indoor Air Quality

Something that comes up specifically in Reedley and the surrounding Kings County agricultural corridor: during active irrigation periods, outdoor mold spore counts can be elevated relative to baseline readings taken in drier months. This matters for air sampling interpretation. An indoor-to-outdoor spore ratio that looks alarming in February may read differently in July when field irrigation is running nearby. Experienced inspectors account for seasonal outdoor baselines when interpreting results, a detail that generic national testing kits sold at hardware stores cannot account for, because they provide no outdoor reference sample at all.

If you are scheduling a mold assessment during the summer growing season, mention it when you call. We time outdoor baseline sampling to coincide with the indoor collection so the comparison is valid for the conditions on that specific day.
