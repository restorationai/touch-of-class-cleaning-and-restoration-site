## Why Reedley Properties Are Prone to Mold Growth

The San Joaquin Valley's agricultural economy is Reedley's backbone, but it also means the surrounding landscape is heavily irrigated from spring through fall. Subsurface moisture levels stay elevated, and crawl spaces beneath older ranch-style homes in the area can accumulate ground vapor even when there's been no visible plumbing failure. Combine that with the region's temperature swings, triple-digit summer highs followed by cool, foggy winters, and you have a climate that stresses building envelopes repeatedly over time.

Many homes in Reedley were built during the postwar residential expansion of the 1950s through 1970s. Construction from that era typically used less vapor barrier material than current code requires, and original plumbing is now old enough to develop slow, hidden leaks behind walls. Those slow leaks are particularly problematic because the surface may look and feel dry while the framing behind it stays damp long enough for mold colonies to take hold.

## Our Mold Inspection and Testing Process in Reedley

A thorough mold assessment is not a visual walk-through with a flashlight. The inspection process begins with a structured interview about the property's history, any past water intrusion, HVAC issues, or musty odors, followed by a systematic room-by-room evaluation using moisture meters and thermal imaging to locate areas of elevated moisture that aren't visible to the eye.

Where conditions warrant, air samples are collected using calibrated spore-trap cassettes and sent to an accredited third-party laboratory for analysis. Surface or bulk samples may also be collected if there is visible suspect growth. The laboratory results identify the genera of mold present and the spore concentration levels, giving you and any remediation contractor a clear, documented picture of what you're dealing with. Because Arch Environmental Group holds NIOSH 582 Microscopist certification, our team understands how to interpret those analytical results, not just receive them.

The final deliverable is a written inspection report that documents findings, sample locations, laboratory data, and recommendations. That report is what your insurance adjuster, contractor, or real estate attorney will need if the situation escalates.
