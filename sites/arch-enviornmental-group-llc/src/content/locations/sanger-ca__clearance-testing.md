## Our Clearance Testing and Post-Remediation Verification Process in Sanger

Clearance testing is not a single air sample and a signature. The process follows a structured sequence designed to catch failures that visual inspection misses.

First, the remediated area is inspected for physical completeness, no visible mold growth, no remaining debris, no damaged materials left in place. If that baseline isn't met, sampling doesn't begin; testing a space that isn't visually clean produces misleading results and wastes the property owner's time and money.

Once the visual inspection passes, air-o-cell or spore-trap cassette samples are collected inside the remediated area and simultaneously outdoors. The indoor-to-outdoor ratio tells the story: if indoor counts are at or below outdoor levels for the same species, the remediation is performing as intended. If certain indicator species, Stachybotrys, Chaetomium, or Aspergillus/Penicillium at elevated concentrations, appear indoors but not outdoors, the remediation is incomplete.

For projects involving older building materials, bulk samples or air samples for asbestos are collected under chain-of-custody protocols and submitted to an accredited laboratory. Results are compiled into a written clearance letter that documents sampling locations, laboratory findings, and the professional's determination, the document an insurance adjuster, a contractor, or a future buyer needs to close the file.
