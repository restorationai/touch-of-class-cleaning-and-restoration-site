## Why Hanford Homes Accumulate Allergens at Higher Rates

The San Joaquin Valley's air quality challenges are well documented, but indoor allergen loads are a separate problem that outdoor AQI readings don't capture. Hanford's climate is hot and dry for much of the year, which pushes residents to keep homes sealed and HVAC systems running continuously from late spring through early fall. That extended recirculation time lets dust mite allergen, pet dander, and tracked-in agricultural particulates build up in carpet fibers, upholstered furniture, and ductwork without the natural flushing that cooler, ventilated climates provide.

Homes built in Hanford during the postwar suburban expansions of the 1950s through 1970s often have original ductwork with accumulated debris, and some retain single-pane windows and door gaps that allow fine particulate infiltration from nearby fields. Newer construction on the city's eastern and western edges tends toward tighter building envelopes, which reduces outdoor infiltration but can trap pet dander and dust mite colonies indoors more effectively if HVAC filtration is not maintained.

## What Allergen Testing Actually Involves

Allergen testing is not the same as air quality sampling. The process targets settled dust, which holds a more stable and measurable concentration of allergens than airborne spot samples. Our technicians collect dust samples from specific locations: mattress surfaces, upholstered seating, carpeted floors, and HVAC return vents. Each sample is labeled, chain-of-custody documented, and submitted to an accredited laboratory for analysis.

The lab report identifies and quantifies specific allergen proteins, most commonly dust mite allergen (Der p 1 and Der f 1), cat allergen (Fel d 1), dog allergen (Can f 1), and cockroach allergen (Bla g 2). Results are returned with concentration levels measured in micrograms per gram of dust, which can be compared against established thresholds associated with sensitization risk. We review the findings with you in plain language, room by room, so you understand where concentrations are elevated and what remediation or mitigation steps make sense.
