## Why Ivanhoe-Area Properties Warrant Careful Environmental Due Diligence

The San Joaquin Valley's agricultural economy has shaped Ivanhoe's land in ways that are not always visible at the surface. Decades of pesticide and herbicide application, on-site fuel storage for farm equipment, and the presence of older agrochemical mixing and storage areas mean that recognized environmental conditions (RECs) appear with notable frequency during Phase 1 reviews of rural and transitional parcels in this part of Tulare County.

Soil composition in the valley floor around Ivanhoe tends toward clay-heavy profiles that restrict vertical drainage. When a petroleum release or chemical spill occurs in these soils, contamination can spread laterally rather than percolating straight down, which complicates both detection and any subsequent Phase 2 sampling design. A consultant who understands this drainage behavior will design a more accurate scope of work than one applying a generic template.

Older structures in the area, packing sheds, equipment barns, and rural commercial buildings constructed before modern material regulations, also carry a meaningful probability of containing asbestos-containing materials or lead-based paint. Arch Environmental Group holds certifications as a Certified Asbestos Consultant and Lead Inspector Assessor, which means the same team conducting your ESA can identify these additional concerns without requiring a separate contractor engagement.

## Our Environmental Site Assessment Process in Ivanhoe

A Phase 1 ESA follows the ASTM E1527-21 standard, which establishes the scope of records review, site reconnaissance, and interviews that must occur before a report is issued. In practice, applying that standard to a Tulare County agricultural property involves a few steps that differ from urban or suburban assessments.

Records research for Ivanhoe parcels draws on Tulare County Assessor records, California GeoTracker and EnviroStar databases, historical aerial photography, and Sanborn fire insurance maps where available. Agricultural parcels often lack the dense paper trail of commercial urban properties, so historical aerials become especially important for identifying former structures, waste disposal areas, or chemical storage that may no longer appear on current records.

Site reconnaissance in this area frequently involves walking active or recently active agricultural land, which requires coordination with property owners or tenants and awareness of irrigation schedules and crop cycles. Our team documents current conditions, photographs areas of concern, and notes any staining, stressed vegetation, or surface features that warrant attention in the report.

If Phase 1 findings identify a REC that requires further investigation, a Phase 2 ESA involves soil borings, groundwater sampling, or soil vapor surveys designed to characterize the nature and extent of any contamination. Sampling locations and depths are planned around what Phase 1 revealed, not around a generic grid.

## Local Note

One detail that comes up specifically on Ivanhoe-area parcels: properties that were historically used for citrus packing or cold storage sometimes have underground ammonia refrigeration infrastructure that was decommissioned in place rather than properly removed. These systems are not always disclosed in title records and do not always appear in obvious surface features, but they represent a potential recognized environmental condition under ASTM standards.
