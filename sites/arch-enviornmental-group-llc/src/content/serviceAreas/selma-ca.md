## Restoration emergencies common in Selma

Selma sits in the heart of the San Joaquin Valley, and the Valley's climate creates a predictable cycle of property damage. Winters bring atmospheric river events, multi-day rain that overwhelms flat-lot drainage and pushes water through foundation stem walls, garage slabs, and aging roof penetrations. Summers bring triple-digit heat that stresses plumbing joints and accelerates mold colonization inside walls that never fully dried after a winter leak. The region's dense agricultural activity means irrigation canals and high seasonal water tables can elevate soil moisture around foundations even during dry months. Selma's housing stock, much of it built during the postwar boom and again in the 1980s and 1990s, includes a mix of slab-on-grade ranch homes and wood-frame construction that can conceal moisture for weeks before surface signs appear. That combination of climate, soil, and building age is what drives most of the water, mold, and fire calls in this city.
