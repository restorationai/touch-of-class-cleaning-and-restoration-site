## Restoration emergencies common in Selma

Selma sits in the heart of the San Joaquin Valley, where the climate creates a specific and recurring set of property risks. Summers are long and dry with temperatures routinely climbing past 100°F, which accelerates moisture evaporation at the surface, but can mask slow leaks behind walls and under slabs that go undetected until mold has already colonized. Winter brings the valley's notorious tule fog and periodic atmospheric river events that can deliver several inches of rain in a short window, overwhelming older drainage systems and pushing water into crawlspaces and low-lying foundations.

The valley's clay-heavy soils expand when wet and contract sharply when dry, a cycle that stresses slab foundations and the plumbing lines running beneath them. A pinhole leak in a supply line under a concrete slab can go unnoticed for weeks, feeding moisture into the subfloor assembly and creating ideal conditions for mold growth. Wildfire smoke from fires burning in the Sierra Nevada foothills is a seasonal reality as well, leaving fine particulate and odor compounds on interior surfaces that standard cleaning does not fully address.

## Services we provide in Selma

### Mold testing and assessment

Mold in Selma homes often traces back to plumbing failures, roof penetrations, or inadequate crawlspace ventilation rather than flooding events. Because the valley's dry summers can mask active moisture intrusion, mold colonies sometimes establish behind drywall or under flooring before any visible sign appears. Arch Environmental Group conducts air sampling, surface sampling, and moisture mapping to identify the scope of contamination and provide a written assessment that insurance carriers and remediation contractors can act on.

### Water damage evaluation

Water damage in Selma frequently involves slow, hidden sources, a failing water heater, a corroded galvanized supply line, or a roof flashing failure discovered only after the ceiling shows a stain. The evaluation process includes thermal imaging and moisture meter readings across wall cavities, subfloor assemblies, and ceiling planes to locate saturation that reads dry at the surface.

### Air quality and environmental testing

Valley air quality is among the most affected in California due to agricultural activity, vehicle emissions, and wildfire smoke. Interior air quality testing identifies particulate levels, VOCs, and biological contaminants, useful after a smoke event, after a remediation project, or when occupants report unexplained respiratory symptoms. Results are documented in a format suitable for disclosure, insurance claims, or regulatory review.

### Hazardous material identification

Asbestos-containing materials and lead-based paint are common in Selma's pre-1980 housing stock. Before any renovation or restoration scope disturbs suspect materials, sampling and laboratory analysis establishes what is present and informs the remediation plan.

## Building stock, site conditions, and permits in Selma

Selma's residential neighborhoods reflect two distinct construction periods. The older core near downtown contains homes built from the 1940s through the 1960s, typically wood-frame with stucco exteriors, composition or clay tile roofs, and either slab-on-grade or raised-floor foundations with crawlspaces. Plumbing in these homes commonly includes galvanized steel supply lines and cast iron drain lines, both prone to corrosion and pinhole failures that can go undetected for extended periods. Pre-1980 construction in this stock should be assumed to contain asbestos-containing materials in floor tiles, duct insulation, and textured ceiling coatings, and lead-based paint on interior and exterior surfaces.

Newer subdivisions built from the 1980s onward are predominantly slab-on-grade with copper or PEX supply lines. The San Joaquin Valley's expansive clay soils create ongoing movement stress on slab foundations, which can open gaps at plumbing penetrations over time.
