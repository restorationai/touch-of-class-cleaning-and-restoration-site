## Restoration emergencies common in Reedley

Reedley sits in the southern San Joaquin Valley, where the climate swings between dense tule fog in winter and triple-digit heat in summer. That combination does real damage to buildings over time. Moisture trapped inside wall cavities during cool, humid winters can begin supporting mold growth within days, particularly in older homes where vapor barriers were never installed or have degraded. When summer heat arrives, it accelerates off-gassing from water-damaged materials and drives up indoor humidity in homes without adequate mechanical ventilation.

The Kings River runs close enough to Reedley's agricultural fringe that localized flooding is a seasonal reality, especially after heavy Sierra Nevada snowmelt in late winter and early spring. Irrigation infrastructure throughout the surrounding farmland also means that subsurface water pressure can shift unexpectedly, affecting foundations and crawlspaces in older residential blocks. Combine that with aging supply plumbing in mid-century housing and you have a city where slow leaks, hidden moisture, and mold are among the most common reasons property owners call for an environmental assessment.

## Building stock, site conditions, and permits in Reedley

Reedley's housing stock is predominantly mid-twentieth century, with a meaningful share of homes built between the 1940s and 1970s. Construction from that era in the San Joaquin Valley typically means wood-frame walls on slab-on-grade foundations, though some older neighborhoods include raised foundations with unconditioned crawlspaces.
