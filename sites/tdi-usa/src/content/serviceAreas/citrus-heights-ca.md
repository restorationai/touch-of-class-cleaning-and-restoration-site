## Restoration emergencies common in Citrus Heights

Citrus Heights sits on the eastern edge of the Sacramento Valley, where the climate delivers long, dry summers and a compressed wet season that typically runs from November through March. When the atmospheric river storms that regularly hit the greater Sacramento region arrive, they can drop several inches of rain in a short window — more than the clay-heavy soils common in this part of Sacramento County can absorb quickly. That means surface water backs up against foundations, window wells flood, and roof penetrations that held through the dry season suddenly don't.

The city's housing stock amplifies the risk. A large share of Citrus Heights homes were built between the late 1950s and the early 1980s, when slab-on-grade construction dominated and interior drainage details were minimal by today's standards. Aging supply lines and drain assemblies in that era's homes fail quietly — a slow pinhole leak behind a cabinet can saturate wall cavities and subfloor framing for weeks before a homeowner notices a soft spot or a musty smell. Sacramento Valley summers also bring wildfire smoke events that push fine particulate into HVAC systems and attic spaces, leaving odor and residue that standard cleaning won't reach.

## Services we provide in Citrus Heights

**Water damage restoration** — Citrus Heights slab homes don't have crawlspaces to ventilate, so when water intrudes under a slab or wicks up through a concrete floor, moisture has nowhere to go. TDI® USA uses thermal imaging and calibrated moisture meters to locate water that reads dry at the surface while the underlying assembly stays saturated, then deploys commercial drying equipment to pull moisture out before secondary damage — warped flooring, mold colonization, compromised drywall — takes hold.

Restoration here goes beyond surface cleaning: it means sealing or replacing affected structural materials, deodorizing ductwork, and addressing any asbestos-containing materials that a fire may have disturbed in pre-1980 construction.

**Reconstruction** — When a loss requires structural repair — replacing framing, subfloor, drywall, or roofing — TDI® USA handles the rebuild phase so property owners work with one point of contact from emergency response through finished restoration.

## Building stock, site conditions, and permits in Citrus Heights

Most of Citrus Heights was developed between the late 1950s and early 1980s, producing a housing stock that is predominantly slab-on-grade with wood-frame walls and composition shingle roofing. Homes built before 1980 may contain asbestos in floor tile, popcorn ceilings, or duct insulation, and lead-based paint in older interior finishes — both of which affect how a restoration scope is written and what abatement steps precede drywall or flooring replacement.

Soils in this part of Sacramento County include expansive clay that swells with seasonal moisture, which can stress slab edges and foundation perimeters over time. Supply plumbing in homes from the 1960s and 1970s is often galvanized steel, which corrodes from the inside out; copper became standard through the 1980s. Polybutylene supply lines, installed in some late-1970s through mid-1990s construction, are prone to failure and worth identifying during any water-loss investigation.

Structural repairs and reconstruction in Citrus Heights fall under Sacramento County's building and planning authority. Work that affects structural members, electrical, plumbing, or the building envelope typically requires a permit, and restoration scopes should account for inspection scheduling when sequencing drywall closure.
