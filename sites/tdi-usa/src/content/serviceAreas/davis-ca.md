## Restoration emergencies common in Davis

Davis sits in the Sacramento Valley floor, where the combination of a shallow water table, heavy clay soils, and the region's pronounced wet-dry seasonal cycle creates predictable stress on buildings. Winter atmospheric river events can drop several inches of rain in 24 hours, overwhelming gutters, area drains, and crawlspace vapor barriers. Because much of Davis was built during the postwar suburban expansion and again through the UC Davis enrollment booms of the 1960s through 1980s, a large share of the housing stock carries aging supply lines, original slab penetrations, and insulation that was never designed to shed a serious intrusion. In summer, the valley's triple-digit heat drives HVAC systems hard; condensate line failures and refrigerant-coil drip pans are a routine source of slow, hidden moisture that feeds mold before a homeowner notices a stain. Wildfire smoke from the Sierra foothills and Coast Range corridors — increasingly common in late summer and fall — infiltrates attics and HVAC returns, leaving odor and fine-particulate residue that surface cleaning alone will not resolve.

## Services we provide in Davis

### Water damage mitigation
Water damage in Davis frequently involves slab-on-grade construction, where moisture wicks upward through concrete and traps under luxury vinyl or hardwood flooring long after the surface reads dry. TDI® USA uses thermal imaging and penetrating moisture meters to map the actual wet boundary before any drying equipment is placed, then documents conditions for your insurance carrier throughout the process.

### Fire and smoke damage restoration
Smoke from a kitchen fire or a structure fire nearby behaves differently in Davis's older plaster-and-lath homes than in newer drywall construction — it penetrates deeper into porous surfaces and lingers in wall cavities. Restoration here typically requires content pack-out, HEPA air scrubbing, and odor counteractant treatment of structural cavities, not just surface wiping.

### Mold remediation
The combination of clay soil, limited drainage slope in many Davis neighborhoods, and older vapor barriers creates conditions where mold can colonize within 24 to 48 hours of a water event. TDI® USA follows EPA mold remediation guidelines and contains work areas to prevent cross-contamination during removal — important in the multi-unit housing common near the UC Davis campus.

### Biohazard and specialty cleanup
Unattended situations, sewage backups, and other biohazard events require proper containment, PPE protocols, and disposal under California Department of Public Health guidelines. These are not DIY repairs, and the scope of work needs to be documented carefully for both health compliance and insurance purposes.

### Reconstruction
Once mitigation is complete, the same team can carry structural repairs through to finish — drywall, flooring, cabinetry, painting — so you are not coordinating a separate general contractor while your home is still partially open.

## Building stock, site conditions, and permits in Davis

Most of Davis's single-family housing was constructed between the 1950s and the 1990s, predominantly slab-on-grade with wood-frame walls and composition shingle roofs. Homes built before the mid-1970s may contain asbestos-containing materials in floor tile, joint compound, or roofing, and lead-based paint on interior trim — both of which require abatement planning before any restoration scope disturbs them. Supply plumbing in that era typically ran galvanized steel or copper; galvanized lines corrode from the inside out and can fail with little visible warning. Davis's soils are predominantly heavy clay, which expands when wet and contracts in the dry season, placing cyclic stress on slab edges and foundation perimeter drains. The City of Davis Building Division issues permits for structural repair, re-roofing, and any work that alters the building envelope; a restoration project that involves opening walls or replacing structural members will generally require a permit and inspection before close-up. California's Title 24 energy code governs insulation and vapor management in any permitted repair, which affects how a restoration contractor must document and replace wall assemblies.
