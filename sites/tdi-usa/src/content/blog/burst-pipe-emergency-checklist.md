If a pipe just burst in your home, go straight to your main water shutoff valve and turn it off — clockwise until it stops. That single action limits how much water enters the structure and gives you time to think. Once the water is off, cut power to any rooms that are flooded at the breaker panel, then start documenting everything with your phone camera before you touch a thing. The steps below walk you through the full response, from the first five minutes to the weeks that follow.

## Immediate Steps: The First 15 Minutes

Sequence matters here. Do these in order.

1. **Shut off the main water supply.** In most Sacramento homes, the main shutoff is at the meter box near the street or on the exterior wall closest to the street-facing side of the house. Older homes in the Midtown and East Sacramento neighborhoods often have gate valves (round wheel handle) that require several full turns. Newer construction typically has a ball valve (lever handle) that shuts off with a quarter-turn.

2. **Kill the electricity to wet areas.** Water and live circuits are a serious hazard. Go to your breaker panel and switch off any circuits serving rooms where water is visible or suspected — kitchens, bathrooms, laundry rooms, finished basements. If the panel itself is in a wet area, do not touch it. Call your utility (SMUD or PG&E, depending on your neighborhood) and ask them to disconnect service at the meter.

3. **Open faucets to drain remaining pressure.** After the main is off, open the lowest faucet in the house — usually a hose bib or a first-floor sink — to release residual pressure and drain water still sitting in the lines. This reduces how much continues to seep from the break point.

4. **Photograph and video everything before cleanup.** Walk every affected room and record standing water depth, waterlines on walls, wet flooring, and the visible break point if you can safely access it. Your insurance adjuster will ask for this documentation, and it is much harder to reconstruct after the fact.

5. **Locate the burst.** Common failure points in Sacramento homes include supply lines under sinks and behind toilets (especially braided steel lines older than seven years), the section of pipe where it enters through an exterior wall, and older galvanized or polybutylene lines in homes built before the mid-1990s. If the burst is inside a wall, you may hear dripping but see only a wet patch or a bubbling paint surface — the actual break can be several feet from where the water appears.

## What NOT to Do

Some instincts that feel helpful will make the situation worse.

- **Do not use a shop vac or household vacuum on standing water if power is still on in that room.** Extension cords and plug-in equipment in standing water can be lethal.
- **Do not assume the floor is dry because the surface feels dry.** Hardwood and engineered wood floors can look fine while holding significant moisture in the subfloor below. Tile can feel dry while the mortar bed and the OSB or plywood underneath are saturated.
- **Do not run fans and think that is sufficient drying.** Household fans move air but cannot measure moisture content or reach inside wall cavities. They can also spread mold spores if mold has already begun to colonize — and in Sacramento's warm summers, mold can begin establishing within 24 to 48 hours of a water event.
- **Do not discard damaged materials before your adjuster sees them.** Pull wet items away from walls and out of standing water to prevent further wicking, but keep them on-site and photographed. Disposing of flooring, drywall, or cabinetry before the claim is documented can complicate your coverage.
- **Do not delay calling your insurance company.** Most homeowner policies have notification requirements. Waiting several days to report a loss — even while you are trying to handle cleanup yourself — can give a carrier grounds to question coverage.

## When the Situation Requires a Professional

Some burst pipe situations are genuinely manageable with a plumber and a few days of careful drying. Many are not. Call a water damage restoration company when:

- **Water reached more than one room or one floor.** Water migrates fast and follows framing channels, electrical conduit paths, and HVAC ductwork. What looks like a single-room event often has hidden spread.
- **The affected area includes a bathroom, kitchen, or laundry room.** These rooms have multiple supply and drain lines, and a burst in one can compromise connections in others.
- **Water was running undetected for more than a few hours.** The longer water sits, the deeper it penetrates into subfloor assemblies and wall framing. A loss that ran overnight while you were asleep is a different problem than one you caught in the first ten minutes.
- **You see or smell anything that suggests prior moisture damage.** A burst pipe in a wall that already had a slow leak behind it can expose a mold condition that predates the current event. That requires a different remediation approach than a clean-water loss.
- **Your home was built before 1978.** Disturbing drywall or plaster in older Sacramento homes can release lead paint dust, which requires specific handling protocols.

A professional restoration team will use thermal imaging cameras and moisture meters to map the actual extent of the water — not just the visible wet area — and establish a drying plan using commercial dehumidifiers and air movers calibrated to your specific conditions. This is the work that prevents a burst pipe from becoming a mold remediation project three weeks later.

## The Longer Recovery: What Happens After the Water Is Gone

Drying a structure properly takes longer than most homeowners expect. Even after standing water is extracted, materials like plywood subfloor, wall framing, and insulation hold moisture well beyond what you can feel with your hand. Professional drying typically involves several days of continuous equipment operation, with moisture readings taken at multiple points each day to confirm the structure is returning to normal levels.

Once the structure is dry and documented, the repair and rebuild phase begins. Depending on the scope, this can include:

- Replacing sections of drywall or plaster that were removed for drying access or that absorbed too much water to salvage
- Reinstalling insulation (wet insulation loses its R-value and can harbor mold even after it appears dry)
- Refinishing or replacing flooring
- Repainting affected walls and ceilings

If the burst was caused by a failing appliance connection — a washing machine supply hose, a refrigerator ice-maker line, or a dishwasher fitting — the cleanup overlaps with what is typically categorized as appliance leak cleanup, which follows the same drying and documentation process but may involve different sections of your policy.

Keep all receipts, contractor invoices, and equipment rental records. Your adjuster will need an itemized account of mitigation costs separate from the repair estimate.
