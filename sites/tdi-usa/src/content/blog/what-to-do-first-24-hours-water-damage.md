Stop what you're doing and read this first: if water is still entering your home, locate your main shutoff valve and turn it off before anything else. For most Sacramento homes, that valve is near the front of the house at the meter box or where the supply line enters the foundation. Once the water stops, you have a narrow window — roughly 24 to 48 hours — before wet materials begin supporting mold growth and structural damage compounds. Here is exactly what to do, in order.

## Step 1: Stop the Source and Make the Space Safe

If the water came from a burst pipe, failed appliance supply line, or overflowing fixture, shutting off the main supply stops the bleeding. If it came from outside — a storm surge, a backed-up storm drain, or a flooded yard — you may not be able to stop it, but you can redirect your energy to protecting what's inside.

Before you wade in, check two things:

- **Electricity.** If water has reached outlets, baseboards, or your electrical panel, do not enter the room. Turn off the breaker for that zone at the panel — from a dry location — or call your utility company. Standing water and live circuits are a fatal combination.
- **Sewage.** If the water is gray or black, smells foul, or came from a toilet backup or floor drain, treat it as contaminated. Gloves and rubber boots minimum; ideally, stay out until a professional assesses it.

Once you've confirmed the space is safe to enter, open windows and interior doors to start airflow. If outdoor humidity is lower than indoor humidity — common in Sacramento's dry summer months — this helps. In winter or during a storm, keep windows closed and run your HVAC fan only if the system itself wasn't flooded.

## Step 2: Document Everything Before You Touch It

Your insurance claim lives and dies on documentation. Before you move a single piece of furniture or pull up a rug, photograph and video every affected surface from multiple angles. Capture:

- Standing water depth (hold a ruler or your hand in frame for scale)
- Waterlines on walls, cabinets, and baseboards
- Damaged personal property in place, before it's moved
- The source of the water, if visible (the cracked pipe, the failed valve, the point of entry)

This takes ten minutes and can save you thousands in disputed claims. Most insurance carriers require evidence of the original damage, not just the aftermath.

After photographing, note the time and date. Water migration is time-stamped — adjusters and restoration professionals use moisture readings over time to understand how far damage traveled, and your documentation anchors that timeline.

## Step 3: Remove Water and Wet Materials — Carefully

Speed matters here. Every hour that porous materials stay saturated, the harder they are to dry and the more likely secondary damage becomes.

**What you can do yourself:**

- Use a wet/dry shop vac to pull standing water from hard floors. Empty it frequently.
- Pull up area rugs and move them outside or to a dry garage. Wet rugs trap moisture against subfloors and are almost never worth saving once fully saturated.
- Blot (don't rub) water from upholstered furniture. Move pieces off wet flooring if you can do so without spreading contaminated water.
- Place aluminum foil or wood blocks under furniture legs sitting on wet carpet to prevent rust stains and tannin bleed from wood finishes.
- Run box fans across wet surfaces and point them toward open doors or windows.

**What requires professional equipment:**

Carpet padding is a sponge. Even if the carpet surface feels almost dry after vacuuming, the padding beneath it holds many times its weight in water. Without a truck-mounted extractor or commercial drying equipment, you will not get it dry in time. The same is true for water that has migrated behind drywall or under hardwood flooring — the surface can read dry to the touch while the subfloor or wall cavity stays saturated for days.

Professional water damage restoration uses thermal imaging cameras and calibrated moisture meters to find water you cannot see or feel. That's not a sales pitch — it's the reason DIY drying so often leads to mold showing up six weeks later in a wall you thought was fine.

## What NOT to Do in the First 24 Hours

Some common instincts make things worse:

- **Don't use a household vacuum on standing water.** Standard vacuums are not rated for liquids and can electrocute you or destroy the motor.
- **Don't run your central HVAC to dry things out** if the system's air handler, ducts, or drain pan were affected. You risk spreading contaminated air or mold spores through the whole house.
- **Don't pull up hardwood flooring immediately.** Solid hardwood can sometimes be dried in place with the right equipment. Ripping it up before a professional assesses it may void your claim or destroy flooring that could have been saved.
- **Don't use gas-powered equipment indoors.** Generators and gas-powered pumps produce carbon monoxide. Run them outside only.
- **Don't wait to call your insurance carrier.** Most policies require prompt notification. Waiting days to report a loss can complicate or reduce your claim, even if the damage itself happened suddenly.

## When to Call a Water Damage Professional

Call a restoration company the same day if any of the following are true:

- The affected area is larger than roughly 10 square feet of flooring, or water has reached more than one room
- Water has been present for more than a few hours before you discovered it
- The water source was a toilet, floor drain, dishwasher, or anything that may carry bacteria
- You can smell a musty or sewage odor
- Water has contacted drywall, insulation, or wood framing
- You have a crawl space and water may have reached it

Sacramento's older housing stock — particularly bungalows and ranch homes built before the 1970s — often has plaster walls, older subfloor assemblies, and cast-iron or galvanized plumbing that behaves differently than modern construction under water intrusion. A restoration professional familiar with the region's housing will assess those materials differently than a generic checklist would.

If you're unsure whether your situation qualifies, call anyway. A brief phone assessment costs nothing and gives you a clearer picture of what you're dealing with.

## What Happens After the First 24 Hours

If professional drying equipment is deployed quickly, most residential water losses dry within three to five days — though that timeline depends on the materials affected, the volume of water, and ambient conditions. During that window, expect:

- Industrial air movers and dehumidifiers running continuously. They are loud and they generate heat.
- Daily moisture readings logged by the technician to track drying progress.
- Possible removal of baseboards or small sections of drywall to allow wall cavities to dry — a process called flood cuts. This sounds alarming but is standard and far less expensive than replacing mold-damaged framing later.

Once materials reach their drying targets, equipment is removed and the documentation package — moisture logs, photos, equipment records — goes to your insurance carrier. Reconstruction of removed materials follows.

The faster the first 24 hours go, the shorter and less expensive the rest of the process tends to be.
