Davis sits in the heart of the Sacramento Valley, where summer temperatures regularly push past 100°F and the occasional winter storm can send water through aging roofs and windows faster than most homeowners expect. When smoke, soot, or floodwater reaches the contents of a Davis home — the furniture, artwork, documents, clothing, and keepsakes that make a house livable — the heat and humidity that follow a loss event can accelerate damage within hours. Contents restoration and storage is the service that pulls those belongings out of harm's way, cleans and treats them off-site, and returns them in documented, pre-loss condition once your home is ready.

## Why Davis Properties See Contents Damage Differently

Davis is a university town, and its housing stock reflects that. Older craftsman bungalows and mid-century ranch homes built before modern vapor barriers were standard sit alongside dense apartment clusters near the UC Davis campus. In the older single-family homes — many of them wood-framed with original hardwood floors and plaster-and-lath walls — smoke and soot penetrate porous surfaces more deeply than they do in newer construction. Upholstered furniture and area rugs absorb odor compounds quickly in enclosed rooms, and the valley's dry summer heat can bake those compounds into fabric fibers if pack-out is delayed even a day or two.

Water losses carry a similar urgency. The Sacramento Valley's clay-heavy soils drain slowly, and a backed-up sewer line or a washing machine overflow can leave standing water on flooring long enough to wick into baseboards and the lower inches of furniture legs. Contents that look undamaged on the surface — a solid-wood dresser, a bookcase — can be holding moisture that will warp or crack them within a week if they stay in a humid, closed room.

## Our Contents Pack-Out and Restoration Process

The process begins with a room-by-room inventory before a single item leaves your home. Every piece is photographed, described, and logged so that you — and your insurance adjuster — have a complete record of what was removed and its pre-loss condition. Items are packed using materials rated for fragile and high-value goods, loaded carefully, and transported to a secure, climate-controlled facility.

At the facility, each category of content gets the treatment its material requires:

- **Textiles and soft goods** — clothing, bedding, curtains — go through ozone or hydroxyl treatment to neutralize smoke odor, then specialized laundering or dry-cleaning.
- **Hard surfaces** — wood furniture, cabinetry, electronics housings — are cleaned with ultrasonic equipment or hand-wiped with appropriate solvents depending on the finish and contamination type.
- **Documents and photographs** — freeze-drying is used when water damage is involved; smoke-affected paper is cleaned in a low-humidity environment to prevent further brittleness.
- **Electronics** — sent to a certified electronics restoration partner for internal cleaning and testing before any item is declared restored.

Once your home has been dried, rebuilt, or cleared, contents are returned on a scheduled delivery, re-inventoried at the door, and placed back in the rooms they came from.

## Davis Insurance Coordination

Most homeowner policies cover contents restoration as part of a fire, smoke, or water loss claim, but the documentation requirements vary by carrier. The room-by-room inventory and photo log created at pack-out becomes the foundation of your contents claim — it gives your adjuster itemized evidence rather than a general estimate. TDI® USA works directly with insurance carriers and can communicate with your adjuster throughout the restoration process so you are not translating between two technical conversations at once.

## Local Note

Davis homes built in the 1940s through the 1960s — a common era in the neighborhoods west of downtown and along the older residential streets near the university — often have original wood-framed windows with single-pane glass. During a fire event, those windows can fail early, allowing smoke to travel through the house in unexpected directions and deposit soot in rooms that were not near the fire's origin. When we assess contents in homes of that era, we check secondary rooms and closets carefully, because smoke migration in older, less airtight construction is rarely limited to the obvious areas.
