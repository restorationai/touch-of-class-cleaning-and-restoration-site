## Why Folsom Crawl Spaces Are Particularly Vulnerable

Folsom's geography does most of the work against unprotected crawl spaces. The city's older neighborhoods — many built during the rapid expansion of the 1980s and 1990s on graded hillside lots — often have shallow crawl spaces with limited airflow. Expansive clay soils common throughout the foothills region retain moisture long after rain stops, keeping ground-level humidity elevated well into spring. Homes closer to Lake Natoma and the American River corridor can see even higher ambient moisture levels during the rainy season, as the river influence extends further than most homeowners expect.

Vented crawl spaces, which were standard practice under California building codes for decades, rely on cross-ventilation to control moisture. In Folsom's climate, that strategy breaks down during the humid shoulder seasons — late October through March — when outdoor air carries enough moisture that venting actually introduces more water vapor than it removes. The result is condensation on joists, insulation that absorbs and holds moisture, and a microclimate under the floor that rarely fully dries out.

## Our Crawl Space Encapsulation Process in Folsom

Encapsulation is not a single product — it is a system, and the components have to be matched to what is actually happening in the crawl space before any material goes down.

The process begins with a thorough inspection: moisture readings at multiple points across the subfloor framing, a check for existing mold or wood rot, assessment of existing vents and their condition, and documentation of any standing water or drainage issues. In Folsom homes where the crawl space grade slopes toward the foundation — common on hillside lots — we identify low points where water pools before we seal anything.

Once the space is prepped and any remediation needs addressed, we install a heavy-duty vapor barrier across the entire crawl space floor, running it up the foundation walls and sealing it at the perimeter. Seams are overlapped and taped; penetrations for plumbing and HVAC are detailed individually. Depending on the space's existing ventilation configuration and the homeowner's goals, we may recommend closing existing foundation vents and conditioning the space, which aligns with current California energy code direction for new construction and is increasingly common in retrofit work.

The system is completed with a dehumidifier sized to the actual cubic footage of the space — not a residential unit from a hardware store, but commercial-grade equipment calibrated to maintain relative humidity below the threshold where mold growth becomes a risk.
