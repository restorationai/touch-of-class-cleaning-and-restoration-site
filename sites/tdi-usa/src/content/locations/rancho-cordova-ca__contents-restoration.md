## Why Rancho Cordova Properties Face Distinct Contents Damage Challenges

Much of Rancho Cordova's residential housing stock dates from the postwar boom of the 1950s through the 1970s, when tract builders used materials — particleboard cabinetry, older insulation batts, single-pane windows — that absorb smoke odor and moisture far more aggressively than modern composites. A house fire that looks contained to one room can leave a fine soot film on upholstered furniture two rooms away within hours, because the HVAC system circulates smoke particles throughout the ductwork before anyone thinks to shut it off.

Rancho Cordova also experiences the Sacramento Valley's notorious "Tule fog" season from late November through February. When a loss event happens during that window — a roof leak from a winter storm, a pipe that freezes during an overnight cold snap — the ambient humidity outside can actually slow the drying of contents that have been moved to a garage or carport for temporary staging. Professional climate-controlled storage eliminates that variable entirely.

## Our Contents Restoration and Storage Process in Rancho Cordova

Every pack-out starts with a room-by-room photo inventory before a single item is moved. Each piece is tagged, photographed, and logged against a digital manifest — the kind of documentation that Sacramento-area insurance adjusters expect to see when a claim includes personal property losses.

From there, contents move into our controlled cleaning and restoration workflow:

- **Ultrasonic cleaning** for hard goods, collectibles, and non-porous items — effective on smoke residue that hand-wiping cannot reach in engraved surfaces or tight joints
- **Ozone and hydroxyl treatment** for soft goods, upholstered furniture, and clothing with embedded smoke or mold odor
- **Document and photo drying** using freeze-drying or air-drying protocols depending on saturation level
- **Climate-controlled storage** in a secure facility while your Rancho Cordova home undergoes structural repairs

Items are returned only after the structure passes drying validation — not before, because reintroducing cleaned contents into a still-elevated-humidity environment undoes the restoration work.
