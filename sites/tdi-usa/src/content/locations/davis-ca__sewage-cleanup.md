## Why Davis Properties See Sewage Backup Issues

Davis's clay-dominant soil profile is a significant factor. Clay expands when wet and contracts when dry, and the Central Valley's swing between wet winters and baked summers puts constant lateral pressure on buried sewer laterals. Over time, that movement cracks older clay or cast-iron pipe sections, creating entry points for root intrusion — especially from the mature street trees that line many of Davis's older residential blocks. When roots partially obstruct a line, any surge in flow (a heavy rain event, a sudden increase in household use) can push sewage back through the lowest fixture in the house.

The city's significant stock of mid-century rental housing — much of it built to serve UC Davis's growing student population from the 1960s through the 1980s — adds another layer of risk. These properties often have original sewer laterals that have never been lined or replaced, and the high-turnover nature of student rentals means slow drains go unreported until a full backup occurs. Crawl space foundations common in that era allow sewage to pool under the structure, soaking into wood framing and insulation before anyone realizes the extent of the problem.

## Our Sewage Cleanup and Sanitization Process in Davis

Sewage is classified as Category 3 water — the most contaminated category — which means standard water extraction protocols are not enough. Every sewage job follows a structured containment-and-removal sequence before any drying or sanitization begins.

**Containment first.** We establish a work perimeter to prevent cross-contamination to unaffected rooms. In homes with open floor plans or shared HVAC systems, this step is especially important.

**Extraction and removal.** Standing sewage and saturated materials — soaked insulation, compromised drywall, subfloor sections that cannot be adequately sanitized — are removed and bagged for proper disposal per California Department of Public Health guidelines.

**Structural cleaning and disinfection.** All affected surfaces are treated with EPA-registered disinfectants. In crawl space losses, this includes the underside of the subfloor decking, floor joists, and any vapor barrier that was compromised.

**Drying and monitoring.** Once the contamination is cleared, we deploy desiccant or refrigerant dehumidifiers and air movers calibrated to the structure's material composition. Our IICRC-certified technicians monitor moisture readings daily until the structure reaches dry standard per the IICRC S500.

**Documentation throughout.** Every step is photographed and logged — moisture readings, material removal, disinfection application — which matters when you're filing an insurance claim.
