## Why Citrus Heights Homes Present Distinct Renovation Challenges

The Sacramento Valley's climate does real work on residential structures over time. Summers push temperatures well past 100°F, causing wood framing and trim to expand and contract in cycles that loosen joints, crack stucco, and warp cabinetry. Winter rains arrive fast and heavy, and Citrus Heights — sitting on relatively flat ground with clay-heavy soils common throughout Sacramento County — can see drainage back up against foundations and into crawl spaces before a homeowner notices anything wrong.

Homes built in the 1960s and 1970s, which make up a significant share of the city's housing stock, frequently have original galvanized plumbing, aluminum wiring in some circuits, and single-pane windows that no longer meet current energy standards. A kitchen or bathroom remodel in one of these homes often reveals conditions behind the walls that need to be addressed before finish work can begin — outdated supply lines, undersized drain stacks, or subfloor damage from a slow leak that predates the current owner. Scoping that work honestly at the start is what separates a smooth remodel from one that stalls mid-project.

Sacramento County building permits are required for most structural, electrical, plumbing, and mechanical work. Citrus Heights, as an incorporated city, runs its own permitting and inspection process through the Community Development Department. Knowing which projects trigger a full permit versus a simple over-the-counter approval — and how to sequence inspections so a remodel doesn't sit idle waiting for a sign-off — is part of what a local general contractor brings to a job.

## Local Note

Homes in the older sections of Citrus Heights — particularly those built in the late 1950s through early 1970s — frequently have stucco exteriors applied directly over wood sheathing without a modern moisture barrier. Any renovation touching an exterior wall in these homes is worth probing before assuming the substrate is sound.
