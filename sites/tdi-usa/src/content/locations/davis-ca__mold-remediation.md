## Why Davis Properties See Mold Issues

The Sacramento Valley's Mediterranean climate means Davis endures a long dry season followed by a compressed wet season — and that compression matters. When heavy rain arrives in November or December, it hits soil that has been baked and compacted all summer. Water sheds off the surface and finds the path of least resistance: foundation cracks, aging crawl space vents, and the gap between a slab and a door threshold. Older homes throughout Davis — particularly the ranch-style and split-level construction common in neighborhoods built out during the university's expansion decades — frequently have pier-and-beam or raised foundations with minimal vapor barriers. Moisture that pools under the floor doesn't announce itself. It quietly feeds mold colonies on the subfloor sheathing and floor joists for weeks before a musty smell drifts up through the hardwood.

The university's influence on the rental market also matters. High tenant turnover means deferred maintenance is common: a slow drip under a bathroom vanity gets reported late, a window seal fails and goes unpatched through a rainy season, and by the time a landlord schedules a look, the wall cavity behind the tile is already colonized. Mold doesn't care whether a property is owner-occupied or rented — but the timeline from first moisture to visible growth is the same either way.

## Our Mold Remediation Process in Davis

Every job starts with a thorough inspection — not a visual scan, but moisture mapping with calibrated meters and thermal imaging to find wet material that looks dry at the surface. Mold growth is always a moisture problem first; removing visible colonies without eliminating the water source guarantees a return visit.

Once the source is identified and stopped, the affected area is sealed under negative air pressure using 6-mil poly containment barriers and HEPA-filtered air scrubbers. This keeps spores from migrating to unaffected rooms during removal. Contaminated porous materials — drywall, insulation, subfloor sections — are removed, bagged, and disposed of per California Department of Public Health guidelines. Non-porous surfaces are HEPA-vacuumed and treated with an EPA-registered antimicrobial agent.

After remediation, the space is dried to pre-loss moisture levels using desiccant or refrigerant dehumidifiers and air movers sized to the square footage. We don't sign off on a job until clearance readings confirm that airborne spore counts and surface moisture are within normal range. For Davis rentals subject to California's implied warranty of habitability, that documented clearance matters — it's the paper trail a landlord or property manager needs.

Davis's older rental housing stock — much of it built quickly to accommodate UC Davis enrollment growth in the late 1960s and 1970s — frequently used T1-11 plywood siding and minimal flashing details around window rough openings.
