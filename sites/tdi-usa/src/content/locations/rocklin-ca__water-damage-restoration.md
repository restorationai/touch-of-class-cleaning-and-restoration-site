## Why Rocklin Properties See Water Damage Issues

Rocklin's residential growth accelerated significantly through the 1990s and 2000s, which means a large share of the housing stock is now 20–30 years old — old enough for original supply lines, washing machine hoses, and water heaters to be approaching or past their expected service life. Slab-on-grade construction is common throughout the city, and a slow leak beneath a slab can saturate the concrete and wick moisture up through flooring for weeks before a homeowner notices warped hardwood or a musty smell in a back bedroom.

The clay-heavy soils common across Placer County also play a role. During heavy winter rain events, clay soil saturates quickly and drains slowly, which increases hydrostatic pressure against foundation walls and can drive groundwater into crawl spaces and lower-level living areas. Homes built on lots with poor grading — a frequent finding in older subdivisions that predate current drainage standards — are especially vulnerable during multi-day storm systems.

## Our Water Damage Restoration Process in Rocklin

Every job starts with a thorough moisture assessment using thermal imaging cameras and calibrated moisture meters. Water that looks contained at the surface is often not — it travels along wall cavities, under baseboards, and into subfloor assemblies before it shows itself. Knowing where moisture actually is, not just where it's visible, determines how many drying zones we set up and how long the equipment stays on-site.

From there, the process moves through extraction, evaporation, and dehumidification in sequence:

- **Extraction**: Truck-mounted and portable extractors remove standing water and pull moisture from carpet, pad, and porous flooring materials.
- **Structural drying**: High-velocity air movers are positioned to create airflow across wet surfaces; commercial-grade desiccant or refrigerant dehumidifiers remove the evaporated moisture from the air. Drying is documented daily and follows IICRC S500 standards.
- **Monitoring and clearance**: Moisture readings are logged at each visit. Equipment is not removed until affected materials reach manufacturer-specified dry standards — not just when things feel dry to the touch.

If building materials cannot be dried in place — saturated drywall, compromised insulation, swollen cabinetry — controlled demolition and removal happen before drying begins, not after. Trying to dry materials that will not dry is one of the most common reasons mold colonizes a structure within days of a water loss.

## Local Note

Rocklin's newer planned communities — many built in the late 1990s and early 2000s — frequently used engineered wood subfloor panels rather than traditional plywood. Oriented strand board (OSB) subfloor swells and delaminates faster than plywood when wet, and it does not dry as predictably. When moisture meters show saturation in an OSB subfloor, the drying timeline needs to be assessed conservatively; surface readings can normalize while the core of the panel remains wet. Catching that early prevents a floor replacement from turning into a mold remediation job on top of it.
