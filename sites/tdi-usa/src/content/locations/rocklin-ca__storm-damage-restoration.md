## Why Rocklin Properties Are Vulnerable to Storm Damage

Rocklin's geology plays a bigger role in storm outcomes than most homeowners realize. The Rocklin area sits on decomposed granite and clay-loam soils that become nearly impermeable when saturated. During a heavy atmospheric river event, surface water has nowhere to go quickly — it pools against foundations, backs up through area drains, and finds its way under slab edges and into crawl spaces. Homes built in the 1980s and 1990s subdivisions, many of which predate modern grading and drainage code revisions, are particularly susceptible because their lot drainage was designed for typical Sacramento Valley rainfall patterns, not the heavier events that have become more common in recent years.

Wind damage compounds the picture. The oak and pine trees that give Rocklin neighborhoods their character are also a liability in a sustained windstorm. A mature valley oak with a root system compromised by years of drought stress can topple without warning, punching through a roof deck and exposing the interior to whatever rain follows. Even a partial branch strike can displace flashing, crack ridge tiles, or split a gutter system in a way that channels water directly into the wall cavity — damage that reads as minor from the street but is soaking insulation and framing within hours.

## Our Storm Damage Restoration Process in Rocklin

When we arrive, the first priority is making the structure weather-tight. That means emergency tarping of any breached roof sections, boarding compromised windows or doors, and securing debris before it causes additional damage to the property or adjacent structures. We document everything photographically before anything is moved — a step that matters enormously when an insurance claim is in play.

Once the structure is stabilized, we shift to moisture mapping. Rocklin's stucco-clad homes — common throughout the area — are particularly deceptive after a storm because the exterior surface can appear intact while water has migrated behind the lath and into the wall assembly. We use thermal imaging cameras and calibrated moisture meters to trace the actual path water traveled, not just the visible wet spots. Structural drying follows IICRC S500 standards, with industrial air movers and desiccant or refrigerant dehumidifiers sized to the affected area. We monitor drying progress on a documented schedule until materials reach target moisture content.

Tree removal coordination, debris hauling, and temporary fencing are handled as part of the project scope when needed — homeowners shouldn't have to manage three separate contractors in the middle of a loss.
