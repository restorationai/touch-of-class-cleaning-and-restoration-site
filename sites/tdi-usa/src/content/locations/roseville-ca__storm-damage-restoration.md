## Why Roseville Properties See Storm Damage

The Sacramento Valley's "Pineapple Express" storms are the main culprit. These atmospheric river events arrive from the Pacific with warm, moisture-heavy air and can produce sustained rainfall that overwhelms gutters, undermines foundations, and drives water through roof penetrations that performed fine in lighter rain. Roseville's clay-heavy soils — common throughout Placer County — don't percolate quickly, so surface water pools against foundations and finds its way into crawl spaces and garages before drainage systems can catch up.

Wind damage follows a different pattern. The gap between the Coast Range and the Sierra acts like a funnel, and Roseville sits squarely in the path of the outflow. Mature valley oaks and ornamental trees planted in older subdivisions are especially vulnerable after a wet winter: waterlogged root zones lose their grip, and a wind event that would barely register in summer can drop a 60-foot tree onto a roof or fence line. Newer HOA communities on the western and northern edges of the city often have shared landscaping agreements that complicate debris removal — knowing who is responsible for a downed tree on a common-area easement matters before any crew starts cutting.

## Our Storm Damage Restoration Process in Roseville

Storm damage rarely presents as one clean problem. A single event can leave you with a breached roof, wind-driven rain inside wall cavities, saturated insulation in the attic, and standing water in the garage — all at the same time. The sequence in which those issues are addressed matters.

The first step is stabilization: tarping or boarding any opening in the building envelope to stop additional water intrusion. Until the structure is weathertight, drying equipment is fighting a losing battle. Once the exterior is secured, moisture mapping with thermal imaging and calibrated meters identifies where water has traveled — often far beyond the visible wet area. Water that enters through a roof penetration can track along roof sheathing, wick into wall framing, and show up as elevated moisture readings two rooms away from where the ceiling stained.

Structural drying follows IICRC S500 and S520 standards, using industrial air movers and desiccant or refrigerant dehumidifiers sized to the affected volume. Roseville's relatively low ambient humidity in the post-storm window (once the storm system clears) actually assists drying — conditions that would extend a timeline in a coastal market can be leveraged here to hit drying goals faster. Throughout the process, daily moisture readings are logged and documented, which becomes the foundation of the insurance claim file.
