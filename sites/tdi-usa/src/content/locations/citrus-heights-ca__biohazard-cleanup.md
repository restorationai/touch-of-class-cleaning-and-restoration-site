## Why Citrus Heights Properties Present Unique Biohazard Challenges

Much of Citrus Heights was built during the post-war suburban expansion of the 1950s through 1970s. That era of construction often means original hardwood subfloors, plaster-and-lath interior walls, and crawl spaces with limited ventilation — all surfaces that absorb and retain biological material differently than modern drywall and concrete slab. Porous materials hold contaminants below the visible surface, which means a cleanup that looks complete at a glance may still harbor infectious material at depth.

The region's hot, dry summers also mean that biological matter can desiccate quickly on hard surfaces, making it appear contained while microscopic hazards remain. Conversely, Citrus Heights' winter wet season — with fog and rain pushing indoor humidity higher — can slow surface drying and create secondary concerns if remediation isn't thorough. Neither condition is forgiving of a partial response.

## Our Biohazard Cleanup Process in Citrus Heights

Every situation is different, and the first thing our OSHA-trained, IICRC-certified team does on arrival is assess — not assume. We identify the affected materials, establish containment to protect unaffected areas of the property, and work systematically through removal, cleaning, and treatment using EPA-registered disinfectants rated for the specific biological hazard present.

For properties with older construction — common across Citrus Heights — we pay particular attention to subfloor assemblies and wall cavities where material may have migrated beyond the visible impact zone. Structural components that cannot be adequately decontaminated are documented and removed rather than left in place. The goal is a property that is genuinely safe, not one that simply looks addressed.

Throughout the process, we work discreetly. Unmarked vehicles, no unnecessary foot traffic, and communication routed through whoever the property owner or manager designates — because the people involved in these situations deserve privacy, not a spectacle.
