## Why Davis Properties See Water Cleanup Issues

The Yolo County clay-loam soil that gives Davis its agricultural identity also creates drainage problems that residential lots were never fully engineered around. During the wet season — roughly November through March — saturated ground has nowhere to push water except toward foundation perimeters and crawl spaces. Older homes built before the city's major growth periods of the 1970s and 1980s often have minimal vapor barriers and original cast-iron or galvanized supply lines that corrode from the inside out, failing quietly until a ceiling stain or warped hardwood floor signals the damage already done.

## Our Water Cleanup Process in Davis

The first thing a crew does on arrival is locate the moisture boundary — not just the visible wet area, but the full perimeter of saturation using thermal imaging cameras and calibrated moisture meters. Water in Davis homes frequently travels farther than it appears to: a dishwasher leak that looks contained to the kitchen can wick under the toe-kick, through the subfloor, and into the adjacent dining room wall cavity before the surface shows any sign.

Once the boundary is mapped, standing water is extracted with truck-mounted or portable extraction units depending on access. Saturated materials — carpet, pad, drywall, insulation — are assessed for salvageability. Where materials can be dried in place, the crew sets a drying system calibrated to the structure: refrigerant or desiccant dehumidifiers paired with air movers positioned to create directed airflow through the wet assembly. Moisture readings are logged at each visit so the drying curve is documented — data your insurance adjuster will need and that confirms work was completed to the IICRC S500 standard rather than stopped early.

For slab-on-grade homes, which are common throughout Davis, drying beneath flooring requires either floor mat systems that draw moisture up through the slab or controlled demolition of the finish floor to expose the concrete. The right call depends on the flooring type, the moisture reading at the slab surface, and how long the water has been present.

## Local Note

Davis homes built in the 1950s and 1960s — particularly in the older neighborhoods west of downtown — frequently used lath-and-plaster wall construction rather than modern drywall. Plaster absorbs water more slowly than drywall, which means it can look and feel dry at the surface while the wood lath behind it remains saturated. Moisture meters read differently on plaster than on gypsum board, and crews unfamiliar with the material can pull equipment too early and leave a hidden moisture reservoir that feeds mold growth within a week. Knowing what you're reading — and what the meter is actually measuring — matters in a city with this much pre-1970 housing stock.
