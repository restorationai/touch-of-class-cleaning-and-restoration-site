## Why Rancho Cordova Properties Face Distinct Board-Up and Tarping Challenges

Rancho Cordova's building stock spans several distinct eras. Older residential neighborhoods developed in the 1950s and 1960s feature wood-frame construction with single-pane windows and original wood fascia — materials that are faster to breach and slower to reseal cleanly than modern construction. Newer subdivisions built during the 1990s and 2000s growth boom often have tile roofs, which complicate emergency tarping: a tarp anchored improperly over concrete or clay tile can crack additional tiles and expand the claim.

The region's climate adds another layer. Rancho Cordova's dry summers mean that an unprotected roof opening can bake and warp exposed sheathing within days. When the rainy season arrives — typically November through March — even a modest gap in a roofline can admit enough water to saturate insulation and ceiling assemblies quickly. Properties that sit unprotected between seasons often arrive at restoration with compounded damage that a same-day board-up would have prevented.

## Our Emergency Board-Up and Tarping Process in Rancho Cordova

When a call comes in, a crew is dispatched immediately — 24 hours a day, seven days a week. On arrival, the first priority is a rapid exterior assessment: identifying every compromised opening, measuring spans accurately, and checking structural stability before anyone works at elevation.

For window and door board-ups, we use minimum ⅝-inch plywood cut to fit flush with the frame, secured with structural screws rather than nails so the panel can be removed cleanly when permanent repairs begin. For fire-damaged openings where framing is compromised, we add header bracing before installing the panel.

Roof tarping on Rancho Cordova's tile roofs requires a different approach than a standard composition shingle job. We use ballast boards and non-penetrating anchor systems wherever possible to avoid cracking intact tile sections. The tarp is lapped at least four feet past the breach on all sides and weighted at the ridge and eaves — not simply stapled at the edges, which fails in the region's Diablo wind events that occasionally push through the Sacramento Valley in fall.

Every opening is photographed before and after boarding, with measurements documented for the insurance adjuster.
