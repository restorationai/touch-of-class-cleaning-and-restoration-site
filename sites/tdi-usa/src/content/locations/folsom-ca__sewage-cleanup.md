## Why Folsom Properties Experience Sewage Backups

Folsom's growth over the past three decades has layered new construction on top of older infrastructure. The city's established western corridors contain homes built in the 1980s and early 1990s — an era when cast-iron and Orangeburg sewer laterals were still common. These materials corrode, collapse, and root-intrude at predictable rates, and by the time a homeowner notices slow drains, the lateral may already be partially obstructed. One hard rain — the kind that rolls off Folsom's clay-dense hillsides and surges the municipal system — can be enough to push sewage back through floor drains, toilets, and washing machine standpipes simultaneously.

Properties on the eastern edge of the city that rely on private septic systems face a different but related problem. The foothill soils drain unevenly, and a wet winter can saturate a drain field faster than the system can process effluent. Septic overflow cleanup requires different containment protocols than municipal sewer backup work, and the regulatory reporting obligations differ as well — something crews unfamiliar with El Dorado and Sacramento County overlap zones sometimes miss.

## Our Sewage Cleanup and Sanitization Process in Folsom

Sewage contamination is classified as Category 3 water — the most hazardous type — regardless of whether it originates from a municipal sewer backup or a septic overflow. The process we follow is calibrated to that reality, not to the appearance of the damage.

**Containment and PPE deployment** come first. Crew members arrive in full personal protective equipment, and affected areas are isolated to prevent cross-contamination to clean zones of the home.

**Extraction and removal** follows. Standing sewage is extracted using truck-mounted and portable units. Porous materials — carpet, pad, drywall below the flood line, saturated insulation — are removed and bagged for disposal per California Department of Public Health guidelines for Category 3 waste.

**Structural cleaning and antimicrobial treatment** addresses what extraction leaves behind. Hard surfaces, framing lumber, and concrete are cleaned, then treated with EPA-registered disinfectants formulated for sewage-contaminated environments. This step is not optional or cosmetic — it is what separates sanitization from simple drying.

**Drying and monitoring** uses calibrated air movers and dehumidifiers, with moisture readings logged against the IICRC S500 standard until structural materials return to acceptable baselines.

**Clearance documentation** is provided before the job closes — a record of pre- and post-treatment moisture readings and the sanitization steps performed, which your insurance adjuster and any future buyer's inspector can review.
