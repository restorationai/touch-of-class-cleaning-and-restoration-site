## Why West Sacramento Properties Face Distinct Board-Up and Tarping Challenges

The city straddles Yolo County and carries a mixed housing stock that reflects its history as an industrial and working-class counterpart to Sacramento. Older single-family homes — many built in the postwar boom of the late 1940s through the 1960s — often have wood-frame construction with original single-pane windows and aging rooflines. When fire scorches a rafter or a break-in shatters one of those windows, the opening is rarely a clean rectangle. Irregular framing, settled foundations, and non-standard window dimensions common in that era make a precise, weather-tight board-up more involved than slapping plywood over a modern vinyl frame.

The Sacramento Valley also experiences the "Tule fog" season from late fall through early spring — dense ground fog that brings sustained dampness even without rain. An unsecured roof opening during a Tule fog event can allow moisture infiltration that begins softening OSB decking and insulation within hours. Tarping in those conditions requires overlap margins and ballast weighting that go beyond a fair-weather installation.

The Broderick and Bryte neighborhoods along the river — lower-lying areas of West Sacramento — also sit in a floodplain-adjacent zone where soil saturation during wet years can compound structural stress on already-compromised buildings. A fire-damaged or storm-struck structure in those areas needs a board-up that accounts for potential ground-level water intrusion, not just the breach at the point of damage.

## Our Emergency Board-Up and Tarping Process in West Sacramento

When a call comes in, the process starts before the crew leaves the yard. The team confirms the loss type — fire, storm, vandalism, vehicle impact — because each dictates different materials and sequencing. A fire damage board-up requires assessing structural stability before anyone climbs a ladder or cuts plywood; smoke and heat can compromise rafters that look intact from the ground.

On arrival, the crew does a rapid exterior and interior walk to identify every breach: broken windows, kicked-in doors, roof penetrations, and any compromised soffits or vents. Measurements are taken for each opening. Plywood panels are cut to fit and secured with structural screws into solid framing — not just into drywall or stucco — so the board-up holds against wind gusts and resists forced re-entry. For roof damage, heavy-duty polyethylene tarps (6-mil or heavier, depending on the exposure) are laid with a minimum 12-inch overlap at ridges and sealed at edges with weighted battens or cap strips to prevent wind lift.

Documentation runs parallel to the physical work: photographs of each breach before and after boarding, measurements, and a written scope that your insurance adjuster can use directly.
