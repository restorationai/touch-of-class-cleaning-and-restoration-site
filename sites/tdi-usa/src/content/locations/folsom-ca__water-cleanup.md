## Why Folsom Properties See Water Damage Differently

Folsom's building stock spans several distinct eras. The older neighborhoods clustered near Sutter Street and the historic core were built with materials and methods that predate modern moisture barriers — crawl spaces with minimal vapor control, plaster-over-lath walls, and galvanized supply lines that corrode from the inside out. When those pipes fail, water doesn't just pool on a surface; it wicks into framing and plaster before it ever reaches the floor.

The newer master-planned communities that expanded south and east of the historic core present a different challenge. Homes built in the 2000s and 2010s often use engineered lumber, OSB sheathing, and composite flooring — materials that are structurally efficient but absorb water fast and delaminate under sustained moisture. HOA-governed communities in these areas also add a coordination layer: exterior work, equipment staging, and dumpster placement may require advance notice or board approval, which affects how quickly certain phases of a job can proceed.

Folsom's proximity to Folsom Lake and the American River also means that during significant rain events, storm drainage systems in lower-lying subdivisions can back up, pushing water into garages and finished basements. That's not a plumbing failure — it's a hydrostatic and drainage issue, and it requires a different extraction and drying strategy than a burst supply line does.

## Our Water Cleanup Process in Folsom

Every water loss is different, but the sequence of decisions that determines whether a structure dries correctly is consistent. When TDI® USA arrives at a Folsom property, the first step is a moisture mapping survey — thermal imaging and calibrated moisture meters to locate water that has migrated beyond the visible wet zone. Water behind drywall or under flooring often reads dry at the surface while the framing behind it remains saturated.

Once the affected area is fully mapped, standing water is extracted using truck-mounted and portable units. Folsom's climate works in our favor during the drier months — low ambient humidity accelerates evaporation when combined with industrial air movers and desiccant or refrigerant dehumidifiers. In winter months, when outdoor humidity climbs and interior conditions are less predictable, we adjust equipment placement and monitor daily to keep drying on schedule per the IICRC S500 standard.

Materials that cannot be dried in place — saturated insulation, buckled flooring, compromised drywall — are documented photographically and removed before they become a mold substrate. In Folsom's warmer months, mold colonization can begin within 24 to 48 hours of a water event, which is why the extraction and demo phases are treated as time-critical, not optional.
