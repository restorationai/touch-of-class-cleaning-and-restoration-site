## Why Rancho Cordova Properties See Storm Damage Differently

The Sacramento Valley's Mediterranean climate means most of the annual rainfall arrives in compressed windows — late fall through early spring — after months of dry conditions that leave soil hard and slow to absorb water. When a heavy storm hits, runoff moves fast across compacted ground before it can percolate, and that surface water finds its way into garages, crawl spaces, and low-lying slab foundations before a homeowner has time to react.

Rancho Cordova's housing stock adds another layer of complexity. Neighborhoods developed in the 1950s through 1970s often feature wood-frame construction with composition shingle roofs that are now at or past their rated lifespan. A single storm event — wind-lifted shingles, a snapped valley oak branch, a clogged gutter that sends a sheet of water behind the fascia — can open a roof to water intrusion that spreads inside wall assemblies faster than the surface damage suggests. Newer master-planned sections built in the 1990s and 2000s tend to have tile roofs, but those come with their own vulnerability: cracked or displaced tiles after high-wind events create small openings that are easy to miss in a post-storm visual inspection.

## Our Storm Damage Restoration Process in Rancho Cordova

When we arrive, the first priority is stopping active water entry and making the structure safe — tarping compromised roof sections, boarding broken windows, and clearing debris that poses a slip or structural hazard. That stabilization work happens before any restoration discussion, because every hour of open exposure adds moisture load to materials that are already stressed.

Once the structure is secured, we move into damage mapping. Moisture meters and thermal imaging cameras show us what the eye can't: water that has traveled laterally through wall cavities, saturated insulation behind intact drywall, or wet sheathing under a roof deck that still looks solid from below. We document everything photographically and in writing — not because it's paperwork, but because your insurance adjuster needs that evidence to process the claim accurately.

Drying follows the IICRC S500 and S520 standards, which means we're not guessing at drying times — we're measuring ambient conditions, material moisture content, and airflow daily and adjusting equipment placement until readings confirm the structure has returned to pre-loss moisture levels. Structural repairs, from replacing damaged sheathing to re-roofing sections and repairing interior finishes, are scoped after the drying is verified, not before.

## Rancho Cordova Insurance Coordination

Storm damage claims in the Sacramento area tend to involve a specific friction point: the line between what your homeowner's policy covers as sudden storm damage versus what it may try to reclassify as deferred maintenance. A cracked ridge cap that let water in during last night's storm is a covered event; an adjuster may argue the cap was already deteriorated. Our documentation — timestamped photos, moisture readings, weather data cross-referenced to the event — gives your carrier the evidence it needs to process the claim as storm damage rather than push back on coverage.

We work directly with most major carriers and can communicate scope and line items in the format adjusters use, which shortens the back-and-forth cycle and gets remediation started faster.
