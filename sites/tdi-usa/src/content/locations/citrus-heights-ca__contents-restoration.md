## Why Citrus Heights Homes See Significant Contents Damage

Much of Citrus Heights was built during the post-war suburban expansion of the 1950s through 1970s. That housing stock tends to feature original wood-framed construction, older plumbing with galvanized or copper supply lines, and in many cases, attic or crawl-space configurations that allow smoke and moisture to migrate into living areas faster than in newer builds. When a pipe fails or a kitchen fire gets out of hand, the contents in those rooms — furniture, clothing, electronics, family documents — absorb odors and particulates quickly because older homes often lack the tight building envelopes that slow contamination spread.

The Sacramento Valley's summer heat compounds the problem. Temperatures regularly climb above 100°F from June through September, and heat accelerates the bonding of smoke residue to porous surfaces. A couch that sat in a smoke-damaged room for even 48 hours during a July heat wave will need more aggressive cleaning than the same piece in a cooler climate. Conversely, the wet winters — when the American River watershed sees heavy rainfall and the region's clay-heavy soils shed water rather than absorb it — can push water into garages and lower levels, soaking stored belongings before a homeowner realizes there's a problem.

## Our Contents Pack-Out and Restoration Process in Citrus Heights

Contents restoration starts with a detailed inventory before anything leaves your home. Every item is photographed, catalogued, and assigned a condition rating — this documentation matters both for your insurance claim and for ensuring everything is returned to the right room when the job is done.

From there, the pack-out moves contents to a secure, climate-controlled facility. In Citrus Heights's summer heat, climate control is not optional — uncontrolled storage can cause secondary damage to wood furniture, leather, and electronics that survived the original loss. At the facility, our IICRC-certified technicians sort contents by material type and damage category:

- **Hard goods** (furniture frames, appliances, ceramics) are cleaned using appropriate solvents and ultrasonic equipment where applicable.
- **Soft goods** (upholstery, clothing, bedding, rugs) are assessed for smoke odor, soot, and biological contamination before cleaning or ozone treatment.
- **Documents and photos** receive specialized drying and stabilization — these are often the items homeowners are most anxious about.
- **Electronics** are evaluated by technicians before any power-on attempt, since moisture and smoke residue inside a device can cause secondary failures.

Once the structure is cleared and rebuilt, contents are returned in the same documented sequence they left — room by room, matched against the original inventory.
