## Why Rancho Cordova's Building Stock Shapes Every Remodel

The postwar subdivisions that define much of Rancho Cordova were built quickly and to the standards of their era — slab-on-grade foundations, galvanized steel supply lines, and single-pane aluminum-frame windows. By the time a homeowner is ready to gut a kitchen or open up a living space, those original systems are often at or past their service life. A kitchen remodel that looks straightforward on a mood board can reveal galvanized pipe that's nearly closed with mineral scale, or a subfloor that's been quietly wicking moisture from a slow slab leak for years.

Rancho Cordova also sits in a seismic zone, and the California Building Code requirements for structural work — shear wall retrofits, hold-downs, anchor bolts — apply to permitted remodels here just as they do across Sacramento County. Skipping permits to save time is a risk that surfaces at resale; Sacramento County's building department requires final inspection records, and unpermitted work can stall or kill an escrow. Every project TDI® USA manages is permitted where required and inspected to close.

## Our Renovation and General Contracting Process in Rancho Cordova

The process starts with a thorough walkthrough — not just of the rooms being remodeled, but of the systems that feed them. Before a bathroom remodel begins, the supply and drain lines get assessed. Before a kitchen is opened up, the electrical panel is checked against the load the new layout will demand. This pre-construction audit is especially important in homes built before the 1980s, where the original wiring may be aluminum branch circuit or the panel may still be a fuse box.

From there, the sequence is straightforward:

- **Scope and permit documentation** — drawings, material specs, and permit applications submitted to Sacramento County Building Inspection before demolition begins.
- **Selective demolition** — careful removal that protects adjacent finishes and flags anything unexpected (asbestos-containing materials in pre-1980 homes, hidden water damage, undersized framing).
- **Rough-in trades** — framing, plumbing, electrical, and HVAC rough-in coordinated and inspected before walls close.
- **Finish work** — tile, cabinetry, countertops, paint, and trim installed to the agreed specifications.
- **Final inspection and punch list** — county sign-off obtained, then a walk-through with the homeowner to address any remaining items.

For post-damage rebuilds — properties that need reconstruction after water, fire, or mold remediation — TDI® USA's IICRC-certified background means the rebuild starts from a verified dry, clean substrate, not a guess.

## Local Note: Slab Foundations and the Wet-Dry Soil Cycle

Rancho Cordova's expansive clay soils — common throughout this part of the Sacramento Valley — swell during the wet season and contract sharply in summer. Over decades, that movement works on slab-on-grade foundations in ways that show up as interior cracks at door corners, sticking doors, and tile grout that fractures in a pattern following the slab's stress lines. When a remodel opens up flooring or walls, it's common to find evidence of this movement that wasn't visible from the surface. Knowing to look for it — and knowing how to document it for a homeowner's insurance carrier or a structural engineer — is the difference between a remodel that solves the problem and one that covers it up temporarily.
