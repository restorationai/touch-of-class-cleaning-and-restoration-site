## Why Citrus Heights Properties See Mold Issues

The Sacramento Valley's Mediterranean climate — long dry stretches punctuated by concentrated winter rainfall — puts Citrus Heights homes through a specific stress cycle. Roofs, gutters, and window flashing that hold up fine through summer can fail under the first heavy atmospheric river of November or December, driving water into attic sheathing and wall cavities before a homeowner even notices a stain on the ceiling.

Citrus Heights also has a substantial housing stock built between the 1950s and 1980s. Homes from that era were commonly framed with paper-faced drywall and insulated with materials that absorb and hold moisture far longer than modern alternatives. Vapor barriers were not standard practice in much of that construction period, which means wall cavities in older neighborhoods can trap humidity against wood framing for weeks after a leak is patched at the surface. That sustained moisture is exactly what Stachybotrys (black mold) and Cladosporium need to colonize.

Crawl spaces are another recurring factor. Many single-story ranch homes throughout Citrus Heights sit on raised foundations with vented crawl spaces, and when ground moisture or a slow plumbing leak goes undetected underneath, mold can establish itself on floor joists and subfloor sheathing well before it migrates into living areas.

## Our Mold Remediation Process in Citrus Heights

Every remediation starts with a thorough inspection — not just a visual scan, but moisture readings taken with calibrated meters at the wall cavity, subfloor, and ceiling plane levels. Mold that reads dry at the surface often has a saturated core behind it, and skipping that step means leaving the fuel source in place.

Once the scope is confirmed, the crew establishes containment using 6-mil poly sheeting and negative air pressure with HEPA-filtered air scrubbers. Negative pressure keeps disturbed spores from migrating into unaffected rooms — a step that matters especially in older Citrus Heights homes where HVAC ductwork often runs through walls and attic spaces that connect multiple zones of the house.

Affected materials are removed, bagged, and disposed of per California Department of Public Health guidelines. Remaining structural surfaces are treated and HEPA-vacuumed. Drying equipment — commercial dehumidifiers and air movers — runs until moisture readings confirm the substrate has returned to normal range. The process follows the IICRC S520 Standard for Professional Mold Remediation, which governs containment, removal, and clearance criteria.
