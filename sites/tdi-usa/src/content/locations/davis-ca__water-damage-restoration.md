## Why Davis Properties See Water Damage Issues

The same agricultural drainage network that made Yolo County farmland productive for generations also shapes how water behaves beneath Davis neighborhoods. Clay-dominant soils hold moisture rather than releasing it, which means a slow leak under a slab can saturate the subgrade for weeks before a homeowner notices warped hardwood or a musty smell in the hallway. Homes built in the 1950s through 1970s — a significant portion of Davis's residential stock — often have galvanized steel supply lines that have passed their service life. Galvanized pipe corrodes from the inside out; by the time a pinhole leak appears, the surrounding framing may already be damp.

Davis also experiences a pronounced wet season concentrated between November and March. Back-to-back atmospheric river events — the kind that have become more frequent across the Central Valley — can overwhelm gutters, push water through foundation vents, and flood crawl spaces in a matter of hours. Flat lot grades common in many Davis subdivisions give runoff nowhere to go except toward the foundation.

## Our Water Damage Restoration Process in Davis

Every job starts with moisture mapping, not guesswork. The crew uses thermal imaging cameras and calibrated moisture meters to trace water migration through walls and floors before a single piece of flooring comes up. In Davis homes with original plaster-and-lath construction, this step is especially important — plaster can read nearly dry at the surface while the lath and framing behind it remain saturated, a condition that standard visual inspection misses entirely.

Once the scope is confirmed, water extraction begins using truck-mounted and portable extraction units sized to the loss. Structural drying follows the IICRC S500 standard: negative air pressure, industrial-grade desiccant or refrigerant dehumidifiers, and high-velocity air movers positioned to move moisture out of cavities rather than just off surfaces. Drying logs are recorded daily and provided to the homeowner and their insurance adjuster. The job is not closed until moisture readings return to regionally appropriate baselines — in Davis, that means accounting for the ambient humidity that lingers through the rainy season, not just a single dry-weather reading.
