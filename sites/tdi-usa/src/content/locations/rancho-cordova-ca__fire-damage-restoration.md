## Why Rancho Cordova Properties Face Distinct Fire Damage Challenges

Rancho Cordova's housing stock spans several distinct eras, and each era carries its own fire-damage profile. The city grew rapidly during the postwar aerospace boom along the Aerojet corridor, and neighborhoods developed through the 1960s and 1970s often feature wood-framed construction with original attic insulation — materials that absorb smoke odor deeply and are slow to release it. Homes built during that period frequently have plaster-over-lath interior walls rather than modern drywall, which means soot can settle into surface pores while the underlying lath holds odor compounds long after a surface cleaning.

More recent subdivisions built during the 1990s and 2000s construction wave introduced engineered lumber and composite materials that char differently and can release chemical off-gassing that standard deodorization protocols need to account for. Rancho Cordova also sits in a region where wildfire smoke events — even from fires burning miles away in the Sierra Nevada foothills — can pre-load HVAC systems with particulate before a structure fire ever occurs, complicating post-fire air quality assessment.

## Our Fire Damage Restoration Process in Rancho Cordova

Every fire loss is different, but the sequence matters. The process begins with a full structural assessment — documenting fire-affected materials, mapping smoke migration through the HVAC system, and identifying hidden heat damage behind walls and above ceilings. Once the scope is established, the process moves through controlled demolition of unsalvageable materials, dry and wet soot cleaning calibrated to the surface type (porous masonry and plaster require different chemistry than painted drywall), thermal fogging and hydroxyl treatment for odor, and final air quality verification.
