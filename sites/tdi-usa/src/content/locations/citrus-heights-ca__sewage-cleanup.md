## Why Citrus Heights Properties See Sewage Backup More Often Than Owners Expect

Citrus Heights sits on the eastern edge of the Sacramento Valley where clay-heavy soils shift significantly between the dry summer months and the saturated winters that follow. That seasonal soil movement puts lateral sewer lines — the privately owned pipes running from your home to the city main — under repeated stress. Cast-iron and Orangeburg pipe, common in homes built through the 1970s across many Citrus Heights neighborhoods, corrodes, collapses, and root-intrudes in ways that PVC simply doesn't. When roots find a crack, they expand it; when Orangeburg softens, it deforms under soil pressure and restricts flow until a full backup occurs.

The city's relatively flat topography also means gravity drainage has less slope to work with than hillside communities. Slower-moving waste lines accumulate grease and debris faster, and a partial blockage that would clear itself on a steeper grade becomes a full backup here. Homes on larger lots with mature trees — a common sight throughout the city's established residential corridors — face ongoing root intrusion that can recur even after a professional cleaning if the underlying pipe condition isn't addressed.

## Our Sewage Cleanup and Sanitization Process in Citrus Heights

Raw sewage is classified as Category 3 water — the highest contamination tier — which means the cleanup protocol is more involved than a standard water loss. Our IICRC-certified technicians begin with containment: isolating the affected area to prevent cross-contamination to clean portions of the home. Affected porous materials — drywall, insulation, carpet, and pad — are removed and bagged for disposal in accordance with Sacramento County waste handling requirements. Nothing porous that has absorbed Category 3 water is dried in place.

Once bulk waste and compromised materials are out, we apply EPA-registered disinfectants to all hard surfaces, subfloor assemblies, and framing members in the affected zone. Dwell times matter — a quick spray-and-wipe doesn't satisfy the IICRC S500 standard for sewage losses, and we don't cut that corner. Structural drying equipment runs until moisture readings in framing and subfloor confirm the assembly is dry, not just surface-dry. A final antimicrobial treatment is applied before any reconstruction begins, and we document every step with photos and moisture logs for your insurance carrier.
