## Why West Sacramento Properties Face Elevated Sewage Backup Risk

The Sacramento Valley's wet-season rainfall — concentrated between November and March — puts significant hydraulic pressure on municipal sewer infrastructure across Yolo County. West Sacramento's combined age of housing stock and its position in the floodplain amplify that risk. Many properties in the Broderick and Bryte areas were built in the mid-20th century, when sewer laterals were commonly installed using clay tile or Orangeburg pipe. Both materials degrade over decades: clay joints separate and allow root intrusion, while Orangeburg softens and collapses. When the main line surcharges during a heavy rain event, a compromised lateral becomes a direct path for raw sewage to travel backward into the lowest fixture in the home — typically a floor drain, a basement toilet, or a washing machine drain.

The Bridge District and newer mixed-use developments along the riverfront use more modern PVC infrastructure, but those properties carry a different risk: the sewer system in that corridor handles a denser load, and any upstream blockage can push effluent back through connections that residents assume are protected. Regardless of where in West Sacramento the backup occurs, the biological hazard is the same: Category 3 water containing bacteria, viruses, and parasites that require full PPE, containment, and EPA-registered disinfectants — not a shop vac and bleach.

## Our Sewage Cleanup and Sanitization Process in West Sacramento

When TDI® USA arrives at a West Sacramento property, the first priority is stopping ongoing exposure. That means identifying the source — whether it's a sewer line backup, a failed septic system, or an overflowing ejector pump — and coordinating with the homeowner or property manager to shut off contributing water sources. From there, the process follows a structured sequence:

**Containment and PPE establishment.** The affected area is isolated to prevent cross-contamination to unaffected rooms. Technicians work in full personal protective equipment throughout extraction and sanitization.

**Solid and liquid waste extraction.** Truck-mounted extraction equipment removes standing sewage. Porous materials — carpet, pad, saturated drywall, compromised subfloor — that cannot be safely decontaminated are removed and bagged for proper disposal per California Department of Public Health guidelines.

**Surface disinfection and antimicrobial treatment.** All hard surfaces, framing, and remaining structural materials are treated with EPA-registered disinfectants. This step is not cosmetic; it addresses pathogens that survive on surfaces long after visible sewage is removed.

**Structural drying.** Once the space is sanitized, drying equipment — air movers, dehumidifiers, and where necessary, desiccant systems — is deployed. Moisture readings are logged daily until materials reach dry standard per the IICRC S500. West Sacramento's high ambient humidity during the rainy season can extend drying timelines compared to drier inland climates, and we account for that in equipment placement.

**Clearance documentation.** Final moisture readings and a written scope of work are provided for insurance and, where applicable, for permit-required inspections through the City of West Sacramento Building Department.
