## Our Storm Damage Restoration Process in Citrus Heights

Every storm loss is different, but the sequence of decisions matters. The crew's first priority on arrival is safety — identifying compromised structural members, live electrical hazards from downed lines or flooded panels, and any gas concerns before anyone enters the structure. Once the site is safe, the assessment covers the full loss: roof and attic penetrations, wall cavity intrusion, subfloor saturation, and any secondary damage from debris impact.

Emergency tarping and board-up happen the same visit when the structure needs it — open roofs and broken windows left overnight invite additional water intrusion and create liability. From there, the process moves into structured drying using commercial-grade desiccant and refrigerant dehumidifiers, high-velocity air movers, and moisture mapping with calibrated meters and thermal imaging. Because Citrus Heights homes from the 1960s frequently used plaster-over-lath wall construction rather than modern drywall, drying timelines can run longer — plaster holds moisture in the substrate well after the surface reads dry, and crews account for that when setting equipment and scheduling monitoring visits.
