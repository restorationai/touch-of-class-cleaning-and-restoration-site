## Why Folsom Properties See Storm Damage Differently

Folsom's housing stock spans a wide range of construction eras and styles. Newer master-planned communities built in the 1990s and 2000s use engineered roof trusses and OSB sheathing that can delaminate quickly once water infiltrates. Older neighborhoods closer to the historic district feature heavier wood-framing and sometimes original clay or concrete tile roofing — materials that hold up well until a branch impact cracks a tile and opens a channel straight to the underlayment. Either way, the Sierra foothill climate adds a specific wrinkle: storms often arrive after weeks of dry weather, meaning the soil is hard-packed and runoff moves fast rather than absorbing. That rapid surface flow finds every low point — window wells, garage slabs, crawl spaces — and enters structures faster than it would in areas with regularly saturated ground.

Wind is the other factor. Folsom is not in a traditional tornado corridor, but the gap between the coastal ranges and the Sierra creates pressure-driven wind events that can exceed 50 mph during strong storm systems. At those speeds, unsecured patio covers, fence panels, and even rooftop HVAC equipment become projectiles. The damage pattern is often less about a single catastrophic impact and more about multiple small breaches — a popped ridge cap here, a lifted flashing there — that collectively let in enough water to saturate structural cavities.

## Our Storm Damage Restoration Process in Folsom

The first priority on any storm call is stopping active water intrusion. Our IICRC-certified crews arrive with tarping materials, board-up supplies, and moisture meters to assess how far water has already traveled inside the structure. Visible damage at the roof or wall is rarely the full picture — water migrates along rafters, runs behind wall sheathing, and pools in subfloor cavities well beyond the obvious breach point.

Once the structure is secured against further weather exposure, we move into systematic drying. Structural drying in Folsom's climate requires attention to outdoor humidity levels, which swing significantly between storm events and the dry days that follow. Our IICRC ASD-certified technicians use that dry post-storm air strategically, calibrating equipment to accelerate evaporation without over-drying materials to the point of cracking or warping. Affected insulation — particularly blown-in attic insulation common in homes here — is almost always removed rather than dried in place, because saturated loose-fill insulation loses its R-value and becomes a mold substrate even after surface readings normalize.

Documentation runs parallel to every physical step. We photograph and log affected materials, moisture readings, and equipment placement in a format that insurance adjusters can work with directly, which shortens the claims cycle for homeowners.

## Local Note

Folsom's mature valley oak and blue oak trees are part of what makes the city visually distinctive — and they are also the source of some of the most structurally complex storm damage calls in the area. These oaks develop large lateral limbs over decades, and when a limb fails under wind load it rarely breaks cleanly. Instead it partially separates and levers against the roof structure, creating a combination of impact damage at the point of contact and uplift stress on the surrounding framing. Crews working these calls need to assess the structural integrity of the roof framing before removing the limb, because the branch itself may be providing temporary support to a compromised rafter. We coordinate with a structural assessment before full debris removal on any significant limb-fall call.
