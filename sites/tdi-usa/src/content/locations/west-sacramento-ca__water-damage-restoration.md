## Why West Sacramento Properties See Water Damage Issues

The Sacramento River levees that protect West Sacramento are engineered and maintained, but the soil beneath the city is a different story. Much of West Sacramento sits on river alluvium — deep, fine-grained sediment that drains slowly and holds moisture long after the surface appears dry. When a slab-on-grade home takes on water from a slab leak or a backed-up floor drain, that moisture migrates laterally through the subgrade before it ever shows up on a surface reading. A moisture meter at the baseboard may read normal while the concrete subfloor beneath it is still saturated.

The housing stock adds another layer. Neighborhoods like Broderick and Bryte — two of West Sacramento's oldest residential areas, developed largely in the mid-20th century — contain a mix of wood-frame construction with original cast-iron or galvanized-steel plumbing that has long exceeded its design life. Galvanized pipe corrodes from the inside out; pinhole leaks can run inside walls for weeks before they stain drywall or register on a water bill. By the time a homeowner calls, the framing cavity may already be damp enough to support mold colonization within the next day or two.

The city's rainy season — concentrated between November and March, with the heaviest events driven by atmospheric rivers off the Pacific — also creates a recurring pattern of roof intrusion and window-frame infiltration. Flat or low-slope roofs common on mid-century ranch homes are particularly vulnerable when gutters back up during a sustained multi-day rain event.

## Our Water Damage Restoration Process in West Sacramento

Every water loss is different, but the sequence that protects a West Sacramento property follows a consistent logic: stop the source, document the extent, extract standing water, then dry the structure to measured targets — not to a technician's estimate.

**Assessment and documentation** come first. TDI® USA technicians use thermal imaging cameras and calibrated moisture meters to map the full wet boundary, including areas behind cabinets, under flooring, and inside wall cavities. In West Sacramento's older wood-frame homes, this step often reveals moisture migration that extends well beyond the visible damage.

**Water extraction** follows immediately. Truck-mounted extraction units pull standing water from carpet, hardwood, and concrete surfaces. For slab-on-grade homes — common throughout West Sacramento — we use specialty drying mats that draw moisture up through concrete rather than waiting for it to evaporate from the surface.

**Structural drying** is calibrated to the IICRC S500 standard and monitored with daily moisture readings logged to a drying report. Desiccant or refrigerant dehumidifiers and high-velocity air movers are placed based on the psychrometric conditions in the structure, not on a generic room count. Drying goals are set to the material's dry standard, documented, and shared with your insurance adjuster.

**Affected material decisions** — whether drywall, insulation, or flooring stays or goes — are made based on moisture readings and the IICRC's material classification guidelines, not on convenience. We document every decision with photographs and written scope notes.
