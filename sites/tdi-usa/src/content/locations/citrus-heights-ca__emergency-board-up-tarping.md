## Why Citrus Heights Properties Face Distinct Board-Up and Tarping Needs

Much of Citrus Heights was built during the postwar suburban expansion of the 1950s through the 1980s, which means a large share of the housing stock uses wood-frame construction with stucco exteriors and composition shingle roofing. That combination has specific vulnerabilities. Stucco can mask structural damage at the sheathing level — a broken window or fire-damaged wall opening that looks contained from the street may have already allowed smoke and heat to penetrate the wall cavity. Composition shingle roofs, particularly those on homes approaching 25 to 30 years old, are more susceptible to tearing in the Diablo wind events that occasionally push through the Sacramento Valley in fall and winter, leaving roof decks exposed before a storm system moves in.

The city's position in the Sacramento Valley also means it sits in a fire-risk corridor. Wildfire ember cast — where embers from fires burning miles away land on roofs and in attics — is a documented hazard in this region. After a structure fire or a nearby wildfire event, an open roof or broken skylight becomes an ember entry point for any subsequent ignition risk, making rapid tarping a safety measure, not just a weather precaution.

## Our Emergency Board-Up and Tarping Process in Citrus Heights

When you call, the first question is always: what is exposed, and what is actively at risk? A broken window from a burglary and a fire-damaged roof opening require different approaches, and we size the response accordingly.

For window and door board-up, we use plywood cut to fit and secured with exterior-grade fasteners at the framing — not just taped or friction-fit into the opening. On stucco homes, we anchor to the structural framing behind the stucco rather than to the stucco surface itself, which can crack or delaminate under load. For fire damage board-up, we assess whether the framing around the opening is structurally sound before anchoring; compromised framing gets supplemental blocking before the board goes in.

For roof tarping, we use heavy-duty polyethylene tarps weighted and secured at the ridge and anchored with lumber battens rather than just bungee cords or rope. In Citrus Heights's summer heat, a poorly secured tarp can shift or degrade within days — proper batten anchoring keeps the tarp in place through the duration of the insurance claim process and into reconstruction.

All work is documented with timestamped photographs before, during, and after — a record your insurance adjuster will need.
