## Why Davis Properties Present Unique Post-Construction Cleaning Challenges

Davis sits in Yolo County's valley floor, and the region's climate creates conditions that complicate post-construction cleanup in ways that aren't always obvious. The Sacramento Valley's dry summers push fine silica dust — stirred up by grading, concrete cutting, and drywall sanding — deep into HVAC ductwork and onto every horizontal surface. That same dust, when it contacts the marine-influenced moisture that rolls in during the cooler months, can cake onto window tracks, tile grout, and millwork in ways that require chemical agitation, not just vacuuming.

Davis also has a substantial stock of mid-century residential construction — ranch-style homes and older apartment complexes built with plaster-over-lath wall systems rather than modern drywall. When these properties undergo renovation, the demolition phase releases a different dust profile than a new build: finer, more alkaline, and more likely to embed in porous surfaces like original hardwood floors or unglazed tile. A final clean on a 1960s Craftsman-adjacent home near the UC Davis campus requires a different chemical protocol than a brand-new mixed-use development on the city's outskirts.

## Our Post-Construction Cleaning Process in Davis

Every post-construction clean follows a sequenced approach rather than a single sweep-and-mop pass. The phases matter because skipping steps creates callbacks.

**Rough clean** removes bulk debris — cut lumber, packaging, drywall scraps, and adhesive backing — before any detail work begins. Attempting to detail-clean around debris just redistributes it.

**Detail clean** addresses the surfaces that subcontractors leave behind: paint overspray on hardware, caulk smears on tile, sticker residue on windows, and joint compound dust on light fixtures. This phase uses microfiber systems, low-residue degreasers, and HEPA-filtered vacuums rated for fine particulate — not shop vacs that recirculate dust.

**Final clean** is the white-glove pass that happens after all trades have cleared the site. Streak-free glass, clean switch plates, dust-free cabinet interiors, and polished floors. This is the phase that determines whether a property manager, homeowner, or commercial tenant walks in satisfied or calls back.

For specialty cleaning needs — post-renovation deep cleans on occupied properties, pressure washing of exterior flatwork, or cleaning after in-place remediation — the process is scoped individually based on site conditions.

## Local Note: Dust Behavior in Davis's Valley Climate

Anyone who has worked construction sites in the Sacramento Valley knows that the region's persistent summer wind patterns — particularly the afternoon delta breeze that funnels through from the Bay — carry fine particulate back into partially enclosed structures long after framing is complete. On Davis job sites, it's common to find a fresh layer of dust on surfaces that were cleaned just days earlier, simply because the building envelope wasn't fully sealed during finishing work. A thorough post-construction clean here accounts for that re-deposition: we sequence the HVAC cleaning and filter replacement as a late-phase task, not an early one, so the system isn't pulling construction dust back through a freshly cleaned space. It's a small adjustment that prevents a common callback.
