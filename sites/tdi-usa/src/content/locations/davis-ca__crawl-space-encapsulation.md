That swing between bone-dry heat and sustained winter dampness creates exactly the conditions that make crawl spaces suffer — and because a significant share of Davis's housing stock was built in the postwar boom years when vapor barriers were an afterthought, many homes in the city are working with crawl spaces that have never been properly sealed.

## Why Davis Properties See Crawl Space Problems

The Sacramento Valley's Mediterranean climate sounds forgiving until you spend a winter in it. From November through March, Davis receives the bulk of its annual rainfall in concentrated bursts. Clay-heavy soils common throughout Yolo County drain slowly, and that standing or slowly percolating groundwater has nowhere to go but up — through foundation vents, around stem walls, and into the air pocket beneath your floors.

Once moisture enters an unsealed crawl space, the physics work against you fast. Relative humidity in an unencapsulated crawl can climb well above 70% during a wet stretch, which is the threshold at which wood-decay fungi and mold colonies begin to establish. Fiberglass batt insulation stapled to floor joists — standard in Davis homes built through the 1980s — absorbs that humidity, sags, and eventually falls, leaving the subfloor exposed. Homeowners often notice the symptom before the cause: floors that feel soft underfoot, a musty smell that lingers even after the windows are open, or HVAC systems that seem to run constantly without cooling the house efficiently.

Davis's agricultural surroundings also mean that irrigation runoff from nearby fields and the city's own extensive urban tree canopy can push additional surface water toward residential foundations during the growing season — a pattern that surprises homeowners who assume crawl space moisture is strictly a winter problem.

## Our Crawl Space Encapsulation Process in Davis

Encapsulation in Davis isn't a one-size job. Before any material goes down, a technician performs a thorough inspection — probing floor joists for soft spots, reading moisture content with a calibrated meter, and checking for existing mold or pest activity that needs to be addressed before sealing the space. Encapsulating over an active moisture or mold problem traps the issue rather than resolving it.

Once the crawl space is cleared and dry, the process moves in layers:

- **Ground cover installation**: A heavy-mil reinforced polyethylene vapor barrier is run across the entire floor, overlapped at seams, and sealed with moisture-resistant tape. In Davis homes where the crawl space floor is uneven or has gravel fill, the barrier is secured to prevent shifting.
- **Wall and pier coverage**: The barrier is extended up the stem walls and sealed, closing off the primary pathway for ground moisture to enter the air column.
- **Vent sealing**: Open foundation vents — standard in older Davis construction — are sealed. This is a point of confusion for many homeowners who were told vents were required; California's building code has evolved, and sealed, conditioned crawl spaces consistently outperform vented ones in humid climates.
- **Drainage and dehumidification**: Where bulk water intrusion is a recurring issue, a perimeter drainage channel and sump system may be added. A crawl space dehumidifier sized to the square footage keeps residual humidity in check through the wet season.

All drying and encapsulation work follows IICRC standards, and the team is OSHA trained for confined-space entry.
