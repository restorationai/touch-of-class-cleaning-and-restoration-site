## Why Davis Properties See Distinctive Storm Damage

The Sacramento Valley's wet season arrives compressed and intense. Davis receives most of its annual rainfall between November and March, and when a strong Pacific system stalls over the Coast Range, the valley floor can absorb days of rain with nowhere for the water to go. Flat topography means sheet-flow drainage rather than natural runoff — water pools against foundations, seeps under slab-on-grade construction, and finds every gap in aging weatherstripping.

Davis also has a substantial stock of mid-century ranch homes and post-WWII tract construction, many with original wood-framed roofs, composition shingles that have cycled through decades of heat and cold, and attic insulation that was never designed to handle bulk water intrusion. When a storm tears away flashing or drops a limb through an aging roof deck, water reaches the attic insulation and ceiling joists quickly — and because the material is older, it holds moisture longer than modern assemblies. Stucco exteriors common in Davis can look intact while concealing saturated sheathing behind them, which is why surface inspection alone is not enough after a significant storm.

## Our Storm Damage Restoration Process in Davis

Every job starts with a full exterior and interior assessment before any debris is moved. We document wind-driven damage, roof breaches, and water intrusion points with photographs calibrated for insurance carrier review. Temporary protective measures — tarping, board-up, emergency glazing — go up immediately to stop ongoing exposure.

Once the structure is stabilized, the process shifts to moisture mapping. We use thermal imaging and calibrated moisture meters to trace water migration through wall cavities, subfloor assemblies, and ceiling planes. In Davis homes with plaster-over-lath interiors, which appear in older neighborhoods throughout the city, water can track laterally along the lath for several feet before showing any surface staining — our readings account for that behavior rather than relying on visible damage alone.

Structural drying follows the IICRC S500 and S520 standards, with equipment placement and daily monitoring logs that document drying progress for both the homeowner and the insurance adjuster. Debris removal, roof repair coordination, and interior reconstruction are staged so trades are not waiting on each other.
