## Why Rancho Cordova Properties See Water Damage Issues

The city's housing stock spans several decades and construction styles, and that range matters when water gets in. Subdivisions built in the 1970s and 1980s — common throughout eastern Rancho Cordova — frequently used galvanized steel supply lines that have long exceeded their useful life. Pinhole leaks in these pipes often go undetected inside wall cavities until a homeowner notices a soft spot in drywall or a musty odor that won't clear. Meanwhile, concrete slab foundations, standard across most of the city, leave no crawl space to inspect, so moisture migrating through the slab can saturate flooring adhesive and subfloor material for weeks before it surfaces.

Rancho Cordova also sits adjacent to the American River corridor. While the river itself is managed by Folsom Dam upstream, localized flooding from storm drains and retention basins during heavy rain events is a recurring pattern in lower-lying parts of the city. That kind of water — mixed with street runoff and sediment — is categorically different from a clean supply-line break and requires a more thorough extraction and sanitation process.

## Our Water Damage Restoration Process in Rancho Cordova

Every job starts with a thorough moisture assessment. Thermal imaging cameras and calibrated moisture meters let us map saturation that isn't visible to the eye — particularly important on slab-on-grade homes where moisture migrates laterally under flooring before it becomes obvious. Once we understand the full scope, we extract standing water with truck-mounted and portable extraction units, then establish a drying system using industrial air movers and low-grain refrigerant dehumidifiers sized to the actual square footage and material types involved.

Structural drying in Rancho Cordova follows the IICRC S500 standard, which means we're measuring temperature, relative humidity, and material moisture content at every check — not guessing. Drying logs are documented daily, which your insurance adjuster will expect and which protects you if a dispute arises later. When materials like hardwood flooring, plaster, or original tile cannot be dried in place without risking further damage, we explain the options clearly before any demolition begins.
