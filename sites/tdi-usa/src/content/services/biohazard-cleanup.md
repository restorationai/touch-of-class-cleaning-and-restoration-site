When something happens inside a home or property that leaves behind blood, bodily fluids, or other infectious material, the clock matters in ways most people don't expect. Pathogens can survive on porous surfaces for hours or days. Odors bind into subflooring and drywall quickly. And the longer a scene sits unaddressed, the more material migrates — tracked through hallways, absorbed into carpet padding, drawn into HVAC returns. Biohazard cleanup is not a cleaning job. It is a regulated remediation process, and the difference is significant.

## What biohazard cleanup actually involves

Biohazard remediation covers a range of situations: unattended deaths, trauma scenes, blood and bodily fluid exposure, sharps and needle cleanup from drug activity, and infectious material cleanup following a health event. What these situations share is that the visible contamination is rarely the full picture.

Professional biohazard cleanup uses ATP testing and luminescence tools to locate biological material that isn't visible to the naked eye. Affected porous materials — carpet, pad, drywall, subfloor — are removed and packaged as regulated medical waste, not bagged and left at the curb. Enzymatic and hospital-grade disinfectants break down organic matter at a molecular level rather than masking it. The air in the affected space is treated and filtered throughout the process.

Timeline depends on the size of the affected area, the surfaces involved, and how long the situation went unaddressed. A contained single-room scene may be remediated in a single day. Situations involving structural penetration — fluids that have migrated under flooring or behind walls — require additional assessment and may extend the scope.

TDI® USA is an IICRC Certified Firm with OSHA-trained technicians and operates 24/7. Work is performed under full PPE protocols, and all regulated waste is transported and disposed of under a proper waste manifest — a legal requirement in California that not every operator meets.

## Our process

1. **Scene assessment and PPE staging.** Before any work begins, technicians assess the full scope of contamination — including areas that may not be immediately visible — and establish appropriate personal protective equipment and containment protocols. Access to unaffected areas of the property is maintained where possible.

2. **Containment and air control.** The affected area is isolated using physical barriers and negative air pressure where indicated. HEPA-filtered air scrubbers run continuously to prevent cross-contamination to adjacent spaces during removal work.

3. **Biohazardous material removal and surface treatment.** Contaminated porous materials are carefully removed, double-bagged, and labeled as regulated biohazardous waste. Remaining hard surfaces are treated with enzymatic cleaners followed by EPA-registered hospital-grade disinfectants, applied with appropriate dwell times — not wiped off immediately.

4. **ATP testing and verification.** After treatment, surfaces are tested using ATP bioluminescence meters to verify that biological contamination has been reduced to safe levels. This is the step that separates a professional remediation from a surface clean.

5. **Regulated waste transport and manifest documentation.** All biohazardous waste leaves the property under a California-compliant waste manifest. A copy is provided for your records — and for your insurance carrier if needed.

## What separates a good biohazard response from a bad one

The most common failure in biohazard remediation is treating it like a deep clean. General cleaning crews — even experienced ones — are not equipped or licensed to handle regulated biological waste, and they typically lack the testing tools to confirm that contamination has been fully addressed. What looks clean to the eye can still harbor active pathogens.

A second common gap is incomplete scope assessment. Fluids follow gravity and capillary action. A surface stain may represent a fraction of the actual contamination if material has migrated into subfloor layers or wall cavities. Operators who don't probe beyond the visible surface often leave behind the contamination that causes persistent odor and long-term structural damage.

Insurance adjusters reviewing biohazard claims look for a documented scope of work, photographic evidence of affected materials before and after removal, ATP test results, and a waste manifest. Without these, carriers may dispute the claim or require a re-inspection. TDI® USA provides complete documentation as a standard part of every job.

Discretion is also a professional standard, not a courtesy. Technicians arrive in unmarked vehicles when requested and work without drawing attention to the property.
