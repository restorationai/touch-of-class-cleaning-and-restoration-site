A restoration job ends when the damaged material is gone. A renovation job ends when the space actually works again — and that gap is where most property owners get stuck. Whether you're rebuilding a kitchen gutted by a burst pipe, converting a flood-damaged basement into livable square footage, or simply remodeling a bathroom that hasn't been touched since the 1980s, the work requires permits, sequenced trades, and a general contractor who can hold the schedule together. TDI® USA handles that entire arc, from the first permit application to the final walk-through, in Sacramento and the surrounding region.

## What renovations, remodels and general contracting actually involves

General contracting is coordination work as much as construction work. A kitchen remodel, for example, touches demolition, rough plumbing, rough electrical, insulation, drywall, cabinetry, finish plumbing, finish electrical, tile, and paint — each trade dependent on the one before it. A delay in the rough electrical inspection holds up insulation, which holds up drywall, which holds up everything downstream. A general contractor's job is to anticipate those dependencies and keep the schedule from compressing into a pile-up at the end.

For post-damage rebuilds, the coordination layer is even more complex. Structural repairs may need to be documented before drywall closes the wall cavity. Scope changes requested by an insurance adjuster mid-project have to be incorporated without blowing the timeline. Materials affected by supply delays need substitutions that meet the original spec — or the adjuster's approval if the carrier is paying.

Timelines vary significantly by scope. A single bathroom remodel in an older Sacramento bungalow typically runs four to eight weeks once permits are issued. A full kitchen gut-and-rebuild runs eight to fourteen weeks. Whole-home renovation projects are planned in phases, with livability milestones built into the schedule so the property isn't uninhabitable for the entire duration.

## Our process

1. **Scope of work and design consultation.** Before any permit is pulled or subcontractor is scheduled, TDI® USA walks the space with you to define exactly what's being built, changed, or restored. For post-damage projects, this includes reviewing the adjuster's scope and identifying any line items that need to be supplemented before work begins.

2. **Permitting and plan submission.** Most structural, electrical, plumbing, and mechanical work in Sacramento requires a permit through the City of Sacramento Community Development Department or the relevant county authority. TDI® USA prepares and submits the required documentation, coordinates with the plan check process, and schedules inspections at each required phase — so you're not chasing inspectors while trades are standing by.

3. **Demolition and rough-in.** Selective demolition removes only what the scope requires. Rough framing, rough plumbing, and rough electrical are completed and inspected before any wall or ceiling surfaces are closed. For older Sacramento homes — many built before 1978 — lead-safe work practices apply to any surface disturbance, and TDI® USA's OSHA-trained crews follow those protocols throughout.

4. **Finish work and fixture installation.** Drywall, insulation, tile, cabinetry, countertops, fixtures, and trim are installed in trade sequence. Each subcontractor is scheduled against the inspection calendar, not just the calendar date, so finish work doesn't begin before rough-in sign-off.

5. **Final inspection and owner walk-through.** TDI® USA coordinates the final inspection with the authority having jurisdiction, then walks the completed space with you before closing out the project. Any punch-list items are resolved before the project is considered complete.

## What separates a good renovation from a bad one

The most common failure point in residential remodeling isn't craftsmanship — it's sequencing. Finish materials installed before rough-in inspections are signed off create a situation where the inspector requires destructive access to verify concealed work. That means opening walls that were just drywalled and tiled, adding cost and weeks to a project that was supposed to be done.

For post-damage rebuilds specifically, a frequent problem is scope creep that isn't documented. An adjuster approves a scope, the homeowner requests changes during construction, and those changes aren't captured in a written supplement — leaving the contractor and homeowner arguing over who pays for work that everyone agreed to verbally. TDI® USA documents every scope change in writing before the work is performed.

Older Sacramento housing stock presents its own set of technical considerations. Homes built before the 1980s often have original cast-iron drain lines, knob-and-tube or aluminum branch wiring, and plaster-over-lath wall systems. A remodel that opens those walls without accounting for what's behind them can turn a straightforward bathroom update into a code-compliance project. Experienced general contractors price and schedule for what they're likely to find, not just what the plans show.

## Seasonal and regional considerations

Sacramento's dry summers and wet winters create a predictable remodeling calendar. Exterior work — roofing, siding, exterior painting, foundation drainage — is easiest to schedule between May and October, before the Valley's rain season begins. Interior remodels run year-round, but permit processing times at local building departments tend to lengthen in spring when residential remodeling activity peaks across the region. Submitting permit applications in late fall or early winter often results in faster plan-check turnaround.

For homes in Sacramento's older neighborhoods — Curtis Park, Land Park, East Sacramento, Midtown — the housing stock is predominantly pre-1950 construction with wood-frame foundations, original plaster walls, and plumbing that may predate copper supply lines. These homes remodel beautifully, but the scope of work needs to account for what's behind the walls before the budget is finalized.
