## Restoration emergencies common in Worthing

Worthing sits in a flat stretch of Lincoln County where the Big Sioux River drainage basin keeps the water table closer to the surface than most homeowners expect. That matters when spring snowmelt hits — the ground saturates quickly, and crawl spaces and basement slabs that held up fine through a dry winter can take on water within hours of a sustained rain event. The clay-heavy soil doesn't drain fast, so moisture lingers against foundation walls long after the storm passes.

The housing stock tells the rest of the story. As a growing bedroom community just south of Sioux Falls along I-29, Worthing has seen a steady wave of new construction over the past decade — subdivisions built quickly to meet demand, with sump systems and vapor barriers that vary in quality. Older homes near Downtown Worthing tend to have aging cast-iron drain lines and original vapor barriers that weren't designed for today's rainfall patterns. Newer builds in East Worthing sometimes have installation shortcuts that only show up after the first hard freeze or the first wet spring. Both ends of the housing spectrum generate restoration calls for different reasons.

South Dakota's temperature swings compound the problem. Freeze-thaw cycles between November and March crack foundation walls, split supply lines in uninsulated chases, and push moisture into wall cavities where it sits undetected until mold colonizes — which can happen in as little as 24 to 48 hours in the right conditions. Fire calls spike in winter when space heaters and wood-burning inserts work overtime.

**Water damage mitigation** is the most common call we receive from Worthing.
