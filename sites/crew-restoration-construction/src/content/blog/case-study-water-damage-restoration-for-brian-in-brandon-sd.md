## What Flood Cuts Are and Why They Matter

The project notes reference flood cuts completed 2 feet high throughout the bathroom. In a typical water damage loss, flood cuts (sometimes called demo cuts) are horizontal cuts made in drywall at a set height above the waterline. The purpose is to remove saturated drywall before trapped moisture can migrate further up the wall cavity or promote microbial growth behind the surface.

On a standard loss like this, technicians use moisture meters to verify how far water has wicked into the wall assembly before determining the cut height. Cutting at a consistent, measurable height (2 feet in this case) also simplifies the drywall repair phase: replacement panels can be cut to match, and the seam lands at a predictable location for finishing. Keeping the cut line uniform across multiple rooms, as documented with a red-highlighted floor plan here, helps ensure no wet pockets are left behind and gives the repair crew a clear scope to work from.

## Salvage Decisions: Trim, Carpet, and Cabinetry

One of the more nuanced parts of any water damage restoration is deciding what materials can be salvaged versus what must be removed entirely. The project notes for Brian's job reflect several of those decisions in real terms.

The maple base trim (4 ¼″ profile) was assessed and determined to be reusable, which is a meaningful cost and material savings. On a typical loss, solid wood trim can sometimes be carefully removed, dried, and reinstalled if it has not warped or delaminated. Engineered or MDF trim is far less forgiving and usually requires full replacement.

Carpet removal is standard practice when flooring has been saturated, particularly when no pad is present in the affected area, as noted here. Pad acts as a sponge and almost always requires disposal; without it, the carpet itself holds the moisture against the subfloor. The homeowner's preference to save the existing carpet is noted in the project file, which is a common request. Whether salvage is feasible depends on the water category, saturation duration, and subfloor condition, factors a restoration technician evaluates on site.

The vanity removal, closet door jamb replacement, and drywall repairs in both the bathroom and bedroom round out the demolition and rebuild scope. Each of these items represents a standard phase in a water damage restoration: remove what is wet or compromised, dry the structure, then rebuild to pre-loss condition.
