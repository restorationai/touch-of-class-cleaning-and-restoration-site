## How Much Does Water Damage Restoration Cost in South Dakota?

In South Dakota, water damage restoration typically costs $1,500 to $12,000 for residential losses, with most homeowners landing between $3,000 and $7,500. That wide range exists because a dripping supply line caught in 24 hours is a fundamentally different job than a sewer backup that soaked a finished basement for three days.

Here are typical cost ranges by scenario:

| Scenario | Typical Cost Range |
|---|---|
| Single room, clean water (Cat 1), caught within 24 hours | $1,200 to $3,500 |
| Multi-room, clean water, drywall and flooring affected | $3,500 to $7,000 |
| Finished basement, clean water, carpet and drywall | $4,500 to $9,000 |
| Gray water loss (Cat 2) - washing machine, dishwasher overflow | $3,000 to $8,500 |
| Sewage backup or black water (Cat 3) - any size | $5,000 to $15,000+ |
| Whole-home flooding, multiple floors affected | $10,000 to $25,000+ |

These are industry-typical figures for the Sioux Falls metro and surrounding South Dakota communities. Every loss is different, and Crew Restoration & Construction provides a written scope of work before any work begins so you know exactly what you are paying for.

## What Factors Move the Cost Up or Down?

Six factors determine where your job lands in those ranges.

**Water category.** The [IICRC S500 standard](https://www.iicrc.org) classifies water losses into three categories. Category 1 (clean water from a supply line or rain intrusion) is the least expensive to remediate. Category 2 (gray water from appliances, toilet overflow without feces) requires more aggressive sanitization. Category 3 (black water, also called grossly contaminated water, including sewage and floodwater) requires full PPE, antimicrobial treatment, and often complete removal of affected porous materials. Cat 3 jobs cost significantly more because nothing porous that absorbed that water can stay.

**Water class and drying difficulty.** Class 1 means a small area with minimal absorption. Class 4 means water has wicked into specialty materials like hardwood floors, concrete, or plaster that require low-grain refrigerant (LGR) dehumidifiers and extended drying times. Longer drying cycles mean more equipment-days, which adds cost.

**How long the water sat.** Mold can begin colonizing wet materials within 24 to 48 hours according to the [EPA](https://www.epa.gov/mold). A loss caught in the first few hours is a drying job. A loss discovered three days later may also be a mold remediation job, which adds a separate scope and cost.

**Square footage and number of affected rooms.** More area means more equipment, more labor hours, and more material removal.

**Affected materials.** Drying vinyl plank flooring in place costs far less than tearing out hardwood, concrete board, and tile. Drywall that absorbed water for less than 24 hours can sometimes be dried in place with flood cuts. Drywall that absorbed sewage water comes out entirely.

**Structural repairs and reconstruction.** Mitigation (extraction, drying, and demolition of unsalvageable materials) is one scope. Reconstruction (new drywall, flooring, paint, cabinetry) is a separate scope. Many restoration companies, including Crew Restoration & Construction, handle both under one contract, which simplifies the insurance claim process considerably.

## Will Homeowners Insurance Cover This?

Most standard homeowners insurance policies cover sudden, accidental water damage but exclude gradual leaks and flooding from outside the home.

**Typically covered:** Burst pipe, appliance supply line failure, ice dam water intrusion, accidental overflow from a tub or sink, and sudden roof leak from a storm. Your insurer will pay for mitigation and reconstruction minus your deductible.

**Typically not covered:** A slow leak under the sink that went unnoticed for months, sewer backup (unless you added that endorsement), and rising floodwater from outside the home (that requires a separate [National Flood Insurance Program](https://www.fema.gov/flood-insurance) policy or private flood policy).

**The documentation that protects your claim.** Adjusters look for evidence of sudden onset, not long-term neglect. A restoration company that documents moisture readings, photographs the water source, and produces a written scope with line-item pricing gives your adjuster exactly what they need to approve the claim. Crew Restoration & Construction works directly with insurance carriers and handles that documentation as part of the job.

Deductibles in South Dakota typically run $1,000 to $2,500 for water losses. If your damage estimate is close to your deductible, it may make sense to pay out of pocket to avoid a claim on your record. A written scope from a restoration company lets you make that comparison before committing.

## What Does the Water Damage Restoration Process Look Like?

Understanding the steps helps you evaluate any estimate you receive and know what you are actually paying for.

**1. Emergency response and water extraction.** The first priority is stopping the source (if it hasn't been stopped) and removing standing water. Truck-mounted extractors and portable submersible pumps pull hundreds of gallons quickly. This step happens in the first few hours.

**2. Moisture mapping and documentation.** Technicians use thermal imaging cameras and penetrating moisture meters to map how far water traveled through walls, floors, and ceilings. This documentation becomes the baseline for the insurance claim and the drying plan.

**3. Controlled demolition (flood cuts and material removal).** Wet drywall is cut to a few inches above the waterline, a technique called a flood cut, to allow wall cavities to dry. Carpet, pad, and flooring that cannot be dried in place are removed. Category 3 losses require removal of all porous materials that contacted the water.

**4. Structural drying.** Industrial air movers and LGR dehumidifiers run continuously, typically for three to five days. Technicians return daily to take moisture readings and adjust equipment placement. Drying is complete when readings return to pre-loss baselines, not just when surfaces feel dry to the touch. See our post on [how long water damage restoration actually takes](/blog/how-long-does-water-damage-restoration-take/) for a full breakdown.

**5. Antimicrobial treatment.** After drying, affected structural cavities receive EPA-registered antimicrobial treatment to suppress mold growth.

**6. Clearance and documentation.** Final moisture readings are documented and provided to the insurance carrier as proof of completed drying.

**7. Reconstruction.** New drywall, insulation, flooring, paint, and any other removed materials are reinstalled. For Sioux Falls homeowners in neighborhoods like McKennan Park or Cathedral Historic District, where older homes have plaster walls or hardwood floors, reconstruction scope can be more involved than in newer construction.
