## Why Brookings Roofs Take a Harder Hit Than Most

Sitting on the Coteau des Prairies, Brookings sits in the path of unobstructed northwest winds that drive snow horizontally across rooftops for weeks at a time. That wind loading is compounded by temperature swings that can move 40 or 50 degrees in a single day during shoulder seasons — March and November being the worst offenders. Asphalt shingles expand and contract with those swings, and the adhesive strips that seal tab to tab eventually lose their grip. Once a shingle lifts even slightly, wind gets underneath and the damage compounds quickly.

The housing stock near South Dakota State University adds another layer of complexity. Many rental properties and older owner-occupied homes in the SDSU corridor were built in the 1950s through 1970s with lower roof pitches and minimal attic ventilation by today's standards. Poor ventilation traps heat in summer and moisture in winter, shortening shingle life and creating the warm-deck conditions that generate ice dams. A replacement that doesn't also address ventilation is a replacement that will need to happen again too soon.

Every project starts with a full roof inspection — not just the shingles, but the decking, underlayment, ridge and soffit vents, flashing at chimneys and valleys, and the condition of gutters and fascia.
