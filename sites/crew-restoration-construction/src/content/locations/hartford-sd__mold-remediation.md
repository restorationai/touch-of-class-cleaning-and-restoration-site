## Why Hartford Properties See Mold Issues

Hartford is a growing community, and that growth means two very different housing realities sitting side by side. Newer subdivisions built in the last decade were framed with engineered lumber and OSB sheathing — materials that absorb moisture faster than dimensional lumber and can begin supporting mold growth within 24 to 48 hours of a water intrusion. Older homes closer to Downtown Hartford, some with original basement walls and minimal vapor barriers, face a different challenge: moisture migrates up through concrete and masonry slowly and persistently, creating the kind of chronic low-level dampness that feeds surface mold year after year.

The clay-heavy soils common across this stretch of Minnehaha County drain poorly after heavy rain, which means water pools against foundations longer than homeowners expect. A slow weeping crack in a block wall — the kind that only shows up after a two-inch rain — can introduce enough moisture to colonize insulation batts and floor joists without ever producing a visible puddle on the floor.

## Our Mold Remediation Process in Hartford

When Crew Restoration & Construction arrives at a Hartford property, the first step is containment — sealing off the affected area with poly sheeting and establishing negative air pressure so spores cannot migrate to unaffected rooms while work is underway. That matters especially in homes where HVAC systems run continuously; an unsealed return air vent can distribute spores through every room in a house within hours.

After containment, we conduct a thorough moisture mapping of the structure. In Hartford's newer builds, that often means checking OSB roof decking and wall sheathing for elevated moisture readings, not just the obvious wet surfaces. In older homes, we pay close attention to rim joists and sill plates — areas where cold exterior air meets warm interior air and condensation accumulates through South Dakota winters.

Physical removal of contaminated materials comes next: affected drywall, insulation, and porous materials are bagged and removed per EPA guidelines. Structural wood that tests positive but remains structurally sound is treated with an EPA-registered antimicrobial and dried to documented moisture levels before any rebuild begins. We do not consider remediation complete until moisture readings confirm the structure is dry — not just surface-dry.
