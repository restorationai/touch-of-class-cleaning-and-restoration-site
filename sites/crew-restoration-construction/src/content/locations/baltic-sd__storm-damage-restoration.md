## Why Baltic Properties See Elevated Storm Damage Risk

Baltic's position along the Big Sioux River corridor is the defining factor. River-adjacent lots absorb runoff from upstream drainage long before local rain totals would suggest a problem, and the flat topography around Downtown Baltic gives stormwater few natural outlets. That combination makes foundation seepage and crawl space flooding common calls after any significant weather event — not just the catastrophic ones.

The housing stock adds another layer. A meaningful share of Baltic's homes were built in the mid-twentieth century, when roof sheathing was often board-on-board rather than modern OSB panels. Those older assemblies can hold up fine under normal conditions, but a storm that drives wind-blown rain horizontally — something a Great Plains derecho does routinely — can force water through gaps that wouldn't matter in a vertical downpour. Once that moisture reaches the attic insulation, it can sit undetected for days.

Then there are the trees. Mature cottonwoods and elms are common on residential lots throughout the area, and the same spring storms that push the Big Sioux toward flood stage also produce the wind gusts most likely to drop a large limb onto a roof or fence line.

## Our Storm Damage Restoration Process in Baltic

The first thing we do on any storm call is a full exterior and interior assessment before any work begins — photographing every point of entry, every compromised material, and every secondary damage pathway. Insurance adjusters need that documentation, and it protects you if additional damage surfaces during repairs.

For roof and structural breaches, we tarp and board immediately to stop ongoing water intrusion. That step matters more in Baltic than it might in a drier climate because the region's spring and early-summer storm seasons mean a second weather event can arrive within 24 to 48 hours of the first. Leaving an open roof unprotected overnight is a real risk here.

Once the structure is secured, we move to moisture mapping. We use thermal imaging and pin-type moisture meters to trace water migration through wall cavities, subfloor assemblies, and insulation. Older homes in the area sometimes have kraft-faced batt insulation that holds water against the vapor barrier, creating conditions that support mold colonization within 48 to 72 hours if not addressed. We extract standing water, set industrial drying equipment, and monitor readings daily until materials reach target moisture content.

Tree and debris removal is coordinated as part of the same scope — we don't leave homeowners managing two separate contractors when the roof damage and the fallen limb are part of the same event.
