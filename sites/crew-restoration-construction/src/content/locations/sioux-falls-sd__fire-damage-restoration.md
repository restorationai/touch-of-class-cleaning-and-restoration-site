## Why Sioux Falls Properties Face Distinct Fire Damage Challenges

Sioux Falls has a housing stock that spans more than a century. The Cathedral Historic District and the blocks surrounding All Saints are filled with pre-1940s construction — balloon-frame walls, original plaster-and-lath ceilings, and wood-lathe cavities that act like chimneys during a fire, pulling smoke and heat vertically through the entire structure before occupants even know there's a problem. That framing style means fire can travel farther, faster, and less visibly than in modern platform-frame construction.

On top of the older building stock, Sioux Falls winters regularly push temperatures below zero. When firefighters suppress a blaze in January or February, the standing water they leave behind can begin freezing inside wall cavities within hours. That secondary damage — burst pipes, frost-heaved subfloors, ice dams forming at exterior sheathing — compounds the original fire loss significantly. Restoration work in this climate has to account for both the fire and the freeze simultaneously.

## Our Fire Damage Restoration Process in Sioux Falls

Every fire loss is different, but the sequence of work follows a proven framework calibrated to what we see here on the northern plains.

**Board-up and stabilization** comes first. Open windows, collapsed rooflines, and compromised doors expose the structure to the elements — a critical concern when temperatures in the 57105 and 57104 ZIP codes can swing 40 degrees overnight in shoulder seasons. We secure the envelope before anything else.

**Soot and smoke mapping** comes next. Soot is not uniform — dry soot from fast-burning synthetic materials behaves differently than the oily, wet soot produced by slow-smoldering upholstery or insulation. We identify the soot type in each room because the wrong cleaning chemistry will set stains permanently rather than lift them.

**Structural drying and dehumidification** runs concurrently with smoke remediation. The water introduced during suppression needs to be extracted and dried to IICRC S500 standards before reconstruction begins — skipping this step leads to mold colonization, which in Sioux Falls's humid summer months can begin in as little as 48 to 72 hours.

**Odor neutralization** is the final technical hurdle. Thermal fogging and hydroxyl generation are both used depending on the materials involved. In homes with original plaster walls — common in older Sioux Falls neighborhoods — odor molecules penetrate more deeply than in drywall, and neutralization typically requires an additional treatment cycle.

**Reconstruction** closes the job. As a licensed construction firm, we handle structural repairs, drywall, cabinetry, and finish work in-house, so property owners deal with one point of contact from emergency response through final walkthrough.

Fire losses generate some of the most complex insurance claims in residential restoration. Adjusters working Sioux Falls claims will typically require a scope of loss document, a line-item estimate in Xactimate format, and photographic evidence of damage before and after each phase of work.
