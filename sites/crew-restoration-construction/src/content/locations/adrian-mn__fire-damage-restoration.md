## Why Adrian Properties See Particular Fire Damage Challenges

Adrian's housing stock tells a story in wood and plaster. Much of the residential construction along the older blocks near St. Adrian Catholic Church dates to the mid-20th century or earlier, meaning balloon-frame construction is common. In a balloon-frame house, wall cavities run uninterrupted from the basement sill plate to the roof rafters — a design that lets smoke, heat, and water travel vertically through the entire structure with almost no resistance. What looks like a contained kitchen fire can leave soot deposits two floors away and smoke odor embedded in attic insulation the homeowner never thought to check.

Southwest Minnesota's climate adds another layer of complexity. Winters along the I-90 corridor push temperatures well below zero, and when a fire occurs in January or February, the suppression water that saturates framing and insulation can begin to freeze inside wall cavities before a restoration crew finishes the initial assessment. Frozen moisture doesn't evaporate — it expands, stresses framing joints, and delays the drying phase significantly. Restoration work in Adrian during winter months requires heated drying equipment and a sequencing plan that accounts for ambient temperatures, not just moisture readings.

## Our Fire Damage Restoration Process in Adrian

Every job starts with a systematic walk-through — not a quick visual scan, but a room-by-room evaluation that documents structural damage, maps smoke migration paths, and identifies water intrusion from suppression efforts. We photograph and log everything before a single piece of debris moves, because that documentation becomes the foundation of your insurance claim.

Once the scope is clear, the work moves in a deliberate sequence:

- **Board-up and tarping** to secure the structure against weather and unauthorized entry
- **Debris removal and selective demolition** of materials too compromised to restore
- **Soot and smoke residue cleaning** using dry-chemical sponges, HEPA vacuuming, and chemical sponging calibrated to the surface — painted drywall, bare wood, plaster, and brick each respond differently
- **Odor neutralization** through thermal fogging and hydroxyl generation, which reach into wall cavities and ductwork where surface cleaning can't
- **Structural drying** with industrial dehumidifiers and air movers, monitored daily with moisture meters until readings return to baseline
- **Reconstruction** of damaged framing, drywall, flooring, and finishes — handled by the same crew, so you're not coordinating between a restoration company and a separate contractor

For homes near East Adrian where lot sizes are tighter and neighbor structures are closer, we pay particular attention to exterior siding and eave vents, which can pull smoke residue into adjacent attic spaces during a fire event.

## Local Note

Older homes in Adrian with original plaster-and-lath interior walls present a specific challenge after a fire: plaster is dense and non-porous compared to modern drywall, so it doesn't absorb smoke odor as readily on the surface — but the wood lath behind it does. Homeowners sometimes assume a plaster wall that wipes clean is odor-free, only to notice the smell returning weeks later as temperatures rise. We probe behind plaster surfaces with moisture meters and, when necessary, remove small sections of lath to assess odor penetration before closing up walls.
