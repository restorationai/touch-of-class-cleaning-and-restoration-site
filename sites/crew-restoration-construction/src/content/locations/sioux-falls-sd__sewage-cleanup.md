When a sewer line backs up or a septic system overflows in Sioux Falls, the damage clock starts immediately — and in a city where spring snowmelt and heavy clay soils push groundwater toward aging municipal laterals every March and April, that clock moves fast. Raw sewage carries Category 3 "black water" contamination: bacteria, viruses, and pathogens that soak into subfloor assemblies and wall cavities within hours. Crew Restoration & Construction responds to sewage backups across the Sioux Falls metro, from the older bungalows near McKennan Park to newer construction in Prairie Green, with the goal of stopping contamination spread before it compounds the cost and the health risk.

## Why Sioux Falls Properties See Sewage Backup Problems

Sioux Falls sits on a mix of glacial till and expansive clay soils that shift significantly with freeze-thaw cycles. That movement stresses buried sewer laterals — the pipes connecting a home to the city main — especially in neighborhoods where those laterals were installed decades ago using clay tile or Orangeburg pipe. The Cathedral Historic District and the All Saints neighborhood both have significant concentrations of pre-1960 housing stock where original clay tile laterals are still in service. When those pipes crack, root-intrude, or collapse, sewage has nowhere to go but back through the lowest floor drain.

The city's combined sewer infrastructure in older ZIP codes like 57103 and 57104 can also surcharge during heavy rain events — meaning stormwater and sanitary flow share the same pipe, and when that pipe fills, it reverses. Homeowners often discover the problem as a slow gurgle in a basement floor drain before the full backup occurs. That early warning matters: the faster extraction begins, the less porous concrete, insulation, and wood framing absorbs.

## Our Sewage Cleanup and Sanitization Process in Sioux Falls

Sewage cleanup is not the same as water damage mitigation, and the process reflects that. Every job starts with a containment assessment — identifying how far contaminated water traveled and whether it reached HVAC returns, wall cavities, or subfloor insulation. In finished basements, which are common across Sioux Falls neighborhoods, drywall that wicked sewage even a few inches above the waterline typically requires removal because standard drying equipment cannot eliminate the pathogen load inside the material.

Once the source is confirmed controlled (by a plumber, if the line hasn't already been cleared), extraction of standing sewage begins using truck-mounted or portable extraction units. All removed material — saturated carpet, pad, insulation, drywall — is bagged, documented, and disposed of in accordance with South Dakota Department of Agriculture and Natural Resources guidelines for Category 3 waste. After physical removal, affected surfaces are treated with EPA-registered disinfectants appropriate for sewage contamination, applied in the correct dwell times to be effective. Drying equipment then runs for a monitored period, with moisture readings logged daily until structural materials return to pre-loss baselines.

## Insurance Coordination for Sioux Falls Sewage Claims

Sewer backup coverage is a separate endorsement from standard homeowners insurance in South Dakota — many Sioux Falls homeowners discover this for the first time when they file a claim. If you carry the endorsement, Crew Restoration & Construction documents the loss thoroughly: photographs of contamination extent, moisture readings before and after, a detailed scope of removed materials, and a written estimate formatted for adjuster review. We communicate directly with your carrier throughout the process so you are not translating between restoration terminology and insurance language on your own.

If you are not sure whether your policy includes sewer backup coverage, your declarations page will list it as a rider or endorsement — typically described as "water backup and sump overflow." It is worth confirming before you need it.

## Local Note: Clay Soil and Lateral Pipe Age in Older Sioux Falls Neighborhoods

One pattern that comes up repeatedly on jobs in the older core neighborhoods of Sioux Falls — particularly around McKennan Park and the Cathedral Historic District — is that the sewer lateral has already failed or is partially collapsed before the backup ever happens. Homeowners assume the blockage is a clog, a plumber snakes it, and it backs up again six months later. The real issue is a structurally compromised pipe that will keep failing. On sewage cleanup jobs where we see repeated backup history or where the backup was unusually severe relative to the reported cause, we flag it for the homeowner and recommend a camera inspection of the lateral before we close out the job. That conversation is not in our scope of work — but skipping it means the same basement gets contaminated again.
