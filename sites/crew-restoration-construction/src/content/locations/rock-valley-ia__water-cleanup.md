## Our Water Cleanup Process in Rock Valley

Every job starts with a thorough moisture assessment. Technicians use thermal imaging cameras and calibrated moisture meters to map where water has traveled, not just where it's visible. In Rock Valley homes with finished basements, water often migrates horizontally under flooring before showing up at the walls, so surface-level inspection isn't enough.

Once the scope is mapped, extraction comes first. Truck-mounted and portable extraction units pull standing water and saturated material from carpet, pad, and subfloor. After bulk water is removed, the drying phase begins: industrial air movers and refrigerant or desiccant dehumidifiers are positioned based on the moisture map, not placed at random. Readings are logged daily so drying progress is documented and verifiable, something your insurance adjuster will want to see.

For Rock Valley properties with older plaster walls or tongue-and-groove subfloor construction, drying timelines can run longer than in newer builds. Plaster releases moisture more slowly than modern drywall, and dense wood subfloor assemblies hold water deep in the grain. Technicians account for this when setting drying targets rather than pulling equipment prematurely.
