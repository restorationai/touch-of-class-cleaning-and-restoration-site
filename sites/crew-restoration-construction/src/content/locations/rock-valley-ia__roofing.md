## Why Rock Valley Roofs Wear Out Faster Than You'd Expect

Sioux County averages roughly 30 inches of annual precipitation, but the real stress on residential roofing here isn't rain — it's the freeze-thaw cycle that runs from November through March. Water that seeps under lifted shingles or compromised flashing freezes overnight, expands, and pries the substrate apart from the inside. By spring, what looked like a minor repair job in October has become a decking replacement. Homes along the low-lying stretches near the Rock River are especially vulnerable: the Rock River's documented flooding history means some properties have dealt with repeated moisture intrusion from below and above simultaneously, accelerating rot in roof sheathing that was already stressed by thermal cycling. Older homes in and around Downtown Rock Valley — many built in the mid-20th century with original wood decking — are particularly susceptible because that decking has had decades to absorb seasonal moisture before modern underlayment standards existed.

## Our Roofing Installation and Replacement Process in Rock Valley

Every project starts with a documented inspection — not a walk-around estimate, but a systematic evaluation of the decking, underlayment, flashing at every penetration point, ridge and soffit ventilation, and the condition of the existing shingle layer. Once the old material is stripped, we assess the decking for soft spots, delamination, or prior water damage. In Rock Valley's climate, it's not unusual to find sections of OSB decking that have swelled and separated at the edges — those sections get replaced before anything goes back on top. Ice-and-water shield goes along the eaves and in every valley — Iowa code requires it at the eaves, and we extend it further in areas where ice damming is a realistic risk.

Shingle selection matters here. We carry architectural shingles rated to 130 mph wind uplift, which is the appropriate specification for open-country Iowa exposure. Ridge cap, drip edge, and all metal flashing are installed to current IRC standards, and every penetration — pipe boots, skylights, chimney saddles — gets flashed and sealed individually rather than relying on caulk alone.
