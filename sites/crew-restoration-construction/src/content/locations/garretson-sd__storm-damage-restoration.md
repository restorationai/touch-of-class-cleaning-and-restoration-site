## Why Garretson Properties See Serious Storm Damage

Garretson's geography creates a specific vulnerability. The Sioux quartzite bluffs at Palisades State Park are beautiful, but the creek corridors and low-lying areas near Devil's Gulch funnel storm runoff quickly. Properties close to Split Rock Creek can see water intrusion within minutes of a heavy rain event — not hours. Combine that with the region's exposure to Great Plains supercell thunderstorms, which can produce baseball-sized hail and wind gusts exceeding 80 mph, and the damage profile here is more severe than what you'd see in a sheltered suburban neighborhood.

The housing stock in and around Downtown Garretson adds another layer of complexity. Many homes date to the early-to-mid 1900s, meaning original wood framing, older roofing substrates, and in some cases cedar or redwood siding that responds differently to hail impact than modern vinyl. A storm that leaves a newer build with cosmetic dents can leave an older home with compromised structural sheathing, cracked window glazing, and water infiltration that doesn't show up until the next rain.

## Our Storm Damage Restoration Process in Garretson

Every job starts with a thorough damage assessment — not a quick walk-around, but a systematic inspection of the roof deck, attic, exterior walls, windows, and foundation perimeter. We photograph and document every affected area before a single nail is pulled, because that documentation is what drives a fair insurance settlement.

From there, the process follows a clear sequence:

- **Emergency stabilization** — tarping open roof sections, boarding broken windows, and removing hanging tree limbs or debris that pose an immediate hazard.
- **Water extraction and drying** — if storm water entered the structure, we deploy truck-mounted extractors and industrial drying equipment to bring moisture levels down before mold has a chance to establish. In South Dakota's humid summer storm season, mold can begin colonizing wet materials within 24 to 48 hours.
- **Structural and envelope repair** — roof decking, rafters, wall framing, and exterior cladding are repaired or replaced to match existing materials and local building codes.
- **Interior restoration** — drywall, insulation, flooring, and finish work brought back to pre-loss condition.

Because we handle both restoration and construction under one roof, there's no handoff gap between the mitigation crew and the rebuild crew — the same team sees the job through.

## Insurance Coordination for Garretson Storm Claims

Storm damage claims in South Dakota are among the most contested in residential insurance — carriers often dispute whether hail damage meets the threshold for a full roof replacement versus a patch repair. Our documentation process is built around that reality. ## Local Note

One thing that catches homeowners near the Palisades area off guard: the quartzite bedrock that makes this part of South Dakota geologically unique also means soil drainage behaves differently than it does on the clay-heavy soils further west in Sioux Falls. During a heavy storm event, water that can't percolate through the rock shelf tends to sheet across the surface and collect against foundation walls. We've seen basement water intrusion in Garretson homes that showed no obvious roof or window damage — the entry point was a foundation crack that only became active under the hydrostatic pressure of a fast-moving surface flood. If you had standing water in your basement after a storm, it's worth having the foundation perimeter inspected even if the roof looks intact.
