Dakota Dunes sits at the confluence of the Missouri and Big Sioux Rivers, and that geography does things to exterior cladding and drainage systems that homeowners in drier parts of South Dakota rarely encounter. The river-bottom humidity, the freeze-thaw cycles that arrive fast and hard each November, and the residual soil instability left behind by the 2011 Missouri River flood all conspire to work siding loose, buckle trim boards, and overwhelm gutters that were sized for a drier climate. Crew Restoration & Construction handles siding and gutter work specifically in the 57049 corridor, and we understand what this particular stretch of the Missouri valley demands from an exterior.

## Why Dakota Dunes Properties See Siding and Gutter Problems Earlier Than Expected

The housing stock in Dakota Dunes skews newer and upscale — think vinyl and fiber cement on homes built in the 1990s and 2000s — but age is only part of the equation. The bigger driver is moisture load. Properties near The Dunes golf community and along the Two Rivers area sit on ground that stays saturated longer than the surrounding uplands. When that ground moisture wicks upward and combines with wind-driven rain off the Missouri, it attacks the bottom courses of siding first: the J-channel fills with debris, water backs up behind the cladding, and by the time a homeowner notices bubbling or soft spots, the sheathing underneath has already started to degrade.

Gutters compound the problem. A standard 5-inch K-style gutter is undersized for the roof pitches common on larger homes in Dakota Dunes. During a heavy Missouri River valley storm — the kind that drops two inches in under an hour — those gutters overflow before the downspout can clear the volume, sending water directly against the foundation and the lower siding band. Upgrading to 6-inch seamless gutters with properly spaced hangers makes a measurable difference in how long the siding below them lasts.

## Our Siding and Gutter Process in Dakota Dunes

Every project starts with a full exterior assessment, not just the section that looks damaged. On Dakota Dunes homes, that means checking the flashing at every penetration point — windows, doors, hose bibs, electrical boxes — because river-valley wind direction shifts enough that water intrusion often shows up on the side of the house a homeowner least expects. We photograph existing conditions before we touch anything, which matters later if an insurance or HOA review comes into play.

For siding replacement, we remove the damaged courses, inspect the housewrap or felt paper underneath, and replace any compromised sheathing before new material goes on. We match profiles and colors as closely as current manufacturer lines allow — a practical concern in a community where HOA aesthetic standards are taken seriously. For gutter installation, we fabricate seamless runs on-site from coil stock, which eliminates the joints where leaks typically start. Downspout extensions are routed to discharge at least six feet from the foundation, a step that matters especially on lots where the 2011 flood left subsurface drainage patterns that haven't fully normalized.

## Dakota Dunes HOA and Insurance Coordination

The Dakota Dunes Country Club area and surrounding planned sections of the community operate under HOA covenants that govern exterior color palettes, material types, and in some cases the profile of gutters visible from the street. Before ordering materials, we review any applicable HOA guidelines with the homeowner and, when needed, submit the product specifications for pre-approval. That step prevents the frustration of completing a job only to receive a compliance notice afterward.

When storm damage is the trigger for siding or gutter work, the project often involves a property insurance claim. We document the damage with photos and measurements in a format that adjusters can use directly, and we're straightforward about what is storm-related versus what is normal wear — because conflating the two creates problems down the line for the homeowner.
