## Why Alton Properties See Accelerated Roofing Wear

Sioux County sits in a climate zone that delivers both extremes: summer hailstorms that can strip granules from asphalt shingles in a single event, and January wind chills that make brittle shingles crack during installation if a contractor isn't paying attention to temperature thresholds. The Floyd River lowlands around Alton also create localized humidity gradients — moisture that rises from the river bottom can accelerate attic condensation, which in turn degrades roof decking from the inside out long before the surface shows visible damage.

Older homes near Downtown Alton and around St. Mary's Catholic Church tend to carry original roof decking that is narrower-cut lumber rather than modern OSB panels. That older wood can be sound, but it also hides rot in ways that a quick visual inspection misses. A proper replacement job here means probing the decking once shingles are stripped, not just laying new material over what's already compromised.

## Our Roofing Installation and Replacement Process in Alton

Every project starts with a full roof inspection — ridge to soffit, including attic ventilation — before a single shingle is quoted. Ventilation problems are common in Sioux County farmhouses and in the compact residential lots of East Alton, and installing new roofing without correcting airflow just repeats the same failure cycle.

Once scope is confirmed, the process runs in a deliberate sequence:

1. **Full tear-off** of existing layers (Iowa code limits the number of shingle layers that can be stacked, and we follow that limit rather than skip it for speed).
2. **Decking inspection and repair** — any soft spots, delaminated OSB, or rotted boards are replaced before underlayment goes down.
3. **Ice-and-water shield installation** along all eaves and valleys, critical given northwest Iowa's ice-dam exposure.
4. **Synthetic underlayment** over the field of the roof for a secondary moisture barrier.
5. **Shingle installation** per manufacturer specs, including proper nail placement for wind-resistance ratings — a detail that matters when a Sioux County storm rolls through.
6. **Flashing, ridge cap, and ventilation finishing** — the components most often done poorly on rushed jobs.

Materials are selected for the local climate: impact-resistant shingles rated Class 3 or Class 4 are worth the modest cost premium in a hail-prone corridor like this one.
