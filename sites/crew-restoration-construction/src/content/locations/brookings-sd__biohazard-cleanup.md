## Why Brookings Properties Present Unique Biohazard Considerations

The housing stock around Brookings reflects decades of growth tied closely to SDSU's expansion. Older rental homes in the neighborhoods surrounding the university — many built in the 1960s and 1970s — often feature porous concrete block foundations, original hardwood subfloors, and uninsulated crawl spaces. These materials are far less forgiving than modern construction when contamination occurs: porous surfaces absorb biological material quickly, and without thorough remediation, odor and microbial risk can persist long after visible cleanup is done.

South Dakota's climate adds another layer of complexity. Brookings winters regularly push temperatures well below zero, which affects how biological material behaves in unheated spaces — a garage, a detached storage unit, or a basement that lost heat during a cold snap. Frozen conditions can slow decomposition but do not eliminate contamination risk, and the thaw cycle in March and April can reactivate odors in materials that seemed stable during winter. Any remediation plan here has to account for seasonal conditions, not just the immediate situation.

## Our Biohazard Cleanup Process in Brookings

Every job begins with a confidential assessment. A project manager reviews the affected area, identifies the scope of contamination, and outlines a remediation plan before any work begins. Affected materials — flooring, drywall, insulation, personal items — are evaluated individually. What can be safely restored is documented; what must be removed is bagged, sealed, and transported according to South Dakota Department of Environment and Natural Resources guidelines for infectious waste disposal.

Decontamination follows a structured sequence: containment of the affected zone, removal of porous materials that cannot be fully cleaned, application of hospital-grade disinfectants to hard surfaces, and HEPA-filtered air scrubbing to capture airborne particulates. For properties with older construction — the kind common in the Sexauer Park area or along the older residential corridors near Downtown Brookings — we pay particular attention to subfloor assemblies and wall cavities, where contamination can wick into materials that look unaffected from the surface.

Throughout the process, the property is kept as private as possible. Unmarked vehicles, plain packaging for removed materials, and direct communication only with the authorized contact are standard practice — not an upgrade.
