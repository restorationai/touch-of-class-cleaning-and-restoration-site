## Why Flandreau Properties See More Siding and Gutter Wear

Flandreau sits in Moody County, where temperature swings between January lows and July highs can exceed 100°F across a single year. That thermal range is punishing on exterior cladding. Vinyl panels expand and contract with every cycle; when they were installed without adequate expansion gaps — common on homes built in the 1970s and 1980s — the panels buckle or crack at the seams, and water finds its way behind the wall assembly before the damage is visible from the street.

Gutters face a related problem. Cottonwood and elm trees are common throughout the older residential blocks near Downtown Flandreau, and their seed pods and small leaf litter compact into a paste that standard 4-inch gutters can't shed on their own. When that paste sits through a freeze, it forms an ice dam at the fascia line — backing water up under the first course of siding and into the soffit. Upgrading to 5- or 6-inch K-style gutters with leaf guards is often the most cost-effective long-term fix for properties in this part of town.

## Our Siding and Gutter Process in Flandreau

Every project starts with a visual inspection of the full exterior envelope — not just the section that's obviously failing. On older Flandreau homes, a damaged panel on the south face often signals that the north face has been holding moisture longer, because the north side dries more slowly through the short South Dakota sun window in winter.

From there, the process follows a clear sequence:

1. **Remove and document** — existing siding or gutters come off carefully so we can photograph the sheathing and fascia condition underneath. If we find rotted OSB or compromised housewrap, we address it before anything new goes on.
2. **Substrate repair** — any sheathing damage, rotted fascia board, or deteriorated flashing gets replaced with materials rated for the regional climate.
3. **Installation** — siding panels are installed with manufacturer-specified expansion gaps for South Dakota's temperature range. Gutters are pitched and hung to drain toward downspouts positioned to move water away from the foundation, which matters especially on lots with clay-heavy soil common in this part of the Big Sioux River valley.
4. **Final inspection and cleanup** — we walk the perimeter with the property owner before we leave the site.
