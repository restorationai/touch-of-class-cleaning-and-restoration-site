## Our Emergency Water Cleanup Process in Kingsley

When a call comes in, the first priority is stopping the source. If a supply line has failed, that means locating the shutoff. If the water is storm-related, it means understanding where it is still moving before equipment goes in. From there, the process follows a clear sequence.

Standing water is extracted first using truck-mounted and portable extraction units capable of pulling hundreds of gallons from flooring, carpet, and crawl spaces. Once bulk water is removed, moisture mapping begins. Technicians use thermal imaging cameras and calibrated moisture meters to trace water migration behind walls, under subfloors, and into insulation cavities, areas where the surface can feel dry while saturated material continues to degrade underneath.

Structural drying follows, using industrial air movers and desiccant or refrigerant dehumidifiers sized for the affected area. Drying logs are recorded daily so the process is documentable for insurance purposes. Antimicrobial treatment is applied to surfaces where organic material and moisture have combined long enough to create biological risk. Nothing is closed up until readings confirm materials have returned to acceptable moisture levels.

## Local Note

Homes near Kingsley-Pierson School and along the older residential streets of Downtown Kingsley frequently have full or partial basements with poured concrete or block walls from the 1940s through 1960s. Block-wall basements are particularly prone to moisture migration through the mortar joints during sustained wet periods, water does not always enter as a visible stream but rather seeps through the entire wall face. Standard extraction handles the standing water, but drying a block-wall basement takes longer than a poured-concrete one because the blocks themselves hold moisture and release it slowly into the air space. Crews working in these homes set dehumidifiers for extended run times and check readings more frequently rather than assuming a single drying cycle is sufficient.
