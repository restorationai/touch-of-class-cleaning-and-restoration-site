## Why Parker Properties Face Particular Board-Up and Tarping Challenges

Parker's housing stock tells a story in wood and brick. Many homes within a few blocks of Downtown Parker were built before World War II, which means balloon-frame construction, older window openings sized for single-pane glass, and rooflines that weren't engineered to current wind-load standards. When a window breaks or a roof section lifts, the openings are often irregular — not the standard 36" × 48" cutouts that fit off-the-shelf plywood panels cleanly. Securing those openings correctly takes field measurement and on-site cutting rather than a grab-and-go approach.

Turner County also sits in a region where late-spring and early-summer storm seasons overlap with the Turner County Fairgrounds area's peak activity calendar. South Dakota's temperature swings add another layer. A tarp installed without proper ballasting and edge sealing in July can become a sail in an August thunderstorm. In winter, a board-up that doesn't account for snow load on a compromised roof can accelerate structural failure. Every installation we do is calibrated to what the weather is likely to do next, not just what it did yesterday.

## Our Emergency Board-Up and Tarping Process in Parker

When we arrive at a Parker property, the first ten minutes are spent on a structured walk-around — roof, foundation perimeter, every exterior wall — before a single sheet of plywood goes up. That assessment determines the sequence: which openings allow wind-driven rain to reach the interior fastest, whether the roof deck is stable enough for workers to move on, and whether any utility lines near the damage zone need to be flagged before we stage equipment.

For **roof tarping**, we use reinforced polyethylene tarps rated for high-wind exposure, anchored with lumber battens screwed through the tarp into the decking rather than simply weighted at the edges. On older Parker homes where the decking may be 1×6 board sheathing rather than plywood, we probe for soft spots before committing to anchor points.

For **fire damage board-up** and **broken window board-up**, we cut OSB panels to the exact rough opening, fasten them with structural screws at code-compliant spacing, and apply foam backer rod at the perimeter to limit air infiltration. Panels are labeled on the interior face with the opening dimensions so your contractor or adjuster can document them accurately.

We photograph every opening before and after securing it, and we provide that photo documentation directly to you and to your insurance carrier's adjuster — a step that matters when the claim timeline stretches across weeks.

## Local Note

Something worth knowing if you own an older home in the blocks around Downtown Parker: the original window frames in pre-1950 construction were often set with lime mortar or early caulking compounds that have long since failed. When we remove broken glass to board up a window, we sometimes find that the frame itself is no longer square — the opening has racked slightly over decades of settling. A standard rectangular panel won't seat flush, which creates gaps that let in wind-driven rain even with the board nominally "in place." Our crews carry a speed square and a hand plane on every board-up job specifically because of situations like this. It takes an extra fifteen minutes, but it's the difference between a board-up that holds through the next storm and one that doesn't.
