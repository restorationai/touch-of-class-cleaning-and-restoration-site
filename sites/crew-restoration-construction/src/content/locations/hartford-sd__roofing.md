## Why Hartford Roofs Wear Out Faster Than the Calendar Suggests

The housing stock in and around Downtown Hartford skews toward homes built in the post-interstate expansion era of the 1970s and 1980s, when three-tab asphalt shingles were the default choice. Those shingles were rated for 20–25 years under moderate conditions. On a south- or west-facing slope in Hartford, where afternoon sun bakes the surface and northwest winds scrape across it from October through March, that lifespan compresses. Granule loss accelerates, the fiberglass mat becomes brittle, and the first significant hail event — and Minnehaha County averages several each season — can punch through to the decking in spots that are invisible from the ground.

The Buffalo Ridge area on the western edge of town adds another variable: slight elevation changes that funnel wind differently than the flatter parcels closer to the school district. Homes on those lots tend to see faster wear on the windward rake edges and ridge caps, which are often the last places a homeowner looks when doing a visual check from the driveway.

## Our Roofing Installation and Replacement Process in Hartford

Every project starts with a full decking inspection, not just a shingle count. Once old material is stripped, the crew walks every sheet of OSB or plywood for soft spots, delamination, or areas where ice-dam moisture has worked into the wood over multiple winters. In Hartford's climate, it's common to find two or three sheets that need replacement even on a roof that looked structurally sound from the attic. Those are replaced before anything else goes down.

From there, the installation sequence runs: ice-and-water shield along all eaves and valleys (critical given the freeze-thaw cycles here), synthetic underlayment across the field, new drip edge, and then the shingle system itself. For most Hartford residential projects, we recommend architectural shingles rated at a minimum 130 mph wind uplift — a step up from the 110 mph rating common on builder-grade products, and a meaningful difference when a derecho moves through the corridor. Ridge ventilation is set to manufacturer specs to prevent the attic heat buildup that ages shingles from the underside.

One pattern worth knowing: homes in the Buffalo Ridge area that were built on crawl spaces rather than full basements sometimes have inadequate attic ventilation because the original builders assumed the open crawl would provide enough air movement through the structure.
