## Why Harrisburg's Rapid Growth Creates Specific Fire Damage Challenges

Harriston is one of the fastest-growing communities south of Sioux Falls, and that growth has a direct effect on how fire damage unfolds here. The majority of homes in neighborhoods like Journey and Willow Run were built within the last fifteen years, which means construction relies heavily on engineered wood products — LVL beams, OSB sheathing, and I-joists — rather than the solid-sawn lumber found in older Sioux Falls neighborhoods. Engineered lumber loses structural integrity faster under fire exposure than dimensional lumber does. A beam that looks intact from the outside may be compromised at its core, which is why structural assessment has to happen before any restoration work begins.

Harriston also sits in a climate zone where South Dakota winters push interior heating systems to work hard for five or six months of the year. Forced-air furnaces and ductwork that run continuously are efficient at distributing heat — and equally efficient at pulling smoke particulate through every room of the house during and after a fire event. By the time a homeowner calls us, the soot has often traveled far beyond the room of origin.

## Our Fire Damage Restoration Process in Harrisburg

The first step on any fire loss is a thorough walk-through to separate what can be restored from what has to be replaced. We document everything photographically — not just for your records, but because insurance carriers require detailed line-item documentation before they release funds. Once the scope is set, the work moves in a deliberate sequence:

**Structural stabilization** comes first. If the fire compromised roof decking, exterior walls, or load-bearing framing, we board up or tarp the structure to prevent weather intrusion — a real concern in Harrisburg winters when an open roof can allow snow and ice to compound the damage overnight.

**Debris and soot removal** follows. Dry soot from fast-burning fires behaves differently than the oily, wet residue left by slow smoldering fires. We match the cleaning chemistry to the residue type rather than applying a single method across the board.

**Odor neutralization** is often the longest phase. Thermal fogging and hydroxyl generation reach into wall cavities and ductwork where surface cleaning cannot. On homes with forced-air systems — the standard in Harrisburg's newer builds — we clean or replace duct runs before clearing the job as complete.

**Reconstruction** closes out the project. Because Crew Restoration & Construction handles both mitigation and rebuild, there is no handoff gap between the team pulling out damaged drywall and the crew hanging new material.
