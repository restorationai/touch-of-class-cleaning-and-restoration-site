## Why Pipestone Properties Face Distinct Board-Up and Tarping Challenges

Pipestone sits at the edge of the Coteau des Prairies, where unobstructed winds regularly exceed 30 mph and winter temperature swings of 40 degrees in a single day are common. That kind of thermal stress is hard on roofing materials, window glazing, and exterior cladding — especially on homes built before modern building codes. Many properties in and around the 56164 ZIP code were constructed in the early 1900s, with wood-framed windows, original plaster interiors, and aging rooflines that were never designed to shed the volume of ice and snow a bad February can deliver.

The Downtown Pipestone Historic District adds another layer of complexity. The area's signature Sioux quartzite construction is extraordinarily durable, but the mortar joints, wooden window surrounds, and interior plaster in those buildings are far more vulnerable than the stone itself. A fire or storm that breaches one of those structures needs a board-up response that accounts for irregular window openings, heavy masonry surrounds, and the preservation standards that apply to historically significant properties. Standard plywood cuts and generic tarp throws aren't enough here.

## Our Board-Up and Tarping Process, Calibrated for Pipestone

When we arrive at a Pipestone property, the first priority is a rapid structural read — identifying every opening that is exposing the interior to weather or creating a security risk. That means broken windows, compromised doors, roof penetrations from fire or impact, and any soffits or fascia that have been displaced by wind.

For roof tarping, we use reinforced polyethylene tarps weighted and anchored with lumber battens rather than simple tie-downs. In Pipestone's wind environment, a tarp secured only at its grommets will fail in the first strong gust. Battens distribute load across the tarp surface and keep it from lifting at the edges, which is where water infiltration almost always starts.

For window and door board-up, we cut plywood panels to fit each opening precisely, using exterior-grade fasteners that won't pull out of aging wood framing. On historic or ornate window surrounds — the kind you find near the Historic Calumet Inn or on older residential blocks downtown — we take care not to damage decorative trim that would be expensive or impossible to replicate. We photograph every opening before and after boarding so your insurance adjuster has a clear record of pre-existing conditions versus storm or fire damage.

One thing that catches out-of-area crews off guard in Pipestone is how many homes and commercial buildings have low-slope or nearly flat roof sections — a design common in this part of Minnesota where rooftop snow loads were once managed by shoveling rather than steep pitch.
