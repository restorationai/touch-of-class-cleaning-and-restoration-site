## Why Rock Valley Properties Face Distinct Fire Damage Challenges

The housing stock along and near Downtown Rock Valley skews older — many homes date to the 1940s through 1960s, when balloon-frame construction was standard. In a balloon-frame house, wall cavities run continuously from the foundation sill to the roof line with no fire blocking between floors. That means smoke and heat travel vertically through the structure far faster than in modern platform-frame homes, pushing soot and pyrolysis gases into attic spaces and upper-floor finishes even when the fire originated in a basement or first-floor kitchen.

Northwest Iowa winters add another layer of complexity. After a fire, the structure is often open to outdoor air — broken windows, cut roof vents, holes made by the fire department for ventilation. In January or February, temperatures in Sioux County regularly drop below 0°F. Residual moisture from fire suppression can freeze inside wall assemblies and beneath flooring before a restoration crew arrives to extract it, creating secondary damage that looks like a water loss on top of the fire loss. Coordinating board-up, temporary heat, and moisture extraction in the right sequence matters enormously here.

## Our Fire Damage Restoration Process in Rock Valley

The first priority on any fire loss is stabilization. That means boarding or tarping openings, documenting the full extent of damage with photos and moisture readings, and establishing a safe perimeter so the structural assessment can happen without further hazard. Crew Restoration & Construction works through a documented scope before any demolition begins — that scope drives the insurance claim and prevents disputes later.

Once the structure is stable, the work moves through three overlapping phases:

**Debris removal and selective demolition.** Charred materials that cannot be restored are removed carefully, with attention to what lies behind them. In older Rock Valley homes, that often means encountering knob-and-tube wiring, asbestos-containing floor tile, or original horsehair plaster — materials that require specific handling protocols before they can be disturbed.

**Smoke and soot cleaning.** Dry soot from fast-burning fires behaves differently than the wet, oily residue left by slow smoldering fires. The cleaning chemistry and technique — dry sponging, chemical sponging, alkaline cleaners, thermal fogging — is chosen based on the residue type, not a one-size approach. HVAC ductwork, attic insulation, and subfloor cavities are inspected because soot migrates far beyond the visible burn zone.

**Odor neutralization and structural drying.** Smoke odor that isn't fully addressed at the molecular level will return when humidity rises — a real concern in a region that sees humid summers along the Rock River corridor. Thermal fogging and hydroxyl generation are used in combination with structural drying equipment to address both odor and residual moisture simultaneously.
