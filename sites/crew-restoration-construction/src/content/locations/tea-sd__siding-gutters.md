## Why Tea Properties See Siding and Gutter Issues

The climate southwest of Sioux Falls is unforgiving on building exteriors. Tea sits in an exposed stretch of the Sioux Falls metro where wind loads are higher than in neighborhoods sheltered by the city's tree canopy. Sustained gusts during spring and fall storms routinely lift vinyl panel edges, force water behind J-channel, and rip gutter spikes out of fascia boards that were never properly backed with solid wood blocking. The freeze-thaw cycle compounds the problem: water that gets behind siding in October expands through the winter, widening gaps and pushing panels out of alignment by spring.

Newer subdivisions like Prairie Meadows were built with speed as a priority, and that sometimes means thinner vinyl profiles, minimal overlap at seams, and gutters hung with spike-and-ferrule systems that loosen faster than hidden-hanger alternatives. Homes in these neighborhoods often show their first gutter failures within five to seven years — earlier than homeowners expect. On the flip side, older properties closer to Downtown Tea may have aluminum siding or even original wood clapboard underneath vinyl that was installed as a retrofit, meaning a full replacement involves more layers and more decisions than a standard swap.

## Our Siding and Gutter Process in Tea

Every project starts with a full exterior inspection — not just the panels or gutters in obvious distress, but the underlying sheathing, housewrap condition, fascia boards, and soffit. In Tea's newer construction, we frequently find OSB sheathing that has swelled at the edges from moisture intrusion behind improperly lapped siding. Replacing siding without addressing that sheathing means the new panels are going up over a compromised substrate.

For siding, we work through a defined sequence: remove existing panels and trim, inspect and repair sheathing, install new housewrap or vapor barrier, and then install new siding with proper overlap and fastener spacing for South Dakota wind-load requirements. Material choices matter here — heavier-gauge vinyl (0.044" or thicker) or engineered wood products like LP SmartSide hold up significantly better in this climate than builder-grade 0.040" profiles.

For gutters, we default to seamless aluminum fabricated on-site to the exact run length, which eliminates the mid-run seam joints that are the most common failure point on sectional systems. We install with hidden hangers on 16-inch centers — closer spacing than the standard 24-inch — because Tea's ice dam and snow load conditions put extra stress on gutter attachment points through the winter.

## Local Note

One pattern we see repeatedly in Tea's newer subdivisions: gutter downspouts that were extended across the yard during original construction but never properly buried or tied into a drain. Over time, the surface extensions get mowed over or disconnected, and water starts discharging directly against the foundation. In Tea's clay-heavy soil, that water doesn't percolate — it pools and eventually finds its way into the basement or crawl space. When we're replacing gutters on a home in Sunrise Ridge or Prairie Meadows, we always walk the downspout discharge points and flag any that are directing water toward the foundation rather than away from it.
