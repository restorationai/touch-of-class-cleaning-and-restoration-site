## Why Worthing Properties See Asbestos Issues

Worthing sits in a stretch of southeastern South Dakota where a significant portion of the housing stock was built between the 1950s and early 1980s — the exact window when asbestos was used most heavily in residential construction. Popcorn ceilings, vinyl floor tiles, pipe insulation, roofing felt, and joint compound from that era routinely contain asbestos fibers. The area's climate compounds the risk: South Dakota's freeze-thaw cycles put stress on older building materials year after year, causing them to crack, crumble, or flake. When those materials become friable — meaning they can be crumbled by hand pressure — asbestos fibers become airborne far more easily.

The growth corridor running south from Sioux Falls through the Interstate 29 Exit 68 area has also accelerated teardowns and gut renovations in East Worthing, where some of the oldest residential lots sit. ## Our Asbestos Abatement Process in Worthing

Every project starts with an asbestos inspection and bulk material sampling before any demolition or disturbance occurs. Samples are submitted to an accredited laboratory, and results typically return within a few business days. If asbestos-containing materials are confirmed, the affected work area is isolated with negative air pressure containment — plastic sheeting, sealed HVAC registers, and HEPA-filtered air scrubbers running continuously throughout removal.

Our crew wears full personal protective equipment and follows regulated disposal procedures: all removed material is wetted to suppress fiber release, double-bagged in labeled poly bags, and transported to a licensed disposal facility. Once removal is complete, a final air clearance test is conducted before containment is broken and the space is released back to other trades or occupants. Nothing is rushed — cutting corners on asbestos abatement creates liability for everyone on the project, and we document every step for your records and any insurance or permit requirements.

## Local Note

One pattern we've noticed in Worthing's older ranch homes is that original popcorn ceiling texture was sometimes applied directly over a skim-coat plaster base rather than drywall. When that's the case, the ceiling system may contain asbestos in both the texture layer and the joint compound beneath it — two separate materials that each require sampling. If your home was built before 1985 and has never had a full material survey, it's worth asking for composite sampling across all ceiling layers, not just the visible surface texture.
