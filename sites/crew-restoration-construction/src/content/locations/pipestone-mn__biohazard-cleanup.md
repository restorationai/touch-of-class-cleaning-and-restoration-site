## Why Pipestone Properties Present Unique Biohazard Challenges

Much of Pipestone's residential and commercial building stock was constructed before modern ventilation and subflooring standards were established. Older homes — particularly those near the Hiawatha area and along the historic corridors downtown — often have original hardwood over board sheathing, plaster-and-lath walls, and crawl spaces with limited airflow. These materials are porous in ways that modern construction is not. Biological contamination does not stay on the surface; it wicks into subfloor layers, seeps behind baseboards, and settles into plaster cavities. A cleanup that looks complete at the surface level may leave behind infectious material and odor-causing compounds deeper in the structure.

Southwest Minnesota's climate adds another variable. Pipestone winters are long and cold, and properties that are unoccupied or inadequately heated during the season can develop condensation cycles that accelerate microbial activity following a biohazard event. Summer humidity, while shorter in duration, creates similar conditions. Timing the remediation correctly and verifying that the structure is properly dried and treated before it is reoccupied is part of every job we complete here.

## Our Biohazard Cleanup Process in Pipestone

Every biohazard situation is different, but the process follows a consistent, regulated framework. When we arrive, the first priority is containment — isolating the affected area from the rest of the property to prevent cross-contamination. We use engineering controls including negative air pressure and physical barriers where the layout of the structure requires it.

All biological material is removed and packaged according to state and federal infectious waste regulations. In older Pipestone homes, this sometimes means removing sections of subfloor, plaster, or insulation that have absorbed contamination beyond what surface treatment can address. We do not cut corners on material removal because leaving compromised materials in place creates long-term health and odor problems that are harder and more expensive to resolve later.

After removal, affected surfaces are treated with EPA-registered disinfectants appropriate for the specific type of contamination. We then conduct a final inspection and, where warranted, ATP testing to verify that biological residue has been reduced to safe levels before we close out the job. Documentation of the process is provided for insurance purposes.
