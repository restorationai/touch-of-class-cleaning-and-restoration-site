## Why Merrill Properties See Sewage Backup Issues

Merrill's housing stock skews older, and many homes in and around Downtown Merrill were built with clay or cast-iron sewer laterals that have had decades to corrode, root-infiltrate, and shift. When those pipes fail, the backup typically surfaces in the lowest point of the home — a basement utility room, a laundry area, or a floor drain that suddenly reverses. Compounding the problem is the Floyd River corridor: during spring snowmelt or a heavy rain event, the municipal system can experience surcharge conditions, pushing wastewater backward through connected laterals into private homes before the city's infrastructure can catch up. Homes in East Merrill, closer to lower-lying ground near the river, are particularly susceptible to this kind of sewer surcharge.

Septic systems on the edges of town carry their own risks. A saturated drain field after a wet spring can force partially treated effluent back toward the tank and, in some cases, into the structure itself. The clay-heavy soils common in this part of Iowa absorb and release moisture slowly, which means a failing drain field doesn't always announce itself until the problem is already inside.

## Our Sewage Cleanup and Sanitization Process in Merrill

Raw sewage is classified as Category 3 water — the most contaminated type — which means standard wet-vac cleanup is not sufficient. The process starts with containment: affected areas are isolated to prevent cross-contamination from spreading to clean living spaces. Porous materials that have absorbed sewage — drywall, insulation, carpet, subflooring — are removed and bagged for proper disposal per Iowa Department of Natural Resources guidelines for biohazardous waste.

Once the bulk material is out, standing water and residual waste are extracted using truck-mounted or portable extraction equipment. Every surface that contacted sewage is then treated with EPA-registered disinfectants capable of neutralizing the bacteria, viruses, and pathogens present in raw sewage. This isn't a single-pass wipe-down — high-contact surfaces receive multiple applications, and the space is allowed appropriate dwell time before technicians move to the drying phase. Industrial air movers and dehumidifiers run until moisture readings in walls, floors, and framing return to normal baselines, because residual moisture left behind after sewage cleanup creates ideal conditions for mold colonization — often within 24 to 48 hours in Iowa's humid summers.
