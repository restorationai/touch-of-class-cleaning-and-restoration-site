## Why Dakota Dunes Properties Face Elevated Asbestos Risk

The housing stock here skews older than many people expect. The Dunes golf community and the neighborhoods surrounding Dakota Dunes Country Club were largely developed between the mid-1970s and early 1990s, a window when asbestos appeared in joint compound, vinyl floor backing, roofing felt, duct tape on HVAC systems, and spray-applied ceiling texture. Homes built before 1981 are statistically the highest-risk category — and a large portion of the single-family residences in this community fall squarely in that range.

The 2011 Missouri River flood added another layer of complexity. Many homes along the river corridor underwent emergency repairs and partial reconstruction after that event. Flood-driven gut jobs often disturb existing asbestos-containing materials without proper abatement, and subsequent patch repairs sometimes introduced new materials layered over the old ones. That means a home that looks fully updated on the surface may still contain legacy asbestos in subflooring, wall cavities, or mechanical chases — discovered only when the next renovation begins.

## Our Asbestos Abatement Process in Dakota Dunes

Every project starts with a thorough inspection and bulk sample collection before any demolition or disturbance occurs. Samples go to an accredited laboratory, and we don't begin abatement until results confirm what materials are present and in what concentration. That step protects you, your family, and any contractors working alongside us.

Once confirmed, the work area is isolated with negative air pressure containment — plastic sheeting, HEPA-filtered air scrubbers running continuously, and a decontamination chamber at the entry point. Workers wear full personal protective equipment throughout. Asbestos-containing materials are wetted to suppress fiber release, carefully removed, double-bagged in labeled poly bags, and transported to a licensed disposal facility in compliance with South Dakota Department of Agriculture and Natural Resources requirements. The job isn't complete until post-abatement air clearance sampling confirms fiber levels are below regulatory thresholds.

For popcorn ceiling asbestos removal — one of the more common requests in this housing stock — the process is the same: containment first, wet removal, air clearance last. Skipping any step to save time is not something we do.
