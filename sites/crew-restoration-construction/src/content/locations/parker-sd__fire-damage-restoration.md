## Our Fire Damage Restoration Process in Parker

The first priority after the fire department clears a structure is stabilization: boarding windows, tarping the roof, and assessing whether the structure is safe to enter. From there, the process moves through several defined phases.

**Damage assessment and documentation** — Every affected surface is catalogued with photos and moisture readings. This documentation matters for your insurance claim and for scoping the work accurately before a single demo cut is made.

**Soot and smoke residue removal** — Different fire types leave different residues. A fast-burning grass or paper fire leaves a dry, powdery soot that brushes off relatively cleanly. A slow, smoldering fire — common when synthetic materials like furniture foam or electrical insulation burn — leaves a wet, oily soot that smears if wiped incorrectly and requires chemical sponges and specific cleaning agents matched to the surface type.

**Structural drying and odor neutralization** — If fire suppression water is present, it's extracted and the structure is dried using commercial air movers and dehumidifiers before odor treatment begins. Thermal fogging or hydroxyl generation can then be used to neutralize smoke odor molecules embedded in porous materials.

**Reconstruction** — Once cleaning and drying are complete, damaged framing, drywall, flooring, and finishes are rebuilt to match the pre-loss condition as closely as possible.
