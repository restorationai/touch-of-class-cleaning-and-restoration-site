## Why Baltic Properties See Asbestos Concerns

Baltic (ZIP 57003) is a small community, but its residential character is shaped by decades of modest, well-built homes that predate modern building codes. The materials common in that era — textured spray ceilings, vinyl floor tiles, pipe and boiler insulation, roofing felt, and joint compound — frequently contained chrysotile or amosite asbestos. In the Big Sioux River area specifically, some older homes also show signs of deferred maintenance tied to periodic spring flooding, which can accelerate the deterioration of pipe insulation and ceiling materials. Damaged or friable asbestos is the kind that releases fibers into the air; intact asbestos generally is not an immediate hazard, but water intrusion changes that calculus quickly.

Baltic School and other community buildings constructed in the mid-twentieth century followed the same material conventions. If you're a property owner managing a building of that vintage — residential or commercial — asbestos testing is the only way to know what you're actually dealing with before a contractor's demo work turns a contained situation into an airborne one.

## Our Asbestos Abatement Process in Baltic

Every project starts with a thorough asbestos inspection and bulk sampling. A trained inspector collects small samples from suspect materials — ceiling texture, floor tile mastic, pipe insulation, drywall compound — and sends them to an accredited laboratory for polarized light microscopy analysis. You get a written report identifying which materials contain asbestos and at what concentration before any removal work is scheduled.

If abatement is warranted, the work follows a structured sequence:

- **Containment setup**: Polyethylene sheeting seals the work area; negative air pressure units with HEPA filtration prevent fiber migration to adjacent spaces.
- **Wet removal**: Regulated asbestos-containing material (RACM) is wetted before and during removal to suppress fiber release — a non-negotiable step under South Dakota Department of Agriculture and Natural Resources (DANR) requirements.
- **Waste handling**: All removed material is double-bagged in 6-mil poly, labeled per EPA and NESHAP standards, and transported to a licensed disposal facility.
- **Air clearance**: Post-abatement air sampling confirms fiber levels are within acceptable limits before containment is removed and the space is released for reconstruction.

In Baltic's older homes, we frequently encounter multiple suspect materials in a single project — a popcorn ceiling over original floor tile over pipe-wrapped ductwork. Scoping the full picture upfront prevents mid-project surprises and keeps the timeline predictable.

One pattern we've noticed in Baltic and the surrounding Minnehaha County communities along the Big Sioux River corridor: homes that experienced basement or crawlspace flooding in past springs sometimes have pipe insulation that looks intact from a distance but has softened or delaminated where it contacted standing water.
