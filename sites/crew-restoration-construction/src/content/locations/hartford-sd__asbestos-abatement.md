## Why Hartford Properties See Asbestos Concerns

Hartford sits in Minnehaha County, and much of its residential building stock dates to the 1960s through the early 1980s — precisely the decades when asbestos was used most aggressively in residential construction. Ranch-style homes common in the Buffalo Ridge area often feature original floor tile adhesive (the mastic beneath vinyl squares is frequently asbestos-containing), blown-in attic insulation, and duct tape on HVAC systems that predates modern materials. South Dakota's extreme temperature swings — from subzero January nights to humid August afternoons — cause building materials to expand and contract repeatedly, which can fracture aging asbestos-containing products and increase the risk of fiber release over time. That physical stress on older materials is one reason an inspection before any demo work is worth scheduling rather than skipping.

Popcorn ceiling asbestos removal is one of the most common requests we receive from Hartford homeowners. Textured ceilings applied before 1978 have a high likelihood of containing chrysotile asbestos, and sanding or scraping them without testing first is a regulatory violation under EPA guidelines — not just a safety risk.

## Our Asbestos Abatement Process in Hartford

Every project begins with an asbestos inspection and bulk sampling of suspect materials. Samples are sent to an accredited laboratory, and results typically return within a few business days. If asbestos is confirmed, we establish a regulated work area using negative air pressure containment and HEPA filtration equipment — no fibers migrate to adjacent rooms or your HVAC system during removal.

Wet methods are used throughout the removal phase to suppress fiber release. Asbestos-containing materials are double-bagged in labeled, sealed poly bags and transported to a licensed disposal facility in compliance with South Dakota DENR requirements. Once removal is complete, a final visual inspection and air clearance testing confirm the space is clean before containment comes down. You receive written documentation of the entire process — something your contractor, real estate agent, or lender may require before the project moves forward.
