## Why Tea Properties See Water Damage Differently Than Older Sioux Falls Neighborhoods

Tea sits on the flat, clay-heavy soils common to the James River lowlands, and that soil profile matters more than most homeowners realize. Clay doesn't drain — it sheds. During a hard spring rain or a rapid snowmelt event, water that can't percolate downward moves laterally, finding its way toward foundations through window wells, crawl space vents, and the gap between a poured wall and its footing. Newer subdivisions in Tea were graded at build time, but as lots settle over the first five to ten years, drainage patterns shift. A yard that sloped away from the house in 2018 may now direct runoff straight toward the foundation by 2025.

Beyond grading, the speed of Tea's construction boom means a significant portion of the housing stock uses engineered lumber — LVL beams, OSB subfloor, and I-joists — rather than the dimensional lumber common in older Sioux Falls homes. Engineered wood products absorb moisture and begin to delaminate faster than solid lumber. A wet OSB subfloor that looks intact on the surface can have its glue bond compromised within 24 to 48 hours of saturation, which is why early extraction and drying matter more in these homes than in a 1960s ranch with solid-pine framing.

## Our Water Damage Restoration Process in Tea

The first step on any job is moisture mapping — not a visual inspection, but a systematic pass with thermal imaging and calibrated moisture meters to find water that has already migrated beyond the visible wet area. In Tea's newer homes, that often means checking inside wall cavities adjacent to the loss, under LVL beams at the perimeter, and beneath any floating luxury vinyl plank flooring, which traps moisture underneath it even when the surface feels dry.

Once the extent of the damage is documented, we extract standing water with truck-mounted and portable extraction units, then set a calculated drying system: commercial-grade desiccant or refrigerant dehumidifiers, axial air movers positioned to create a directed airflow pattern, and in some cases, injectidry panels to dry inside wall cavities without unnecessary demolition. Drying logs are recorded daily so the insurance adjuster has a clear, timestamped record of the job's progress — something that matters when a claim involves a newer home where replacement costs are higher than average.

Demolition is performed only where materials cannot be dried in place. When removal is necessary, we document everything photographically before cutting, which protects both the homeowner and the carrier during the claims process.
