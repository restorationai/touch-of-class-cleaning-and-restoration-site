## Why Orange City Properties Face Distinct Board-Up and Tarping Risks

Northwest Iowa's climate is genuinely punishing in ways that set it apart from the rest of the state. Sioux County averages some of the highest annual snowfall totals in Iowa, and the flat, open terrain around Orange City means wind speeds that regularly exceed 40 mph during winter storms — enough to drive rain and snow horizontally through a compromised roof deck or a broken pane. That wind loading also means a poorly anchored tarp can become a liability rather than a solution.

The housing stock adds another layer of complexity. Orange City was platted in the 1870s by Dutch settlers, and the older neighborhoods near Downtown Orange City and the Tulip Festival grounds include homes built before modern sheathing standards — balloon-frame construction with minimal interior compartmentalization. When a fire or impact event breaches the exterior envelope of one of these homes, the open wall cavities allow air and moisture to travel vertically from basement to attic with very little resistance. Getting a board-up in place fast is not just about keeping rain out; it is about stopping that stack-effect moisture migration before it reaches the framing.

## Our Emergency Board-Up and Tarping Process in Orange City

Every job starts with a perimeter walk to catalog every breach — broken windows, compromised doors, sections of missing or lifted roofing — before a single sheet of plywood is cut. We photograph everything, both for your insurance adjuster and to make sure nothing gets missed in low-light conditions after a nighttime fire or storm.

For window and door board-ups, we use ¾-inch ACX plywood fastened with structural screws rather than nails, which provides a more secure hold against the sustained wind loads common in this part of Iowa and is easier to remove cleanly when your contractor is ready to begin permanent repairs. Openings are sealed at the edges with foam backer rod and construction tape to block wind-driven rain.

Roof tarping follows a specific sequence: we clear loose debris first, then lay 6-mil polyethylene tarps with a minimum 4-foot overhang past the ridge and secure them with 2×4 lumber battens screwed through the tarp into the decking — not just weighted down with sandbags, which can shift or blow off under Sioux County wind conditions. If the structural integrity of the roof deck is in question, we stage from the interior rather than walking the surface.

## Local Note

Orange City's older homes — particularly the Dutch Colonial and Craftsman-era houses within a few blocks of the Tulip Festival grounds — were often built with true-dimension lumber and plaster-over-lath wall systems. When we are boarding up a window opening in one of these homes, the rough opening dimensions rarely match modern standard sizes, and the window trim profiles are often irreplaceable millwork.
