## Our Storm Damage Restoration Process in Merrill

The first priority after any storm event is stopping ongoing intrusion. Before any drying or repair work begins, the structure needs to be protected — roof tarps, board-up of broken windows or doors, and temporary weatherproofing of any penetrations the storm created. Leaving those openings unaddressed, even for a day or two, allows rain, humidity, and Iowa's summer heat to drive moisture deep into wall cavities and subfloor assemblies.

Once the structure is secured, the process moves to a full moisture mapping of affected areas. Thermal imaging cameras and calibrated moisture meters identify water that has migrated beyond the obvious wet spots — inside wall assemblies, under flooring, and into insulation bays. In older Merrill homes with plaster-and-lath interior walls, this step is especially important: plaster absorbs water slowly but releases it even more slowly, and drying timelines need to be extended accordingly compared to modern drywall construction.

Structural drying follows, using industrial air movers and dehumidifiers sized to the actual cubic footage of the affected space. Moisture readings are logged daily so drying progress can be documented — a record that also supports the insurance claim. Once materials reach target moisture levels, reconstruction begins: roofing, siding, window replacement, interior finishes, or whatever the storm required.
