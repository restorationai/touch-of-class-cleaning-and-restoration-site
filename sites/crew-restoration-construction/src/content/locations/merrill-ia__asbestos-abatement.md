## Why Merrill Properties Are Prone to Asbestos-Containing Materials

Merrill grew steadily from the late 1800s through the 1970s, and a large share of its residential and light-commercial building stock reflects construction practices from that era. Homes near Downtown Merrill — particularly those built between the 1940s and early 1970s — frequently contain multiple layers of resilient floor tile, each generation of renovation laid over the last. The adhesive ("mastic") bonding those tiles to concrete slabs or wood subfloors is one of the most common sources of chrysotile asbestos found in Iowa homes of this age. Beyond flooring, vermiculite attic insulation, textured "popcorn" ceiling spray, and wrap insulation on basement heating pipes are all materials that were standard practice before the federal regulatory phase-outs of the late 1970s and early 1980s.

Iowa's freeze-thaw cycle is also relevant. Merrill winters are hard on older building envelopes, and decades of moisture intrusion, frost heave, and settling can crack or crumble materials that were once intact. Friable asbestos — material that can be crumbled by hand pressure — releases fibers into the air. A basement pipe wrap that held together for fifty years can become a hazard after a single wet winter and a subsequent renovation disturbance.

## Our Asbestos Abatement Process in Merrill

Every project begins with a formal asbestos inspection and bulk sampling. A trained inspector collects samples from suspect materials — floor tile, ceiling texture, pipe insulation, drywall joint compound — and sends them to an accredited laboratory for polarized light microscopy (PLM) analysis. Results typically return within a few business days, and we walk you through the findings before any abatement work is scheduled.

When abatement is warranted, the work area is isolated with poly sheeting and negative air pressure units equipped with HEPA filtration. Workers wear full-face respirators and disposable protective suits. Regulated waste is wetted, double-bagged in labeled poly bags, and transported to a licensed disposal facility — Iowa requires disposal at a permitted solid waste facility that accepts asbestos-containing material, and we handle all manifesting and chain-of-custody documentation. After abatement, the contained area is visually inspected and air clearance sampling is performed before containment is removed and the space is released for re-occupancy or reconstruction.

For popcorn ceiling asbestos removal specifically, we take extra care to wet the material thoroughly before scraping to minimize fiber release — a step that matters in occupied homes where adjacent rooms cannot always be fully vacated.
