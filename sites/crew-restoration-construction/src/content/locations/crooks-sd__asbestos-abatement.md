## Why Crooks Properties See Asbestos Issues

Most of the housing stock in and around the 57020 ZIP code was constructed between the 1950s and early 1980s — the exact window when asbestos use in residential construction peaked. Vermiculite attic insulation, 9×9 vinyl floor tiles, textured "popcorn" ceilings, pipe wrap, and duct tape on HVAC joints were all routinely manufactured with asbestos fiber during this era. South Dakota's wide temperature swings — from subzero January nights to humid July afternoons — cause building materials to expand and contract repeatedly over decades. That thermal cycling can cause older asbestos-containing materials to crack or crumble, moving them from a stable "non-friable" state into a friable one that releases airborne fibers more readily. A home that looked fine last spring may have materials that need attention after another hard winter.

The area's proximity to agricultural operations also means older outbuildings and converted structures sometimes contain asbestos-wrapped pipe insulation or corrugated roofing panels — materials that are easy to overlook when a property changes use.

## Our Asbestos Abatement Process in Crooks

Every project starts with a thorough inspection before any material is touched. Suspected asbestos-containing materials are sampled and sent to an accredited laboratory for analysis — we don't skip this step, even when a material looks like a textbook case. Results typically return within a few business days.

If the lab confirms asbestos, the work area is isolated using polyethylene sheeting and negative air pressure units equipped with HEPA filtration. This containment keeps fibers from migrating into adjacent rooms or the HVAC system — a real concern in the open floor plans common in Crooks-area ranch homes where ductwork runs through multiple zones. Wet methods are used throughout removal to suppress fiber release, and all waste is double-bagged in labeled, sealed containers and transported to a licensed disposal facility in compliance with South Dakota Department of Agriculture and Natural Resources (DANR) requirements.

After removal, air clearance sampling confirms fiber levels have returned to acceptable thresholds before containment is dismantled. You receive documentation of the entire process — lab reports, waste manifests, and clearance results — which you'll need for permits, insurance, and any future sale of the property.

## Popcorn Ceiling and Interior Material Removal

Popcorn ceiling texture is one of the most common asbestos discoveries in Crooks homes built before 1978. Homeowners often assume it's safe to scrape off themselves, but if the texture was applied before the late 1970s, there's a meaningful chance it contains chrysotile asbestos. Dry scraping releases fibers directly into the living space. The same caution applies to the 9×9 and 12×12 floor tiles found under carpet in many split-level and ranch-style homes in the area — the tiles themselves may be low-risk if intact, but the black mastic adhesive beneath them frequently tests positive.

We handle both materials under full containment protocols, and we can coordinate with your flooring or drywall contractor so the project timeline doesn't stall between abatement and finish work.
