## Why Alton Properties See Siding and Gutter Issues

The same geography that makes Sioux County farmland productive makes it hard on building exteriors. Wind gusts during spring storm season routinely exceed 60 mph across open ground, and there are few tree lines or neighboring structures to buffer homes near the edge of town. That wind drives rain horizontally into lap siding joints, forcing moisture behind panels and into sheathing before it ever reaches a gutter.

Downtown Alton's older commercial blocks — many of them built in the mid-20th century with wood or aluminum siding — face a compounding problem: original fasteners corrode over decades, and once a panel begins to work loose, wind does the rest quickly. Gutter systems on these buildings often use undersized 4-inch profiles that were standard at the time but can't handle the volume of a heavy Iowa thunderstorm. Overflow saturates foundation plantings and, over time, works into basement walls.

Residential properties in East Alton tend to be newer, but vinyl siding installed in the 1990s and early 2000s is now reaching the end of its rated service life. Chalking, fading, and hairline cracking at panel seams are common signs that replacement — not just patching — is the right call.

## Our Siding and Gutter Process in Alton

Every project starts with a full exterior inspection before a single panel comes off. We photograph existing conditions, probe for moisture behind siding with a pin meter, and check fascia and soffit for rot that would undermine new gutter hangers. If we find compromised sheathing or fascia, we document it and walk you through the scope before work begins — no surprises mid-project.

For siding replacement, we remove existing material down to the sheathing, install a continuous weather-resistant barrier, and then apply new siding. In this climate, we strongly favor fiber cement and engineered wood products over standard vinyl because they resist impact from hail and don't become brittle in sub-zero temperatures the way thinner vinyl can. That said, if budget or HOA guidelines point toward vinyl, we use heavier-gauge panels with a higher wind-load rating than builder-grade stock.

Gutter installation follows the siding work so new panels aren't scratched during hanging. We size gutters to the actual roof pitch and square footage of each drainage plane — most residential roofs in this area warrant 5-inch K-style gutters, while larger commercial rooflines benefit from 6-inch profiles. All downspouts are extended to discharge at least four feet from the foundation, which matters especially on lots where the grade is relatively flat and water has nowhere to go quickly.

One pattern we've noticed on older homes near the Floyd River floodplain: the ground-level siding courses on these properties sometimes show moisture wicking that has nothing to do with rain — it's capillary action pulling soil moisture up through the foundation into the bottom two or three courses of siding.
