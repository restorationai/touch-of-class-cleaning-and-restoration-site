## Why Worthington Properties Face Elevated Board-Up and Tarping Risks

Northwest Minnesota's climate is not gentle. Worthington sits in a part of Nobles County where late-summer thunderstorms can stack hail on top of straight-line winds, and winter brings freeze-thaw cycling that widens any crack a storm starts. Homes in the Lake Okabena area are particularly exposed — the lake's open fetch means wind gusts hit lakeside and near-shore properties with less buffer than homes deeper in town. A broken window or a lifted roof section that might stay stable for a day in a sheltered suburban neighborhood can admit gallons of water overnight when a front rolls through from the northwest.

Downtown Worthington's older commercial and mixed-use buildings present a different challenge: masonry facades, aging mortar joints, and flat or low-slope roofs that pond water quickly once a membrane is breached. Fire damage to a storefront on a block of connected buildings isn't just a single-property problem — an unsecured opening can compromise adjacent units through shared walls or attic spaces. Board-up work in those situations requires careful planning around structural load points, not just plywood over a hole.

## Our Emergency Board-Up and Tarping Process in Worthington

Every job starts with a fast visual assessment of the full structure before any material goes up. We're looking at the size and location of the breach, what's immediately behind it (insulation, electrical, finished living space), whether the roof deck is still structurally sound enough to anchor tarp straps, and whether there's standing water or active smoke residue that needs to be noted for the insurance documentation.

For window and door openings, we use exterior-grade OSB cut to fit the rough opening, secured with structural screws rather than nails — nails back out under wind pressure, screws hold. For roof breaches, we use heavy-duty polyethylene tarps rated for the load, anchored with 2x4 burl boards along the ridge and eaves so the tarp doesn't lift in the next gust. On larger commercial roof sections near the Nobles County Fairgrounds area, we've used ballasted tarping systems when foot traffic or equipment staging makes standard anchor points impractical.

Every opening is photographed before and after boarding — dimensions, materials used, anchor points — because that documentation feeds directly into the insurance claim.

## Worthington Insurance and Documentation

Most homeowner and commercial property policies cover emergency protective measures — board-up and tarping — as part of the initial loss. The key is documentation: insurers want to see that the work was necessary, that it was performed promptly to mitigate further damage, and that the scope is itemized.
