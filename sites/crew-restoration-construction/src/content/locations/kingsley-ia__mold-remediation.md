## Why Kingsley Properties See Mold Issues

Kingsley is a farm town that grew up fast in the late 1800s and early 1900s, which means a significant portion of the housing stock — particularly in and around Downtown Kingsley — was built before modern vapor barriers and cavity insulation were standard practice. Rim joists were left open. Crawlspaces were vented inconsistently. Basement walls were poured without waterproofing membranes. Those construction details weren't mistakes at the time; they just weren't designed for today's understanding of moisture control.

The regional climate compounds the issue. Western Iowa sees wide humidity swings between humid summers and cold, dry winters. When a heated interior meets an uninsulated foundation wall in February, condensation forms on the cold surface — exactly the damp, dark, low-airflow environment where *Cladosporium*, *Penicillium*, and the more serious *Stachybotrys* (commonly called black mold) colonize. Homes near Kingsley City Park, where mature tree canopy reduces direct sunlight and keeps soil moisture elevated, tend to see this pattern more frequently.

## Our Mold Remediation Process in Kingsley

Every job starts with a thorough visual inspection and moisture mapping — we use thermal imaging and pin-type moisture meters to find wet building materials that aren't visible to the naked eye. In Kingsley's older homes, that often means checking behind plaster-and-lath walls, under original hardwood subfloors, and inside wall cavities around plumbing chases.

Once we've identified the affected area, we establish negative-air containment using 6-mil poly barriers and HEPA-filtered air scrubbers to prevent cross-contamination to clean rooms. Affected porous materials — drywall, insulation, damaged wood framing — are removed and bagged for proper disposal. Remaining structural surfaces are treated with an EPA-registered antimicrobial agent. We then run commercial-grade dehumidifiers and air movers until moisture readings return to acceptable baselines, typically 19% or below for wood framing.

We don't close out a job until the numbers confirm the environment is dry — not just visually clean.

## Local Note

One thing that comes up specifically in Kingsley and other Loess Hills communities: the silt-loam soil holds moisture at the surface long after a rain event clears, which means a basement that dried out fine last spring may show new mold growth the following fall without any new plumbing failure. Homeowners sometimes treat the visible mold and assume the problem is solved — but without addressing the ongoing moisture intrusion at the wall assembly, regrowth is likely within a season.
