## Our Water Damage Restoration Process in Luverne

Every water damage job starts with a thorough moisture assessment before any equipment is staged. We use thermal imaging cameras and calibrated moisture meters to map how far water has traveled — through wall cavities, under flooring, into insulation — because visible wet areas are rarely the full picture. In Luverne's older homes, water often tracks along original hardwood subfloors or through gaps in stone foundation walls to reach areas two or three rooms away from the source.

Once the scope is mapped, water extraction comes first. Truck-mounted extractors pull standing water from carpets, hardwood, and concrete floors faster than portable units, which matters when you're trying to prevent secondary mold growth. In southwestern Minnesota's humid summers, mold colonization can begin within 24 to 48 hours on wet organic materials — so the speed of extraction directly affects whether the job stays a drying project or becomes a remediation project.

After extraction, we set a calculated drying system: industrial-grade dehumidifiers, air movers positioned to create directed airflow through wall cavities, and drying mats for hardwood floors where appropriate. We monitor moisture readings daily and adjust equipment placement until materials reach their target dry standard. We don't pull equipment based on a calendar — we pull it based on the numbers.
