## Why Rock Valley Properties See Siding and Gutter Issues

The Sioux County climate is not gentle on exteriors. Rock Valley averages around 26 inches of annual snowfall, and the real damage often comes not from the snow itself but from the repeated thaw-and-refreeze that follows. Water works into micro-gaps in lap siding, expands when temperatures drop overnight, and slowly pries panels away from sheathing. Vinyl siding installed more than 15 years ago becomes brittle in these conditions and cracks rather than flexing.

Gutters face a parallel problem. Ice dams form at the roofline when attic heat escapes unevenly, and the weight of backed-up ice can pull standard K-style gutters away from the fascia entirely. Properties near the Rock River area sit in low-lying terrain where standing water after snowmelt lingers longer than in elevated neighborhoods — meaning downspouts that drain too close to the foundation are genuinely hazardous, not just a cosmetic issue. We see this pattern repeatedly on older ranch-style homes throughout the area, where original 4-inch gutters were simply undersized for the roof pitch and square footage they were meant to handle.

## Our Siding and Gutter Process in Rock Valley

Every project starts with a full exterior inspection — not just the panels that are visibly damaged, but the housewrap or felt paper underneath, the condition of the J-channel and trim, and the slope and attachment points of the existing gutter run. Skipping that step is how contractors miss rotted OSB sheathing that will ruin a new siding installation within two seasons.

For siding replacement, we remove damaged panels, inspect and replace any compromised sheathing, install a continuous water-resistive barrier, and then hang new material. We work with fiber cement, engineered wood, and vinyl depending on the property's exposure and the owner's budget. Fiber cement holds up particularly well in northwest Iowa's temperature swings and doesn't absorb moisture the way older wood lap siding does.

For gutters, we size the system to the actual roof drainage load — a step that matters more in Rock Valley than in drier climates. Larger 6-inch seamless aluminum gutters paired with wider downspouts are often the right call here, especially on homes with steep-pitched roofs or large unbroken roof planes. We also position downspout extensions to discharge water well away from foundations, which is especially important for properties in low-lying sections near the Rock River.
