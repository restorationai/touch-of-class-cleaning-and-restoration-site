## Why Luverne Properties Are Vulnerable to Severe Fire Damage

Luverne sits at the edge of the Coteau des Prairies, where wind gusts during a structure fire can change direction without warning, pulling flames into rooms that would otherwise be spared. Many homes in the older sections of Downtown Luverne were built with balloon-frame construction — a method common before the 1950s where wall cavities run uninterrupted from the basement sill to the roof rafter. In a balloon-frame fire, flames and superheated gases travel vertically inside the walls almost instantly, meaning a kitchen fire can deposit smoke residue in attic insulation before the fire department has the blaze under control. That hidden smoke migration is one of the most underestimated parts of a fire loss in this region, and it's the reason a thorough post-fire inspection here goes well beyond what's visible.

The area's hard water and older galvanized plumbing also matter after a fire: when firefighting water mixes with soot and mineral deposits, it creates an acidic slurry that accelerates corrosion on metal fixtures, electrical panels, and HVAC components. Catching that corrosion early — within the first 24 to 48 hours — is the difference between salvaging a furnace and replacing it.

## Our Fire Damage Restoration Process in Luverne

Every job starts with a structured assessment before any cleaning begins. We document the extent of smoke migration, char depth, and water intrusion from suppression efforts using moisture meters and thermal imaging — not just a visual walk-through. From there, the process moves in a deliberate sequence:

**Stabilization and securing the structure.** Board-up, roof tarping, and temporary fencing protect the property from weather and unauthorized entry while the detailed scope is being built.

**Controlled demolition of non-salvageable materials.** Charred framing, saturated insulation, and smoke-impregnated drywall that cannot be restored are removed and disposed of properly. We separate what can be cleaned from what needs to go — that distinction directly affects your insurance payout and rebuild timeline.

**Smoke and soot remediation.** Dry-ice blasting, HEPA vacuuming, and chemical sponge wiping address different soot types — wet smoke from slow-burning fires leaves a different residue than the dry, powdery soot from a fast-moving grass or structure fire common in prairie climates. Odor neutralization uses thermal fogging and hydroxyl generators rather than masking agents.

**Structural drying.** Suppression water left in subfloors and wall assemblies will cause secondary mold growth within days. Industrial desiccant dehumidifiers and high-velocity air movers run until moisture readings return to pre-loss baselines.

**Reconstruction.** Our construction crews handle framing, insulation, drywall, and finish work, which means one point of contact from demo through move-in.
