## Why Baltic Properties See Sewage Backup Issues

Baltic is a small but growing community on the eastern edge of Minnehaha County, and its housing stock reflects that history — a mix of older homes near Downtown Baltic with original cast-iron or clay-tile sewer laterals, and newer construction pushing outward toward the Big Sioux River corridor. Both ends of that spectrum carry risk.

Older clay-tile laterals crack and shift over decades of freeze-thaw cycling. South Dakota's winters are hard on underground pipe joints, and once a lateral cracks, root intrusion from mature trees — common on the older residential streets near Downtown Baltic — can partially or fully block the line within a season or two. When that blockage meets a heavy rain event or a spring thaw, sewage has nowhere to go but back through floor drains and basement fixtures.

Homes closer to the Big Sioux River area face a different pressure: high seasonal groundwater. ## Our Sewage Cleanup and Sanitization Process in Baltic

Raw sewage is classified as Category 3 water — the most contaminated classification in the IICRC S500 framework — which means every material it contacts must be treated as potentially harboring pathogens. Our process in Baltic follows that standard precisely.

**Step 1 — Containment and PPE setup.** Before extraction begins, affected areas are isolated to prevent cross-contamination to clean spaces. Crew members work in full personal protective equipment.

**Step 2 — Solid and liquid waste extraction.** Truck-mounted extraction units remove standing sewage. Porous materials — drywall, insulation, carpet, subfloor sections — that absorbed Category 3 water are removed and bagged for proper disposal. There is no drying a sewage-soaked carpet back to safe condition.

**Step 3 — Structural cleaning and disinfection.** Remaining hard surfaces — concrete, block, treated lumber — are scrubbed and treated with EPA-registered disinfectants rated for sewage pathogens. This step is documented with photos and product application records, which matter when you're filing an insurance claim.

**Step 4 — Drying and dehumidification.** Commercial desiccant dehumidifiers and air movers run until moisture readings in walls and subfloor assemblies return to baseline. In Baltic's humid spring months, this phase can take longer than it would in a drier climate — we monitor daily rather than pulling equipment on a fixed schedule.

**Step 5 — Clearance verification.** Before reconstruction begins, we confirm that moisture and odor readings are within acceptable ranges. Sewage odor that lingers after cleanup is a sign that contaminated material was missed or that drying is incomplete.
