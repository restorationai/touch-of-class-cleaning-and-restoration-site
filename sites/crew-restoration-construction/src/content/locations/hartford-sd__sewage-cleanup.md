Hartford sits on the eastern edge of the James River lowlands, and the clay-heavy soils common to this stretch of South Dakota don't drain freely. When ground saturation rises after heavy spring rains or rapid snowmelt — both routine events in this part of the state — that hydrostatic pressure pushes back against aging sewer laterals and septic distribution lines. These aren't freak events — they're predictable consequences of infrastructure that has simply reached the end of its service life.

## Our Sewage Cleanup and Sanitization Process in Hartford

Sewage cleanup is not standard water damage work. Raw sewage is classified as Category 3 — the most contaminated water type — and every material it contacts must be treated accordingly. Here's how the process unfolds on a Hartford job:

**Containment first.** Before extraction begins, affected areas are isolated to prevent cross-contamination into unaffected rooms. In finished basements — common in Hartford homes — this step protects flooring and wall assemblies that haven't yet been reached.

**Extraction and removal.** Standing sewage is extracted using truck-mounted equipment. Porous materials that have absorbed Category 3 water — carpet, pad, drywall below the flood line, insulation — are removed and bagged for proper disposal. There is no safe way to dry and restore these materials in place.

**Sanitization.** Hard surfaces, framing, and concrete are treated with EPA-registered disinfectants rated for sewage contamination. This is not a single wipe-down; it's a multi-step application with appropriate dwell times to neutralize bacteria, viruses, and other biological hazards.

**Drying and documentation.** After sanitization, structural drying begins using commercial dehumidifiers and air movers. Moisture readings are logged at every visit so there's a clear record for your insurance carrier.
