## Why Remsen Properties See Siding and Gutter Problems

Remsen sits in Plymouth County, where prevailing winds blow predominantly from the northwest with little natural windbreak. That sustained wind exposure accelerates wear on siding in ways that don't show up in southern Iowa markets. Vinyl panels crack at the locking channels. Fiber cement develops hairline fractures along fastener points. Wood lap siding — common on the older two-story homes near St. Mary's Catholic Church and throughout the St. Mary's district — checks and splits as moisture infiltrates the grain, then freezes and expands.

Gutters face a parallel problem. Remsen's mature tree canopy, particularly around Remsen City Park, drops significant leaf and seed debris each fall. When that debris isn't cleared before the first hard freeze, gutters fill, water backs up under the first course of shingles, and ice dams form along the eave line. By the time spring arrives, the fascia board behind the gutter is often rotted through — a repair that costs far more than a seasonal cleaning would have.

The town's housing stock skews older. Many homes in the Downtown Remsen area were built between the 1920s and 1960s, which means original wood framing, narrower eave overhangs than modern construction standards call for, and in some cases, original cedar or aluminum siding that hasn't been touched in decades. Working on these homes requires more careful substrate inspection before any new siding goes on — you can't just nail over problems that have been building for sixty years.

## Our Siding and Gutter Process in Remsen

Every project starts with a thorough inspection of the existing exterior — not just the siding face, but the housewrap or felt paper underneath, the condition of the sheathing, and the state of the fascia and soffit. In Remsen's climate, it's common to find moisture intrusion behind siding that looks serviceable from the street. We document what we find before any material is ordered.

For siding replacement, we remove the old material completely, assess and replace any damaged sheathing, install a proper weather-resistant barrier, and then apply the new siding system — whether that's engineered wood, fiber cement, or vinyl — according to manufacturer specs and Iowa residential code requirements. Flashing details around windows and doors get particular attention; that's where most long-term leaks originate.

Gutter installation follows the roof line and drainage logic of each specific property. We size gutters to the actual roof surface area rather than defaulting to a standard dimension, set proper slope toward downspouts, and position downspout exits to move water well away from the foundation. On older Remsen homes with shallow lot grades, that last step matters more than most homeowners realize.
