## Why Edgerton Properties Are Especially Vulnerable to Storm Damage

Edgerton's flat, unobstructed terrain means there is almost nothing to slow a storm system before it reaches town. Wind speeds that might drop to 40 mph after hitting a tree line in eastern Minnesota can still be pushing 70 mph or more when they arrive here. That matters because the housing stock in and around Downtown Edgerton includes a significant number of older homes — many built in the mid-20th century or earlier — with wood-framed roofs, aging asphalt shingles, and soffit systems that were never engineered for modern wind-load standards.

Hail is a particular concern. Southwest Minnesota sits in a well-documented hail corridor, and the shallow-pitched roofs common on older Edgerton homes collect impact damage differently than steeper modern designs. A single hailstorm can compromise thousands of shingles without leaving obvious holes, allowing water infiltration to go undetected for weeks. By the time interior staining appears on a ceiling, the sheathing underneath may already be saturated.

Trees along Chanarambie Creek and throughout residential areas of West Edgerton add another layer of risk. Mature cottonwoods and elms are beautiful — and brittle in high winds. A downed tree on a roof is not just a structural problem; it creates an immediate water intrusion point that can soak insulation, wall cavities, and even floor systems within hours if the opening is not sealed.

## Our Storm Damage Restoration Process in Edgerton

When we arrive at a storm-damaged property in Edgerton, the first priority is always stopping additional damage. That means emergency tarping of breached roofs, boarding broken windows, and removing any debris that is actively holding moisture against the structure. We document everything with photographs before we touch a surface — that documentation is what your insurance adjuster needs to process the claim accurately.

Once the property is stabilized, we conduct a full structural assessment. For older homes, that includes checking the attic for hidden moisture that has migrated past damaged shingles, inspecting wall cavities where wind-driven rain may have entered through gaps in siding or trim, and evaluating any load-bearing members that a fallen tree may have stressed. We use moisture meters and thermal imaging to find water that isn't visible to the eye.

Drying comes next — commercial-grade dehumidifiers and air movers placed strategically based on the moisture readings, not just set in the middle of a room. Once materials are confirmed dry, reconstruction begins: roofing, siding, windows, drywall, insulation, and finish work, all coordinated through a single point of contact so you are not managing a dozen separate contractors.
