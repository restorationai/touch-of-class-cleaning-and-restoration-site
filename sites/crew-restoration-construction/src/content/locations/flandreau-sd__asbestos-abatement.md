## Why Flandreau Properties See Asbestos Concerns

South Dakota's northern plains climate creates a specific set of conditions that accelerate asbestos risk in older buildings. Flandreau experiences wide temperature swings — brutal cold in January, humid heat in July — and that thermal cycling causes building materials to expand and contract repeatedly over decades. Pipe wrap insulation, duct tape on HVAC systems, and floor tile adhesive all become brittle and friable over time, meaning they can release microscopic fibers with minimal disturbance.

Homes and commercial buildings in the 57028 ZIP code that were constructed between roughly 1940 and 1978 are the most likely to contain asbestos in multiple locations simultaneously. That means a bathroom renovation, a basement finishing project, or even replacing a water-damaged ceiling tile can inadvertently disturb more than one asbestos-containing material at once. Popcorn ceiling asbestos removal is one of the most common requests in this area, but it rarely travels alone — the drywall compound underneath, the joint tape, and the floor tile in the same room may all require testing before work begins.

## Our Asbestos Abatement Process in Flandreau

Abatement isn't a single step — it's a sequence that begins before anyone enters a work area with protective gear. The process starts with a thorough asbestos inspection and bulk sampling of suspect materials. Samples are sent to an accredited laboratory, and no abatement work begins until results confirm what's present and in what concentration.

Once materials are confirmed, the affected area is isolated using negative air pressure containment. This keeps disturbed fibers from migrating into adjacent rooms — critical in Flandreau's older homes where HVAC ductwork often runs through multiple floors. Asbestos removal is performed wet to suppress airborne fibers, and all waste is double-bagged, labeled, and transported to a licensed disposal facility in accordance with South Dakota Department of Agriculture and Natural Resources (DANR) regulations. A final air clearance test confirms fiber levels have returned to acceptable thresholds before containment comes down.

For popcorn ceiling asbestos removal specifically, the process also accounts for what's underneath. In many mid-century Flandreau homes, the substrate is original drywall or even plaster — both of which may need their own evaluation before the ceiling work is considered complete.
