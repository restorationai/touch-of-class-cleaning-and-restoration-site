## Our Board-Up and Tarping Process in Sioux Center

Every job starts with a rapid walkthrough to identify every breach — not just the obvious ones. Fire damage, for example, often leaves secondary openings where smoke vented through soffits or where firefighters cut for access. We photograph and document each opening before any material goes up, which creates a clear record for your insurance adjuster.

For window and door openings, we use structural-grade plywood cut to fit and secured with exterior screws rather than nails, which hold against wind load far better and don't loosen as framing dries or swells. For roof damage, we deploy heavy-duty polyethylene tarps — minimum 6-mil, typically 10-mil for larger spans — anchored with weighted lumber or screw-down battens rather than simple bungee cords. In Sioux Center's wind environment, a tarp secured only at the corners will fail in the first serious gust.

We also check that any board-up doesn't trap moisture already inside the structure. Sealing a wet wall cavity behind plywood accelerates mold growth rather than preventing it, so ventilation is factored into every installation.
