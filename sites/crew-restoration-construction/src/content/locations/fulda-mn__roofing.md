## Why Fulda Properties See Roofing Deterioration Faster Than You'd Expect

Murray County averages more than 45 inches of snow in a heavy winter, and the temperature swings between January lows and late-March thaws are some of the most dramatic in southwest Minnesota. That thermal cycling expands and contracts roofing materials repeatedly — asphalt shingles crack at the tab slots, flashing separates from chimney masonry, and ridge caps lift just enough to let wind-driven rain find a path inside.

The housing stock in and around Downtown Fulda skews older, with a meaningful share of homes built before 1970. Those roofs often have a single layer of original shingles with a second layer added sometime in the 1990s — a common cost-saving move that's now creating problems. Two layers of aging shingles add dead weight, trap moisture, and mask deck rot that only becomes visible once both layers are stripped. A proper replacement means taking everything down to bare wood, inspecting every sheet of OSB or plank sheathing, and replacing what's soft before the new underlayment ever goes down.

The lakes also matter. Properties on the Lakeside Fulda shoreline face persistent humidity from late spring through early fall, which accelerates granule loss on asphalt shingles and promotes algae streaking — that black striping you see on north-facing slopes. Algae doesn't just look bad; it retains moisture against the shingle surface and speeds up the breakdown of the mat underneath.

## Our Roofing Installation and Replacement Process in Fulda

Every project starts with a full roof inspection — not a drive-by estimate, but someone on the roof with a moisture meter and a camera. We document the condition of the decking, the flashing at every penetration (pipes, vents, chimneys), the soffit and fascia condition, and the attic ventilation setup. Poor ventilation is one of the most overlooked contributors to premature roof failure in this climate; if warm, moist air from the living space can't escape the attic, it condenses on the underside of the decking and rots it from the inside out.

Once the scope is clear, we strip down to the deck, replace any damaged sheathing, and install a synthetic underlayment rated for the temperature range this region sees. Ice-and-water shield goes along the eaves and in the valleys — the two zones where ice dams do the most damage. We then install the new shingles, flash every penetration with step flashing or two-piece counter-flashing as the detail requires, and cap the ridge with a ventilated ridge cap that actually moves air rather than just covering the peak.

Final walkthrough includes a debris sweep with a rolling magnet for fasteners, a gutter inspection, and photos of every completed detail for your records — useful if you ever need to file a claim or sell the property.
