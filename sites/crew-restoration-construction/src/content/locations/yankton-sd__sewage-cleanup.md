## Why Yankton Properties See Sewage Backup Issues

Yankton's housing stock along the Riverside area and Downtown includes homes built decades before modern PVC sewer laterals became standard. Cast iron and clay tile pipes — common in houses built before the 1970s — corrode, crack, and collect root intrusion over time. When a lateral finally fails, the backup doesn't just flood a basement floor drain; it can push effluent through every low-lying fixture in the home.

The soil profile near the Missouri River floodplain also plays a role. Heavy clay soils don't drain quickly, which means that during prolonged rain events or rapid snowmelt — both common in this part of South Dakota — the ground becomes saturated and municipal sewer systems can experience surcharge conditions. Homes in the 57078 ZIP code that sit in low-lying areas are particularly vulnerable to that kind of system-wide pressure event. Lakeside properties near Gavins Point Dam face an additional layer of risk: seasonal fluctuations in Lewis and Clark Lake water levels can raise the local water table enough to stress septic drain fields and push effluent back toward the home.

## Our Sewage Cleanup and Sanitization Process in Yankton

Sewage cleanup is a category-3 water loss — the most contaminated classification — which means the work follows a specific sequence designed to protect both occupants and technicians.

The process starts with containment. Affected areas are isolated to prevent cross-contamination to unaffected rooms. Standing sewage and solid waste are extracted using truck-mounted equipment, and porous materials that have absorbed effluent — carpet, pad, drywall below the flood line, insulation — are removed. Salvageable hard surfaces are then treated with EPA-registered disinfectants formulated for category-3 contamination, not general-purpose cleaners.

After the visible material is gone, the structural drying phase begins. Subfloor assemblies in older Yankton homes often include tongue-and-groove boards over floor joists rather than plywood sheeting, and those assemblies hold moisture differently. Drying equipment placement is adjusted accordingly, and moisture readings are logged throughout the process to document that structural materials have reached acceptable levels before any reconstruction begins.

Final sanitization — a second pass with antimicrobial treatment after drying is confirmed — closes out the remediation phase before any rebuild work starts.

## Local Note: Clay Soil and Drain Field Timing in Yankton

One thing that comes up specifically in Yankton's older Riverside and Westside neighborhoods: when a septic system overflows into a yard during a wet spring, the saturated clay soil holds effluent near the surface far longer than sandy or loam soils would.
