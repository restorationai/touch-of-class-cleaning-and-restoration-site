## Why Luverne Properties See Asbestos Concerns

Rock County's building boom tracked the broader mid-century Midwest pattern: affordable, durable materials were used everywhere, and asbestos was the workhorse of that era. Homes in the 56156 ZIP code built before 1980 have a statistically high likelihood of containing at least one asbestos-containing material (ACM). The most common finds in this area are vinyl floor tiles and their adhesive mastic (often 9"×9" tiles in basements and kitchens), pipe wrap on older forced-air and steam systems, and the popcorn or skip-trowel ceiling texture that was sprayed on throughout the 1960s and 1970s.

Luverne's prairie climate adds a layer of complexity. Freeze-thaw cycles that run hard from November through March cause foundation movement and settling that can crack older pipe insulation and disturb ceiling materials. A home that was perfectly intact in 2010 may have friable — meaning crumbling, airborne-ready — asbestos today simply because the structure has shifted with the seasons. Proximity to Blue Mounds State Park also means some older cabins and outbuildings on the rural fringe carry asbestos roofing materials that were common on agricultural and recreational structures built before 1975.

## Our Asbestos Abatement Process in Luverne

Every project starts with a formal inspection and bulk sampling — we collect material samples from suspected ACMs and send them to an accredited laboratory. You get a written report identifying exactly what is present, where it is, and at what concentration. That report drives every decision that follows.

If abatement is required, the work area is isolated with poly sheeting and negative air pressure units equipped with HEPA filtration. This containment keeps fibers from migrating into the rest of the home — critical in older Luverne houses where open floor plans or unfinished basements can allow air to travel freely. Wet methods are used during removal to suppress fiber release, and all waste is double-bagged in labeled, sealed containers and transported to a licensed disposal facility in compliance with Minnesota Pollution Control Agency (MPCA) regulations.

After removal, air clearance testing confirms that fiber counts have returned to safe levels before containment is dismantled. You receive documentation of the clearance — something your contractor, your insurance adjuster, and any future buyer will want to see.
