## Why Elk Point Properties Are Particularly Vulnerable After a Disaster

Elk Point is one of the oldest incorporated towns in South Dakota, and that history shows up in the housing stock. Many homes in Downtown Elk Point and the surrounding blocks were built before modern building codes standardized roof sheathing thickness, window framing dimensions, and exterior wall construction. Older balloon-frame structures — common in towns platted in the late 1800s and early 1900s — have wide-open wall cavities that channel wind, water, and smoke through a building far faster than platform-frame construction does. A broken window on a balloon-frame home isn't just a broken window; it's a direct pathway for moisture to travel from the first floor to the attic in hours.

The Missouri River corridor also creates a specific weather pattern worth understanding. Cold air masses dropping south from the Dakotas collide with warmer, wetter air moving north, producing hailstorms and straight-line wind events that can strip roofing material cleanly off a structure. After a fire, those same wind conditions can push rain through a compromised roof deck before a homeowner has even spoken with their insurance adjuster. Getting a heavy-duty polyethylene tarp anchored and battened down correctly — not just draped over the peak — is the difference between a contained loss and a soaked interior.

## Our Board-Up and Tarping Process in Elk Point

The first thing the crew does on arrival is a rapid exterior assessment: documenting every opening with photos, noting structural concerns that could affect safe access, and identifying the sequence in which openings need to be secured. Insurance carriers require this documentation, and it protects the homeowner if scope questions come up later.

For window and door board-up, we use ¾-inch CDX plywood cut to fit each opening, secured with structural screws rather than nails so the panels can be removed cleanly when permanent repairs begin. On fire-damaged structures, we also check whether the door and window frames themselves are still plumb — a warped frame won't hold a board securely, and we address that before fastening.

For roof tarping, the crew installs cap boards along the ridge and weights or screws the tarp edges to the roof deck rather than relying on gravity. In the Missouri River wind corridor, a tarp that isn't mechanically fastened will be gone by morning. We use 6-mil reinforced poly tarps rated for the load, sized to overlap the damaged area by at least three feet on each side to account for wind-driven rain.

## Local Note

Older homes in Downtown Elk Point frequently have wood-framed storm windows — a second layer of glazing mounted on the exterior that was standard before insulated glass units became common. After a fire or impact event, these storm windows often detach or shatter separately from the primary window, leaving two layers of framing to address rather than one. Crews unfamiliar with this construction detail sometimes board only the inner frame, leaving the outer storm frame loose and rattling — or worse, leaving a gap that funnels wind and rain directly into the wall cavity. We account for both layers during the assessment and secure or remove the storm window assembly before installing the board.
