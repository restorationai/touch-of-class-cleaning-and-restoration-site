## Why Dell Rapids Properties See Sewage Backup Issues

The geology around The Dells of the Big Sioux is beautiful — and unforgiving to plumbing. Quartzite bedrock sits close to the surface in many parts of town, which limits how deep drain lines can be laid and creates conditions where lateral sewer lines run at shallower grades than ideal. Shallow grades mean slower flow, more opportunity for grease, root intrusion, and solids to accumulate, and a higher likelihood of a full backup when the system is stressed.

Rainfall events that push the Big Sioux out of its banks also raise the water table across low-lying residential lots. When that happens, floor drains and basement toilets can become entry points rather than exit points — sewage from the municipal line reverses direction and surfaces inside the home. Older properties in the Downtown Dell Rapids Historic District are especially vulnerable because many were built before backflow prevention valves were standard, and cast-iron drain stacks from that era corrode from the inside out, narrowing over decades until a single heavy rain is enough to trigger a backup.

## Our Sewage Cleanup and Sanitization Process in Dell Rapids

Raw sewage is classified as Category 3 water — the most contaminated type — which means standard wet-vac extraction is not enough. The process starts with containment: sealing off the affected area to prevent cross-contamination to adjacent rooms, then removing standing sewage using truck-mounted extraction units capable of handling solids and debris.

Once the bulk material is out, porous materials that absorbed sewage — drywall, insulation, carpet, wood subfloor — are removed and bagged for proper disposal. What remains gets treated with EPA-registered hospital-grade disinfectants, applied in multiple passes to hard surfaces, framing, and concrete. Dehumidification and air movement equipment runs until moisture readings in the structural materials return to pre-loss baselines. A final ATP surface test (adenosine triphosphate testing) confirms that biological contamination has been reduced to safe levels before any reconstruction begins.

For homes along the Big Sioux River area where the backup was triggered by flooding, we also assess whether the source of intrusion is resolved — because sanitizing a basement that will flood again in the next rain event only solves half the problem.

## Dell Rapids Insurance Coordination for Sewage Losses

Sewage backup coverage is one of the more misunderstood line items in a standard homeowner's policy. Most base policies exclude sewer backup damage unless a specific rider was added at purchase — something many homeowners in Dell Rapids discover for the first time when they're standing in a flooded basement. Crew Restoration & Construction documents the loss thoroughly regardless: photos of affected materials, moisture readings, contamination scope, and itemized removal logs. ## Local Note

One thing that comes up specifically in the older blocks of the Downtown Dell Rapids Historic District: original basement floors in homes built before 1940 are often bare quartzite slab or rough concrete with no vapor barrier beneath them. When sewage pools on that surface, it wicks laterally into the base of the foundation walls and into any soil exposed at the perimeter. Standard extraction clears the visible liquid, but the contamination has already moved into material that looks dry. On these properties, we extend the perimeter testing further than we would on a modern poured-concrete basement, and drying times run longer because the dense stone releases absorbed moisture slowly. Knowing that going in prevents the mistake of closing out a job that still has active contamination at the wall base.
