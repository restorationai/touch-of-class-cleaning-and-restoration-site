## Why Madison Properties See Water Damage Issues

Madison sits in a region where the soil shifts between expansive clay and sandy glacial till — both problematic for foundations. Clay-heavy lots hold moisture against basement walls long after a heavy rain, creating hydrostatic pressure that forces water through hairline cracks in block or poured concrete foundations. ## Our Water Damage Restoration Process in Madison

Every job starts with a thorough moisture assessment — not just the visible wet area, but the full moisture map. Technicians use thermal imaging cameras and calibrated moisture meters to trace water migration behind walls, under flooring, and into ceiling assemblies. Water travels along framing members and through insulation in ways that aren't obvious from the surface, and missing a wet pocket is how mold problems start.

Once the scope is clear, extraction comes first. Truck-mounted and portable extraction units pull standing water from carpets, hardwood, and concrete before it can wick further into the structure. After extraction, the drying phase begins: industrial air movers are positioned to create directional airflow across wet materials, while commercial dehumidifiers pull moisture from the air and draw it out of building assemblies. Drying typically takes three to five days depending on material type, ambient humidity, and how long the water was present before extraction started.

Throughout the process, moisture readings are logged daily. Drying is not complete when materials feel dry to the touch — it's complete when readings return to baseline levels for the material type and local climate conditions. Documentation of those readings matters for insurance claims and for confirming the structure is genuinely dry before any reconstruction begins.
