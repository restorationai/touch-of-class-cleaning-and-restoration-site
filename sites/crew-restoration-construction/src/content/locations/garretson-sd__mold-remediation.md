## Why Garretson Properties See Mold Issues

Garretson sits in a climate band that delivers hot, humid summers and brutally cold winters — a combination that stresses building envelopes in ways that milder climates never do. Condensation forms on the interior face of rim joists and foundation walls when indoor air hits cold concrete, and that moisture has nowhere to go in a tightly insulated home. Many homes in and around the 57030 ZIP code were built in the mid-twentieth century, when vapor barriers were either minimal or absent entirely. Older window framing, original plaster-and-lath ceilings, and stone or block foundations all hold moisture longer than modern assemblies, giving mold spores the damp, dark environment they need to colonize.

The area around Devil's Gulch also sees localized runoff events after heavy spring rains. Properties that sit in lower-lying lots can experience groundwater intrusion that never fully dries out before the next storm cycle arrives. By the time a homeowner notices visible mold, the growth is typically well established behind wall cavities or beneath flooring — not just on the surface.

## Our Mold Remediation Process in Garretson

Every mold remediation job starts with a thorough inspection — not just a visual scan, but moisture mapping with thermal imaging and pin-type meters to locate wet material that isn't visible to the eye. Once we understand the full extent of the affected area, we establish containment using negative air pressure and poly sheeting to prevent spores from migrating to unaffected rooms during the work.

Contaminated porous materials — drywall sections, insulation, wood framing with active growth — are removed, bagged, and disposed of properly. Remaining structural surfaces are treated with EPA-registered antimicrobial agents. HEPA air scrubbers run continuously throughout the project to capture airborne spores. Before any reconstruction begins, we verify that moisture readings have returned to acceptable baselines. Rebuilding over wet or inadequately treated material is how mold comes back, and we don't cut that corner.
