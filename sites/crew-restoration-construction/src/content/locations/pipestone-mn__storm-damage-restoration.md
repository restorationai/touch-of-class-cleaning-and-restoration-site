## Our Storm Damage Restoration Process in Pipestone

The first priority after any storm event is stopping ongoing water intrusion. That means emergency tarping of breached roofs, boarding broken windows, and placing interior containment to protect undamaged areas while a full damage assessment is completed. We photograph and document every affected surface before any material is removed — a step that matters enormously when working with insurance adjusters.

From there, the process moves in a deliberate sequence:

- **Structural triage**: identify compromised rafters, wall sheathing, or load-bearing elements before crews walk on damaged rooflines.
- **Water extraction and drying**: standing water under flooring or inside wall cavities begins promoting mold colonization within 24 to 48 hours in Minnesota's humid summer conditions. We deploy desiccant and refrigerant drying systems sized to the affected area.
- **Debris removal**: downed trees and branches are cleared with attention to utility line proximity — coordination with local utilities is standard before any chainsaw work near service drops.
- **Reconstruction**: siding, roofing, windows, and interior finishes are restored to pre-loss condition, with material selections that match existing profiles where code and aesthetics require it.

## Local Note

One detail that catches out-of-area crews working in Pipestone: the native Sioux quartzite used in many downtown buildings — including structures near the Pipestone National Monument — is extremely hard and does not accept standard masonry patching compounds well. When storm impact cracks a quartzite block or dislodges mortar, the repair requires a lime-based mortar mix matched to the original joint profile, applied in conditions above 40°F and protected from rapid drying. Using a modern Portland cement mix on those joints causes differential expansion and accelerates further cracking. It is a small detail, but getting it wrong on a historic structure can create a larger problem than the storm itself caused.
