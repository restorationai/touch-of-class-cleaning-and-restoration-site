## Why Adrian Properties See Mold Issues

Adrian sits in a flat, low-drainage corridor along I-90 in southwest Minnesota, and that geography matters when it comes to moisture. Spring snowmelt has nowhere to go quickly, and basement walls — especially in the older homes clustered around Downtown Adrian — are often uninsulated concrete block that wicks ground moisture inward for weeks after a wet season. Crawl spaces in these homes frequently lack adequate vapor barriers, creating a humid microclimate underneath living areas that mold spores find ideal.

The region also sees significant temperature swings between seasons. In winter, warm interior air hitting a cold exterior wall can condense inside wall cavities — a process that happens invisibly until a musty smell or a dark stain on drywall finally surfaces. By then, the colony may have been growing for weeks. Homes near the East Adrian area, many of which were built in the mid-20th century, tend to have attic insulation gaps that compound this problem, allowing moisture-laden air to pool in roof assemblies.

## Our Mold Remediation Process in Adrian

Every job starts with a thorough moisture and mold assessment — not just a visual scan, but thermal imaging and moisture meter readings to locate hidden saturation behind walls and under flooring. Once the extent of growth is mapped, the affected area is sealed off with polyethylene sheeting and negative-air pressure machines fitted with HEPA filtration. This containment step keeps spores from migrating to unaffected rooms while work is underway.

Contaminated materials — drywall, insulation, subfloor sections — are removed, double-bagged, and disposed of properly. Remaining structural surfaces are treated with EPA-registered antimicrobial agents and then dried to documented moisture content targets before any reconstruction begins. We don't close a job until moisture readings confirm the environment won't support regrowth. In older Adrian homes with plaster walls or original hardwood subfloors, that drying phase often takes longer than it would in newer construction, and we account for that in the project timeline rather than rushing it.
