## Why George Properties Face Distinct Storm Damage Challenges

George sits just inside the Lyon County line in Iowa's northwest corner, squarely in a corridor where cold Canadian air and warm Gulf moisture collide repeatedly from late spring through early fall. That collision produces hail events that strip shingles in minutes and straight-line winds that routinely exceed 70 mph — enough to shear off large branches or push whole trees onto rooflines. The town's older homes, many built before modern engineered lumber became standard, use dimensional lumber rafters that can crack or deflect under sudden point loads from falling debris rather than absorbing the impact cleanly.

Soil matters here, too. The heavy clay soils common in this part of Lyon County drain slowly, so when a storm drops two or three inches of rain in an hour, water pools against foundations and finds its way into basements and crawl spaces. Sump pumps that were adequate for normal rainfall can be overwhelmed in a matter of minutes, leaving standing water that begins damaging subflooring and framing before the storm has even passed.

## Our Storm Damage Restoration Process in George

The first priority when we arrive is a systematic exterior and interior assessment — documenting every point of water intrusion before anything is moved or dried. We photograph roof penetrations, window failures, and siding breaches, then cross-reference them with interior moisture readings using thermal imaging and pin-type meters. This documentation matters for your insurance adjuster, and it also tells us exactly where water has traveled inside the wall assembly.

Once the entry points are identified, we install emergency tarping or board-up as needed to stop ongoing exposure. From there, the work shifts to extraction and structural drying — industrial air movers and desiccant or refrigerant dehumidifiers sized to the actual cubic footage of the affected space, not a one-size-fits-all setup. Debris removal, including tree limbs and damaged roofing materials, is handled before interior drying begins so crews aren't working around hazards. After drying is confirmed with final moisture readings, reconstruction — framing, sheathing, roofing, siding, drywall — follows in sequence.
