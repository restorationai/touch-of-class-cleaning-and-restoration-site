## Why Harrisburg Properties See Mold Issues

Lincoln County's climate sits in a zone where summer humidity regularly climbs above 70 percent, then swings to sub-zero winters that push moisture into wall cavities as warm interior air meets cold exterior sheathing. That condensation cycle is particularly punishing in homes built quickly to keep pace with Harrisburg's growth — builders working fast sometimes leave small gaps in housewrap or install insulation before framing lumber has dried to acceptable moisture content. The result is mold that hides inside walls for months before it becomes visible or smellable.

Basements in the 57032 ZIP code also tend to sit on expansive clay soils that hold groundwater close to the surface after heavy spring rains. A slow foundation seep — even one that dries out between rain events — is enough to keep concrete block and wood framing at moisture levels where mold thrives. Homeowners often assume that because standing water is gone, the problem is solved. It isn't. Mold can colonize porous materials in as little as 24 to 48 hours after a moisture event, and it doesn't need standing water to keep growing.

## Our Mold Remediation Process in Harrisburg

Every job starts with a thorough visual inspection and moisture mapping. We use thermal imaging and calibrated moisture meters to find wet zones that aren't visible to the eye — inside wall cavities, under flooring, behind cabinetry. Once we understand the full extent of contamination, we establish containment: negative air pressure barriers that prevent spores from migrating to clean areas of the home while work is underway.

Contaminated materials — drywall, insulation, wood framing that can't be cleaned to bare substrate — are removed, bagged, and disposed of according to South Dakota solid waste guidelines. Remaining structural surfaces are HEPA-vacuumed and treated with an EPA-registered antimicrobial. Air scrubbers with HEPA filtration run continuously throughout the process to capture airborne spores. Before containment comes down, we conduct a final moisture check to confirm that the source of the problem has been addressed — because remediation without fixing the underlying moisture issue just means mold comes back.

If the job involves reconstruction — new drywall, insulation, or finish work — Crew Restoration & Construction handles that phase too, so you're not coordinating a separate contractor after the remediation crew leaves.

Mold claims in South Dakota can be complicated. Many homeowners' policies cover mold remediation only when it results directly from a covered peril — a burst pipe, for example — and exclude mold that developed from long-term seepage or deferred maintenance. One pattern we've noticed in Harrisburg's newer subdivisions is that homes with three-car garages and finished bonus rooms above them tend to develop mold in the bonus room ceiling when the garage below isn't conditioned in winter.
