## Why Harrisburg Properties See Sewage Backup Issues

Harrisburg sits in one of the fastest-growing corridors in South Dakota, and that pace of development creates specific plumbing vulnerabilities. Many homes in the Journey subdivision and surrounding areas were built on expansive clay-heavy soils common to the eastern South Dakota prairie. ## Our Sewage Cleanup and Sanitization Process in Harrisburg

Sewage is classified as Category 3 water — the most contaminated classification in the industry — which means the cleanup protocol is more involved than a standard water loss. When we arrive at a Harrisburg property, the first step is containment: isolating the affected area to prevent cross-contamination to unaffected rooms, especially in open-concept floor plans common in newer construction.

From there, the process moves through these stages:

- **Extraction and removal** — Standing sewage water is extracted using truck-mounted or portable units. Porous materials that have absorbed contamination — drywall, carpet, pad, insulation — are carefully removed and bagged for disposal per South Dakota solid waste guidelines.
- **Surface disinfection** — Hard surfaces, framing lumber, and concrete are treated with EPA-registered disinfectants rated for Category 3 contamination. We apply, dwell, and reapply as needed based on surface porosity.
- **Structural drying** — After sanitization, remaining structural materials are dried using commercial dehumidifiers and air movers. Moisture readings are logged until materials reach acceptable levels.
- **Deodorization** — Sewage odor penetrates deeply. We use hydroxyl or thermal fogging treatments to address odor at the molecular level, not just mask it.

Because many Harrisburg homes have finished basements — a popular feature in this market — we pay close attention to wall cavities and under-slab areas where sewage moisture can hide long after the visible mess is gone.

## Local Note

One thing that comes up in Harrisburg's newer subdivisions that doesn't get talked about enough: many homes built in the last decade use engineered lumber — LVL beams, I-joists, and OSB subfloor — rather than traditional dimensional lumber. Engineered wood products absorb Category 3 sewage contamination differently than solid wood. OSB in particular can delaminate quickly once it's wet, and it doesn't dry out the same way solid lumber does. In practice, this means we often recommend removing and replacing OSB subfloor sections rather than attempting to dry them in place — a call that saves homeowners from hidden structural degradation months down the road.
