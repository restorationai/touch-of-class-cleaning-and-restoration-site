## Our Water Cleanup Process in Parker

Effective emergency water cleanup is a sequence, not a single step. The process starts with locating the source and stopping active water flow, then moves into extraction, structural drying, and moisture verification before any repair work begins.

**Extraction first.** Truck-mounted and portable extraction units pull standing water from hard floors, carpet, and subfloor assemblies. In homes with finished basements, that means moving furniture, pulling saturated pad, and getting equipment into tight corners where water collects.

**Drying to a standard, not a schedule.** Desiccant dehumidifiers and high-velocity air movers work together to drive moisture out of framing, insulation, and wall cavities. Work is calibrated using moisture meters and thermal imaging so drying targets are based on actual readings, not guesswork. The IICRC S500 standard guides the process from extraction through final clearance.

**Documentation throughout.** Moisture readings, equipment placement logs, and photographs are captured at each stage. This documentation matters for insurance claims and gives you a clear record of what was affected and how it was addressed.

## Local Note

One thing worth knowing about older homes in the Downtown Parker area: many were built with plaster-and-lath interior walls rather than modern drywall. Plaster absorbs water more slowly than drywall, which can make a wall feel sound at the surface even when the lath behind it is saturated. Moisture meters read differently on plaster, and drying times run longer because the material releases moisture gradually. If a crew treats a plaster wall the same way they would treat drywall and pulls equipment too early, hidden moisture can persist and create conditions for mold growth weeks after the job closes. Knowing the construction type before setting drying targets makes a real difference in outcome.
