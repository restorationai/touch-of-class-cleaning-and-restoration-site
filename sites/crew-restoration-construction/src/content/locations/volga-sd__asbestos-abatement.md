## Why Volga Properties Are Prone to Asbestos-Containing Materials

South Dakota's rural communities grew steadily through mid-century, and Volga was no exception. Builders in this region relied heavily on materials that were affordable, fire-resistant, and widely available — descriptions that fit asbestos-containing products almost perfectly during that era. Popcorn ceilings (also called acoustic or textured ceilings) were applied in homes and small commercial buildings through the late 1970s. Vinyl floor tiles and the black mastic adhesive beneath them, which remain common in Volga-area basements and kitchens, frequently contain chrysotile asbestos. Pipe wrap insulation in older mechanical rooms is another recurring find.

The climate adds a layer of complexity. Eastern South Dakota experiences significant freeze-thaw cycling each winter, and that movement stresses building materials over decades. Brittle or crumbling asbestos-containing materials — what regulators call "friable" asbestos — release fibers more readily than intact materials. A basement wall that has shifted slightly, a ceiling that has absorbed moisture from a roof leak, or ductwork that has been patched and re-patched over the years can all present elevated fiber-release risk by the time a renovation crew shows up.

## Our Asbestos Abatement Process in Volga

Every project starts with a proper inspection and bulk sampling — not guesswork. Suspect materials are collected following EPA sampling protocols and sent to an accredited laboratory. Results typically come back within a few business days, and we walk you through what the report means in plain language before any abatement work is scheduled.

Once asbestos-containing materials are confirmed, the abatement itself follows a structured sequence:

- **Containment setup**: Poly sheeting, negative air pressure units, and HEPA-filtered exhaust fans isolate the work area from the rest of the structure. This is especially important in older Volga homes where HVAC ductwork may run through multiple floors.
- **Wet removal**: Asbestos materials are kept wet throughout removal to suppress fiber release. Dry scraping is never acceptable practice.
- **Waste packaging and disposal**: All removed material is double-bagged in labeled, sealed poly bags and transported to a licensed disposal facility. South Dakota follows EPA NESHAP requirements for asbestos waste disposal, and we document the chain of custody.
- **Air clearance**: After removal and cleanup, air samples are collected to confirm fiber counts are below clearance thresholds before containment is torn down and the space is returned to you.
