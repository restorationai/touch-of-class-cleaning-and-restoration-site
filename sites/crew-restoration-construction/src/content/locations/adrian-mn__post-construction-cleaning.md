## Why Adrian Properties See Distinct Post-Construction Cleaning Challenges

Adrian sits squarely in southwest Minnesota's agricultural corridor, and that geography shapes what ends up on a jobsite. Wind off the open prairie carries fine topsoil through any gap in temporary weatherproofing, and that soil — rich in clay and organic matter — sticks to freshly painted walls and freshly laid LVP flooring in ways that standard sweeping won't fix. Construction crews working in the 56110 ZIP code also deal with a shorter outdoor working window in spring and fall, which means more muddy boot traffic through interior spaces before exterior work is complete.

Older structures in Downtown Adrian — some dating to the early 20th century — present a specific challenge: original plaster ceilings and wood-lath walls absorb construction dust differently than modern gypsum board. That dust doesn't wipe off; it has to be HEPA-vacuumed in sequence before any damp wiping begins, or you end up grinding particles deeper into a porous surface. Newer builds on the east side of town tend to use engineered materials that are more forgiving but still require careful sequencing — clean the ceiling plane before the floors, clean the floors before the fixtures, and never use alkaline cleaners on aluminum window frames that are common in recent construction.

## Our Post-Construction and Specialty Cleaning Process in Adrian

Every post-construction clean follows a top-down, inside-out sequence. We start with rough-out: removing bulk debris, bagging drywall scraps, and clearing any materials the trades left behind. From there, the process moves through three distinct phases.

**Phase 1 — Detail clean.** HEPA vacuuming of all surfaces including inside cabinets, window tracks, HVAC registers, and light fixture boxes. This step is non-negotiable in Adrian's climate because fine prairie dust that bypasses this step will redistribute the moment the HVAC system kicks on.

**Phase 2 — Wash and wipe.** Hard surfaces get wiped with appropriate cleaners matched to the material — stone countertops, painted drywall, tile grout, and stainless fixtures each require a different chemistry. We remove construction adhesive from glass using plastic razors rather than metal, protecting the low-e coatings common on energy-efficient windows installed in newer Minnesota builds.

**Phase 3 — Final inspection and touch.** We walk the space with a high-lumen flashlight at a raking angle — the same technique used to inspect paint finishes — to catch dust, smears, or adhesive residue that overhead lighting hides. Floors get a final damp mop, and fixtures are polished before we hand off.

Specialty cleaning requests — pressure washing exterior flatwork, cleaning post-fire or post-flood construction debris, or sanitizing spaces after renovation in occupied buildings — are scoped individually and quoted before any work begins.
