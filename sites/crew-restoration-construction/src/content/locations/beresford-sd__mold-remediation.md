## Why Beresford Properties See Mold Issues

The housing stock in and around Downtown Beresford skews older, with a significant number of homes built before modern vapor barriers and ventilation standards became code requirements. Crawl spaces in these properties often lack adequate cross-ventilation, and basement walls are frequently uninsulated concrete block — a material that wicks groundwater readily during the heavy spring snowmelt that follows a typical South Dakota winter. When that moisture has nowhere to go, it condenses on interior surfaces and feeds mold colonies behind drywall, under subfloors, and inside wall cavities long before the problem becomes visible.

The flat, clay-heavy soil common to this part of Union County also drains slowly. A backed-up sump pump or a slow roof leak during a spring storm can saturate a crawl space in hours, and the standing water lingers. Homes near the Beresford Golf Course area, where lots tend to sit at slightly lower grades, can be especially vulnerable to that kind of subsurface moisture intrusion after a heavy rain event.

## Our Mold Remediation Process in Beresford

Every job starts with a thorough visual inspection and moisture mapping — we use thermal imaging and calibrated moisture meters to find wet building materials that aren't visible to the naked eye. That step matters here because older Beresford homes sometimes have multiple layers of flooring or original plaster beneath newer finishes, and mold can be actively growing in those hidden layers while the surface looks dry.

Once we've identified the affected zones, we establish containment using heavy-gauge poly sheeting and negative air pressure to prevent spores from migrating to unaffected rooms while work is underway. Affected porous materials — drywall, insulation, subfloor sections — are removed and bagged for proper disposal. Non-porous structural elements are cleaned using EPA-registered antimicrobial solutions and dried to target moisture levels before any reconstruction begins. We don't close a job until moisture readings confirm the structure is dry, because sealing over elevated moisture is how mold comes back.
