## Why Yankton Properties See Recurring Mold Problems

Yankton's position along the Missouri River means the local water table is rarely far from the surface, and seasonal fluctuations at Lewis and Clark Lake push moisture into foundations that weren't engineered with today's waterproofing standards. Many homes in the Riverside neighborhood and older sections of Downtown Yankton were built before vapor barriers were standard practice — some date to the 1920s and 1930s — which means crawl spaces and basement walls absorb ground moisture through the concrete itself rather than through a visible leak.

The region also sees freeze-thaw cycling through late winter and early spring. When temperatures swing between the teens and the upper 40s over the course of a week, condensation forms on cold surfaces inside walls and attic sheathing. If that moisture isn't driven out quickly, mold colonies can begin establishing within 24 to 72 hours on organic materials like wood framing and paper-faced insulation. By the time a musty smell is noticeable in a living space, the growth is often already several weeks old.

## Our Mold Remediation Process in Yankton

Every job starts with a thorough moisture mapping inspection — not just a visual check of the visible surface, but thermal imaging and moisture meter readings across walls, floors, and ceiling assemblies. This matters in older Yankton homes because mold frequently hides behind original plaster, underneath vinyl flooring laid over wood subfloor, or inside wall cavities where a slow supply-line drip went unnoticed for months.

Once we've identified the full scope, we establish containment using negative air pressure and heavy-duty poly barriers to prevent spores from migrating to unaffected areas of the home during removal. Affected porous materials — drywall, insulation, compromised wood framing — are removed and bagged per EPA guidelines. Structural wood surfaces that can be cleaned rather than removed are treated with an EPA-registered antimicrobial agent and dried to measured moisture content before any reconstruction begins.

We don't consider the job done when the visible mold is gone. Air quality verification — either through our own post-remediation testing or a third-party industrial hygienist — confirms that spore counts in the treated area have returned to levels consistent with the outdoor baseline before we close out the work order.
