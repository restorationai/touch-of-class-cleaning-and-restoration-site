## Why Kingsley Properties See Sewage Backup Issues

Kingsley sits in the rolling Loess Hills at an elevation that makes natural drainage unpredictable. The fine, wind-deposited loess soil compacts under frost and then shifts when it thaws, which puts lateral sewer lines under seasonal stress year after year. Homes built before the 1970s — a significant portion of the housing stock in and around the 51028 ZIP code — were often plumbed with vitrified clay pipe. That material is brittle, prone to root intrusion from the large cottonwoods and elms that line many residential streets, and difficult to inspect without a camera. A single root mass can cause a partial blockage that goes unnoticed until a heavy rain or a full household load pushes sewage back through the lowest floor drain.

Septic systems are equally common on the rural edges of town, and an overfull or failing drain field can push raw effluent into a basement just as quickly as a municipal line backup. Properties near South Kingsley, where lots tend to be larger and septic service is more prevalent, see this pattern regularly in late spring when the water table is highest.

## Our Sewage Cleanup and Sanitization Process in Kingsley

Sewage cleanup is not the same as water damage cleanup, and the process reflects that difference at every step.

**Containment first.** Before extraction begins, affected areas are isolated to prevent cross-contamination from spreading to unaffected rooms. Porous materials — carpet, pad, lower sections of drywall, insulation — that have contacted raw sewage are removed and bagged for disposal rather than dried in place. There is no safe way to dry Category 3 contamination out of a porous substrate.

**Extraction and removal.** Truck-mounted extraction equipment pulls standing liquid from floors and subfloor cavities. In older Kingsley homes with tongue-and-groove hardwood subfloors, sewage can wick laterally several feet beyond the visible puddle, so moisture mapping with thermal imaging guides the scope of removal.

**Sanitization.** After structural materials are removed, all remaining hard surfaces — concrete, block, floor joists, wall framing — are treated with EPA-registered disinfectants formulated for Category 3 contamination. This is not a one-pass wipe-down; dwell time matters, and surfaces are treated in sequence to ensure contact time is met.

**Drying and clearance.** Once contaminated materials are out and surfaces are disinfected, desiccant or refrigerant dehumidifiers and air movers bring structural moisture levels back to pre-loss readings. We document moisture readings throughout so there is a clear record that drying was completed — not assumed.
