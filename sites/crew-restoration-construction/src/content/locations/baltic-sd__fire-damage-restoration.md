## Why Baltic Properties Face Particular Challenges After a Fire

Baltic's housing stock leans heavily toward older single-family homes, many built when balloon-frame construction was standard. In balloon framing, wall cavities run continuously from the foundation sill to the roof rafters — there are no fire stops between floors. That means smoke, heat, and soot travel vertically through the entire wall system even when the visible fire is confined to one room. What looks like a kitchen fire on the surface often means smoke odor and residue have migrated into attic insulation and basement rim joists before the flames are out.

The region's climate adds another layer. Baltic sits in a zone where winter temperatures can drop well below zero and spring brings rapid snowmelt into the Big Sioux River floodplain. Homes that have already dealt with moisture intrusion — cracked foundations, damp crawl spaces — are more vulnerable to accelerated mold colonization after firefighting water is introduced. Restoration crews working here need to account for pre-existing moisture conditions, not just the water from hoses.

## Our Fire Damage Restoration Process in Baltic

Every fire loss is different, but the sequence of work follows a disciplined order that protects both the structure and the people living near it.

**Emergency stabilization** comes first: boarding windows and doors, tarping compromised roof sections, and shutting off utilities if that hasn't already been done by the responding fire department. This step matters especially in Baltic winters, where an unsecured opening can drop interior temperatures fast enough to freeze wet materials and complicate drying.

**Soot and smoke removal** follows a surface-by-category approach. Dry soot from fast-burning fires (common in wood-frame homes) requires dry chemical sponges before any wet cleaning — introducing moisture to dry soot smears it deeper into porous surfaces. Wet or oily soot from synthetic materials needs alkaline cleaning agents. Crew Restoration & Construction's technicians assess the soot type in each room before selecting the cleaning method, rather than applying a single approach throughout.

**Structural drying and odor control** run concurrently. Industrial air movers, dehumidifiers, and hydroxyl generators work together to pull residual firefighting moisture out of framing and subfloors while beginning to neutralize smoke odor compounds embedded in wood and insulation. In Baltic's older homes, this phase often takes longer than it would in newer construction because aged lumber and plaster hold moisture differently than modern engineered materials.

**Reconstruction** closes the project — framing repairs, drywall, insulation replacement, painting, and finish work — so the property is returned to pre-loss condition rather than left as a remediated shell.
