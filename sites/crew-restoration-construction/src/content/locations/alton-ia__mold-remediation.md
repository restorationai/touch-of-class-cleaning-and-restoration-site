## Why Alton Properties See Mold Issues

Alton sits in a part of Iowa where the climate swings hard — frigid winters drive condensation into poorly insulated rim joists and band boards, and humid summers push moisture through foundation walls that were never designed to handle modern vapor loads. Many homes near Downtown Alton were built in the mid-20th century, when vapor barriers were minimal or absent entirely. That older construction style means mold often hides behind original wood paneling, inside wall cavities above basement windows, and beneath vinyl flooring that was laid directly over concrete without a moisture break.

The town's proximity to the Floyd River also means that low-lying properties — particularly those on the east side of town — can experience rising groundwater during heavy rain events. When that water wicks up through a foundation and sits undetected for even a few days, the conditions for black mold growth become nearly ideal. Sioux County's clay-heavy soil retains water longer than sandy substrates, which extends the drying window for any moisture intrusion and increases the risk of secondary mold development.

## Our Mold Remediation Process in Alton

Every job starts with a thorough visual inspection and moisture mapping. We use thermal imaging cameras and calibrated moisture meters to find hidden saturation behind walls and under flooring — areas that look dry to the eye but are still feeding active mold colonies. Once the scope is documented, we establish physical containment using negative air pressure and poly barriers to prevent spores from migrating to unaffected rooms during the removal process.

Contaminated materials — drywall, insulation, subflooring, framing in severe cases — are removed, bagged, and disposed of according to Iowa solid waste guidelines. We then treat affected structural surfaces with EPA-registered antimicrobial agents, followed by HEPA air scrubbing to capture airborne spores. Drying equipment stays on-site until moisture readings return to regional baseline levels, which in Alton's climate typically means accounting for ambient outdoor humidity before declaring a space dry. We do not close out a job until the numbers confirm it.
