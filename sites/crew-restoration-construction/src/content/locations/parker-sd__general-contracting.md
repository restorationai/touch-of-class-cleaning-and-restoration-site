## Why Parker Properties Present Unique Renovation Challenges

South Dakota's climate is genuinely hard on buildings. Parker experiences temperature swings that can exceed 100°F between a January low and a July high, and that thermal cycling works on every joint, every sill, and every masonry course in an older structure. Frost heave is a real concern in Turner County — clay-heavy soils shift seasonally, and foundations that were adequate in 1955 may have settled unevenly by now. Before any kitchen remodel or bathroom renovation touches finish materials, a thorough look at the subfloor, the load-bearing walls, and the foundation condition tells us whether cosmetic work alone is the right call or whether the structure needs attention first.

Many of the homes within a few blocks of the Turner County Fairgrounds area were built during the mid-twentieth century building boom that followed rural electrification. That era produced solid bones — balloon framing, real hardwood floors, plaster walls — but also some challenges: knob-and-tube wiring that hasn't been fully replaced, galvanized supply lines that restrict water pressure, and insulation levels that fall well short of current South Dakota energy code. A general contracting team that doesn't account for those realities will quote a bathroom remodel and then discover mid-project that the walls need rewiring before tile can go up.
