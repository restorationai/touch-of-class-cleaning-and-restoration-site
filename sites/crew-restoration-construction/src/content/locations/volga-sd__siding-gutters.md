## Why Volga Properties See Siding and Gutter Problems

Volga sits on the eastern edge of Brookings County where prevailing northwest winds hit exterior walls with little natural windbreak. That consistent wind exposure accelerates the failure of older lap siding — paint chalks, seams open, and moisture infiltration follows. Many of the homes in the Oakwood area and along the streets feeding into Downtown Volga were built in eras when vinyl and fiber cement weren't yet standard, meaning original wood siding is still common. Wood holds up beautifully when it's maintained, but once a caulk joint fails or a section of gutter overflow soaks the same board repeatedly, rot sets in fast — sometimes within a single wet season.

Gutters on these properties face a compounding problem: the cottonwood and oak trees that give the Oakwood area its character shed debris heavily in fall, and a single overnight freeze can turn a partially clogged gutter into a solid ice dam that pries the hanger hardware right off the fascia. Seamless aluminum gutters with properly spaced hangers and covered leaf guards make a measurable difference in how often you're climbing a ladder in November.

## Our Siding and Gutter Process in Volga

Every project starts with a full exterior inspection — not just the section that looks damaged, but the entire envelope. Wind-driven rain in this part of South Dakota doesn't respect the corner you didn't check. We probe for soft spots behind siding, inspect flashing at windows and rakes, and run a downspout flow test to confirm water is actually exiting where it should.

For siding replacement, we remove the existing material down to sheathing, assess for moisture damage or deteriorated house wrap, and install new material with manufacturer-specified fastening patterns for high-wind zones. Fiber cement is a strong choice for Volga's climate — it doesn't expand and contract with humidity swings the way vinyl can, and it holds paint for decades rather than years. For gutter work, we size the system to the actual roof square footage and pitch rather than defaulting to a standard 5-inch profile. Properties near Oakwood Lakes State Park, where seasonal lake-home use means structures can sit unoccupied through winter, often benefit from oversized 6-inch gutters that reduce overflow risk when no one is around to notice a blockage.
