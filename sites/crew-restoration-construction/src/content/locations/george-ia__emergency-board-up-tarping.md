## Our Emergency Board-Up and Tarping Process in George

When a call comes in from the George area, the first thing the crew does is assess the scope remotely — number of openings, roof pitch, approximate square footage of exposed decking — so the right materials are loaded before the truck leaves. Arriving underprepared to a rural Iowa address and having to make a second supply run wastes time that the structure doesn't have.

On-site, the process follows a deliberate sequence:

1. **Perimeter walk** — every opening, crack, and compromised section is photographed and logged before anything is touched. This documentation matters for your insurance adjuster.
2. **Roof tarping** — heavy-duty polyethylene tarps (typically 6 mil or heavier for Iowa weather) are secured with furring strips or cap nails driven into undamaged decking, not just draped and weighted. A tarp that lifts in a 40 mph gust is worse than no tarp at all.
3. **Window and door board-up** — plywood is cut to fit the actual opening, not approximated. Edges are sealed and fasteners are set to hold through multiple weather cycles, not just one night.
4. **Interior moisture check** — if rain or fire suppression water has already entered, the crew notes it in the report so the remediation team knows what they're walking into.

## George Insurance Coordination

Board-up and tarping is almost universally covered under the "protection of property" clause in standard homeowner's policies — it's considered a mitigation step, not a repair, which means it typically doesn't count against your deductible the same way structural repairs do. We photograph every opening before and after boarding, document material quantities, and provide a written scope that matches the format most Iowa adjusters expect. If your carrier wants a second look before permanent repairs begin, the board-up stays in place until that inspection is complete.

## Local Note

One thing that catches out-of-area crews in small Lyon County towns like George: the older homes here often have non-standard roof overhangs and shallow fascia boards that don't give you much purchase for tarp anchor points. A crew used to working newer construction in larger markets will sometimes anchor into the fascia itself, which can pull free in high wind and cause secondary damage. The right approach in these homes is to run furring strips across the roof deck above the damaged section and anchor the tarp there — it takes an extra 20 minutes but holds through a genuine Iowa storm.
