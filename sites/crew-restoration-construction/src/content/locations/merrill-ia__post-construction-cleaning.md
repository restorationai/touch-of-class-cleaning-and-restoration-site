## Why Merrill Properties Present Unique Post-Construction Cleaning Challenges

Merrill sits in northwest Iowa where the climate swings hard — humid summers push moisture into freshly drywalled spaces before the HVAC system is even commissioned, and cold winters mean construction often continues indoors through February, trapping fine particulate dust that has nowhere to go. Older homes in East Merrill, many built in the mid-20th century, frequently get gut-renovated rather than torn down, which means crews are working around original plaster, older millwork, and cast-iron plumbing that generates a different debris profile than new construction. Plaster dust is finer and more alkaline than standard drywall compound dust — it can etch glass and dull chrome fixtures if it sits too long after a renovation wraps.

Newer builds on the outskirts of town face a different issue: Iowa's heavy clay soils mean construction traffic churns up a thick, reddish-brown mud that gets tracked through unfinished interiors and dries into a concrete-like crust on subfloors and concrete slabs. Removing that without damaging new flooring requires the right tools and the right sequence — not just a mop and a bucket.

## Our Post-Construction Cleaning Process in Merrill

Every post-construction clean follows a top-down, inside-out sequence that prevents re-contamination of surfaces you've already cleaned.

**Rough clean first.** Before any detail work, the crew removes bulk debris — scrap lumber, packaging, drywall cutoffs, and any materials the trades left behind. This stage also includes vacuuming rough-in cavities and chases before drywall is sealed, if the schedule allows.

**Detail clean.** This is where the time goes. Window tracks, door hardware, cabinet interiors, light fixtures, grout lines, and HVAC registers all get individual attention. On renovation projects in older Downtown Merrill homes, this often means cleaning around original woodwork and leaded-glass windows that can't be treated with standard commercial degreasers.

**Final clean.** Immediately before the client walkthrough, the team does a light final pass — buffing glass, wiping switch plates, and spot-cleaning any surfaces disturbed during punch-list repairs. This is the stage most cleaning crews skip, and it's the one homeowners notice most.

**Specialty cleaning add-ons.** Post-construction work sometimes surfaces needs beyond standard cleanup: pressure washing exterior concrete, cleaning HVAC ductwork after drywall work, or removing paint overspray from windows and hardware. These can be scoped in during the initial site visit.
