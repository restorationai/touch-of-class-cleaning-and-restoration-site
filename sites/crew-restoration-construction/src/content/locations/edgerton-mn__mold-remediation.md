## Why Edgerton Properties See Mold Issues

Edgerton sits in Pipestone County where the clay-heavy prairie soil drains slowly. During spring thaw and heavy rain events, that soil holds water against foundation walls longer than sandier soils further east. Older homes in the community — many built in the mid-20th century to serve the area's agricultural families — often have unfinished or minimally insulated basements with block or poured-concrete walls that wick moisture inward. Once relative humidity inside a basement climbs above 60%, mold spores that are always present in the air find the conditions they need to take hold.

The community's Dutch heritage shows in its tightly built, well-maintained homes, but tight construction cuts both ways: good insulation traps heat in winter, and it also traps moisture vapor that has nowhere to escape. Attic spaces and crawlspaces are particularly vulnerable when ventilation wasn't designed to handle modern heating loads.

## Our Mold Remediation Process in Edgerton

When we arrive at a property in the 56128 ZIP code, the first step is always assessment — not demolition. A technician walks the structure to identify moisture sources, measure humidity levels with a calibrated meter, and locate hidden mold growth using thermal imaging where wall temperatures suggest concealed wet zones.

From there, the process follows a defined sequence:

- **Containment**: Plastic sheeting and negative air pressure isolate the affected area so spores aren't redistributed through the HVAC system or into clean rooms during work.
- **HEPA filtration**: Air scrubbers run continuously throughout the job, capturing airborne particulates down to 0.3 microns.
- **Removal of affected materials**: Porous materials — drywall, insulation, carpet padding — that test positive and cannot be cleaned are removed and bagged for disposal. Structural lumber that shows surface mold but remains sound is treated and encapsulated rather than replaced wherever possible, which matters in older homes where dimensional lumber is harder to match.
- **Antimicrobial treatment**: Framing, subfloor, and masonry surfaces are treated to address residual spores.
- **Drying and verification**: Moisture readings are taken again before any reconstruction begins. If the source of moisture isn't corrected first, mold returns — so we document the moisture intrusion point and can coordinate repairs as part of our reconstruction work.
