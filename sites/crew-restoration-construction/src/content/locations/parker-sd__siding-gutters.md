## Why Parker Properties See Siding and Gutter Issues

Turner County sits in a climate corridor where freeze-thaw cycles are aggressive. Water finds its way behind even well-installed siding, freezes, expands, and widens the gap on the next cycle. Over several winters, that process can crack fiber cement, warp wood lap, and cause aluminum siding — common on homes built in the 1960s and 1970s throughout Parker — to separate at the seams. Gutters face a parallel problem: ice damming at the eave pushes standing water under the first course of siding, and if the gutter is already pulling away from a rotting fascia board, the damage accelerates fast.

The housing stock around Downtown Parker and the blocks surrounding the Turner County Courthouse skews older, with many homes originally built before modern housewrap and moisture barriers were standard. That means a siding replacement project here isn't simply a swap of one panel system for another — it often involves assessing the underlying sheathing, addressing any rot at the sill or corner boards, and installing a proper water-resistive barrier before new material goes up.
