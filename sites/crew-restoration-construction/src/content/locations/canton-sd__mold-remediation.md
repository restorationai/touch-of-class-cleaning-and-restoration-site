## Why Canton Properties See Mold Issues

Canton sits in a humid continental climate zone — hot, humid summers followed by freeze-thaw cycles that stress masonry and window seals every single year. Homes in the Downtown Canton area and along streets closer to the Big Sioux River tend to be older, often with stone or poured-concrete foundations that were never designed with modern vapor barriers in mind. Those foundations wick ground moisture upward, especially after a wet spring or a heavy summer storm.

Crawl spaces are a particular problem here. Many Canton homes were built with vented crawl spaces that made sense under older building science but actively pull in humid exterior air during July and August. When that air hits cooler surfaces below the floor deck, condensation forms — and condensation is all mold needs to get started. Add in any minor plumbing leak, a slow dryer vent discharge, or a poorly sealed rim joist, and you have the conditions that show up repeatedly on mold jobs in this part of Lincoln County.

## Our Mold Remediation Process in Canton

Every job starts with a thorough visual inspection and moisture mapping. We use thermal imaging cameras and calibrated moisture meters to find elevated readings behind drywall, under flooring, and inside wall cavities — areas where mold is actively growing before it's visible. This step matters especially in older Canton homes where original plaster-and-lath construction is still present; plaster holds moisture differently than modern drywall and can harbor mold deep in the substrate even when the surface looks dry.

Once we understand the full scope, we establish containment. Negative air pressure barriers isolate the affected area from the rest of the home so that disturbing mold colonies during removal doesn't spread spores through your HVAC system or into clean rooms. HEPA-filtered air scrubbers run continuously throughout the remediation.

Physical removal follows — affected porous materials (drywall, insulation, wood framing where necessary) are bagged and removed per EPA guidelines. Hard surfaces are treated with EPA-registered antimicrobial agents. We then dry the structural cavity to confirmed moisture levels before any reconstruction begins. Skipping that drying phase is the most common reason mold returns after a remediation job, and we don't skip it.

## Local Note

One pattern we see repeatedly in Canton's older housing stock: homes built before the 1970s often have original cellulose insulation packed into wall cavities, sometimes combined with a later addition of fiberglass batts. Cellulose is highly hygroscopic — it absorbs and holds moisture far longer than fiberglass — and it provides an ideal growth medium for mold once it gets wet. When we open walls in these homes, the visible mold on the drywall face is often just the leading edge of a much larger colony embedded in the insulation behind it. Scoping that correctly at the inspection stage changes the remediation plan significantly, and it's something we look for specifically on Canton jobs in the pre-1970 neighborhoods near Downtown.
