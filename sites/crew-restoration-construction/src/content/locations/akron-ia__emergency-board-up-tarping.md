## Why Akron Properties Are Especially Vulnerable After a Storm

Akron sits at the western edge of Iowa, right where the Big Sioux River marks the South Dakota line. That geography puts local homes in the path of weather systems that have already built momentum across open prairie before they arrive. The town's housing stock reflects its agricultural roots — a significant share of homes near Downtown Akron and along the River district were built in the mid-20th century or earlier, when construction standards for wind uplift were far less rigorous than today's Iowa building code.

Older roof decking in this era was often 1×6 or 1×8 tongue-and-groove boards rather than modern OSB panels. That material is durable under normal conditions, but when a shingle layer is compromised, water infiltrates the seams quickly and the decking can begin to delaminate within 24 to 48 hours. A properly installed polyethylene tarp — secured with 2×4 battens screwed through the tarp and into the decking, not just bungee-corded over the ridge — can hold against sustained winds and keep that decking dry until a roofer can schedule permanent repairs.

Windows are another vulnerability. Homes in the blocks around Akron-Westfield School and the older residential streets feeding into Downtown Akron frequently have single-pane or older double-pane units in wood frames. A broken window in those frames isn't just a weather opening — it's a structural one, because the frame itself may have softened from decades of moisture cycling. Boarding those openings correctly means assessing the frame condition first, not just slapping plywood over the hole.

## Our Board-Up and Tarping Process in Akron

Every job starts with a quick exterior walk to catalog every opening — broken windows, compromised doors, roof breaches, and any soffit or fascia damage that could let wind-driven rain enter the attic. We photograph everything before touching it, both for your insurance documentation and to establish a baseline for the repair estimate.

For roof tarping, we use 6-mil reinforced poly tarps sized to overlap the ridge by at least two feet on each side. Battens are screwed — never stapled — into the decking at 12-inch intervals along every edge. On older homes with steeper pitches common to this part of Iowa, we use fall-arrest anchoring while working, which slows the job slightly but keeps our crew safe and keeps us on the job site rather than in an emergency room.

For window and door board-up, we cut ¾-inch CDX plywood to fit each opening, pre-drill the edges, and fasten into the surrounding framing rather than the window casing. That approach holds against wind pressure without causing additional damage to the trim — important when the goal is to preserve as much of the original material as possible for the insurance adjuster's review.

We document measurements, materials used, and labor time in a written scope that translates directly into the format most Iowa insurance carriers expect to see.
