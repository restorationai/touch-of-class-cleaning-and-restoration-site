## Why Adrian Properties Present Unique Renovation Challenges

Adrian sits right along I-90 at Exit 26 in Nobles County, which puts it squarely in a climate zone that swings from -20°F wind chills in January to 95°F humidity in July. That thermal range stresses every building system — framing, insulation, windows, mechanical — and it shapes every renovation decision. Homes built before 1970, which make up a significant portion of Adrian's residential stock, were often insulated to standards that made sense at the time but leave modern homeowners with sky-high utility bills and moisture problems in wall cavities.

Foundation movement is another recurring issue. The clay-heavy soils common to this part of Nobles County expand when wet and contract when dry, which means settling is gradual but relentless. A bathroom remodel that ignores a slightly out-of-level subfloor today can mean cracked tile and failed grout joints within two winters. Crew Restoration & Construction scopes every renovation project with those structural realities in mind, not just the finish work a homeowner can see.

One thing that catches out-of-area contractors in Adrian: the older homes near St. Adrian Catholic Church and the surrounding blocks were built with full-dimension lumber — true 2×4s that measure a full four inches, not the 3.5-inch studs in modern framing.
