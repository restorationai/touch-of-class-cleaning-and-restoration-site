## Why Remsen Properties See Asbestos Issues

Plymouth County's rural building stock skews older than the Iowa statewide average, and Remsen is no exception. The neighborhoods closest to Downtown Remsen and the St. Mary's district contain a high concentration of homes constructed between the 1920s and the early 1970s — precisely the window when asbestos was a standard ingredient in building materials. Vermiculite attic insulation, 9×9 vinyl floor tiles, textured "popcorn" ceilings applied before 1978, and duct wrap on older furnace systems are the most common sources we encounter during inspections.

Iowa does not require a licensed asbestos inspector for every renovation, but the EPA's Renovation, Repair, and Painting (RRP) rule and NESHAP regulations do apply to demolition and large-scale disturbance. Contractors who skip testing before a gut renovation can unknowingly release airborne asbestos fibers — a risk that is both a health concern and a liability issue for property owners.

## Our Asbestos Abatement Process in Remsen

Every project begins with a thorough asbestos inspection and bulk sampling of suspect materials. Samples are submitted to an accredited laboratory, and we walk you through the results before any abatement work begins — no pressure to proceed until you understand what you're dealing with.

Once abatement is authorized, the work area is isolated with negative-air pressure containment using HEPA-filtered air scrubbers. Workers wear full PPE and follow Iowa Department of Natural Resources disposal protocols throughout. Removed materials are sealed in labeled, double-bagged containers and transported to a licensed disposal facility — not a general construction dumpster.

After abatement, air clearance sampling confirms fiber levels have returned to background before containment is removed. That final clearance report is yours to keep — useful for insurance documentation, future resale disclosures, or a building permit file.
