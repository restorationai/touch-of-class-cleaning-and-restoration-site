## Why Salem Homes and Buildings Have Distinct Renovation Needs

The I-90 corridor west of Sioux Falls delivers brutal freeze-thaw cycles every spring. In Salem, that means foundations shift, crawl spaces accumulate moisture, and older wood-framed homes — many built between the 1920s and 1960s — develop subfloor movement that shows up as squeaky floors, sticking doors, and cracked plaster long before a homeowner realizes there's a structural issue underneath. Renovation work here often uncovers surprises: knob-and-tube wiring tucked behind original lath, inadequate vapor barriers in crawl spaces, or undersized load-bearing headers that were fine for a 1940s kitchen but won't support an open-concept layout.

South Dakota's climate also means exterior renovations — siding, windows, roofing — need materials rated for temperature swings that can span 100°F between a July afternoon and a January night.
