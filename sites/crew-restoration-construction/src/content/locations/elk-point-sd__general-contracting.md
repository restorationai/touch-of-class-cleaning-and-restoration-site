## Why Elk Point's Older Housing Stock Shapes Every Remodel

Union County is one of South Dakota's oldest organized counties, and Elk Point's residential neighborhoods carry that age in their bones — sometimes literally. Homes within a few blocks of the Union County Courthouse frequently feature original plaster walls, knob-and-tube wiring tucked behind lathe, and basement foundations poured without modern waterproofing membranes. None of that makes a remodel impossible, but it does mean a general contractor working here needs to plan for discoveries that wouldn't come up in a subdivision built in 2005.

The Missouri River corridor also brings a soil dynamic worth understanding. Expansive clay soils common to this part of the Big Sioux and Missouri river valleys shift seasonally — swelling with spring moisture and contracting through dry summers. That movement telegraphs into slab floors, door frames, and load-bearing walls over time. A kitchen remodel that ignores a slightly racked door frame or a bathroom renovation that doesn't account for a settling subfloor will show those shortcuts within a few years. We scope the structural picture before we touch a single cabinet.
