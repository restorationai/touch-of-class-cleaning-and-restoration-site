## Our Mold Remediation Process in Rock Valley

Every job starts with a visual inspection and moisture mapping. Technicians use thermal imaging and pin-type moisture meters to locate hidden wet zones behind walls and under flooring — areas that look dry but read elevated on instruments. This step matters especially in older construction where plaster or original wood paneling can mask moisture that standard visual checks miss.

Once the affected area is identified, containment goes up before any disturbance work begins. Negative air pressure is established using HEPA-filtered air scrubbers to prevent spores from migrating to unaffected rooms. Contaminated porous materials — drywall, insulation, carpet padding — are removed, bagged, and disposed of per Iowa solid waste guidelines. Non-porous surfaces are cleaned with EPA-registered antimicrobial solutions and allowed to dry to acceptable moisture levels before any reconstruction begins.

Drying is not optional or abbreviated. Structural materials must reach equilibrium moisture content before encapsulation or rebuilding, or mold will return under the new finish. Crew Restoration uses commercial-grade desiccant and refrigerant dehumidifiers alongside high-velocity air movers, with daily moisture readings logged until target levels are reached.
