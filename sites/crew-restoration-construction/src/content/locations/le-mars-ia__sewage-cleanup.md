## Why Le Mars Properties See Sewage Backup Issues

Le Mars sits in northwest Iowa's glacially flattened terrain, where clay-heavy soils drain slowly and ground saturation after heavy spring rainfall can pressurize aging lateral sewer lines. When the ground is saturated, municipal sewer mains have less capacity to absorb surges, and the pressure finds the path of least resistance — often back up through floor drains, toilets, or utility sinks in lower-level spaces.

The housing stock around Downtown Le Mars and the Foster Park area includes a significant number of homes built before 1960, many of which still have original clay tile or cast-iron lateral lines connecting to the city main. Those materials are prone to root intrusion from the large elms and cottonwoods common along older residential streets. Root intrusion narrows the pipe bore over years until a single heavy-use event — a large family gathering, a long weekend of laundry — triggers a full blockage and backup. Newer homes on the edges of the city are generally on PVC lines, but they can still be affected when the municipal main itself surcharges during a significant rain event.

## Our Sewage Cleanup and Sanitization Process in Le Mars

Raw sewage is classified as Category 3 water — the most contaminated classification in the restoration industry — which means the cleanup protocol is more involved than a standard water loss. The process we follow in Le Mars homes and commercial properties moves through several distinct phases:

**Containment and safety assessment.** Before any extraction begins, we assess which areas have been contacted by sewage and establish containment to prevent cross-contamination to unaffected rooms. Porous materials — drywall, insulation, carpet, subflooring — that have direct sewage contact are typically removed rather than dried in place, because pathogens can survive in porous substrates even after apparent drying.

**Extraction and removal.** Truck-mounted extraction equipment removes standing sewage and contaminated water. Solid waste and saturated materials are bagged, documented, and disposed of in accordance with Iowa Department of Natural Resources guidelines for biohazardous waste.

**Structural cleaning and disinfection.** Hard surfaces — concrete floors, block walls, floor joists — are cleaned mechanically and then treated with EPA-registered disinfectants appropriate for Category 3 losses. We pay particular attention to floor drain assemblies, which are common re-contamination points if not thoroughly cleaned and disinfected.

**Drying and air quality control.** Once contaminated materials are removed and surfaces are disinfected, commercial desiccant dehumidifiers and air movers are deployed to bring structural moisture levels back to acceptable ranges. In Le Mars's humid summer months, this phase requires more aggressive equipment staging than it would in a drier climate.

**Documentation for insurance.** Every step is photographed and logged, producing the documentation your adjuster will need to process the claim.
