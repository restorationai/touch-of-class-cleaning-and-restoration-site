## Why Madison Properties See Roofing Problems Earlier Than Expected

The 57042 ZIP code sits in a climate zone that cycles through extremes most roofing materials weren't optimized for when they were installed a decade or two ago. Daytime temperatures in summer can push past 95°F, and January nights regularly drop below 0°F — that 100-degree annual swing causes asphalt shingles to expand and contract until the mat underneath becomes brittle and granule loss accelerates. Add prevailing winds that funnel across the open farmland west of town and you have a recipe for lifted tab shingles long before the manufacturer's rated lifespan.

Homes near the Lake Herman area face a secondary problem: proximity to water means higher ambient humidity during spring and fall, which slows the drying of any moisture that does get under a compromised roof deck. Lakeside cabins and year-round residences along that corridor often discover rotted decking when a replacement project begins — material that looked marginal from the attic turns out to be structurally soft once old shingles are stripped. Budgeting for deck repair before the project starts is almost always the right call in that neighborhood.

Downtown Madison's older commercial and mixed-use buildings present a different challenge. Many of those structures carry low-slope or flat roof sections alongside pitched areas, and the original built-up roofing membranes are well past their design life. Flashing details at parapet walls and HVAC penetrations are common failure points, and a failed seal there can send water into wall cavities for months before it shows up as a ceiling stain.

## Our Roofing Installation and Replacement Process in Madison

Every project starts with a thorough inspection — not a sales walk, but a documented assessment of decking condition, flashing integrity, ventilation, and existing underlayment. We photograph everything before a single shingle is removed, which matters both for your records and for any insurance claim documentation.

Once the scope is agreed upon, we strip the existing roofing down to the deck and inspect each sheet of OSB or plywood in person. Any soft, delaminated, or water-stained sections are replaced before new underlayment goes down. In Madison's climate, we use a peel-and-stick ice-and-water barrier along all eaves and in valleys — not just the code minimum, but extended coverage that accounts for the ice dam potential during the hard freeze cycles this region sees from November through March.

Shingle selection matters here. We walk homeowners through impact-resistance ratings (Class 3 and Class 4 shingles can translate directly to insurance premium savings in hail-prone South Dakota counties) and wind ratings appropriate for the open prairie exposure. Ridge ventilation is sized to the attic volume, not just ticked off a checklist, because inadequate ventilation is one of the fastest ways to void a shingle warranty in a climate with this much temperature swing.

Final inspections include checking every penetration, pipe boot, and step flashing detail before cleanup begins. We haul all torn-off material and leave the site the way we found it.
