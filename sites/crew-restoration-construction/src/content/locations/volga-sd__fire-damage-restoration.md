## Why Volga Properties Face Distinct Fire Damage Challenges

Volga sits in a climate band that swings from brutal January cold snaps to dry, windy summers — and both extremes create conditions that amplify fire damage in specific ways. In winter, a structure opened by fire suppression efforts loses heat rapidly, and water used to fight the blaze can freeze inside wall cavities and under flooring before a restoration crew even arrives. That freeze-thaw cycle stresses already-weakened framing and can crack masonry foundations that were otherwise sound.

The housing stock in and around Downtown Volga also skews older, with a number of homes built in the mid-twentieth century using construction methods — plank sheathing, older electrical wiring, and original wood-lath interiors — that behave differently under fire and smoke exposure than modern builds. Older wood absorbs smoke odor compounds more deeply, requiring more aggressive thermal fogging and hydroxyl treatment rather than a surface wipe-down. Structural assessments after a fire here often reveal secondary concerns — aging electrical panels, original insulation — that need to be documented for the insurance claim and addressed before reconstruction begins.

## Our Fire Damage Restoration Process in Volga

The first priority after the fire department clears a scene is stabilization. That means emergency board-up and tarping to protect the structure from weather — critical given how quickly a South Dakota cold front can arrive — followed by a detailed damage assessment that separates cosmetic smoke damage from structural compromise.

From there, the process moves through several concrete phases:

- **Debris and char removal**: Burned materials are catalogued, documented with photos, and removed. This step feeds directly into the insurance scope, so accuracy matters.
- **Smoke and soot cleaning**: Dry-chemical sponges handle porous surfaces first; then wet cleaning agents address residues on hard surfaces. Smoke residue left on metal fixtures or HVAC components continues to corrode if not neutralized.
- **Odor elimination**: Thermal fogging penetrates the same pathways smoke used, neutralizing odor molecules rather than masking them. Hydroxyl generators run continuously in enclosed spaces for deeper deodorization.
- **Structural drying**: If firefighting water is present, drying runs concurrently using industrial air movers and dehumidifiers sized for the affected square footage.
- **Reconstruction**: Once the structure is clean, dry, and documented, the rebuild phase begins — framing, drywall, insulation, finishes — handled by the same crew rather than handed off to a separate contractor.
