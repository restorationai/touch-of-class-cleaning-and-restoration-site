## Why Parker Properties Present Specific Biohazard Challenges

Turner County's housing stock includes a meaningful number of homes built in the mid-twentieth century or earlier — farmhouses, modest bungalows, and older rental properties near the Turner County Courthouse and throughout the surrounding blocks. Older construction means porous subfloor materials, unsealed concrete basements, and original hardwood that can absorb biological fluids far more deeply than modern materials. What looks like a surface-level situation in a newer home can penetrate several layers in a structure built before modern vapor barriers and sealed subflooring were standard.

Parker's continental climate adds another layer of complexity. Summers are warm and humid enough that biological material left unaddressed — even briefly — can accelerate microbial growth. Winters bring the opposite problem: if a property has been unoccupied during cold months, frozen pipes or heating failures can complicate access and slow the remediation timeline. Both conditions affect how quickly work needs to begin and how thoroughly materials must be treated before a space is safe to reoccupy.

## Our Biohazard Remediation Process in Parker

Every job begins with a contained assessment — our crew identifies the full extent of the affected area before any cleaning begins. Biohazardous material is not always confined to the visible surface; we check adjacent materials, wall cavities where relevant, and flooring layers beneath the point of contact.

From there, the process follows regulated protocols for the containment, removal, and disposal of infectious material, sharps, needles, and bodily fluids. Affected porous materials that cannot be decontaminated to a safe standard are removed and transported for proper disposal in accordance with South Dakota Department of Environment and Natural Resources guidelines. Hard surfaces are treated with hospital-grade disinfectants rated for the specific pathogens of concern. Air quality is addressed where aerosolization is a risk. When the work is complete, the space is documented — photographs, treatment records, and a written scope — which matters both for your own records and for any insurance claim that follows.

Throughout the process, our crew works in personal protective equipment and uses a sealed work area to prevent cross-contamination to unaffected parts of the property. Discretion is standard practice: unmarked vehicles are available on request, and we do not discuss job details outside the crew and the authorized point of contact.
