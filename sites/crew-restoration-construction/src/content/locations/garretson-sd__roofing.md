## Why Garretson Properties See Roofing Problems Early

The same Split Rock Creek valley topography that makes this area scenic also funnels wind. Properties near the Palisades area and along the creek corridor experience channeled gusts that accelerate shingle lift and granule loss faster than you'd expect from a roof's stated lifespan. Many homes in and around Downtown Garretson were built in the mid-20th century, when roof decking was often cut from narrower boards with gaps that allow more movement — and more opportunity for water infiltration — than modern plywood or OSB sheathing. Combine that with South Dakota's dramatic temperature swings (70°F differentials between summer highs and January lows are common), and you have conditions that age a roof faster than the manufacturer's warranty anticipates.

Hail is the other factor. Minnehaha County sits in a hail corridor that sees measurable hail events multiple times per season. A single storm can leave a roof that looks intact from the street but has sustained impact damage to the mat beneath the granule layer — damage that won't show as a leak for another year or two, by which time the decking may be involved.

## Our Roofing Installation and Replacement Process in Garretson

Every project starts with a thorough inspection — not a quick visual from the driveway. We walk the roof, probe the decking for soft spots, check the condition of existing underlayment, and assess flashing around chimneys, vents, and valleys. For older homes in the Downtown Garretson area, we frequently find that the original flashing was lead or galvanized steel that has long since corroded, and that addressing it is as important as the shingles themselves.

Once the scope is clear, we strip the existing material down to the deck rather than layering over it. Layering is common because it's faster and cheaper upfront, but it adds weight, traps moisture, and masks deck damage that will eventually fail. A clean tear-off lets us inspect every square foot of sheathing, replace damaged sections, and start fresh with a proper ice-and-water shield along the eaves — critical in a climate where ice dams are a real seasonal risk.

Material selection is a conversation, not a prescription. Architectural asphalt shingles rated for high-wind and impact resistance (Class 4 impact rating, when applicable) are a practical choice for this region and can affect your homeowner's insurance premium. Metal roofing is an increasingly popular option for properties that want a longer service life and better performance in freeze-thaw conditions. We walk through the tradeoffs honestly so you can make the right call for your building and budget.
