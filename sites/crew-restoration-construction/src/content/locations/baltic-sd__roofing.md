Baltic winters are hard on roofs. The freeze-thaw cycles that roll through Minnehaha County from November through March — where temperatures can swing 40 degrees in a single day — work ice and water under shingles, expand flashing gaps, and accelerate granule loss faster than most homeowners expect. For properties in the 57003 ZIP code, those stresses compound quickly, and a roof that looked serviceable in September can be actively leaking by February. Crew Restoration & Construction handles roofing installation and replacement for Baltic homes and commercial properties, working from our Sioux Falls base with the kind of regional familiarity that matters when you're scheduling around South Dakota weather windows.

## Why Baltic Properties See Roofing Problems Early

Baltic's housing stock skews older, and many of the homes closest to Downtown Baltic were built during an era when 3-tab asphalt shingles and minimal attic ventilation were standard practice. Those roofs were engineered for a 20-year lifespan. A lot of them are well past it. Inadequate ventilation traps heat in summer and moisture in winter, which shortens shingle life from the inside out — you lose years off a roof without ever seeing a single storm.

The Big Sioux River corridor adds another layer of complexity. Homes in the Big Sioux River area deal with higher ambient humidity during spring thaw and periodic flooding that can push moisture into soffits and fascia boards, the components that anchor your roof's edge. When those boards rot, even a structurally sound roof deck becomes vulnerable. A full replacement that skips fascia inspection is a replacement that may need to be redone in five years.

South Dakota also sits in a hail corridor. Baltic takes direct hits from Great Plains storm cells that track northeast, and insurance claims for hail-damaged roofing spike every June and July. If your roof is more than 10 years old and hasn't been inspected since a significant storm season, there's a reasonable chance you have impact damage that's quietly voiding your manufacturer warranty.

## Our Roofing Installation and Replacement Process in Baltic

Every project starts with a physical inspection — not a drone pass, not a satellite estimate. We put someone on the roof to check decking condition, measure actual slope and square footage, assess existing ventilation, and document any flashing failures around chimneys, skylights, or HVAC penetrations. That inspection drives the material and labor estimate, so there are no surprises when tear-off begins.

For most Baltic residential replacements, we work with architectural asphalt shingles rated for Class 4 impact resistance — the highest rating available — because the hail exposure here justifies the upgrade, and many insurance carriers in South Dakota offer premium discounts for Class 4 materials. We also evaluate whether existing ventilation meets current code before we close up the new roof. Trapping the same ventilation problem under new shingles is a mistake we won't make.

Tear-off, decking repair, underlayment, and shingle installation typically run two to three days for a standard single-story home, weather permitting. We stage materials to minimize disruption to driveways and landscaping, and we run a magnet sweep for nails before we leave the property each day.
