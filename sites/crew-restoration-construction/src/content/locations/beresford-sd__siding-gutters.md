## Why Beresford Properties See Siding and Gutter Issues

The South Dakota plains don't ease up on exteriors. Beresford averages roughly 25–30 inches of annual precipitation, a significant share of it arriving as freezing rain or heavy wet snow between November and March. That freeze-thaw cycle is the primary enemy of both siding and gutters here. Water infiltrates a hairline gap in vinyl or fiber cement siding, freezes overnight, and widens the gap by spring. Repeat that cycle across a full winter and you're looking at warped panels, compromised housewrap, and in older homes, rot working its way into the sheathing underneath.

Gutters face the same pressure from a different angle. Ice dams form at the eave line when heat escapes through the roof, melts snow, and the meltwater refreezes before it clears the gutter. The weight of that ice — sometimes 50 to 100 pounds per linear foot — pulls standard K-style gutters away from the fascia board. Homes in East Beresford that were built in the 1960s and 1970s often have original wood fascia that has already softened from decades of this cycle, meaning gutter replacement requires fascia repair before any new hardware goes up.
