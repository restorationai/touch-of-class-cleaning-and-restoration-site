Elk Point is one of South Dakota's oldest communities, and that history shows in its buildings. ## Our Storm Damage Restoration Process in Elk Point

Every job starts with a systematic exterior and interior inspection — roof, siding, windows, foundation vents, and any attached structures like garages or covered porches. We document everything photographically before touching a single material, because that documentation is what supports your insurance claim.

Once we understand the scope, stabilization comes first: tarping compromised roof sections, boarding broken windows, and extracting any standing water from interior spaces. We use commercial-grade drying equipment — industrial air movers and desiccant or refrigerant dehumidifiers — calibrated to the moisture readings we take with thermal imaging and pin-type moisture meters. Drying targets follow the IICRC S500 standard for water damage, which means we're not pulling equipment based on a guess; we're pulling it when the numbers confirm the structure has returned to acceptable moisture levels.

After drying, reconstruction begins: shingle replacement, siding repair, window installation, and interior work including drywall, insulation, and paint. We handle the full scope so you're not coordinating between a restoration company and a separate general contractor.

## Local Note: What Older Elk Point Homes Do After a Storm

Here's something that catches homeowners off guard: properties built before 1960 in and around Downtown Elk Point often have original cellulose or horsehair insulation packed into wall cavities. After a storm breach, that material absorbs water like a sponge and compresses, losing its insulating value permanently even after it dries. Unlike fiberglass batts, which can sometimes be dried in place, saturated cellulose almost always needs to be removed and replaced — and because it settles into irregular cavities, extraction takes longer than a standard insulation swap. Knowing this upfront changes the drying plan and the reconstruction estimate, and it's the kind of detail that only surfaces when you've worked in older South Dakota housing stock.
