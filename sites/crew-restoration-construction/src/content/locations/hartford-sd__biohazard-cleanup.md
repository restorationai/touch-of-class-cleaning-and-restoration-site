## Our Biohazard Cleanup Process in Hartford

Every job begins with a contained assessment — we identify the full extent of affected materials before any work starts, so there are no surprises mid-project. Contaminated porous materials (flooring, insulation, drywall) are bagged, labeled, and transported in compliance with South Dakota Department of Environment and Natural Resources requirements for regulated biological waste. Non-porous surfaces are treated with hospital-grade disinfectants and verified clean using ATP testing, an objective measure rather than a visual inspection.

For sharps and needle cleanup situations — which do come up in residential and rural-adjacent properties in the Hartford area — each item is handled with puncture-resistant containment and documented for proper disposal. Nothing is left to chance, and nothing is left behind.

Throughout the process, we work to minimize disruption to the rest of the property. Unmarked vehicles, contained work areas, and direct communication with whoever is coordinating on-site — whether that's a family member, a property manager, or an estate attorney — are standard practice, not an add-on.

## Local Note

One thing worth knowing about Hartford's newer subdivision builds: many of them were constructed with luxury vinyl plank flooring over concrete slabs rather than traditional wood subfloor systems. When a biohazard event affects that type of flooring, the LVP itself is typically removed and discarded — it's not salvageable — but the concrete beneath requires careful attention because biological fluids can wick into hairline cracks in the slab surface.
