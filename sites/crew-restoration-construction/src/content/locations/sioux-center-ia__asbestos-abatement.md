## Our Asbestos Abatement Process in Sioux Center

Abatement in an older Sioux Center home or commercial property follows a structured sequence that protects occupants, workers, and neighboring properties.

**Inspection and sampling** come first. A visual assessment identifies suspect materials — floor tiles, ceiling texture, pipe wrap, roofing felt, drywall joint compound — and bulk samples are collected and submitted to an accredited laboratory. Results typically return within a few business days, giving you a clear picture before any removal begins.

**Containment setup** isolates the work area with polyethylene sheeting and negative air pressure units that exhaust filtered air to the exterior. In Sioux Center's colder months, we account for the pressure differential that an older home's drafty envelope can create, adjusting containment anchoring accordingly so the barrier stays intact through temperature swings.

**Abatement and disposal** follow state and federal protocols. Iowa DNR regulations govern asbestos removal and require proper notification for projects above threshold quantities. Waste is double-bagged in labeled, sealed containers and transported to an approved disposal facility — not a general construction dumpster.

**Air clearance testing** after abatement confirms fiber levels are within acceptable limits before containment is removed and the space is returned to use.

## Local Note

One pattern that comes up repeatedly in Sioux Center's older residential neighborhoods: original linoleum floor tiles installed over wood subfloor in kitchens and bathrooms were often set with an asbestos-containing black mastic adhesive. The tiles themselves may or may not contain asbestos, but the mastic almost always does in pre-1980 homes. Homeowners who pull up old flooring themselves — often during a bathroom remodel or after water damage — can unknowingly disturb this adhesive layer and spread fibers through the home's HVAC system.
