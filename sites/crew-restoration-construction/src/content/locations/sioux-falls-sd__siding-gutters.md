## Why Sioux Falls Exteriors Take a Beating

The eastern South Dakota climate is genuinely hard on building envelopes. Sioux Falls sits in a zone where annual temperature swings can exceed 120°F between January lows and July highs. That range causes siding materials — vinyl, fiber cement, even wood — to expand and contract repeatedly, loosening fasteners, opening seams, and eventually letting moisture into the wall cavity beneath. Once water gets behind the cladding, it doesn't need long: mold can begin colonizing damp insulation within 24 to 48 hours.

Gutters compound the problem. Clogged or undersized gutters during a heavy spring melt — the kind that follows a 14-inch snowpack — push water against fascia boards and into soffit vents. Older homes in the Cathedral Historic District and All Saints neighborhoods, many of them built before 1960 with wood fascia and narrow 4-inch gutters, are especially vulnerable. Modern 5- and 6-inch K-style gutters with properly pitched runs handle that volume far better, and seamless aluminum gutters eliminate the leaky joints that sectional systems develop after a few South Dakota winters.

Hail is the other major driver. Sioux Falls sits near the northern edge of Tornado Alley, and storms that spin up over Nebraska frequently drop golf-ball-sized hail across the 57105 and 57103 ZIP codes before anyone has time to react. Impact-rated siding — Class 4 products in particular — can significantly reduce the frequency of insurance claims and out-of-pocket repairs for homeowners who've been through two or three hail events in a decade.

## Our Siding and Gutter Process for Sioux Falls Properties

Every project starts with a thorough exterior inspection — not just a visual scan from the driveway. We check behind siding at accessible penetration points, probe fascia for soft spots, and run a level along gutter runs to confirm pitch. On homes near Prairie Green and other newer subdivisions built on expansive clay soils, we pay close attention to foundation grading: if the lot has settled and water is pooling against the foundation, new gutters alone won't solve the drainage problem, and we'll tell you that upfront.

For siding replacement, we remove the existing cladding entirely rather than layering over it. That step lets us inspect the housewrap or felt paper underneath, replace any damaged sheathing, and install a continuous weather-resistive barrier before the new panels go on. We work with fiber cement, engineered wood, and vinyl products depending on budget, HOA requirements, and the architectural character of the home. On historic properties, matching profile and exposure matters — a lap siding pattern that looks right on a 1920s craftsman in the Cathedral Historic District is not the same as what belongs on a 1990s ranch.

Gutter installation follows a measure-and-fabricate workflow: we cut seamless aluminum gutters on-site to the exact run length, eliminating the joints that are the most common failure point. Downspout extensions are sized and positioned to discharge at least six feet from the foundation — a detail that matters more in Sioux Falls than in drier climates because spring runoff has nowhere to go quickly on flat lots.

## Working With Insurance After Hail or Wind Events

Most siding and gutter work in Sioux Falls following a significant storm qualifies as an insured loss under standard homeowners policies. The claim process is easier when the damage is documented before any repairs begin. We photograph affected areas systematically — panel dents, gutter deformation, soffit damage, downspout separation — and can provide that documentation to your adjuster. We work with all major carriers active in South Dakota and are familiar with the scope formats adjusters use in this market.

If your adjuster's estimate omits line items that are legitimately part of the repair — ice-and-water shield replacement, for example, or damaged housewrap — we can walk through the estimate with you and help you understand what a complete scope should include. We don't negotiate on your behalf (that's a public adjuster's role), but we make sure the technical picture is accurate.

One thing that surprises homeowners who've moved here from warmer states: Sioux Falls requires gutters to handle not just rain but rapid snowmelt, and the two events often happen simultaneously during a warm front in March.
