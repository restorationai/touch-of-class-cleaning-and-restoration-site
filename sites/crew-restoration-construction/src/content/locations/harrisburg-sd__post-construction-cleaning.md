## Why Harrisburg's Growth Creates Unique Post-Construction Cleaning Demands

Harrisburg's building boom isn't a slow, steady drip — it's a sprint. Subdivisions like Journey have added hundreds of homes in just a few years, and that pace compresses timelines. Builders hand off keys faster, subcontractors overlap trades, and the window between "construction complete" and "move-in ready" shrinks. The result is that post-construction cleaning here often has to address multiple trades' residue simultaneously: concrete dust from the garage slab, insulation fibers from attic work done the same week as cabinet installation, and grout haze on tile that was laid while drywall finishing was still happening in the next room.

South Dakota's climate adds another layer. Harrisburg sits in a zone where late-spring construction often coincides with high winds pulling topsoil and fine prairie grit through any unsealed opening. That exterior dust infiltrates new builds aggressively — settling into ductwork, coating window tracks, and embedding in unfinished flooring before the final coat of polyurethane goes down. A standard wipe-down won't reach it. Cleaning has to be systematic and sequenced to avoid redistributing what's already been disturbed.

## Our Post-Construction and Specialty Cleaning Process in Harrisburg

Every post-construction clean starts with a walkthrough to assess trade sequence and surface types — because cleaning a new luxury vinyl plank floor requires different chemistry and technique than cleaning a polished concrete slab, and both are common in Harrisburg's current housing stock. From there, the process moves top to bottom, rough to fine:

**Rough clean first.** Debris, scrap material, and bulk dust are removed before any detail work begins. This includes vacuuming HVAC returns and supply registers — a step that's easy to skip and expensive to regret once a homeowner runs the system for the first time.

**Surface cleaning by material type.** Windows get razor-blade treatment for paint and sticker adhesive, then streak-free polish. Cabinets are wiped inside and out, including the interiors of drawers that collect sawdust during installation. Countertops are cleaned based on material — stone, laminate, and butcher block each need a different approach to avoid etching or raising grain.

**Final detail pass.** Light fixtures, outlet covers, door hardware, and baseboards get individual attention. Floors are cleaned last, in the correct sequence for the finish type, so foot traffic from earlier cleaning phases doesn't undo the work.

**Specialty cleaning add-ons.** Some Harrisburg projects call for services beyond the standard final clean — pressure washing of new concrete flatwork, cleaning of exterior windows on two-story homes, or deep cleaning of a kitchen after a renovation that left grease and construction residue layered together. These are scoped individually and priced accordingly.

## Local Note

One thing that comes up specifically in Harrisburg's newer subdivisions: homes in Journey and similar developments often have attached three-car garages with epoxy-coated floors specified by the builder. Those coatings are typically applied in the final weeks of construction, and they're sensitive — certain alkaline cleaners will dull or streak the finish permanently. A cleaning crew that treats an epoxy garage floor the same way they'd treat a poured concrete slab will leave marks that are visible every time the homeowner parks. The right approach is a pH-neutral cleaner, a soft-bristle push broom, and a rinse that doesn't pool near the floor drain. It's a small detail, but it's the kind of thing that separates a final clean from a real final clean.
