## Why Le Mars Homes See Elevated Contents Damage

Le Mars has a housing stock that spans more than a century. The blocks radiating out from Downtown Le Mars include homes built in the 1910s through the 1940s — balloon-frame construction with plaster-and-lath walls, original hardwood floors, and built-in cabinetry that simply cannot be replaced at a hardware store. When water or fire touches these interiors, the contents aren't just damaged; they're often irreplaceable. Smoke from a kitchen fire in a 1930s craftsman bungalow penetrates differently than it does in a 2010 vinyl-sided build — the porous plaster and older wood trim act like a sponge for odor compounds, and that same chemistry transfers to upholstered furniture, drapes, and stored clothing.

Northwest Iowa winters also create a specific freeze-thaw risk. Pipes in older, minimally insulated crawl spaces fail during cold snaps, and the resulting water loss can saturate area rugs, bookshelves, and lower cabinets before a homeowner even realizes what's happening. Acting on contents within the first 24 to 48 hours is critical — that's the window before secondary mold colonization begins on porous materials like upholstery and cardboard.

## Our Contents Pack-Out and Restoration Process in Le Mars

Every contents job starts with a detailed room-by-room inventory. Each item is photographed, logged, and assigned a condition rating before anything leaves your property. That documentation matters for your insurance claim and gives you a clear record of what was taken and where it's going.

From there, items are transported to a controlled facility where technicians sort by material type — textiles go through ozone or hydroxyl deodorization and ultrasonic or hand-cleaning depending on fabric sensitivity; hard goods like lamps, artwork, and small appliances are cleaned with appropriate solvents and dried thoroughly. Electronics are assessed by a technician before any cleaning begins, because moisture and heat damage to circuit boards often isn't visible on the surface.

Items that can't be restored immediately — or that need to wait while your home is being repaired — go into climate-controlled storage. That's especially important in Le Mars, where summer humidity can climb into the 70–80% relative humidity range. Storing salvaged furniture in an uncontrolled space during July can undo the cleaning work done in the first week.

When your home is ready, we coordinate the return delivery and place items back in their original rooms using the inventory photos as a reference.
