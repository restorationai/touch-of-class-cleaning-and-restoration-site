## Why Slayton Properties See Roofing Issues Earlier Than Expected

The building stock around Downtown Slayton and the Lake Sarah area includes a significant number of homes built in the mid-20th century, many with original or once-replaced roofing systems that are now pushing 25 to 40 years old. Asphalt shingles have a rated lifespan of 20 to 30 years under moderate conditions — but southwestern Minnesota is not moderate. The freeze-thaw cycle here is aggressive: water infiltrates small cracks in shingle seams, freezes, expands, and widens those gaps every winter. Over time, what starts as a hairline separation becomes a path for meltwater to reach the decking.

Properties near Lake Sarah face an additional layer of exposure. Moisture-laden air off the lake accelerates the oxidation of metal flashing and fasteners, and the slight humidity differential between lakeside and inland lots can show up in attic condensation that softens roof decking from the inside out — damage that often goes unnoticed until a replacement is already overdue.

Hail is the other major driver. Murray County sees multiple significant hail events most years, and even quarter-sized hail can bruise asphalt shingles enough to reduce their remaining lifespan by half without leaving obvious visible damage from the ground.

## Our Roofing Installation and Replacement Process in Slayton

Every project starts with a thorough on-site inspection — not a drive-by estimate. We walk the roof, check decking condition, inspect flashing at chimneys, valleys, and penetrations, and assess ventilation. In older Slayton homes, we frequently find that inadequate soffit-to-ridge ventilation has been trapping heat and moisture in attic spaces, which degrades shingles from below even as weather degrades them from above. Correcting ventilation during a replacement is far less expensive than addressing it as a separate project later.

Once the scope is clear, we remove existing shingles down to the decking, replace any soft or damaged decking boards, and install a quality underlayment before any new shingles go down. For properties in wind-exposed locations — and most of Murray County qualifies — we use enhanced starter strips and a six-nail fastening pattern rather than the four-nail minimum, which meaningfully improves wind-uplift resistance in the kind of straight-line wind events that come through this part of Minnesota.

We work with architectural asphalt shingles as the standard recommendation for this climate, though we also install metal roofing systems for property owners who want a longer service interval and superior performance in ice and snow. Before we leave, gutters are cleared of debris, and the site is magnetically swept for fasteners.
