## Why Merrill Properties Are Vulnerable to Water Damage

Merrill sits in a low-lying stretch of Plymouth County where the Floyd River drainage basin keeps the water table relatively high compared to upland communities. That elevation reality means basement slab seepage is common after prolonged rain events — not just during named flood years. The soil profile here is predominantly clay loam, which sheds water slowly and holds hydrostatic pressure against foundation walls long after the rain stops.

The housing stock adds another layer of complexity. Many homes near Downtown Merrill were built in the mid-twentieth century, when vapor barriers were minimal and rim joist insulation was nonexistent. When water intrudes into those older structures, it wicks into wood framing and subfloor sheathing faster than it does in newer construction, and it stays there longer. A leak that might dry in 48 hours inside a 2005 build can linger for five or six days inside a 1955 ranch if the drying setup isn't calibrated correctly.

## Our Water Damage Restoration Process in Merrill

Every job begins with a thorough moisture assessment — thermal imaging and calibrated moisture meters map exactly where water has traveled, including inside wall cavities and beneath finished flooring where visible inspection misses it. That map drives every decision that follows.

Once the scope is documented, water extraction comes first. High-capacity truck-mounted and portable extraction units pull standing water from hard surfaces and subfloor assemblies before structural drying begins. In Merrill homes with older hardwood floors over plank subfloors — common in the blocks surrounding Merrill City Park — we use specialty floor mat drying systems that draw moisture upward through the wood rather than forcing demolition prematurely.

After extraction, commercial-grade desiccant and refrigerant dehumidifiers, paired with high-velocity air movers, are positioned according to a drying plan calculated for the structure's square footage and material types. Moisture readings are logged at each visit so the drying curve is documented, not guessed. When readings reach industry dry standards, equipment is removed and a final inspection confirms the structure is ready for any needed reconstruction.
