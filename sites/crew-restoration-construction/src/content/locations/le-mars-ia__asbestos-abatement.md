## Why Le Mars Properties Face a Higher Asbestos Risk

Iowa's northwest corner saw a wave of residential construction between the 1930s and 1960s, and Le Mars was no exception. The city's mix of historic and mid-century homes — many of them well-maintained but never fully updated — means asbestos-containing materials (ACMs) are still present in a significant share of the housing stock. Vermiculite attic insulation, chrysotile-wrapped HVAC ducts, vinyl floor tiles bonded with cutback adhesive, and joint compound from that era all tested positive for asbestos at rates that surprised many homeowners who assumed older meant "already dealt with."

The climate adds pressure. Northwest Iowa winters drive repeated freeze-thaw cycles that crack and shift older building materials. When asbestos-containing pipe insulation or ceiling texture becomes brittle and friable — meaning it crumbles under hand pressure — fibers can become airborne without anyone touching the material intentionally. A cold snap followed by a warm spell in February can do more damage to a deteriorating asbestos wrap than a decade of normal use.

## Our Asbestos Abatement Process in Le Mars

Every project starts with a thorough asbestos inspection and bulk sampling. Suspect materials are collected by a trained technician, labeled, and sent to an accredited laboratory — results typically come back within a few business days. If ACMs are confirmed, the abatement scope is documented before any containment goes up.

Containment is built to isolate the work area completely: heavy-gauge poly sheeting, negative air pressure maintained by HEPA-filtered air scrubbers, and a decontamination chamber at the entry point. Workers wear full-face respirators and disposable Tyvek suits throughout the removal. Wet methods are used whenever possible to suppress fiber release during material removal.

All waste is double-bagged in labeled, 6-mil poly bags and transported to an approved disposal facility — Iowa DNR regulations govern both the handling and the disposal chain, and we follow that paperwork from job start to final manifest. A post-abatement air clearance sample is collected before containment comes down, confirming fiber levels have returned to background before the space is released back to you.

## Local Note

One thing that comes up repeatedly in Le Mars's older residential blocks: popcorn ceilings were applied over original plaster rather than drywall. That layering matters during abatement because the plaster substrate is heavier and more fragile than modern drywall — aggressive scraping can crack the base layer and create secondary debris. Our crews slow down on those ceilings, using a wet misting technique and working in smaller sections than a standard popcorn removal job would require. It takes longer, but it keeps the ceiling intact and prevents a cosmetic project from turning into a full ceiling replacement.
