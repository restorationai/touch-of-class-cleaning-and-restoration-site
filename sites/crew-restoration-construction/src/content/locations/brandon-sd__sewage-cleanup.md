## Why Brandon Properties See Sewage Backup Issues

Brandon's housing stock is younger than most of Sioux Falls, but "newer" doesn't mean immune to sewage problems — it often means different ones. Many subdivisions developed over the last 15 years were built on clay-heavy soils common to eastern South Dakota. Brandon also sits in a region where spring snowmelt and heavy summer thunderstorms can overwhelm municipal sewer capacity. ## Our Sewage Cleanup and Sanitization Process in Brandon

Raw sewage is classified as Category 3 water — the most contaminated type — and the cleanup process reflects that. When the crew arrives at a Brandon address, the first priority is stopping the source: confirming the main shutoff, coordinating with the homeowner on any septic system isolation, or contacting the city utility if the backup is tied to a municipal line failure.

Once the source is controlled, extraction begins using truck-mounted or portable units capable of removing standing sewage along with the solids it carries. Porous materials — carpet, pad, drywall below the flood line, insulation — are removed rather than dried in place, because no amount of drying eliminates the pathogens embedded in those materials. After bulk removal, affected framing, concrete, and hard surfaces are treated with EPA-registered disinfectants and allowed to dwell at the contact time specified on the product label. Antimicrobial application follows, and the area is documented with moisture readings before any reconstruction begins.

In Brandon's newer construction, this process often involves coordinating with the builder's warranty department or the HOA management company if the backup originated from a shared line — something the crew handles routinely on subdivision jobs.
