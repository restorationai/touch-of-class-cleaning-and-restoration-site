## Why Canton Properties See Asbestos Issues

Canton's position as one of Lincoln County's oldest communities means a significant share of its residential and commercial buildings were constructed during the era when asbestos was a standard ingredient in building products. Vermiculite attic insulation, 9×9 floor tiles, acoustic "popcorn" ceiling texture, duct wrap, and boiler pipe lagging were all common in homes built between the 1940s and late 1970s. The Big Sioux River area in particular has pockets of housing that date to the early twentieth century — structures that may have been renovated multiple times, layering new materials over old ones without ever removing the original asbestos-containing substrates underneath.

South Dakota does not require a licensed asbestos abatement contractor for all residential projects, but the South Dakota Department of Agriculture and Natural Resources (DANR) does regulate asbestos demolition and renovation notifications above certain threshold quantities. For commercial properties — including any building near the Lincoln County Courthouse that might fall under county or state oversight — those notification requirements kick in before a single tile is disturbed. Understanding which rules apply to your specific property type and square footage is part of what an asbestos inspection accomplishes before any physical work begins.

## Our Asbestos Abatement Process in Canton

Every project starts with a thorough asbestos inspection and bulk sampling. A technician collects material samples from suspected areas — ceiling texture, floor tile and mastic, drywall joint compound, pipe insulation — and sends them to an accredited laboratory for polarized light microscopy (PLM) analysis. Results typically return within a few business days, giving you a clear picture of what's present and where before any abatement work is scoped or priced.

When asbestos-containing materials are confirmed, the abatement phase follows a strict containment protocol: the work area is sealed with polyethylene sheeting, negative air pressure is established using HEPA-filtered air scrubbers, and workers wear full-face respirators and disposable protective suits. Wet methods are used throughout removal to suppress fiber release. Waste is double-bagged in labeled, leak-tight containers and transported to a licensed disposal facility — in South Dakota, that typically means an approved Class I landfill that accepts regulated asbestos waste. After removal, a final air clearance test confirms fiber levels are within acceptable limits before containment is broken and the space is returned to you.
