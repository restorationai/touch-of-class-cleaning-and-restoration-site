## Why Baltic Properties See Water Damage Issues

Baltic sits in a low-lying stretch of eastern South Dakota where the soil — heavy clay in many areas — doesn't drain quickly after heavy rain or rapid snowmelt. That slow percolation pushes water toward foundations rather than away from them. Homes close to the Big Sioux River face an additional layer of risk: when the river runs high in March and April, groundwater tables rise throughout the surrounding area, and sump pumps that worked fine all winter suddenly can't keep pace.

Downtown Baltic's older housing stock adds another variable. Many homes here were built before modern vapor barriers and waterproofing membranes were standard practice, meaning basements and crawl spaces have limited protection against hydrostatic pressure. A single failed sump pump or a cracked foundation wall during a wet spring can push hundreds of gallons of water into a finished lower level in a matter of hours.

## Our Water Damage Restoration Process in Baltic

The first step on any water loss is stopping the source — whether that's shutting off a supply line, coordinating with a plumber, or sandbagging a point of entry. Once the water stops moving in, the extraction begins. Crew Restoration & Construction uses truck-mounted and portable extraction units to pull standing water from carpet, hardwood, concrete, and soil-floor crawl spaces.

After extraction, the work shifts to structural drying. Moisture doesn't stay where you can see it — it wicks into wall cavities, subfloor assemblies, and insulation batts. The team places industrial air movers and refrigerant dehumidifiers in a calculated pattern, then monitors moisture readings in the affected materials daily using calibrated meters. Drying timelines vary, but most residential water losses in Baltic reach target moisture levels within three to five days when equipment is properly deployed from day one.

If building materials — drywall, insulation, flooring — have absorbed too much water to dry in place, selective demolition removes only what can't be saved. That controlled teardown also gives the crew a clear view of framing and subfloor so nothing is sealed back up wet.
