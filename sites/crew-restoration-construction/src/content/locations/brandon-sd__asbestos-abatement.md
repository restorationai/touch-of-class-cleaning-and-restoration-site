## Why Brandon Properties Encounter Asbestos

Brandon's building history is layered. The older residential corridors near Split Rock Creek and along the edges of what's now the Brandon Valley area were developed well before the late 1970s federal restrictions on asbestos use. Homes built between roughly 1940 and 1978 commonly contain asbestos in joint compound, duct insulation, attic vermiculite, floor adhesives, and the spray-applied "popcorn" texture on ceilings that was nearly universal in that era. Even a house that looks updated on the surface may have original materials underneath — a layer of new flooring over old 9×9 tiles, or fresh drywall mud applied over original textured plaster.

South Dakota's climate adds another variable. The freeze-thaw cycles that hit the Sioux Falls metro hard each winter cause structural movement that can crack or disturb previously stable asbestos-containing materials. A hairline crack in an old floor tile is a low risk when the tile is intact; once it's friable — crumbling or releasing fibers — the calculus changes entirely.

## Our Asbestos Abatement Process in Brandon

Every project starts with a visual inspection and, where warranted, bulk sample collection sent to an accredited laboratory. We don't skip the testing phase to save time — a confirmed sample result is what determines the correct abatement protocol and protects you if questions arise later during a sale or renovation permit review.

Once results are back, the abatement scope is scoped to the specific material and location:

- **Popcorn ceiling removal**: The room is isolated with poly sheeting and negative air pressure equipment, the ceiling is wet-misted to suppress fiber release, and material is carefully hand-scraped into sealed, labeled disposal bags.
- **Floor tile and mastic removal**: Depending on tile condition, we may use low-speed grinding or hand removal under wet conditions. Intact tiles in good condition are sometimes encapsulated rather than removed, which is a legitimate and code-compliant option in South Dakota.
- **Pipe and duct insulation**: Friable pipe wrap is among the highest-priority materials. We remove it in full containment, decontaminate the work area, and conduct a final air clearance test before re-entry.

All regulated asbestos waste is transported to a permitted disposal facility — not a general construction dumpster. South Dakota Department of Environment and Natural Resources (DENR) rules govern disposal, and we follow the notification and manifest requirements those rules impose.
