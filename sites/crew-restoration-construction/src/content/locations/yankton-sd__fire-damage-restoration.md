## Why Yankton's Housing Stock Shapes Fire Damage Restoration

Yankton's residential neighborhoods span more than a century of construction. Downtown Yankton and the Riverside area contain a significant number of homes built before 1960 — many with original balloon-frame construction, knob-and-tube wiring that may have been partially updated, and plaster-and-lath walls rather than modern drywall. That matters for fire and smoke restoration because plaster is porous and holds smoke odor compounds deep in the substrate. Surface cleaning alone won't eliminate the smell; the material itself has to be tested, and in many cases, sections need to be removed rather than simply sealed.

Older balloon-frame walls also allow fire and smoke to travel vertically inside the wall cavity with very little resistance — a kitchen fire can deposit soot in the attic before the flames are even out. Post-fire restoration in these homes requires opening wall cavities to inspect and clean, not just treating visible surfaces.

## Our Fire Damage Restoration Process in Yankton

Every job starts with a structured assessment before any cleaning begins. We document the full extent of the loss — charred structural members, smoke-saturated insulation, heat-damaged mechanicals — with photographs and written scope that your insurance adjuster can work from directly.

From there, the process moves in a deliberate sequence:

- **Board-up and stabilization**: Open windows, compromised roof sections, and damaged doors are secured to prevent weather intrusion and unauthorized entry while the property is being worked.
- **Debris and char removal**: Burned materials are removed and disposed of properly. We separate salvageable structural components from those that need replacement.
- **Soot and smoke residue cleaning**: Dry soot on walls and ceilings is removed before any wet cleaning — applying moisture to dry soot drives it deeper into surfaces. We use dry chemical sponges, HEPA vacuuming, and appropriate chemical agents matched to the surface type.
- **Odor neutralization**: Thermal fogging and hydroxyl generation reach into wall cavities, ductwork, and soft goods that surface cleaning can't address. In Yankton's humid summers, skipping this step means odor returns as moisture activates residue left behind.
- **Structural repair and reconstruction**: Once the environment is clean and documented, we move into repair — framing, drywall, insulation, and finish work handled by our own construction crew rather than handed off.
