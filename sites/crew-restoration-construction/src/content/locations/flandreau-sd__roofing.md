## Why Flandreau Properties See Roofing Problems

Flandreau sits in a climate band that delivers some of the harshest roofing conditions in the upper Midwest. Average annual snowfall pushes past 40 inches, and the temperature can swing 50 degrees in a single week during shoulder seasons. That expansion-and-contraction cycle is hard on asphalt shingles, particularly the three-tab products that were standard on homes built in the 1960s and 1970s — a construction era well represented in neighborhoods near Downtown Flandreau. Those older shingles lose granules faster under freeze-thaw stress, leaving the underlying mat exposed and vulnerable to UV degradation by the time summer arrives.

High plains wind is the other factor. Flandreau sits in open terrain with limited natural windbreaks, and sustained gusts during spring storm systems routinely exceed 60 mph. Ridge caps and hip shingles take the worst of it. On homes near the Flandreau Santee Sioux Reservation and Royal River Casino corridor, we've seen entire ridge lines lifted on roofs where the original installation used too few fasteners — a code-minimum approach that doesn't hold up in this wind exposure zone. South Dakota's residential building code now requires six nails per shingle in high-wind areas; older roofs were often installed with four.

## Our Roofing Installation and Replacement Process in Flandreau

Every project starts with a thorough inspection — not just the visible surface, but the decking underneath. In Flandreau's older homes, we frequently find 1×6 skip sheathing beneath the original shingles, a common construction method before plywood became standard. Skip sheathing can be left in place and overlaid with OSB or plywood, but that decision has to be made before materials are ordered, because it changes the fastener schedule and the overall weight load calculation.

Once the scope is confirmed, we strip the existing material down to bare decking, inspect every sheet or board for rot or delamination, and replace compromised sections before anything else goes on. Ice-and-water shield goes down along all eaves and valleys — in this climate, a minimum 36-inch run at the eaves is standard practice, though we extend that on low-slope sections where ice damming is more likely. Synthetic underlayment covers the field, followed by the new shingles, ridge cap, and all flashing details at penetrations, walls, and valleys.

We work with homeowners on material selection based on their specific exposure. Architectural shingles with a Class 4 impact rating have become the practical choice for most Flandreau residential roofs — they carry better wind warranties and hold up against the hail events that move through Moody County most summers.

## Local Note

One detail that catches out-of-area contractors in Flandreau: the older homes near the Big Sioux River area were frequently built on crawl spaces rather than full basements, and attic ventilation was often an afterthought. Poor attic airflow is a direct contributor to ice dam formation — warm air escapes through the ceiling, heats the roof deck, melts the snowpack, and the meltwater refreezes at the cold eaves. Before we close out any roof replacement on a pre-1980 Flandreau home, we check the ridge and soffit vent ratio. If the ventilation is undersized, we flag it and can add intake or exhaust capacity as part of the same project. Replacing shingles on a poorly ventilated attic is a short-term fix; the next roof will fail the same way.
