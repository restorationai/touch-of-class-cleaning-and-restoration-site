## Why Alton Properties See Asbestos Concerns

Sioux County's rural towns, including Alton, developed steadily through the mid-twentieth century, and the building materials available at the time reflected national supply chains that leaned heavily on asbestos for its fire resistance and insulating properties. Homes near Downtown Alton — many of them modest single-story builds with original mechanical systems — frequently still contain asbestos-containing materials (ACMs) in places that are easy to overlook: the wrap around old furnace ducts, the adhesive beneath vinyl floor tiles, the joint compound on older drywall seams.

Iowa's wide temperature swings add another layer of concern. Freeze-thaw cycles stress building materials year after year, and when pipe insulation or floor tiles begin to crack and crumble, previously stable asbestos fibers can become friable — meaning they release into the air. A material that was safely encapsulated in 1965 may not be safely encapsulated today.

## Our Asbestos Abatement Process in Alton

Every project begins with a thorough inspection and, when material is suspect, bulk sampling sent to an accredited laboratory. We do not skip this step — visual identification of asbestos is not reliable, and Iowa regulations require confirmed testing before abatement work begins on regulated quantities of ACMs.

Once testing confirms the presence of asbestos, the work follows a structured sequence:

- **Containment setup**: Polyethylene sheeting and negative air pressure units seal the work area so fibers cannot migrate to other rooms or HVAC systems.
- **Wet removal**: ACMs are kept wet throughout removal to suppress fiber release — a critical step that separates proper abatement from a demo crew pulling materials dry.
- **Waste packaging and disposal**: All removed material is double-bagged in labeled, sealed poly bags and transported to an approved disposal facility in compliance with Iowa DNR requirements.
- **Air clearance**: A final air sample confirms fiber levels are below action thresholds before containment is removed and the space is returned to you.

For popcorn ceiling asbestos removal specifically, we treat each room as its own contained zone, which matters in older homes where HVAC returns can connect rooms that appear separate.
