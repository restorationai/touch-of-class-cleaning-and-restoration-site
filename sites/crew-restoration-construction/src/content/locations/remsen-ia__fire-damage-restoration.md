## Why Remsen Homes Present Specific Fire Damage Challenges

Remsen's housing stock skews older. Many homes in and around the St. Mary's district were built in the mid-20th century or earlier, reflecting the town's deep-rooted German Catholic community that established much of its residential fabric generations ago. That means plaster-and-lath wall construction, knob-and-tube or early Romex wiring in some structures, and original wood framing that has dried out over decades — all of which burn differently and absorb smoke more aggressively than modern construction materials.

Plaster walls, in particular, are porous in a way that drywall isn't. Smoke particulates and odor-causing compounds penetrate deeper into plaster surfaces, which means standard surface cleaning isn't enough. Encapsulation or full replacement is often necessary on walls that look only lightly soiled. Older balloon-frame construction — common in homes built before the 1950s — also allows fire and smoke to travel vertically inside wall cavities with little resistance, sometimes moving damage two floors above the room where the fire originated.

Northwest Iowa winters add another layer of complexity. After a fire, a home loses its thermal envelope — broken windows, burned roof sections, and missing insulation leave the structure exposed. In January or February, interior temperatures can drop below freezing within hours, which means any water used in suppression can ice inside wall cavities or under flooring before drying equipment can address it. Our process accounts for that: we prioritize weatherization and temporary heat before beginning detailed assessment in cold-weather losses.

## Our Fire Damage Restoration Process in Remsen

Every fire loss is different, but the sequence of work follows a logical order that prevents secondary damage from compounding the original problem.

The first priority after the fire marshal clears the structure is stabilization — boarding windows, tarping roof penetrations, and securing the building against weather and unauthorized entry. In Remsen, where a damaged home near Remsen City Park might sit on a corner lot with open sightlines, that board-up step also matters for liability and security.

Once the structure is stabilized, we conduct a full scope assessment: documenting affected materials room by room, testing surfaces for soot type (dry, wet, or protein-based — each requires different cleaning chemistry), and evaluating structural members for integrity. We photograph everything for the insurance claim file.

Content pack-out comes next for salvageable belongings — furniture, clothing, documents, and personal items are inventoried, removed, and cleaned off-site using ultrasonic and ozone treatment where appropriate. Structural cleaning follows: HEPA vacuuming of all surfaces, chemical sponge and wet-cleaning of walls and ceilings, duct cleaning to remove smoke particulates from the HVAC system, and odor neutralization using thermal fogging or hydroxyl generators depending on the severity.

Reconstruction — framing, drywall, insulation, trim, painting — closes out the project so the home is returned to pre-loss condition rather than left as a cleaned shell.
