## Why George Properties See Sewage Backup More Than You'd Expect

George sits close to the Lyon County line in a region where the water table fluctuates significantly between wet springs and dry summers. Many homes in and around Downtown George were built decades ago, when sewer lateral lines were commonly made from clay tile or Orangeburg pipe — materials that degrade, root-infiltrate, and collapse over time. When heavy spring rains saturate the ground, hydrostatic pressure can force groundwater into those compromised laterals, pushing sewage back up through floor drains, toilets, and basement utility sinks.

Septic systems are common on the rural edges of town, and an overloaded or frozen drain field — a real risk during Iowa's hard winters — can send raw effluent surfacing in crawl spaces or backing up into living areas. The combination of aging infrastructure, frost-susceptible soils, and seasonal flooding patterns makes sewage events here more than a plumbing inconvenience; they're a recurring structural and health risk that requires proper remediation, not just a shop-vac and a bottle of bleach.

## Our Sewage Cleanup and Sanitization Process in George

Sewage water is classified as Category 3 — the most contaminated type of water loss — which means the cleanup protocol is more involved than a standard water damage job. Here's how the process typically unfolds on a George property:

**Containment and extraction first.** Before anything else, the affected area is isolated to prevent cross-contamination to clean spaces. Standing sewage is extracted using truck-mounted or portable units capable of handling solids and debris, not just water.

**Removal of contaminated materials.** Porous materials — drywall, insulation, carpet, and subflooring — that have direct sewage contact are removed. There's no way to reliably sanitize saturated porous materials, and leaving them behind creates ongoing odor and microbial risk.

**Disinfection and deodorization.** Hard surfaces, framing, and concrete are treated with EPA-registered disinfectants rated for Category 3 contamination. This step addresses bacteria, viruses, and the persistent odor compounds that linger long after the visible mess is gone.

**Structural drying.** After sanitization, drying equipment — dehumidifiers and air movers — runs until moisture readings in walls and floors return to baseline. In George's older housing stock, concrete block foundations absorb moisture slowly, so this phase often takes longer than it would in a newer build with poured concrete or framed walls.

**Documentation.** Moisture logs, photos, and material removal records are compiled throughout, which matters significantly when an insurance claim is involved.
