## Why Tea Properties See Mold Issues

Tea sits in a climatic middle ground that doesn't get enough credit for how hard it is on homes. Winters bring repeated freeze-thaw cycles that stress foundation walls and window frames, creating micro-gaps where condensation accumulates. Summers swing into humid stretches where dew points climb into the uncomfortable range for days at a time. For the newer construction that defines most of 57064, this matters because modern vapor barriers and spray-foam insulation — when installed correctly — manage moisture well. When they're not, or when a plumbing connection fails behind a finished wall, moisture has nowhere to escape. It pools, it wicks into framing, and mold follows.

Older lots closer to Downtown Tea sometimes have homes with crawl spaces that were built before modern vapor barrier standards. Those crawl spaces are a consistent source of ground-moisture intrusion, especially after heavy spring rains when the water table rises across the Sioux Falls metro's southwestern edge.

## Our Mold Remediation Process in Tea

The first step on any Tea job is containment — not cleaning. Before a single spore-laden material is disturbed, the affected area is isolated with polyethylene sheeting and negative air pressure is established using HEPA-filtered air scrubbers. This keeps cross-contamination out of the rest of the home, which matters especially in open floor-plan builds common in Prairie Meadows and similar newer developments where a single air handler can circulate air through the entire structure.

Once containment is in place, affected materials are assessed. Porous materials — drywall, insulation, carpet — that test positive for mold growth are removed and bagged for disposal per EPA guidelines. Non-porous surfaces like framing lumber are HEPA-vacuumed and treated with an EPA-registered antimicrobial agent. Moisture readings are taken at multiple points using pin and pinless meters to confirm the underlying moisture source has been addressed — because remediating mold without correcting the moisture problem is a temporary fix at best.

Drying equipment — commercial-grade dehumidifiers and air movers — runs until structural materials reach acceptable moisture content for the region. Only then is containment removed and the space cleared for reconstruction.
