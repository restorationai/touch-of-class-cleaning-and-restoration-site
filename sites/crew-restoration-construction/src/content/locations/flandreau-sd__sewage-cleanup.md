## Why Flandreau Properties See Sewage Backup Issues

Flandreau's older residential blocks — many of them built in the mid-20th century when the town grew around the Flandreau Santee Sioux community and the agricultural economy — tend to have clay tile or cast-iron lateral sewer lines that are now decades past their design life. These materials crack, root-infiltrate, and collapse in ways that PVC pipe simply doesn't. When a lateral fails, sewage doesn't just slow down; it reverses direction. Homes closer to Downtown Flandreau, where the street-level infrastructure is oldest, see this pattern more often than newer construction on the outskirts.

South Dakota's freeze-thaw cycle compounds the problem. Ground movement through repeated winter freezes shifts buried pipe joints, opening gaps that roots exploit aggressively by spring. By the time a homeowner notices a slow drain, the lateral may already be partially blocked — one heavy-use weekend is enough to push it into full backup territory.

## Our Sewage Cleanup and Sanitization Process in Flandreau

Raw sewage is classified as Category 3 water — the most contaminated class — and the cleanup protocol reflects that. Our crew arrives with personal protective equipment, containment materials, and truck-mounted extraction units capable of pulling standing sewage from basements, crawl spaces, and utility rooms.

The process moves in a defined sequence:

1. **Containment** — We isolate the affected area to prevent cross-contamination to clean living spaces, sealing HVAC returns if the system is nearby.
2. **Extraction** — Standing sewage and saturated material are removed using high-capacity pumps. Porous materials — drywall, insulation, carpet, subfloor — that absorbed contaminated water are removed and bagged for disposal per South Dakota solid waste guidelines.
3. **Cleaning and disinfection** — Hard surfaces are cleaned with EPA-registered disinfectants rated for Category 3 contamination. This step is not optional or cosmetic; pathogens including E. coli, hepatitis A, and norovirus can survive on surfaces for days.
4. **Drying and dehumidification** — After sanitization, we deploy desiccant or refrigerant dehumidifiers and air movers to bring structural moisture readings back to pre-loss levels. In Flandreau's humid summer months, this phase requires careful monitoring because ambient humidity slows evaporation.
5. **Documentation** — Moisture readings, photo logs, and material removal records are compiled throughout, giving your insurance adjuster a complete picture of the loss.
