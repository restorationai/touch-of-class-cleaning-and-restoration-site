Lennox sits in a corridor that channels some of the most aggressive weather in eastern South Dakota. Spring and early summer bring hail events capable of punching through asphalt shingles in seconds, and the same storm cells that drop hail often trail straight-line winds strong enough to crack window frames or lift sections of soffit. Because the town sits largely without the windbreak that larger urban cores provide, properties near the Lennox City Park area and along the residential streets feeding into Downtown Lennox can catch the full force of a storm moving northeast across open farmland.

Beyond weather, fire is a consistent driver of board-up calls in smaller Lincoln County communities. Roof tarping and window board-up aren't cosmetic steps — they're the difference between a contained loss and a total rebuild.

## Our Emergency Board-Up and Tarping Process in Lennox

Every job starts with a fast visual assessment of the full structure before any material goes up. We look at the load-bearing condition of damaged framing, check whether interior ceilings show signs of active water intrusion, and photograph everything for your insurance file before we touch a single panel.

For roof tarping, we use reinforced polyethylene tarps rated for high-wind exposure — not the lightweight blue tarps available at a hardware store. Tarps are anchored with wood battens and screwed into solid decking rather than draped loosely, which matters in a region where a follow-up wind event the next night is a realistic possibility. For window and door board-up, we cut OSB panels to fit each opening precisely, secure them with structural screws, and seal the perimeter to limit air and moisture infiltration. On fire-damaged openings, we also assess whether the surrounding framing is stable enough to hold a board safely before we commit weight to it.

Documentation runs throughout the process. Every damaged opening, every material used, and every measurement is logged so your adjuster has a complete picture when they arrive.

## Lennox Insurance Coordination

Most homeowner policies cover emergency protective measures — board-up and tarping are typically classified as "mitigation" costs, which insurers expect to see documented and billed separately from the underlying structural repair. We photograph every opening before and after boarding, note dimensions and materials, and provide a line-item summary formatted the way most adjusters want to see it.

If your carrier has a preferred vendor list or requires prior authorization for mitigation work, call us first anyway. We can often begin work on a verbal authorization and sort out the paperwork with your adjuster in parallel, which prevents the exposure from sitting open while approvals move through the system.

## Local Note

Older homes on the west side of Lennox — many of them built during the mid-20th century expansion of the town as Lincoln County agriculture brought more families into the area — tend to have lower roof pitches than newer construction. That shallower pitch means water pools on a compromised section rather than sheeting off, and a tarp that isn't battened down with extra overlap at the low edge will allow water to wick underneath during a heavy rain. When we work on those older structures, we add an additional 18-inch overlap at the downslope edge and weight it more aggressively than we would on a steeper modern roof. It's a small adjustment that prevents a second water intrusion call two days after the first one.
