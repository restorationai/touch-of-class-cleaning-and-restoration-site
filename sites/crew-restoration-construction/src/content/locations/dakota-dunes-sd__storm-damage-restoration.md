## Why Dakota Dunes Properties See Severe Storm Damage

Dakota Dunes occupies a low-lying floodplain that was dramatically reshaped during the 2011 Missouri River flood — one of the most destructive flooding events in the region's recorded history. That event exposed a hard truth about the area's topography: even homes built well above the 100-year flood line can be compromised when saturated soils shift, drainage systems back up, and standing water lingers for days. Today's storm damage in Dakota Dunes rarely comes from a single source. A severe thunderstorm might drive wind-blown rain under roof flashing, drop a mature oak limb through a garage, and overwhelm a sump pump all in the same hour.

The housing stock here skews newer and upscale — many homes feature finished lower levels, custom millwork, and high-end flooring that are expensive to replace if water isn't extracted quickly. Brick and stone veneers common in the golf community can trap moisture behind the facade, and that hidden saturation is a setup for mold colonization within 24 to 48 hours if drying doesn't start promptly.

## Our Storm Damage Restoration Process in Dakota Dunes

Every storm damage job starts with a thorough site assessment before a single piece of equipment is placed. We document structural damage, identify active water intrusion points, and use moisture meters and thermal imaging to map saturation that isn't visible to the eye — especially relevant in finished basements where water can travel under luxury vinyl plank or tile before surfacing.

From there, the process moves in a logical sequence:

- **Emergency board-up and tarping** — if a tree has breached the roof or a window is blown out, we secure the envelope first to stop ongoing weather intrusion.
- **Water extraction** — truck-mounted extractors pull standing water from carpets, subfloors, and hard-surface areas. In lower-level living spaces, this step often takes multiple passes.
- **Structural drying** — commercial-grade desiccant dehumidifiers and high-velocity air movers run in a calculated pattern based on the room's square footage and material types. We monitor moisture readings daily until materials reach dry standard.
- **Debris and tree damage cleanup** — limb removal, cleanup of wind-driven debris, and coordination with roofing and siding trades for permanent repairs.
- **Reconstruction** — Crew Restoration & Construction handles both the mitigation and the rebuild, so you're not managing two separate contractors while your home is exposed.

## Dakota Dunes Insurance & HOA Coordination

The Dakota Dunes community is governed by a homeowners association with specific standards for exterior repairs and contractor access. Before work begins on any visible structural element — roofing, fencing, siding, driveways — we confirm what documentation the HOA requires and whether a variance or pre-approval is needed. Skipping that step can delay a final repair permit or trigger a fine, which is the last thing a homeowner needs after a storm.

On the insurance side, we photograph and document every affected surface before extraction or demolition begins. That photo record, paired with a detailed scope of loss, gives your adjuster what they need to process the claim accurately. We work directly with most major carriers and can communicate scope changes as hidden damage surfaces during drying — which happens frequently in storm claims where the initial visible damage understates the full loss.

## Local Note

Homes along the Missouri River-facing edges of Dakota Dunes often have sump pump systems that discharge toward the river bank — and during a significant storm, that discharge path can become overwhelmed or even reverse-flow when the river stage rises. We've seen this pattern repeatedly in the Two Rivers area: the sump runs constantly, appears to be working, but the pit is actually receiving more water than it can move. If your basement took on water during a storm and your sump was running the whole time, that's worth flagging to us during the assessment. It changes how we approach the source mitigation, not just the drying.
