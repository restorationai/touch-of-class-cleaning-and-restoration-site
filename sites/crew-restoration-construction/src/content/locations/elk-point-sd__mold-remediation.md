## Why Elk Point Properties See Mold Issues

Union County is the oldest settled county in South Dakota, and Elk Point reflects that history in its housing stock. Many homes near Downtown Elk Point were built before modern vapor barriers and cavity insulation were standard, which means crawl spaces and basement walls breathe moisture rather than block it. When a wet spring pushes groundwater up or a plumbing leak goes unnoticed for a week, the wall cavities in these older structures can begin supporting mold growth within 24 to 48 hours.

The Missouri River floodplain also influences indoor humidity levels in ways that newer construction in drier parts of South Dakota doesn't experience. Sump pumps work harder here. Window wells collect water faster. And because Union County sees genuine freeze-thaw cycling through late winter and early spring, ice damming and condensation on cold interior surfaces add another pathway for moisture to enter wall assemblies. All of that creates conditions where mold remediation isn't just a one-time fix — it requires understanding why moisture got in before sealing things back up.

## Our Mold Remediation Process in Elk Point

When we arrive at a property, the first step is a thorough moisture and visual assessment — not just of the visible mold, but of the surrounding materials and air pathways. Mold you can see is rarely the full picture, especially in homes with plaster walls or older balloon-frame construction common in the blocks around the Union County Courthouse.

From there, the process follows a structured sequence:

- **Containment** — Plastic sheeting and negative air pressure isolate the affected area so spores don't migrate to clean parts of the home during removal.
- **HEPA filtration** — Air scrubbers run continuously throughout the job, capturing airborne particulates down to 0.3 microns.
- **Material removal** — Unsalvageable drywall, insulation, and porous materials are bagged and removed per South Dakota solid waste guidelines.
- **Antimicrobial treatment** — Structural surfaces are treated and allowed to dry to documented moisture content levels before any reconstruction begins.
- **Clearance verification** — We recommend post-remediation testing through an independent third party before closing walls, particularly on jobs involving larger affected areas or sensitive occupants.

For homes in West Elk Point where additions and finished basements are common, we pay close attention to how the newer construction ties into the original structure — those transition points are frequent moisture traps.

## Local Note

One thing that comes up repeatedly on older Elk Point properties is the presence of horsehair plaster on interior walls — a material common in homes built before the 1950s that was once standard throughout this part of the Missouri River valley. Horsehair plaster is denser than modern drywall and absorbs moisture more slowly, but once it's saturated, it holds that moisture for a long time. During mold remediation, this means drying times can run significantly longer than they would in a comparable newer home, and moisture readings need to be taken at multiple depths before a wall is considered dry. Rushing that step in a plaster-walled home almost always leads to mold returning within a season.

Mold remediation coverage varies considerably depending on the cause of the loss.
