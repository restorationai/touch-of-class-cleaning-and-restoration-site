## Why Elk Point Properties Present Unique Biohazard Challenges

Many of the homes in and around Downtown Elk Point and the surrounding residential areas were built in the mid-twentieth century or earlier, when construction methods and materials were very different from what you'd find in a newer subdivision. Older subflooring, unsealed concrete basements, and porous plaster or lathe-and-plaster wall assemblies can absorb biological material in ways that modern drywall simply does not. Contamination that appears surface-level can wick into structural layers, which means a thorough assessment — not just a visual inspection — is essential before any remediation begins.

The region's climate adds another layer of complexity. Union County winters are cold enough that properties left unheated for any period can develop conditions that slow the effectiveness of standard disinfectants. Summer humidity near the Missouri River corridor, on the other hand, can accelerate the growth of secondary biological hazards if a scene is not addressed promptly. Neither extreme is forgiving of a delayed or incomplete response.

## Our Biohazard Remediation Process in Elk Point

Every job begins with a contained, documented assessment. Crew Restoration & Construction technicians arrive in unmarked vehicles when requested — a detail that matters in a close-knit community like Elk Point, where neighbors notice unfamiliar trucks. The work area is isolated using physical barriers and negative air pressure containment to prevent cross-contamination to unaffected parts of the property.

All biological material — blood, bodily fluids, sharps, and infectious waste — is collected and disposed of in accordance with South Dakota Department of Environment and Natural Resources guidelines for regulated medical waste. We do not simply bag and leave; disposal is handled through licensed waste carriers, and documentation is provided to the property owner for their records and for any insurance claim.

After removal, affected surfaces are treated with hospital-grade disinfectants rated for the specific pathogens relevant to the situation. Porous materials that cannot be adequately decontaminated — sections of subfloor, drywall, carpet padding — are removed and disposed of properly rather than left in place. A final ATP or surface testing step confirms that treated areas meet clearance standards before containment comes down.

## Local Note

Older properties in the Elk Point area — particularly those on the west side of town and along streets that predate the postwar building boom — often have unfinished or partially finished basements with bare concrete floors and walls. In biohazard situations, bare concrete is deceptively difficult to fully decontaminate because it is inherently porous. Standard surface cleaning is not sufficient; affected concrete typically requires mechanical abrasion followed by application of an encapsulant or, in more serious cases, removal of the top layer.
