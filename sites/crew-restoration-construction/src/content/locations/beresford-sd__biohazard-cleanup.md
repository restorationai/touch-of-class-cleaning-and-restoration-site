## Why Beresford Properties Present Unique Biohazard Considerations

Beresford's housing stock includes a meaningful number of older homes — many built in the mid-twentieth century or earlier — with characteristics that complicate remediation work. Subfloor materials, older insulation, and porous wall assemblies can absorb biological material more deeply than modern construction, which means surface cleaning alone is rarely sufficient. Crawl spaces under older homes in the area can trap contaminated moisture and harbor conditions that are difficult to assess without proper testing equipment.

The region's continental climate adds another layer of complexity. South Dakota winters push residents indoors for extended periods, and in unoccupied or minimally occupied properties — a common situation in rural communities near Beresford — a biohazard situation can go undetected long enough for secondary issues to develop. Prompt, thorough remediation is not just about cleanliness; it is about preventing a contained problem from becoming a structural one.

## Our Biohazard Cleanup Process in Beresford

Every job begins with a careful assessment of the affected area before any work starts. We identify the scope of contamination, determine which materials can be restored and which must be removed, and document everything for insurance purposes. Personal protective equipment, containment barriers, and regulated waste disposal are standard on every job — not optional add-ons.

For situations involving blood, bodily fluids, sharps, or other infectious material, we follow established industry protocols for decontamination and disposal. Contaminated porous materials — carpet, padding, certain types of subfloor — are bagged, sealed, and transported to licensed disposal facilities in compliance with South Dakota Department of Environment and Natural Resources requirements. After physical removal, affected surfaces are treated with hospital-grade disinfectants and verified clean before containment is lifted.

We handle the paperwork trail that insurance carriers and property managers often require: written scope of work, photographic documentation, and a remediation summary you can share with your adjuster or attorney if needed.
