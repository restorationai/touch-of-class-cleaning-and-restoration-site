## Why Slayton Properties See Repeated Storm Damage

Murray County sits in a corridor where cold Canadian air collides with warm, moisture-laden systems tracking up from the Gulf — the meteorological recipe for violent convective storms. Slayton's relatively flat topography means wind has no natural brake. Properties in the Lake Sarah area face a compounding problem: lake-effect moisture already keeps soil saturated through much of spring and early summer, so when a storm drops two inches of rain in forty minutes, the ground simply cannot absorb it. Water backs up against foundation walls, finds every crack in aging mortar, and pools in crawl spaces before most homeowners realize what's happening.

The housing stock adds another layer of complexity. A significant portion of Slayton's residential properties were built between the 1920s and 1960s, when construction methods and materials differed substantially from current codes. Older wood-framed roofs with original decking can delaminate after a single severe hail event in ways that modern oriented strand board does not. ## Our Storm Damage Restoration Process in Slayton

The first priority after a storm is stopping the damage from growing. That means emergency tarping and board-up on any roof or wall breach before a follow-up rain event can compound the loss. Once the structure is stabilized, the assessment phase begins — and in Slayton's older housing stock, that assessment goes deeper than a visual inspection. We use moisture meters and thermal imaging to map water migration behind plaster walls and under original hardwood floors, because water that isn't found in the first 24 to 48 hours creates the conditions for mold colonization within three to five days.

After documentation, extraction and drying equipment is staged inside the structure. Industrial air movers and desiccant or refrigerant dehumidifiers run continuously until moisture readings return to pre-loss baselines. Structural repairs — roof decking replacement, fascia and soffit work, siding, windows — follow once the building envelope is confirmed dry. We coordinate the sequencing so that interior trades aren't working in a structure that still has active moisture, which is a mistake that leads to callbacks and failed inspections.
