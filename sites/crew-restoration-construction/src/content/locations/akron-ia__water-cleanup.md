## Why Akron Properties See Water Damage Issues

Akron's location on the Big Sioux River floodplain is the single biggest factor shaping how water damage plays out here. The river corridor through the River District can experience rapid rises after upstream precipitation in South Dakota, and low-lying lots along those banks have limited natural drainage. That geography is compounded by the town's housing stock: many homes in and around Downtown Akron were built in the early to mid-1900s, when basement walls were poured with a lower concrete mix strength and drainage tile systems that have long since deteriorated or clogged. Older cast-iron and galvanized supply lines in those same homes are also prone to pinhole leaks and sudden failures, especially after a hard freeze.

Soil composition in Plymouth County tends toward clay-heavy profiles that don't absorb surface water quickly. When the ground is already saturated from snowmelt or prolonged rain, even a modest storm can send water toward foundations rather than away from them. That slow percolation means water finds its way through foundation cracks and window wells before a homeowner realizes anything is wrong.

## Our Emergency Water Cleanup Process in Akron

The first priority when a crew arrives is stopping the source if it hasn't been addressed yet, whether that's shutting off the main supply or sandbagging a basement window. From there, the process follows the IICRC S500 standard for water damage restoration:

- **Water extraction**: Truck-mounted and portable extractors pull standing water from floors, carpet, and crawl spaces. In older Akron homes with plank subfloors rather than OSB, extraction takes longer because water migrates between boards and into the joist bays below.
- **Moisture mapping**: Thermal imaging and calibrated moisture meters locate water that has wicked into wall cavities, plaster, and insulation. Plaster walls, common in pre-1960 construction throughout Downtown Akron, can feel dry at the surface while the lath behind them stays saturated for days.
- **Structural drying**: Industrial air movers and refrigerant dehumidifiers run in a calculated pattern based on the room's cubic footage and the measured moisture load. We monitor readings daily and adjust equipment placement until materials reach acceptable dryness levels.
- **Documentation**: Every affected area is photographed and logged with moisture readings, which becomes the foundation of your insurance claim file.

## Akron Insurance Coordination

Most standard homeowner policies cover sudden and accidental water damage, which includes burst pipes and appliance failures. Flood damage from the Big Sioux River overflowing its banks is a different category and typically requires a separate flood insurance policy through the National Flood Insurance Program. Knowing which type of loss you have matters before the claim is filed, because the documentation requirements differ. ## Local Note

Homes near the Akron-Westfield School district and along the older residential streets feeding into Downtown Akron frequently have full or partial basements with original clay tile drain systems. These systems were designed for a different era of water volume and often collapse or root-intrude over decades. When a sump pump fails during a storm event and water enters through a compromised tile drain, the water carries fine silt that settles into carpet backing and subfloor seams. That silt layer traps moisture long after the visible water is gone, which is why surface-dry doesn't mean structurally dry in these homes. Extraction alone isn't enough; the drying phase has to account for that embedded material.
