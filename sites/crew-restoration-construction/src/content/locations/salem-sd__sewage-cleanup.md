## Why Salem Properties See Sewage Backup Issues

Salem sits on the James River plain, where the underlying clay-heavy soils expand and shift with the freeze-thaw cycles that define a South Dakota winter. That ground movement puts lateral sewer lines — the pipes running from your foundation to the municipal main or your septic tank — under chronic stress. Cast-iron and clay-tile drain pipes common in homes built near Downtown Salem before the 1970s are especially vulnerable: joints crack, roots intrude, and a single hard freeze can finish off a pipe that was already compromised.

Septic systems are also common on the rural edges of McCook County, particularly on properties near the McCook County Fairgrounds area where lots are larger and municipal sewer connections thin out. When a septic tank reaches capacity or a distribution line fails, the overflow doesn't just stay in the yard — it finds the path of least resistance, which is often back through floor drains, toilets, or laundry tubs inside the home. That's Category 3 water: the most contaminated classification, requiring full protective equipment, antimicrobial treatment, and in many cases, removal of affected drywall, insulation, and flooring.

## Our Sewage Cleanup and Sanitization Process in Salem

Every sewage job starts with containment, not cleaning. Before any extraction begins, we seal off affected areas to prevent cross-contamination to unaffected rooms — a step that matters especially in older Salem homes where HVAC returns are often centrally located and can spread airborne pathogens quickly.

From there, the process follows a defined sequence:

1. **Extraction** — Standing sewage and saturated materials are removed using truck-mounted or portable extraction units. We document volumes and affected square footage for your insurance claim.
2. **Controlled demolition** — Drywall, insulation, and subfloor materials that have absorbed Category 3 water are removed. There is no safe way to dry contaminated porous material in place; the biology doesn't allow it.
3. **Antimicrobial treatment** — Hard surfaces, framing, and concrete are treated with EPA-registered disinfectants. We apply, dwell, and reapply per label instructions — not a single spray-and-wipe pass.
4. **Structural drying** — Industrial dehumidifiers and air movers run until moisture readings in framing and concrete return to pre-loss baselines. In Salem's humid summers, this can take longer than the same job in a drier climate.
5. **Clearance documentation** — We provide written moisture logs and photo documentation before any reconstruction begins, which your insurance adjuster will need.
