## Why Hawarden Properties See Water Damage Issues

Hawarden's position along the Big Sioux puts it in a natural flood corridor, but river flooding isn't the only threat. ## Our Water Damage Restoration Process in Hawarden

The first priority on any water damage call is stopping the spread. Before extraction equipment rolls in, the source needs to be confirmed — whether that's a ruptured supply line, a sump pump failure, or groundwater intrusion through the foundation. Once the source is controlled, standing water is removed using truck-mounted extraction units capable of pulling hundreds of gallons quickly from basement floors, carpeted living areas, and subfloor cavities.

After bulk water removal, the work shifts to structural drying. Industrial air movers and refrigerant dehumidifiers are positioned based on moisture readings taken with calibrated meters — not guesswork. Walls, subfloors, and ceiling assemblies are monitored daily until they reach target dryness levels consistent with industry drying standards. In older Hawarden homes with original hardwood floors or plaster-and-lath wall construction, drying timelines often run longer than they would in a newer build; those materials hold moisture differently and require more careful monitoring to avoid hidden pockets of residual dampness that invite mold growth.

Documentation runs throughout the entire process — moisture readings, photographs, affected material logs — because that record is exactly what your insurance adjuster needs to process the claim accurately.
