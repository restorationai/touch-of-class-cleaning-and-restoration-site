## Our Water Damage Restoration Process in Le Mars

When we arrive, the first priority is stopping active water intrusion if it hasn't already stopped on its own — that means identifying the source, whether it's a failed appliance supply line, a backed-up floor drain, or storm-driven seepage through the foundation. From there, the process follows a structured sequence:

**Extraction first.** Truck-mounted and portable extraction units pull standing water from flooring, carpet padding, and low-lying cavities before evaporation equipment goes in. Skipping this step and jumping straight to air movers is one of the most common mistakes in the industry — it spreads moisture vapor rather than removing it.

**Moisture mapping.** Thermal imaging cameras and calibrated moisture meters document exactly how far water has migrated into walls, subfloors, and framing. In older Le Mars homes with plaster walls, water can wick laterally through the scratch coat and reach studs several feet from the visible wet area. We map the full extent before we set equipment.

**Structural drying.** High-capacity dehumidifiers and directed air movers are placed according to the moisture map, not just pointed at the wet spot. Drying targets are set using psychrometric calculations based on the current indoor conditions and the material types involved. We monitor and log readings on every visit until materials reach documented dry standards.

**Controlled demolition when needed.** If flooring, drywall, or insulation is too saturated to dry in place, we remove it — carefully, with attention to what's behind it. In homes built before 1978, we treat any disturbed materials with lead-safe work practices as a precaution.

One thing that comes up specifically in Le Mars's older neighborhoods: plaster walls absorb water more slowly than modern drywall, but they also release it more slowly.
