## Why Salem Properties See Siding and Gutter Problems

McCook County's climate sits in a freeze-thaw cycle that runs roughly five months of the year. Water that infiltrates behind vinyl or steel siding panels in late fall freezes, expands, and works the fasteners loose — sometimes without any visible cracking until the panel physically bows or separates. Gutters face a related problem: ice dams that form at the eave line trap standing water, and when that water finally moves, it often takes caulked seams and downspout connections with it.

The housing stock around Downtown Salem skews older, with a meaningful share of structures built before the widespread adoption of housewrap and moisture barriers. That means siding replacement here frequently uncovers deteriorated sheathing or compromised flashing that wouldn't appear on a newer build. Catching that before new panels go up is the difference between a repair that lasts and one that fails in three winters.

Hail is the other consistent driver. Storms that track northeast from the Nebraska panhandle and southwest South Dakota hit this stretch of the I-90 corridor regularly. Dented aluminum fascia, cracked vinyl panels, and bent gutter sections are common post-storm findings — and they're the kind of damage that insurance adjusters look for when reviewing exterior claims.

## Our Siding and Gutter Process in Salem

Every project starts with a detailed exterior inspection — not a quick visual pass, but a methodical check of panel seams, flashing transitions, soffit vents, and gutter pitch. For siding replacement, we strip existing panels carefully to assess the sheathing underneath. If we find moisture damage or rot, we address it before new material goes on — skipping that step is how contractors end up with callbacks two years later. We work with fiber cement, engineered wood, and steel siding products that are rated for the temperature swings South Dakota delivers, and we seal every penetration point: windows, doors, utility entries, and corners.

Gutter work in this region requires attention to slope and capacity. Salem's spring snowmelt events can deliver a large volume of water in a short window. We size downspouts and ensure gutter pitch is sufficient to move that volume without pooling, and we position downspout extensions to direct water away from foundations — a detail that matters more here than in drier climates.
