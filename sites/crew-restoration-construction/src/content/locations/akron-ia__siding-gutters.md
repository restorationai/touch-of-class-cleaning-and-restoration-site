## Why Akron Properties See Siding and Gutter Failures Earlier

The housing stock in and around Downtown Akron skews older, with a significant number of homes built before vinyl siding was common. That means a lot of original wood lap siding or early-generation aluminum panels that were never designed to handle the kind of sustained wind exposure the river corridor delivers. Aluminum siding installed in the 1970s and 1980s tends to oxidize and chalk, then lose its ability to shed water cleanly — which puts extra load on the gutters below it. When gutters are already undersized (5-inch K-style on a steep-pitch roof is common on older homes here), they overflow, and that overflow saturates the soil directly against the foundation.

The freeze-thaw pattern in 51001 is also worth understanding. Temperatures in northwest Iowa can swing 40 degrees in a single day during March and April. Meltwater that gets behind a loose panel or into a gutter seam refreezes overnight and expands, widening gaps that were barely noticeable the previous fall. By the time a homeowner notices paint bubbling or a gutter pulling away from the fascia, the damage is usually a season or two old.

## Our Siding and Gutter Process in Akron

Every project starts with a full exterior walkthrough — not just the obvious damage, but the transitions: where the siding meets window and door trim, where downspouts discharge, and whether existing flashing is still doing its job. On older homes near the River District, we frequently find that the original flashing was lead or galvanized steel that has corroded through at the corners.

For siding replacement, we pull the damaged panels and inspect the housewrap or felt paper underneath before anything new goes on. If the sheathing has taken on moisture, we address that before closing the wall back up — skipping that step is how a new siding job fails in three years instead of thirty. We match profiles and finishes to what's on the rest of the structure so the repair doesn't read as a patch.

For gutters, we size the system to the actual roof area and pitch rather than defaulting to whatever was there before. On steeper roofs or long runs, that often means stepping up to 6-inch gutters and larger downspouts. We also pay attention to where water is directed at grade — discharging a downspout against a foundation on a flat lot is a drainage problem waiting to happen.
