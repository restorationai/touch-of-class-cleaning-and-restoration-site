## Why Le Mars Properties Present Unique Post-Construction Cleaning Challenges

Le Mars sits in Plymouth County, where northwest Iowa's climate swings hard between humid summers and dry, frigid winters. That thermal cycling matters after a renovation. Freshly installed drywall compound and caulk off-gas differently in January than in July, and the fine dust those materials produce behaves differently in low-humidity winter air — it stays airborne longer and travels farther through open ductwork. Homes built before 1960, which make up a meaningful share of the housing stock in the Downtown Le Mars corridor, often have original plaster walls and older forced-air systems with larger duct openings. Construction debris — insulation fibers, plaster chips, sawdust — migrates into those ducts faster than it would in a newer sealed system, meaning a thorough post-construction clean here has to include duct inspection and often a HEPA-filtered duct wipe-down before the HVAC is run for the first time post-renovation.

Newer builds and additions on the edges of town present a different set of issues: concrete slab work and spray-foam insulation leave residues that standard janitorial products won't touch. The 51031 ZIP code covers both the older downtown core and newer residential development, so a single project can involve both historic plaster and modern engineered materials in the same structure.

## Our Post-Construction and Specialty Cleaning Process in Le Mars

Every post-construction clean starts with a walk-through before a single piece of equipment is unloaded. We document what trades were on-site, what materials were used, and what the general contractor's punch list leaves for us. That conversation shapes the sequence of the clean — because sequence matters. Cleaning windows before the drywall dust is fully removed from ceilings and walls just means cleaning them twice.

The general order of operations:

- **Rough clean**: Remove bulk debris — cut lumber, packaging, mortar droppings, adhesive tubes. Sweep and vacuum all surfaces with HEPA-filtered equipment before any wet methods are introduced.
- **Detail clean**: Wipe down every surface in sequence from ceiling to floor. Window tracks, door hardware, cabinet interiors, light fixtures, and grout lines all get individual attention. Construction adhesive and paint overspray are treated with appropriate solvents matched to the substrate.
- **Final clean**: The white-glove pass. Glass polished, floors finished per the flooring manufacturer's spec (critical for new hardwood and LVP that can be damaged by the wrong cleaning agent), and a final HEPA vacuum of all soft surfaces before the space is handed back.
- **Specialty services**: If the project involved any lead-containing materials in an older Le Mars home — original window trim, pre-1978 painted surfaces disturbed during demo — we follow EPA RRP-compliant cleanup protocols for debris containment and disposal.
