## Why Alton Properties See Water Damage Issues

Alton sits in Sioux County's agricultural flatlands, where the Floyd River drainage basin shapes how stormwater moves through and around town. During heavy spring rains or rapid snowmelt, surface water has limited places to go quickly, and low-lying lots can accumulate standing water faster than sump pumps can keep up. The soil in this part of northwest Iowa tends toward clay-heavy composition, which means it saturates quickly and drains slowly, keeping hydrostatic pressure against foundation walls long after the rain stops.

The housing stock in and around Downtown Alton includes a meaningful number of homes built before 1960. Older construction often means cast-iron or galvanized plumbing that has reached the end of its service life, basement walls without modern waterproofing membranes, and wood framing that absorbs moisture more aggressively than engineered lumber. A slow leak inside a wall cavity in one of these homes can go undetected for weeks, with the damage already spreading by the time a stain appears on the drywall.

## Our Water Cleanup Process in Alton

When we arrive at a property, the first step is containment and assessment, not guesswork. Technicians use thermal imaging cameras and moisture meters to map exactly where water has traveled, including behind plaster, under flooring, and inside wall cavities. Standing water is extracted using truck-mounted and portable extraction units before any drying equipment is placed.

Once extraction is complete, we establish a drying system calibrated to the specific materials and square footage involved. Alton's climate matters here: high humidity in summer months means ambient air alone will not dry a flooded basement, and dehumidifiers must be sized to overcome outdoor moisture levels, not just the water from the loss itself. Drying logs are recorded throughout the process so you and your insurance adjuster have documentation of every reading.

Affected materials are evaluated individually. Saturated drywall and insulation typically need to come out because they hold moisture long after the surface reads dry. Hardwood floors are assessed for cupping and subfloor saturation before a decision is made on salvage versus replacement.
