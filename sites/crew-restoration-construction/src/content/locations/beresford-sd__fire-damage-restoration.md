## Local Note

Older homes in the blocks near Downtown Beresford frequently have plaster-and-lath interior walls rather than modern drywall. Plaster is dense and absorbs smoke odor compounds deeply, but it also holds structural integrity better than drywall after a fire — meaning it can sometimes be cleaned and sealed rather than demolished. The decision requires close inspection: if the lath behind the plaster is charred or the plaster is cracked and delaminating, removal is the right call. If the plaster is intact and the smoke penetration is surface-level, encapsulation with an oil-based primer followed by a specialty sealant can preserve original wall character and reduce rebuild costs. Knowing which approach fits which room is a judgment call that comes from working in older South Dakota housing stock, not from a generic restoration checklist.
