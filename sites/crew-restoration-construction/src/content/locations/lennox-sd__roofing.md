## Why Lennox Properties Face Distinct Roofing Challenges

Lennox sits in the path of weather systems that roll across Lincoln County farmland with very little to slow them down. Hailstorms that might drop golf-ball-sized ice on the south side of Sioux Falls often reach Lennox with the same intensity, and the town's steady residential growth means newer construction sits alongside homes built in earlier decades under different code standards.

Older homes near Downtown Lennox frequently have roof decks that were never designed for modern synthetic underlayments or heavier architectural shingles. When those roofs finally fail — whether from a single severe storm or years of freeze-thaw cycling — the replacement process sometimes uncovers deteriorated decking, inadequate ventilation, or flashing that was never properly sealed around chimneys and dormers. Catching those issues during a planned replacement is far less disruptive than dealing with them after interior water damage has already started.

Wind is the other persistent factor. Proper fastener patterns and starter-strip installation matter more in exposed locations like Lennox than they would in a sheltered urban neighborhood.

## Our Roofing Installation and Replacement Process in Lennox

Every project begins with a thorough inspection of the existing roof system — not just the shingles, but the decking, ventilation, soffit and fascia condition, and all penetration points. In Lennox, that inspection often turns up ice-dam damage along north-facing eaves, a predictable result of the region's hard winters and homes that weren't built with adequate attic insulation depth.

Once the scope is clear, material selection is matched to the specific property. Architectural asphalt shingles rated for high-wind uplift are the most common choice for residential roofing in this area, but metal roofing has grown in popularity among homeowners who want a longer service interval and better performance against hail impact. We walk through the tradeoffs honestly — upfront cost, expected lifespan, insurance implications — so the decision fits the homeowner's situation rather than a sales script.

During installation, old materials are stripped to the deck, the deck is inspected and repaired where needed, and ice-and-water shield is applied along the eaves and valleys before any underlayment goes down. In South Dakota's climate, skipping or shortchanging that membrane is one of the most common causes of premature roof failure. Flashing is replaced rather than reused, and ridge ventilation is verified or upgraded to prevent the moisture buildup that shortens shingle life from the inside out.
