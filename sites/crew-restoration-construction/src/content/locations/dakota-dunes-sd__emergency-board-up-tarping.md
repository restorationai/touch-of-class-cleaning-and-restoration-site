## Our Emergency Board-Up and Tarping Process in Dakota Dunes

When we arrive at a property in the 57049 ZIP code, the first step is a rapid exterior assessment — identifying every breach point before any material is staged. On a fire-damaged structure, that means checking for compromised load-bearing members before anyone works near the roofline. On a storm-damaged home, it means probing the decking around the tarp zone to confirm it will hold anchor points safely.

For window and door board-ups, we cut plywood to fit the rough opening and fasten it with structural screws rather than nails, which matters when a property may sit boarded for weeks while an insurance claim processes. For roof tarping, we use ballasted or mechanically anchored systems depending on the pitch and material — a steep cedar shake roof in the Dunes golf community gets a different anchor approach than a low-slope membrane roof on a commercial pad near the country club corridor. Every tarp is weighted or secured at the ridge and draped past the eave line so wind can't get underneath and lift it.

We photograph every opening before and after boarding, document the materials used, and leave a written summary on-site — documentation that feeds directly into the insurance claim file.

One thing that's easy to miss on Dakota Dunes properties after a flood or storm: the soil here is Missouri River alluvial fill, which drains slowly and stays saturated longer than upland clay soils.
