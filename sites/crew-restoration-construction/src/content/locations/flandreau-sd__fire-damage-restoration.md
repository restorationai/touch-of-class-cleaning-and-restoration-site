## Why Flandreau Properties Face Particular Challenges After a Fire

Flandreau's housing stock skews older. A significant portion of homes in and around Downtown Flandreau were built during the mid-20th century, when balloon-frame construction was common. In a balloon-frame house, wall cavities run uninterrupted from the basement to the attic — meaning smoke and heat travel vertically through the structure with almost no resistance. What looks like a contained kitchen fire can leave soot deposits two floors away. That framing style also makes thermal imaging during post-fire inspection especially important, because hot spots and hidden char are easy to miss without it.

The region's climate adds another layer. South Dakota winters are dry and cold, but spring thaw and summer humidity cycles mean that fire-damaged structures — stripped of insulation and vapor barriers — can absorb ambient moisture rapidly once exterior walls are breached. A home that burns in February and sits partially open through March is a different remediation project than one addressed the same week.

## Our Fire Damage Restoration Process in Flandreau

Every job starts with a structured assessment: documenting the fire's origin point, mapping smoke migration paths, and identifying materials that are restorable versus those that must be replaced. We photograph and catalog everything before a single item is moved — a step that matters enormously when your insurance adjuster is reviewing the claim.

Demolition of unsalvageable material comes next. Char-damaged drywall, compromised framing, melted insulation — these come out cleanly and completely, because leaving them behind traps odor and creates long-term air quality problems. After demo, we run industrial HEPA air scrubbers and apply thermal fogging or hydroxyl treatment to neutralize smoke odor at the molecular level, not just mask it. Ozone treatment is used selectively in unoccupied spaces where penetration depth matters.

Structural drying follows if suppression water is present, then cleaning of salvageable surfaces — cabinets, concrete, masonry, metal fixtures — using alkaline cleaners formulated for protein-based soot (common in kitchen fires) and dry-ice or chemical sponge methods for delicate finishes. Reconstruction is handled in-house, so you're not coordinating a separate contractor once the remediation phase closes.

## Local Note

Homes in the blocks surrounding First American Baptist Church and the older sections of Downtown Flandreau frequently have plaster-over-lath interior walls rather than drywall. Plaster is denser and more alkaline than gypsum board, which changes how soot bonds to the surface — it tends to penetrate less deeply, which is good, but it also means aggressive scrubbing can damage the finish irreparably. We use low-abrasion dry-sponge methods on plaster surfaces first, testing a small section before committing to a cleaning approach. It's a slower process than wiping down drywall, but it's the difference between restoring a wall and having to replaster it.
