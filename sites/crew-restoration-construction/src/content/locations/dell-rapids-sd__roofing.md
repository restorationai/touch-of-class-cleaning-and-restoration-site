## Why Dell Rapids Properties See Accelerated Roof Wear

The Sioux Falls metro gets roughly 25 inches of precipitation a year, but Dell Rapids homeowners along the Big Sioux River area deal with an added variable: low-lying lots that trap snowmelt and push moisture up through soffits and eaves long after a storm has passed. Quartzite bluffs funnel wind into the valley in ways that don't show up on a regional weather map, creating localized uplift pressure that loosens ridge caps and accelerates granule loss on standard three-tab shingles.

The housing stock adds another layer of complexity. Many homes near the Downtown Dell Rapids Historic District were built in the early-to-mid twentieth century, when roof pitches were steeper and decking was solid board lumber rather than OSB panels. That old-growth lumber can be structurally sound for decades, but it also holds moisture differently — a slow leak that would delaminate modern OSB in one season might stay hidden in board decking for years, rotting from the inside out before it shows up as a ceiling stain.

## Our Roofing Installation and Replacement Process in Dell Rapids

Every project starts with a full deck inspection, not just a shingle count. On older homes near the historic district, that means pulling back damaged sections to check for soft spots, previous patch jobs, and any original skip-sheathing that may need to be filled before new underlayment goes down. We photograph the deck condition before any materials are installed so you have a clear record — useful if an insurance claim is involved.

Underlayment selection matters more in this climate than in warmer regions. We use synthetic underlayment rated for South Dakota's temperature swings rather than felt paper, which can wrinkle and crack in sub-zero conditions. Ice-and-water shield gets extended further up the eave than code minimums require — at least 24 inches past the interior wall line — because the valley's wind patterns can drive ice dams into eave edges even on moderate-pitch roofs.

Flashing at chimneys, dormers, and roof-to-wall transitions gets step-flashed and counter-flashed rather than caulked over, which is the shortcut that causes most of the leak callbacks we're called in to fix on other contractors' work. Ridge ventilation is sized to the actual attic volume, not just installed as a visual checkbox, because proper airflow is what keeps ice dams from forming in the first place.
