## Why Dell Rapids Properties See Asbestos Issues

The building stock in Dell Rapids skews older than many communities its size. The quartzite-lined river corridor made the town a regional hub well before mid-century construction booms, which means a significant share of residential and commercial properties were built between the 1940s and 1970s — the peak decades for asbestos use in American construction. Popcorn ceilings, vinyl floor tiles in 9×9-inch squares, duct insulation wrapped in gray cloth tape, and textured drywall joint compound are the materials that show up most often during our inspections in these neighborhoods.

The climate adds another layer of concern. South Dakota's freeze-thaw cycles put mechanical stress on older building materials year after year. When pipe insulation cracks from repeated thermal expansion, or when a ceiling tile shifts after decades of humidity changes near the river, previously intact asbestos-containing material can become friable — meaning it crumbles and releases fibers into the air. A material that was safe to leave in place last winter may not be safe this spring.

## Our Asbestos Abatement Process in Dell Rapids

Every project starts with a thorough inspection and bulk sampling before any abatement work begins. Suspect materials are collected following established protocols and sent to an accredited laboratory for analysis. We don't skip this step even when a material looks obviously dated — visual identification of asbestos is not reliable, and guessing wrong has serious consequences.

Once lab results confirm the presence of asbestos-containing materials, we develop a site-specific abatement plan. For occupied homes, that means establishing a regulated work area with negative air pressure and HEPA filtration to prevent fiber migration into living spaces. Workers wear full personal protective equipment throughout. Removed materials are wetted to suppress fiber release, double-bagged in labeled disposal containers, and transported to a licensed hazardous waste facility — South Dakota regulations specify both the packaging requirements and the approved disposal sites.

For renovation projects in the Downtown Dell Rapids Historic District, we're also mindful of the additional care older structures require. Quartzite foundations and original plaster walls need to be worked around carefully; aggressive demolition methods that might be fine in a newer build can disturb materials in adjacent areas that weren't part of the original scope.
