## Why Dell Rapids Properties See Water Damage Issues

Dell Rapids sits in a narrow valley carved by the Big Sioux River, and that geography shapes the water risk here in ways that don't apply to a flat suburban neighborhood. The river's quartzite banks create a natural funnel — when upstream snowmelt or storm cells push the Big Sioux above its banks near The Dells of the Big Sioux, floodwater can back up into basements and crawl spaces along the river corridor faster than sump systems can respond.

Beyond flooding, the Downtown Dell Rapids Historic District presents its own challenges. Many of the structures there were built in the late 1800s and early 1900s, when foundation waterproofing was minimal or nonexistent. Older clay tile drainage systems around these foundations can collapse or clog, directing groundwater straight into basements during prolonged wet periods. Galvanized supply lines in pre-1950 homes are also prone to pinhole leaks that go unnoticed behind plaster walls until a ceiling or floor starts to buckle.

South Dakota's freeze-thaw cycle adds another layer of risk. Temperatures in Dell Rapids regularly swing below zero in January and February, and pipes routed through uninsulated exterior walls or unheated garages are vulnerable. A pipe that freezes overnight and bursts at 6 a.m. can discharge hundreds of gallons before the homeowner notices.

## Our Water Damage Restoration Process in Dell Rapids

The first priority on any water damage call is stopping the source — whether that means helping you locate a shutoff valve or coordinating with a plumber before extraction begins. Once the water source is controlled, the process moves through four concrete phases:

**1. Inspection and moisture mapping.** Technicians use thermal imaging cameras and calibrated moisture meters to trace water migration behind walls, under flooring, and into subfloor assemblies. In older Dell Rapids homes with plaster-and-lath construction, water often travels further laterally than it would in modern drywall, so mapping takes extra care.

**2. Water extraction.** Truck-mounted and portable extractors pull standing water and saturated material quickly. In finished basements along the Big Sioux River area, where carpet and pad are common, extraction removes the bulk of moisture before drying equipment is placed.

**3. Structural drying.** Industrial air movers and low-grain refrigerant dehumidifiers are positioned based on the moisture map, not just placed generically around the room. Drying logs are maintained daily so you — and your insurance adjuster — have documented proof of progress.

**4. Antimicrobial treatment and clearance.** Wet materials left more than 24–48 hours can begin supporting mold colonization. After drying is confirmed, affected surfaces receive an EPA-registered antimicrobial treatment before any reconstruction begins.

One thing that comes up specifically in older buildings near the Downtown Dell Rapids Historic District: plaster walls absorb and release moisture at a much slower rate than modern drywall.
