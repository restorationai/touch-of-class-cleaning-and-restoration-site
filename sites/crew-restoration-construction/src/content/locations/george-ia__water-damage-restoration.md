## Our Water Damage Restoration Process in George

The first step on any job is moisture mapping — we use thermal imaging cameras and calibrated moisture meters to find water that has already migrated behind walls, under flooring, or into subfloor sheathing. In George's older homes, this step matters more than in newer construction because plaster walls and tongue-and-groove wood subfloors hold water differently than modern materials. Water can travel six to eight feet from the visible wet area before you'd notice it by touch.

Once we understand the full extent of the loss, we extract standing water with truck-mounted and portable extraction units, then set industrial desiccant or refrigerant dehumidifiers and high-velocity air movers calibrated to the room's cubic footage and the material types present. Drying logs are recorded daily so there's a documented record of progress — something your insurance adjuster will ask for. We don't pull equipment until moisture readings in walls, floors, and structural members return to pre-loss baselines, not just until the surface feels dry.

If flooring, drywall, or insulation is too saturated to dry in place, we remove and dispose of it properly and document everything before demolition begins. Structural drying in George's older homes sometimes requires opening wall cavities to dry the framing directly — a step we don't skip because trapped moisture in a wall cavity is where mold colonization begins, typically within 48 to 72 hours of a water event.
