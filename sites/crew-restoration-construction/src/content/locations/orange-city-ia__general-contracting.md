## Why Orange City's Housing Stock Shapes Every Renovation

Northwest Iowa's climate is unforgiving in ways that leave marks on older homes. Winters routinely push temperatures well below zero, and the freeze-thaw cycling that follows every spring creates foundation movement, moisture infiltration at rim joists, and cracked masonry that compounds year over year. Many of the homes within a few blocks of Northwestern College and throughout the older residential streets were built in the mid-20th century or earlier, which means original plaster walls, knob-and-tube wiring that has often been partially updated but not fully replaced, and basement slabs poured without modern vapor barriers.

These aren't deal-breakers — they're just realities that have to be planned around. A kitchen remodel in a 1940s bungalow near the Tulip Festival grounds isn't the same project as one in a 2010 suburban build. Subfloor systems differ, load-bearing wall configurations differ, and the mechanical systems hiding behind the drywall often surprise even experienced crews. Knowing that going in changes how a project is scoped, bid, and sequenced.
