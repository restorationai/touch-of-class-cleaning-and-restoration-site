## Why Hull Properties See Siding and Gutter Issues

Sioux County sits in one of the more demanding climate zones in Iowa. Average January temperatures regularly drop below zero, and the freeze-thaw cycle that follows — sometimes multiple times in a single week during shoulder seasons — is what does the most damage. Water works into small gaps behind siding panels, freezes, expands, and pries the material away from the sheathing beneath. On homes near Hull City Park and throughout West Hull, where a good share of the housing stock dates to the mid-twentieth century, original wood lap siding and older aluminum profiles are especially vulnerable to this cycle. Gutters on these homes were often installed before modern 5-inch and 6-inch K-style profiles became standard, so they overflow during the kind of 2-inch-per-hour rain events that have become more common in northwest Iowa.

Agricultural dust is a less obvious factor. Hull sits surrounded by row-crop fields, and during dry stretches in late summer and fall, fine particulate settles into gutter troughs and accelerates the breakdown of painted surfaces. Downspouts clog faster here than they would in an urban environment, and that debris load adds weight that stresses gutter hangers over time.
