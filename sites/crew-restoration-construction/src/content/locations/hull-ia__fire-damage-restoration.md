## Why Hull Properties Face Distinct Fire Damage Challenges

Hull's housing stock tells the story of a tight-knit Dutch-American community that built steadily through the mid-twentieth century. ## Our Fire Damage Restoration Process in Hull

The first step after the fire department clears the scene is a thorough structural and smoke assessment. Fire damage isn't only what burned — it's the soot deposited on every surface the smoke touched, the water damage left by suppression efforts, and the odor molecules that have bonded to porous materials like insulation, carpet, and wood framing. We document all of it with photographs and moisture readings before any material is moved, which matters both for your insurance claim and for building a complete scope of work.

From there, the process moves through board-up and weatherproofing (critical during Iowa winters when an open structure can freeze within hours), debris removal, structural drying if suppression water is present, and then the detailed cleaning phase — dry sponging and chemical sponging of soot from walls and ceilings, HEPA vacuuming of duct systems, and thermal fogging or hydroxyl treatment for odor. In older Hull homes with plaster walls, soot cleaning requires a lighter touch than drywall; aggressive scrubbing can pull the surface and create more damage than the smoke left behind. Reconstruction — framing, drywall, insulation, paint — follows once the structure is confirmed dry and clean.
