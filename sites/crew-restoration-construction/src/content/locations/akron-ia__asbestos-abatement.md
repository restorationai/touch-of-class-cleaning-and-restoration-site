## Why Akron Properties See Asbestos Concerns

The housing stock along the River district and surrounding blocks reflects decades of construction that relied heavily on asbestos as an insulating and fireproofing material. Homes built between the 1940s and late 1970s commonly contain asbestos in floor tiles (especially 9"×9" vinyl squares), duct wrap, attic insulation, roof shingles, and popcorn ceiling texture. In a small agricultural community like Akron, many of those buildings have changed hands multiple times without a formal asbestos inspection — meaning the hazard can sit undisturbed for decades until a remodel or storm damage stirs it up.

The climate here adds another layer of risk. Harsh Iowa winters cause repeated freeze-thaw cycles that stress older building materials. When pipe insulation wrapped in asbestos-containing tape cracks from thermal expansion, or when a water intrusion event damages a textured ceiling, friable asbestos fibers can become airborne. That's when a material that was safely encapsulated becomes a genuine health concern.

## Our Asbestos Abatement Process in Akron

Every project starts with a thorough asbestos inspection and bulk material sampling before any removal begins. Suspected materials are collected following established protocols and sent to an accredited laboratory for analysis. You get a clear written report — not a verbal estimate — that identifies exactly what's present, where it is, and what the recommended course of action is.

When abatement is required, the work area is isolated using negative air pressure containment with HEPA filtration to prevent cross-contamination to the rest of the structure. Regulated materials are wetted during removal to suppress fiber release, then sealed in labeled disposal bags and transported to a permitted landfill that accepts asbestos waste. Iowa requires specific disposal documentation, and we handle that paperwork so the chain of custody is clean from start to finish.

For popcorn ceiling asbestos removal — one of the most common requests we get from homeowners updating older Akron properties — the process involves careful wet scraping under full containment, not a dry scrape with a shop vac. The difference matters enormously for air quality.
