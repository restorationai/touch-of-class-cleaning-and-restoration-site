## Why Dakota Dunes Properties Face Elevated Water Damage Risk

The 2011 Missouri River flood is still a reference point for long-time residents of this community. That event demonstrated what happens when the river overwhelms its banks in a low-lying, planned development like Dakota Dunes: water doesn't drain, it pools. Even in years without major flooding, the water table near the Missouri River corridor stays high through spring and into early summer. Crawl spaces and slab-on-grade foundations in neighborhoods like Two Rivers can show moisture intrusion weeks after a rain event that wouldn't register as a problem in a higher-elevation Sioux Falls subdivision.

The housing stock here also matters. Dakota Dunes was largely developed in the late 1980s and 1990s, which means many homes have finished lower levels, carpeted basements, and HVAC systems tucked into tight utility spaces. Water that reaches those areas spreads quickly across multiple material types, carpet pad, OSB subfloor, drywall, and fiberglass insulation, each with a different drying rate and each requiring a different extraction or drying approach.

## Our Emergency Water Cleanup Process in Dakota Dunes

When Crew Restoration & Construction arrives at a property in the 57049 ZIP code, the first priority is stopping ongoing water intrusion and identifying hidden moisture. That means thermal imaging and calibrated moisture meters on walls, floors, and ceilings, not just a visual inspection. Extraction comes next, using truck-mounted and portable units depending on access. After extraction, the team maps the drying zone, sets industrial air movers and dehumidifiers, and monitors moisture readings daily until materials reach acceptable levels per the IICRC S500 standard. Nothing is closed up until the numbers confirm it.
