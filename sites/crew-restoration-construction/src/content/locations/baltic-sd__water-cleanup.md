## Why Baltic Properties See Water Damage Issues

Baltic sits in the Big Sioux River valley east of Sioux Falls, and that geography shapes how water behaves here. Spring snowmelt across Minnehaha County pushes the river's floodplain outward, and homes in the Big Sioux River area face groundwater pressure that can overwhelm perimeter drainage and push moisture through foundation walls even when no surface flooding is visible.

The housing stock adds another layer of complexity. Many Baltic homes were built in eras when basement walls were poured with minimal waterproofing membrane, and older drain tile systems have had decades to crack, shift, or clog with root intrusion. When those systems fail during a heavy rain event, water doesn't just pool on the basement floor, it wicks into block or poured-concrete walls and migrates toward wood framing faster than a surface inspection would suggest.

Interior failures are just as common. Older galvanized supply lines, common in homes built before the 1970s, corrode from the inside out and can fail without warning. A slow leak behind a finished wall in Downtown Baltic can saturate insulation and framing for weeks before a stain appears on the drywall surface.

## Our Water Cleanup Process in Baltic

The first priority on any water loss is stopping ongoing exposure. Before extraction equipment is staged, the source of water entry is identified and, where possible, controlled. That step matters more in Baltic than it might in a newer suburban development: older plumbing configurations and shared utility chases can mean water has traveled further from the visible wet area than expected.

Once the source is addressed, the process follows a structured sequence:

- **Extraction:** Truck-mounted and portable extraction units pull standing water from hard surfaces and carpet. In finished basements, water under floating floors is located with moisture meters and addressed before it migrates into adjacent framing bays.
- **Structural drying:** Industrial air movers and refrigerant or desiccant dehumidifiers are positioned based on a drying plan, not guesswork. Readings are logged at each visit so the drying curve is documented.
- **Monitoring:** Moisture content in walls, subfloors, and framing is tracked across multiple visits until materials reach acceptable dry standards. Drying is not declared complete based on appearance alone.
- **Controlled demolition if needed:** When wet materials cannot be dried in place, saturated insulation, swollen OSB subfloor, compromised drywall, selective removal is performed to allow airflow to structural components behind them.
