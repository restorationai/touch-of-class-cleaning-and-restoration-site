## Why Hinton Properties Are Vulnerable After Storm and Fire Events

Hinton sits in a stretch of northwest Iowa where late-spring and early-fall storm systems can produce straight-line winds exceeding 70 mph, hail the size of golf balls, and rapid pressure drops that stress older rooflines. Many homes in the 51024 ZIP code were built in the mid-twentieth century — a period when roof sheathing was often 1×6 skip-sheathing rather than modern plywood or OSB panels. That construction style means a single blown shingle or cracked ridge cap can expose gaps in the deck that standard tarps need to bridge carefully, because there is no continuous substrate underneath to anchor into.

Fire events compound the problem. A kitchen fire that appears contained can compromise the structural integrity of roof rafters and exterior wall sheathing in ways that aren't obvious until an adjuster or contractor gets eyes on it. Leaving those openings unprotected — even overnight — allows moisture intrusion that turns a fire-damage claim into a combined fire-and-water-damage claim, which complicates both remediation and settlement.

## Our Board-Up and Tarping Process in Hinton

Every job starts with a perimeter assessment before any material goes up. We photograph all breach points, note structural concerns, and identify utilities — relevant in older Hinton properties where electrical service entries sometimes run close to damaged eaves. Board-up work on broken windows and compromised door frames uses 5/8-inch plywood cut and secured with structural screws, not staples, so the barrier holds against wind rather than just deterring casual entry.

For roof tarping, we use reinforced polyethylene tarps rated for high-wind exposure, anchored with weighted lumber battens and screwed into solid framing members rather than the damaged decking itself. On homes near the West Fork Little Sioux River where ground saturation can already be a factor, getting a watertight seal over the roof breach quickly is especially critical — a compromised attic above saturated soil creates conditions where interior humidity spikes rapidly, accelerating the timeline for secondary damage.

Once the structure is secured, we document everything with timestamped photos and written notes formatted for insurance submission, which reduces back-and-forth with adjusters on what was present at the time of emergency securing.
