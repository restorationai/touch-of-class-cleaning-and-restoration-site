## Why Edgerton Properties See Water Damage Differently

Southwest Minnesota's freeze-thaw cycle is hard on older plumbing. Pipes that run through uninsulated exterior walls or crawl spaces, which is common in Edgerton's pre-1960 housing stock, are vulnerable to bursting during the sharp cold snaps that roll through Pipestone County each winter. When a pipe lets go inside a wall cavity, the surface can feel dry to the touch while the framing and insulation behind it stay saturated for days. That hidden moisture is what drives mold colonization, which can begin in as little as 24 to 48 hours under the right conditions.

Chanarambie Creek flooding also creates a specific challenge: groundwater intrusion rather than clean supply-line water. Groundwater carries sediment, bacteria, and organic material, which means cleanup requires not just extraction and drying but also sanitizing affected surfaces to a standard that protects your family. ## Our Emergency Water Cleanup Process in Edgerton

The first step on any water loss is stopping the source, whether that means shutting off the main supply, coordinating with you on sump pump failure, or documenting the entry point for your insurance adjuster. From there, the process follows a clear sequence.

Standing water comes out first using truck-mounted and portable extraction units capable of pulling hundreds of gallons from finished and unfinished spaces. Once bulk water is removed, we use moisture meters and thermal imaging to map exactly where water has migrated, including inside wall cavities, under flooring, and into subfloor assemblies. Drying equipment, including industrial air movers and low-grain refrigerant dehumidifiers, is placed based on that mapping, not guesswork. Readings are logged and checked throughout the drying period so we can document the structure reaching accepted moisture levels per the IICRC S500 standard.

In homes with original plaster walls, which are common in Downtown Edgerton's older residential blocks, drying timelines run longer than they would in a newer drywall construction. Plaster is dense and releases absorbed moisture slowly, so equipment stays in place longer and monitoring is more frequent.
