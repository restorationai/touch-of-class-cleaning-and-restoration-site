## Why Luverne Properties See Mold Issues

The same prairie weather that makes Blue Mounds State Park dramatic to visit creates real moisture challenges for local homeowners. Spring snowmelt combines with clay-heavy soils that drain slowly, pushing groundwater against foundation walls for weeks at a stretch. Basements in Luverne — particularly in older homes built before the widespread adoption of modern vapor barriers — absorb that moisture through block or poured concrete walls, creating chronically damp conditions that mold thrives in.

Summer humidity compounds the problem. When warm, moist air from storm systems moving up from the south meets cooler interior surfaces — an uninsulated rim joist, a poorly ventilated crawl space, the backside of basement drywall — condensation forms. That film of moisture, invisible to the eye, is enough to feed a mold colony within days. Homes near Downtown Luverne, many of which date to the early and mid-twentieth century, often have original wood framing and plaster construction that holds moisture longer than modern materials, giving mold more time to establish before it's detected.

## Our Mold Remediation Process in Luverne

Every job starts with a thorough visual inspection and moisture mapping. We use thermal imaging and pin-type moisture meters to find wet material behind finished surfaces — because mold growing inside a wall cavity won't show itself until the colony is well established. Once we've identified the scope, we set up physical containment barriers with negative air pressure to keep spores from migrating to unaffected rooms while work is underway.

Remediation itself follows a methodical sequence: remove and bag contaminated porous materials (drywall, insulation, wood trim) that can't be cleaned to an acceptable standard, HEPA-vacuum structural surfaces, apply an EPA-registered antimicrobial treatment to affected framing, and run commercial air scrubbers to filter particulates from the contained work zone. Drying equipment — desiccant or refrigerant dehumidifiers depending on the season — runs until moisture readings in structural materials return to normal range. We don't close up walls until those numbers confirm the environment won't support regrowth.
