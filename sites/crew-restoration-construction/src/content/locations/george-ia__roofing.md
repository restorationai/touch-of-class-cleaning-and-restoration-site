## Why George Properties Face Accelerated Roof Wear

George is a working farm town, and its housing stock reflects that history. A significant share of homes in and around Downtown George were built between the 1920s and 1960s, when roof pitches were often low and decking was solid board lumber rather than modern OSB. That combination holds moisture longer when ice dams form along eaves — and in Lyon County, ice dams are a near-annual event. The town's position near the Lyon County line also means it sits in a wind corridor that funnels sustained gusts out of the northwest during winter storms, creating uplift pressure that standard three-tab shingles handle poorly over time.

Beyond age and exposure, the soil composition in this part of Iowa contributes to subtle foundation settling that can rack a roof frame just enough to cause ridge irregularities and valley leaks. These aren't dramatic failures — they're slow, quiet damage that shows up first as a stain on a bedroom ceiling and gets misread as a plumbing issue until someone gets on the roof.

## Our Roofing Installation and Replacement Process in George

Every project starts with a documented inspection — not a quick walk-around, but a systematic assessment of decking condition, flashing integrity, ventilation, and underlayment. In older George homes, we frequently find that original roof vents are undersized for current attic square footage, which traps heat in summer and accelerates shingle degradation from the underside. Correcting ventilation before laying new material is standard practice on our jobs, not an upsell.

Once the scope is confirmed, we strip existing layers down to the deck. Iowa code allows a second layer of shingles over an existing layer in some circumstances, but on homes with board-lumber decking we typically recommend a full tear-off so we can inspect and sister any soft spots. New synthetic underlayment goes down before any shingles, providing a secondary water barrier that matters especially during the spring storm season that hits Lyon County hard. We use architectural (dimensional) shingles rated for high-wind zones as our standard specification — they carry a significantly better uplift rating than three-tab and are better suited to the exposure George properties face.

Flashing around chimneys, dormers, and pipe penetrations gets replaced as part of the installation, not reused. Reusing old step flashing is one of the most common reasons a new roof leaks within two years.
