## Why Hawarden Properties See Water Damage Issues

Hawarden sits in the Big Sioux River floodplain, and that geography creates a specific set of risks that differ from inland Iowa communities. The river corridor can raise the local water table during prolonged rain events, which means sump pumps in older homes work harder and fail more often. Much of Hawarden's residential housing stock predates 1970, and homes built in that era frequently have stone or poured-concrete block foundations rather than modern poured-wall construction. Those older foundation systems develop hairline cracks over decades, and once the surrounding soil is saturated, hydrostatic pressure pushes water through gaps that were never a problem in drier years.

The Downtown Hawarden area includes commercial buildings and older residences with original clay-tile drain lines that can collapse or back up under heavy flow, sending water into finished spaces from below rather than above. That kind of intrusion is harder to detect at first glance because the surface may look dry while water is already wicking up through the slab or subfloor.

## Our Emergency Water Cleanup Process in Hawarden

When a call comes in, the first priority is stopping the source if it is still active, whether that means coordinating with a plumber on a burst pipe or working around a still-draining sump crock. Once the water source is controlled, the process moves through four stages calibrated to Hawarden's older housing conditions:

1. **Extraction** using truck-mounted and portable units to pull standing water from floors, carpeting, and subfloor cavities. In homes near the Calliope Village area, where crawl spaces are common, we deploy submersible pumps alongside standard extractors.
2. **Moisture mapping** with thermal imaging and calibrated moisture meters to find water that has traveled behind plaster, under hardwood, or into wall cavities. Older plaster walls common in pre-1960 Hawarden homes absorb water differently than modern drywall: the surface can feel firm while the lath behind it stays saturated for days.
3. **Structural drying** using industrial air movers and refrigerant dehumidifiers placed to create a drying system across the affected area, not just pointed at the visible wet spots.
4. **Monitoring and documentation** with daily moisture readings logged and photographed to support your insurance claim and confirm the structure is genuinely dry before any reconstruction begins.
