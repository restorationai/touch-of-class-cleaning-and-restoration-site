## Why Luverne Properties See Accelerated Roof Wear

The geography that makes Luverne a gateway to Blue Mounds State Park is the same geography that punishes roofing materials. The Coteau des Prairies escarpment channels wind in ways that feel different from flat-plain towns to the east — sustained gusts rather than brief bursts, which work at sealant joints and ridge caps over years of repeated stress. Hail frequency in Rock County ranks among the higher averages in Minnesota, and a single storm can granulate asphalt shingles enough to cut their remaining lifespan in half without leaving obvious visible damage from the ground.

Older homes near Downtown Luverne, many built in the mid-20th century, often have roof decks that were never designed for the heavier ice-and-water shield underlayments now standard in cold climates. When we pull back deteriorated shingles on those structures, we regularly find original skip sheathing — boards with gaps between them — that requires solid decking before any modern roofing system can be installed correctly. Skipping that step is how re-roofing jobs fail within five years.

## Our Roofing Installation and Replacement Process in Luverne

Every project starts with a full roof inspection: we photograph decking condition, measure slopes, note penetrations (vents, chimneys, satellite mounts), and document any existing storm damage for insurance purposes. Once the scope is agreed upon, we sequence the work to minimize the window your home is open to the sky — a real concern given how quickly afternoon storms build over the prairie in late spring and summer. Tear-off, decking repair, underlayment, and starter courses are completed before any crew breaks for the day when weather is uncertain. We install ice-and-water shield along all eaves and valleys to the depth required by Minnesota code, which is more aggressive than many warmer-state standards and for good reason given Rock County winters.

Material selection is a conversation, not a catalog hand-off. Architectural shingles rated for 130 mph winds perform differently than basic three-tab in this climate, and we walk through the trade-offs — cost, warranty, wind resistance class, and how each option looks on your specific roof pitch — before any order is placed.
