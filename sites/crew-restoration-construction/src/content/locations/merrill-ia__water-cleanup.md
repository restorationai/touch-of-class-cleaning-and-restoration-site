## Why Merrill Properties Face Distinct Water Damage Risks

Merrill sits in the Floyd River floodplain, and Plymouth County's clay-heavy soils drain slowly after heavy rain or snowmelt. That combination means water doesn't just come from inside a home, it migrates laterally through foundation walls and up through floor drains when the water table rises. Homes built before the 1970s, which make up a significant portion of the housing stock in and around Downtown Merrill, often have poured concrete or stone block foundations with aging waterproofing that was never designed for today's rainfall intensity. Sump pits in these basements can be undersized, and sump pumps that run continuously during a wet spring are failure candidates at exactly the wrong moment.

Even homes farther from the river aren't immune. Older cast-iron and galvanized supply lines common in mid-century construction can corrode from the inside out, failing without visible warning. When they do, water tracks along floor joists and into wall cavities before it ever pools visibly on the floor.

## Our Emergency Water Cleanup Process in Merrill

The first thing a crew does on arrival is identify the water source and stop the loss if it hasn't been stopped already. From there, the process follows a deliberate sequence calibrated to the actual conditions in the structure:

- **Moisture mapping** using thermal imaging and calibrated meters to find water that has migrated beyond the visible wet area, including inside wall assemblies and under flooring.
- **Standing water extraction** with truck-mounted and portable extractors. Older Merrill homes with hardwood subfloors or original wood flooring require slower, more controlled extraction to avoid cupping or delamination.
- **Structural drying** with industrial air movers and refrigerant or desiccant dehumidifiers positioned to create calculated airflow patterns. Drying goals are set to IICRC S500 standards, not guesswork.
- **Daily monitoring** with logged moisture readings so the drying record is complete and defensible for your insurance carrier.
- **Controlled demolition** only where necessary, wet insulation, saturated drywall, or flooring that cannot be dried in place gets removed to prevent secondary mold growth.
