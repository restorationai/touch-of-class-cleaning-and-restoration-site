## Why Brandon Properties Face Accelerated Roofing Wear

Brandon sits in a climate corridor that delivers the full range of South Dakota's worst roofing conditions. Late-spring hailstorms — often severe enough to qualify as insurance events — track through the Sioux Falls metro and hit the 57005 ZIP code with regularity. That impact damage weakens the mat beneath asphalt shingles even when the surface looks intact, shortening effective roof life by years. Winter brings freeze-thaw cycling that forces water into any micro-crack along flashing, ridge caps, or around pipe boots, then expands it with each overnight freeze. 

Brandon's newer housing stock adds another layer of complexity. Many subdivisions built in the last fifteen years used builder-grade shingles rated for a 25- or 30-year lifespan under ideal conditions — but South Dakota conditions are rarely ideal. Roofs in the Brandon Valley corridor that were installed during the mid-2000s building boom are now showing the kind of widespread granule loss and tab cracking that signals it's time for full replacement rather than another round of spot repairs.

## Our Roofing Installation and Replacement Process in Brandon

Every project starts with a detailed inspection — not a quick walk-around, but a systematic evaluation of decking condition, flashing integrity, ventilation performance, and underlayment. In Brandon, we pay particular attention to attic ventilation because inadequate soffit-to-ridge airflow is common in the tract-built homes near Aspen Park and contributes directly to ice damming in winter and premature shingle baking in summer.

Once the scope is clear, we walk you through material options suited to South Dakota's climate: impact-resistant shingles rated Class 3 or Class 4 (which can affect your insurance premium), architectural shingles with heavier mat construction, and metal roofing for homeowners who want a longer-term solution. We pull the required permits through Lincoln County, handle all staging and debris removal, and install new synthetic underlayment and ice-and-water shield along all eaves and valleys before a single shingle goes down. The job isn't finished until every piece of flashing is sealed, every ridge vent is seated correctly, and the site is clean.

## Local Note

One thing we've noticed working in Brandon's newer subdivisions: many homes built in the early 2000s were framed with engineered roof trusses designed to tight tolerances, and some of those trusses show signs of truss uplift — a condition where the bottom chord separates from interior walls during temperature swings. It doesn't affect the structural integrity of the roof deck itself, but it does mean we inspect the decking attachment points carefully before re-roofing, because a truss that's cycling seasonally can work fasteners loose over time. Catching it during a replacement project is far less disruptive than discovering it later.
