## Our Emergency Board-Up and Tarping Process in Baltic

Every job starts with a walk of the full perimeter. We photograph every opening, every compromised roof section, and every point where weather or fire has weakened the structure before we touch anything. That documentation matters for your insurance adjuster and gives us a clear sequence for closing the building envelope efficiently.

For window and door openings, we cut and fasten exterior-grade plywood to the surrounding framing — not just taped or propped, but anchored so it can withstand wind loads. For roof damage, we use reinforced polyethylene tarps rated for the kind of sustained wind and UV exposure that South Dakota spring and summer weather demands. Tarps are secured with weighted battens and screwed-down lumber caps along ridges and eaves, not just bungee cords or rope. When fire damage has weakened load-bearing elements, we assess whether the opening can be safely boarded from inside before anyone goes up on the roof.

If the structure has smoke or fire odor, we note it in the job report. That detail often matters when a homeowner is filing a claim and the adjuster needs to understand the full scope of the initial loss.
