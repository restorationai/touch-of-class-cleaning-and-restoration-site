## Why Orange City Properties See Elevated Storm Damage Risk

Orange City's Dutch-heritage building stock is part of what makes the community distinctive — and part of what makes storm damage more complicated here. A meaningful share of homes in the Downtown Orange City corridor and on the streets radiating out from Windmill Park were built between the 1920s and 1960s, when roof decking was often solid board sheathing rather than modern OSB panels. Board-sheathed roofs can flex and gap under high-wind uplift in ways that allow water infiltration even when shingles appear intact from the ground. That slow, hidden intrusion is easy to miss until ceiling drywall begins to sag or attic insulation is already saturated.

Sioux County also sits in a hail belt that sees multiple significant hail events most years. Stones large enough to crack asphalt shingles and dent aluminum soffit are not unusual between April and September. Because the town's tree canopy — particularly the mature elms and oaks near Northwestern College and along the park corridors — is dense in places, falling limbs during wind events add structural puncture damage on top of the roofing and siding losses that hail alone causes.

## Our Storm Damage Restoration Process in Orange City

When we arrive at a storm-damaged property, the first priority is stopping active water entry. That means emergency tarping of breached roof sections, boarding compromised windows, and documenting every point of entry with photographs before anything is moved or covered. That documentation record becomes the backbone of your insurance claim.

Once the structure is stabilized, we move into a systematic damage assessment. We use moisture meters and thermal imaging to trace where water has migrated inside wall cavities and floor assemblies — because in older homes with plank subfloors and plaster walls, water travels farther and hides longer than it does in newer construction. Affected materials are catalogued, and a scope of work is written in line with Xactimate pricing, the standard format most Iowa-licensed insurers use for residential claims.

Drying equipment — commercial-grade desiccant dehumidifiers and high-velocity air movers — is placed based on the moisture map, not guesswork. We monitor readings daily and adjust equipment placement until structural readings return to acceptable baselines. Only then do reconstruction repairs begin: roofing, siding, drywall, insulation, and finish work handled by the same crew rather than handed off to a separate contractor.
