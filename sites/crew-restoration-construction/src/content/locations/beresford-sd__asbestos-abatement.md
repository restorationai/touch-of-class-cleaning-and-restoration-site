## Why Beresford Properties Face Asbestos Exposure Risks

South Dakota's building boom in the mid-twentieth century coincided almost exactly with peak asbestos use in residential construction. Homes built between roughly 1940 and 1980 — a category that covers a large share of the housing stock in and around Downtown Beresford — commonly contain asbestos in floor tile adhesive, pipe insulation, attic insulation, roofing shingles, and the spray-applied acoustic texture that became known as popcorn ceiling. That last material is one of the most frequently disturbed during remodels: a homeowner scrapes a bedroom ceiling, and what looked like a cosmetic project becomes a remediation job.

Beresford's climate adds another layer of concern. Harsh South Dakota winters drive freeze-thaw cycling that stresses older building materials year after year. When pipe insulation wrapping expands and contracts repeatedly, it can crack and begin shedding fibers. When an older roof takes on ice damming, the underlying sheathing and any asbestos-containing felt beneath it can degrade. These aren't hypothetical risks — they're the kinds of conditions our team encounters regularly when we're called out to properties in this part of the state.

## Our Asbestos Abatement Process in Beresford

Every job starts with an inspection and bulk sampling before a single tool touches suspect material. Samples go to an accredited laboratory, and we don't proceed with removal until results are in hand. That step matters especially in Beresford, where older homes sometimes have multiple generations of renovation layered on top of one another — original 1950s floor tile covered by 1970s sheet vinyl covered by modern laminate, each layer potentially containing different hazardous materials.

Once testing confirms asbestos, we establish a regulated work area. That means physical containment with polyethylene sheeting, negative air pressure using HEPA-filtered equipment, and strict decontamination procedures for anyone entering or exiting the zone. Asbestos-containing material is wetted before removal to suppress fiber release, double-bagged in labeled disposal bags, and transported to a licensed disposal facility in compliance with South Dakota Department of Agriculture and Natural Resources requirements. After removal, air clearance testing confirms the space is safe before containment comes down.

For popcorn ceiling asbestos removal specifically, we work room by room so you're not displaced from your entire home at once — a practical consideration for families who need to stay on-site during the project.
