## Our Water Damage Restoration Process in Rock Valley

The first thing that matters after water intrusion is stopping further absorption. When we arrive at a Rock Valley property, we begin with a full moisture assessment — probing walls, floors, and cavities with calibrated meters to map where water has traveled, not just where it's visible. Standing water gets extracted with truck-mounted or portable units depending on access, and that step happens before any drying equipment is placed.

Once extraction is complete, we establish a drying system calibrated to the structure. In Rock Valley homes with older plaster or tongue-and-groove wood floors, drying timelines run longer than in newer construction — those materials hold moisture deeper and release it more slowly than modern drywall or engineered flooring. Industrial air movers and refrigerant dehumidifiers run continuously, and we monitor moisture readings on return visits to confirm the structure is trending toward dry rather than just surface-dry.

If building materials — drywall, insulation, flooring underlayment — are saturated beyond recovery, we document them thoroughly before removal. That documentation matters for your insurance claim and for any reconstruction work that follows.

Most water damage claims in Iowa are filed under the dwelling coverage section of a homeowner's policy, and the documentation phase is where claims get approved or disputed.
