## Why Tea Properties Are Particularly Vulnerable After a Loss

The Lincoln County corridor around Tea sees some of the most intense convective weather in the region — late-spring supercells, straight-line wind events, and hail that can exceed golf-ball size. Because so much of Tea's housing stock was built within the last fifteen years, many homes have OSB roof decking and engineered lumber framing rather than the dense old-growth lumber found in older Sioux Falls neighborhoods. OSB absorbs standing water quickly; once a roof breach is left open through even a single overnight rain, the decking can swell and delaminate, turning what was a roofing claim into a structural one.

Fire damage is the other major driver for board-up calls in the area. A kitchen fire that chars a wall or blows out a window leaves the interior exposed to both weather and opportunistic entry. Securing the structure fast — before the adjuster even arrives — protects the remaining contents and keeps the insurance scope from expanding unnecessarily.

## Our Emergency Board-Up and Tarping Process in Tea

When a call comes in from the 57064 area, the first step is a rapid assessment of what the structure needs: roof tarping, window and door board-up, or both. For roof tarping, we use reinforced polyethylene tarps secured with weighted battens and mechanical fasteners rather than just stapling edges down — a method that holds through the wind gusts that are common on Tea's open lots where there are no mature trees or neighboring structures to break the force.

For window and door board-up, we cut plywood panels to fit each opening precisely, fasten them with structural screws rather than nails, and seal the perimeter to limit water intrusion. On fire-damaged structures, we also assess whether the opening is load-bearing before staging any materials nearby, since fire can compromise headers and lintels that aren't visually obvious. Every job is photographed in detail before, during, and after — documentation your insurance carrier will want.
