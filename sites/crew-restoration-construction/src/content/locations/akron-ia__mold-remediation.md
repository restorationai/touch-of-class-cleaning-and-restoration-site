## Why Akron Properties See Mold Issues

Akron sits at the confluence of the Big Sioux River valley and the South Dakota border, which means it catches weather systems rolling in from both directions — wet springs from the east and dry-then-wet cycles from the Great Plains to the west. That swing between drought and saturation stresses foundation walls, causes soil to shift and crack, and opens pathways for water to enter basements and crawl spaces that weren't there the season before.

The housing stock along the River District and near Downtown Akron includes a significant number of homes built before modern vapor barriers and waterproofing membranes were standard. Older block foundations, unvented crawl spaces, and plaster-over-lath interior walls absorb and hold moisture in ways that newer construction simply doesn't. By the time a homeowner notices a musty smell or a dark stain spreading across a basement wall, mold has often been actively growing for weeks — sometimes longer if the source was a slow plumbing leak rather than a visible flood event.

Farm-adjacent properties on the outskirts of town face an additional factor: grain dust and organic debris in outbuildings and attached garages give mold spores an easy food source if humidity climbs. That combination of older construction and agricultural context makes mold abatement in Akron a different job than it is in a newer suburban development.

## Our Mold Remediation Process in Akron

Every job starts with a thorough visual inspection and moisture mapping. We use thermal imaging and calibrated moisture meters to trace water migration behind walls and under flooring — because the visible mold growth is rarely the full picture. In Akron's older homes, moisture often travels several feet from the original intrusion point before it finds a surface to colonize.

Once we understand the scope, we establish physical containment using negative air pressure and heavy-duty poly barriers. This keeps mold spores from spreading to unaffected rooms during the removal process — a step that matters especially in occupied homes near Akron-Westfield School families who may have young children or residents with respiratory sensitivities.

After containment is in place, affected materials are removed following mold abatement protocols: porous materials that cannot be adequately cleaned are bagged and disposed of properly, while structural components are HEPA-vacuumed and treated with an EPA-registered antimicrobial agent. Industrial air scrubbers run continuously throughout the project to capture airborne spores. Before we close out any job, we verify that moisture readings have returned to acceptable levels — because mold cleanup without addressing the underlying moisture source is a temporary fix at best.
