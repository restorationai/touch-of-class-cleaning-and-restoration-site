## Why Tea Properties See Elevated Storm Damage Risk

Tea's position just southwest of Sioux Falls puts it squarely in the corridor where supercell thunderstorms tend to track during late spring and early summer. The flat, open terrain offers no natural windbreak, so gusts that might lose energy in a more sheltered area arrive at full force. That's a real problem for the vinyl siding profiles common on homes built in the last decade — hail that would dent aluminum simply punches through vinyl, leaving dozens of small punctures that funnel water into the wall cavity.

The town's growth rate also means a large share of the housing stock was framed within the last fifteen years, during a period when engineered lumber trusses became standard. Those trusses perform well under normal loads but can rack or separate at connection points when a tornado or straight-line wind event applies lateral force — damage that isn't always visible from the street but shows up clearly during a thorough structural inspection.

Legacy Park and the surrounding residential streets have also seen repeated episodes of surface flooding after intense rain events, because the storm drainage infrastructure in some of those areas was sized for a smaller population than Tea now has. Pooling water against foundation walls accelerates moisture intrusion even when the storm itself caused no direct structural damage.

## Our Storm Damage Restoration Process in Tea

Every job starts with a documented inspection — photographs, moisture readings, and a written scope before any material is moved. That documentation matters for your insurance claim and for identifying damage that isn't obvious: lifted shingles that look intact from the ground, water that has tracked along a rafter and pooled two rooms away from the actual roof breach, or window flashing that separated just enough to allow slow infiltration.

Once the scope is established, the work moves in a logical sequence: emergency tarping or board-up to stop active water intrusion, water extraction and structural drying if interior moisture is present, debris removal (including tree sections, which require coordinated equipment staging in tighter residential lots), and then the reconstruction phase — roofing, siding, windows, drywall, and finish work handled under one contractor rather than handed off between trades.

For homes in Prairie Meadows and similar newer subdivisions, we pay particular attention to the interface between the roofing system and the attic insulation layer. Blown-in insulation that absorbs storm water becomes a slow-release moisture source that can sustain elevated humidity in the attic for weeks, creating conditions favorable for mold colonization even after the visible damage is repaired.

## Insurance Coordination for Tea Storm Claims

South Dakota storm claims — especially hail claims — move faster when the documentation is thorough from the first day. We photograph every affected surface, note the hail diameter where it can be measured from impact marks, and produce a written estimate formatted to align with the line items your adjuster will be working from. That reduces the back-and-forth that delays approval and pushes your repair timeline out by weeks.

If your adjuster's scope and ours differ on the extent of damage — a common situation with hail that caused hidden fascia or decking damage — we can walk the property with the adjuster directly. We bill most major carriers and can discuss supplement documentation if additional damage is identified once work begins.
