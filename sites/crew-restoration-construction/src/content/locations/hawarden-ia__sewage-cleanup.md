## Why Hawarden Properties Are Prone to Sewage Backup

Hawarden's housing stock tells a lot of the story. Many homes in and around the Central Avenue district were built in the mid-20th century or earlier, when clay-tile sewer laterals were the standard. Those pipes crack, root-infiltrate, and collapse over decades — and when they fail, they fail completely. A single heavy rain that raises the Big Sioux's water table can push enough hydrostatic pressure into a compromised lateral to send sewage back through floor drains and basement toilets before a homeowner even realizes there's a problem.

Sioux County's clay-heavy soil compounds the issue. Unlike sandy soil that drains quickly, clay holds moisture against foundation walls and around buried pipe joints, accelerating deterioration and creating conditions where a partial blockage becomes a full backup after the first significant rainfall of the season. Homes with older septic systems on the outskirts of town — particularly properties near the Hawarden Golf Course corridor — face additional risk from drain fields that can become saturated during wet springs, pushing effluent back toward the structure.

## Our Sewage Cleanup and Sanitization Process in Hawarden

Sewage cleanup is a Category 3 water loss — the most contaminated classification — and it requires a different protocol than a burst supply line or appliance leak. The process Crew Restoration & Construction follows is built around that reality.

**Containment first.** Before any extraction begins, affected areas are isolated to prevent cross-contamination into clean zones of the home. In finished basements — common in Hawarden's older ranch-style and two-story homes — this means sealing HVAC registers and doorways before touching the standing waste.

**Extraction and removal.** Truck-mounted extraction equipment removes standing sewage. Contaminated porous materials — drywall, insulation, carpet, pad — are removed and bagged for proper disposal. Subfloor plywood and OSB are assessed; if saturation has compromised the wood, those sections come out too. Salvageable materials are documented for insurance purposes.

**Disinfection and sanitization.** After the structure is cleared of contaminated material, all affected surfaces — concrete, framing, joists — are treated with EPA-registered disinfectants rated for Category 3 contamination. This isn't a single spray-and-wipe step; it involves dwell time, agitation, and a second application on porous substrates.

**Drying and verification.** Industrial air movers and dehumidifiers run until moisture readings in framing and concrete return to baseline. In Hawarden's humid summers, that process can take longer than it would in a drier climate — ambient humidity slows evaporation, and we account for that in drying timelines rather than pulling equipment prematurely.
