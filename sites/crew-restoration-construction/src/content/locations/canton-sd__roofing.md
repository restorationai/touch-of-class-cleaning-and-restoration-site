## Why Canton Properties See Roofing Problems

The older housing stock in and around Downtown Canton tells the story plainly. Many of the homes within a few blocks of the Lincoln County Courthouse were built in the early to mid-twentieth century, and their original roof decks — often skip-sheathing or board-and-batten rather than modern OSB — have been covered and recovered multiple times. By the time a homeowner calls about a leak, there may be two or three layers of shingles hiding deteriorated underlayment and soft decking underneath. That's not a repair situation; it's a replacement.

The Big Sioux River area compounds this with moisture. Properties on the lower elevations near the river see higher ambient humidity during spring thaw and after heavy rain events. That moisture migrates upward, and in an attic with inadequate ventilation — common in pre-1960 construction — it condenses against the underside of the roof deck and begins breaking down the wood from the inside out. A roof that looks serviceable from the street can have serious structural rot that only shows up once you're pulling shingles.

Hail is the other consistent driver. Lincoln County sits in a hail corridor that sees damaging storms most summers. Impact-rated shingles (Class 4 IR) are worth the conversation on any Canton replacement project — some carriers offer premium discounts for them, and they genuinely outlast standard three-tab or architectural shingles in this climate.

## Our Roofing Installation and Replacement Process in Canton

Every project starts with a full roof inspection — not a drive-by estimate, but a physical examination of the decking, flashing, ridge, valleys, and penetrations. We document what we find with photos before anything comes off the roof, which matters if you're filing an insurance claim for storm damage.

Demolition means stripping down to the deck and assessing every sheet or board for soft spots, delamination, or rot. Damaged decking gets replaced before new underlayment goes down — skipping that step is how a new roof fails in five years. We use synthetic underlayment rather than felt in most Canton applications because it handles the temperature swings better and doesn't wrinkle or tear during installation the way saturated felt paper can.

Flashing details get particular attention on older Canton homes. The step flashing along dormers and chimneys, the valley metal, and the drip edge at the eaves are where most long-term leaks originate. We replace all of it on a full roof replacement rather than reusing existing flashing — it's a small material cost relative to the labor already on the roof.

Ridge ventilation is installed or upgraded as part of every replacement. Proper attic airflow is the single most effective way to extend shingle life in South Dakota's climate, and it's frequently missing or undersized in the older homes near Newton Hills State Park and throughout the Canton area.

One thing that comes up specifically on Canton properties near the Big Sioux River floodplain: some of those lots have mature cottonwood and elm trees with canopies that overhang the roofline. Cottonwood seed and debris pack into valleys and gutters faster than almost any other tree species, and the moisture those packed debris pockets hold against the shingles accelerates granule loss and algae growth noticeably faster than on comparable homes without tree cover.
