## Why Rock Valley Properties See Contents Losses

The Rock River has shaped the land around Rock Valley for generations, and properties near its banks carry a specific risk profile. Seasonal snowmelt and spring rain can push the river into low-lying yards and, in significant events, into finished living spaces. When that happens, contents on lower floors absorb contaminated water quickly. Fabric and upholstered furniture wick moisture upward; wood furniture swells and warps within hours; paper documents begin to degrade almost immediately.

The housing stock in and around Downtown Rock Valley includes older construction where basements were finished with materials that hold moisture rather than shed it. Carpet padding, particleboard shelving, and older drywall all act as reservoirs, keeping the humidity around your belongings elevated long after the visible water is gone. That extended exposure is what turns a salvageable piece of furniture into a mold-colonized loss, and it happens faster in Iowa's humid summer months than most homeowners expect.

## Our Contents Restoration Process in Rock Valley

The first step is a detailed inventory. Before anything leaves your property, every item is photographed, cataloged, and assigned a condition rating. This documentation protects you during the insurance claim process and gives you a clear record of what was removed and where it went.

Items that can be restored on-site are treated there when conditions allow. Items requiring deeper cleaning, deodorization, or drying are packed out to a climate-controlled storage facility where they are processed away from the ongoing structural work. Textiles go through thermal or ozone deodorization to address smoke and floodwater odors at the fiber level rather than masking them. Hard surfaces are cleaned with appropriate agents matched to the material. Electronics are assessed by technicians before any power is applied.

For contents affected by Rock River flooding, we pay particular attention to contamination level. Category 3 water, which floodwater typically is, requires a different cleaning protocol than a clean supply-line break. Items that cannot be brought to a safe hygiene standard are documented for your adjuster rather than returned to you as if they were fine.

Once structural drying and repairs are complete, contents are returned, unwrapped, and placed according to your direction. The IICRC-certified team at Crew Restoration follows established industry standards throughout, which matters when your carrier reviews the claim.
