## Why Pipestone Properties See Mold Issues

Pipestone sits in a region where continental winters deliver sustained cold followed by rapid spring thaws. That temperature swing is hard on building envelopes — especially in homes built before 1970, which make up a significant portion of Pipestone's residential housing stock. When warm, humid air from spring storms meets surfaces that are still cold from winter, condensation forms inside wall cavities and attic spaces where it can go undetected for weeks.

The Hiawatha area, with its mix of older single-family homes, sees this pattern regularly. Basements in this part of town tend to have minimal vapor barriers and older drainage tile that can allow ground moisture to wick inward after heavy snowmelt. Add to that the fact that Pipestone's soil has high clay content, which holds water longer than sandy soils, and you have conditions that keep foundation walls damp well into summer. Mold only needs 24 to 48 hours and a moisture source to begin colonizing — and clay-heavy soil gives it a long window.

## Our Mold Remediation Process in Pipestone

Every job starts with a thorough moisture assessment using thermal imaging and calibrated moisture meters. We identify not just where mold is visible but where elevated moisture readings suggest it may be growing behind surfaces. That matters especially in older Pipestone homes where plaster-and-lath construction can trap moisture in ways that standard drywall does not.

Once the scope is clear, we establish negative-air containment barriers to prevent spores from spreading to unaffected areas of the home during removal. Affected materials — whether that's drywall, insulation, subfloor sheathing, or wood framing — are removed, bagged, and disposed of per EPA guidelines. Remaining structural surfaces are treated with an EPA-registered antimicrobial agent. We then run commercial-grade HEPA air scrubbers throughout the containment zone until air quality readings confirm the area is clear before we close out containment.

Drying the underlying moisture source is just as important as removing the mold itself. We use desiccant or refrigerant dehumidifiers sized to the space, and we leave equipment running until moisture readings in structural materials return to acceptable ranges — typically below 16% in wood framing. Skipping that step is the most common reason mold returns.
