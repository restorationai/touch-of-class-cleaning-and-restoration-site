## Why Garretson Properties See Water Damage Issues

Garretson sits in a shallow valley carved by Split Rock Creek, and the surrounding landscape doesn't drain the way flat prairie does. The Sioux quartzite bedrock that makes Palisades State Park and Devil's Gulch so visually striking also means the soil above it has limited depth — water that can't percolate down tends to move laterally, directly toward foundations. Older homes in and around Downtown Garretson were often built on shallow footings without modern waterproofing membranes, so even a moderate rain event can push moisture through block or stone foundation walls.

South Dakota's freeze-thaw cycle compounds the problem. Pipes in exterior walls or uninsulated crawl spaces are vulnerable from November through March. When a pipe bursts at 2 a.m. in January, water can spread across hardwood floors and into subfloor framing within minutes. The longer it sits, the deeper it migrates — and mold can begin colonizing wet framing in as little as 48 to 72 hours.

## Our Water Damage Restoration Process in Garretson

Every job starts with a thorough moisture assessment. We use thermal imaging cameras and calibrated moisture meters to map exactly where water has traveled — not just the visible wet spots, but the moisture that has wicked into wall cavities, insulation, and subflooring. This matters especially in Garretson's older housing stock, where original plaster walls and tongue-and-groove subfloors hold water differently than modern construction.

Once we understand the full scope, water extraction comes first. We deploy truck-mounted and portable extraction units to pull standing water quickly, then set industrial air movers and desiccant or refrigerant dehumidifiers to begin structural drying. Drying timelines are tracked daily with moisture readings — we don't pull equipment until the structure reads dry, not just until it feels dry. Any materials that can't be dried in place — saturated insulation, compromised drywall — are removed and documented for your insurance claim before disposal.

## Local Note: What Garretson's Geology Means for Drying Times

Here's something that comes up specifically in the Palisades area and properties close to Split Rock Creek: homes built near the creek corridor often have crawl spaces or basements that sit on or very close to the quartzite shelf. That rock holds cold temperatures well into spring, which keeps the ambient temperature in those spaces lower than the rest of the house. Cold air holds less moisture, which sounds helpful — but it also means dehumidifiers work harder and drying takes longer than the industry average. We account for this by running supplemental heat in affected crawl spaces alongside dehumidification, which brings the dew point up and allows equipment to pull moisture out of materials more efficiently. It's a small adjustment that makes a real difference in total drying time.
