## Why Garretson Homes Face Distinct Fire Damage Challenges

Garretson's housing stock skews older. Many homes near Downtown Garretson were built in the early-to-mid twentieth century, when balloon-frame construction was the norm. In a balloon-frame house, wall cavities run uninterrupted from the foundation sill to the roof ridge — which means fire and smoke travel vertically through the structure with almost nothing to slow them. A kitchen fire that looks contained to one room can push smoke residue into attic insulation and wall cavities two floors above the point of origin.

The region's climate adds another layer of complexity. South Dakota winters are dry and cold, and a fire that occurs between November and March leaves a structure exposed to sub-zero temperatures almost immediately after suppression. Water used by the Garretson Fire Department to extinguish the blaze can freeze inside wall cavities within hours, causing secondary damage to framing and masonry that wasn't touched by the fire itself. Addressing that freeze risk is part of our first-response stabilization, not an afterthought.

The Sioux quartzite geology that makes the Palisades area so visually striking also influences how smoke behaves in older stone or masonry-clad structures near the park. Porous quartzite surfaces absorb soot and acidic smoke residue differently than standard brick or concrete block, requiring pH-adjusted cleaning agents and extended contact times rather than a standard wipe-down.

## Our Fire Damage Restoration Process in Garretson

Every job starts with a structured assessment — not a sales walk, but a room-by-room documentation of char depth, smoke migration, water intrusion from suppression efforts, and structural integrity. We photograph and log everything before a single piece of debris is moved, because that documentation becomes the foundation of your insurance claim.

From there, the process follows a deliberate sequence:

**Board-up and stabilization** comes first. Broken windows, compromised roof sections, and damaged entry points are secured to prevent weather intrusion and unauthorized access while the property is uninhabitable.

**Debris removal and controlled demolition** follows. Charred framing, saturated insulation, and smoke-damaged drywall that cannot be cleaned to an acceptable standard are removed and disposed of properly. In older Garretson homes, this step sometimes reveals lead paint or asbestos-containing materials in floor tiles or pipe insulation — we identify those materials early and handle them according to applicable protocols rather than discovering them mid-project.

**Smoke and soot cleaning** is the most labor-intensive phase. Dry chemical sponges, HEPA vacuuming, and wet cleaning with appropriate solvents are applied surface by surface. Ductwork is cleaned or sealed to prevent residual odor from recirculating once the HVAC system is restored.

**Structural drying** addresses suppression water. Industrial air movers and dehumidifiers run until moisture readings in framing and subfloor return to pre-loss baselines.

**Odor neutralization** closes the loop. Thermal fogging and hydroxyl generation reach into cavities and porous surfaces where surface cleaning alone cannot penetrate.

**Reconstruction** — drywall, trim, flooring, paint — is handled by the same crew, so there's no handoff gap between mitigation and rebuild.
