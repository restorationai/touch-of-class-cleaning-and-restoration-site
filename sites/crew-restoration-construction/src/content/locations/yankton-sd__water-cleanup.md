## Why Yankton Properties Face Recurring Water Damage

Yankton sits at the confluence of the Missouri and James Rivers, and the geography shapes how water behaves here. Gavins Point Dam regulates flow on Lewis and Clark Lake, but controlled releases during high-runoff seasons still push groundwater tables upward across low-lying sections of the city. Properties in the Riverside neighborhood are particularly exposed during spring snowmelt, when saturated soil has nowhere to drain and basement walls begin to seep before any visible flooding occurs.

The housing stock adds another layer of complexity. A significant portion of Yankton's residential neighborhoods were built before modern moisture barriers and drainage standards were common. Older homes often have rubble-stone or poured-concrete foundations without waterproof coatings, and the gap between interior finish materials and the exterior wall assembly is narrow. Water that enters through a foundation crack or a failed sump can migrate behind plaster or original hardwood subfloors before it becomes visible on the surface.

Westside properties on higher ground are not immune either. Aging supply lines and galvanized plumbing in homes from the mid-twentieth century fail without warning, and a slow pinhole leak inside a wall cavity can saturate insulation and framing for weeks before a homeowner notices a soft spot in the floor or a stain on the ceiling.

## Our Emergency Water Cleanup Process in Yankton

When a call comes in, the first step on-site is containment and assessment. Technicians use moisture meters and thermal imaging to map where water has traveled, including areas that feel dry to the touch. Standing water is extracted using truck-mounted and portable units capable of pulling water from carpet, subfloor assemblies, and crawl spaces.

Once bulk water is removed, the drying phase begins. Industrial air movers and dehumidifiers are positioned based on the moisture readings, not by guesswork. Readings are logged daily so drying progress is documented, which matters both for structural integrity and for insurance claim purposes. Materials that cannot be dried in place, such as saturated insulation or swollen particleboard, are removed and documented before disposal.

In Yankton's older homes, this process often takes longer than it would in newer construction. Plaster walls, for example, absorb water more slowly than drywall but also release it more slowly. Drying targets are set using the IICRC S500 standard, and equipment stays in place until those targets are met, not just until the surface feels dry.
