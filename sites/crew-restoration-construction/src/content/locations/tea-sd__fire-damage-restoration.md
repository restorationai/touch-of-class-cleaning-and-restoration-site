## Why Tea's New Construction Creates Specific Fire Damage Challenges

The same building boom that put Tea on the map also created a restoration challenge that isn't obvious until you're standing inside a fire-damaged home in the 57064 ZIP code. Engineered wood products like LVL beams and I-joists lose structural integrity faster under heat than solid lumber — which means a structural assessment has to happen before any cleaning or content removal begins. Beyond the framing, the synthetic materials common in post-2010 construction off-gas during combustion, leaving behind a protein-rich, acidic soot that etches chrome fixtures, discolors grout, and corrodes copper wiring if it isn't neutralized within the first 24 to 48 hours. Prairie Meadows homes, many of which feature open-concept great rooms, give that smoke a wide path to travel — by the time the fire is out, the odor and residue are often in every room.

## Our Fire Damage Restoration Process in Tea

The first step on any fire loss is a full structural and safety assessment — no cleaning crews enter until load-bearing elements are confirmed sound and utilities are verified safe. From there, the process moves in a deliberate sequence:

**Board-up and weather protection.** South Dakota weather doesn't wait. A structure opened by fire suppression in spring or fall can take on rain or wind damage within hours. We secure openings fast to stop secondary losses before they compound the claim.

**Soot and smoke mapping.** Using thermal imaging and air sampling, we trace exactly how far smoke traveled through the structure. In open-plan Tea homes, this often reveals contamination in bedrooms and closets that look untouched.

**Dry ice blasting and HEPA vacuuming.** For structural surfaces — framing, subfloor, joists — dry ice blasting removes char and soot without adding moisture, which matters in a region where humidity swings can slow drying. HEPA vacuuming follows on contents and finished surfaces.

**Deodorization.** Thermal fogging and hydroxyl generation run simultaneously to break down odor molecules embedded in insulation, drywall, and HVAC ductwork. Duct cleaning is almost always necessary in Tea's newer forced-air homes.

**Reconstruction.** Once the structure is clean and dry, our construction team handles drywall, flooring, cabinetry, and finish work — so you're coordinating with one company, not three.
