## Why Tea Properties See Sewage Backup Issues

Tea sits on the eastern edge of the James River Lowlands, where expansive clay soils shift noticeably with freeze-thaw cycles. That soil movement puts lateral sewer lines under stress year after year, and in newer subdivisions the PVC connections between a home's private lateral and the city main have not yet had time to settle. The result is offset joints and root intrusion that show up as slow drains in the fall and full backups by the time spring snowmelt saturates the ground. Older sections near Downtown Tea sometimes have cast-iron or Orangeburg-era pipe that is simply at the end of its service life. On the septic side, many properties on the western fringe of Tea are still on private systems, and a single wet season can overwhelm a drain field that was sized for a house with two occupants and now serves a family of five.

## Our Sewage Cleanup and Sanitization Process in Tea

Raw sewage is classified as Category 3 water — the most contaminated type — which means the work goes well beyond pumping out visible standing water. The process Crew Restoration & Construction follows in Tea homes starts with containment: sealing off the affected area to prevent cross-contamination to finished living spaces while the standing waste is extracted using truck-mounted equipment. Every porous material that absorbed sewage-laden water — drywall below the flood line, carpet, insulation, subflooring — is removed and bagged for proper disposal. What remains is treated with EPA-registered disinfectants applied at the correct dwell times, not sprayed and immediately wiped. Structural drying follows using industrial dehumidifiers and air movers, with moisture readings documented at each check until the materials reach target levels. A final sanitization pass confirms the space is safe before any reconstruction begins.
