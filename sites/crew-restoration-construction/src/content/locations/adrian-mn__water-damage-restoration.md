## Why Adrian Properties See Water Damage Issues

Adrian sits in a low-relief prairie landscape where spring snowmelt and heavy summer thunderstorms have nowhere to drain quickly. The clay-heavy soils common across Nobles County don't absorb standing water well, which means saturated ground pushes hydrostatic pressure against foundation walls and floor slabs for days after a storm event — long after the rain has stopped. Sump pumps in older homes near Downtown Adrian work overtime during these windows, and a single mechanical failure can put several inches of water across a finished basement in under an hour.

The region also sees hard freeze-thaw cycles every winter. Supply lines that run through exterior walls or uninsulated rim joists — a common construction detail in homes built before 1970 — are vulnerable to freezing when temperatures drop into the single digits, which happens multiple times each January and February in this part of Minnesota. When those pipes burst, the water often runs inside wall cavities for some time before it reaches a visible surface, meaning the hidden damage is frequently worse than what you can see.

## Our Water Damage Restoration Process in Adrian

When we arrive at a property, the first priority is stopping the source if it hasn't been stopped already — shutting off the main supply line or isolating the affected zone. From there, the process follows a structured sequence:

**Moisture mapping.** We use thermal imaging and calibrated moisture meters to trace water migration through subfloor assemblies, wall cavities, and insulation bays. In older Adrian homes with plaster-and-lath construction, water wicks laterally through the plaster much farther than the visible stain suggests, so we map well beyond the obvious wet zone.

**Water extraction.** Truck-mounted and portable extraction units pull standing water from hard surfaces and saturated carpet or pad. In basements with floor drains — common in the farm-era homes found throughout East Adrian — we confirm the drain is clear before extraction so we're not working against a blocked outlet.

**Structural drying.** Commercial-grade desiccant and refrigerant dehumidifiers, paired with high-velocity air movers, are positioned to dry structural materials to IICRC S500 standard target levels. Drying typically runs three to five days depending on material saturation and ambient conditions, and we monitor readings daily.

**Documentation.** Every moisture reading, photo, and equipment placement log is recorded and formatted for insurance submission. We work directly with adjusters so the claim file reflects the actual scope of the loss.
