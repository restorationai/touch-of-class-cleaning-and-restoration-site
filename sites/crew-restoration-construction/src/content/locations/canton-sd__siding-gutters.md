## Why Canton Properties See Siding and Gutter Problems

The combination of freeze-thaw cycling and proximity to the Big Sioux River creates conditions that accelerate exterior wear faster than the regional average. Canton's winters regularly push below zero, and the temperature swings between January nights and March afternoons can cycle dozens of times in a single season. Every freeze expands whatever moisture has worked its way behind a siding panel or into a gutter seam; every thaw contracts it. Over five or ten seasons, that movement separates joints, lifts flashing, and cracks caulk lines that were never meant to flex that hard.

The housing stock near Downtown Canton compounds the issue. A significant share of homes in the 57013 ZIP code were built between the 1920s and 1960s, when sheathing was often board-on-board rather than modern OSB or plywood. When water gets behind siding on those homes, it doesn't wick into a sheet product — it travels along individual boards, spreads laterally, and can rot framing members before the damage is visible from the street. Identifying the true extent of that damage before new siding goes on is a critical step that gets skipped when the focus is only on the visible surface.

Gutters on older Canton homes are frequently 4-inch K-style systems that were sized for the roof pitch and square footage of the original structure. Additions, garage conversions, and re-roofing projects over the decades have often increased effective roof area without upgrading the gutter capacity, leaving downspouts overwhelmed during the heavy spring storms that move through Lincoln County.

## Our Siding and Gutter Process in Canton

Every project starts with a documented exterior inspection — not a sales walkthrough, but a systematic review of the substrate behind the existing siding. We probe fascia and soffit for soft spots, check flashing at window and door penetrations, and photograph any areas where moisture has already reached the sheathing. That documentation matters both for accurate material estimates and for insurance claims when storm damage is involved.

For siding replacement, we remove existing material in sections rather than all at once, which keeps the structure protected if weather moves in mid-project. We inspect and replace any compromised sheathing before new panels go on, and we flash every penetration to current standards regardless of what was done originally. Material selection — fiber cement, engineered wood, or premium vinyl — is matched to the specific exposure of each elevation. A south-facing wall in Canton that gets direct sun and wind-driven rain needs a different product spec than a sheltered north wall.

Gutter installation is sized to actual roof load, not to whatever was there before. We calculate square footage per downspout run, factor in roof pitch, and specify 5-inch or 6-inch systems where the math calls for it. Downspout extensions are routed to carry water at least four feet from the foundation — a detail that matters especially on lots near the Big Sioux River area where the water table can be seasonally elevated.
