## Why Sioux Falls Properties Are Especially Vulnerable After Sudden Damage

Sioux Falls sits squarely in a corridor that sees some of the most intense convective weather in the upper Midwest. Hail events are frequent enough that local insurers track them by season, and straight-line wind events regularly push past 70 mph through the metro. That combination creates a specific board-up and tarping profile that differs from, say, a coastal market.

The housing stock adds another layer of complexity. Neighborhoods like the Cathedral Historic District and All Saints contain a high concentration of homes built before 1950 — many with original wood-framed roof decks, older slate or clay tile roofing, and single-pane windows in historic casings. When a branch or hail impact breaches those older assemblies, the damage pathway is faster than in newer construction. Moisture reaches structural framing quickly, and a roof deck that was already showing its age can delaminate within hours of exposure. Getting a properly weighted tarp down — not just draped — over that older framing matters more than it does on a 2015 build with OSB sheathing.

Fire damage board-up presents its own set of Sioux Falls-specific considerations. After a structure fire, the Sioux Falls Fire Rescue typically releases a scene within hours, but the property is left open to the elements and to unauthorized entry. Boarding up window and door openings with code-compliant plywood (minimum ½-inch, secured into the framing rather than just the trim) is often a condition of the insurance adjuster's initial inspection approval.

## Our Board-Up and Tarping Process in Sioux Falls

When a call comes in from a property in the 57105 or 57103 ZIP codes — or anywhere across the metro — the first step is a rapid damage assessment. We identify every compromised opening: broken windows, kicked-in doors, sections of roof decking exposed by missing shingles or structural damage, and any wall penetrations from fire or impact.

For roof tarping, we use reinforced polyethylene tarps rated for high-wind conditions, secured with furring strips screwed into undamaged decking rather than relying on weight bags alone. In Sioux Falls, wind direction shifts fast — a tarp that's only weighted on the south edge can billow and tear loose in a north gust within the same storm system. We overlap tarp edges by at least 18 inches and run the ridge line past the peak so water sheds away from the opening rather than pooling at the edge.

For broken window board-up and fire damage board-up, we cut plywood to fit the rough opening, pre-drill to avoid splitting older trim, and fasten into structural members. On historic properties in the Cathedral Historic District, we take care not to damage original millwork that can be salvaged during the subsequent repair phase.

Every job gets photographed before, during, and after — documentation that your insurance adjuster will ask for.

## Local Note: What Sioux Falls Crews Know About Wind and Tarp Failure

One thing that catches out-of-town contractors working in the Sioux Falls area is how quickly wind direction reverses during and after a Plains thunderstorm. A tarp secured for a southwest wind — the most common storm approach direction here — can be completely undermined within the same event when the back side of the cell pushes a north or northeast gust. Local crews account for this by securing all four edges independently rather than relying on a single anchor direction, and by checking tarp tension after the initial storm passes if a second cell is in the forecast. It's a small detail that prevents a second call-out and a second damage event on the same property.
