## Why Fulda Properties Are Vulnerable to Severe Weather Damage

Murray County sits squarely in a corridor that funnels weather systems northeast off the South Dakota plains. Fulda's position between two lakes amplifies that exposure — evaporation off Seven Mile Lake and the surrounding Fulda Lakes basin keeps low-level moisture elevated even when the broader region is dry, which intensifies localized thunderstorm activity during spring and early summer.

The housing stock adds another layer of risk. A significant share of homes in and around Downtown Fulda were built in the mid-twentieth century, when roof sheathing was often board-on-board rather than modern OSB panels. That sheathing holds up fine under normal conditions, but once a shingle lifts or a ridge cap blows off, water infiltrates the gaps between boards quickly and begins wicking into the attic insulation and ceiling joists within hours. Older wood framing also tends to have more accumulated paint layers and organic material that mold can colonize — IICRC guidelines note that mold can begin establishing within 24 to 48 hours on wet organic material, so the window between storm and remediation call matters.

Tree canopy is another factor. Mature trees throughout Lakeside Fulda and the residential streets near Downtown Fulda provide welcome shade in summer, but large cottonwoods and elms are prone to limb failure under the combination of saturated soil and high wind. A fallen limb that punches through a roof section or tears away a fascia board creates an immediate water intrusion point that needs both structural repair and moisture mitigation — two scopes that have to be coordinated, not treated separately.

## Our Storm Damage Restoration Process in Fulda

The first priority after any storm event is stopping active water intrusion. Before any drying equipment is staged, the crew performs a roof-to-foundation inspection to identify every breach — missing shingles, cracked flashing, broken soffits, displaced gutters, and any structural compromise from fallen trees or debris. Temporary tarping and board-up happen the same visit so the interior is protected while the longer restoration scope is being documented.

Once the envelope is secured, moisture mapping begins. We use thermal imaging and calibrated moisture meters to trace how far water has traveled through wall cavities, subfloor assemblies, and insulation bays. In older Fulda homes with plaster ceilings, this step is especially important — plaster absorbs water slowly and releases it slowly, so the visible water stain on a ceiling often understates how saturated the material above it actually is.

Structural drying follows, using industrial air movers and dehumidifiers sized to the actual cubic footage of the affected space. Readings are logged daily so drying progress is documented — documentation that also feeds directly into the insurance claim file. Once materials reach target moisture levels, reconstruction begins: sheathing replacement, roofing, interior drywall, insulation, and finish work handled by the same crew rather than handed off to a separate contractor.
