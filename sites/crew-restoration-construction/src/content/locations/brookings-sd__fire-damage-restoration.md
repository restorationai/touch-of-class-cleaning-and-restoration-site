## Why Brookings Properties See Distinct Fire Damage Patterns

Brookings winters are punishing — sustained cold snaps push residents to run space heaters, overload older electrical circuits, and sometimes leave kitchen appliances running longer than they should. That seasonal pressure on aging wiring is one of the more common fire triggers in the college-town rental stock near SDSU, where homes built in the 1950s and 1960s may still carry original knob-and-tube or early aluminum wiring. When those fires happen, they tend to spread faster through open attic spaces common in that era of construction.

Beyond ignition patterns, South Dakota's dry winter air accelerates the bonding of smoke residue to porous surfaces. Soot that might stay loosely suspended in a more humid climate can embed itself into drywall, insulation, and wood framing within hours of a fire being extinguished. That timeline matters — the longer smoke residue sits, the deeper it penetrates and the harder it is to remove without replacing the underlying material.

## Our Fire Damage Restoration Process in Brookings

Every job starts with a full structural and content assessment. We photograph and document damage room by room before anything is moved or cleaned — that documentation becomes the foundation of your insurance claim. From there, the process follows a clear sequence:

**Board-up and stabilization.** If windows, doors, or roof sections are compromised, we secure the structure against the elements and unauthorized entry before restoration begins. Brookings winters leave no margin for an unsecured opening.

**Soot and smoke removal.** Dry soot is vacuumed with HEPA-filtered equipment before any wet cleaning begins. Applying water or cleaning agents to dry soot smears it deeper into surfaces. We work from the ceiling down, treating each material type — painted drywall, exposed wood, masonry — with the appropriate chemistry.

**Structural drying and dehumidification.** Water from firefighting efforts soaks into subfloors, wall cavities, and insulation. We deploy commercial-grade air movers and desiccant dehumidifiers calibrated to the moisture readings we take throughout the structure, not just in the rooms that look wet.

**Odor neutralization.** Smoke odor is not a surface problem — it lives in HVAC ductwork, wall cavities, and soft contents. We use thermal fogging and hydroxyl generation to reach areas that surface cleaning cannot, then clear the space with fresh-air ventilation before any reconstruction begins.
