## Why Dell Rapids Properties Are Especially Vulnerable After a Loss

Dell Rapids sits in a narrow corridor where the Big Sioux River has carved through ancient quartzite, and that geography shapes the risk profile of local properties in a few distinct ways. Lots along the Big Sioux River area sit in low-lying flood zones where a single hard rain can push water into basements and crawl spaces within hours of a storm that also damages roofing. When a roof is compromised at the same time as ground-level flooding, the window for securing the structure before secondary damage begins is extremely short.

The housing stock adds another layer of complexity. Dell Rapids was platted in the late 1800s, and many homes — particularly those close to the Dell Rapids Museum and the older blocks of the downtown district — were built with balloon-frame or early platform construction. These frames have larger open wall cavities than modern builds, which means wind can travel through a breach and lift sheathing from the inside out. A board-up that would hold on a 1990s ranch may need heavier fastening and additional bracing on a century-old two-story.

South Dakota's climate is the third factor. The region sees late-spring hail events, summer derecho-style straight-line winds, and rapid freeze-thaw cycles in shoulder seasons. A tarp installed without proper ballasting and edge sealing can fail overnight when temperatures drop 30 degrees — a pattern that happens regularly in this part of the state between October and April.

## Our Board-Up and Tarping Process in Dell Rapids

When we arrive on site, the first step is a rapid structural assessment — not a full inspection, but a trained read of what's load-bearing, what's actively failing, and what poses an immediate safety hazard. We document everything photographically before we touch a single board, because that documentation is what your insurance adjuster will use to establish the pre-mitigation condition of the loss.

For broken window board-up and fire damage board-up, we use ⅝-inch OSB fastened with structural screws rather than nails, which allows cleaner removal during the rebuild phase and holds better against lateral wind load. Openings larger than a standard window get a secondary frame built into the rough opening before the panel goes on.

Roof tarping on Dell Rapids homes follows a similar logic. We use reinforced polyethylene tarps — minimum 6-mil, heavier for large spans — and secure them with batten boards screwed through the tarp into the decking rather than relying on rope or sandbags alone. On steeper-pitched roofs common in older neighborhoods, we extend the tarp well past the ridge so wind cannot get underneath and peel it back. Every edge is sealed or weighted. We do not leave a tarp that will be gone by morning.
