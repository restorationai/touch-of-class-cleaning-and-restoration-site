## Why Salem Properties Present Unique Biohazard Challenges

Salem sits in a part of South Dakota where temperature swings are dramatic — winters that push well below zero and summers that climb past 90°F. That thermal cycling works on older building materials in ways that matter for biohazard remediation. Many homes in and around Downtown Salem were built before 1960, when subflooring was often solid board lumber rather than plywood or OSB. Board subfloors are more porous and have more seams, which means biological material can wick deeper into the structure before anyone calls for help. Remediation in these homes requires more aggressive containment and longer treatment times than a newer build would.

The rural and agricultural character of McCook County also shapes the types of situations we respond to — from outbuildings and equipment storage areas to rental properties that have sat unoccupied through a South Dakota winter. Unoccupied structures in this climate can develop secondary concerns quickly once a biohazard situation goes unaddressed, making early intervention critical.

## Our Biohazard Cleanup Process in Salem

Every job begins with a thorough assessment before any materials are moved or treated. We identify the full extent of the affected area — including surfaces that aren't immediately visible — and establish containment to prevent cross-contamination to unaffected parts of the property. This step is especially important in older Salem homes where open floor joist bays and unsealed wall cavities can allow material to travel beyond the obvious impact zone.

Once containment is in place, our team removes and properly packages all affected materials following infectious material cleanup protocols. Porous materials that cannot be decontaminated to a safe standard are bagged, labeled, and disposed of in compliance with South Dakota Department of Agriculture and Natural Resources guidelines for regulated waste. Hard surfaces are treated with EPA-registered disinfectants and verified clean before containment is removed. We document every step — both for your peace of mind and for insurance purposes.

Sharps and needle cleanup, when present, follows a separate handling protocol with puncture-resistant containers and chain-of-custody documentation. We do not skip this step or consolidate it with general debris removal.
