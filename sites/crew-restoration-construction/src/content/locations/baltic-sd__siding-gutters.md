## Why Baltic Properties See Siding and Gutter Issues

The stretch of homes near the Big Sioux River area experiences freeze-thaw cycles that are harder on exterior materials than a straight cold winter would be. Water infiltrates a small gap in vinyl or fiber cement siding, freezes overnight, and mechanically widens that gap by spring. Repeat that process across a South Dakota winter and what started as a hairline separation becomes a channel for bulk water. Gutters compound the problem: when they pull away from the fascia — common on homes that have settled or shifted — runoff pours directly against the foundation wall rather than being directed away from it.

Downtown Baltic's older commercial and mixed-use buildings add another layer of complexity. Many of those structures were built with wood lap siding or older aluminum profiles that are no longer in production, meaning a partial repair often requires sourcing compatible materials or transitioning the full elevation to a current product. Getting that match right matters for both weatherproofing and curb appeal on a main-street property.

## Our Siding and Gutters Process in Baltic

Every project starts with a thorough exterior inspection — not just the obvious damaged panel, but the flashing at windows and doors, the condition of the housewrap or building paper underneath, and the pitch and attachment points of the existing gutter system. Skipping that step is how a siding job turns into a callback six months later.

For siding, the work sequence runs: remove damaged or deteriorated cladding, inspect and dry the sheathing if moisture has penetrated, install or repair the water-resistive barrier, then hang and fasten the new material to manufacturer specs and local wind-load requirements. South Dakota's plains wind exposure means fastener pattern and panel overlap aren't details to cut corners on — a panel that looks fine in July can catch wind like a sail in a November storm.

Gutter work follows a similar discipline. We size the gutters to the roof's drainage area rather than defaulting to a standard width, set the correct slope toward downspouts, and position downspout extensions to discharge well away from the foundation. On properties near the Big Sioux River area where ground saturation is already elevated in spring, that discharge distance matters more than it would on a drier lot.
