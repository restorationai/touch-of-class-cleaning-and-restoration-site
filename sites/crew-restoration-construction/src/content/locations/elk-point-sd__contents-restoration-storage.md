## Why Elk Point Homes Create Specific Contents Challenges

Many of the residences near Downtown Elk Point and along the older blocks west of the Union County Courthouse were built in eras when interior finishes meant horsehair plaster, solid-wood millwork, and furniture constructed with hide glue and veneer over solid cores. These materials respond to water and smoke very differently than modern construction. Plaster walls release absorbed moisture slowly, which means the air inside an older Elk Point home stays humid longer after a water loss, and that extended humidity accelerates damage to everything stored inside: fabric, leather, paper, electronics, and wood furniture all continue to degrade while the structure is still drying.

Seasonal swings compound the problem. Winter temperatures in this part of South Dakota can push pipes to their limits, and a freeze-thaw event that cracks a supply line can release water for hours before it's noticed. Summer thunderstorm seasons along the Missouri River corridor bring their own risk: wind-driven rain through older window frames, basement seepage from saturated soil, and the kind of rapid humidity spikes that accelerate mold colonization on porous contents within 24 to 48 hours of initial exposure.

## Our Contents Restoration & Storage Process in Elk Point

The process begins with a detailed inventory before anything leaves your property. Every item is photographed, catalogued, and assessed for the type of damage present: smoke odor, soot coating, water saturation, or mold growth. That documentation matters both for your own records and for the insurance claim process.

Items that can be restored are transported to our facility, where the work is matched to the material. Ultrasonic cleaning removes soot from hard surfaces and delicate items without abrasion. Ozone and hydroxyl treatment addresses smoke odor in soft goods, clothing, and upholstered furniture. Documents and photographs go through a separate drying and stabilization process. Electronics are assessed by technicians before any attempt at restoration. Textiles are cleaned and dried under controlled conditions, not simply laundered and returned.

While restoration work is underway, items that are undamaged but need to be out of the way during construction are held in climate-controlled storage. That matters in Elk Point's climate: a storage environment that isn't temperature and humidity controlled will simply re-damage belongings that survived the original loss.

Crew Restoration & Construction is IICRC certified, which means the drying and restoration protocols we apply to contents follow the same evidence-based standards used for structural drying.
