## Why Salem Properties Face Compounding Fire Damage Challenges

Salem sits along the I-90 corridor as the McCook County seat, and much of its residential housing stock reflects that history — a mix of older farmhouse-style builds and mid-century homes with wood-lath construction, plaster walls, and original balloon framing. These structural features matter enormously after a fire. Balloon-framed walls have open cavities that run floor-to-roofline, meaning smoke and heat travel vertically with almost no resistance. A kitchen fire that looks contained to one room can leave smoke odor and soot deposits two stories up inside the wall system before the flames are even extinguished.

South Dakota's climate adds another layer. The region's low winter humidity accelerates surface drying but also causes fire-damaged wood to crack and warp faster. In summer, high humidity slows the off-gassing of smoke compounds from porous materials like insulation and carpet pad, which is why post-fire odor problems tend to linger longer in warm-weather losses. Understanding these seasonal patterns shapes how the restoration timeline is built.

## Our Fire Damage Restoration Process in Salem

Every fire loss is different, but the sequence of work follows a disciplined order that prevents one phase from undermining the next.

**Emergency stabilization** comes first — boarding windows and doors, tarping roof damage, and establishing a moisture baseline before any drying or cleaning begins. If firefighting water is still present, it gets extracted immediately; standing water left in a fire-damaged structure can begin supporting mold growth within 24 to 48 hours.

**Soot and smoke removal** is the most labor-intensive phase. Dry soot from fast-burning fires behaves differently than the oily, wet soot left by slow smoldering fires — the cleaning chemistry and technique change accordingly. Protein-based smoke from kitchen fires leaves an almost invisible film that carries a sharp odor and bonds to painted surfaces; it requires enzymatic treatments rather than standard detergents.

**Structural assessment and selective demolition** follows. Charred framing members are evaluated for load-bearing integrity. In older Salem homes with plaster-and-lath walls, we often find that smoke has migrated behind intact plaster surfaces — thermal imaging helps identify hidden contamination zones before walls are closed back up.

**Deodorization and air treatment** closes out the remediation phase, using hydroxyl generators or thermal fogging calibrated to the specific smoke type and building size.

**Reconstruction** then restores the structure to pre-loss condition, coordinated directly with your insurance adjuster.

## Local Note

One thing we've learned working in older McCook County homes: original plaster walls absorb smoke compounds at a different rate than modern drywall, and they release those compounds slowly over weeks. A homeowner who thinks the odor problem is resolved after surface cleaning often finds the smell returning once the house warms up in spring. In these structures, we extend the deodorization phase and use vapor-barrier primers on plaster surfaces before repainting — a step that's easy to skip but almost always necessary in pre-1960 construction to prevent odor bleed-through on the finished walls.
