## Why Crooks Properties See Water Damage Issues

The land around Crooks sits on expansive clay-heavy soils common to eastern South Dakota. When that soil freezes and thaws repeatedly through a Midwest winter, it shifts — and it pushes against foundations in ways that open hairline cracks wide enough for groundwater to seep through. Homes near Crooks City Park and throughout West Crooks that were built in the 1970s and 1980s often have older drain tile systems that were never designed for today's rainfall intensity. Beyond seasonal flooding, the Crooks area sees its share of burst pipes. ## Our Water Damage Restoration Process in Crooks

The first thing we do on arrival is locate and stop the water source if it hasn't been shut off already. From there, the process follows a clear sequence:

**1. Inspection and moisture mapping.** We use thermal imaging cameras and calibrated moisture meters to trace water migration through walls and under flooring. Water that looks contained at the surface is often traveling further than it appears — especially in homes with OSB subfloor, which wicks moisture laterally.

**2. Water extraction.** Truck-mounted extraction units pull standing water from carpet, hardwood, and concrete quickly. For crawl spaces and tight corners, we use portable extractors sized for the space.

**3. Structural drying.** Industrial-grade desiccant dehumidifiers and high-velocity air movers are placed according to a drying plan calibrated to the room's square footage, material types, and current ambient humidity. We monitor readings daily and adjust equipment placement as materials dry.

**4. Antimicrobial treatment.** Anywhere moisture has been present for more than 24–48 hours is treated to inhibit microbial growth before we close up walls.

**5. Documentation for your claim.** Every moisture reading, photo, and drying log is compiled into a report formatted for insurance adjusters.
