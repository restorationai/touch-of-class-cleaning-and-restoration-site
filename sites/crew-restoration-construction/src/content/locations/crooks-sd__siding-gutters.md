## Why Crooks Properties See Siding and Gutter Problems

South Dakota's climate swings are hard on exterior materials in ways that don't get enough attention. Temperatures in the Sioux Falls metro can drop 40 degrees in a single day during shoulder seasons, and that thermal cycling causes vinyl siding to expand and contract repeatedly until fasteners loosen and panels begin to buckle or gap. When those gaps open, wind-driven rain and snowmelt find their way behind the cladding and into the wall cavity — often without any visible sign until moisture has already reached the sheathing.

Gutters take a parallel beating. Ice dams are common here from December through February. When meltwater refreezes at the eave line, the weight and expansion can pull gutter sections away from the fascia, bend hangers, and crack seams. A gutter that looks intact from the ground in March may be pulling free at every third hanger after a hard winter. Homes near Crooks City Park, where mature trees line the lots, also deal with accelerated debris loading — oak and cottonwood leaves pack into 5-inch gutters fast and create standing water that accelerates corrosion at the seams.

## Our Siding and Gutter Process in Crooks

Every project starts with a full exterior walk — not just the side that's visibly damaged. Hail hits all four elevations, and wind damage often wraps around corners in ways that a quick front-yard look misses. We document conditions with photos before any material is removed, which matters both for insurance claims and for making sure the replacement scope is complete.

For siding, we remove damaged sections back to a clean break point, inspect the housewrap or felt paper underneath, and replace any compromised sheathing before the new panels go on. We match profiles and finishes as closely as current manufacturer lines allow — on older homes in West Crooks where original lap profiles may be narrower than today's standard offerings, we source from specialty distributors rather than forcing a mismatch.

For gutters, we use seamless aluminum stock cut on-site, which eliminates the mid-run seam joints that are the first place sectional gutters fail in freeze-thaw conditions. Hangers are spaced at 24 inches maximum — tighter on long runs — and we set slope to drain fully rather than leaving low spots where water pools and freezes. Downspout extensions are positioned to discharge at least four feet from the foundation, which matters on the flatter lots common in this part of Minnehaha County.

## Local Note

One thing we've noticed on homes in the older sections of Downtown Crooks: some of the original wood fascia boards are thinner stock than what's used today — closer to 3/4 inch actual thickness rather than the nominal 1-inch boards standard now. When gutter hangers are pulled out and reset, that thinner fascia can split if you use the same hanger spacing and screw gauge you'd use on newer construction. We pre-drill on those houses and use a slightly longer screw with a wider washer head to distribute the load. It's a small thing, but it's the difference between a gutter that holds through the next ice season and one that starts pulling away again by January.
