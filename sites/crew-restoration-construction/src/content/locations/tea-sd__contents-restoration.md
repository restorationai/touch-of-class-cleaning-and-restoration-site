## Our Contents Restoration and Storage Process in Tea

The first step after we arrive is a room-by-room contents inventory. Every item is photographed, tagged, and logged before anything moves. That documentation matters for your insurance claim and gives you a clear chain of custody for everything we take off-site.

From there, items are sorted into three categories: salvageable through cleaning, salvageable with specialized treatment, or non-restorable. Soft goods like upholstered furniture and textiles go through thermal fogging and ozone treatment for odor neutralization. Hard surfaces — wood furniture, cabinetry components, decorative items — are cleaned using ultrasonic equipment or hand-cleaning methods matched to the material. Electronics and documents get routed to specialists when the damage warrants it.

Once cleaned, items move into climate-controlled storage. This matters more in Tea than people expect: temperature swings between a cold truck and a warm home can cause secondary damage to wood furniture and electronics if items aren't stored properly during the repair window. We hold everything in a controlled environment until the structure is ready, then coordinate a scheduled return and placement.

## Tea Insurance Coordination for Contents Claims

Contents losses are often the most disputed part of a property insurance claim. Adjusters want itemized documentation, replacement cost values, and evidence that restoration was attempted before replacement was approved. The pack-out inventory we create at the start of every job is built with that process in mind — line-item photos, condition notes, and cleaning records that support your claim rather than leaving gaps for an adjuster to question.

Most homeowners in Tea carry standard HO-3 policies that cover sudden and accidental losses. If the damage stems from a slow leak or deferred maintenance, coverage gets complicated.
