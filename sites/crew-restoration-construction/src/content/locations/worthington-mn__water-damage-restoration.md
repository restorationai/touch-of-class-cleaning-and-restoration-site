## Why Worthington Properties See Water Damage Issues

The soil around Lake Okabena is clay-heavy, which means it holds moisture rather than draining it. During a wet spring or after a prolonged rain event, that saturated clay presses against foundation walls and finds any crack, gap around a utility penetration, or aging drain tile. Homes in the Lake Okabena area — many of them built in the mid-20th century — often have original block or poured concrete foundations that have settled and shifted over decades. A hairline crack that looks minor in July can become a steady seep by April.

Downtown Worthington and the surrounding residential blocks add a different layer of risk: older plumbing. Supply lines and drain stacks in homes built before 1970 are more likely to fail without warning — galvanized pipes corrode from the inside out, and the first sign of a problem is often water already spreading across the subfloor. When a pipe lets go in a two-story home, water migrates through ceiling cavities and wall assemblies faster than most people expect, reaching rooms that look completely dry on the surface.

Worthington's cold winters create freeze-thaw stress on exterior hose bibs, crawl space pipes, and any supply line running through an uninsulated exterior wall. A pipe that freezes and bursts on a January night can discharge hundreds of gallons before anyone notices.

## Our Water Damage Restoration Process in Worthington

The first priority when we arrive is stopping the source — shutting off the water supply, identifying the failure point, and containing the spread. From there, the process follows a clear sequence calibrated to what the structure actually needs.

**Extraction** comes first. Standing water gets removed with truck-mounted and portable extraction units. Water hiding under hardwood floors, inside wall cavities, or beneath vinyl plank gets located with thermal imaging and moisture meters — not guessed at.

**Structural drying** follows. We place industrial air movers and dehumidifiers based on the room's square footage, material types, and measured moisture readings. Concrete basement floors in Worthington homes often retain moisture longer than the walls above them, so drying plans account for that. We monitor readings daily and adjust equipment until materials reach acceptable moisture levels.

**Documentation** runs throughout. Every affected area is photographed, moisture readings are logged with timestamps, and the scope of work is written in a format that insurance adjusters can use directly. That paperwork matters — a well-documented claim moves faster and is less likely to result in a disputed line item.

Once the structure is dry, we handle repairs: replacing damaged drywall, insulation, flooring, and trim so the space is fully restored, not just dried out.
