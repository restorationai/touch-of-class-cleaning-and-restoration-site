## Why Luverne Properties See Water Damage

Luverne sits at the edge of the Coteau des Prairies, where the landscape offers almost no natural buffer against storm runoff. When a severe thunderstorm or spring snowmelt event hits, water finds the path of least resistance: window wells, foundation cracks, sump pits that overflow before the pump can keep pace.

The housing stock tells part of the story. Downtown Luverne and the surrounding residential blocks include a significant number of homes built before 1960, many with stone or brick foundations that were never designed with modern waterproofing membranes. Those foundations wick moisture laterally through the mortar joints, producing wall cavities that stay damp long after the visible water is gone. Older cast-iron and galvanized supply lines in these homes are also more prone to freeze-and-burst events during Rock County's hard winters, when overnight lows regularly drop below zero.

On the commercial side, the buildings near the Rock County Courthouse and along Main Street share the same aging infrastructure, and a single pipe failure above a drop ceiling can saturate insulation and framing before anyone notices.

## Our Water Cleanup Process in Luverne

When we arrive, the first priority is stopping ongoing water intrusion if it has not already been addressed. From there, the process follows a documented sequence calibrated to the conditions we typically find here.

**Moisture mapping.** We use thermal imaging cameras and calibrated moisture meters to trace water migration behind walls and under flooring. In older Luverne homes with plaster-and-lath construction, water can travel several feet from the visible wet area before it shows on the surface.

**Extraction.** Truck-mounted and portable extraction units pull standing water and saturated material from carpet, subfloor, and concrete. In finished basements, we work methodically to salvage flooring and framing where drying is still viable.

**Structural drying.** Commercial-grade desiccant dehumidifiers and high-velocity air movers are positioned according to the IICRC S500 standard. Drying logs are recorded daily so you and your insurance adjuster have a clear timeline of progress.

**Antimicrobial treatment.** Water damage that sits more than 24 to 48 hours creates conditions where microbial growth can begin. We apply EPA-registered antimicrobial agents to affected surfaces before closing up walls.

## Local Note

One thing worth knowing about older homes in and around Downtown Luverne: many of them were built with full-depth stone or poured-concrete basements that lack a drainage tile system entirely. When groundwater pressure builds after a heavy rain, water enters through the floor-wall joint rather than through a visible crack, and it can look like the floor is simply wet from humidity. That pattern is easy to misread. A moisture meter reading at the base of the wall and on the slab itself tells the real story, and it changes the drying plan significantly. Skipping that step and running dehumidifiers alone will not resolve the problem if the source is still active.
