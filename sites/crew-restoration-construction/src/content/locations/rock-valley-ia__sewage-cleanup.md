## Why Rock Valley Properties See Sewage Backup More Than You'd Expect

Rock Valley sits in Sioux County's flat glacial till plain, and the Rock River corridor cuts through some of the lowest ground in town. When the river swells — especially during the March–April snowmelt window that northwest Iowa sees almost every year — hydrostatic pressure builds against foundation walls and can overwhelm sewer lateral connections. Older homes in the Rock River area, many built before modern backflow prevention was standard, are especially vulnerable because their drain lines run at grades that made sense decades ago but leave little margin when the municipal system is already under stress.

Downtown Rock Valley's commercial and mixed-use blocks add another layer of complexity. ## Our Sewage Cleanup and Sanitization Process in Rock Valley

Raw sewage is classified as Category 3 — "black water" — by industry standards, meaning it carries pathogens, bacteria, and viruses that require more than a wet-vac and a mop. The process has to be methodical:

**Containment first.** Before extraction begins, affected areas are isolated with physical barriers to prevent cross-contamination to unaffected rooms. HVAC registers and returns are sealed if the system is in the impact zone.

**Extraction and removal.** Truck-mounted extraction equipment pulls standing sewage and saturated material. Porous materials — drywall, insulation, carpet — that have contacted black water are removed rather than dried in place. There is no safe way to fully sanitize saturated porous material after Category 3 exposure.

**Antimicrobial treatment.** Once the space is cleared, all hard surfaces — concrete, block, framing lumber — are treated with EPA-registered antimicrobial agents. This step addresses pathogens that extraction alone cannot eliminate.

**Drying and documentation.** Commercial desiccant dehumidifiers and air movers run until moisture readings in structural materials return to pre-loss baselines. Every reading is logged, which matters when you file an insurance claim.

**Clearance and reconstruction.** Once the space passes inspection, Crew's construction side can handle the rebuild — new drywall, insulation, flooring — so you're not coordinating a second contractor.

## Local Note: What Rock Valley's Soil Conditions Mean for Drying Timelines

The heavy clay soils common throughout Sioux County hold moisture longer than the sandy or loam soils found in other parts of Iowa. In a basement sewage event, that matters because concrete block foundations in clay-heavy soil can wick ground moisture back into the structure even after the interior has been extracted and treated. We account for this by running drying equipment longer than the standard baseline and taking daily moisture readings from both the interior face and, where accessible, the exterior-facing side of foundation walls. Skipping that step — or pulling equipment too early — is one of the most common reasons a remediated basement develops a mold problem two weeks after cleanup.
