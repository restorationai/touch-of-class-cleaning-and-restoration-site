## Why Hartford Properties See Water Damage Issues

Hartford's growth over the past decade has produced a housing stock that spans two very different eras. Older homes near Downtown Hartford were often built on shallow crawl spaces or full basements that weren't engineered for today's drainage loads. When the clay-heavy soils around this part of Minnehaha County become saturated — which can happen fast after a rapid snowmelt — hydrostatic pressure pushes groundwater through foundation cracks and floor-wall joints before a sump pump can keep pace.

Newer construction on the western and southern edges of town presents a different problem. Homes built quickly during Hartford's expansion sometimes have HVAC penetrations and vapor barrier gaps that allow moisture to accumulate inside wall assemblies long before it's visible on the surface. A slow drip behind drywall in a home that's only five years old can look like nothing for weeks, then present as a full-wall mold colony by the time the smell becomes noticeable.

In the Buffalo Ridge area, where lots tend to run larger and homes sit farther from the street, drainage grades occasionally direct surface runoff toward the foundation rather than away from it — a grading issue that makes water intrusion events more likely during heavy rain and accelerates the damage once water finds a way in.

## Our Water Damage Restoration Process in Hartford

The first step on any water loss is moisture mapping — not a visual scan, but thermal imaging and calibrated moisture meters that trace exactly where water has traveled inside wall cavities, under flooring, and into structural framing. Water follows the path of least resistance, and in a slab-on-grade home it can travel 20 feet from the source before pooling somewhere visible.

Once the full extent of the damage is documented, standing water is extracted using truck-mounted or portable extraction units. After bulk water is removed, the drying phase begins: commercial-grade desiccant or refrigerant dehumidifiers and high-velocity air movers are positioned based on a drying plan calibrated to the material types and square footage involved. Drying logs are recorded daily — not just for quality control, but because your insurance adjuster will need that data to process the claim.

Affected materials that cannot be dried in place — saturated insulation, buckled hardwood flooring, Category 3 drywall — are removed, documented, and disposed of properly. Structural framing is dried to manufacturer-specified moisture content before any reconstruction begins. Crew Restoration & Construction also handles the rebuild side, so there's no gap between the mitigation crew leaving and the contractor arriving.
