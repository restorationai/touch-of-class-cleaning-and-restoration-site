## Our Biohazard Cleanup Process in Worthington

Every job begins with a contained assessment — we identify the full scope of affected materials before any removal begins. For properties with older construction, that assessment includes checking subfloor and wall cavity penetration, not just the visible surface.

Once the scope is established, the area is isolated using physical containment barriers and negative air pressure where the structure allows. All biological material, contaminated porous materials, and sharps are collected, packaged, and transported according to Minnesota Department of Health and federal OSHA standards for regulated medical and biohazardous waste. We do not leave disposal to chance or to the property owner.

After removal, affected surfaces are treated with EPA-registered hospital-grade disinfectants. We then verify that odor-causing compounds have been neutralized — not masked — before clearing the space. If structural materials such as subfloor sections or drywall panels require removal, we document that clearly for insurance purposes and coordinate reconstruction through the same crew so the property owner is not managing two separate contractors.
