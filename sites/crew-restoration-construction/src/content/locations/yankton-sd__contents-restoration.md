When floodwater backs up from the Missouri River corridor or a burst pipe soaks a century-old Riverside bungalow, the damage to your furniture, clothing, documents, and keepsakes can feel just as devastating as the structural loss. Contents restoration is the part of recovery most homeowners don't think about until they're standing in a wet living room wondering whether their grandmother's armoire can be saved. Crew Restoration & Construction handles the full cycle — careful pack-out, professional cleaning, climate-controlled storage, and coordinated return — so your belongings get the same attention as your walls and floors.

## Why Yankton Homes See Recurring Contents Damage

Yankton's position along the Missouri River puts a specific category of properties at persistent risk. Lakeside homes near Gavins Point Dam and the Lewis and Clark Lake shoreline face seasonal water-table fluctuations that can push moisture into basements and crawl spaces even in years without a major flood event. When that moisture travels, it doesn't stop at the subfloor — it wicks into stored furniture, cardboard boxes, fabric, and paper within 24 to 48 hours.

The older housing stock in Downtown Yankton compounds the problem. Many homes in the 57078 ZIP code were built before 1960, which means wood-framed interiors, plaster-and-lath walls, and furniture that predates modern synthetic materials. Antique wood absorbs odor and smoke residue differently than particleboard or MDF, and it requires gentler, slower drying to avoid warping and cracking. Fire and smoke losses in these homes also tend to leave a heavier residue because older construction materials — horsehair plaster, original pine framing — burn with a dense, oily smoke that penetrates fabric and upholstery deeply.

## Our Contents Restoration and Storage Process in Yankton

The first step on any loss site is a room-by-room contents inventory. Every item gets photographed, logged by condition, and tagged before it leaves your property. That documentation becomes the foundation of your insurance claim and protects both you and the carrier from disputes about pre-loss value.

From there, the process branches based on what each item needs:

- **Pack-out and transport**: Items too damaged or at-risk to stay on-site are wrapped, crated, and moved to a secure, climate-controlled storage facility. For Yankton properties, that means protecting contents from the humidity swings that come with Missouri River valley summers — warm, wet air accelerates mold colonization on fabric and paper if items sit in an uncontrolled environment even for a few days.
- **On-site or off-site cleaning**: Smoke-affected upholstery, hard goods, and electronics each require different cleaning chemistry and technique. Ultrasonic cleaning works well for small hard items — jewelry, figurines, tools — while ozone or hydroxyl treatment addresses odor in fabric and soft goods without the heat that damages antiques.
- **Drying and dehumidification**: Wet documents, books, and photographs go into controlled freeze-drying or air-drying protocols depending on saturation level. Acting within the first 48 hours is the difference between salvageable and a total loss.
- **Coordinated return**: Once your structure is cleared, contents are returned, unwrapped, and placed per your direction. We don't consider the job done until the last box is off the truck.

Homes within a few blocks of the Missouri River floodplain — particularly in the Riverside area — tend to have a moisture baseline that's already elevated before a loss event occurs.
