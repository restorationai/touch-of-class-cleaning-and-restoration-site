## Why Fulda Homes Demand a Different Renovation Approach

Murray County's climate is not gentle on building materials. Winters routinely push below zero, and the ground heaves as frost penetrates three to four feet deep in a cold year. Older homes in and around Downtown Fulda — many built in the early to mid-1900s — were constructed with materials and techniques that made sense then but create complications now. Original plaster walls, knob-and-tube wiring tucked behind lath, and single-pane windows are common finds once a renovation gets underway. Lakeside Fulda properties face an additional layer of complexity: proximity to the water means higher ambient humidity, which accelerates wood rot in sills, subflooring, and rim joists if ventilation hasn't kept pace with the home's age.

None of that is a reason to put off a remodel — it's a reason to work with a contractor who expects those discoveries rather than being surprised by them. Scoping a kitchen remodel or bathroom remodel in a home like this means budgeting realistically for what's behind the walls, not just what's visible from the showroom floor.

## Local Note: What Working Near the Lakes Actually Means for a Remodel

Homes within a few blocks of Seven Mile Lake and Fulda Lakes tend to carry measurably higher interior humidity readings than properties further inland — even in winter, when the lakes are frozen. That matters when you're installing hardwood flooring, hanging new drywall, or painting finish coats. Wood flooring installed without adequate acclimation time in a lakeside home can cup or gap within a season. We account for this by acclimating materials on-site before installation and, when conditions warrant, running dehumidification during the finish phase. It's a small step that prevents a callback six months later.

If your renovation includes a bathroom remodel or laundry addition, we also pay close attention to exhaust ventilation sizing — undersized fans in a high-humidity environment are a mold problem waiting to happen.
