South Dakota winters are no joke — and neither is what a burst pipe or kitchen fire leaves behind in a Sioux Falls home. When freezing temps crack a supply line or a grease fire scorches the cabinets in a Cathedral Historic District bungalow, the damage rarely stops at the walls. Furniture, clothing, electronics, family keepsakes — everything in the path of smoke, soot, or standing water needs to be evaluated, carefully packed out, professionally cleaned, and stored somewhere safe while your home is being rebuilt. That's exactly what contents restoration and storage is built to do.

## Why Sioux Falls Homes Face Unique Contents Damage Challenges

Sioux Falls sits in a climate band that swings from -20°F wind chills in January to humid 90°F summers in July. That range creates two distinct seasons of contents risk. Winter brings frozen pipes — especially in older homes in McKennan Park and the All Saints neighborhood, where cast-iron supply lines and minimal insulation in crawl spaces are still common. A pipe that lets go overnight can dump hundreds of gallons before anyone notices, soaking upholstered furniture, hardwood floors, and stored items in closets and basements.

Summer brings a different threat: severe thunderstorms that push water through foundation walls and window seals faster than sump pumps can keep up. The clay-heavy soil across much of the metro drains slowly, which means a heavy rain event can saturate a finished basement — and everything in it — for hours before the ground absorbs the excess. Smoke and fire damage compounds the problem because soot from a structure fire doesn't stay in one room; it migrates through HVAC systems and settles on fabrics, artwork, and electronics throughout the house.

## Our Contents Pack-Out and Restoration Process

Before any cleaning begins, the team does a room-by-room contents inventory — photographing, barcoding, and cataloging every item. This documentation matters for your insurance claim and gives you a clear record of what was recovered and what condition it was in when it left your home.

Items that can be restored are transported to a secure, climate-controlled storage facility. Soft goods like clothing, bedding, and upholstery go through specialized laundering and ozone or hydroxyl treatment to eliminate smoke odor at the molecular level — not just mask it. Hard surfaces — furniture, cabinetry, artwork, electronics — are cleaned using methods matched to the material. A smoke-damaged oil painting requires a completely different approach than a soot-covered refrigerator. Porous items that have absorbed contaminated water are assessed individually; some can be dried and sanitized, others cannot, and we'll be honest about which is which.

Once your home is cleared for re-occupancy, contents are returned, room by room, to their documented positions. The pack-out-to-return process is designed so that nothing gets lost in the shuffle — a real risk when dozens of boxes move in and out of a job site over several weeks.

## Working with Insurance on Contents Claims in Sioux Falls

Most homeowner policies in South Dakota cover contents losses under the same claim as structural damage, but the documentation requirements are specific. Carriers want itemized lists, pre-loss values, and photographic evidence — not a general statement that "everything in the basement got wet." The room-by-room inventory completed at pack-out is built to satisfy those requirements. Line-item documentation goes directly to your adjuster, which shortens the back-and-forth and helps move your claim forward.

If your policy includes additional living expenses (ALE), contents storage costs are often reimbursable while your home is under repair. Ask your adjuster about that coverage early — it can make a meaningful difference in out-of-pocket costs for families displaced from homes in neighborhoods like Prairie Green or Whittier, where rebuild timelines can stretch several weeks depending on contractor scheduling and permit processing through the city.

## Local Note

Homes in the Cathedral Historic District and surrounding blocks of central Sioux Falls (ZIP 57104) were built primarily between 1900 and 1940, and many still have original plaster-and-lath walls. Plaster absorbs airborne soot differently than modern drywall — the texture traps fine particles in a way that standard dry-sponge wiping misses. When we're packing out contents from these older homes after a fire, we flag any plaster-surface items like built-in shelving or decorative molding that may have transferred soot onto adjacent furniture, so the cleaning crew knows to treat those pieces with a finer-grade chemical sponge before moving to wet cleaning methods. It's a small detail that prevents re-soiling finished items after they've already been cleaned.
