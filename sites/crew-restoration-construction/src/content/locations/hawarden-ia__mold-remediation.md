## Why Hawarden Properties See Mold Issues

Hawarden sits in the Big Sioux River floodplain, and even properties well above the waterline feel the effects of a high water table and saturated soils after heavy rain. Crawl spaces and unfinished basements — common in the older bungalows and two-story homes that line the streets near Downtown Hawarden — are especially vulnerable. When ground moisture wicks through block or poured-concrete foundations, it creates the persistent humidity that mold needs to colonize insulation, floor joists, and stored belongings.

Northwest Iowa also sees dramatic seasonal swings. Winter condensation forms on cold exterior walls and rim joists; spring thaws push groundwater against foundations that have been contracting and expanding all season. Homes built before 1970, which make up a significant share of Hawarden's housing stock, were often constructed without modern vapor barriers or drainage membranes. That combination — older building materials, high seasonal moisture, and river-adjacent soils — means mold problems here tend to be structural rather than surface-level.

## Our Mold Remediation Process in Hawarden

Every job starts with a thorough visual inspection and moisture mapping. We use thermal imaging cameras and pin-type moisture meters to trace water intrusion back to its source, because removing visible mold without addressing the underlying moisture guarantees a return visit.

Once we understand the scope, we establish negative-air containment — sealing off the affected area with poly sheeting and running HEPA-filtered air scrubbers to prevent cross-contamination to the rest of the home. This step matters especially in older Hawarden properties where open floor plans or unfinished basements connect directly to living areas.

Remediation itself involves removing unsalvageable porous materials (drywall, insulation, carpet padding), HEPA vacuuming exposed framing, and applying an EPA-registered antimicrobial treatment to cleaned surfaces. We then run commercial dehumidifiers and air movers until moisture readings return to acceptable levels. Before we close up walls, we document final readings so you have a written record — useful for insurance claims and for any future buyer inspections.
