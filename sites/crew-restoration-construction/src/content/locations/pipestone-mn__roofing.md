## Why Pipestone Properties See Roofing Wear Faster Than You'd Expect

The same Sioux Quartzite that makes Pipestone's Downtown Historic District architecturally striking also tells you something about the local environment: this is hard-rock country with flat, wind-exposed terrain. There are no hills or tree lines to buffer the sustained northwest winds that accelerate shingle granule loss and drive ice dams along eave edges. Asphalt shingles rated for 25 years in a sheltered suburban climate can show meaningful wear in 15 to 18 years on a fully exposed Pipestone property.

The housing stock compounds the challenge. A large share of Pipestone's residential blocks — particularly in the Hiawatha area — includes homes built between the 1920s and 1960s. Those structures often have lower-pitch rooflines, original wood decking underneath layers of re-roofing, and minimal ventilation designed for a time when attic airflow wasn't a code consideration. When we pull old material on a job like that, we routinely find soft decking, deteriorated underlayment, and ridge vents that were never installed. Replacing the surface without addressing what's underneath is a short-term fix on a long-term problem.

## Our Roofing Installation and Replacement Process in Pipestone

Every project starts with a full deck inspection — not a photo from the ground, but a physical assessment of sheathing condition, fastener pull-through, and existing ventilation. On older Pipestone homes, that inspection frequently changes the material order before we ever start tearing off shingles.

Once the scope is confirmed, we strip to bare decking, replace any soft or delaminated panels, and install a self-adhering ice-and-water barrier across the full eave and valley sections. In a climate where ice dams form reliably every winter, running that membrane only 36 inches up from the eave — the minimum in some interpretations of code — is not enough. We extend it to the interior wall line as a standard practice here.

Drip edge, underlayment, and starter course go down before a single field shingle is placed. Ridge ventilation is sized to match the soffit intake so the attic breathes as a system rather than trapping heat that accelerates shingle aging from the underside. Final walkthrough includes a debris sweep and a gutter check, because a clogged gutter on a new roof is a fast path back to ice dam problems.
