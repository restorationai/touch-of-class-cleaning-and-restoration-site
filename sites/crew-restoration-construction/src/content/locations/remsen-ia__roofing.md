## Why Remsen Roofs Wear Out Faster Than You'd Expect

Northwest Iowa's climate is genuinely hard on roofing materials. Remsen sits in a corridor that sees freeze-thaw cycling from October through March — temperatures can swing 30 degrees in a single day, and that repeated expansion and contraction works shingle tabs loose, cracks ridge caps, and opens up the nail-line seams that hold a roof together. Add in the wind exposure that comes with flat agricultural terrain (there are no hills or tree lines to slow a storm down once it crosses the Missouri River corridor), and asphalt shingles here face more mechanical stress per year than the same product would in a sheltered suburban neighborhood.

The housing stock compounds the challenge. Many homes near Downtown Remsen and the blocks surrounding St. Mary's Catholic Church were built between the 1890s and the 1950s. Older roof decks are often 1×6 skip sheathing or diagonal board sheathing rather than modern OSB — structurally sound in many cases, but it changes how new shingles lay and how underlayment must be installed. A contractor unfamiliar with that construction can install a technically correct roof that still fails early because the deck prep wasn't right for the substrate underneath.

## Our Roofing Installation and Replacement Process in Remsen

Every project starts with a thorough roof inspection — not just the shingles, but the deck, the fascia, the soffit vents, and the flashing around any penetrations (chimneys, plumbing stacks, satellite mounts). We photograph everything before a single nail comes out, which matters both for your records and for any insurance documentation.

Once the scope is confirmed, we pull the required permit through Plymouth County and schedule the tear-off. On older homes with board sheathing, we assess each section of deck as it's exposed — soft spots, rot, or delamination get addressed before new underlayment goes down. We install synthetic underlayment rather than felt on most Remsen jobs because it handles the moisture that comes with Iowa's spring snowmelt season better than traditional #30 felt, which can wrinkle and buckle when it gets wet during a multi-day installation.

Shingle selection is a real conversation, not a catalog flip. For Remsen's wind exposure, we typically recommend impact-resistant architectural shingles rated for at least 110 mph wind uplift — the same wind that makes Oktoberfest weekend feel brisk can strip a standard 3-tab shingle in a severe storm. We walk through color, warranty, and manufacturer options with you before ordering materials.

Final inspection includes ridge ventilation confirmation, a full perimeter check of all flashing, and a debris sweep of the yard and gutters.
