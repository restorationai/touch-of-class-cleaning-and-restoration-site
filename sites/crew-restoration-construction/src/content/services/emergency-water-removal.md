## What 24/7 Emergency Water Removal actually involves

Water doesn't wait for business hours, and neither does the damage it causes. Category 1 water from a supply line can shift to Category 2 within 24 to 48 hours as it picks up contaminants from flooring, drywall, and subfloor materials. In a Sioux Falls basement, that window closes even faster once water finds its way into block wall cavities or under engineered hardwood. Emergency water removal means extraction equipment, moisture meters, and drying gear arriving fast enough to catch the loss before it reclassifies into a more expensive, more invasive job.

The work itself starts with truck-mounted or portable extraction units pulling standing water off floors and out of carpet pad, followed by thermal imaging and penetrating moisture meters to map exactly how far water has traveled into walls, subfloor, and framing. From there it's air movers, low-grain-refrigerant dehumidifiers, and daily moisture readings until the structure reads dry, not just to the touch, but on the meter. A straightforward one-room loss might dry in three to five days. A multi-room loss with wet insulation or hardwood flooring can run longer, and the drying log is what documents that timeline for your insurance file.

## Our process

1. **Extraction first.** Standing water gets pulled immediately with extraction units matched to the volume and surface type, carpet, tile, hardwood, or concrete.
2. **Moisture mapping.** Meters and thermal imaging identify wet zones that aren't visible on the surface, including behind baseboards, under cabinet toe kicks, and inside wall cavities.
3. **Containment and category classification.** Water is classified by source and contamination level (clean supply line versus sewage backup, for example), which determines what materials can be dried in place versus removed.
4. **Structural drying with daily monitoring.** Air movers and dehumidifiers are placed based on the psychrometric readings, not guesswork, and equipment placement is adjusted as readings change.
5. **Verification before equipment leaves.** Materials are re-tested against dry standard benchmarks before drying equipment is pulled, so the job doesn't get called finished early.

## What separates a good water removal response from a bad one

The most common mistake isn't missed water, it's water that's found but not measured. A crew that pulls visible standing water and sets a few fans without documenting moisture content at multiple depths is guessing at when the job is actually dry. That guess either leaves hidden moisture behind drywall (which shows up as mold or a musty smell weeks later) or keeps equipment running, and billing, longer than necessary.

Adjusters reviewing a water claim look for a daily drying log with moisture readings, photos of the affected areas before demolition of any wet materials, and a clear record of what was Category 1, 2, or 3 water and why. Structural drying performed and documented per the IICRC S500 standard gives both the homeowner and the carrier a consistent reference point, rather than a subjective call about whether something is "dry enough."

## Seasonal & regional considerations

Sioux Falls winters bring freeze-thaw cycles that crack aging supply lines and put stress on sump pumps right when they're needed most during spring thaw. Older neighborhoods with clay or cast iron plumbing see more supply line failures as pipes age past their expected service life. Heavy spring rain and rapid snowmelt also push groundwater against foundation walls, which is why basement moisture calls tend to cluster in March and April here.
