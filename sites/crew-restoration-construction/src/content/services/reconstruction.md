When the drying equipment is gone and the mitigation crew has packed up, most homeowners are left staring at open stud walls, a bare subfloor, or a kitchen missing half its cabinets. That gap between demolition and a livable home again is where reconstruction services come in. This is the rebuild phase: framing, drywall, flooring, trim, and finish work that turns a gutted room back into a room, done to code and matched to what an insurance adjuster expects to see on the estimate.

## What Reconstruction Services actually involves

Post-damage reconstruction covers everything from a single wall of drywall and paint to a full structural rebuild after a fire burned through joists or a sewer backup rotted out a subfloor. The scope depends entirely on what the demolition left behind. A small bathroom job might mean new subfloor, tile, and fixtures over two or three weeks. A fire damage reconstruction involving load-bearing framing, electrical rough-in, and insulation can run several months once permitting and inspections are factored in. Crew Restoration & Construction, license #3814, handles both ends of that range, coordinating the trades, materials, and inspections so the rebuild lines up with the original scope of loss.

## Our process

1. **Scope of work.** We document the damaged structure room by room and build a detailed scope that mirrors the line items an insurance adjuster will review, so there's no guesswork about what's being rebuilt and why.
2. **Permitting.** Structural, electrical, and plumbing work in Sioux Falls requires permits through the city's building services division. We pull those before framing starts, not after, which avoids failed inspections later.
3. **Framing and rough-in.** This is where structural reconstruction happens: replacing damaged joists or studs, rebuilding subfloor, coordinating rough electrical and plumbing behind the walls before they close up.
4. **Insulation and drywall.** Once rough-ins pass inspection, we insulate to current code, hang and finish drywall, and prime.
5. **Finish work and walkthrough.** Trim, flooring, paint, fixtures, and a final punch list walkthrough with the homeowner before we call the job closed.

## What separates a good reconstruction response from a bad one

The most common mistake in post-disaster rebuilding is starting the finish work before the structure underneath is verified dry and structurally sound. Drywall hung over framing that hasn't been moisture-tested can trap conditions for mold to take hold behind a wall that looks perfectly fine. Another frequent problem: contractors who skip permits on load-bearing repairs to save time, which creates real issues when the home is sold and the buyer's inspector finds unpermitted structural work. A careful reconstruction crew also keeps material selections, quantities, and labor hours documented in a way that matches the insurance carrier's estimate line by line, because mismatched paperwork is the single biggest reason supplements get delayed or denied. Adjusters are looking for consistency between what was scoped, what was built, and what's on the invoice.

## What does Reconstruction Services cost?

Reconstruction costs vary widely because the scope is dictated by what was destroyed, not by a flat rate. A single room needing drywall and paint runs very differently than a kitchen rebuild after a fire that reached the framing. These figures are typical industry ranges, not a quote: every property and every loss is different, and Crew Restoration & Construction provides a written scope of work before any reconstruction begins.

Most homeowners insurance policies cover reconstruction costs tied to a covered peril, such as fire or a sudden pipe failure, though coverage details and depreciation schedules vary by policy and carrier.

## Seasonal & regional considerations

Sioux Falls winters bring hard freezes that affect both scheduling and materials. Framing and roofing work slow down once temperatures drop, and concrete or masonry repairs often need to wait for a thaw window or temporary heat and enclosure. Permitting timelines can also stretch during the city's busier building seasons in spring and summer, which is worth planning around if a reconstruction project is tied to an insurance claim with a deductible clock running. Material lead times, especially for cabinetry and specialty flooring, tend to run longer in winter months as well.
