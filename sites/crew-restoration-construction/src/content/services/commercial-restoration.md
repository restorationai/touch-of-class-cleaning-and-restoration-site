When a sprinkler head fails over a retail sales floor at 2 a.m., or a rooftop unit backs up and sends water through a drop ceiling into three tenant suites, the math is different than it is at a house. You're not just drying out drywall, you're managing lost revenue, lease obligations, employee safety, and a building that may need to stay partially open while the work happens. Commercial restoration in Sioux Falls means treating the loss and the business operation as two problems that have to be solved together.

## What Commercial Restoration actually involves

Commercial losses tend to be larger in square footage and more complicated in construction than residential ones. You're dealing with suspended ceiling grids, VCT or carpet tile over concrete slabs, HVAC systems that can spread contamination between suites, fire suppression systems, and sometimes multiple tenants or a property management company all needing updates at once. Equipment scales up accordingly: commercial-grade desiccant dehumidifiers, larger axial air movers positioned across open floor plans, and moisture monitoring across multiple zones instead of a single room. Timelines vary widely depending on the extent of the loss and whether the space needs to stay occupied during the work, but the structural drying itself typically follows the same IICRC S500 principles used on any water loss, scaled to the footprint.

Fire and smoke losses in commercial spaces bring their own complications, since retail merchandise, office electronics, and server equipment often need separate handling from the building structure itself. Soot type, HVAC contamination, and whether the fire involved synthetic materials all affect how the cleanup is scoped.

## Our process

1. **Loss assessment and business impact triage.** We walk the space with whoever is authorized to make decisions, whether that's a facilities manager, tenant, or building owner, and identify which areas are safe to occupy, which need to close, and which trades are involved beyond water or fire mitigation.
2. **Containment and phased scheduling.** In multi-tenant buildings or spaces that need to stay partially open, we section off the affected area and sequence the work so unaffected parts of the business can keep operating where it's safe to do so.
3. **Mitigation and structural drying.** Extraction, commercial dehumidification, and air movement go in immediately, with moisture readings logged across every affected material, including under flooring assemblies where trapped water is easy to miss.
4. **Trade coordination for rebuild.** Electrical, mechanical, drywall, flooring, and ceiling grid work gets scheduled and documented to meet code for occupied commercial space, not just residential standards.
5. **Final walkthrough and documentation.** We provide moisture logs, photos, and a written scope of the completed work for insurance, property management, or tenant sign-off.

## What separates a good commercial restoration response from a bad one

The most common mistake on commercial jobs is treating them like an oversized residential loss. A crew that doesn't check under VCT or carpet tile for trapped moisture, or that skips HVAC inspection after a fire, will leave conditions that surface again weeks later as odor, delamination, or mold. Adjusters reviewing commercial claims typically look for moisture logs across every affected zone, clear before-and-after documentation, and a scope that separates structural mitigation from contents or business personal property. Good operators also flag life-safety and code issues early, like compromised fire-rated assemblies or damaged ceiling tiles in exit corridors, instead of leaving them for a later inspection to catch. Miscommunication between multiple decision-makers, a property manager, a tenant, and an insurance adjuster, is another place commercial jobs go sideways if documentation isn't kept clear from day one.

## Seasonal & regional considerations

Sioux Falls winters bring freeze-thaw cycles that stress commercial roofing, parapet walls, and sprinkler lines, and a frozen or burst line above a retail or office space can cause damage that spreads across several tenant suites before it's noticed. Spring and early summer storms bring hail and wind that test flat commercial roofing systems, and summer humidity gives mold a window to establish itself in buildings where a water loss wasn't fully dried. Scheduling restoration work around business hours or off-peak periods is often part of minimizing disruption for commercial clients here.
