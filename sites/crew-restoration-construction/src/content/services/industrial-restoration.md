When a burst supply line floods a manufacturing floor overnight, or a compressor fire fills a warehouse with soot before the first shift arrives, the math is different than it is in a house. You're not just drying carpet and drywall. You're racing against corrosion on exposed steel, contamination risk on production equipment, and every hour a line sits idle. Industrial restoration in Sioux Falls means moving fast on large volumes of space while keeping the parts of the facility that weren't affected running.

## What Industrial Restoration actually involves

Industrial and plant restoration covers water, fire, and smoke losses in warehouses, manufacturing facilities, distribution centers, and processing plants, spaces that are typically measured in tens of thousands of square feet rather than rooms. The work involves high-capacity extraction and desiccant dehumidification equipment sized for open floor plans and high ceilings, moisture mapping across concrete slabs and structural steel, and coordination with facility maintenance staff who know where the electrical panels, HVAC returns, and process piping run. A single event, a sprinkler head discharge in a storage bay, a roof leak over a production line, a fire in an electrical room, can affect multiple building systems at once, so the scope usually spans structural drying, equipment protection, and often reconstruction of affected areas before the space is back in full use.

## Our process

1. **Facility assessment and operational triage.** We walk the affected area with facility management to map the loss, identify what's structural versus contents, and flag anything that threatens continued operations, live electrical near standing water, compromised racking, or smoke intrusion into HVAC returns.
2. **Large-scale extraction and containment.** Standing water gets pulled with truck-mount and high-volume extraction equipment sized for open industrial floors, and we set containment barriers to keep unaffected production zones running during the work.
3. **Structural drying with monitoring.** Desiccant dehumidifiers and directed air movement dry concrete slabs, steel framing, and building envelope assemblies, with moisture readings logged against the IICRC S500 standard until materials reach dry targets, not just until surfaces feel dry to the touch.
4. **Equipment and asset protection.** Exposed machinery, electrical panels, and inventory get assessed for corrosion risk, moisture wicking, or soot deposition, and we coordinate with your equipment vendors or insurance adjuster on anything that needs specialized cleaning or replacement.
5. **Reconstruction and return to service.** Damaged flooring, wall assemblies, insulation, or ceiling systems are rebuilt to match code and facility use, with a final walkthrough before the space goes back into full production.

## What separates a good industrial restoration response from a bad one

The most common mistake on industrial losses is treating a 40,000 square foot warehouse like a scaled-up version of a residential job, undersized equipment that takes days longer than it should, or drying to "looks dry" instead of documented moisture content in concrete and steel. Concrete slabs hold moisture far longer than drywall, and steel framing that reads dry on the surface can still be sweating underneath insulation, which is exactly the kind of gap that leads to hidden corrosion or mold months later. Adjusters reviewing industrial claims want to see moisture logs, equipment placement records, and a clear timeline tying the drying process to a recognized standard, not just a final invoice. A response that documents thoroughly and communicates with facility staff about what's safe to reoccupy, and what isn't yet, tends to close out faster and with fewer disputes.

## Seasonal & regional considerations

Sioux Falls winters bring long stretches below freezing, and unheated sections of warehouses and loading docks are where frozen and burst pipes tend to show up first. Facilities along the I-90 and I-29 corridors, where much of the area's warehouse and distribution space sits, often have large unconditioned zones that need extra attention during a freeze event. Spring thaw and heavy rain can also push groundwater and surface water into loading dock pits and lower-level storage, so drainage around the building envelope is worth checking before the ground thaws each year.
