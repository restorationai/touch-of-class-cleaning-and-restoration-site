After a fire, flood, or sewage backup, the structure gets most of the attention. But the belongings inside, furniture, clothing, documents, electronics, family photos, absorb smoke, soot, and moisture just as aggressively as drywall does. The difference is that contents can often be cleaned and returned to pre-loss condition if they are handled quickly and correctly. Left in a damaged structure too long, or packed out without proper documentation, those same items become a total loss on your claim and a permanent loss to your household.

## What contents restoration and storage actually involves

Contents restoration is the process of inventorying, removing, cleaning, deodorizing, and storing your personal property during a structural restoration. It is not moving your furniture to the garage. It is a documented, methodical process that tracks every item from pack-out through cleaning to final return.

Depending on the type of loss, contents work may involve ultrasonic cleaning tanks that use high-frequency sound waves to lift soot and residue from hard surfaces without abrasion, ozone chambers for smoke odor embedded in soft goods, dry-cleaning coordination for textiles, and freeze-drying for water-saturated documents and photographs. Electronics require a separate evaluation for corrosion and short-circuit risk before they are powered on again.

Storage happens in a climate-controlled facility, not a warehouse with a roll-up door. Temperature and humidity swings during storage can cause secondary damage to wood furniture, leather, and electronics, damage that did not exist at pack-out and complicates your claim later.

Timeline depends on the scope of the structural work. A straightforward water loss with two to three weeks of drying and repair may have contents back in place within a month. A fire with extensive smoke penetration and reconstruction can extend that to several months. Either way, your items are inventoried, photographed, and tracked throughout.

## Our process

1. **Pre-pack-out inventory and documentation.** Before anything moves, every item in the affected area is photographed and logged. This inventory becomes part of your insurance claim file. Items that are clearly non-salvageable are documented separately so your adjuster can process them as a total loss without dispute.

2. **Triage and sorting.** Contents are sorted on-site into three categories: items that can be cleaned and restored, items that require specialist treatment (documents, electronics, fine art, textiles), and items that are beyond restoration. Sorting before transport prevents cross-contamination and keeps cleaning costs from ballooning unnecessarily.

3. **Pack-out and secure transport.** Salvageable items are packed using appropriate materials for each category and transported to a climate-controlled storage facility. Fragile items, electronics, and high-value goods are packed separately and noted in the chain-of-custody log.

4. **Cleaning and deodorization.** Depending on the loss type, cleaning methods include ultrasonic tanks for hard goods, ozone or hydroxyl treatment for smoke odor, HEPA vacuuming for soot-covered surfaces, and hand-cleaning for delicate items. Textiles go through dry-cleaning or specialty laundering. The goal is pre-loss condition, not just visibly clean.

5. **Return and re-installation.** Once structural work is complete and the space passes moisture and air quality checks, contents are returned, unpacked, and placed according to a room-by-room diagram created at pack-out. Nothing is guessed at from memory.

## What separates a good contents response from a bad one

The most common failure in contents restoration is the absence of a proper inventory. When items are packed out without documentation, the homeowner and the insurance adjuster are working from memory when it comes time to settle the claim. Missing or damaged items become disputes instead of line items.

A second common problem is treating all contents the same. Smoke damage from a kitchen grease fire leaves a wet, protein-based residue that smears if wiped with standard cleaners. Synthetic smoke from burning plastics leaves a different chemical residue that requires different solvents. Using the wrong cleaning method on the wrong soot type can permanently set the stain rather than remove it.

Insurance adjusters look for a documented chain of custody from pack-out to return, itemized cleaning costs per category, and a clear separation between restored items and total-loss items. Adjusters also look for whether storage costs are reasonable and whether the facility is genuinely climate-controlled. A claim with a complete, photographed inventory moves faster and pays out more accurately than one reconstructed after the fact.

IICRC-trained technicians follow established protocols for contents evaluation and cleaning, which gives adjusters a recognized framework to work from.

## Seasonal and regional considerations

Sioux Falls winters create a specific contents risk that warmer climates do not see as often. When a pipe bursts during a cold snap and the home loses heat, contents can freeze before crews arrive. Frozen upholstered furniture and electronics require a controlled thaw before cleaning begins. Rushing that process causes additional damage. Summer humidity in the region also matters during storage: a facility that is not actively humidity-controlled can allow mold to colonize soft goods within days, turning a restorable item into a loss.
