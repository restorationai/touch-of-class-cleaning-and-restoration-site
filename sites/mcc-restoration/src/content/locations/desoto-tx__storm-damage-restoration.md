## Why DeSoto Properties See Elevated Storm Damage

DeSoto's housing stock is a big part of the story. The large-lot brick homes built throughout Thorntree and Windmill Hill between the mid-1970s and early 1990s were constructed before modern wind-load roofing standards became common in North Texas. That means original decking, aging felt underlayment, and in many cases the first or second generation of shingles since the home was built. When a hailstorm like the April 2022 event hammers the area — baseball-size stones were documented in parts of the Best Southwest corridor — those older roof assemblies absorb far more damage than they appear to from the street.

The expansive clay soil that runs under most of DeSoto compounds the problem. Heavy rain that follows a storm saturates the ground fast, and clay that's already swollen from spring moisture has nowhere to put the water. It migrates toward foundations, into crawl spaces, and through any gap that impact damage opened up. ## Our Storm Damage Restoration Process in DeSoto

Every job starts with a systematic exterior and interior inspection — not a quick visual from the driveway. We document impact damage to roofing, siding, gutters, soffits, and windows with photographs and written notes calibrated for insurance submission. If a tree strike has opened the structure, we deploy emergency tarping or board-up the same visit to stop additional moisture from entering.

Once the structure is secured, we assess interior water intrusion. In DeSoto's older brick homes, water that enters through a compromised roof or a broken window often travels inside wall cavities before it shows up as a ceiling stain. We use moisture meters and thermal imaging to trace the actual migration path rather than guessing at the boundary of wet material. Affected drywall, insulation, and wood framing are removed to the dry line, and industrial drying equipment runs until readings confirm the structure is back to acceptable moisture levels. Reconstruction — patching, reframing, reroofing, painting — follows once the building envelope is dry and stable.
