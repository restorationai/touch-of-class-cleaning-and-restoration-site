## Why Duncanville Homes Are Particularly Vulnerable to Smoke Damage

The Blackland Prairie clay beneath Duncanville is relentless. It expands and contracts with every wet-dry cycle, and that seasonal movement gradually opens hairline cracks in slab foundations and the brick veneer walls above them. Those micro-gaps aren't just a structural footnote — they're pathways. During a fire, smoke and soot migrate into wall cavities through cracks that wouldn't be visible on a normal day, depositing residue behind drywall or inside the original plaster-and-lath construction common in homes near the Main Street district.

North Texas also sits squarely in hail alley. Spring storms routinely compromise roof decking and attic insulation across Duncanville, and a home with an already-damaged attic envelope is far more susceptible to smoke infiltration when fire occurs. Attic insulation that has absorbed smoke odor is one of the most commonly missed sources of persistent smell after a fire — it holds volatile organic compounds long after visible soot has been cleaned from living spaces.

## Our Smoke Damage Restoration Process in Duncanville

Every job starts with a room-by-room assessment that maps soot type — dry, wet, or protein-based — because each requires a different chemistry to neutralize. Dry soot from fast-burning fires smears if wiped incorrectly; protein residue from kitchen fires is nearly invisible but produces an intense odor that standard cleaning won't touch. In Duncanville's older homes, that assessment also includes testing brick mortar, original hardwood floors, and any remaining plaster surfaces for deep absorption.

From there, the process moves through:

- **Controlled demolition** of materials too saturated to restore (drywall, insulation, cabinetry with delaminated finishes)
- **HEPA-filtered dry sponge and chemical sponge cleaning** of ceilings, walls, and structural framing
- **Thermal fogging and hydroxyl generation** to reach odor molecules inside wall cavities and HVAC ductwork — critical in homes where original duct runs were built into the slab or ceiling plenum
- **Duct cleaning and sealing**, because smoke travels through return-air pathways and will re-contaminate cleaned rooms if the HVAC system isn't addressed
- **Odor verification** before any reconstruction begins, so new drywall isn't installed over a residual smell problem

Reconstruction — drywall, texture, paint, trim, and finish carpentry — is handled in-house, which eliminates the coordination gap between the cleanup crew and the rebuild crew that often extends timelines on fire jobs.
