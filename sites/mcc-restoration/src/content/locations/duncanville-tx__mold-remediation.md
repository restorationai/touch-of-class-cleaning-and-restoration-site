## Why Duncanville Properties See Mold Problems

Three converging factors make mold a recurring issue in Duncanville specifically. First, the housing stock: a large share of homes in ZIP code 75116 were built between 1955 and 1985, when vapor barriers and subfloor ventilation standards were far less rigorous than today. Moisture that enters through a cracked slab or a slow supply-line drip has nowhere to escape, and it migrates into OSB subfloor, fiberglass batt insulation, and drywall paper — all ideal mold substrates.

Second, the climate cycle. Spring hail — Duncanville sits squarely in North Texas hail alley — punches through roof decking and attic insulation. A compromised roof that goes unpatched through a rainy April can deliver enough moisture into an attic to trigger mold growth on rafters and sheathing within two weeks. Third, hard freeze events like February 2021's Winter Storm Uri caused burst pipes across the city simultaneously, and many homeowners dried surfaces quickly without fully addressing moisture trapped inside wall assemblies. Those incomplete dry-outs are still surfacing as mold discoveries years later.

## Our Mold Remediation Process in Duncanville

Every job starts with a thorough visual inspection and moisture mapping — we use thermal imaging and calibrated moisture meters to trace the water source, not just the visible growth. Skipping that step is how mold comes back six months later.

Once we understand the moisture pathway, we establish containment. In the tighter floor plans common to Cedar Ridge and older neighborhoods near Armstrong Park, that typically means negative-air pressure chambers with HEPA-filtered air scrubbers to prevent cross-contamination to adjacent living spaces. We remove and bag affected materials — drywall, insulation, trim — following established remediation protocols, then treat structural surfaces with EPA-registered antimicrobial agents.

Drying comes next. Because Duncanville summers are humid, we run commercial dehumidifiers calibrated to the grain depression needed to bring structural wood down to acceptable moisture content — not just until surfaces feel dry to the touch. After drying is verified with post-remediation moisture readings, the space is ready for reconstruction. MCC handles both sides of that work: remediation and the rebuild, so homeowners deal with one point of contact instead of coordinating two separate contractors.
