## Our Fire Damage Restoration Process in DeSoto

Every fire loss is different, but the sequence matters enormously. We start with a thorough structural assessment — checking load-bearing walls, roof decking, and the subfloor, because in DeSoto's older brick homes the interior framing sometimes sustains more heat damage than the exterior masonry suggests. From there:

**Securing the structure.** Board-up and roof tarping happen before any cleaning begins. An open structure in a Dallas-area spring storm can take on water within hours, turning a fire loss into a combined fire-and-water claim.

**Smoke and soot removal.** Soot from a wood-framing fire behaves differently than soot from a synthetic-material fire. Protein residue from a kitchen fire — common in the galley-style kitchens typical of 1980s DeSoto homes — is nearly invisible but penetrates cabinet interiors, HVAC ducts, and wall cavities. We use dry chemical sponges, HEPA vacuuming, and thermal fogging to address each residue type rather than applying a single cleaning method across the whole structure.

**Odor neutralization.** Smoke odor in a home with original plaster or paneled walls requires more dwell time with hydroxyl or ozone treatment than a newer build. We set equipment timelines based on what the structure actually contains, not a standard-hours formula.

**Structural repairs and reconstruction.** MCC handles both the remediation and the rebuild, which means one point of contact for your insurance adjuster and no gap between the mitigation crew leaving and the reconstruction crew arriving.
