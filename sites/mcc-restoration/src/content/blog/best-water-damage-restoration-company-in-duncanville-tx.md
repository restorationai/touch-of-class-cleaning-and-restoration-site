## What Should You Look for When Choosing a Water Damage Restoration Company?

The right restoration company for a water damage loss in Duncanville handles extraction, drying, documentation, and repairs, and coordinates with your insurance carrier throughout. Here is what to verify before you sign an authorization.

**Scope of services.** Some restoration companies handle mitigation only (water extraction and drying) and refer reconstruction to a separate contractor. Others, like MCC Restoration, carry the job from water removal through finished repairs. If your loss damaged drywall, flooring, or cabinetry, a company that handles both phases saves you the coordination cost and reduces the gap risk between contractors.

**Insurance documentation.** A good restoration company produces a written scope of work, moisture logs, and photo documentation that your adjuster needs to process the claim. Ask how they handle direct billing and what documentation they provide. For a detailed walkthrough of what policies typically cover, see [does homeowners insurance cover water damage](/blog/does-homeowners-insurance-cover-water-damage/).

**Local knowledge.** Duncanville's clay soil, aging plumbing, and North Texas weather patterns create specific loss types that a locally experienced company recognizes quickly. A crew that has dried out dozens of 1960s brick ranches in the Red Bird area or near Armstrong Park knows where moisture hides in that construction type.

**Written scope before work begins.** No reputable restoration company should start significant work without a written scope and cost estimate. Get it in writing. This protects you and creates the paper trail your insurer needs.

**Mold risk awareness.** In a Texas summer, any structure that stays wet for more than 24-48 hours is at elevated mold risk. The [IICRC S520 standard](https://www.iicrc.org) governs mold remediation the same way S500 governs water damage. Ask whether the company monitors for secondary mold growth during the drying phase. For more on the timeline, MCC Restoration's post on [how quickly mold grows after water damage](/blog/mold-after-water-damage/) covers what to expect.

## What to Do Right Now If You Have Water Damage

If water is actively entering your home, stop the source first if it's safe to do so: shut off the supply valve to the affected fixture or the main shutoff if a pipe has burst. Then document everything with your phone before any cleanup begins. Photos and video of the water, the affected materials, and the source of the loss are the foundation of your insurance claim.

Call a restoration company before you call your insurer. The restoration company's scope of work and moisture documentation is what the adjuster will use to evaluate your claim. Starting that process early protects you.
