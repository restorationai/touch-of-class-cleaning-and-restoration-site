**TL;DR:** A slab leak is a break in the water or drain lines running beneath your concrete foundation. In North Texas homes built on Blackland Prairie clay, the soil shifts seasonally and puts constant stress on those pipes. The most common warning signs are a sudden jump in your water bill, warm or damp spots on the floor, the sound of running water when all fixtures are off, and new cracks in your slab or walls. If you notice any of these, call a licensed plumber to locate and repair the break, then contact a water damage restoration company to assess and dry whatever moisture has already entered the structure.

If your water bill jumped $80 last month and nothing changed in your household routine, or you felt a warm patch on your tile floor near the kitchen and brushed it off, this post is for you. Slab leaks are the leading water-damage source in Duncanville and the surrounding Best Southwest communities, where 1950s-1980s brick ranches sit on clay that expands in wet months and contracts in dry ones. That movement works the supply lines under your foundation until something gives. The longer a slab leak runs undetected, the more water saturates the subfloor, framing, and drywall above it, and in a humid Texas summer that moisture becomes a mold job within 24 to 48 hours.

## What are the most common signs of a slab leak?

The seven most reliable signs of a slab leak are: (1) an unexplained spike in your water bill, (2) the sound of running water when every fixture and appliance is off, (3) a warm or hot spot on the floor, (4) damp or wet flooring with no obvious source, (5) low water pressure at fixtures, (6) new cracks in your slab, tile, or interior walls, and (7) a musty or mildew smell coming from the floor level.

Here is what each one looks and feels like in a real home:

**1. Unexplained water bill spike.** A supply-line slab leak runs continuously. A pinhole break in a 3/4-inch copper line can lose 8 to 10 gallons per hour. If your bill jumped by 20 percent or more with no change in household use, treat it as a leak until proven otherwise.

**2. Sound of running water with everything off.** Turn off every faucet, the dishwasher, the washing machine, and the ice maker. Stand near your water meter. If you can hear water moving, or if the meter dial is still turning, water is escaping somewhere in the system.

**3. Warm or hot spot on the floor.** Hot-water supply lines run under the slab just like cold lines do. When a hot-water line breaks, the escaping water heats the concrete above it. A section of tile or hardwood that feels noticeably warmer than the surrounding floor is a textbook slab-leak indicator. In Swan Ridge and Cedar Ridge neighborhoods, where many homes have original copper lines from the 1960s and 1970s, this is one of the first signs residents notice.

**4. Damp, soft, or buckling flooring.** Water wicking up through a cracked slab will saturate wood subfloor, laminate, and carpet from below. If a section of flooring feels spongy underfoot or the laminate is bubbling, moisture is coming from beneath it, not from a surface spill.

**5. Low water pressure.** A significant break in a supply line reduces the pressure available to the rest of the house. If your shower pressure dropped noticeably and your neighbors are not reporting the same issue, the loss is happening inside your foundation.

**6. Cracks in the slab, tile, or walls.** The Blackland Prairie clay under Duncanville homes swells when it absorbs water from a slab leak. That expansion can lift sections of the foundation unevenly, opening new cracks in floor tile, the slab itself, and the drywall above. A crack that appeared recently and is growing deserves immediate attention.

**7. Musty or mildew smell at floor level.** Moisture trapped beneath flooring and inside wall cavities near the slab creates the conditions mold needs. If you notice a persistent earthy smell that is strongest near the floor, especially in a bathroom, kitchen, or laundry room, a slow slab leak may already be feeding a hidden mold colony. ## What causes slab leaks in North Texas clay soil?

Slab leaks in North Texas are caused primarily by soil movement. The Blackland Prairie clay that underlies Duncanville, DeSoto, Cedar Hill, and the surrounding Best Southwest communities is some of the most expansive in the country. It absorbs water and swells, then dries out and contracts, repeatedly, across every wet season and every drought. That cycle exerts lateral and vertical pressure on the copper and galvanized-steel supply lines embedded in the slab.

Three other factors accelerate the process in this area:

- **Pipe age.** Homes built between 1950 and 1985 often have original copper lines. Copper corrodes from the outside when it contacts alkaline soil and from the inside when the water chemistry is aggressive. After 40 to 60 years, the pipe wall thins to the point where soil pressure or a minor shift causes a break.
- **Abrasion.** Pipes that pass through or rest against concrete move slightly every time water pressure cycles on and off. Over decades, the concrete abrades the pipe wall at contact points.
- **Water hammer.** Sudden pressure spikes from appliances shutting off quickly stress joints and fittings. In older homes without pressure-reducing valves, this is a chronic problem.

The February 2021 Winter Storm Uri freeze-thaw cycle accelerated pipe failures across the region. Homes in the Wheatland Road corridor and Red Bird area that had minor pre-existing weaknesses in their under-slab lines often developed active leaks in the months following that event.

## Does homeowners insurance cover a slab leak?

Homeowners insurance typically covers the water damage caused by a sudden, accidental slab leak but does not cover the plumbing repair itself. The distinction matters: your policy may pay to dry the structure, replace damaged flooring, and rebuild affected walls, but the cost to locate and fix the broken pipe is usually excluded as a maintenance item.

Key coverage factors to check with your adjuster:

- **Sudden vs. gradual.** A pipe that burst during a freeze event is generally covered. A slow pinhole leak that has been running for months is often denied as gradual damage the homeowner should have caught earlier. Document when you first noticed symptoms and report promptly.
- **Access coverage.** Some policies include a "tear-out" or "access" provision that pays to break through the slab to reach the pipe. Read your policy or ask your agent specifically about this.
- **Mold endorsement.** Standard policies limit mold coverage. If the slab leak has been running long enough to grow mold, a separate mold endorsement may be needed to cover remediation costs.

For a full breakdown of what Texas homeowners policies typically cover, see our guide on [Does Homeowners Insurance Cover Water Damage?](/blog/does-homeowners-insurance-cover-water-damage/)

Documentation is what protects your claim. Photograph every affected area before anything is moved or dried. A restoration company that provides a written scope of work, moisture readings, and drying logs gives your adjuster the evidence needed to approve the structural portion of the claim.

## What should you do when you suspect a slab leak?

If you suspect a slab leak, take these steps in order: shut off the water supply if the leak is active and causing visible damage, call a licensed Texas plumber to locate and confirm the break, then call a water damage restoration company to assess how far moisture has traveled into the structure.

Here is the sequence in more detail:

1. **Shut off the main water supply** if you see active flooding or the floor is already wet. The shutoff is typically near the water meter at the street or at the main valve inside the home.
2. **Do not run water** until the plumber has assessed the situation. Every gallon that flows through a broken line adds to the saturation below the slab.
3. **Call a licensed Texas plumber** to perform leak detection (usually electronic or acoustic) and give you a repair scope. This is the plumbing repair side of the job, which requires a Texas plumbing license.
4. **Call a restoration company** to assess the water damage independently. A plumber's job ends at the pipe. The structural drying, flooring removal, and mold prevention work is a separate scope that a restoration contractor handles.
5. **Call your insurance company** to open a claim. Have your policy number ready and be prepared to describe when you first noticed symptoms. The restoration company's written scope and moisture readings will support your claim.
6. **Do not delay drying.** The [IICRC S500 standard](https://www.iicrc.org/page/IICRCS500) for water damage restoration defines the drying window as 24 to 48 hours before secondary damage, including mold growth, becomes likely. In a Texas summer, that window is shorter. See our post on [How Quickly Does Mold Grow After Water Damage?](/blog/mold-after-water-damage/) for what happens if drying is delayed.
