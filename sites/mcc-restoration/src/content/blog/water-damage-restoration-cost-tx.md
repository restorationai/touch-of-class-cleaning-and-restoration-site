## How Much Does Water Damage Restoration Cost in Texas?

In Texas, water damage restoration typically costs $1,500 to $12,000 for a single-family home, with the average residential job landing between $3,000 and $7,000. Small, contained losses (a dishwasher leak caught the same day) sit at the low end; multi-room losses from burst pipes or slab leaks that went undetected for days sit at the high end.

These are industry-typical ranges based on the scope of work involved: water extraction, structural drying, demolition of unsalvageable materials, and antimicrobial treatment. They do not include reconstruction (replacing drywall, flooring, or cabinetry), which is a separate line item.

### Cost by Scenario

| Scenario | Typical Range (TX) |
|---|---|
| Single room, clean water (Cat 1), caught same day | $1,200 - $3,500 |
| Two to three rooms, appliance or supply-line leak (Cat 2) | $3,000 - $6,500 |

| Sewage backup or Category 3 (black water) loss | $4,500 - $12,000+ |
| Full-floor flood, storm-driven water intrusion | $6,000 - $15,000+ |

Reconstruction costs (new drywall, flooring, cabinetry) are separate and vary widely based on materials and scope.

## What Factors Drive the Cost Up or Down?

Five factors move the number more than anything else.

**1. Water category.** The [IICRC S500 standard](https://www.iicrc.org) classifies water losses into three categories. Category 1 is clean water from a supply line or appliance; Category 2 (gray water) comes from washing machines, dishwashers, or toilet overflows without solids; Category 3 (black water) includes sewage backups, floodwater, and any water that has sat long enough to become grossly contaminated. Cat 3 jobs require full PPE, antimicrobial treatment of all affected surfaces, and disposal of porous materials that cannot be safely dried in place. That adds significant labor and material cost.

**2. Water class (how far it spread).** The IICRC also classifies losses by how much of the structure is wet. Class 1 is a small, contained area with minimal absorption. Class 4 involves specialty drying of dense materials like hardwood subfloors, concrete slabs, or plaster walls. Duncanville's 1950s-1980s brick ranches often have original hardwood floors and plaster ceilings that require Class 3 or 4 drying protocols, which means more equipment running longer.

**3. How long the water sat.** Water that is extracted within 24 hours has a much better chance of a lower total cost. After 24-48 hours, secondary damage begins: drywall paper delaminates, wood framing swells, and mold can begin colonizing porous surfaces. A job that could have been $3,500 on day one can become a $7,000 job by day three if extraction is delayed. This is especially true in Texas summers, where indoor humidity accelerates microbial growth. See our post on [how quickly mold grows after water damage](/blog/mold-after-water-damage/) for a detailed breakdown.

**4. Materials affected.** Hardwood flooring, tile set in mud beds, plaster walls, and insulated exterior walls cost more to dry and more to replace than standard drywall and vinyl plank. Many Duncanville homes in the Swan Ridge and Cedar Ridge neighborhoods have original hardwood floors that require specialty drying equipment and extended monitoring before a determination can be made about whether they can be saved.

**5. Accessibility.** Slab leaks and under-foundation plumbing failures, which are common in Duncanville due to Blackland Prairie clay movement, require accessing water that has migrated under the slab. That may mean drilling weep holes, using specialty drying mats, or extended drying cycles. These jobs consistently land in the $4,000-$9,000 range before any plumbing repair.

## What Does the Restoration Process Actually Include?

Water damage restoration is not just running fans. A properly scoped job follows a defined sequence.

**Step 1: Emergency extraction.** Truck-mounted or portable extractors remove standing water. The faster this happens, the lower the total damage.

**Step 2: Moisture mapping.** Technicians use thermal imaging cameras and penetrating moisture meters to document exactly where water has migrated, including inside wall cavities, under flooring, and in ceiling assemblies. This documentation matters for your insurance claim.

**Step 3: Controlled demolition.** Wet drywall, insulation, baseboards, and flooring that cannot be dried in place are removed. This is not optional for Cat 2 or Cat 3 losses. Leaving wet porous materials in place creates conditions for mold growth within 24-72 hours.

**Step 4: Structural drying.** LGR (low-grain refrigerant) dehumidifiers and high-velocity air movers are placed according to a drying plan. Equipment runs continuously, typically for 3-5 days, with daily moisture readings to track progress toward dry standard.

**Step 5: Antimicrobial treatment.** EPA-registered antimicrobials are applied to all affected structural surfaces before the space is closed up.

**Step 6: Documentation and clearance.** Final moisture readings confirm the structure has reached the dry standard. This documentation is part of the claim file.

## Will Homeowners Insurance Cover Water Damage Restoration in Texas?

Most standard Texas homeowners policies cover sudden and accidental water damage but exclude gradual leaks and flooding. Here is how the coverage typically breaks down.

**Covered (typically):** Burst pipes, appliance failures (washing machine supply line, dishwasher), roof leaks caused by a covered storm event, and accidental overflow. If you had a pipe burst during a freeze event like Winter Storm Uri, that is typically covered.

**Not covered (typically):** Gradual leaks (a slow drip under the sink that went unnoticed for months), sewer backup unless you have a specific endorsement, and flood damage from surface water. Flood damage requires a separate [National Flood Insurance Program (NFIP)](https://www.fema.gov/flood-insurance) policy or a private flood policy.

**What protects your claim:** Documentation. The moisture mapping readings, photos taken before and during extraction, and a written scope of work from the restoration company are what the adjuster needs to approve the claim. Restoration companies that work with insurance carriers directly know what documentation format adjusters expect.

## What Should You Do Right Now?

If you are dealing with an active water loss, the first 24 hours determine a significant portion of the final cost. Stop the source if you can (shut off the main water supply if a pipe has burst), move valuables out of the affected area, and do not run fans without professional guidance. Running fans before moisture mapping is complete can spread moisture into unaffected areas.

Then call a restoration company to get a written scope before any work begins. A written scope protects you during the insurance claim process and ensures you know exactly what is included in the price.
