## When to Call a Plumber Instead (or First)

A water damage restoration company handles the damage. A plumber handles the source. If the break is active and you cannot reach the shutoff valve, call a licensed plumber to stop the flow before restoration work begins. Both calls often happen within minutes of each other.

Here's a quick triage guide:

- **Active burst pipe, slab leak, or appliance line failure**: Call a plumber to stop the flow. Call MCC Restoration immediately after, or at the same time from a second phone.
- **Roof leak from storm damage**: The source is weather, not plumbing. Call MCC Restoration directly. They handle [storm damage restoration](/services/storm-damage-restoration/) and can tarp the roof while the interior drying begins.
- **Sewage backup**: This is Category 3 water, also called black water, and it carries pathogens. Do not enter the space without PPE. Call MCC Restoration first. They handle [sewage cleanup](/services/sewage-cleanup/) with the containment protocols that situation requires.
- **Electrical hazard (water near a panel, outlets sparking, appliances submerged)**: Call 911 and do not enter. Wait for the fire department to clear the space before any restoration crew enters.
- **Gas smell**: Leave immediately. Call 911 and Atmos Energy (1-866-322-8667). Do not re-enter until cleared.

## When Should I Call My Insurance Company?

Call your insurance company after mitigation has started, not before. This is the sequence most homeowners get backwards, and it costs them.

Here's why the order matters: your policy's duty-to-mitigate clause requires you to take reasonable steps to prevent further damage. If you wait on hold with your insurer while water continues soaking into subfloor and wall cavities, the adjuster can argue that additional damage was preventable. Get the restoration crew on-site first. MCC Restoration documents everything with photos, moisture readings, and a written scope before any material is removed. That documentation is what protects your claim.

For a plain-English breakdown of what homeowners policies typically cover, see [Does Homeowners Insurance Cover Water Damage?](/blog/does-homeowners-insurance-cover-water-damage/)

Typical North Texas homeowners policies cover sudden and accidental water damage from burst pipes, appliance failures, and roof leaks from storm events. They generally exclude gradual leaks (a slow drip under the sink that went unnoticed for months) and flooding from outside the home. Flood damage from rising water requires a separate NFIP or private flood policy. The [FEMA National Flood Insurance Program](https://www.fema.gov/flood-insurance) covers that gap for eligible properties.

## What Happens When You Call MCC Restoration?

When you call (682) 772-9123, here's what to have ready: your address, the source of the water if you know it, whether the water is still active, and whether there are any electrical or safety hazards. That's it. The crew handles the rest.

The response process follows the [IICRC S500 Standard for Professional Water Damage Restoration](https://www.iicrc.org), the industry's technical benchmark for drying and documentation:

1. **Inspection and moisture mapping.** The crew uses thermal imaging cameras and calibrated moisture meters to find every wet area, including hidden moisture inside wall cavities, under flooring, and beneath cabinets. You cannot dry what you cannot find.
2. **Water extraction.** Truck-mounted or portable extractors remove standing water. This step happens fast because every hour of standing water increases Category and Class of loss, which directly affects drying time and cost.
3. **Structural drying setup.** Industrial LGR (low-grain refrigerant) dehumidifiers and high-velocity air movers are positioned based on the moisture map. In Duncanville's humid late-summer conditions, dehumidifier capacity matters. The goal is to bring affected materials to dry standard (typically below 16% moisture content for wood) before mold has a chance to establish.
4. **Daily monitoring.** Moisture readings are logged each day. Equipment is adjusted or repositioned as materials dry. A typical residential water loss takes 3 to 5 days to dry, depending on the Category and Class of water and the materials affected.
5. **Documentation for your insurer.** Every reading, every photo, every piece of equipment placed is logged. MCC Restoration provides a written scope of work before any demolition or material removal, so you and your adjuster know exactly what was done and why.

If drying reveals mold growth, which is common in Duncanville homes where a slow leak went unnoticed through a humid summer, the crew will identify it and walk you through [mold remediation](/services/mold-remediation/) options. For more on that timeline, see [How Quickly Does Mold Grow After Water Damage?](/blog/mold-after-water-damage/)

Once drying is complete, MCC Restoration also handles [reconstruction](/services/reconstruction/) for any materials that had to be removed, so you're not coordinating a separate contractor to close up walls and replace flooring.
