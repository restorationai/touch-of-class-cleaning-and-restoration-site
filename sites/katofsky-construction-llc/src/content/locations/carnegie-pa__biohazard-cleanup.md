## Why Carnegie's Housing Stock Shapes Biohazard Remediation

A significant share of Carnegie's residential buildings date to the 1910s through 1950s, when construction relied heavily on plaster-and-lath walls, hardwood subfloors with minimal vapor barriers, and tight floor-joist bays. These materials are porous in ways that modern drywall simply is not. Blood, bodily fluids, and other infectious materials can wick into plaster, seep between floorboards, and travel along subfloor framing before the surface appearance suggests anything has penetrated deeply. Remediation in these older structures requires more aggressive material assessment and, in many cases, controlled demolition of affected substrate before the space can be properly disinfected.

Carnegie also has a notable density of rental units and multi-unit buildings along its main corridors. Landlords and property managers dealing with a biohazard situation face an additional layer of coordination: notifying adjacent tenants appropriately, managing access to shared hallways, and documenting the remediation thoroughly for future occupancy. The team at Katofsky Construction handles that documentation as part of the standard scope.

## Our Biohazard Cleanup Process in Carnegie

Every job begins with a thorough assessment before any cleaning starts. The affected area is contained to prevent cross-contamination of adjacent spaces, which matters especially in Carnegie's older attached and semi-detached homes where HVAC systems and open floor plans can distribute airborne particulates quickly.

From there, the process moves through these stages:

- **Removal of affected materials**: Porous items that cannot be decontaminated to a safe standard are bagged, sealed, and transported for proper disposal in accordance with Pennsylvania infectious waste regulations.
- **Disinfection and pathogen neutralization**: Hospital-grade EPA-registered disinfectants are applied to all affected hard surfaces, with dwell times calibrated to the specific pathogens of concern.
- **Odor treatment**: Enzymatic and oxidizing agents address residual odor at the molecular level rather than masking it.
- **Final verification**: The space is inspected before containment is removed, and documentation is provided for insurance and property records.

The crew works in appropriate personal protective equipment throughout, and discretion at the property is standard practice, not an add-on.
