## Why Ross Township Properties See Board-Up and Tarping Emergencies

Allegheny County's western Pennsylvania climate delivers a specific combination of stressors: freeze-thaw cycles that fatigue flashing and caulk joints through the winter, severe convective storms in late spring and summer, and occasional ice dams that back water under shingles on low-slope roofs. Ross Township's housing stock skews toward mid-century construction, with a notable number of Cape Cods and brick ranches built between the late 1940s and early 1970s. Those homes were built to codes that predate modern wind-uplift requirements, and their original roof decking is often thinner than what would be installed today. A storm that a newer subdivision handles without incident can leave an older Ross Township home with a section of decking exposed to the sky.

Fire damage adds a separate layer of urgency. After a structure fire, window glass is frequently broken by firefighting operations, and roof sections may be vented or partially collapsed. Every hour that opening stays unsecured is an hour of rain, debris, and unauthorized entry risk. Board-up and tarping is not cosmetic at that stage; it is the first line of loss containment.

## Our Emergency Board-Up and Tarping Process in Ross Township

When a call comes in, the first question is what type of opening needs to be secured and what is behind it. A broken ground-floor window on a fire-damaged home gets a different treatment than a roof breach on an occupied house after a storm.

For window and door board-up, the crew uses exterior-grade plywood cut to fit the rough opening, secured with structural screws into the surrounding framing rather than just the window trim. This matters on older Ross Township homes where the trim itself may be soft or partially charred. The goal is a board that resists prying, not just wind.

For roof tarping, the team anchors tarps using a batten-and-screw method along the ridge and at the downslope edge, with enough overlap to shed water past the damaged section. On steeper-pitched roofs common in this part of Allegheny County, fall protection is part of the setup, not an afterthought. The tarp is sized to cover the damaged area plus a meaningful margin on all sides, because a tarp that barely covers the hole will leak at the edges during the next rain event.

Documentation runs parallel to the physical work: photographs of the damage before boarding, during, and after securing, along with written notes on materials used. That file goes directly to your insurance adjuster.
