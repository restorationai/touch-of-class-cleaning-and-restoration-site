## Why Shaler Township's Housing Stock Shapes Biohazard Work

A significant portion of Shaler Township's residential properties date to the mid-twentieth century or earlier. Homes built in that era frequently used hardwood subfloors, plaster-and-lath wall construction, and cast-iron or galvanized plumbing, all materials that are more porous and more difficult to fully decontaminate than modern construction. Biological fluids can wick into subfloor seams, travel along wall cavities, or pool beneath vinyl flooring that was laid over the original surface. Surface cleaning alone does not address what has migrated below.

The township's topography adds another layer: properties on hillside lots, common throughout the area, sometimes have basement or lower-level rooms that are partially below grade. Those spaces tend to have limited ventilation and higher ambient humidity, conditions that matter when you are trying to dry and decontaminate after a biohazard event. Proper containment, negative air pressure, and material removal decisions are calibrated to what the actual structure will allow.

On-site, the process follows a consistent sequence:

- **Containment and PPE setup**: The affected area is isolated before any work begins, limiting cross-contamination to other parts of the home.
- **Removal of affected materials**: Porous materials that cannot be fully decontaminated, including carpet, padding, drywall sections, and subfloor panels, are removed, bagged, and transported as regulated biohazardous waste through licensed disposal channels.
- **Disinfection and treatment**: Hard surfaces and structural elements are treated with hospital-grade disinfectants appropriate for bloodborne pathogens and other biological material.
- **Odor control**: Enzymatic treatment and air scrubbing address odor at the source rather than masking it.
- **Documentation**: We photograph affected areas and provide written documentation suitable for insurance submission.

All work is performed by an IICRC Certified Firm, which means the protocols we follow are drawn from industry standards rather than improvised on-site.
