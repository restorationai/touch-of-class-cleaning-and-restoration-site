## Why Green Tree's Building Stock Shapes Every Renovation

Most of Green Tree's residential housing was built between the late 1940s and the early 1970s. That era of construction comes with specific realities: knob-and-tube wiring that may still be live inside walls, galvanized supply lines that corrode from the inside out, and plaster-over-lath wall systems that behave very differently from modern drywall when you open them up for a kitchen or bathroom remodel.

Before a single cabinet goes in or a tile gets set, a thorough pre-construction assessment of what's inside those walls matters. Discovering that a kitchen remodel requires an electrical panel upgrade mid-project is common in homes of this vintage. Planning for it at the estimate stage keeps the timeline and budget from unraveling. Green Tree's compact lot sizes also mean that material staging and dumpster placement require coordination with neighbors and, in some cases, a quick check with the borough on right-of-way rules before work begins.

## Our Renovation and General Contracting Process in Green Tree

Every project starts with a walkthrough that accounts for the specific conditions of the home, not a checklist built for new construction. ## Local Note

Homes in Green Tree's hillside sections, particularly those built on the steeper grades that drop toward Chartiers Creek, often have basement or lower-level additions that were finished informally over the decades. These spaces frequently lack proper egress windows, have ceiling heights that fall just below current code minimums, or were built over crawl spaces with inadequate vapor barriers. Any renovation that touches these lower levels will typically trigger a code compliance review during the permit process. Knowing that going in, rather than discovering it after demolition, is the difference between a smooth project and an expensive surprise.

## Coordinating with Allegheny County on Permits and Inspections

Green Tree falls under Allegheny County's permitting jurisdiction for most structural and mechanical work. Permit timelines vary depending on project scope and current county workload, and some renovation types require inspections at multiple stages before work can proceed. Katofsky Construction manages that process as part of the project, not as an add-on. For homeowners who have never pulled a permit before, understanding what triggers a required inspection and what the inspector is actually looking for removes a significant source of stress from an already demanding project.

For kitchen and bathroom remodels that involve moving plumbing, adding circuits, or altering any load-bearing element, permits are not optional. Working with a general contractor who treats permit compliance as standard practice protects the homeowner at resale and keeps the project insurable.
