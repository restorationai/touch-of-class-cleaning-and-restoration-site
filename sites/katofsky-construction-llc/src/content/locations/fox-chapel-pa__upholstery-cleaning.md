## Why Fox Chapel Homes See Different Upholstery Wear

Fox Chapel is known for large, wooded lots and custom construction, and that housing pattern shapes what shows up on upholstery here. Homes set back from the road on tree-covered acreage tend to have more pollen, leaf tannin, and fine dust tracked in seasonally, which settles into fabric weave and leather seams long before it's visible to the eye. Formal living and sitting rooms in many of these homes also see lighter daily use but longer dwell time between cleanings, so soil and oxidation build up gradually rather than from spills. That combination, heavier natural debris outdoors and infrequent but deep cleaning cycles indoors, means upholstery here often needs a more thorough extraction pass than a downtown condo would, even when it looks only lightly soiled on the surface.

## Our Upholstery Cleaning Process

We start every job with a fabric and fiber test, because Fox Chapel's mix of leather, natural fiber, and high-end synthetic upholstery each responds differently to moisture and cleaning agents. For fabric pieces, we pre-treat soiled areas, agitate to lift embedded soil, then use hot water extraction calibrated to the fiber, pulling out the cleaning solution along with the soil rather than leaving it to dry in place. Leather upholstery gets a separate conditioning step after cleaning, since leather that's cleaned but not reconditioned dries out and cracks faster, especially in homes with dry winter heat running for months at a time. We follow IICRC-recognized textile care guidance throughout, and we always spot-test in an inconspicuous area first on antique or unusual-fiber pieces before treating the whole item.

## Local Note

One thing we watch for specifically in Fox Chapel: homes with large west- or south-facing windows, common in the area's more open, wooded floor plans, get more direct sun exposure on living room furniture through the afternoon. That sun exposure dries out leather and fades fabric dye faster than in a shaded room, so pieces positioned near those windows often need conditioning or protective treatment sooner than the rest of a matching set, even if they were purchased and placed at the same time.
