## What Greensburg's Building Stock Means for Your Renovation

Homes built before 1960 dominate large portions of Greensburg's residential neighborhoods. That era of construction used materials and methods that require a more deliberate approach during any remodel. Plaster walls, for instance, are heavier and more brittle than modern drywall, and cutting into them for electrical or plumbing updates demands care to avoid cascading damage to adjacent surfaces. Original hardwood subfloors are often worth saving but need assessment before new flooring goes on top.

Westmoreland County's climate adds another layer. Freeze-thaw cycles through western Pennsylvania winters put stress on masonry, window frames, and exterior trim year after year. By the time a homeowner is ready to remodel a kitchen or finish a basement, there is often deferred exterior maintenance that needs to be addressed in the same scope of work, or at minimum factored into the sequencing. A general contractor who understands this pattern can help you prioritize so one project does not undo another.

For homes that have experienced water intrusion, fire, or storm damage, renovation work intersects directly with post-damage rebuild needs. The IICRC-certified background Katofsky brings to structural drying and mold remediation informs how we approach rebuilding affected spaces, because the framing and substrate conditions matter as much as the finish selections.

## Local Note

One pattern that comes up repeatedly in Greensburg-area renovations is the presence of older clay tile sewer laterals connecting homes to the municipal system. These laterals are prone to root intrusion and joint separation over time, and a kitchen or bathroom remodel that increases fixture use can surface a slow drain problem that was previously manageable. Before finalizing a scope that adds a bathroom or relocates a kitchen sink, it is worth understanding the condition of the lateral, because a remodel is a natural time to address it rather than discovering the issue after the new tile is down.
