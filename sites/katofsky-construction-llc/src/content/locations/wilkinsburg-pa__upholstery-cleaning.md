## Why Wilkinsburg's Older Homes Complicate Upholstery Care

Wilkinsburg is a dense, inner-ring Allegheny County borough built out largely in the late 1800s and early 1900s, with a lot of duplexes, rowhouses, and converted Victorian-era homes still standing. That vintage housing stock tends to run on older forced-air or radiator heat, which circulates fine dust and, in some blocks, lingering soot from the region's industrial past. Upholstered furniture acts like a filter for all of it. Add Pittsburgh's humid summers and damp shoulder seasons, and fabric fibers hold onto moisture and odor longer than they would in a drier climate, which is part of why a couch that looks fine can still smell stale.

## Our Upholstery Cleaning Process in Wilkinsburg

We start by identifying the fiber and construction of the piece, since a cotton-blend sectional and a leather club chair need entirely different approaches. Fabric upholstery gets pre-vacuumed to lift embedded dust before any moisture touches it, then cleaned with hot water extraction or low-moisture steam depending on the fill and frame underneath. Leather upholstery is cleaned and conditioned separately, since aggressive steam or extraction methods can dry out or crack older leather. In Wilkinsburg's older homes, where furniture often sits closer to radiators or uninsulated exterior walls, we also check for cold-wall condensation patterns on the back or base of the piece before we start, because that moisture history changes how fast the fabric will dry once we're done.

## Fabric vs. Leather: What We Actually Adjust

Fabric upholstery cleaning in Wilkinsburg's older housing stock usually calls for a slower extraction pass and longer air-dry time, since many of these homes have less mechanical ventilation than newer construction. We use fans and, when the room allows it, dehumidification to bring drying time down and avoid the wicking effect where surface fabric looks dry but the padding underneath is still damp. Leather upholstery cleaning is a different job entirely: it's about lifting body oils and surface grime without stripping the finish, then conditioning so the leather doesn't crack in a house with dry winter heat and humid summers back to back. We treat the two as separate services because they are.
