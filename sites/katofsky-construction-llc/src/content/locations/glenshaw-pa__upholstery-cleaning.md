## Our Upholstery Cleaning Process in Glenshaw

We start by identifying the fabric or leather type, since a synthetic blend sofa and a leather recliner need completely different approaches and the wrong one can set a stain permanently. For fabric upholstery, we pre-treat soiled areas, then use hot water extraction to lift embedded soil and allergens without oversaturating the cushion fill, which matters in Shaler Township homes where basements and first floors run cooler and slower to dry. For leather pieces, we use a pH-balanced cleaner and conditioner instead of steam, since leather doesn't respond to moisture the way fabric does and steam can dry out or crack older hides. Either way, we test in an inconspicuous spot first and walk the homeowner through drying expectations before we start.

## Equipment & Methods We Use for Upholstery

Our extraction equipment is calibrated to pull moisture back out immediately after cleaning rather than leaving cushions damp for hours, which cuts down on the musty smell that shows up in humid Pittsburgh summers if furniture stays wet too long. For fabric sofas and sectionals, we adjust water temperature and extraction pressure based on fiber type, since a delicate microfiber and a heavy-duty cotton blend don't tolerate the same settings. Leather gets a separate toolkit entirely: soft brushes, leather-safe solutions, and a conditioning step at the end so the material doesn't dry out and crack once the cleaning is done.
