## Why New Kensington Properties Carry a Higher Asbestos Risk

The timing of New Kensington's construction boom lines up almost exactly with the decades when asbestos use in building materials was at its peak. Aluminum production brought workers and capital, and the city built quickly to house them. The result is block after block of row homes, duplexes, and brick commercial buildings whose original materials have now aged sixty to a hundred years.

Age alone is not the problem. Asbestos-containing materials that are intact and undisturbed generally stay that way. The risk rises the moment someone starts cutting, sanding, drilling, or demolishing. A bathroom retile, a basement remodel, a popcorn ceiling scrape before a home sale: any of these can release fibers if the underlying material was never tested. In a city where this housing vintage is the norm rather than the exception, that risk is present on nearly every block.

Westmoreland County's wet winters also matter. Freeze-thaw cycles work on older masonry and roofing, and when water gets behind original roofing felt or into a wall cavity with pipe insulation, the deterioration that follows can make previously stable asbestos-containing material friable. Friable material is the condition that triggers mandatory abatement under Pennsylvania DEP regulations.

## Our Asbestos Abatement Process in New Kensington

Every project starts with a visual inspection and, where warranted, bulk sampling of suspect materials. Samples go to an accredited laboratory. We do not guess at what is or is not asbestos-containing based on appearance alone, and we do not recommend abatement where testing does not support it.

When testing confirms asbestos-containing material that needs to be addressed, the work follows a defined sequence:

- **Containment:** Poly sheeting, negative air pressure, and HEPA-filtered air scrubbers isolate the work area from the rest of the structure. Heating and cooling systems serving the space are sealed off.
- **Wet removal:** Regulated materials are kept wet throughout removal to suppress fiber release. No dry scraping, no shortcuts.
- **Waste handling:** All removed material is double-bagged in labeled, sealed poly bags and transported to a licensed disposal facility. Pennsylvania requires asbestos waste to go to approved landfills with proper manifesting.
- **Air clearance:** A final air sample, collected after cleanup and before containment is removed, confirms that fiber counts are within acceptable limits before the space is released.

For popcorn ceiling asbestos removal specifically, the process includes careful wetting of the texture coat before any scraping begins. In older New Kensington homes where the ceiling substrate is original plaster rather than drywall, we adjust the approach to avoid saturating the plaster and creating a secondary moisture problem.

## Local Note

One pattern that comes up repeatedly in the Alle-Kiski Valley is the combination of original floor tile and a later layer of vinyl flooring installed directly over it. Homeowners often do not know the original tile is still there until a contractor pulls up the newer material. In New Kensington's older housing stock, that original layer is frequently nine-inch or twelve-inch floor tile from the 1940s and 1950s, a common asbestos-containing product. The adhesive used to set it, sometimes called "black mastic," can also contain asbestos. Before any flooring project in a home of this era, testing both the tile and the adhesive separately is the right call, because they can have different asbestos content even when they were installed at the same time.
