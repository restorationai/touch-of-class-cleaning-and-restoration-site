## Why McCandless Homes Present Distinct Renovation Challenges

The housing stock along the northern townships of Allegheny County reflects several distinct construction eras, and McCandless is a clear example. Homes built in the 1950s and 1960s typically used plaster-and-lath wall systems, cast-iron drain stacks, and original single-pane windows that no longer meet modern energy code. When a kitchen remodel or bathroom renovation reaches into those systems, the scope almost always expands beyond what the initial estimate covers.

Allegheny County's clay-heavy soil also creates movement in foundations over time. Basement finishing projects in McCandless frequently surface evidence of minor seasonal shifting: hairline cracks in block foundations, doors that have drifted out of plumb, or floor joists that have settled unevenly. Addressing those conditions before laying new flooring or finishing drywall is not optional. A general contractor who skips that step is setting up a cosmetic fix that will fail within a few years.

Winter in the Pittsburgh region adds another layer. Freeze-thaw cycling through November into March stresses masonry, flashing, and any exterior work left partially open. Scheduling exterior phases of a remodel around that window matters, and it affects how a project is sequenced from the start.

## Our Renovation and General Contracting Process in McCandless

Every project starts with a thorough walkthrough, not a quick glance. For older McCandless homes especially, that means checking the attic for insulation depth and ventilation, looking at the electrical panel for capacity, and assessing plumbing supply lines before a single permit application goes to Allegheny County. Pulling permits through the county's building department is standard for structural, electrical, and plumbing work, and skipping that step creates real problems at resale.

Once the scope is confirmed, the project is broken into phases with defined milestones so you always know what is happening and what comes next. Subcontractors we bring in for electrical, HVAC, or plumbing work are coordinated under the general contracting umbrella, meaning you have one point of contact rather than four separate schedules to track.

For post-damage rebuilds, the process integrates directly with whatever remediation work preceded it. If water damage or a fire has already been addressed, we pick up at the point where the structure is clean and dry and carry it through to finished, inspected, and livable.

## Local Note

One pattern that comes up repeatedly in McCandless renovations: homes built in the late 1950s and early 1960s in the township's older residential pockets often have bathrooms that were tiled using a thick-bed mortar method over metal lath. That system is durable, but when it fails, it fails in bulk. Removing it takes significantly more time and generates more debris than a standard tile-over-drywall tearout. Homeowners who budget based on a modern bathroom gut are sometimes surprised by the demo phase alone.
