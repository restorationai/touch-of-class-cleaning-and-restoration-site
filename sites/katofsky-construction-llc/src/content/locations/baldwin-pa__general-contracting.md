Baldwin Borough sits just south of Pittsburgh in Allegheny County, and the housing stock here tells a clear story: street after street of mid-century brick ranches, Cape Cods, and two-story frame homes built between the late 1940s and the early 1970s. That construction era brings character, but it also brings specific renovation challenges, undersized electrical panels, plaster-and-lath walls, galvanized supply lines, and floor plans that predate open-concept living. Katofsky Construction LLC works in Baldwin regularly, and the work here calls for a contractor who understands what's behind those walls before the first cut is made.

## Why Baldwin's Housing Stock Shapes Every Renovation

Most of Baldwin's residential neighborhoods were developed during the postwar suburban boom, which means the bones of the home are often solid but the systems are overdue. Knob-and-tube wiring has largely been replaced in updated homes, but galvanized steel water supply lines and cast-iron drain stacks are still common, especially in homes that haven't had a full gut renovation. When a kitchen or bathroom remodel opens up a wall, it's not unusual to find plumbing that needs to be brought up to current Pennsylvania Uniform Construction Code standards before finish work can begin.

Allegheny County's clay-heavy soil also matters. Basement floors and foundation walls in Baldwin homes are prone to seasonal moisture intrusion as the ground expands and contracts through freeze-thaw cycles. A basement finishing project here isn't just about framing and drywall, it starts with an honest look at waterproofing, drainage, and whether the existing slab is a suitable substrate for the intended use.

Post-damage rebuild work adds another layer. When water damage or fire damage has already compromised structural framing, insulation, or subfloor, the renovation scope has to account for what was damaged, not just what the homeowner originally wanted to change.

## Our Renovation and General Contracting Process in Baldwin

Every project starts with a walkthrough that goes further than a surface assessment. For older Baldwin homes, that means checking the attic for insulation type and ventilation, looking at the electrical service entrance, and identifying any load-bearing walls that affect the proposed layout. Surprises mid-project drive up cost and push timelines; finding them before demolition begins keeps the job on track.

From there, the process moves through permitting with Allegheny County and Baldwin Borough as required, subcontractor coordination, and phased construction that keeps the home livable where possible. Kitchen remodels, bathroom remodels, basement conversions, additions, and full post-damage rebuilds each follow a sequenced schedule: rough work and inspections before insulation, insulation before drywall, drywall before finish trades. That sequence isn't bureaucratic, it's how work gets done correctly and passes inspection the first time.

For post-damage rebuilds specifically, Katofsky Construction works from the scope of loss documentation, coordinating with insurance carriers and adjusters to align the rebuild scope with what was approved. Homeowners don't need to manage that back-and-forth alone.

## Permits and Code Compliance in Baldwin Borough

Baldwin Borough issues its own building permits for residential work, and Allegheny County handles inspections for many project types. Permit requirements apply to structural changes, electrical upgrades, plumbing modifications, and additions, the full list depends on project scope. Skipping permits on work that requires them creates problems at resale and can complicate insurance claims if something goes wrong later.

For homes built before 1978, lead paint is a real consideration during demolition and renovation. Pennsylvania requires that contractors working on pre-1978 housing follow EPA Renovation, Repair and Painting (RRP) rules. Katofsky Construction carries IICRC certification and operates within applicable regulatory frameworks for this type of work.

## Local Note

One thing that comes up repeatedly in Baldwin's brick ranch homes: the original interior walls between the garage and the living space were often framed and drywalled with standard half-inch drywall rather than the fire-rated assembly now required by code. When a renovation opens that wall for any reason, current Pennsylvania code requires upgrading to a compliant fire separation. It's a detail that catches homeowners off guard when it shows up mid-project, but it's a known pattern in this housing stock and worth discussing before demolition begins.
