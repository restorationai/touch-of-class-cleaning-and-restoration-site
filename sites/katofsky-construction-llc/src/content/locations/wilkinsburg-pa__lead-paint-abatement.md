## Why Wilkinsburg Properties See Lead Paint Issues

Most of the borough's housing predates the 1978 federal ban on residential lead paint, and a good share of it predates 1950 entirely. Layered paint on double-hung window sashes, exterior trim, and porch spindles is where we find the highest concentrations, since those surfaces were repainted repeatedly before the ban and then often painted over rather than stripped. Friction surfaces, windows that rub against their frames, doors that stick in humid months, generate fine lead dust every time they're opened or closed. Combined with Allegheny County's older housing turnover rate, that's why lead paint testing comes up so often during pre-sale inspections and rental licensing in Wilkinsburg.

## Our Lead Paint Abatement Process in Wilkinsburg

We start with lead paint testing, confirming which components are actually positive rather than treating the whole structure as hazardous by default. From there, containment goes up around the work area, critical barriers, negative air where the scope calls for it, and HEPA filtration running continuously. Removal methods are chosen by substrate: wet scraping and chemical stripping for painted trim we're keeping, full component removal for deteriorated window sashes or porch elements that aren't salvageable. Waste is bagged, labeled, and disposed of as regulated material, and we follow EPA RRP-compliant work practices on any renovation that disturbs painted surfaces in housing built before 1978. The job isn't closed out until dust clearance testing confirms the space is safe to reoccupy.
