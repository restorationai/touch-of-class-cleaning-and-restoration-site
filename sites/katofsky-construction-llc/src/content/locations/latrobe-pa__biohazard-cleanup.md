## Why Latrobe's Housing Stock Shapes Biohazard Cleanup

A significant portion of Latrobe's residential properties were built before 1960, which means porous materials are common: plaster and lath walls, hardwood subfloors over uninsulated crawl spaces, and older grout lines in tile work. These surfaces absorb biological material differently than modern drywall or luxury vinyl plank. Contamination that appears contained at the surface can wick into underlying layers, making thorough remediation more involved than a visual inspection suggests.

Westmoreland County's humid continental climate adds another layer of complexity. Warm, damp summers accelerate microbial activity, which means a delay of even a day or two in addressing a biohazard situation can allow secondary contamination to take hold in absorbent building materials. Prompt, methodical work is not just a preference here; it is a practical necessity given the region's seasonal conditions.

## Our Biohazard Cleanup Process in Latrobe

Every situation is different, and the process is calibrated to what is actually present rather than a fixed checklist. In general terms, the work moves through these stages:

**Assessment and containment.** The affected area is evaluated to establish the scope of contamination, including surfaces that may not be immediately visible. Containment barriers are set to protect unaffected areas of the home.

**Removal of contaminated materials.** Porous materials that cannot be decontaminated to a safe standard are carefully removed and packaged for disposal in compliance with Pennsylvania Department of Environmental Protection guidelines for regulated medical and biological waste.

**Cleaning and disinfection.** Hospital-grade disinfectants are applied to all affected hard surfaces. In Latrobe's older homes, this step often requires attention to gaps between original hardwood planks, plaster wall seams, and subfloor edges where material can migrate.

**Verification.** The area is inspected before containment is removed. Where applicable, ATP testing or other verification methods confirm that surfaces meet safety thresholds before the space is released.

All work is performed by an IICRC Certified Firm, and crew members follow OSHA Bloodborne Pathogen standards throughout.
