## Fabric vs. Leather: Choosing the Right Method

Bethel Park homes tend to have a mix of both, sometimes on the same piece. Fabric upholstery, whether a tight-woven tweed from the 1970s or a modern performance fabric, responds to hot-water extraction and low-moisture encapsulation depending on construction. Older woven fabrics common in mid-century homes require lower pressure to avoid distorting the weave. Leather and bonded leather are a different category entirely: water-based extraction is off the table, and the wrong solvent can strip a finish permanently. Bonded leather, which became common in furniture sold through the early 2010s, is particularly vulnerable and requires a gentler touch than full-grain hides. Knowing which material you have before any solution touches the surface is the step most DIY attempts skip.
