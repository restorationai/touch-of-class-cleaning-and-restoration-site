## Why North Huntingdon Properties See Lead Paint Issues

Westmoreland County's residential development followed the steel industry's expansion, and many North Huntingdon neighborhoods were built quickly to house working families during that era. That construction timeline places a large portion of local homes squarely in the pre-1978 window when lead paint was standard in residential use. Original woodwork, built-in cabinetry, and multiple layers of paint on older plaster walls are common finds during abatement work in this part of the county.

The region's climate adds another layer of concern. Western Pennsylvania winters are hard on painted surfaces. Repeated freeze-thaw cycles cause wood to expand and contract, which loosens paint at joints, sills, and door frames. By the time a homeowner notices peeling or chalking paint, the deterioration may already be releasing lead dust into the living space. Older double-hung windows are a particular problem: every time the sash slides, it grinds painted surfaces against each other and generates fine particles that settle on sills and floors.

## Our Lead Paint Abatement Process in North Huntingdon

Abatement starts with testing, not assumptions. We use XRF (X-ray fluorescence) analysis and paint chip sampling to confirm which surfaces contain lead and at what concentration. That data drives the scope of work so nothing is over-treated or missed.

Once the scope is confirmed, we establish containment. Plastic sheeting seals doorways, HVAC registers, and any gap that could carry dust to clean areas of the home. Negative air pressure units with HEPA filtration run continuously during the removal phase, pulling air through the work zone and exhausting it safely. Removal methods depend on the substrate: chemical strippers for detailed woodwork, HEPA-vacuum-shrouded power tools for flat surfaces, and hand scraping where mechanical methods would damage the underlying material.

Waste generated during abatement is classified as hazardous and disposed of under applicable Pennsylvania Department of Environmental Protection guidelines. After removal, all surfaces are cleaned using wet-wiping methods and HEPA vacuuming in sequence. The job does not close until clearance testing confirms lead dust levels are within acceptable limits.

## Local Note

In North Huntingdon and the surrounding Westmoreland County townships, many homes from the 1950s and 1960s were built with plaster-over-lath wall systems rather than drywall. When abatement work involves plaster surfaces, the removal process is slower and more deliberate than on modern construction. Plaster is brittle, and aggressive mechanical removal can collapse sections of wall, dramatically increasing the volume of hazardous waste and the time needed for cleanup. Experienced abatement crews working in this part of the county know to assess plaster condition before choosing a removal method, sometimes opting for encapsulation with an approved coating rather than full removal when the plaster is sound and the paint is intact.
