McKeesport sits at the confluence of the Youghiogheny and Monongahela rivers, and that geography shapes more than the skyline. The city's aging combined sewer infrastructure, built largely in the early-to-mid twentieth century to serve a booming steel-era population, still carries both stormwater and sanitary waste in the same pipes. When those lines back up, raw sewage doesn't just flood a basement floor. It soaks into concrete block foundations, wicks into floor joists, and leaves behind bacteria and pathogens that a mop and bleach won't address. Katofsky Construction LLC responds around the clock to sewage backup calls throughout McKeesport, bringing IICRC-certified technicians and commercial-grade extraction and sanitization equipment directly to your door.

## Why McKeesport Properties Experience Sewage Backups

The steel industry shaped McKeesport's housing stock as much as it shaped its economy. Most residential properties in the city were constructed before 1960, which means cast-iron or clay-tile sewer laterals that have had decades to crack, shift, and collect root intrusion. When heavy rain rolls through the Mon Valley, the combined sewer system can surcharge quickly, and that pressure finds the path of least resistance, which is often a floor drain or a basement toilet.

The city's topography adds another layer. McKeesport is not flat. Streets and lots step up and down the hillsides above the rivers, and lower-elevation addresses bear the brunt of surcharge events because gravity works against them. Homes on the lower blocks closer to the Youghiogheny waterfront are particularly vulnerable during periods of sustained rainfall, when the sewer system is already running at capacity before a storm peak arrives.

Older homes in McKeesport also frequently have unfinished or partially finished basements with bare concrete floors and block walls, which are porous. Sewage water doesn't stay on the surface. It moves laterally into the block cells and downward into the subgrade, making thorough extraction and drying a multi-day process rather than a single-afternoon job.

## Our Sewage Cleanup and Sanitization Process in McKeesport

Sewage is classified as Category 3 water loss, the most contaminated category, and every step of the process reflects that. When a crew arrives at a McKeesport property, the first priority is containment. Affected areas are isolated to prevent cross-contamination to living spaces, and any standing sewage is extracted using truck-mounted or portable units capable of handling solid-laden water.

Once standing material is removed, all non-salvageable porous materials are bagged and removed. In the older homes common throughout McKeesport, that often includes sections of wood subfloor, deteriorated drywall at the base of walls, and compromised insulation. What remains is treated with EPA-registered antimicrobial agents applied to all affected surfaces, including concrete, block, and structural framing. This is not a surface wipe-down. Dwell times matter, and the chemistry has to contact every surface the sewage reached.

After sanitization, the drying phase begins using commercial dehumidifiers and air movers calibrated to the size and construction of the space. Moisture readings are logged throughout the process so there is a documented record showing the structure returned to acceptable moisture levels before the job is closed out. That documentation matters for insurance claims and for your own peace of mind.

## McKeesport Insurance Coordination

Sewage backup coverage is not always included in a standard homeowner's policy. Many carriers require a separate sewage backup rider, and whether that rider is in place determines how the claim is handled. The Katofsky team documents the loss thoroughly from arrival, including photographs of affected materials, moisture readings, and a written scope of work, so your adjuster has everything needed to evaluate the claim. If coverage is limited or disputed, that same documentation supports your conversation with the carrier about what the remediation actually required.

## Local Note

McKeesport's hillside lots mean that some basement-level floor drains were installed at elevations where backflow preventers were never added, because the original construction assumed the grade would protect them. That assumption no longer holds in many locations, particularly after decades of ground settlement and infrastructure aging. If your home has experienced even one sewage backup event, it is worth asking a licensed plumber about a backflow preventer on the lateral once the cleanup is complete.
