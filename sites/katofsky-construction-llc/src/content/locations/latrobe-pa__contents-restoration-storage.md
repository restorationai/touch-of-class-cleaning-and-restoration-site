## Why Latrobe Properties See Contents Losses

The Loyalhanna Creek watershed that runs through the Latrobe area creates drainage conditions that push water into low-lying foundations during heavy rain events. Many of the homes in and around Latrobe were constructed between the 1920s and the 1960s, which means stone and block foundations, plaster-and-lath interior walls, and basement storage areas that were never designed with flood resilience in mind. When water gets in, it doesn't stay on the floor. It wicks into wood furniture legs, saturates cardboard boxes, and travels up upholstered pieces faster than most homeowners expect.

Fire losses in older Latrobe-area homes carry their own contents challenge. Coal and oil heat was standard for much of the twentieth century, and homes that have been converted to gas or electric still carry residue in wall cavities and ductwork. A kitchen fire in a house like that can push smoke odor into soft goods and textiles throughout the structure, not just the room where the fire started.

## Our Contents Restoration Process in Latrobe

The first step on any contents job is a room-by-room inventory. Every item is photographed, catalogued, and assessed before anything is moved. This documentation matters for insurance claims and for your own peace of mind during a stressful time.

Items that can be restored are packed out and transported to a climate-controlled storage facility while the structure dries and repairs are completed. That pack-out step protects your belongings from secondary damage, cross-contamination from mold growth, or additional smoke exposure. Items in storage are tracked against your inventory so nothing is misplaced.

Restoration methods depend on the material and the type of loss. Ultrasonic cleaning removes smoke and soot from hard goods, electronics housings, and decorative items without abrasion. Ozone and hydroxyl treatment addresses odor in textiles and soft furnishings. Documents and photographs go through a separate process, including air-drying or freeze-drying depending on how long they've been wet. Once the structure is cleared and repaired, contents are returned, unpacked, and placed.

## Insurance Coordination for Latrobe Claims

Contents losses are often the most contested part of a property insurance claim. Adjusters will want documentation of pre-loss condition, replacement cost values, and evidence that restoration was attempted before replacement was approved. The inventory and photo documentation collected during pack-out serves exactly that purpose. The team works directly with carriers to submit the contents portion of the claim alongside the structural loss, which reduces the back-and-forth that can delay settlements.

Homeowners in Westmoreland County should be aware that contents coverage limits vary significantly by policy. Some policies cover contents at actual cash value, which accounts for depreciation, while others cover replacement cost. Knowing which applies to your policy before the adjuster arrives helps you understand what to expect from the settlement.

## Local Note

Latrobe-area homes built before World War II frequently used horsehair plaster, which behaves very differently from modern drywall when exposed to smoke or water. The plaster itself is dense and slow to release absorbed moisture, but the wooden lath behind it can stay saturated long after the surface reads dry on a moisture meter. During pack-out on these properties, furniture and textiles stored against plaster walls often carry higher moisture content than items stored against exterior walls, because the wall itself has been off-gassing humidity into the room. Knowing this pattern changes how contents are prioritized during the initial assessment.
