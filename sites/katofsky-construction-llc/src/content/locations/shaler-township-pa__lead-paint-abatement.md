## Why Shaler Township Properties See Lead Paint Issues

The Pittsburgh metro's pre-war and postwar building boom produced a dense concentration of homes in Shaler Township that predate the federal ban on residential lead paint in 1978. Many of these properties were built during the 1920s through the 1950s, when lead-based paint was standard for both interior trim and exterior siding because of its durability and adhesion. Decades of repainting over original layers means that lead is often buried under multiple coats, invisible until a chip, a renovation cut, or a friction surface like a window sash starts releasing dust.

The freeze-thaw cycle that western Pennsylvania winters deliver every year accelerates this process on exterior surfaces. Paint expands and contracts with temperature swings, and on older wood siding and porch trim, that cycling creates micro-cracks that allow moisture in and eventually push paint away from the substrate. What starts as a peeling exterior becomes a lead dust hazard at ground level and on the surfaces where children play.

Property transfers also bring lead paint to the front of the conversation. Pennsylvania's disclosure requirements and federal RRP rules mean that sellers, landlords, and contractors working on pre-1978 homes need to understand what they are dealing with before work begins.

## Our Lead Paint Abatement Process in Shaler Township

The process starts with testing, not assumptions. A thorough assessment identifies which surfaces contain lead paint, what condition those surfaces are in, and whether the appropriate response is full removal, encapsulation, or enclosure. Each approach is governed by EPA standards, and the right choice depends on the specific substrate, the planned use of the space, and the condition of the existing paint film.

For abatement work, containment comes first. Work areas are sealed with poly sheeting and negative air pressure is maintained to prevent dust from migrating into clean spaces. HEPA-filtered vacuums and wet methods keep airborne particles to a minimum throughout the work. Waste is bagged, labeled, and disposed of according to applicable regulations.

After the work is complete, clearance testing confirms that lead dust levels meet the standards required before re-occupancy. That final step matters as much as the work itself, and it is not something to skip in the interest of moving faster.

Katofsky Construction holds EPA RRP certification, which is the federal credential required to perform renovation, repair, and painting work in pre-1978 homes and child-occupied facilities. That certification is not optional for this type of work; it is the baseline.
