Shaler Township sits in a river valley corridor north of Pittsburgh where temperature swings between seasons are sharp and basements in older hillside homes collect moisture with little encouragement. When a burst pipe, basement flood, or kitchen fire leaves your belongings soaked, smoke-saturated, or coated in soot, getting those items out of the damaged space quickly is often the difference between salvage and total loss. Contents restoration and storage is a specialized process, and in a community where a large share of the housing stock dates to the mid-twentieth century, the materials inside those homes, wood furniture, wool rugs, framed photographs, upholstered pieces, require careful handling that generic moving and storage companies simply are not equipped to provide.

## Why Shaler Township Homes Present Unique Contents Challenges

The hillside topography throughout Shaler Township means that many homes were built into slopes, with lower levels partially below grade. That construction pattern concentrates water damage risk in finished basements and utility rooms where families often store irreplaceable items: holiday decorations, family records, children's keepsakes. When water intrudes, it does not announce itself at the surface. Flooring can feel dry underfoot while moisture is already wicking into the legs of wooden furniture or the backing of area rugs stored nearby.

Older homes in this part of Allegheny County also tend to have plaster walls and original hardwood floors, both of which off-gas moisture slowly during a loss event. That slow release affects the air quality in the space where your belongings are sitting, accelerating mold colonization on porous items within 24 to 48 hours of initial saturation. Getting contents out of that environment and into a controlled storage facility is not optional if salvage is the goal.

## Our Contents Restoration and Storage Process

The process begins with a room-by-room inventory before anything is moved. Every item is photographed, catalogued, and assigned a condition rating. This documentation serves two purposes: it protects your belongings through the chain of custody, and it gives your insurance adjuster the itemized record they need to process the claim accurately.

From there, items are packed using materials appropriate to their fragility and transported to a secure, climate-controlled facility. Restoration work, which may include ultrasonic cleaning for hard goods, ozone or hydroxyl treatment for smoke odor on soft goods, and specialized drying for water-affected documents or artwork, is performed off-site where temperature and humidity can be precisely managed. Items are returned only after the structure itself has been dried, cleaned, and cleared, so your belongings are not brought back into a space that could re-damage them.

Katofsky Construction is an IICRC Certified Firm with technicians holding Water Restoration Technician and Applied Microbial Remediation Technician credentials, which matters specifically for contents that have been exposed to contaminated water or secondary mold growth. Those credentials govern how affected items are handled, what can be safely restored, and what must be documented as a loss.

The inventory and documentation process described above is built specifically to support an insurance claim: itemized lists, condition photographs, and written assessments of what is restorable versus what must be replaced.
