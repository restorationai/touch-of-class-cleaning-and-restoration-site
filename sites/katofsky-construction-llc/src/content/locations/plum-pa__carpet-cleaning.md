## Why Plum Properties See Carpet Issues

The geology under much of Allegheny County is not kind to carpets. The reddish-brown clay soil common throughout the Plum area bonds to carpet fibers on contact and resists standard vacuuming. Once it dries and sets, it acts as a fine abrasive that shortens carpet life from the backing up. Combine that with the borough's humid summers, when indoor relative humidity can stay elevated for weeks at a stretch, and you have conditions that accelerate both soiling and odor development.

Homes built in the 1970s and 1980s, which make up a large share of Plum's residential stock, were often carpeted wall to wall, including finished basements. Basement carpets in this region face an additional pressure: the water table rises after heavy rain events, and even a home with no visible flooding can experience enough moisture vapor through a concrete slab to keep carpet padding damp for days. That sustained dampness is exactly the window mold needs to establish itself.

## Our Carpet Cleaning Process in Plum

Every job starts with a fiber and backing assessment. Older cut-pile carpets common in 1970s and 1980s construction respond differently to hot-water extraction than modern loop or berber styles, and using the wrong agitation method on aged fibers accelerates wear. We identify the fiber type first, then calibrate water temperature, dwell time, and extraction pressure accordingly.

The core method is truck-mounted hot-water extraction, which pulls soil and cleaning solution back out of the pile rather than leaving residue behind. Residue left in carpet fibers is one of the main reasons carpets re-soil quickly after cleaning, a complaint we hear often from Plum homeowners who have used lower-powered portable equipment in the past. After extraction, we apply a post-treatment rinse to neutralize pH and then measure moisture levels in both the carpet and the padding before we leave. If the pad is holding water, we address it directly rather than leaving it to create an odor problem later.

For stain removal, the approach depends on the stain chemistry. Pet urine, for example, requires an enzyme-based treatment that breaks down uric acid crystals rather than simply masking the odor. Red clay tracked in from a Plum yard needs a different pre-spray than a food-based stain. There is no single product that handles every situation well, and using the wrong one can set a stain permanently.

## Equipment and Methods We Use for Carpet

Truck-mounted extraction units generate significantly more heat and suction than portable machines, which matters for two reasons in this climate: higher water temperature improves soil release from clay-based grime, and stronger suction means carpets dry faster, reducing the window for mold or mildew to develop in the padding.
