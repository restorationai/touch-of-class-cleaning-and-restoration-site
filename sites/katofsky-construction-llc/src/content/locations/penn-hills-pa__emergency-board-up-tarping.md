## Why Penn Hills Properties See Board-Up and Tarping Issues

Much of Penn Hills was built during the postwar housing boom of the late 1940s through the 1960s, and that construction era carries specific vulnerabilities. Roofs on homes from that period often used lighter-gauge framing than current code requires, and decades of freeze-thaw cycling in Allegheny County winters have worked the flashing loose on countless chimneys and dormers. When a fire, windstorm, or fallen limb creates an opening in one of these roofs, the underlying structure is rarely as sound as it looks from the curb. The same era of construction frequently used single-pane windows with wood frames that have softened over time, meaning a broken window from impact or heat exposure leaves a larger, less stable opening than a modern frame would.

Penn Hills also sits in a corridor that sees meaningful ice-storm activity in January and February. Ice accumulation on a partially damaged roof can triple the weight load on rafters that are already compromised, so speed in getting a tarp anchored correctly is not just about keeping the rain out tonight. It is about preventing a secondary structural failure tomorrow.

## Our Emergency Board-Up and Tarping Process in Penn Hills

Every call starts with a quick assessment of the opening type, the roof pitch, and the condition of the surrounding structure. For window and door board-ups, the crew uses exterior-grade plywood cut and fastened to the structural framing rather than the window casing, which is often too deteriorated on older Penn Hills homes to hold fasteners under wind load. Panels are secured with screws rather than nails so they can be removed cleanly when reconstruction begins.

For roof tarping, the pitch and the ridge configuration matter. Many Penn Hills homes have complex rooflines with dormers and valleys, and a tarp that is not wrapped over the ridge and weighted properly on the far slope will lift in the first gust off the Allegheny ridgeline. The crew uses ballasted anchor boards rather than staples alone, and tarps are overlapped at seams by at least eighteen inches to prevent channeling water directly into the opening they are supposed to protect.

Fire damage board-ups require an additional check: heat can compromise the structural integrity of the framing around the opening, and a panel fastened to charred wood gives false security. The crew probes the surrounding framing before anchoring and, where the framing is too damaged to hold, builds a freestanding brace system inside the opening.

## Local Note

Older Penn Hills homes with hip roofs and deep overhangs present a specific tarping challenge that does not come up as often in newer construction. The overhang creates a gap between the tarp edge and the fascia that acts like a scoop in wind-driven rain, pulling water up and under the tarp rather than shedding it away. The fix is to run the tarp down past the drip edge and fold it back up against the fascia board, then secure it there, rather than letting it hang free. It is a small adjustment, but it is the difference between a tarp that holds through a three-day rain event and one that fails by morning.
