## Why Monroeville Properties See Lead Paint Issues

Monroeville sits in a part of Allegheny County where residential development happened in waves. The oldest sections of the borough contain homes built before the mid-century boom, and those properties frequently retain their original painted millwork, plaster walls, and steel-framed windows. Lead paint does not become a hazard simply by existing on a wall, but it becomes one quickly when surfaces are disturbed: a contractor grinding a window frame, a child scraping a painted railing, or moisture causing paint to bubble and peel. Western Pennsylvania's seasonal humidity swings accelerate that deterioration. Summers bring warm, damp air that works into painted wood; winters dry everything out sharply. That repeated expansion and contraction is exactly the cycle that causes lead paint to crack and chalk.

Rental properties in Monroeville face additional pressure. Pennsylvania's landlord-tenant law and federal rules under the Residential Lead-Based Paint Hazard Reduction Act require disclosure and, in some cases, remediation before a unit can be legally leased to families with young children. Property managers who skip lead testing before a renovation can find themselves liable for violations that dwarf the cost of abatement.

## Our Lead Paint Abatement Process in Monroeville

Every project starts with testing, not assumptions. We use XRF (X-ray fluorescence) analysis and paint chip sampling to identify which surfaces contain lead and at what concentration. That data determines the abatement strategy: full removal, encapsulation, or enclosure, depending on the surface condition, the planned renovation scope, and the occupancy situation.

For removal work, the affected area is sealed with poly sheeting and negative air pressure is established before any scraping or sanding begins. Workers wear full PPE and follow EPA RRP protocols throughout. Waste is containerized, labeled, and transported to a licensed disposal facility. After the work area is cleaned, a clearance inspection using wipe sampling confirms that lead dust levels meet federal thresholds before the space is re-occupied. We do not hand a homeowner the keys back based on a visual check alone.

For Monroeville properties where full removal is not practical, such as intact painted plaster in a historic interior, encapsulation with an EPA-approved coating can be an appropriate alternative. We document the method and location so future contractors know what is behind the surface.

## Local Note

One pattern we see in older Monroeville-area homes is that lead paint is often heaviest on friction surfaces: the edges of double-hung windows and the jambs of interior doors that have been painted over repeatedly across decades. These are also the surfaces that generate the most dust during normal use, which is why a visual inspection that finds intact paint can still miss an active exposure risk. If a home has original wood windows and the sashes stick or drag, that friction is producing fine lead dust every time the window is opened. It is worth having those surfaces tested specifically, even if the rest of the painted surfaces appear stable.
