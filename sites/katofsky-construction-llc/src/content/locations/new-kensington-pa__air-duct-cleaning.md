That constant demand, combined with the area's substantial stock of mid-century construction, creates conditions where dust, debris, and biological growth accumulate inside ductwork faster than most homeowners expect. ## Why New Kensington Homes See Duct Buildup Faster Than Newer Construction

A significant portion of New Kensington's residential housing was built between the 1940s and 1970s, when duct design standards were looser and galvanized steel was the dominant material. Older galvanized ducts develop surface corrosion over time, and that rough interior texture traps dust, pet dander, and insulation fibers far more readily than the smooth sheet metal or flex duct used in newer builds. Homes that were converted from oil or coal heat to forced-air gas systems often have duct runs that were retrofitted rather than designed from scratch, which can mean irregular sizing, sharp bends, and seams that were never properly sealed.

The valley geography also plays a role. New Kensington and the surrounding Alle-Kiski Valley corridor experiences temperature inversions that trap particulates close to ground level during winter months. Outdoor air quality events like these push more fine particles through return-air intakes, which accelerates the rate at which filters and ductwork load up with debris.

## Our Air Duct Cleaning Process in New Kensington

Every job starts with a visual inspection of the supply and return registers, the air handler, and accessible duct sections. For older homes common to this area, we pay close attention to duct connections at the plenum and any flex duct transitions, since those joints are the most likely places for gaps that pull in unconditioned air and the particulates that come with it.

Cleaning is performed using negative-air pressure equipment: a high-powered vacuum unit creates suction at the main trunk line while technicians work register by register with rotary brush tools and compressed air whips to dislodge buildup along the duct walls. The debris travels toward the vacuum rather than back into the living space. Supply ducts, return ducts, and the air handler cabinet are all addressed as part of the same service visit.

If dryer vent cleaning is needed, that is handled separately but can be scheduled on the same day. A clogged dryer vent is a fire risk independent of your HVAC system, and the lint accumulation in older homes with long or kinked vent runs can be substantial.

Where sanitization is requested, we apply an EPA-registered antimicrobial agent to the interior duct surfaces after cleaning. This step is particularly relevant for homes that have experienced any moisture intrusion near the air handler or supply plenum.
