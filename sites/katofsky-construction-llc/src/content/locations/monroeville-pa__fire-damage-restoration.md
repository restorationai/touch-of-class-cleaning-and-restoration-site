## Why Monroeville Properties Are Particularly Vulnerable After a Fire

Monroeville's housing stock skews heavily toward construction from the 1950s through the 1970s, the decades when the borough grew rapidly alongside the Parkway East corridor. Homes from that era were built with materials that respond to fire and smoke differently than modern construction: plaster-and-lath walls, solid-wood framing, and asbestos-containing insulation in some older utility spaces. After a fire, smoke oils penetrate plaster much more deeply than they do drywall, and the alkaline chemistry of older mortar can actually trap soot particles in ways that make surface cleaning insufficient. A thorough fire and smoke restoration in these homes requires testing the substrate before choosing a cleaning protocol, not just wiping visible residue and repainting.

The climate adds another layer. Western Pennsylvania winters are cold and damp, and a fire that compromises a roof or exterior wall in January exposes the interior to freeze-thaw cycles almost immediately. Water used in suppression can freeze in wall cavities overnight, expanding and cracking framing that survived the fire itself. Addressing that moisture before it freezes is part of responsible post-fire restoration here in ways that matter less in drier climates.

## Our Fire Damage Restoration Process in Monroeville

The first step on any fire loss is a thorough scope of the damage, including areas that look unaffected. Smoke migrates through every gap in a structure, and what reads as a kitchen fire on the surface can mean contaminated insulation in the attic and soot-coated ductwork throughout the house. Our IICRC-certified team documents conditions room by room, photographs all affected materials, and establishes a drying and decontamination baseline before any cleaning begins.

From there, the process moves through debris removal, structural drying of any water introduced by suppression, dry and wet chemical sponge cleaning calibrated to the surface type, thermal fogging or hydroxyl treatment for odor neutralization, and encapsulation or replacement of materials that cannot be cleaned to a safe standard. Every step is documented for your insurance carrier. We bill most major carriers directly, so you are not managing two separate conversations while your home is uninhabitable.

## Local Note

Older Monroeville homes built on the hillside lots off of William Penn Highway and similar terrain often have partial basements or crawl spaces that collect suppression water and are difficult to ventilate. In flat-lot construction, that water drains or evaporates relatively quickly. On a sloped lot with a low-clearance crawl, it can sit for days, creating a secondary mold risk on top of the fire damage. When we scope a fire loss in this part of Allegheny County, we always inspect below-grade and under-floor spaces specifically, because that is where slow-developing problems hide after the obvious damage is already being addressed.
