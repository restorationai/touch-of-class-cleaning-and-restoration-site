## Why McKeesport's Older Building Stock Shapes Every Renovation

Homes built during McKeesport's industrial peak frequently feature knob-and-tube wiring tucked inside plaster-and-lath walls, cast-iron drain stacks, and balloon-frame construction that transfers heat, cold, and moisture between floors in ways modern platform framing does not. Opening a wall for a bathroom remodel in a 1920s or 1930s home here is rarely a straight-line project. Asbestos-containing materials in floor tiles, pipe insulation, and ceiling texture were standard through the mid-1970s, so any renovation that disturbs those layers requires proper handling before finish work begins.

The climate adds its own pressure. Southwestern Pennsylvania winters are wet and variable, and McKeesport's position along the Monongahela River means freeze-thaw cycles hit hard on masonry foundations and exterior surfaces. Brick pointing deteriorates faster here than in drier climates, and that deterioration lets moisture into wall cavities that then shows up as a remodeling complication rather than a straightforward weatherproofing job.
