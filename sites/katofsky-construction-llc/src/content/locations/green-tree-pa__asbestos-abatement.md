## Why Green Tree Properties Face Elevated Asbestos Risk

The housing stock throughout Green Tree and the surrounding communities along the Parkway West corridor reflects decades of construction practices that routinely incorporated asbestos. Popcorn ceilings applied through the 1970s commonly contained chrysotile asbestos fibers. Vinyl floor tiles and the black mastic adhesive beneath them, especially in basements and kitchens of homes built before 1980, are among the most frequently confirmed asbestos-containing materials found during pre-renovation inspections in this part of Allegheny County.

Pipe wrap insulation on older boiler systems, duct tape on HVAC connections, and textured joint compound on drywall seams are additional sources that surface during gut renovations. In Green Tree's older split-levels and ranch-style homes, the mechanical rooms and crawl spaces where these materials concentrate are often cramped, which makes disturbance during routine maintenance more likely than homeowners realize. Asbestos fibers are not visible to the naked eye, and materials that look intact can still shed fibers when cut, drilled, or scraped.

## Our Asbestos Abatement Process in Green Tree

Every project starts with a thorough inspection and, where suspect materials are present, bulk sampling sent to an accredited laboratory. Testing results determine whether abatement is required and which materials need to be addressed before any demolition or renovation work begins. This step protects you, your contractor, and any future occupants.

Once abatement is confirmed, the work area is isolated with polyethylene sheeting and placed under negative air pressure using HEPA-filtered air scrubbers. Workers wear full personal protective equipment throughout the removal process. Asbestos-containing materials are wetted to suppress fiber release, carefully removed, and double-bagged in labeled, sealed containers that meet Pennsylvania Department of Environmental Protection disposal requirements. Final air clearance testing confirms that fiber levels are within acceptable limits before containment is removed and the space is released for renovation.

Pennsylvania DEP regulations govern asbestos abatement projects, and Allegheny County properties are subject to notification requirements before certain project types begin. Katofsky Construction handles the regulatory coordination so that your project timeline is not disrupted by paperwork gaps.

## Local Note

One pattern that comes up repeatedly in Green Tree's postwar ranch homes is asbestos-containing floor tile installed directly over a concrete slab, with a second layer of vinyl flooring glued on top of it. Homeowners often do not know the original tile exists until a flooring contractor lifts the newer material. In Allegheny County, this layered situation requires testing both the tile and the adhesive separately, because each can have a different asbestos content. Skipping that step and assuming the top layer is the only concern is a mistake that can turn a straightforward flooring project into a regulatory problem. If your Green Tree home was built before 1980 and still has its original flooring or has had flooring added over the original, a pre-renovation inspection is worth scheduling before any demo begins.
