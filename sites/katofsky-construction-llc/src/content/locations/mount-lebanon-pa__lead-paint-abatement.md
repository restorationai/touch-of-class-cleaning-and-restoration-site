## Why Mount Lebanon Properties See Lead Paint Issues

Mount Lebanon was largely built out between the 1920s and the 1960s, a period when lead-based paint was applied to virtually every interior and exterior surface: window sashes, door frames, porch ceilings, radiator enclosures, and baseboards. The borough's characteristic brick and frame construction means lead paint often exists in layers, with newer coats applied directly over original lead-bearing surfaces rather than stripped and replaced.

The climate adds pressure. Pittsburgh-area winters bring freeze-thaw cycles that cause wood trim to expand and contract repeatedly, which accelerates paint film failure. By spring, what looked like a stable painted surface in October may be chalking, flaking, or peeling along window sills and exterior soffits. That deterioration is exactly the condition that creates the highest exposure risk, particularly for children under six and pregnant women.

Mount Lebanon's housing density also matters. Many properties sit on modest lots with close setbacks, meaning exterior abatement work requires careful containment to prevent lead dust from migrating to neighboring yards or shared driveways.

## Our Lead Paint Abatement Process in Mount Lebanon

Every project begins with lead paint testing to confirm the presence and concentration of lead-bearing materials before any work begins. We use XRF (X-ray fluorescence) analysis and paint chip sampling to map affected surfaces accurately, so the scope of work is defined by evidence, not assumption.

Once testing is complete, we establish full containment: plastic sheeting over floors and HVAC registers, negative air pressure where interior work is involved, and perimeter barriers for exterior projects. Removal methods are selected based on surface type and condition. Wet scraping and chemical stripping minimize airborne dust; HEPA-filtered power tools are used where mechanical removal is necessary. All debris is bagged, labeled, and disposed of as regulated waste through licensed haulers.

Post-abatement, surfaces are cleaned using HEPA vacuuming and wet-wiping protocols. Clearance testing follows to confirm that lead dust levels meet federal standards before containment is removed and the space is returned to normal use. We document every step, which matters both for permit records and for any future real estate transaction.
