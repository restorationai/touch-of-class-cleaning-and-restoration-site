## Why Hempfield Township Homes Present Specific Fire Damage Challenges

Westmoreland County's housing stock tells the story of postwar Pennsylvania growth. A significant portion of Hempfield Township's residential properties were built between the late 1940s and the 1970s, which means original wood-framed construction, older insulation materials, and in some cases knob-and-tube or early aluminum wiring that can be a contributing factor in residential fires. When fire moves through a home built in that era, it often travels inside wall cavities and along balloon-frame channels in ways that modern platform-frame construction resists. That hidden char and smoke penetration is not always visible from the surface.

The climate adds another layer. Western Pennsylvania winters bring sustained cold and high humidity that slow the off-gassing of smoke residue and make structural materials hold odor longer than they would in a drier climate. Soot that settles into porous brick, original hardwood flooring, or plaster walls during a fire becomes harder to neutralize once it has been through a freeze-thaw cycle. Getting a professional assessment on the structure quickly, before a cold snap sets in, matters more here than in many other regions.

## Our Fire Damage Restoration Process in Hempfield Township

The first priority after a fire is stabilizing the structure. That means boarding or tarping openings, assessing load-bearing elements for heat damage, and establishing which areas are safe to enter. From there, the work moves in a deliberate sequence rather than all at once.

Dry smoke residue and wet smoke residue require different cleaning chemistry. Dry smoke from fast-burning fires leaves a powdery, easier-to-brush residue; wet smoke from slow, smoldering fires leaves a thick, oily film that bonds to surfaces and carries a sharp, persistent odor. Older Hempfield Township homes with plaster walls and original woodwork are particularly vulnerable to wet smoke penetration because those porous surfaces absorb residue deeply. Our IICRC-certified technicians identify the smoke type in each room and select the appropriate cleaning agents and methods before touching anything.

Odor control follows cleaning. Thermal fogging and hydroxyl generation are the primary tools for neutralizing smoke odor in the structure itself. Ductwork in older homes often needs separate attention because HVAC systems pull smoke through the entire house during a fire event, depositing residue in places that are easy to overlook.

## Local Note: Older Framing and Hidden Smoke Paths

In the mid-century neighborhoods that make up a large share of Hempfield Township's residential areas, balloon-frame construction is more common than most homeowners realize. Unlike platform framing, balloon framing runs wall studs continuously from the foundation sill to the roof, creating open vertical channels inside the walls. During a fire, smoke and heat travel through those channels from the basement to the attic without passing through any horizontal fire stop. After the fire is out, residue and odor can be present in the attic even when the fire itself was confined to a lower floor. Any thorough fire damage assessment in a Hempfield Township home of that era needs to include the attic and the wall cavities, not just the rooms where visible burning occurred.
