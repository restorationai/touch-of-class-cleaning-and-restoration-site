## Why Murrysville Properties See Distinct Fire Damage Challenges

Murrysville sits in Westmoreland County where the housing stock spans a wide range of construction eras. Many homes in the area were built with older framing techniques, plaster-and-lath interior walls, and original hardwood subfloors that absorb smoke odor and suppression water very differently than modern OSB and drywall construction. When a fire burns in a home with plaster walls, the porous surface traps soot particles deep in the material. Wiping the face of the wall may look like progress, but the odor compounds are still locked inside the substrate.

The region's climate adds another layer. Western Pennsylvania winters are wet and cold, and a fire that happens between November and March leaves a structure open to freezing temperatures during the board-up and initial stabilization phase. Moisture from suppression hoses can freeze inside wall cavities before drying equipment is fully deployed, which complicates the structural drying timeline significantly.

## Our Fire Damage Restoration Process in Murrysville

The first step after a fire is stabilization: boarding openings, tarping the roof if the structure was breached, and securing the property. From there, the work moves through a deliberate sequence.

**Damage assessment and documentation.** Every affected surface is catalogued before anything is removed. This documentation supports the insurance claim and establishes a baseline for what needs to be restored versus replaced.

**Soot and smoke residue removal.** Different fire types produce different residues. A kitchen grease fire leaves a wet, sticky soot that smears if treated incorrectly. A structural fire burning drywall and insulation produces a dry, powdery residue that becomes airborne during cleanup if not managed carefully. The cleaning method is matched to the residue type, not applied uniformly across the whole structure.

**Odor neutralization.** Thermal fogging and hydroxyl generation reach into the same spaces the smoke did, including inside HVAC ductwork, wall cavities, and attic framing. Surface cleaning alone does not eliminate fire odor.

**Structural drying.** Suppression water is treated the same way as any water intrusion event, following the IICRC S500 standard for drying protocols. Moisture readings are taken throughout the process and documented until the structure reaches acceptable levels.

**Content handling.** Salvageable contents are inventoried, packed out, and cleaned off-site when the structure itself is being worked on.

## Local Note

Homes in the Murrysville area built during the mid-twentieth century frequently have balloon-frame construction rather than the platform framing used in newer builds. In a balloon-frame home, wall cavities run continuously from the foundation sill to the roof rafters without the fire stops that platform framing provides. That means smoke and heat travel vertically through the entire wall system during a fire, and the odor and residue end up in attic spaces even when the fire itself was contained to a lower floor. Restoration scopes that do not account for this construction type often miss significant contamination in the attic framing and insulation.

If your home in Murrysville was built before roughly 1960, it is worth asking specifically about attic inspection during the initial assessment.
