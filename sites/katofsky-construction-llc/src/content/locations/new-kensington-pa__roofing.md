## Why New Kensington Roofs Wear Out Faster Than You'd Expect

The Allegheny Valley's climate is genuinely hard on roofing systems. Temperatures in the New Kensington area can swing 40 degrees in a single day during late winter, and that expansion-and-contraction cycle works at every nail hole, flashing joint, and ridge cap. Ice dams form when attic heat escapes through older, under-insulated decking, melts snow at the peak, and refreezes at the cold eaves. The water that backs up underneath shingles doesn't announce itself until it's already inside the wall cavity.

Much of the housing stock in New Kensington dates to the mill-era building boom of the early-to-mid twentieth century. Those homes often have steeper roof pitches designed to shed snow, but the original sheathing, underlayment, and ventilation details were never meant to last a century. When replacement time comes, the deck itself frequently needs attention before a single shingle goes down.

## Our Roofing Installation and Replacement Process in New Kensington

Every project starts with a full deck inspection, not just a shingle count. On older New Kensington homes, we pull back damaged sections to check for soft spots, rot, and deteriorated skip sheathing before committing to a material spec. What looks like a straightforward shingle replacement from the street can reveal a deck that needs partial or full replacement once the old roofing comes off.

Once the deck is confirmed sound or repaired, we install a synthetic underlayment rated for the temperature range this region actually sees. Flashing at valleys, chimneys, and dormers gets particular attention because those are the failure points that show up in Allegheny Valley homes after hard winters. Ridge ventilation is sized to match the attic volume, which matters for preventing the ice dam cycle from starting over under a new roof.

Material selection is a conversation, not a default. Architectural shingles with a higher impact rating hold up better against the hail that moves through the valley in spring and early summer. Metal roofing is increasingly popular on the steeper-pitched homes throughout the area because it sheds snow cleanly and carries a longer service life. We walk through the trade-offs in plain terms so you can make a decision that fits both the house and the budget.

## New Kensington Insurance Coordination

Storm damage claims are common in Westmoreland County, and navigating them takes documentation that adjusters will actually accept. We photograph the full roof surface, note hail strike patterns, identify wind-lifted sections, and produce a written scope that aligns with the carrier's line-item format. That documentation matters because it is the difference between a claim that covers a full replacement and one that gets reduced to a patch allowance.

If your carrier sends an independent adjuster, we are available to walk the roof with them and answer technical questions directly. Homeowners should not have to translate between their contractor and their insurance company.
