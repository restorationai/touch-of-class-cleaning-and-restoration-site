## Why Greensburg Homes Face Particular Challenges for Contents

Westmoreland County's older housing stock is a real factor here. Much of Greensburg's residential construction dates to the mid-twentieth century or earlier, which means homes with plaster-and-lath walls, original hardwood floors, and furniture that has been in families for generations. When water or fire damage occurs in these homes, the contents inside carry both financial and sentimental weight that mass-produced replacements simply can't match.

The region's climate adds pressure to the timeline. Greensburg averages meaningful freeze-thaw cycling through late winter and early spring, which stresses older plumbing and increases the risk of pipe failures. When a pipe lets go in February, the combination of cold ambient air and standing water creates conditions where mold colonization can begin within 24 to 48 hours on porous materials like fabric, paper, and unfinished wood. Getting contents out of the affected space quickly is not a courtesy; it is a preservation decision.

Soot from house fires behaves differently in older construction as well. Plaster walls and wood-framed cavities hold odor compounds in ways that drywall does not, and smoke residue migrates into closets, drawers, and upholstery far faster than most homeowners expect. Contents that look unaffected on the surface may carry embedded odor that only becomes obvious days later.

## Our Contents Restoration Process in Greensburg

The process begins with a detailed inventory before anything leaves the property. Every item is photographed, catalogued, and assigned a condition rating. This documentation matters for insurance purposes and gives you a clear record of what was taken, where it is, and what condition it was in when it left your home.

From there, contents are transported to a controlled facility for cleaning and restoration. Depending on what the damage is, that work may involve ultrasonic cleaning for hard surfaces and non-porous items, ozone or hydroxyl treatment for odor, or specialized fabric and upholstery cleaning for soft goods. Electronics, documents, and artwork each require different handling, and items that are restorable are separated from those that are not before any cleaning begins.

Storage is climate-controlled and secured throughout the process. Once your home is dried, repaired, and ready, contents are returned and placed back in the rooms where they belong. The pack-out and pack-back are coordinated to minimize disruption to the rest of your recovery timeline.

## Working with Your Insurance Carrier

Contents restoration claims in Westmoreland County follow the same general process as elsewhere in Pennsylvania, but the documentation requirements matter. Carriers want itemized inventories, condition photos, and cleaning or replacement cost estimates before they authorize payment. The inventory and photo documentation Katofsky produces at pack-out is built specifically to support that process, reducing back-and-forth with adjusters and keeping your claim moving.

If your policy includes actual cash value rather than replacement cost coverage, the condition ratings assigned during inventory become especially important. Thorough documentation of pre-loss condition can make a meaningful difference in what the carrier authorizes.

## Local Note

Older homes in and around Greensburg frequently have basement storage areas with stone or block foundation walls rather than poured concrete. These spaces absorb and release moisture seasonally, and contents stored there, including furniture, holiday items, and archived documents, are often the first things damaged when a sump pump fails or a foundation crack lets groundwater in. If you have items stored in a below-grade space and you're dealing with water intrusion, let the crew know when you call. Items in contact with a wet masonry floor require different handling than those on upper levels, and getting that information early helps prioritize the pack-out sequence.
