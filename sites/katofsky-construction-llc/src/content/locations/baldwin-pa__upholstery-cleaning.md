## Our Upholstery Cleaning Process in Baldwin

Every piece of furniture is different, and the cleaning method has to match the material. Before any moisture is introduced, the fabric or leather is inspected for colorfastness, fiber type, and existing wear. A pre-treatment solution is applied to loosen bonded soils, calibrated to the specific fabric construction, synthetic microfiber requires a different dwell time than a natural-fiber linen blend, and leather needs conditioning chemistry rather than a detergent-based approach.

For fabric upholstery, hot-water extraction pulls suspended soils and pre-treatment residue out of the fill and backing. The extraction pressure and temperature are set conservatively for older or more delicate pieces, aggressive extraction on a worn fabric can distort seams or cause shrinkage. For leather, the process shifts to low-moisture cleaning followed by a pH-balanced conditioner that restores suppleness without leaving a greasy surface film.

Drying time in Baldwin's shoulder seasons, particularly October through March, runs longer than in drier climates. Air movers are positioned to accelerate evaporation, and in humid conditions, a dehumidifier may be used in the room to bring ambient moisture levels down enough for the piece to dry fully before it's put back into use.

## Equipment and Methods for Upholstery Cleaning

The truck-mounted extraction unit used for carpet cleaning is not appropriate for most upholstery, the suction and heat levels are calibrated for floor installations, not furniture fills. Upholstery work uses portable, lower-pressure extraction equipment with upholstery-specific wand attachments that control water delivery precisely. This matters particularly for cushion cores: overly wet extraction saturates foam fill, which can take days to dry and may develop an odor problem of its own if not managed correctly.

For pieces with visible staining from pet accidents, food, or water marks, targeted spot treatment is applied before the overall clean. Enzyme-based treatments are used for organic staining; tannin treatments address coffee, tea, and wine. The goal is to address the stain chemistry specifically rather than masking it with fragrance or surface brighteners.
