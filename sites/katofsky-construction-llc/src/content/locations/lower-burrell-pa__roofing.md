Lower Burrell sits in a river valley where Westmoreland County's weather patterns hit hard: cold snaps arrive early, freeze-thaw cycles chew through flashing and shingles faster than in flatter terrain, and the hillside lots common throughout the city create drainage challenges that a flat-country roofer might not anticipate. When a roof reaches the end of its service life here, the consequences show up quickly, and a replacement done without accounting for local slope and ventilation conditions often fails ahead of schedule. Katofsky Construction LLC works in Lower Burrell regularly and understands what this area's roofs actually face.

## Why Lower Burrell Roofs Wear Out Faster Than Expected

The Allegheny Valley corridor, which Lower Burrell borders along the Allegheny River, channels cold air and moisture in ways that accelerate shingle degradation. Asphalt shingles lose granule coverage faster when they cycle through freezing and thawing repeatedly across a single winter, and the steep pitches common on the hillside properties throughout the city concentrate ice damming at eaves and valleys. Ice dams form when heat escapes through an under-insulated attic, melts snow at the ridge, and refreezes at the cold overhang, forcing water under shingles and into the decking below.

Many homes in Lower Burrell were built during the postwar decades when the steel industry was still drawing workers to the Kittanning Strip corridor. That era of construction typically used board sheathing rather than plywood or OSB, and original ventilation designs did not meet current standards. When a roof replacement is planned on a home from that period, the decking condition and attic airflow have to be assessed before any new material goes down, not after.

## Our Roofing Installation and Replacement Process in Lower Burrell

Every project starts with a full inspection of the existing roof system: sheathing, ridge, valleys, flashing at penetrations, and the attic below. On hillside lots, we also look at how water sheds off the property, because a new roof that drains correctly onto a compromised gutter system or toward a foundation still creates problems.

Once the scope is clear, we remove existing material down to the deck, inspect and replace any rotted or compromised sheathing, and install a code-compliant underlayment before any finish material goes on. In this part of Westmoreland County, proper ice-and-water shield coverage at eaves and valleys is not optional, it is the difference between a roof that holds through a hard winter and one that leaks by February. Ridge ventilation is sized to match intake ventilation so the attic breathes correctly year-round, which extends shingle life and reduces energy costs.

Material selection depends on the home's pitch, the neighborhood's aesthetic, and the homeowner's budget. Architectural asphalt shingles are the most common choice in Lower Burrell because they perform well in the local climate and carry manufacturer warranties that hold up in freeze-thaw conditions. We walk through options with each homeowner before any material is ordered.

## Coordinating with Insurance After Storm Damage

Hail and wind events move through the Allegheny Valley regularly, and a significant storm can affect entire blocks at once. If your roof replacement is driven by storm damage rather than age, the documentation process matters as much as the installation itself. Katofsky Construction LLC photographs and records damage systematically so your adjuster has the evidence needed to process the claim accurately. We communicate directly with carriers throughout the project and can provide supplemental documentation if the initial scope is revised once the old roof is removed and the deck is visible.
