## Why Mount Lebanon Properties See Elevated Contents Damage

The borough sits in a part of Allegheny County where cold, wet winters and periodic freeze-thaw cycles put real stress on older plumbing. Cast-iron supply lines and galvanized drain stacks that were standard in mid-century construction are now well past their design life in many homes. When those pipes fail, water spreads fast across hardwood floors and soaks into the kind of dense plaster-and-lath assemblies that hold moisture far longer than modern drywall. That extended saturation window gives water more time to reach furniture, cabinetry, books, artwork, and clothing before anyone can intervene.

Fire losses carry a parallel problem. The tight, compartmentalized floor plans common in Mount Lebanon's brick colonials and cape cods allow smoke and soot to travel through wall cavities and settle on contents in rooms that never saw a flame. Items that look untouched can carry odor and residue that will only worsen if they sit in a damaged structure while repairs are underway.

## Our Contents Restoration Process in Mount Lebanon

The first step on any job is a room-by-room inventory. Every item is photographed, catalogued, and assessed before anything is moved. This documentation matters both for your insurance claim and for ensuring that nothing is misplaced during the restoration process.

Items that can be restored are sorted from those that cannot. Salvageable contents are packed out and transported to a climate-controlled facility where they are processed using methods matched to the material: ultrasonic cleaning for hard goods, ozone and hydroxyl treatment for odor, and specialized laundering for soft contents like drapes, clothing, and upholstery. Delicate items, including antiques and artwork that are common in older Mount Lebanon homes, are handled separately with appropriate care for their age and fragility.

Storage is maintained in a secure, climate-controlled environment for as long as the restoration at your property requires. Items are returned only when the structure is ready, so nothing goes back into a space that still has elevated humidity or active repairs.

Contents restoration claims involve more documentation than most homeowners expect.
