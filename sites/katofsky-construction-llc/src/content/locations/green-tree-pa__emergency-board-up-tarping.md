## Why Green Tree Properties Face Distinct Board-Up and Tarping Needs

Much of Green Tree's housing stock dates to the mid-twentieth century, when brick veneer over wood-frame construction was the standard. That combination holds up well over decades, but it creates specific vulnerabilities after a damaging event. Brick facades can mask cracked lintels above window openings, meaning a board-up crew that doesn't account for the masonry condition risks anchoring panels into compromised material. Similarly, older roof decks in this area are frequently skip-sheathed boards rather than plywood, so a tarp installation requires careful attachment planning to avoid pulling fasteners through deteriorated wood.

The borough's position in the river valley also means it catches weather systems that stall against the surrounding ridgelines. Heavy rain events that move through Allegheny County can linger over communities like Green Tree longer than forecast models suggest, making a properly weighted and sealed tarp the difference between a dry interior and a soaked subfloor.

## Our Emergency Board-Up and Tarping Process in Green Tree

When a call comes in, the first step is a rapid site assessment to identify every opening: fire-damaged roof sections, blown-out windows, compromised doors, and any wall penetrations left by firefighting crews or structural failure. Nothing gets boarded or tarped until the full scope is mapped, because a missed opening at the rear of a structure can let in as much water as the primary breach.

For window and door board-up, panels are cut to fit the rough opening and secured with structural screws into the surrounding framing, not just the window trim. On older Green Tree homes where trim profiles are ornate and worth preserving, the crew uses standoff blocking to protect the millwork while still achieving a secure closure.

Roof tarping follows a similar logic. Tarps are run past the ridge when possible so water sheds to both sides rather than pooling at the peak. Ballast boards are screwed through the tarp into the deck at regular intervals, and edges are wrapped and fastened rather than left to flap. A tarp that lifts in a valley windstorm is not a secured structure.

## Insurance Coordination After Emergency Securing

Most homeowner policies cover emergency board-up and tarping as a mitigation expense, meaning the cost of securing the structure is separate from, and does not reduce, the dwelling coverage available for actual repairs. Katofsky documents the damage with photographs before any panel or tarp goes up, producing a timestamped record that supports the insurance claim. If a carrier's adjuster needs to inspect before permanent repairs begin, the documentation package makes that conversation straightforward. The crew can also note pre-existing conditions that are unrelated to the covered loss, which protects the homeowner from disputes later.
