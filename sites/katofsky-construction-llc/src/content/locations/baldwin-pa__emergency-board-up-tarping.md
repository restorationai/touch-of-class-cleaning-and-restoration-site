## Why Baldwin Properties See Board-Up and Tarping Needs

Much of Baldwin's housing stock dates from the postwar building boom of the late 1940s through the 1960s, when brick-veneer and frame construction went up quickly on the borough's rolling lots. That era of construction means roof decks are often board-sheathed rather than plywood-paneled, and original single-pane windows are still common in older sections. When a branch punches through a board-sheathed roof or a fire vents a window frame, the opening is irregular and harder to seal cleanly than a modern OSB deck. Wind-driven rain can soak attic insulation within hours, and Allegheny County winters give it no time to dry on its own.

Baldwin also sits in a zone where lake-effect moisture from the west combines with cold air funneling down river valleys, making late-fall and winter storms particularly damaging. Ice damming along eave lines is a recurring problem on the steeper-pitched roofs common here, and when an ice dam backs water under shingles, the interior damage can appear days after the storm itself.

## Our Emergency Board-Up and Tarping Process in Baldwin

When you call (412) 304-9284, the first conversation is about what happened and what is currently exposed. That information lets the crew arrive with the right materials rather than making a second trip.

On site, the process moves in a deliberate sequence:

1. **Hazard assessment** before any work begins. Fire-damaged structures may have compromised framing or live utilities; broken-glass openings need clearing before boarding.
2. **Measurement and cutting** of plywood panels to fit the actual opening. On Baldwin's older homes, window and door openings are often non-standard sizes that require field cuts rather than stock panels.
3. **Secure fastening** into sound framing, not just the damaged surround. On brick-veneer exteriors common in the borough, that sometimes means drilling into the masonry block behind the veneer for anchor points.
4. **Roof tarping** uses heavy-duty polyethylene sheeting weighted and fastened at the ridge and eaves, with attention to the roof pitch. Baldwin's steeper lots mean steeper roofs, and a tarp that shifts on a 9-in-12 pitch in a wind gust provides no protection at all.
5. **Documentation** of every opening covered, with photographs, for your insurance claim.
