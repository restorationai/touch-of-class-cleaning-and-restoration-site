## Why Hempfield Township Properties See Asbestos Concerns

Westmoreland County's housing stock skews older than many suburban rings around Pittsburgh. Hempfield Township in particular grew quickly during the 1950s through the 1970s, when asbestos was a standard additive in building products across the industry. Homes built during that window commonly contain asbestos in roofing shingles, drywall joint compound, vinyl floor tile, duct insulation, and the spray-applied texture on ceilings that later got called "popcorn." The township's colder winters accelerate freeze-thaw cycling on older pipe insulation, which can cause that insulation to crack and shed fibers into crawl spaces and mechanical rooms. Renovation projects, even minor ones like replacing a bathroom floor or opening a wall for plumbing access, can disturb intact materials and create an exposure risk that requires professional abatement before work continues.

Pennsylvania's Department of Environmental Protection regulates asbestos abatement under state law, and Westmoreland County properties must follow those requirements regardless of whether the project is residential or commercial. That means licensed abatement, proper containment, regulated disposal at an approved facility, and air clearance testing before the containment is removed.

## Our Asbestos Abatement Process in Hempfield Township

Every project starts with a thorough inspection and bulk sampling of suspected materials. Samples go to an accredited laboratory, and no abatement work begins until results confirm the presence and percentage of asbestos fibers. This step matters because not every popcorn ceiling or old floor tile contains asbestos, and testing prevents unnecessary work while protecting everyone on site when the material does test positive.

Once confirmed, the work area is isolated with negative air pressure containment using HEPA-filtered air scrubbers. This keeps fibers from migrating into living spaces during removal. Wet methods are used throughout to suppress dust, and all removed material is sealed in labeled, leak-tight bags for transport to a licensed disposal facility. Air clearance sampling by a qualified third party follows removal before containment is broken down. That final clearance step is not optional under Pennsylvania DEP standards, and it is the documentation a homeowner needs before any finishing contractor re-enters the space.

## Local Note: What Hempfield Township Contractors Often Encounter

In Hempfield Township's older neighborhoods closer to Greensburg, it is common to find homes where previous owners added a second layer of vinyl flooring directly over original 9-inch or 12-inch floor tiles from the 1950s and 1960s. Those original tiles, and the black mastic adhesive beneath them, frequently contain asbestos. Because the top layer of newer flooring looks clean and modern, homeowners and even general contractors sometimes assume the subfloor is safe to disturb. A visual inspection alone cannot confirm that. Any floor tile removal in a home built before 1980 in this area should be preceded by bulk sampling, because cutting or grinding through that adhesive layer without containment creates a serious fiber release risk.
