## Why Murrysville's Housing Stock Shapes Every Renovation

Much of Murrysville's residential development happened in waves: modest ranch and cape-cod construction through the 1960s and 1970s, followed by larger colonials and two-story builds as the township grew through the 1980s and 1990s. That layering matters when you open a wall. Homes from the earlier era frequently carry knob-and-tube or early-generation aluminum wiring that needs to be evaluated before any kitchen or bathroom remodel moves forward. Subfloor materials shift from board sheathing to plywood to OSB depending on the decade, which changes how tile, hardwood, and luxury vinyl plank need to be prepared and fastened.

Clay-dense soil throughout this part of Westmoreland County also means basement slabs and foundation walls move more than homeowners expect. Before any basement finishing project, we assess for moisture intrusion and slab heave, because finishing over an active moisture problem only traps the damage behind new drywall.

## Our Renovation and General Contracting Process in Murrysville

Every project starts with a walkthrough that treats the existing structure as a variable, not an assumption. We document what is behind the walls, under the floors, and above the ceiling before a single permit application goes to Murrysville's building department. ## Post-Damage Rebuilds and Insurance Coordination

When a renovation follows a water, fire, or mold loss, the project has a different starting point than a standard remodel. The remediation scope defines what was removed, and the rebuild has to match that scope while also meeting current code, which sometimes differs from what was originally installed. We work from the adjuster's documentation, photograph all phases of the rebuild for the carrier's file, and flag supplemental items in writing when hidden damage expands the scope.

This matters in Murrysville because older homes here often reveal surprises during demo: undersized drain lines, original cast-iron stack sections, or insulation that was never brought up to current energy code. Addressing those items correctly during a rebuild avoids a second disruption down the road.

## Local Note

Homes built in Murrysville during the 1970s and early 1980s frequently used a textured drywall finish style that is difficult to match with standard modern spray techniques. When a water loss or renovation requires partial wall replacement in these homes, a full-room retexture is often the cleaner solution than trying to blend a patch. It costs a little more in labor but eliminates the visible seam that shows up under raking light, and it is the kind of detail that affects how a finished room actually looks and feels.
