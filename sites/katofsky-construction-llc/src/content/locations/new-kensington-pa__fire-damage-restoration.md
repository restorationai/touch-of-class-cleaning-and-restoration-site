## Why New Kensington Properties See Distinct Fire Damage Patterns

The housing stock throughout New Kensington leans heavily toward pre-1950 construction. Balloon-frame walls, original plaster, and knob-and-tube wiring that has been partially updated over the decades create conditions where fire behaves differently than it does in a newer subdivision. Open stud bays in balloon-frame homes act as flues, pulling smoke from a first-floor kitchen fire up into attic spaces before the fire itself reaches them. That means structural members and insulation well above the burn origin can carry heavy smoke contamination that a surface inspection alone will miss.

The valley's climate adds another layer. Allegheny County winters bring cycles of cold and damp that cause fire-damaged materials to absorb ambient moisture quickly once a structure loses its thermal envelope. A roof compromised by fire in January can allow water intrusion within hours, and wet char is harder to remediate than dry char. Timing matters enormously here.

## Our Fire Damage Restoration Process in New Kensington

The first step on any fire loss is a thorough structural assessment, not just a visual walk-through. For the older homes common in New Kensington, that means probing wall cavities, checking attic spaces for smoke migration, and documenting every affected material before anything is removed. Accurate documentation protects your insurance claim and prevents disputes about what was damaged versus what was pre-existing.

From there, the process moves through controlled demolition of unsalvageable materials, HEPA-filtered dry vacuuming of smoke residue, chemical sponge cleaning of soot from surfaces, and thermal fogging or hydroxyl treatment to address embedded odor. Structural drying follows if water was introduced during firefighting. The sequence matters: cleaning before drying traps contaminants, and drying before cleaning can set stains permanently into porous materials like plaster and brick.

Katofsky Construction LLC holds IICRC certification, and the restoration work follows industry-standard protocols for content and structural cleaning.

## New Kensington Insurance Coordination

Fire losses in Westmoreland County typically move through standard homeowner's policy channels, but the documentation burden on older homes is higher than on newer construction. When a home has original plaster walls, original hardwood floors, or period millwork, replacement cost calculations require detailed photographic evidence and sometimes material sampling before the adjuster can assign accurate values. The crew photographs and catalogs affected materials before any demolition begins, creating a record that supports your claim rather than leaving gaps for the carrier to dispute.

If your policy includes additional living expense coverage, the documentation package also supports that portion of the claim by establishing which areas of the home are uninhabitable during restoration.

## Local Note

In New Kensington's hillside neighborhoods, older homes often share narrow lot lines with minimal setback between structures. A fire that vents through a side window or soffit can deposit a significant smoke plume against an adjacent home's exterior, coating siding, window frames, and even infiltrating the neighboring attic through soffit vents. If your neighbor's home was involved in a fire near yours, or vice versa, it is worth having the exterior and attic of your own property assessed even if you saw no flames. Smoke contamination at that proximity is common and is frequently a covered loss under your own policy.
