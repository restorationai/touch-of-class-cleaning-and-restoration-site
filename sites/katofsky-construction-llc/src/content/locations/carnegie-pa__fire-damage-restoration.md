Carnegie sits just southwest of Pittsburgh along Chartiers Creek, and the borough's housing stock tells the story of a working-class steel-era community: row houses, brick doubles, and wood-frame colonials built mostly between the 1910s and 1950s. When fire moves through one of those older homes, it does not behave the way it does in a newer build. Balloon-frame construction, common in Carnegie's pre-war housing, allows smoke and heat to travel vertically through wall cavities with almost no resistance, spreading soot and char to floors and attic spaces that look untouched from the outside. If you are standing in a Carnegie home after a fire, the visible damage is rarely the full picture.

## Why Carnegie Properties See Elevated Fire Damage Complexity

The age and construction style of Carnegie's residential neighborhoods create specific challenges that affect how fire damage spreads and how restoration has to proceed. Balloon framing, used extensively before platform framing became standard after World War II, lacks the floor-to-floor fire blocking that newer homes have. A kitchen fire that appears contained to one room can push smoke into the attic, into party walls shared with an adjacent unit, and into basement ceiling joists before a crew ever arrives.

Chartiers Creek's proximity also matters. Carnegie's older homes frequently have stone or brick foundation walls with minimal vapor barriers, and the combination of post-fire humidity from suppression water and the borough's naturally damp creek-bottom air can accelerate secondary damage. Wet insulation, saturated plaster lath, and standing water in crawl spaces are common findings after a structure fire here, even when the fire itself was relatively small.

Older plumbing and knob-and-tube wiring, still present in some Carnegie homes, can complicate the structural assessment phase. A fire that trips a breaker in a modern panel may leave hidden heat damage in a home where wiring runs through unprotected cavities.

## Our Fire Damage Restoration Process in Carnegie

The first priority after fire suppression is stabilizing the structure and securing the property. Katofsky Construction LLC responds 24/7, so the call can come at 2 a.m. after the fire department clears the scene and we can begin emergency board-up and tarping the same night to prevent weather intrusion and unauthorized entry.

Once the structure is secured, the process moves through several distinct phases:

**Damage documentation and scoping.** Every affected surface is photographed and catalogued. In Carnegie's older homes this means probing wall cavities, pulling up flooring, and checking attic spaces for smoke migration that is not visible from the room where the fire started.

**Smoke and soot removal.** Soot chemistry varies by what burned. Synthetic materials produce a wet, oily residue that bonds aggressively to plaster and brick. Natural materials leave a dry, powdery soot that is easier to remove but travels further. The cleaning method, HEPA vacuuming, dry chemical sponging, or alkaline detergent washing, is matched to the residue type on each surface.

**Odor neutralization.** Smoke odor in a Carnegie brick home can linger for months if the source is not eliminated at the structural level. Thermal fogging and hydroxyl generation reach into the porous surfaces that surface cleaning cannot fully address.

**Moisture control.** Suppression water is treated as a parallel water damage event. Industrial drying equipment is deployed alongside fire cleanup, and readings are tracked until affected materials reach target moisture levels.

Katofsky Construction LLC is an IICRC Certified Firm, and the water mitigation component of every fire job follows the IICRC S500 standard.
