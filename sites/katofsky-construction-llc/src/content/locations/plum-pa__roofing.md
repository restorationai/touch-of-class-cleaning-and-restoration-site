## Why Plum Properties See Roofing Deterioration Faster Than Expected

Plum's position in the Allegheny River valley corridor means it collects significant moisture from both precipitation and ground-level humidity. Homes here see more wet-dry cycling than properties on higher, better-drained ground to the east. That moisture pattern accelerates shingle degradation, promotes algae streaking on north-facing slopes, and works into flashing seams around chimneys and dormers over time.

The borough's housing stock is also a factor. Many Plum neighborhoods were built out during the postwar suburban expansion of the 1950s through 1970s, meaning original roofing systems have long since exceeded their design life. Homes of that era frequently used lower-slope gable designs that are efficient to build but create slower drainage and more standing-water exposure than steeper pitches. If your home dates to that period and has never had a full roof replacement, the decking underneath may have absorbed decades of minor leaks that don't show up until a contractor pulls the old shingles.

## Our Roofing Installation and Replacement Process in Plum

Every project starts with a physical inspection, not a satellite estimate. We walk the roof, check the decking for soft spots and delamination, examine the ridge and valley lines, and assess the condition of all penetrations including pipe boots, skylights, and chimney step flashing. That inspection shapes the scope before any material is ordered.

For full replacements, we remove existing shingles down to the deck, replace any compromised decking sections, and install a proper underlayment system suited to Pennsylvania's climate requirements. Ice and water shield along eaves and in valleys is standard practice in this region given the freeze-thaw cycle Plum experiences each winter. We then install the new shingles and restore all flashing details before final cleanup and inspection.

For new construction or additions, the process includes coordinating with any active permits through Allegheny County and ensuring the roofing system meets current Pennsylvania Uniform Construction Code requirements. Plum Borough issues its own building permits for residential work, so we confirm local permit status before work begins rather than assuming county-level approvals cover everything.

## Local Note

One thing worth knowing about Plum's older residential sections: many homes built in the 1960s and early 1970s used a lower-grade fiberboard sheathing rather than plywood on the roof deck. That material holds moisture and deteriorates in ways that standard plywood does not. When we pull old shingles on homes from that era, we routinely find sections of fiberboard that have swelled, delaminated, or gone soft at the edges. Replacing those sections before laying new shingles is not optional if you want the new roof to perform as rated. Homeowners sometimes see a higher decking replacement line item on their estimate and assume it's padding. In Plum's older housing stock, it's usually a genuine finding.
