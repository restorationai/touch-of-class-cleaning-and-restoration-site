## Why Baldwin Properties Present Specific Biohazard Challenges

Much of Baldwin's residential housing was built between the 1940s and 1970s, a period when subfloor construction, plaster-and-lath walls, and older HVAC configurations were standard. These materials absorb biological contamination differently than modern drywall or engineered flooring. Porous subfloors and older grout lines in bathrooms can hold infectious material well below the visible surface, which means a cleanup that looks complete at a glance may not be. Identifying how far contamination has migrated requires testing and methodical removal, not just surface treatment.

Baldwin's compact lot sizes also mean that access points, stairwells, and shared entryways in multi-unit properties can complicate safe containment. Establishing a proper decontamination corridor in a narrow row-style home or a split-level with tight interior stairs takes planning that a crew unfamiliar with the area's housing stock might underestimate.
