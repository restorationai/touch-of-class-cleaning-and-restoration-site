## Why Plum Homes See Upholstery Wear Faster

A large share of Plum's housing stock dates from the postwar decades through the 1980s, when wall-to-wall carpeting and heavily upholstered furniture were standard. Many of those homes have been updated, but the bones stay the same: tighter floor plans, lower ceilings, and less natural ventilation than newer construction. In winter, forced-air heat cycles constantly, pushing pet dander, cooking grease, and dust deep into fabric fibers. In summer, windows stay closed for air conditioning, trapping odors in cushions rather than letting them air out.

Allegheny County's seasonal pollen counts are among the highest in western Pennsylvania, and Plum's tree canopy means pollen settles on everything, including the sofa cushions near an open window in May. That layer of fine organic material is exactly what feeds dust mites and causes fabric to look dingy even after regular vacuuming.

## Our Upholstery Cleaning Process in Plum

Every piece starts with a fabric identification check. Microfiber, cotton blends, velvet, and genuine leather each respond differently to moisture and heat, and using the wrong method can shrink seams or strip a protective coating. We test an inconspicuous area before committing to any cleaning agent.

For fabric upholstery, we use low-moisture hot-water extraction calibrated to the fabric weight. The goal is enough heat and suction to lift embedded soil without saturating the cushion core, which can stay damp for days and invite mildew in a closed room. Leather pieces get a pH-balanced cleaner followed by a conditioner appropriate to the finish, whether that is aniline, semi-aniline, or pigmented leather.

Drying time in Plum's climate matters. On a humid August afternoon, a couch that feels dry to the touch can still hold moisture in the foam core. We position air movers to pull that residual humidity out before we leave, reducing the window for odor to return.

## Sofa and Furniture Cleaning: What to Expect on Appointment Day

Most residential upholstery jobs in Plum take two to four hours depending on the number of pieces and the soil level. You do not need to move furniture out of the room, but clearing the area around each piece speeds the work. Cushions come off and are cleaned separately so the base fabric underneath gets the same attention as the visible surfaces.

For sectionals, which are common in the open-plan additions many Plum ranchers and split-levels have seen over the years, we clean each module individually and check the seams where modules connect, since crumbs and pet hair concentrate there.

After cleaning, fabric pieces benefit from staying out of direct sunlight until fully dry to prevent uneven fading. We will walk you through the expected dry time based on the day's humidity before we pack up.
