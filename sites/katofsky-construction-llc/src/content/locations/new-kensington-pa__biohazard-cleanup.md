## Why New Kensington Properties Present Unique Biohazard Challenges

The building stock throughout New Kensington leans heavily toward pre-1960 construction. That matters for biohazard cleanup because older porous materials, original hardwood subfloors, plaster lath, and unsealed concrete in basements and crawlspaces, absorb biological material in ways that modern vinyl plank or sealed concrete does not. Surface cleaning is never enough in these situations. Affected material often has to be removed to the substrate level and the underlying structure treated before any area can be cleared.

Shared-wall construction adds another layer of complexity. In a duplex or row home, a situation in one unit can create concerns about adjacent spaces, particularly where plumbing chases or utility openings connect the structures. A thorough scope of work accounts for those pathways before the crew ever starts.

Westmoreland County also experiences cold, wet winters that slow natural off-gassing and can complicate the drying of treated surfaces. Work done in January in an unheated structure requires different equipment staging than the same job in July.

## Our Biohazard Cleanup Process in New Kensington

Every call starts with a conversation, not a sales pitch. When you reach us at (412) 304-9284, we ask enough questions to understand the scope and dispatch the right crew with the right supplies. We do not send a single technician to assess and then schedule a second visit; the crew that arrives is prepared to begin work.

On site, the process follows a defined sequence:

- **Containment and PPE establishment**: The affected area is isolated before any materials are touched. Adjacent rooms, HVAC returns, and shared spaces are evaluated.
- **Removal of affected materials**: Porous materials that cannot be decontaminated are removed, bagged, and staged for regulated disposal. This is not ordinary trash; biohazardous waste is transported and disposed of under Pennsylvania Department of Environmental Protection requirements.
- **Surface treatment**: Hard surfaces are treated with EPA-registered disinfectants at appropriate dwell times. This step is not abbreviated to save time.
- **Odor control**: Biological odors penetrate structural cavities. We address odor at the source, not by masking it.
- **Documentation**: We photograph affected areas before, during, and after work and prepare a written scope that supports an insurance claim.

As an IICRC Certified Firm, our technicians follow established industry protocols for personal protective equipment, containment, and verification, not improvised procedures.
