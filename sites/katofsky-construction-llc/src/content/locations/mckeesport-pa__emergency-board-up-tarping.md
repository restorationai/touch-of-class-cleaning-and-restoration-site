## Why McKeesport Properties Face Elevated Board-Up and Tarping Needs

McKeesport's housing stock skews heavily toward late-19th and early-20th century construction. Brick facades, wood-frame interiors, slate rooflines, and single-pane windows were built to last, but they respond to sudden trauma differently than modern construction. A fire that vents through a roof on a century-old rowhouse can leave the remaining structure open to the sky within minutes. A broken window on a brick-and-timber building lets in wind-driven rain that saturates wood framing that has already been absorbing moisture for decades.

The Mon Valley's weather patterns add urgency. Storms tracking up the Monongahela corridor can drop significant rain quickly, and the region's freeze-thaw cycles through late fall and early spring mean that an unsecured opening can go from a rain problem to an ice problem overnight. Delaying board-up by even a few hours after a fire or severe weather event can allow secondary water intrusion that turns a manageable loss into a full structural remediation.

## Our Emergency Board-Up and Tarping Process in McKeesport

When you call, the first question is what you're dealing with: fire damage, storm damage, a broken window from a vehicle impact, or a roof failure. That determines what we bring.

For roof tarping, we use heavy-duty polyethylene tarps anchored with wood nailer boards screwed into sound decking rather than simply weighted at the edges. This method holds through sustained wind and doesn't shift when the next storm arrives. For board-up work on windows and doors, we use plywood cut to fit and secured with structural screws into the surrounding framing, not just surface-stapled to the trim. On brick facades common throughout McKeesport's older neighborhoods, we anchor into the masonry surround where the frame has deteriorated.

We photograph every opening before and after securing it. That documentation goes directly into the insurance claim file.

Most homeowner and commercial property policies cover emergency board-up and tarping as part of the initial loss mitigation. Carriers expect the property owner to take reasonable steps to prevent further damage, and a documented, professional board-up satisfies that requirement.
