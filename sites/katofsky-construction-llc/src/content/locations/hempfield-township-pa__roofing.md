Hempfield Township sits in Westmoreland County where the seasons swing hard: ice storms in January, heavy wet snow in February, and summer thunderstorms that roll in fast off the Laurel Highlands. That weather cycle is relentless on roofing systems, and when a roof reaches the end of its service life here, the signs tend to show up all at once rather than gradually. Katofsky Construction LLC installs and replaces roofs for homeowners throughout Hempfield Township, bringing a crew that understands what Western Pennsylvania winters actually do to shingles, underlayment, and ridge caps.

## Why Hempfield Township Properties See Roofing Problems

Westmoreland County's freeze-thaw cycle is the primary culprit behind premature roof failure in this area. Water works into small gaps around flashing or under lifted shingles, freezes overnight, and expands. By the time spring arrives, what started as a minor seam issue can open into a leak path that reaches the decking. Hempfield Township also sits in a region where ice dams form readily on homes with lower-slope rooflines, because attic heat escapes unevenly and melts snow at the ridge while the eaves stay frozen.

The township's residential housing stock spans several decades, and older homes built with dimensional lumber framing sometimes have roof decks that have absorbed moisture over many years. Before new shingles go down, that decking needs to be assessed, and in some cases sections need replacement before the new roof system can perform as designed. Skipping that step is one of the most common reasons a new roof fails within a few years.

High winds are also a consistent issue in this part of Westmoreland County. Shingles rated for standard wind loads can lift or crack during the kind of straight-line wind events that accompany summer storm cells moving through the region. Selecting materials with appropriate wind ratings for this climate is not optional; it is part of doing the job correctly.

## Our Roofing Installation and Replacement Process in Hempfield Township

Every project starts with a thorough inspection of the existing roof system, not just the shingles but the flashing around chimneys, skylights, and dormers, the condition of the decking, and the ventilation setup in the attic space. Poor attic ventilation is a widespread issue in Hempfield Township homes, and it shortens shingle life significantly by trapping heat and moisture below the roof surface.

Once the scope is clear, the old roofing material is stripped completely. We do not layer new shingles over old ones. Full tear-off lets the crew inspect every square foot of decking and replace any sections that have softened or delaminated. A quality underlayment is then installed before any shingles go down, providing a secondary moisture barrier that matters especially in a climate that delivers freezing rain and heavy snow.

Shingle installation follows manufacturer specifications for nailing patterns and overlap, which affects both wind resistance and warranty validity. Ridge ventilation is installed or upgraded as part of the project so the new roof can breathe properly through Hempfield Township's hot summers and cold winters alike. Before the crew leaves, all debris is removed and the site is walked for nails and material scraps.

## Local Note

Hempfield Township includes a mix of ranch-style homes from the 1960s and 1970s built with relatively low roof pitches. Those low-slope sections are particularly vulnerable to ice dam formation because water cannot shed as quickly as it can on steeper rooflines. When we replace a roof on one of these homes, we pay close attention to the eave detail and often recommend a wider application of ice-and-water shield membrane extending further up the slope than the minimum code requirement, because the minimum is calibrated for average conditions and Westmoreland County winters are not always average.
