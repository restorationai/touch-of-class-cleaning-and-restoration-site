## Why McKeesport Properties See Roofing Problems Early

Much of McKeesport's housing stock was built between the 1910s and 1950s, during the steel industry's peak. Those homes typically feature steep-pitched roofs designed to shed heavy snowfall, but the original wood decking underneath has had decades to absorb moisture, warp, and develop soft spots that a surface-level inspection can miss entirely. When a new layer of shingles goes over compromised decking, the underlying rot continues quietly until the next hard winter exposes it.

The Mon Valley's topography compounds the problem. Properties that sit on the hillsides above the Monongahela River face wind exposure from multiple directions, and valleys between ridgelines can funnel gusts that accelerate shingle lift and granule loss. Ice damming is also a recurring issue: cold air pooling at lower elevations keeps eaves frozen while the upper roof surface warms, and the resulting meltwater backs up under shingles and into the attic or wall cavities.

## Our Roofing Installation and Replacement Process in McKeesport

Every project starts with a full deck inspection, not just a visual pass from the ground. On older McKeesport homes, that means walking the roof and probing for soft decking, checking flashing at chimneys and dormers, and assessing the condition of any existing underlayment. If the deck needs partial or full replacement before new materials go down, we identify that before the contract is signed so there are no mid-job surprises.

Material selection is calibrated to the climate. In a region that sees significant ice and snow load, proper ice-and-water shield installation at the eaves and valleys is not optional. We follow manufacturer specifications for overlap and fastening patterns, which also matters for warranty validity. Ventilation is addressed at the same time: many older McKeesport homes are under-ventilated, and a new roof installed over a poorly ventilated attic will fail prematurely regardless of shingle quality.

Flashing details around chimneys, skylights, and roof-to-wall transitions get particular attention. Step flashing on these older homes has often been caulked and re-caulked over the years rather than replaced, and caulk alone is not a long-term solution in a climate with this much thermal movement.
