## Why Bethel Park Properties See Fire Damage Differently

Much of Bethel Park's residential fabric was built between the 1950s and 1970s, a period when homes were framed with old-growth lumber, insulated with materials that are no longer in production, and finished with plaster-and-lath rather than drywall. When fire or intense heat moves through these structures, the aftermath is more complex than in a newer build. Plaster holds smoke odor deep in its pores, and the lath behind it can char without showing visible damage at the surface. Ductwork in these homes is often unlined sheet metal, which absorbs smoke particles and redistributes odor every time the HVAC system runs after a fire.

The South Hills geography also matters. Bethel Park sits on terrain with significant grade changes, and many homes here have walk-out basements or lower-level garages that connect directly to living space. Fire that starts in a garage or utility area can draft upward through the structure faster than it would in a flat-slab ranch, pulling smoke into every floor before suppression crews arrive.

## Our Fire Damage Restoration Process in Bethel Park

The first thing a crew does on arrival is assess structural stability and document the full extent of damage before any materials are moved or discarded. Photographic documentation is thorough because insurance carriers require it, and because scope disputes are harder to resolve once demolition begins.

From there, the process moves through several distinct phases. Affected materials are categorized: what can be cleaned and restored, what must be removed, and what needs further assessment before a decision is made. Soot on hard surfaces is treated with dry chemical sponges before any wet cleaning, because introducing moisture to dry soot turns it into a staining compound that penetrates deeper. Smoke odor in porous materials, including the plaster walls common throughout Bethel Park's older homes, is addressed with thermal fogging and hydroxyl treatment rather than masking agents.

Structural drying, if water was introduced during suppression, runs concurrently. The IICRC S500 standard guides moisture mapping and drying validation so the structure is confirmed dry before any rebuild work begins.
