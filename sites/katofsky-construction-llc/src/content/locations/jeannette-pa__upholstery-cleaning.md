## Why Jeannette Homes See Upholstery Wear Faster Than You Might Expect

Jeannette was built largely during the glass and steel boom of the late nineteenth and early twentieth centuries, and a significant portion of its housing stock reflects that era. Older homes in this part of Westmoreland County tend to have smaller square footage, which means living rooms and family rooms carry heavier daily traffic per piece of furniture. When a single sofa is the primary seating for a household, it accumulates soil, oils, and moisture far more quickly than the same piece would in a larger, more spread-out space.

The regional humidity pattern compounds this. Jeannette sits in a valley corridor that channels moist air off the Allegheny foothills, and homes without modern vapor barriers or updated HVAC systems can hold ambient humidity that keeps upholstery slightly damp for days at a time. That moisture creates the exact conditions fabric mites and mildew spores prefer. You may not see visible mold on a sofa cushion, but a musty odor after a humid stretch is a reliable sign that biological activity has started in the fill material.

## Our Upholstery Cleaning Process in Jeannette

Every piece starts with a fiber identification check. Fabric upholstery, microfiber, velvet, and leather each respond differently to moisture and cleaning agents, and using the wrong method on a delicate weave can cause irreversible shrinkage or color bleed. Once we know what we are working with, we pre-treat stains and high-contact zones using solutions matched to the fabric type.

For fabric upholstery, hot-water extraction pulls embedded soil and allergens from deep within the fill without saturating the cushion core. For leather, we use low-moisture techniques combined with pH-balanced conditioners that clean the surface without drying out the hide. Drying time in Jeannette's climate is a real variable. On a dry summer day with good airflow, fabric pieces can be ready for use in a few hours. On a damp fall or winter day, we set up air-moving equipment to accelerate evaporation and prevent the cushion core from staying wet long enough to develop an odor.

Katofsky Construction holds IICRC certification, which means our cleaning protocols follow the same evidence-based standards used in professional restoration work, not just surface-level spot treatment.

## Fabric vs. Leather: What Jeannette Homeowners Should Know

Leather furniture is popular in Westmoreland County homes partly because it is perceived as easier to maintain, and in some ways that is true. But leather reacts poorly to the freeze-thaw temperature swings that Jeannette sees from November through March. Leather that dries out from forced-air heating and then absorbs humidity when the furnace cycles down will crack along seams and stress points over time. Conditioning after cleaning is not optional in this climate; it is the step that determines whether the piece lasts another decade or starts showing cracks within a year.

Fabric upholstery, particularly synthetic blends common in furniture manufactured in the last twenty years, tends to hold odors from cooking and pets more stubbornly than natural fibers. Hot-water extraction addresses this at the fiber level rather than masking it with fragrance.
