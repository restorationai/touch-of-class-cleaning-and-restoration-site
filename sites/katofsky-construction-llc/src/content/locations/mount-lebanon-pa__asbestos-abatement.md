## Our Asbestos Abatement Process in Mount Lebanon

Abatement begins with inspection and bulk sampling. Suspect materials are collected by a trained technician and submitted to an accredited laboratory for analysis. Results typically determine whether the material is non-friable (intact and low-risk) or friable (easily crumbled, releasing fibers into air), which drives the scope of work.

Once the lab confirms asbestos-containing material, the affected area is isolated with negative air pressure containment. HEPA-filtered air scrubbers run continuously during removal to capture airborne fibers before they can migrate to adjacent spaces. Workers wear full protective equipment and follow Pennsylvania Department of Environmental Protection notification and disposal protocols, which require advance notice to the DEP before abatement begins on regulated quantities of material.

Waste is double-bagged in labeled, sealed poly bags and transported to a licensed disposal facility. After removal, the containment zone is HEPA-vacuumed and wiped down before clearance air sampling is conducted. That final air test is the documented proof that fiber levels meet clearance criteria before the space is reopened.

## Local Note

In Mount Lebanon's older neighborhoods, popcorn ceiling texture is one of the most commonly misunderstood asbestos sources. Homeowners frequently assume that because the ceiling looks intact, it's safe to sand or scrape before painting. But acoustic texture applied before 1978 has a meaningful probability of containing chrysotile asbestos, and sanding it without containment releases fibers throughout the living space. The same caution applies to the joint compound used in drywall finishing from that era.
