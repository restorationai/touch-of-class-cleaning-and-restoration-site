## Why Plum Properties See Fire Damage Complications

A significant share of Plum's residential housing stock dates from the 1950s through the 1970s. Homes from that era were built with open balloon-frame or early platform-frame construction, which means interior wall cavities run continuously from the basement to the roofline. When fire or heavy smoke enters those cavities, it can travel vertically through the structure with little resistance. That same framing also means original knob-and-tube or aluminum branch wiring may still be present in some older sections, a factor that affects both the cause of the fire and the scope of the post-fire electrical assessment before restoration work begins.

Plum's winters add another layer of complexity. After a fire, a home loses insulation value quickly, especially if windows were broken for ventilation during suppression. Sub-freezing temperatures can cause residual water from fire suppression to freeze inside wall cavities and beneath flooring, compounding structural damage that was not visible immediately after the event.

## Our Fire Damage Restoration Process in Plum

The first priority on any fire loss is stabilization. That means boarding openings, tarping the roof if the structure was breached, and securing the property against weather and unauthorized entry. Once the site is safe, the documentation phase begins: photographs, moisture readings, and a room-by-room scope of affected materials.

From there, the process moves through several distinct stages:

- **Soot and residue removal**: Dry soot from fast-burning fires behaves differently than wet, oily residue from slow smoldering. Each type requires a different cleaning chemistry and technique. Porous materials like insulation, carpet, and older plaster may need removal rather than cleaning.
- **Odor neutralization**: Thermal fogging and hydroxyl generation reach into the same cavities smoke traveled through. Masking smoke odor is not the goal; eliminating the source compounds is.
- **Structural drying**: Suppression water left behind by fire crews must be extracted and dried before any rebuild work begins. As an IICRC Certified Firm, drying protocols follow the IICRC S500 standard.
- **Scope documentation for insurance**: Every affected material is itemized with quantities and condition notes to support the insurance claim.
