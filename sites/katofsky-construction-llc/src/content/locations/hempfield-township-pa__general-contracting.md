Hempfield Township sits in a stretch of Westmoreland County where the housing stock ranges from mid-century ranches to older two-story colonials built when the township was still largely agricultural, and that mix creates a renovation landscape unlike what you find in newer suburban developments closer to the city. Whether you are opening up a cramped kitchen, converting an underused basement, or rebuilding a room after water or fire damage, the bones of the house matter as much as the finish work. Katofsky Construction LLC handles the full scope of that work, from initial planning through final inspection, for homeowners and property owners across Hempfield Township.

## What Makes Renovation Work in Hempfield Township Different

Homes in Hempfield Township frequently carry construction details that are invisible until a wall comes open: knob-and-tube wiring that predates modern load demands, plaster-and-lath walls that require a different approach than drywall, and crawl spaces with older vapor barriers that may need replacement before any finished living space goes in above them. The township's clay-heavy soils also mean that basement walls and slab edges are under more lateral pressure than they would be on sandier ground, which affects how we approach any renovation that touches the foundation perimeter or adds load to an existing floor system.

Permitting in Westmoreland County follows the Pennsylvania Uniform Construction Code, and Hempfield Township enforces it through the county's inspection program. That means any structural change, electrical upgrade, or plumbing reroute needs a permit pulled before work begins, not after. Skipping that step creates title problems when the property sells. We handle the permit applications as part of the project, so the paperwork does not fall on you.

## Our Renovation and General Contracting Process

Every project starts with a walkthrough of the space as it exists, not as it appears in a listing photo or a previous owner's memory. We look at what the structure can support, where the mechanicals run, and what the scope of work will realistically require. From there we produce a written estimate that breaks out labor, materials, and any subcontractor costs so you can see exactly where the budget is going.

Demolition is sequenced to protect the parts of the house that are staying. On older Hempfield Township homes, that often means testing for lead paint or asbestos-containing materials before any cutting or sanding begins. Our team holds IICRC certification, and we coordinate environmental testing through qualified third parties when the age of the structure warrants it. Once the space is clear and inspected, framing, mechanical rough-ins, insulation, and finishes follow in the order that keeps inspections on schedule and avoids rework.

For post-damage rebuilds, we work from the remediation scope forward, rebuilding to current code even when the original construction predates those standards. That is not optional in Pennsylvania: a rebuild permit triggers code compliance for the affected systems.

## Kitchen and Bathroom Remodels: What to Expect

Kitchen and bathroom remodels are the most requested renovation work in residential Hempfield Township, and they are also the projects most likely to surface hidden conditions. Galvanized supply lines are common in homes built before the 1970s, and they corrode from the inside out, so a kitchen remodel that includes a sink relocation is often the right moment to repipe the branch lines while the walls are already open. Bathroom tile work on older floors sometimes sits over a mortar bed rather than cement board, which changes the demo sequence and the substrate prep.

We source materials through regional suppliers and can work with customer-supplied fixtures when the spec is clear. Lead times on cabinets and specialty tile are real constraints right now, and we build that into the schedule at the estimate stage rather than discovering it mid-project.

## Local Note

Hempfield Township's older neighborhoods include homes where additions were built in stages over several decades, sometimes without a single set of drawings that captures the whole structure. When we open a wall in one of these layered additions, we occasionally find that the addition's framing does not bear on the original foundation the way the current owner assumes. That discovery mid-project is not a crisis, but it does require a quick structural assessment before framing continues. Homeowners in these situations should expect a brief hold while we confirm the load path, not a restart. Knowing to look for this before demo begins is one of the things that separates a contractor familiar with this area's housing stock from one who is not.
