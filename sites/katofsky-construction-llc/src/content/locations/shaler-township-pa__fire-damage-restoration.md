## Why Shaler Township Properties See Distinctive Fire Damage Patterns

The age of the housing stock here shapes almost every fire damage job. Balloon-frame homes, which were built before platform framing became standard after World War II, lack the fire-blocking that modern construction requires between floor levels. A fire that starts in a first-floor wall can travel vertically through the stud cavity all the way to the attic without breaking through any visible surface. That means a kitchen fire that looks contained can have already deposited smoke and soot two floors up by the time the fire department clears the scene.

Plaster walls compound the challenge. Unlike drywall, plaster is dense and porous in a different way: it holds odor compounds from smoke long after surfaces appear clean. Protein smoke from kitchen fires is especially stubborn in plaster, and it requires thermal fogging or hydroxyl treatment rather than simple surface wiping. Homeowners who attempt to repaint over smoke-affected plaster often find the odor resurfaces within weeks as temperature and humidity fluctuate through Pittsburgh's seasons.

The Allegheny valley also experiences temperature swings that affect how quickly secondary damage sets in after a fire. Water used in suppression freezes in colder months, and the combination of fire damage and freeze-thaw stress on already-compromised structural members creates compounding problems that need to be assessed quickly.

## Our Fire Damage Restoration Process in Shaler Township

The first step on any fire loss is a thorough scope of the damage, including areas that didn't burn. Soot travels with air movement, and in an older Shaler Township home with natural-draft HVAC or a gravity furnace system, smoke can coat ductwork and deposit particulates in rooms that were never near the fire. The scope drives the work plan.

From there, the process moves through debris removal, structural stabilization if needed, and systematic deodorization. Porous materials like plaster, wood framing, and subfloor sheathing absorb smoke differently than hard surfaces, so each material type gets treated with the appropriate method. Thermal fogging penetrates into the same spaces smoke traveled. HEPA air scrubbers run continuously during the cleaning phase to capture fine particulates before they resettle.

Documentation runs in parallel throughout. Every affected surface is photographed and logged, which supports the insurance claim and gives the adjuster a clear picture of scope before any materials are removed.
