## Why Greensburg Properties See Board-Up and Tarping Emergencies

Greensburg sits in a part of western Pennsylvania where the weather swings hard between seasons. Nor'easters and late-spring thunderstorms roll through the Laurel Highlands corridor and funnel directly into the city, producing the kind of sudden, localized wind damage that lifts roof sections and drives rain horizontally through any gap. Ice damming is a regular winter problem on the steeply pitched roofs common throughout the older residential blocks near the courthouse district, where gutters back up and water finds its way under flashing long before a homeowner notices anything inside.

The housing stock compounds the vulnerability. A large share of Greensburg's homes were built before 1950, with wood-framed construction, original single-pane windows, and in many cases slate or clay tile roofing that, while durable when intact, can fail in sections when a single fastener lets go. When a slate tile goes, it often takes surrounding tiles with it, leaving a ragged opening that standard roofing tarps have to span carefully to avoid creating new pressure points. Older window frames in these homes are also more likely to fail completely rather than crack, meaning a broken window board-up needs to account for deteriorated framing that may not hold a standard lag anchor cleanly.

Fire damage board-up calls in Greensburg frequently involve attached structures, since many of the city's older neighborhoods feature homes built close together on narrow lots, sometimes sharing a common wall or a tight alley. Securing one opening properly matters not just for the affected property but for limiting exposure to adjacent structures.

## Our Emergency Board-Up and Tarping Process in Greensburg

When a call comes in, the first step is a fast visual assessment of every compromised opening, not just the obvious one. On older Greensburg homes, a fire that vents through a second-floor window often creates heat damage to the soffit and fascia above it, and those areas need tarping even when they look intact from the street. We photograph every opening before we cover it, which matters for your insurance documentation.

For roof tarping, we use heavy-duty polyethylene tarps weighted and anchored with lumber rather than just draped over the ridge, a method that holds through the wind events common to this part of Westmoreland County. For window and door board-up, we use exterior-grade plywood cut and fitted to the rough opening, secured in a way that keeps the structure weather-tight without causing additional damage to original trim or masonry that may have historic or appraisal value.

We work directly with most major insurance carriers and document the scope in a format adjusters recognize, so the board-up and tarping work is captured as part of the overall claim from the start.
