**Found gray, corrugated pipe wrap or 9x9 floor tile during a Duquesne renovation?** Asbestos abatement in this Mon Valley steel town isn't quite the same job as it is in a suburb built after 1980. Duquesne's housing stock went up fast during the steel mill boom, and a lot of it still has the original insulation, flooring adhesive, and plaster systems installed decades before anyone was testing for fiber content. We handle testing and removal the way this city's building stock actually requires, not the way a generic checklist says it should go.

## Why Duquesne Homes Still Have Asbestos Hiding in Plain Sight

Much of Duquesne's housing was built for steelworkers in the early-to-mid 1900s, and that construction era brings predictable materials: boiler and steam-pipe insulation wrapped in asbestos cloth or corrugated paper, black cutback adhesive under old vinyl floor tile, and troweled plaster ceilings. A good number of those plaster ceilings got a popcorn texture coat during 1960s-70s remodels, and that spray-applied texture is one of the more common asbestos-containing materials we test in this part of the Mon Valley. Basements with coal-converted-to-gas boiler systems are another recurring find, since a lot of that original pipe wrap was never touched when the heating system was upgraded. None of this means a house definitely has an asbestos problem, but it does mean testing before you drill, sand, or demo is worth doing first in a city with this building age profile.

## Our Asbestos Abatement Process in Duquesne

We start with sampling, not assumptions. A technician collects material samples from the suspect area, popcorn ceiling, floor tile, pipe wrap, whatever triggered the concern, and sends them to a lab for fiber analysis. If asbestos-containing material is confirmed, we set up containment with negative air pressure before any disturbance happens, following EPA NESHAP work practice standards and Pennsylvania Department of Environmental Protection abatement protocols for the removal and disposal steps. Materials come out wet-method to keep fibers from going airborne, get double-bagged in labeled containers, and are hauled to a facility licensed to accept asbestos waste. Once the work area is clear, we do a visual check before closing out, and for larger jobs we can coordinate third-party air clearance testing with an independent hygienist so you have documentation the space is safe to reoccupy.

## Local note

One thing we watch for specifically in Duquesne: homes with coal-era boilers that were converted to gas without a full mechanical room renovation. The original pipe insulation often got left in place because it was easier than reinsulating, and it can sit behind a finished basement wall for years before anyone touches it again during a remodel or a sale. If you're planning basement work in an older Duquesne property, it's worth having that insulation tested before the drywall comes down, not after.
