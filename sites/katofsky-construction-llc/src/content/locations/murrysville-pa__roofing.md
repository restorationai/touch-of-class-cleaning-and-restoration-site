## Why Murrysville Properties See Roofing Stress Earlier Than Expected

The hills and ridgelines running through Murrysville create wind-load conditions that flat-terrain roofing specs do not account for. Prevailing westerly winds accelerate as they move across the Allegheny foothills, and roofs on exposed lots take the brunt of that. Ice damming is a recurring problem on homes with lower-pitched rooflines, particularly on the north-facing slopes where shade keeps ice in place long after the rest of the roof has thawed. That melt-refreeze cycle forces water under shingles and into decking before most homeowners notice anything inside the house. Older homes in Murrysville also frequently have original wood sheathing beneath their shingles, and when that decking has softened from years of moisture cycling, a shingle-only replacement is not enough. A thorough inspection of the deck is part of every job before a single new shingle goes down.

## Our Roofing Installation and Replacement Process in Murrysville

Every project starts with a full roof inspection, not a sales walk. The crew evaluates decking integrity, flashing condition at every penetration point (chimneys, pipe boots, skylights, valleys), ventilation adequacy, and the condition of the fascia and soffit. In Murrysville's climate, attic ventilation is not a secondary concern: inadequate airflow is one of the primary drivers of premature shingle failure and ice dam formation. If the ventilation system needs correction, that work is built into the replacement scope.

Once the scope is confirmed, existing materials are stripped to the deck. Any soft or delaminated sheathing is replaced before underlayment goes down. For homes in Murrysville's higher-elevation sections where wind uplift is a real factor, fastener patterns and starter-course installation follow specifications designed for higher wind zones. Ridge and hip details, step flashing at dormers, and kickout flashing at wall intersections all get individual attention because those are the points where water finds its way in first.

Material selection is matched to the structure. Architectural shingles rated for the regional wind and impact exposure are the standard starting point, but metal roofing, standing-seam panels, and synthetic options are available for homeowners who want longer service intervals or a specific aesthetic for their property.
