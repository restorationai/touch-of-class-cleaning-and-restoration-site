## Why Jeannette Homes See Particular Risk to Personal Property

Westmoreland County sits in a climate band that delivers cold, wet winters and humid summers, and Jeannette's older neighborhoods feel that swing acutely. Homes built before World War II often have stone or poured-concrete block foundations that wick ground moisture upward through the floor system. When a sump pump fails during a February thaw or a spring storm backs up a floor drain, water does not stay in the basement. It travels. Cardboard boxes stored on a concrete floor absorb it within minutes. Wooden furniture legs begin to swell. Textiles trap the musty odor that follows within 24 to 48 hours if materials are not moved and treated quickly.

Fire losses in older Jeannette properties carry their own complication: the plaster-and-lath wall systems that are common in pre-1950 construction hold smoke residue in ways that modern drywall does not. Soot particles settle into the fine surface texture of plaster and migrate into the air cavities behind it, meaning that furniture and soft goods left in place continue to re-absorb odor long after the visible fire damage is addressed. Removing contents to a controlled environment is not optional in those situations, it is the only way to break the contamination cycle.

## Our Contents Restoration Process in Jeannette

Every contents job begins with a room-by-room inventory before anything is moved. Items are photographed, catalogued, and assigned a condition rating so that your insurance adjuster has a documented baseline. That step protects you if a carrier questions the pre-loss value of a piece.

From there, the process branches based on what each item needs:

- **Pack-out and secure storage**: Salvageable items are packed using industry-standard methods and transported to a climate-controlled facility. This removes them from the loss environment entirely, which is critical when structural drying or mold remediation work is ongoing in the home.
- **Ultrasonic cleaning**: Hard goods, collectibles, and non-porous items are cleaned in ultrasonic tanks that use high-frequency sound waves to lift soot, smoke residue, and microbial contamination from surfaces that hand-wiping cannot fully reach.
- **Ozone and hydroxyl treatment**: Soft goods, upholstered furniture, and clothing that carry smoke or mold odor are treated in a controlled chamber. This neutralizes odor at the molecular level rather than masking it.
- **Drying and dehumidification**: Water-affected wood furniture and documents are dried under monitored conditions. Solid hardwood pieces common in older Jeannette homes require slower, more controlled drying than engineered-wood furniture to prevent warping and joint separation.
- **Pack-back**: Once the structure is cleared and dry, items are returned, unwrapped, and placed back in their original rooms.

The IICRC-certified team at Katofsky Construction follows Water Restoration Technician (WRT) and Applied Microbial Remediation Technician (AMRT) standards throughout, which matters when your adjuster reviews the scope.
