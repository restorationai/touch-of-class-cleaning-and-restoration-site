## Why Plum Properties See Lead Paint Issues

Most of the housing stock in Plum that raises lead paint concerns was built between roughly 1930 and 1978, the year the federal government banned lead-based paint in residential construction. Homes from that era commonly have multiple layers of paint on woodwork, doors, and window sashes, and lead tends to concentrate in those layers closest to bare wood. The problem becomes acute when friction surfaces like double-hung windows or older wooden doors wear paint into fine dust through normal use, or when deferred maintenance allows exterior paint to chalk and peel.

Allegheny County's climate adds pressure. Freeze-thaw cycles through the winter months cause wood substrates to expand and contract, which accelerates paint adhesion failure on siding and trim. By the time spring arrives, what looked like a cosmetic issue in October can be active peeling by April. That cycle repeats every year on any home where lead paint has not been properly addressed.

Rental properties in Plum face an additional layer of scrutiny. Pennsylvania's landlord-tenant rules and federal HUD guidelines both impose disclosure and remediation obligations on landlords when lead hazards are identified, and local housing inspectors can flag a property during routine rental certification reviews.

## Our Lead Paint Abatement Process in Plum

Abatement is not the same as painting over a problem. The process starts with testing to confirm the presence and concentration of lead in suspect materials, using XRF analysis or paint chip sampling sent to an accredited laboratory. Once the scope is confirmed, the work area is isolated with poly sheeting and negative air pressure containment to prevent dust migration into living spaces.

Depending on the substrate and condition of the paint, removal methods include wet scraping, chemical stripping, or controlled demolition of components like window sashes that are more cost-effective to replace than to strip. All debris is collected, bagged, and disposed of as regulated waste under EPA guidelines. After removal, surfaces are HEPA-vacuumed and wet-wiped before clearance testing is performed to confirm the work area meets post-abatement standards.

For renovation projects that disturb lead paint without full removal, the EPA's Renovation, Repair, and Painting (RRP) Rule applies. Work performed under RRP requires certified contractors and specific work practices to minimize lead dust, which is a different regulatory track than full abatement but equally important for homeowners planning remodels in pre-1978 homes.

One pattern worth knowing about Plum's older neighborhoods: many homes from the postwar decades were built with steel casement windows, which were common in Allegheny County during that era. Steel casements hold paint differently than wood, and the paint-to-metal bond can fail in ways that generate fine dust rather than visible chips, making the hazard less obvious during a visual inspection.
