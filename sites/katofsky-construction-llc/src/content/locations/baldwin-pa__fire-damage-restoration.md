## Why Baldwin Properties Face Distinct Fire Damage Challenges

The housing stock in Baldwin Borough skews heavily toward the postwar decades, with brick veneer exteriors over wood-frame interiors. That combination creates a specific restoration challenge: the brick facade can look intact while the wood structure behind it has absorbed heat and smoke for hours. Attic spaces in these homes are often minimally insulated by modern standards, giving fire a clear path from an interior room to the roof deck.

Winter heating is another factor. Southwestern Pennsylvania winters push residents toward supplemental heat sources, and older homes with aging electrical panels or original knob-and-tube wiring carry elevated ignition risk. A fire that starts in a basement utility room or from an overloaded circuit can smolder inside wall cavities before it ever becomes visible. By the time smoke odor is noticeable in a second-floor bedroom, the damage path may already run the full height of the house.

## Our Fire Damage Restoration Process in Baldwin

The first step on any fire job is a room-by-room structural assessment before any cleaning or demo begins. Soot and smoke residue behave differently depending on what burned: synthetic materials leave a wet, oily residue that bonds to surfaces quickly, while wood and paper fires leave a dry, powdery char that spreads further through HVAC systems. Identifying the fire's fuel source shapes the cleaning chemistry and sequencing.

After assessment, the process moves through controlled demolition of unsalvageable materials, HEPA vacuuming of all horizontal surfaces, chemical sponge and dry-cleaning of walls and ceilings, and thermal fogging or hydroxyl treatment for embedded odor. In Baldwin's older homes, plaster walls and ceilings require a gentler approach than drywall: aggressive scrubbing can damage the finish coat, and plaster is porous enough to hold smoke odor deep in the substrate even after surface cleaning. Encapsulation with a shellac-based primer is often the final step before any repainting.

Katofsky Construction is an IICRC Certified Firm, which means the drying and decontamination protocols follow documented industry standards rather than guesswork.
