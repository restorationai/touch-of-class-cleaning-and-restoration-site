## Why Wilkinsburg Carpets Wear Differently

Much of Wilkinsburg was built out in the early 1900s as a streetcar suburb, which means a large share of the borough's duplexes and rowhomes have carpet laid directly over original hardwood or even older subfloor material. That combination holds moisture longer than carpet over concrete slab, so spills and humidity from Pittsburgh's wet spring and late-summer stretches can seep into the pad and linger instead of evaporating quickly. Add in forced-air heating systems common in homes from that era, which circulate more dust than modern HVAC, and carpet fibers in these older Wilkinsburg properties tend to gray and flatten faster than carpet of the same age in newer construction.

## Our Carpet Cleaning Process

We start with a walk-through to flag traffic patterns, pet areas, and any pre-existing staining, since pretreatment matters more on older, higher-pile carpet than on newer low-pile commercial product. Pretreatment loosens embedded soil before hot water extraction, which is the method we rely on for deep carpet cleaning because it pulls contaminants out of the pad rather than just the surface fiber. For carpet stain removal, we identify the stain type first, protein-based, tannin, or oil, because the wrong treatment can set a stain permanently into older wool or wool-blend carpet still found in some of Wilkinsburg's prewar homes. ## Local note

One detail that catches first-time Wilkinsburg customers off guard: carpet glued directly to concrete in finished basements, a common renovation choice in the borough's older housing stock, dries much slower after wet cleaning than carpet with a standard pad and tack strip. We adjust extraction passes and drying time accordingly on those jobs so the carpet doesn't wick moisture back up from the slab, which is a common cause of musty odor returning a few days after a cleaning that wasn't calibrated for slab-glued installation.
