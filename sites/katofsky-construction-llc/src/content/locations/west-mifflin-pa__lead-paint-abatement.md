## Why West Mifflin's Housing Stock Makes Lead a Persistent Issue

The borough developed rapidly alongside the steel and manufacturing industries that defined this stretch of the Monongahela Valley. That growth produced dense residential neighborhoods filled with homes constructed between the 1920s and 1960s, the exact window when lead-based paint was the standard interior and exterior finish. Older wood-frame construction in particular tends to accumulate multiple paint layers over the decades, and each layer beneath the most recent coat is a candidate for lead content.

Allegheny County's climate adds friction. Freeze-thaw cycles through Pittsburgh's winters cause wood trim, window sashes, and porch components to expand and contract repeatedly, which accelerates paint chalking and chipping. By the time a homeowner notices peeling paint on a window sill or exterior soffit, the deterioration has often been progressing for years. Disturbing that paint without proper containment and EPA-compliant procedures creates a hazard that lingers in dust long after the visible flaking is gone.

## Our Lead Paint Abatement Process in West Mifflin

Every project begins with a thorough assessment of the affected surfaces. If testing has not already been completed, we can coordinate lead paint testing before any physical work begins, so the scope is defined by data rather than assumption.

Once the scope is confirmed, the work follows a structured sequence:

- **Containment setup**: Plastic sheeting, negative air pressure where required, and sealed work zones prevent dust migration into living areas or adjacent rooms.
- **Surface preparation and removal**: Depending on the substrate and condition, abatement may involve wet scraping, chemical stripping, encapsulation with approved materials, or full component replacement. The method is chosen based on what the surface requires, not what is fastest.
- **HEPA vacuuming and wet wiping**: All work areas are cleaned using HEPA-filtered equipment and damp wiping protocols to capture fine particulate that ordinary vacuums recirculate.
- **Waste disposal**: Lead-containing debris is bagged, labeled, and disposed of in accordance with Pennsylvania DEP requirements for hazardous materials.
- **Clearance testing**: A post-abatement clearance wipe test, conducted by a qualified inspector, confirms that dust lead levels meet the federal clearance standards before the space is reopened.

The work is performed under EPA Renovation, Repair and Painting (RRP) rule requirements, which govern how lead-safe practices are applied in pre-1978 housing.
