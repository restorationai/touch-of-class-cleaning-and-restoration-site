## Why Jeannette Properties See Sewage Backup Issues

Jeannette's housing stock skews heavily toward the early and mid-twentieth century. Many of these homes were built before modern sewer separation standards, meaning storm runoff and sanitary waste share the same underground lines. During heavy rainfall events, those shared lines can surcharge, and the path of least resistance is often a basement floor drain or a low-set toilet. The borough sits in a topographic bowl common to the Laurel Highlands foothills, and runoff from surrounding hillsides concentrates quickly in the valley streets. Clay tile lateral lines, which were the standard in this region for decades, are also prone to root intrusion and joint separation as they age, creating blockages that back flow into the structure. Homes on lots with high water tables compound the problem: when the ground is already saturated, there is nowhere for displaced water to go.

Septic systems are less common within Jeannette's borough limits than in the surrounding townships, but properties on the edges of the municipality sometimes operate on aging private systems. A failing septic tank or a clogged distribution field produces a different contamination profile than a municipal sewer backup, but the remediation urgency is the same.

## Our Sewage Cleanup and Sanitization Process in Jeannette

Raw sewage is classified as Category 3 water, the most contaminated class recognized by the IICRC S500 standard. The process is not simply pumping out standing water and running fans.

The first step is containment. Affected areas are isolated to prevent cross-contamination from foot traffic or HVAC circulation. All standing sewage is extracted using truck-mounted or portable units capable of handling solid-laden water. Porous materials that have absorbed contaminated water, including drywall, carpet, and insulation, are removed and bagged for disposal according to applicable waste handling requirements.

Once the space is cleared, every surface that contacted sewage is treated with EPA-registered disinfectants formulated for Category 3 losses. Structural cavities are inspected with moisture meters and thermal imaging to confirm contamination has not wicked into areas that look dry at the surface. Dehumidification and air movement equipment runs until readings confirm the structure has returned to normal moisture levels. Our team holds IICRC certification in Water Damage Restoration (WRT) and Applied Microbial Remediation (AMRT), which directly governs the protocols used for sewage-affected spaces.

## Jeannette Insurance Coordination

Sewage backup coverage is not automatic under a standard homeowner policy. Many carriers in Pennsylvania require a separate sewer backup endorsement, and some policies cap that coverage at amounts that may not reflect the full scope of a finished basement loss.
