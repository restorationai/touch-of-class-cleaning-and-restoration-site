## Why Penn Hills Properties See Lead Paint Issues

Allegheny County's housing inventory skews older than much of the country, and Penn Hills reflects that pattern clearly. A significant share of the township's single-family homes were built between the 1930s and the early 1970s, when lead-based paint was standard on both interior and exterior surfaces. The region's freeze-thaw cycle is hard on painted surfaces: repeated contraction and expansion causes paint to crack, chalk, and flake, which is exactly how lead dust enters a home's living environment. Older double-hung windows are a particular concern because the friction between sash and frame grinds painted surfaces against each other every time the window moves, generating fine lead-contaminated dust at sill level where children spend time.

Exterior surfaces face additional stress from Pittsburgh's humid summers and cold, wet winters. Peeling paint on soffits, fascia, and porch floors is common on homes throughout the township, and that deteriorating paint can migrate into soil around the foundation, creating a secondary exposure pathway that abatement planning needs to account for.

## Our Lead Paint Abatement Process in Penn Hills

Every project starts with a thorough inspection and testing phase. We collect paint chip samples and use XRF analysis where appropriate to identify lead-bearing surfaces before any disturbance begins. That step matters because disturbing lead paint without knowing where it is can spread contamination rather than contain it.

Once the scope is defined, containment goes up before any removal starts: poly sheeting seals the work area, negative air pressure units with HEPA filtration run continuously, and all HVAC registers in the affected zone are sealed. Removal methods depend on the substrate and the condition of the paint. Wet scraping, chemical stripping, and HEPA-vacuum-assisted sanding are the standard approaches for residential work; heat guns above a safe threshold and dry sanding without HEPA control are not used, because both generate lead dust or fumes at concentrations that contaminate the rest of the home.

Waste is bagged, labeled, and disposed of as hazardous material through licensed disposal channels. After removal, the area is cleaned using HEPA vacuuming followed by wet washing, and clearance testing verifies that dust levels meet EPA standards before containment comes down. The documentation package we provide is suitable for real estate disclosure, contractor records, and health department requirements.

## Local Note

One thing that comes up repeatedly in Penn Hills is the combination of original plaster walls and oil-based trim paint on pre-1950 homes. The plaster itself rarely contains lead, but the layers of oil-based paint on the woodwork surrounding it often do, and those layers can be thick, sometimes six or more coats built up over decades. Thick paint layers require a different removal strategy than thin latex topcoats: chemical strippers are often more appropriate than mechanical methods because they soften the paint without generating the fine airborne dust that mechanical abrasion produces. Knowing that going in saves time and keeps the air quality inside the containment zone manageable throughout the job.
