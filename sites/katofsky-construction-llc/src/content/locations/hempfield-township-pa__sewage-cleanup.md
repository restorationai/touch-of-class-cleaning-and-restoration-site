## Why Hempfield Township Properties See Sewage Backup Issues

Hempfield Township sits on a geology that does not drain quickly. The dense clay-heavy soils throughout much of Westmoreland County are slow to absorb surface water, and during heavy rainfall events, that pressure transfers directly into aging sewer laterals. Many residential properties in the township were built in the mid-twentieth century, when clay or cast-iron pipes were standard. Those materials are now well past their design life, and root intrusion from mature trees is a chronic problem along older residential streets.

Septic systems are also common in the more rural pockets of the township, particularly in areas farther from municipal sewer connections. A saturated drain field after a prolonged wet stretch can force raw effluent back through floor drains, laundry tubs, and basement toilets with very little warning. The combination of older infrastructure and soil conditions that hold moisture makes sewage events here more likely to recur without proper lateral inspection and remediation.

## Our Sewage Cleanup and Sanitization Process in Hempfield Township

Raw sewage is classified as Category 3 water, the most contaminated classification under the IICRC S500 standard, and it requires a structured removal and sanitization sequence rather than a simple mop-and-dry approach.

When a crew arrives, the first priority is stopping ongoing contamination at the source and establishing containment to prevent cross-contamination to unaffected areas of the home. Porous materials that have absorbed sewage, including carpet, pad, drywall, and certain types of insulation, are removed and bagged for proper disposal. Solid waste is extracted using industrial pumping equipment.

Once bulk material is removed, every affected hard surface is treated with EPA-registered disinfectants formulated for Category 3 losses. In Hempfield Township homes with finished basements, this step frequently extends to wall framing and subfloor sheathing, because sewage-contaminated water travels horizontally along the bottom plate before it is ever visible. Moisture readings are taken throughout the process, and structural drying equipment runs until readings confirm the assembly has returned to acceptable levels. Deodorization is the final step, addressing the persistent odor compounds that remain even after visible contamination is gone.

## Hempfield Township Insurance Coordination

Sewage backup coverage is not automatically included in a standard homeowners policy in Pennsylvania. It is typically added as a rider, and whether your carrier covers the loss depends on the cause: a municipal sewer surcharge, a failed internal lateral, or a septic system overflow are each handled differently by adjusters. Katofsky Construction LLC documents the loss thoroughly from arrival, photographs affected materials before removal, and provides the moisture mapping and scope documentation that carriers require to process a claim. That paperwork reduces back-and-forth with your adjuster and keeps the project moving.
