## Why Mount Lebanon Properties See Board-Up and Tarping Needs

Most of Mount Lebanon's residential neighborhoods were built between the 1920s and the 1950s, and that era of construction comes with specific vulnerabilities. Older wood-framed windows sit in casings that have shifted over decades, meaning impact damage from a fallen limb tends to shatter the entire frame rather than just the glass. Slate and clay-tile roofs, common on the larger homes throughout the municipality, are durable under normal conditions but crack under sudden impact and are difficult to patch temporarily without understanding how the underlying decking is laid. When a section of slate lets go, water reaches the attic structure fast.

Allegheny County's weather pattern adds pressure. Freeze-thaw cycles through late winter can push ice into existing small cracks along mortar lines and around chimney flashings. A flashing that held through December may fail in February, and by the time a homeowner notices a water stain on the ceiling, the sheathing above may already be saturated. Quick tarping that accounts for the pitch and material of the roof is not a one-size approach here.

## Our Emergency Board-Up and Tarping Process in Mount Lebanon

When a call comes in, the first step is a rapid assessment of the breach: size, height, material on either side of the opening, and whether the structure is safe to access. For window and door openings, plywood panels are cut and secured with structural screws into the surrounding framing rather than surface-tacked, which matters on older homes where exterior trim may be soft or painted over multiple times. Panels are sealed at the edges with foam backer to limit wind-driven rain infiltration while the property is secured.

For roof tarping, the approach depends on pitch and the material underneath. On the steeper rooflines common in Mount Lebanon, tarps are weighted and anchored using lumber cap strips screwed into the decking on either side of the damaged area rather than draped loosely. This method holds through sustained wind without tearing free or allowing water to pool and back up under the tarp edge. Every tarp installation is photographed before and after for insurance documentation.

## Local Note

Something worth knowing about Mount Lebanon's older brick homes: many of them have wood lintels above window and door openings rather than steel or concrete, which was standard practice in regional construction through the mid-century. When fire or impact damages the masonry around one of these openings, the lintel itself may be compromised even if it looks intact from the outside. A board-up that puts lateral pressure on a weakened lintel without recognizing this can cause additional masonry movement. Crews working in this housing stock check the condition of the surrounding masonry before anchoring panels, and adjust the fastening approach accordingly.
