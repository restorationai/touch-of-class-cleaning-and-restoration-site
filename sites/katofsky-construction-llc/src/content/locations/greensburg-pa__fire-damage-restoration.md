## Why Greensburg Properties Are Especially Vulnerable After a Fire

Much of Greensburg's residential housing stock was built before modern fire-resistant construction standards became common. Balloon-frame construction, which runs wall cavities continuously from basement to attic without fire blocking, is widespread in neighborhoods built before the 1950s. In a fire, that framing allows smoke and heat to travel vertically through wall cavities with little resistance, depositing soot and combustion byproducts in areas far removed from the visible burn zone. What looks like a contained kitchen fire can leave smoke residue inside wall cavities two floors away.

Westmoreland County's winters also matter here. Cold temperatures slow the off-gassing of smoke residue, which means odors that seem manageable in the first days after a fire can intensify as the structure warms back up. Homes that sit unheated after a loss, waiting on insurance authorization or contractor scheduling, often develop secondary odor problems that require additional treatment cycles.

## Our Fire Damage Restoration Process in Greensburg

Every fire loss is different, but the sequence of work follows a disciplined path. The first priority after a fire is structural safety assessment and emergency board-up or tarping to secure the building from weather and unauthorized entry. Greensburg's rainfall patterns mean an unsecured roof opening can allow water intrusion within hours of a storm, compounding the fire damage with moisture damage.

Once the structure is stabilized, the scope of smoke and soot contamination is mapped. Dry soot from fast-burning fires behaves differently than wet, oily soot from slow smoldering combustion, each requires different cleaning chemistry and technique. Porous materials like plaster, original wood trim, and brick absorb odor compounds deeply, so thermal fogging or hydroxyl generation is often necessary alongside surface cleaning to reach embedded residue. Structural materials that cannot be cleaned to a safe standard are documented, removed, and replaced.

As an IICRC Certified Firm, the work follows established industry standards for fire and smoke restoration, giving your insurance adjuster a documented, defensible scope of loss.

## Local Note

Greensburg's older homes frequently have plaster-and-lath wall systems rather than drywall. After a fire, this matters in a specific way: plaster is dense and absorbs smoke odor compounds more deeply than drywall, but it is also more structurally intact after a fire than lath-backed drywall tends to be. That means more of the wall surface can often be cleaned and retained rather than demolished, which reduces rebuild cost and preserves original character. The tradeoff is that cleaning plaster thoroughly takes longer and requires chemistry matched to the alkaline surface. Crews working in Greensburg's pre-war housing stock need to account for this from the start, not discover it mid-project.
