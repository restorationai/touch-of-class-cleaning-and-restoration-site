## Why Munhall Homes See Upholstery Wear Faster

A lot of the Mon Valley's residential stock predates central HVAC. Window units and space heaters cool or warm individual rooms, but they don't manage humidity the way a whole-house system does. That means upholstery in living rooms facing the river side of a house often feels damp to the touch on muggy afternoons, even when nothing has spilled on it. Combine that with decades of foot traffic, pets, and the occasional basement-adjacent family room (common in split-levels built into Munhall's hillside lots), and fabric fibers break down faster while dust and allergens build up inside the cushion fill. Leather pieces fare differently: they crack and dry out when radiators run hard all winter, which is its own maintenance problem separate from soil and odor.

## Our Upholstery Cleaning Process

We start by identifying the fabric or leather type, since a wrong cleaning method can set a stain permanently or damage a hide. For fabric sofas and chairs, that usually means a pre-inspection for fiber type, a dry soil removal pass, then hot water extraction or low-moisture cleaning depending on how the piece is constructed. Furniture steam cleaning works well on synthetic blends and most cotton-poly weaves, but we dial back moisture on pieces with wood trim, older foam, or natural fiber content that doesn't tolerate heavy saturation. Leather upholstery gets a different track entirely: pH-balanced cleaners, conditioning after cleaning, and no steam at all. Given how much humidity already sits in a lot of Munhall living rooms, we also pay close attention to drying time, running air movers when a piece needs faster evaporation so it isn't sitting damp in a room that's already retaining moisture.
