## Why North Huntingdon Properties Are Particularly Vulnerable After a Storm or Fire

North Huntingdon Township sits in a bowl of rolling terrain that funnels weather systems coming off the Laurel Highlands before they flatten out toward the Monongahela Valley. That topography means wind gusts here routinely exceed what the surrounding flat suburbs see, and the township's mix of mid-century ranch homes, 1970s split-levels, and older farmhouse-style construction reflects decades of building practices that predate modern wind-load standards.

Roofs on homes built before the 1980s often used thinner decking and narrower rafter spacing than today's code requires. When a branch punches through or fire opens a hole in that decking, the structural integrity of the remaining sheathing is already marginal. A tarp secured incorrectly, or not at all, lets water wick under the felt, saturate the decking, and begin the clock on mold colonization, which can establish in as little as 48 to 72 hours in an unventilated attic space during humid Pennsylvania summers.

Fire-damaged structures carry an additional risk: smoke and heat compromise window glazing and door frames even when they appear intact, leaving gaps that are invisible until a cold front drops pressure and drives rain sideways through them.

## Our Emergency Board-Up and Tarping Process in North Huntingdon

When a call comes in from a North Huntingdon address, the first question is always structural access. We assess whether the roof is safe to walk before anyone climbs it. For fire losses, that means checking that the decking and rafters can bear crew weight; for storm damage, it means identifying where the sheathing is compromised versus where it is still sound.

Once access is confirmed, the process moves in a deliberate sequence:

- **Roof tarping**: We use reinforced polyethylene tarps rated for high-wind exposure, secured with lumber battens screwed through the tarp into sound decking at the perimeter. Weighted edges alone fail in the wind events North Huntingdon sees.
- **Window and door board-up**: Plywood panels are cut to fit the rough opening, not just slapped over the exterior. Interior furring strips distribute load so the panel does not lever out under pressure.
- **Documentation**: Every opening is photographed before and after securing. That photo set goes directly to your insurance adjuster and becomes part of the claim file.

Because Katofsky Construction LLC is an IICRC Certified Firm, the documentation we produce follows standards adjusters recognize, which tends to reduce back-and-forth on scope disputes.

## Local Note

Older homes throughout North Huntingdon Township were frequently built with board-and-batten or tongue-and-groove roof sheathing rather than plywood panels. When fire or impact damage opens one of those roofs, the individual boards often split or pull away from rafters in unpredictable patterns, leaving irregular gaps that a standard rectangular tarp cannot fully cover without custom overlap staging. Crews who have not worked this housing stock sometimes underestimate how much extra tarp material and batten length those irregular openings require. We account for that in the initial load-out rather than making a second trip.
