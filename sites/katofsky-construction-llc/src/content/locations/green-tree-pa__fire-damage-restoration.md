## Why Green Tree Properties Face Distinct Fire Damage Challenges

The residential character of Green Tree reflects decades of post-war suburban development. Many homes were built before modern fire-blocking requirements became standard in framing codes, which means fire and superheated gases can travel inside wall cavities and between floor assemblies before a homeowner even realizes the spread. Older knob-and-tube or early aluminum wiring, still present in some of the borough's aging housing stock, is a known ignition risk and can complicate post-fire structural assessment because the wiring path itself may need to be traced and documented before reconstruction planning begins.

The borough's proximity to Pittsburgh's western commercial belt also means some properties sit on lots with attached garages or carports, structures that frequently serve as fire origin points when fuel, solvents, or seasonal equipment are stored there. A garage fire in this type of attached configuration can compromise the shared wall and introduce smoke into the living space within minutes.

## Our Fire Damage Restoration Process in Green Tree

After a fire, the visible char is only part of the story. Smoke residue, particularly the oily, protein-based smoke produced by kitchen fires or the dry smoke from fast-burning materials, embeds into porous surfaces and continues to off-gas long after the flames are out. Our process begins with a thorough scope of the structure, documenting affected materials room by room before any cleaning or demolition begins. That documentation matters for your insurance carrier and for ensuring nothing is missed.

From there, we move through controlled demolition of unsalvageable materials, HEPA-filtered air scrubbing to capture airborne particulates, and surface-by-surface cleaning calibrated to the residue type. Plaster walls common in older Green Tree homes require a different approach than modern drywall: the surface may clean up well while smoke odor persists in the substrate beneath. Thermal fogging and hydroxyl treatment address odor at the molecular level rather than masking it. Structural drying equipment stays on-site if water was introduced during firefighting, since wet framing left inside a sealed structure will develop secondary mold damage within days.
