## Why Shaler Township Properties See Sewage Backup Issues

Much of Shaler Township was developed between the 1940s and 1970s, a period when combined sewer systems were standard practice across Allegheny County. Those systems carry both storm runoff and sanitary waste in the same pipe. During a heavy rain event, the volume can exceed pipe capacity, and the pressure finds the nearest low point: a floor drain, a laundry tub, or a basement toilet. Homes built on the hillsides and valley floors throughout the township share this vulnerability, and the clay-rich glacial soils common to this part of western Pennsylvania drain slowly, keeping hydrostatic pressure elevated long after a storm passes.

Older cast-iron and clay tile lateral lines, common in homes built before 1970, are also prone to root intrusion and joint separation. A slow-developing crack in a lateral can go undetected until a full blockage sends sewage backward into the lowest fixtures in the house. Finished basements, which are extremely common in Shaler Township's post-war housing stock, turn a plumbing failure into a full-scale remediation project almost immediately.

## Our Sewage Cleanup and Sanitization Process in Shaler Township

The first priority on any raw sewage job is containment. Technicians establish a work perimeter, identify the source of the backup, and confirm the line is cleared or isolated before extraction begins. Pumping standing sewage out of a basement before the source is controlled only delays the problem.

Once the area is safe to work, extraction removes standing water and solid waste. Affected flooring, drywall, and insulation are assessed: porous materials that have absorbed Category 3 contamination are typically removed rather than dried in place, because drying does not eliminate the pathogens embedded in the material. What remains is treated with EPA-registered disinfectants applied at the concentrations and dwell times the product label requires. Surfaces are then dried with commercial air movers and dehumidifiers, and post-treatment moisture readings confirm the structure is within acceptable parameters before the area is closed.

Documentation runs throughout the process: photographs of affected materials, moisture readings at multiple points, and a written scope. That record matters when an insurance claim is filed, and it matters again if a buyer or inspector ever asks about the property's history.
