## Why Duquesne Properties See Sewage Backup Issues

Duquesne sits along the Monongahela River and was built up fast during the steel era, which means a lot of the housing stock and underground infrastructure dates back to the first half of the twentieth century. Clay and cast-iron sewer laterals from that period are prone to root intrusion, bellying, and cracking, and once a line is compromised, heavy rain or river-stage rises can push flow backward into a house instead of out to the municipal system. Much of the Mon Valley, including Duquesne, is served by a combined sewer system through ALCOSAN, so during significant storms the same pipes carrying stormwater also carry sanitary sewage. When that system gets overwhelmed, backups into low-lying basements and ground-floor units are a known pattern, not a fluke. Homes set into the hillside grid above the river also tend to have older foundation walls where groundwater and sewage can both find a path in through the same cracks.

## Our Sewage Cleanup and Sanitization Process in Duquesne

We start by identifying the contamination category and shutting off the source, whether that's a failed lateral, a sewer line backup, or a septic overflow on a property still running an older private system. Standing sewage gets extracted first, then we remove any porous material that's absorbed contaminated water, drywall, carpet, insulation, subfloor, because those materials can't be sanitized and left in place. In Duquesne's older homes we're frequently dealing with original plaster and lath walls or balloon-frame construction, which trap moisture differently than modern drywall assemblies, so we adjust drying equipment placement and dwell time accordingly rather than running a generic drying schedule. Affected surfaces are then cleaned and treated with EPA-registered antimicrobials, and we verify dryness with moisture meters before closing a job, following IICRC S500 drying guidelines as the baseline standard for the work.
