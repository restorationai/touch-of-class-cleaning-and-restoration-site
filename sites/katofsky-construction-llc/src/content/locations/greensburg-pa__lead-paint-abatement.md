## Why Greensburg Properties Face Elevated Lead Paint Risk

Westmoreland County's residential building boom peaked in the early-to-mid twentieth century, and Greensburg served as its commercial and civic center. That timeline matters because the federal government did not ban lead-based paint in residential housing until 1978. Homes built before that year may contain lead paint; homes built before 1940 almost certainly do, often in multiple layers applied over decades of maintenance and repainting.

In Greensburg specifically, the combination of older plaster-and-lath interior walls and original wood trim creates conditions where lead paint is not just a surface concern. Plaster walls flex and crack with seasonal temperature swings, and western Pennsylvania winters are hard on older structures. That cracking disturbs paint layers, generating dust that settles into window channels, baseboards, and floor gaps. Friction surfaces like double-hung windows and older doors are among the highest-risk locations in any pre-1978 home, because normal daily use grinds painted surfaces together continuously.

The city's historic commercial corridor and surrounding residential streets include properties that have changed hands many times, with each owner adding paint over what came before. Identifying where lead actually exists, and in what concentration, requires testing before any abatement work begins.

## Our Lead Paint Abatement Process in Greensburg

Every project starts with testing. Before any material is disturbed, affected surfaces are sampled using XRF analysis or certified lab swabs to confirm the presence and concentration of lead. This step protects you, your family, and any workers on site, and it shapes the scope of work that follows.

Once testing confirms lead paint is present, the approach depends on the surface, its condition, and the renovation plans for the space. Encapsulation is appropriate for intact painted surfaces that will not be disturbed. Removal is required when surfaces are deteriorated, when friction or impact creates ongoing dust generation, or when the renovation scope demands it. Both methods require containment: plastic sheeting, negative air pressure where needed, and HEPA-filtered equipment to prevent cross-contamination to unaffected areas of the home.

Post-abatement clearance testing verifies that lead dust levels meet the standards set by the EPA and HUD before containment is removed and the space is returned to use. That final clearance step is not optional, and any contractor who skips it is leaving you with unverified air quality and potential liability.

Work in Greensburg also falls under Pennsylvania DEP oversight, and projects involving rental housing or child-occupied facilities carry additional notification and documentation requirements.
