## Why Plum Properties See Sewage Backup Issues

Plum Borough sits in a part of Allegheny County where residential development pushed outward from Pittsburgh through the 1950s, 60s, and 70s. A significant portion of the housing stock was built during that era, and the sewer laterals connecting those homes to the municipal system are now 50 to 70 years old. Cast iron corrodes. Clay tile joints shift. Tree roots, particularly from the mature hardwoods common throughout Plum's residential streets, find their way into lateral seams and slowly restrict flow until a heavy rain event or a grease buildup tips a slow drain into a full backup.

The topography matters too. Plum's rolling terrain means some basements sit lower than others relative to the sewer main. When the municipal system surcharges during a significant storm, the homes with the lowest-elevation connections are the first to see sewage push back through floor drains, laundry tubs, and toilets. That's not a plumbing failure in the traditional sense; it's a hydraulic reality of where the house sits on the land.

## Our Sewage Cleanup and Sanitization Process in Plum

Raw sewage is classified as Category 3 water, the most contaminated category recognized by the IICRC, and the process for handling it reflects that. The crew arrives with personal protective equipment, establishes containment to prevent cross-contamination into unaffected areas of the home, and begins extraction of standing sewage using truck-mounted or portable extraction units depending on access.

Once standing material is removed, affected hard surfaces, porous materials, and structural components are assessed. Drywall, insulation, and flooring that have been saturated with Category 3 water are typically removed rather than dried in place. Attempting to dry contaminated porous materials without removing them leaves pathogens embedded in the structure. After removal, all affected surfaces are treated with EPA-registered disinfectants and allowed appropriate dwell time before any drying equipment is placed. The goal is a structure that's both dry and genuinely sanitized, not just visually clean.

The IICRC S500 standard guides the drying protocol after sanitization is complete. In Plum's older homes, this often means accounting for concrete block foundation walls and original hardwood subfloors that hold moisture differently than modern OSB construction.
