## Why Latrobe Properties See Asbestos Issues

Westmoreland County's building boom ran from roughly the 1920s through the 1960s, and Latrobe was no exception. Homes from that era commonly contain asbestos in places that are easy to overlook: the paper backing on floor tiles, the joint compound on plaster walls, pipe insulation in basements, and the spray-applied texture on ceilings that contractors called "popcorn" finishes. Because these materials were applied in layers over decades, a single renovation project can disturb more than one type of asbestos-containing material at the same time.

The region's freeze-thaw cycle adds another layer of risk. Repeated expansion and contraction through Pennsylvania winters causes older pipe insulation to crack and flake, and it can loosen the adhesive holding floor tiles in place. Once those materials are friable, meaning they crumble when handled, fibers can become airborne with very little disturbance. That physical deterioration is one of the most common reasons Latrobe homeowners call for testing before a basement finishing project or a bathroom remodel.

## Our Asbestos Abatement Process in Latrobe

The process starts with a formal inspection and bulk sampling before any removal work is quoted or scheduled. Samples go to an accredited laboratory, and no abatement work begins until results confirm what materials are present and in what condition. That sequence protects you from paying for removal that may not be necessary and ensures the scope of work is accurate before a crew arrives.

Once confirmed, the work area is isolated with negative air pressure containment and heavy-duty poly sheeting. Air scrubbers with HEPA filtration run continuously throughout the removal to capture any fibers that become airborne during the process. All removed materials are wetted to suppress dust, double-bagged in labeled asbestos waste bags, and transported to a licensed disposal facility that accepts regulated asbestos-containing material. Pennsylvania DEP notification requirements apply to projects above certain thresholds, and we handle that paperwork as part of the job, not as an add-on.

Clearance air testing after abatement confirms that fiber levels are within acceptable limits before containment is removed. That final step is what allows a contractor, flooring installer, or HVAC technician to safely re-enter and continue the renovation.

## Local Note

One pattern that comes up repeatedly in Latrobe-area homes built before 1960 is asbestos-containing floor tile installed directly over a wood subfloor, with a second layer of vinyl tile laid on top of it in a later decade. From the surface, the home looks like it has a single layer of flooring. Once the top layer is pulled, the original nine-inch or twelve-inch tiles underneath are often intact but friable at the edges. Homeowners and general contractors who are not expecting a second layer sometimes proceed without stopping for testing. If your home was built before 1960 and you are pulling up any flooring, it is worth assuming there may be more than one generation of tile underneath until sampling says otherwise.
