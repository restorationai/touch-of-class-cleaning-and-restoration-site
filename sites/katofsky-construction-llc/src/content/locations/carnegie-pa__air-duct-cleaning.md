Carnegie sits just southwest of Pittsburgh in a borough where older housing stock, variable winter humidity, and decades of industrial-era construction have left many homes with ductwork that hasn't seen a proper cleaning in years. If your forced-air system is pushing musty air through rooms or your utility bills have crept up without explanation, the ducts themselves are often the place to start looking.

## Why Carnegie Properties See Duct Issues

Much of Carnegie's residential housing was built between the 1920s and 1960s, when galvanized steel ductwork was installed with wide trunk lines and narrow branch runs that were never designed with future serviceability in mind. Over time, those seams collect fine particulate from the region's historically industrial air, and the damp winters that roll in off the Ohio River valley push humidity into crawl spaces and basement mechanical rooms where supply and return ducts often run. Condensation inside uninsulated duct sections creates exactly the environment where dust, mold spores, and debris accumulate fastest. Homes with original gravity-converted systems or add-on central air are especially prone to this, because the duct geometry was never optimized for the airflow speeds modern air handlers produce.

## Our Air Duct Cleaning Process in Carnegie

The process starts with a camera inspection of the main trunk lines before any equipment is connected. That walkthrough tells us whether debris is concentrated near the air handler, in branch runs, or at the registers themselves, which changes how we sequence the work. We use negative-pressure collection with a HEPA-filtered vacuum unit attached at the main trunk, then work register by register with rotary brush agitation to break loose compacted dust and pull it toward the collection point rather than redistributing it through the house. Every supply and return register is cleaned and reseated. If the system includes a dryer vent, we inspect and clear that separately because lint buildup in dryer vents is a fire risk that operates on its own timeline, independent of how clean the HVAC ducts are. Before we leave, we check static pressure at the air handler to confirm airflow has improved.

## Equipment and Methods

For residential duct cleaning, the equipment that matters most is the collection unit's filtration rating and the agitation method used in branch runs. We use truck-mounted or high-capacity portable negative-air machines with HEPA filtration so that what comes out of the ducts does not re-enter the living space. Rotary brush systems are matched to duct diameter, which matters in Carnegie's older homes where branch runs are frequently non-standard sizes cut to fit around plaster walls and floor joists. For duct sanitization, we apply an EPA-registered antimicrobial to interior duct surfaces after cleaning when conditions warrant it, such as when visible biological growth or persistent odor is present.

## Local Note

One pattern specific to Carnegie's older residential blocks is the presence of converted octopus furnace systems. These gravity-fed units were common through the mid-20th century, and many were retrofitted with blower motors rather than replaced entirely. The large-diameter sheet metal plenum sections on those systems are cavernous compared to modern ductwork and tend to accumulate decades of debris in corners and low spots that standard cleaning wands cannot reach without extended flexible attachments. If your home was built before 1955 and still has the original large-trunk duct configuration, mention it when you call so the crew brings the right tooling.
