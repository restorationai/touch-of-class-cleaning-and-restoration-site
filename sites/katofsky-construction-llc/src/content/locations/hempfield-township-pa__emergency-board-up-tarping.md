## Why Hempfield Township Properties Face Elevated Board-Up and Tarping Needs

Hempfield Township sits in a part of Westmoreland County where the terrain transitions from river valley to rolling upland, and that geography shapes how storms move through. Fast-moving lines of thunderstorms can accelerate as they track across open ground, generating localized wind damage that strips ridge caps and flashing before homeowners even hear the thunder. Ice damming is a recurring issue in colder winters: melt water backs up behind frozen eaves, forces under shingles, and creates interior leaks that go unnoticed until a ceiling stain appears weeks later.

The township also carries a substantial inventory of mid-century homes built between the late 1940s and early 1970s. When a window fails or a roof section lifts, the opening is often larger and less uniform than what you would find on newer construction, which means board-up work has to account for irregular framing and deteriorated surrounding material rather than a clean factory-cut opening.

## Our Emergency Board-Up and Tarping Process in Hempfield Township

When you call, a crew is dispatched immediately. The first step on arrival is a rapid exterior assessment: we identify every compromised opening, photograph the full perimeter for your insurance documentation, and flag any structural concerns that affect safe access. For broken window board-up, we use plywood cut and fitted to the rough opening, secured with screws rather than nails so removal is clean when your glazier arrives. For fire damage board-up, we pay particular attention to vented areas where heat or smoke has traveled, because those pathways often create secondary openings in soffits or gable ends that are easy to miss in the dark.

Roof tarping follows a specific sequence. We anchor heavy-duty polyethylene tarps at the ridge and run them down past the eave line, weighting and securing edges to resist wind uplift. On steeper-pitched roofs common in older Hempfield Township neighborhoods, we use additional anchor boards across the tarp surface to prevent billowing. The goal is a seal that holds through the next rain event while your contractor or adjuster schedules permanent repairs.
