## Why Ross Township Homes Accumulate Duct Debris Faster Than You'd Expect

Allegheny County's mix of older housing stock and variable seasonal humidity creates conditions that accelerate particulate buildup inside duct systems. Homes built in the postwar decades, which make up a significant share of Ross Township's residential neighborhoods, were often constructed with sheet-metal ductwork that has accumulated decades of use. Joints in older systems can develop small gaps over time, pulling in unconditioned air from attic spaces or crawlspaces along with whatever particles are floating there.

The Pittsburgh region's spring pollen season is also notably heavy, and Ross Township's tree canopy contributes to that load. Every time a door opens or a window is cracked during high-pollen weeks, some of that material finds its way into the return air path. Over a full heating and cooling cycle, those particles settle into the duct lining, where they sit until airflow disturbs them or a cleaning crew removes them.

Dryer vent lines are a related concern that often gets overlooked. Lint accumulation in dryer vents is a documented fire risk, and in attached garages or finished basements common in this area, a clogged vent line also pushes excess humidity into living spaces.

## Our Air Duct Cleaning Process in Ross Township

The work begins with an inspection of your supply and return registers, the air handler, and the accessible duct runs. Before any equipment goes into the system, the crew identifies the duct material, checks for any visible damage or disconnected sections, and confirms the layout so nothing gets missed.

Cleaning uses negative-pressure equipment: a high-powered vacuum is connected to the main trunk line to create suction throughout the system while rotating brush attachments work through each branch duct. This combination pulls debris toward the collection point rather than redistributing it through the home. Supply registers are cleaned individually, and the air handler cabinet, blower assembly, and evaporator coil area are addressed as part of the same visit.

For homes where moisture history or visible biological growth is a concern, air duct sanitization is available as an add-on step. This applies an EPA-registered antimicrobial agent to interior duct surfaces after the mechanical cleaning is complete. Katofsky Construction holds IICRC AMRT certification in applied microbial remediation, which informs how the team evaluates whether sanitization is warranted rather than treating it as a default upsell.

Dryer vent cleaning follows a separate process: the vent line is cleared from the dryer connection to the exterior termination cap, and the cap itself is inspected to confirm it opens and closes properly.
