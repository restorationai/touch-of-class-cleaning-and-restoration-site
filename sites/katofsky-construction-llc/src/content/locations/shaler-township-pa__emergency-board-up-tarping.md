## Why Shaler Township Properties See Board-Up and Tarping Emergencies

Shaler Township's housing stock skews older. Much of the residential development here happened in the mid-twentieth century, and those homes were built with dimensional lumber framing, wood-sheathed roofs, and single-pane windows that are less forgiving when a storm hits. A roof deck from the 1960s that loses several courses of shingles in a hailstorm will absorb water quickly because the underlying sheathing was never designed to sit exposed. The same goes for older masonry chimneys common in this part of Allegheny County: when mortar fails and a cap shifts, the flue becomes an open channel straight into the attic.

The terrain adds another layer. Shaler Township's rolling topography channels wind in ways that flat suburban layouts don't. A storm that reads as moderate on a weather radar can produce localized gusts along ridge lines that are strong enough to damage roofing and siding. Fire damage is also a recurring trigger for board-up calls here, particularly in attached garages and older wood-frame structures where a contained fire can still leave multiple openings that need to be secured before the insurance adjuster arrives.

## Our Emergency Board-Up and Tarping Process in Shaler Township

When a call comes in, the first priority is a quick assessment of every compromised opening, not just the obvious one. A broken front window is easy to spot. A soffit panel blown loose on the back of the house, or a door frame warped by fire heat, is easy to miss in the dark. We document each opening with photographs before any material goes up, which matters when your insurance carrier asks for pre-mitigation evidence.

For window and door board-ups, we use exterior-grade plywood cut and secured to protect the opening without damaging surrounding trim any more than necessary. For roof damage, heavy-duty polyethylene tarps are anchored with batten boards and fastened to undamaged decking rather than draped loosely, which is the difference between a tarp that holds through a week of Pittsburgh rain and one that peels back in the next wind event. On steep-pitch roofs common in Shaler Township's older neighborhoods, proper anchor placement takes more time but prevents the tarp from becoming a problem of its own.
