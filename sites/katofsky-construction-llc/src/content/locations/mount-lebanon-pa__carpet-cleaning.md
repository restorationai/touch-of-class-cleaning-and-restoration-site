## Why Mount Lebanon Carpets Take More Abuse Than Most

The Pittsburgh region's four distinct seasons hit Mount Lebanon with a particular combination: heavy spring rainfall, humid summers that keep indoor humidity elevated for months, and winters that drive road salt and sand through front doors on boots and paws. Clay-rich soil from the surrounding hillsides clings to carpet fibers differently than sandy or loamy soil. It compacts into the pile base rather than sitting near the surface, which means a standard vacuum pass barely touches it. Over time, that compacted grit acts like sandpaper on carpet backing, accelerating fiber breakdown well before the carpet looks visibly dirty.

Mount Lebanon's housing stock adds another layer. A significant portion of homes here were built between the 1940s and 1970s, and many still have original or early-replacement carpet installed over concrete slab or older wood subfloors. Those subfloors can hold residual moisture that wicks upward into carpet padding, creating an environment where allergens and microbial growth take hold quietly. Finished basements with wall-to-wall carpet are especially common in this community, and they are consistently the spaces where odor and staining problems go unaddressed the longest.

## Our Carpet Cleaning Process in Mount Lebanon

Every job starts with a fiber and backing assessment before any water or solution touches the floor. Wool blends common in older Mount Lebanon homes require lower-pH cleaning agents and cooler water temperatures than synthetic nylon; getting that wrong causes shrinkage or color bleed that no amount of re-cleaning fixes. Once fiber type is confirmed, a pre-treatment solution is applied to break down the clay-based soil and organic staining that accumulates in this area's conditions.

Hot water extraction follows, calibrated to the pile depth and backing material. For basement installations over concrete, the extraction process is adjusted to pull moisture aggressively from the pad as well as the face fiber, reducing the drying window in a space that already has limited airflow. Air movers and dehumidifiers stay on-site until moisture readings confirm the pad and subfloor are within safe range, not just until the surface feels dry to the touch. That distinction matters in a basement environment where surface drying can mask a saturated pad underneath.

Stain treatment for high-traffic areas, pet spots, and food staining is handled as a separate step after extraction rather than before, which allows the cleaning process to remove the bulk of the contamination first and lets targeted chemistry work on what remains.
