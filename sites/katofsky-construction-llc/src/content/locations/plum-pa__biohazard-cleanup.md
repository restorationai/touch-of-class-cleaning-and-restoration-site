## Why Plum's Housing Stock Matters for Biohazard Remediation

A significant portion of Plum's residential neighborhoods were developed between the 1950s and 1970s, with pockets of even older construction near the borough's original settled areas. Porous substrates, older subfloor assemblies, and crawl spaces common in this housing stock mean that surface-level cleaning is rarely sufficient. Contamination can wick into wall cavities, underlayment, and structural lumber in ways that are not visible to the naked eye.

Biohazard remediation in these homes requires methodical material testing, not assumptions based on what the surface looks like. When infectious material contacts a porous substrate, the affected layer often needs to be removed and properly disposed of as regulated waste rather than simply treated in place.

## Our Biohazard Cleanup Process in Plum

Every job begins with a thorough assessment of the affected area before any cleaning starts. The scope of contamination is mapped, and materials are categorized by porosity and exposure level. Personal protective equipment appropriate to the specific biohazard type is worn throughout.
