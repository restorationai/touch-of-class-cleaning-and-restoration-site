## Why Murrysville Properties See Lead Paint Concerns

Westmoreland County's older residential stock is the core issue. Homes built before 1978 may contain lead-based paint, and homes built before 1940 are especially likely to have multiple layers of it, sometimes applied over original plaster and horsehair walls that predate modern drywall by decades. In Murrysville specifically, the mix of mid-century ranch homes and earlier two-story construction means lead can turn up in places owners do not expect: under vinyl flooring laid over original wood subfloors, inside window channels that have been painted shut and then forced open repeatedly, and along the friction surfaces of stair risings.

Pennsylvania's climate adds a mechanical dimension. Freeze-thaw cycling through Westmoreland County winters causes wood to expand and contract, which gradually breaks the bond between old paint layers and the substrate beneath. By the time a homeowner notices peeling on an exterior soffit or a window casing, the paint has often already been shedding fine particles into the soil below and onto interior sills. That weathering pattern makes exterior lead paint abatement here a genuine seasonal concern, not just a pre-renovation checkbox.

## Our Lead Paint Abatement Process in Murrysville

Every project starts with a lead paint inspection or risk assessment to establish what is present and where. If testing confirms lead-containing materials, the work scope is built around containment first. That means plastic sheeting, negative air pressure where interior work is involved, and HEPA-filtered equipment throughout. Nothing leaves the work area until it has been bagged and labeled for proper disposal under Pennsylvania DEP requirements.

For interior surfaces, we use a combination of chemical stripping, encapsulation, and component replacement depending on the substrate and the condition of the paint. Encapsulation is appropriate when the surface is sound and undisturbed; removal is required when the paint is deteriorating or when the component will be subject to friction or impact during normal use. Exterior work follows the same containment logic, with ground-cloth staging to capture any debris before it reaches soil.

Clearance testing by an independent third-party inspector closes out every abatement project. That final step is not optional under EPA RRP rules, and it gives you documented proof that the hazard has been addressed, which matters when you sell the property or apply for permits on future work.
