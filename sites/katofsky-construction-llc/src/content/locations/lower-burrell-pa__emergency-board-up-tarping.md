## Why Lower Burrell Properties Face Distinct Board-Up and Tarping Challenges

The housing stock in Lower Burrell leans heavily on construction from the mid-twentieth century, when the steel industry was still driving growth in the Alle-Kiski Valley. Homes built in that era often feature large single-pane picture windows, older wood-framed rooflines with minimal sheathing overlap, and masonry facades that can crack or spall when fire or impact damage stresses the structure. Those architectural details matter when a crew is sizing plywood panels or anchoring a tarp, because a standard sheet that fits a modern vinyl-clad window may need custom cutting to cover an oversized aluminum-frame opening from the 1960s.

The local climate adds its own pressure. Western Pennsylvania winters bring freeze-thaw cycles that can widen a compromised roof opening overnight. A tarp installed without proper ballasting and edge sealing will lift in the wind gusts that move up the Kiskiminetas River corridor, turning a temporary fix into a recurring problem. Katofsky crews account for both the building vintage and the weather window when selecting fastener depth, tarp weight, and panel overlap.

## Our Emergency Board-Up and Tarping Process in Lower Burrell

When a call comes in, the first step is a rapid assessment of what type of opening needs to be secured and what caused it. Fire damage board-up requires different staging than broken window board-up after a storm, because smoke and heat can compromise the structural integrity of the surrounding framing. The crew evaluates the load-bearing condition before anchoring anything.

For roof tarping, the process starts at the ridge and works downward, overlapping sections so water sheds away from the breach rather than pooling at the edges. Tarps are weighted and fastened to undamaged decking, not to damaged rafters that may shift. For window and door openings, plywood panels are cut to fit, sealed at the perimeter with foam backer, and secured with screws rather than nails so removal is clean when permanent repairs begin. Every opening is photographed before and after boarding, which creates a clear record for the insurance claim.

## Local Note

Older homes in Lower Burrell and the surrounding Alle-Kiski Valley corridor were frequently built with balloon-frame construction rather than the platform framing used in newer builds. In a balloon-frame house, wall cavities run continuously from the foundation sill to the roof, which means a fire or severe impact on one section of the structure can create hidden pathways for smoke, water, and outside air to travel farther than the visible damage suggests. When boarding up a balloon-frame home, the crew checks adjacent openings and attic access points, not just the obvious breach, because an unsecured soffit vent or gable louver can undermine an otherwise solid board-up.
