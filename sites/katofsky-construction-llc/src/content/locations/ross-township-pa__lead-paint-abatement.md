## Why Ross Township Properties See Lead Paint Issues

Allegheny County's northern suburbs developed rapidly in the post-war decades, but a significant share of Ross Township's residential and commercial buildings predate 1940. Homes built during that era routinely used lead-based paint on interior trim, window sashes, door frames, and exterior siding because it was durable and widely available. The problem compounds over time: repeated layers of paint applied over decades can trap lead-laden material that stays stable until a renovation disturbs it, a window is sanded, or a radiator pipe is cut.

The climate pattern in western Pennsylvania adds another layer of stress. Freeze-thaw cycling through Pittsburgh-area winters causes wood substrates to expand and contract, which eventually cracks paint film and creates fine lead dust at friction surfaces like double-hung windows. In older Ross Township homes with original wood windows, this is one of the most common sources of ongoing lead exposure, and it is one that a coat of fresh paint alone will not solve.

## Our Lead Paint Abatement Process in Ross Township

Every project begins with testing. If you do not already have a lead inspection report or risk assessment from a Pennsylvania-certified inspector, we can help you understand what documentation you need before abatement work begins. Once the scope is confirmed, the process follows EPA Renovation, Repair, and Painting (RRP) protocols, which govern how work areas are contained, how dust is controlled, and how debris is disposed of.

Containment is set up before any disturbing work begins: plastic sheeting isolates the work area, HVAC registers are sealed, and foot traffic paths are protected. Abatement methods depend on the substrate and the condition of the paint. Intact paint on a stable surface may be encapsulated with an approved barrier coating. Deteriorated paint, friction surfaces, and impact surfaces typically require full removal using wet methods, HEPA-equipped tools, and chemical strippers where heat or dry sanding would otherwise generate airborne dust.

After the work is complete, the area is cleaned using HEPA vacuuming and wet wiping before clearance testing is performed. Clearance is conducted by an independent, certified inspector, not by the abatement crew, which is a requirement under Pennsylvania's lead abatement regulations. We coordinate that step so the handoff is smooth and you receive the clearance report you need for permits, insurance, or tenant documentation.

## Local Note

One thing that comes up repeatedly in Ross Township's older residential pockets is the combination of original horsehair plaster walls and multiple generations of oil-based paint. Plaster substrates hold paint differently than modern drywall: the surface is harder and more brittle, which means mechanical removal methods that work cleanly on drywall can fracture the plaster face and create a secondary repair problem. In these situations, chemical stripping or careful encapsulation is often the more practical path, and it is worth discussing substrate condition in detail before settling on a removal method. Skipping that conversation can turn a contained abatement project into a full plaster repair job.
