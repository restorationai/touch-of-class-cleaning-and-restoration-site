## Why Ross Township Roofs Wear Out Faster Than Homeowners Expect

Allegheny County's climate puts roofing materials through a punishing annual cycle. Temperatures in the Pittsburgh area routinely swing from below freezing in January to high humidity in July and August, and that expansion-and-contraction stress shortens the effective life of asphalt shingles. In Ross Township specifically, mature trees lining residential streets deposit leaves and organic debris that hold moisture against shingle surfaces, accelerating algae growth and granule loss. Low-slope sections of roof, common on the ranch-style and split-level homes built throughout the township in the 1950s through the 1970s, are especially vulnerable because water has less momentum to shed before it finds a seam.

Ice dams are another recurring problem. When attic heat escapes through an under-insulated roof deck, snow melts at the peak and refreezes at the cold eaves. That ice ridge forces meltwater back under shingles, and the damage often doesn't show up as an interior stain until the following spring. By the time a homeowner notices, the decking beneath can already be compromised.

## Our Roofing Installation and Replacement Process in Ross Township

Every project starts with a thorough inspection of the existing roof system: shingles, underlayment, decking, flashing, ridge vents, and soffit conditions. We document what we find before any material is removed, which matters both for your records and for insurance purposes if storm damage is part of the picture.

Once the scope is confirmed, we strip the old roofing down to the deck and inspect the sheathing for soft spots, rot, or delamination. Decking repairs happen before anything new goes on, skipping that step is how a new roof develops problems in year three. We install a high-quality synthetic underlayment, then lay the new shingles or roofing system working from the eaves up, with particular attention to valley flashing and any penetrations (chimneys, skylights, plumbing vents) where water most commonly finds its way in. Ridge cap and ventilation are finished last, and the site is cleaned and inspected before we close out.

For homes in Ross Township with steeper pitches or complex rooflines, we factor in additional staging time and safety equipment. Work is coordinated so neighbors aren't blocked for extended periods, which matters on the narrower residential streets throughout the township.

## Local Note

Homes in Ross Township built in the late 1950s and 1960s frequently have original 1x6 skip-sheathing under the existing shingles rather than plywood decking. That construction method was standard at the time, but it means the decking situation under a worn roof can look very different once tear-off begins. Gaps between boards need to be filled or the entire deck re-sheathed before modern synthetic underlayment can be installed correctly. It's not a reason to delay a replacement, but it is something to budget for, and something worth asking about during any estimate on a home of that era in Ross Township.
