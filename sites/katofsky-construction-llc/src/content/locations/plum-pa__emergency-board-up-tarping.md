## Our Emergency Board-Up and Tarping Process in Plum

When the call comes in, the crew loads plywood panels, heavy-mil polyethylene tarps, furring strips, and fastening hardware before leaving the yard. Arriving with materials already staged means no second trip to a supply house while your living room fills with rain.

The first step on-site is a quick safety walk. Fire-damaged structures and storm-struck roofs can have compromised load-bearing members that aren't obvious from the ground. Once the crew confirms it's safe to work, they prioritize the largest openings first, then move to secondary breaches like broken sidelights, kicked-in doors, or cracked skylights.

For roof tarping, panels are anchored with furring strips screwed through the tarp into sound decking on either side of the breach, not just draped and weighted. This matters in Plum's wind corridor: a tarp that's simply laid over a hole can become a sail in a 30-mph gust and cause secondary damage to neighboring shingles or gutters. For window and door board-up, panels are cut to fit the rough opening and fastened into the surrounding framing, not just the trim, so the closure holds against both wind pressure and opportunistic entry.

Documentation runs parallel to the physical work. Crew members photograph every opening before and after closure, capture measurements, and note the materials used. That documentation goes directly to the homeowner and can be handed off to an insurance adjuster the same day.
