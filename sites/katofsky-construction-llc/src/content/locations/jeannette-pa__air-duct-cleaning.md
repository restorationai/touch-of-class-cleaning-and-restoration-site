## Our Air Duct Cleaning Process in Jeannette

The process starts with a visual inspection of the accessible supply and return registers, the air handler, and any visible main trunk lines. For homes with older duct configurations common in Jeannette, that inspection often reveals gaps at takeoffs and elbows that need to be addressed before cleaning so that negative pressure during the job does not pull debris into living spaces.

Cleaning uses high-powered negative air equipment combined with agitation tools sized to the duct diameter. Supply lines are worked from the register back toward the trunk, and return lines are worked from the air handler outward. The air handler cabinet itself, including the blower wheel and evaporator coil housing, is cleaned as part of the same visit rather than treated as a separate job. When the ductwork shows visible microbial growth or the homeowner has documented moisture intrusion near the system, air duct sanitization is applied after mechanical cleaning using an EPA-registered product appropriate for HVAC surfaces.

Dryer vent cleaning follows a separate process. Jeannette homes built before the 1980s often have dryer vents routed through longer-than-code runs or through masonry chases originally designed for other exhaust. Those configurations trap lint at bends and require rotary brush systems rather than simple suction to clear completely.
