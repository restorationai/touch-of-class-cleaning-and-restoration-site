A significant share of Green Tree's residential housing stock dates from the 1950s through the 1970s, when duct systems were often oversized for the furnaces of the era and installed with more joints and turns than modern design allows. ## Our Air Duct Cleaning Process in Green Tree

The work starts with a camera inspection of the main trunk lines and several representative branch runs. This step matters because older duct systems in Green Tree sometimes have flex duct connections that have partially collapsed or sheet metal seams that have separated, and aggressive negative-pressure cleaning on a compromised duct can make a small problem significantly worse. We identify those conditions before any equipment goes to work.

Once the system is mapped, we use truck-mounted or high-capacity portable negative-air equipment to establish continuous suction at the main return. Each supply register is cleaned individually from the inside out, with rotary brush agitation on metal duct and compressed-air whipping tools on flex runs. Debris is captured in a sealed collection system rather than exhausted into the home. Supply and return plenums, the air handler cabinet interior, and the blower wheel are included in a complete cleaning, not treated as add-ons.

If the inspection reveals visible microbial growth or a persistent odor that survives mechanical cleaning, we offer EPA-registered air duct sanitization applied as a fine mist after the debris has been removed. The sanitizer is applied to clean surfaces, not over accumulated dust, which is the only way it performs as labeled.

Dryer vent cleaning is performed as a separate scope using a rotary brush system run from the exterior termination inward. Green Tree homes with longer dryer vent runs, common in split-level layouts where the laundry is on a lower level and the exterior wall is far from the appliance, are at higher risk for lint accumulation and the reduced airflow that precedes dryer fires. We measure static pressure before and after to confirm the vent is clear.
