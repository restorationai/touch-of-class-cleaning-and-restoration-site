## Why Green Tree Properties See Lead Paint Issues

Allegheny County's older boroughs share a common pattern: housing built between the 1930s and 1970s, constructed with lead paint on virtually every painted surface. Green Tree is no exception. The borough's residential stock includes a high concentration of homes where lead paint was applied in multiple layers over decades, then sealed under newer coats of latex. That layering hides the hazard until a renovation, a hard winter, or simple age causes the surface to crack or blister.

The Pittsburgh region's freeze-thaw cycle is particularly hard on exterior painted surfaces. Window frames and porch trim expand and contract through repeated temperature swings, and that movement eventually breaks the paint film. Once the surface is disturbed, lead dust becomes airborne and settles into soil around the foundation, into interior dust, and onto surfaces where children spend time. In older Green Tree homes with original wood double-hung windows, this process happens every winter without anyone noticing until the damage is visible.

## Our Lead Paint Abatement Process in Green Tree

Every project starts with a thorough assessment of the affected surfaces before any removal begins. We identify the scope, document existing conditions with photographs, and establish a containment plan that fits the specific layout of the home. In Green Tree's older bungalows and two-story frame houses, that often means working room by room with sealed polyethylene barriers and negative air pressure to prevent cross-contamination.

Abatement itself depends on the surface type and condition. Intact paint that poses no immediate disturbance risk may be encapsulated rather than removed. Paint on friction surfaces like windows and doors, or on deteriorated surfaces, requires full removal using wet methods that suppress dust. All waste is bagged, labeled, and disposed of as regulated material. Once abatement is complete, the area is cleaned using HEPA vacuuming and wet-wiping protocols before clearance testing can be performed by a qualified third-party inspector.

We follow EPA Renovation, Repair, and Painting rule requirements throughout, and our crew uses proper respiratory protection, disposable coveralls, and decontamination procedures on every job.

## Green Tree Permit and Regulatory Considerations

Allegheny County and the Commonwealth of Pennsylvania both have requirements that govern lead abatement work on residential properties. Pennsylvania's Department of Labor and Industry oversees lead abatement contractor certification, and any firm performing abatement on pre-1978 housing must comply with EPA RRP rules when the work involves renovation, repair, or painting that disturbs lead-based paint. Green Tree homeowners should also be aware that if the property is a rental, federal disclosure rules apply to both the landlord and any contractor performing work.

For properties involved in a sale, a lead inspection report can affect the transaction timeline. Getting abatement completed and documented before listing, rather than scrambling after a buyer's inspection, avoids delays.

## Local Note

One thing that comes up specifically in Green Tree and the surrounding Allegheny County boroughs: homes with original steel casement windows from the 1940s and 1950s often have lead paint bonded directly to the metal frames. These windows were common in the area's post-war construction, and the paint adhesion to steel is different from wood. Removal requires a different approach than standard wood-surface work, and improper grinding or sanding on these frames generates fine metallic dust that carries lead particles well beyond the immediate work area. If your home has the original steel casement windows, make sure that's disclosed to any contractor before work begins.
