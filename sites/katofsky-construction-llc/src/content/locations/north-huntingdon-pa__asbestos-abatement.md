## Why North Huntingdon Properties See Asbestos Concerns

The housing stock throughout North Huntingdon reflects a construction era when asbestos was woven into nearly every layer of a home. Popcorn and stippled ceilings were applied with asbestos-containing spray texture well into the late 1970s. Vinyl floor tiles in kitchens and basements, particularly the 9x9-inch squares common in mid-century builds, frequently contain chrysotile asbestos in both the tile and the adhesive beneath it. Pipe insulation wrapping boilers and hot-water lines in older mechanical rooms is another common source.

Westmoreland County's climate adds a layer of urgency. Freeze-thaw cycling through Pennsylvania winters stresses older building materials, causing joint compound and ceiling texture to crack and flake. Once asbestos-containing material becomes friable, meaning it crumbles or releases dust when handled, the risk of fiber release rises sharply. A ceiling that looked stable last spring may be shedding material by March after a hard winter.

## Our Asbestos Abatement Process in North Huntingdon

Every project begins with sampling and laboratory analysis. Suspect materials are collected by a trained technician, submitted to an accredited lab, and results are reviewed before any removal work begins. This step is not optional and not something to skip in the interest of moving a renovation forward faster.

Once asbestos is confirmed, the work area is isolated with negative air pressure containment. HEPA-filtered air scrubbers run continuously during removal to prevent fiber migration to unaffected areas of the home. Materials are wetted before disturbance to suppress dust, carefully removed, and sealed in labeled, puncture-resistant disposal bags. Pennsylvania Department of Environmental Protection regulations govern how asbestos waste is transported and disposed of, and that chain of custody is documented throughout.

After removal, a visual clearance inspection and air clearance testing confirm the space is safe before containment is dismantled. Homeowners receive documentation of the completed work, which is useful for permit records, real estate disclosure, and insurance purposes.
