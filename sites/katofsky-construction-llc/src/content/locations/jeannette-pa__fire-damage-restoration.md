## Why Jeannette Properties See Fire Damage Spread Differently

Most of Jeannette's residential stock dates to the steel and glass era, built between roughly 1900 and 1950 when the city was a manufacturing hub. That era of construction comes with characteristics that affect how fire damage behaves:

- **Plaster walls and horsehair insulation** absorb smoke particles differently than modern drywall. Soot penetrates the porous surface and can off-gas for weeks if not treated with the right encapsulants.
- **Balloon-frame construction**, common in homes built before the 1940s, allows fire and smoke to travel vertically inside wall cavities from the basement to the roof line with almost no fire-stopping in between. What looks like surface damage in one room can mean hidden smoke contamination two floors up.
- **Older HVAC and ductwork** that predates modern filtration can circulate smoke residue throughout the entire house within minutes of ignition, coating surfaces in rooms that never saw a flame.

Understanding the building is the first step. Restoration that ignores the structure's age and construction type will miss contamination and leave odor problems behind.

## Our Fire Damage Restoration Process in Jeannette

Every fire loss is different, but the sequence of decisions matters. Here is how a typical post-fire restoration unfolds:

**1. Stabilization and safety assessment.** Before any cleaning begins, the structure needs to be confirmed safe to enter. That means checking for compromised load-bearing elements, open utilities, and roof or wall breaches that could allow weather intrusion.

**2. Controlled demolition of unsalvageable material.** Char, heavily saturated insulation, and materials that cannot be decontaminated are removed. In Jeannette's older homes, this often includes original plaster sections where soot has bonded to the lime base coat.

**3. Dry ice blasting and HEPA vacuuming.** Smoke residue on framing members and masonry is treated with methods appropriate to the surface. Dry ice blasting is particularly effective on exposed brick and stone foundations common in this region's older basements.

**4. Odor neutralization.** Thermal fogging and hydroxyl generation address odor at the molecular level, reaching into wall cavities and subfloor spaces where surface cleaning cannot.

**5. Documentation throughout.** Every affected area is photographed and moisture-mapped before, during, and after. This documentation supports your insurance claim and confirms the scope of work completed.
