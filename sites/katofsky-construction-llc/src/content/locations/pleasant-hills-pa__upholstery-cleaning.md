## Why Pleasant Hills Homes See Different Upholstery Wear

Pleasant Hills sits in the South Hills region south of Pittsburgh, and the borough's basements and lower-level family rooms tend to run damp during the humid summer stretch that hits Western Pennsylvania most years. Furniture stored or used in those lower levels, whether it's a den sofa or a finished-basement recliner, picks up a musty smell faster than upholstery upstairs because the fabric holds ambient moisture longer than the drywall around it. Add in older homes where original wool or wool-blend upholstery was never designed for today's synthetic-fiber cleaning chemistry, and you get pieces that look fine on the surface but are holding onto years of trapped moisture, pet dander, and cooking odor in the padding underneath.

## Our Upholstery Cleaning Process

We start with a fiber identification step before anything touches the fabric, because cotton, wool, and leather each call for different agitation and moisture levels, and guessing wrong on an older tweed sofa can set a stain permanently instead of lifting it. From there we pre-treat visible spots, apply a low-moisture hot water extraction for most fabric pieces, and use a separate conditioning process for leather rather than running it through the same steam cycle as a fabric couch. Drying time matters more in a damp Pleasant Hills basement than it does upstairs, so we bring air movers for lower-level jobs to push drying time down and keep the piece from reabsorbing moisture from the room before the fabric has fully released what we just extracted.
