## Why North Huntingdon Properties See Roofing Wear Faster Than Homeowners Expect

The Pittsburgh region's climate is genuinely hard on roofing. North Huntingdon, positioned in the rolling terrain east of the city along the Route 30 corridor, gets the same cold fronts that funnel through the Monongahela and Youghiogheny valleys, and the township's elevation shifts mean some neighborhoods drain well while others pool water against foundations and along eave lines. Repeated freeze-thaw cycles are the primary enemy of asphalt shingles here: water works into a small crack, freezes overnight, expands, and by spring what was a minor granule loss has become a lifted tab or a compromised valley.

Older housing stock compounds the problem. A significant portion of North Huntingdon's residential neighborhoods were built in the 1950s through the 1970s, when roof pitches and ventilation standards differed from current practice. Low-slope sections over additions and attached garages are especially vulnerable to ice damming when attic ventilation is inadequate, because heat escaping through the deck melts snow that then refreezes at the cold eave. If you've noticed water staining on your ceiling near an exterior wall in late January or February, that's the pattern at work.

## Our Roofing Installation and Replacement Process in North Huntingdon

Every project starts with a full deck inspection, not just a shingle count. Before any material goes on, the crew checks the sheathing for soft spots, rot, or delamination, because covering compromised decking with new shingles only delays the next failure. In North Huntingdon's older homes, this step occasionally turns up original board sheathing under a previous layer of plywood, which requires a different fastening approach to hold modern architectural shingles securely.

From there, the process moves through:

- **Tear-off and disposal** of existing layers, with attention to proper disposal of any materials that may contain older roofing compounds
- **Deck repair or replacement** of any sheathing sections that don't pass inspection
- **Ice and water shield installation** along eaves, valleys, and penetrations, sized to Westmoreland County's climate exposure
- **Underlayment, drip edge, and flashing** set before any field shingles go down
- **Shingle installation** to manufacturer specifications, which also satisfies warranty requirements
- **Ridge ventilation** assessed and upgraded where existing attic airflow is insufficient

Permitting requirements in North Huntingdon Township follow Westmoreland County and local municipal codes. Katofsky Construction handles the coordination so the project moves without delays on your end.

## Local Note

North Huntingdon's housing stock from the postwar decades frequently has lower roof pitches on rear additions and garage tie-ins than on the main structure. These low-slope transitions are exactly where ice damming concentrates, and they're also where previous owners sometimes used modified bitumen or rolled roofing as a shortcut rather than a proper low-slope system. When Katofsky crews encounter these sections, the material selection and flashing detail changes, because applying standard architectural shingles on a pitch below 2:12 voids the manufacturer warranty and sets up the same failure the homeowner is trying to get away from. It's a detail that matters specifically in this township's housing stock and one worth asking any roofing contractor about before signing a contract.
