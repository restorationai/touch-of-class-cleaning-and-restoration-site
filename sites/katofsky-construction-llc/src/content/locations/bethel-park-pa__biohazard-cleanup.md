## Why Bethel Park Properties Present Specific Biohazard Challenges

Bethel Park's housing stock leans heavily toward mid-century construction, with many homes built between the 1950s and 1970s. That era of building used materials that absorb and retain biological contamination differently than modern construction. Porous concrete block foundations, original hardwood subfloors, and plaster-and-lath wall assemblies common in that period can hold contaminants below the visible surface long after the surface appears clean. Standard household cleaning products do not reach into these substrates, and improper cleanup can leave behind pathogens that pose ongoing health risks.

The South Hills climate adds another layer of complexity. Bethel Park experiences cold, damp winters and humid summers, conditions that allow biological material to break down in ways that affect adjacent building materials more quickly than in drier climates. In a basement or lower level, where many of these homes store finished living space, temperature and humidity fluctuations can accelerate the spread of contamination into surrounding materials if remediation is delayed.

## Our Biohazard Cleanup Process in Bethel Park

Every job begins with a careful assessment of the affected area. We identify all surfaces and materials that have come into contact with biological material, including areas that are not immediately visible. From there, the process follows a structured sequence.

First, the area is secured and ventilated appropriately. Personal protective equipment is worn throughout. Contaminated materials that cannot be decontaminated are carefully packaged and disposed of according to Pennsylvania Department of Environmental Protection guidelines for regulated medical waste. Remaining surfaces are treated with EPA-registered hospital-grade disinfectants, with contact times observed as required by the product label. Porous materials that have absorbed contamination are removed and replaced rather than treated in place, because surface-level disinfection does not reach into saturated substrate.

Once remediation is complete, the area is cleared and left in a condition ready for any necessary repairs or restoration. We document the work thoroughly, which is useful both for your own records and for any insurance claim you may need to file.
