## Why Carnegie Properties Face Elevated Lead Paint Risk

Allegheny County's older boroughs, Carnegie included, were developed heavily between the 1890s and the 1940s. That era predates the federal ban on residential lead paint by several decades, which means the vast majority of homes built during that window have lead in the paint layers somewhere, whether it is visible or buried under coats of later latex. The risk is not always obvious. Lead paint that is intact and well-adhered poses less immediate hazard than paint that is chalking, chipping, or disturbed by friction at windows and doors. In Carnegie's older housing stock, the combination of original wood windows, plaster walls, and decades of repainting means lead can be present in multiple layers at once.

Renovation work is where exposure risk spikes. Sanding, cutting, or demolishing surfaces without proper containment sends lead dust airborne, and that dust settles into carpet, HVAC returns, and soil along foundation lines. Pennsylvania follows the EPA's Renovation, Repair, and Painting Rule, which requires certified contractors for work that disturbs lead paint in pre-1978 homes. Hiring a contractor who is not certified for lead-safe work is not just a health risk; it can create liability issues when you sell or rent the property.

## Our Lead Paint Abatement Process in Carnegie

Every project begins with a thorough assessment. Before any physical work starts, we identify the scope: which surfaces contain lead, what condition the paint is in, and what the renovation plan requires. Testing can involve XRF analysis (a non-destructive scan) or paint chip sampling sent to a certified laboratory, depending on the situation.

Once the scope is confirmed, we establish containment. Plastic sheeting, negative air pressure, and sealed HVAC registers prevent dust migration into unaffected rooms. Abatement methods are chosen based on the surface and condition: encapsulation for intact surfaces that meet the criteria, enclosure for specific structural elements, and full removal for surfaces that are deteriorated or will be disturbed by renovation. Removed materials are bagged, labeled, and disposed of according to Pennsylvania DEP and EPA requirements.

After abatement, clearance testing verifies that dust levels meet the EPA's post-abatement standards before containment is removed and the space is returned to use. We document the entire process, which matters when you need records for a real estate transaction, a rental inspection, or a permit closeout.

## Local Note

One thing that comes up repeatedly in Carnegie's older residential blocks is the presence of lead paint on exterior porch columns, porch floors, and decorative trim, surfaces that are easy to overlook when the focus is on interior work. These exterior surfaces weather and chalk, which means lead-contaminated paint chips and dust can accumulate in the soil along the foundation and in window wells. If your home has original porch woodwork, it is worth including those surfaces in any lead assessment, not just the interior rooms. Soil testing near the foundation is sometimes warranted, particularly if the exterior paint history is unknown.
