Lead paint abatement in Munhall almost always traces back to the same root cause: the borough's housing stock was built to house steelworkers in the early decades of the 20th century, decades before the 1978 federal ban on lead-based residential paint. Window sashes, porch railings, and stairway trim in these homes often carry multiple layers of old paint, and once that paint starts to chip or chalk, it becomes a hazard that standard renovation crews aren't equipped to handle safely.

## Why Munhall Properties See Lead Paint Issues

Munhall grew up alongside the Homestead Works along the Monongahela River, and the rowhouses and single-family frame homes built for mill families during that era are still the backbone of the borough's residential blocks. Original woodwork, including window casings, baseboards, and exterior trim, was typically painted with lead-based products well into the mid-1900s. Allegheny County's freeze-thaw winters accelerate paint failure on exterior surfaces, so homeowners here tend to see peeling and chipping on south- and west-facing elevations first, right where sun exposure and ice buildup both do the most damage.

## Our Lead Paint Abatement Process in Munhall

Work starts with testing, not guessing. We sample suspect surfaces, interior and exterior, and send them out for lab confirmation before any abatement plan is written. Once lead is confirmed, we seal off the work area with containment barriers and negative air pressure where the scope calls for it, following EPA RRP rule practices for dust containment and cleanup verification. Depending on the surface and substrate, that means encapsulation, enclosure, or full removal of painted components, each followed by HEPA vacuuming and wet-wipe clearance before we consider a room done. For occupied homes, which is most of what we see in Munhall, we phase the work room by room so families aren't displaced for the entire project.

## Local note

One thing crews learn quickly working in Munhall's older housing: many of these homes have had partial renovations over the decades, meaning you can find a 1990s drywall patch sitting a few inches from original lead-painted trim from the 1920s. That mix means testing can't rely on an assumption that "newer means safe"; we test room by room and surface by surface rather than treating a whole house as one uniform risk category, which keeps both the scope and the cost honest.
