## Why West Mifflin Properties Face Elevated Board-Up and Tarping Risks

Much of West Mifflin's residential stock dates to the mid-20th century, built during the steel-industry boom that filled the hillside streets above the Mon Valley. Those homes typically feature wood-frame construction with older window glazing, aging mortar on brick facades, and roof systems that have been patched rather than fully replaced over the decades. A single storm event can crack a compromised window, lift a section of aging shingles, or separate flashing that was already working loose.

The region's freeze-thaw cycle compounds the problem. Temperatures in Allegheny County swing repeatedly across the freezing point through late fall and early spring. Water that enters through a broken window or a breached roof deck on a Tuesday night can freeze inside a wall cavity by Wednesday morning, expanding and widening the breach. Securing the opening within hours, not days, is what limits that secondary damage.

Fire calls in denser residential pockets also leave adjacent units exposed. A duplex or attached row home where one unit sustains fire damage often leaves the neighboring unit's shared wall or roof section vulnerable until both sides are properly boarded and sealed.

## Our Emergency Board-Up and Tarping Process in West Mifflin

When you call (412) 304-9284, the first questions we ask are about the type of opening, the story height, and whether the structure is still accessible. That triage shapes what we load on the truck.

For broken windows and door frames, we use plywood cut to fit and fastened with structural screws rather than staples, which pull loose in wind. We seal the perimeter with foam backer and weatherstrip tape to block air and water infiltration, not just physical entry. For fire-damaged openings where the frame itself is compromised, we build a secondary frame inside the rough opening before the board goes up.

For roof tarping, we use heavy-duty polyethylene tarps secured with batten boards screwed into the decking, never just weighted at the edges. On West Mifflin's steeper residential pitches, that means running the tarp over the ridge when possible so wind cannot get underneath and peel it back. We document every opening with photographs before and after, which your insurance adjuster will need for the claim file.

Board-up and tarping services are typically covered under the "protection from further damage" provision of a standard homeowners policy, but documentation is what makes or breaks that reimbursement. ## Local Note

Older homes throughout West Mifflin and the broader Mon Valley were frequently built with balloon-frame construction, a method common before the 1950s where wall cavities run continuously from the foundation sill to the roof rafters without fire blocking between floors. When a fire or structural breach opens one of these cavities to the exterior, wind-driven rain can travel vertically inside the wall and saturate insulation and framing on multiple floors before anyone realizes the extent of the infiltration. When we board up a balloon-frame home, we pay particular attention to sealing not just the visible opening but the cavity edges at the sill and header, because water finding that path will not show up on the surface until significant damage is already done.
