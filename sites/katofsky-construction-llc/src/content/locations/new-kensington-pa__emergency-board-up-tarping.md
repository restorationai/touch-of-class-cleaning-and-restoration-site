## Why New Kensington Properties Face Elevated Board-Up and Tarping Risk

Much of New Kensington's residential and commercial building stock dates to the early and mid-twentieth century, when the city was a center of aluminum and glass manufacturing. That era produced solid bones, but also construction details that become liabilities after a loss: balloon-frame walls that let fire travel vertically without stopping, single-pane windows that shatter easily under pressure changes, and rooflines with complex valleys and dormers that are difficult to seal quickly. When a window blows out or a roof section is compromised, the opening is rarely a simple rectangle.

The Allegheny River corridor also means moisture is a constant pressure. Fog settles into low-lying streets on cool mornings, and rain events along the valley can be intense and localized. An unsecured opening after a fire or storm does not just let in wind. Within hours, rain-driven moisture can saturate plaster ceilings, swell door frames, and begin the conditions that allow mold to establish. The window between the initial loss and secondary damage is narrow, which is why a fast, properly anchored board-up or tarp installation matters here more than it might in a drier climate.

## Our Emergency Board-Up and Tarping Process in New Kensington

When you call, the first thing we establish is the type and scope of the opening. A fire damage board-up on a century-old brick rowhouse requires different anchoring than a broken window board-up on a postwar ranch. We arrive with pre-cut plywood in standard and custom dimensions, commercial-grade fasteners rated for exterior use, and reinforced poly tarps with weighted edges and ridge-cap overlap designed to handle the wind and rain common to the Allegheny Valley.

For roof tarping, we assess the structural integrity of the decking before any crew member walks the surface. On older homes, fire or impact damage can compromise rafters that look intact from the ground. Once the surface is confirmed safe, tarps are anchored with batten boards screwed through the tarp into solid decking, not just weighted at the edges. A tarp that lifts in a 40-mph gust is not protection.

For fire damage board-up, we seal window and door openings from the exterior using plywood secured to the frame, not the surrounding siding. We also check adjoining openings, attic vents, and soffit gaps that smoke or heat may have compromised. Every opening we identify gets documented with photographs, which we provide directly to your insurance carrier.

## Local Note

Older brick rowhouses common in parts of New Kensington present a specific challenge for exterior board-up: the mortar joints around window frames are often softer than the surrounding brick, especially on structures that have not been repointed in decades. Standard exterior fasteners driven into deteriorated mortar can pull free under wind load. Our crews carry masonry anchors and frame-mount hardware specifically for this situation, so the board-up holds through the claim process rather than failing the first time a storm comes through. It is a detail that matters on this housing stock and one that a crew unfamiliar with the area might not anticipate.
