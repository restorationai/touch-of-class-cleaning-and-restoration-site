Penn Hills sits in one of the most densely built-out corridors of Allegheny County, and a significant share of its housing stock dates to the postwar construction boom of the late 1940s through the 1960s. That era of building leaned heavily on asbestos-containing materials: floor tiles, pipe insulation, joint compound, roofing felt, and the textured spray ceilings that were standard in split-levels and ranch homes across the municipality. When a renovation, a plumbing repair, or a roof replacement disturbs those materials, the risk is real and the regulatory obligations are specific. Katofsky Construction LLC handles asbestos abatement in Penn Hills with the documentation, containment, and clearance testing that Pennsylvania DEP and Allegheny County Health Department requirements demand.

## Why Penn Hills Properties See Asbestos Concerns

Penn Hills was largely developed in a compressed window after World War II, when returning veterans and growing families pushed construction outward from Pittsburgh along the Allegheny River valleys. The result is a community where an enormous proportion of single-family homes, duplexes, and small commercial buildings were built before asbestos was phased out of most construction products in the late 1970s and early 1980s. Textured "popcorn" ceilings, 9-inch vinyl floor tiles with black mastic adhesive, wrap insulation on older boiler pipes, and duct tape on HVAC systems are the materials most commonly disturbed during remodeling projects.

The hilly terrain that defines Penn Hills also plays a role. Homes built into slopes often have crawl spaces and basement utility areas where original pipe insulation has been undisturbed for decades, sometimes showing visible deterioration. Freeze-thaw cycles through Allegheny County winters accelerate the crumbling of friable insulation on pipes that run through unheated crawl spaces. When that insulation becomes friable, meaning it crumbles under hand pressure, it is actively releasing fibers into the air.

## Our Asbestos Abatement Process in Penn Hills

Every project begins with a visual inspection and material sampling by a trained technician. Suspect materials are collected following proper chain-of-custody protocols and sent to an accredited laboratory. Results typically return within a few business days, and we walk you through what they mean before any abatement work is scheduled.

Once a positive identification is confirmed, the work area is sealed with negative air pressure containment using HEPA-filtered air scrubbers. All personnel working inside the containment zone wear full personal protective equipment. Asbestos-containing materials are wetted before removal to suppress fiber release, then double-bagged in labeled, sealed poly bags for disposal at a licensed facility. Pennsylvania requires that asbestos waste be transported and disposed of under specific manifesting rules, and those records are part of the documentation package we provide at project close.

After abatement, a clearance air sample is collected and analyzed before containment is removed. That final clearance result is what allows the space to be safely reoccupied and what satisfies most lender and insurance requirements for documentation.

## Local Note

Penn Hills homes built in the 1950s and early 1960s frequently used a specific type of 9-by-9-inch floor tile in kitchens, bathrooms, and finished basements that almost universally contains chrysotile asbestos in the tile body and in the black cutback mastic beneath it. These tiles are often found under multiple layers of later flooring, which means a homeowner pulling up old carpet or laminate may not realize what they are looking at until the tile is already broken. If you uncover a dark, sticky adhesive layer under old resilient tile anywhere in a Penn Hills home, stop the work and have the material tested before proceeding. Disturbing that mastic with a scraper or floor grinder generates a significant fiber load in a confined space.
