North Huntingdon Township sits in a part of Westmoreland County where the housing stock tells a long story: mid-century ranches built during the postwar boom, older two-stories with plaster walls and knob-and-tube wiring tucked into quiet cul-de-sacs, and a growing number of newer builds along the township's expanding corridors. Renovating or remodeling in that kind of mixed environment takes more than a standard punch list. Katofsky Construction LLC brings general contracting experience calibrated to exactly this kind of community, where what's behind the drywall can surprise even a seasoned crew.

## Why North Huntingdon Homes Present Unique Renovation Challenges

Westmoreland County's clay-heavy soils shift more than homeowners expect across freeze-thaw cycles, and North Huntingdon is no exception. That seasonal movement shows up in foundation cracks that widen over years, door frames that rack out of square, and floor systems that develop soft spots where joists have deflected. Before a kitchen remodel or bathroom renovation can really begin, a thorough general contractor needs to assess what the structure is actually doing, not just what the finishes look like.

Older homes throughout the township also carry legacy materials that affect scope and cost: galvanized supply lines that have narrowed from mineral buildup, cast-iron drain stacks that corrode from the inside out, and plaster-over-lath wall assemblies that behave differently than modern drywall when you're opening walls for electrical or plumbing updates. None of that makes a remodel impossible. It does mean the planning phase matters more than it would on a newer build.

Permitting through Westmoreland County and the township's own code enforcement office adds another layer. Structural changes, electrical panel upgrades, and additions all require inspections that need to be sequenced correctly so the project doesn't stall waiting on a rough-in inspection before insulation can go in.

## Our Renovation and General Contracting Process in North Huntingdon

Every project starts with a walkthrough that goes beyond surface measurements. The goal is to understand the home's actual condition: foundation, framing, mechanicals, and any signs of prior water intrusion or deferred maintenance that could affect the remodel. That upfront assessment is where problems get priced honestly rather than discovered mid-project.

From there, the work moves through a defined sequence: demolition and hazardous material handling if needed, rough framing and structural corrections, mechanical rough-ins, inspections, insulation, drywall or plaster repair, finish carpentry, and final fixtures. For post-damage rebuilds, the same sequence applies, but the documentation phase is more intensive because it feeds directly into the insurance claim process.

Katofsky Construction coordinates subcontractors, schedules inspections, and keeps the homeowner informed at each phase transition. The goal is a finished space that passes final inspection and holds up to the township's climate, not just one that looks good on the day of completion.

## Local Note

North Huntingdon's older neighborhoods include a notable number of homes built on crawl spaces rather than full basements, a common construction choice in this part of Westmoreland County during the 1950s and 1960s. Crawl space conditions here tend to degrade quietly: vapor barriers fail, wood framing picks up ambient moisture from the ground, and by the time a homeowner notices a soft spot in the kitchen floor, the subfloor and sometimes the joists below it need replacement. Any renovation that touches flooring in these homes warrants a crawl space inspection before finish materials are selected. Catching it at the planning stage costs far less than reopening a completed floor.
