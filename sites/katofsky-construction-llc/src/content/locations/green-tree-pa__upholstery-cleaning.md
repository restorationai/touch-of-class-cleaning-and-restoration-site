## Why Green Tree Homes See Upholstery Wear Faster Than Expected

Green Tree's position in the Ohio River valley corridor means the borough experiences a longer shoulder season of high relative humidity than communities farther east. Fabric upholstery acts like a sponge during those stretches, pulling in airborne dust, cooking odors, and pet dander and holding them against the fiber. Leather and bonded leather pieces face a different problem: repeated expansion and contraction from seasonal temperature swings dries out the surface coating and accelerates cracking along seams and seat edges.

Older split-level and ranch-style homes that are common in this part of Allegheny County also tend to have lower air circulation in living areas, particularly in finished lower levels where sectionals and recliners get the heaviest use. Without adequate airflow, soiling compounds between cleanings and cleaning intervals need to shorten to keep fabrics in good condition.

## Our Upholstery Cleaning Process in Green Tree

Every piece starts with a fiber identification step. The cleaning chemistry and extraction pressure that works well on a tightly woven microfiber is wrong for a loose-weave linen or a vintage velvet, and getting that call wrong causes shrinkage, color bleed, or pile distortion that cannot be reversed. Once the fabric type is confirmed, we pre-treat high-contact zones, the armrests, seat fronts, and headrests, with an appropriate dwell-time solution before moving to hot-water extraction or low-moisture encapsulation, depending on what the fabric can tolerate.

For leather upholstery, cleaning is followed by a conditioning treatment. Leather that has been through several Pittsburgh winters without conditioning has usually lost enough natural oils that cleaning alone leaves it looking flat. The conditioning step restores suppleness and helps the surface resist the next round of seasonal drying.

All work is done in your home. There is no pickup, no storage, and no risk of damage in transit.
