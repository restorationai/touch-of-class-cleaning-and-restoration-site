## Our Emergency Board-Up and Tarping Process in Jeannette

When a call comes in, the first priority is a rapid site assessment to identify every breach in the building envelope: broken windows, kicked-in doors, compromised roof sections, and any structural openings left by fire department ventilation cuts. From there, the process moves in a deliberate sequence.

For window and door openings, we cut and fasten exterior-grade plywood to the structural framing, not just to interior trim. On Jeannette's older rowhouses, window openings are often non-standard sizes, so material is cut on-site to fit precisely rather than relying on stock panels that leave gaps at the edges.

Roof tarping on Jeannette properties requires attention to pitch and material. Many homes in the area have steep gable roofs with aging asphalt shingles or, on some older commercial-adjacent buildings, low-slope sections with built-up roofing. Tarps are anchored with weighted boards or screwed battens rather than simply draped, so they hold through wind gusts rather than peeling away overnight. Every tarp installation is sized to overlap the ridge or a dry section of intact roofing by several feet, preventing water from wicking under the edge.

All work is photographed systematically before, during, and after installation, with documentation formatted for insurance adjuster review.

## Local Note

Jeannette's rowhouse blocks present a specific logistical consideration that does not come up in suburban or rural work: party walls.
