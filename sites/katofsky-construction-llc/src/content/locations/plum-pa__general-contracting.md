## Why Plum's Housing Stock Shapes Every Renovation

A significant portion of Plum's residential neighborhoods were developed between the 1950s and the 1980s, which means crews frequently encounter knob-and-tube wiring that was never updated, galvanized supply lines that have narrowed with mineral buildup, and load-bearing walls positioned in ways that don't match modern open-floor-plan expectations. Before a single cabinet is ordered or a tile is selected, a thorough assessment of what's inside those walls matters more than the finish schedule.

Allegheny County's clay-heavy soils also affect foundation performance over time. Hillside lots common throughout Plum can experience seasonal soil movement, which shows up as sticking doors, cracked drywall seams, and out-of-level subfloors. A remodel that ignores those underlying conditions will look good for a year and then start showing the same problems again. Katofsky Construction addresses structural and substrate issues before cosmetic work begins.

Homes in Plum built during the 1960s and 1970s frequently used a framing technique where interior partition walls were set directly on the subfloor without bottom-plate blocking underneath, a common practice of the era that becomes relevant the moment a homeowner wants to move or remove a wall.
