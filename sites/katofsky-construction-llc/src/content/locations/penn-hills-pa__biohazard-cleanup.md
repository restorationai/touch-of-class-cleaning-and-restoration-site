## Why Penn Hills Properties Present Unique Biohazard Challenges

Much of Penn Hills developed between the 1940s and 1970s, and that construction era carries specific considerations for biohazard remediation. Subfloor materials in homes from this period are often board-sheathed rather than plywood, which means porous surfaces extend further beneath finished flooring than in newer construction. Biological material can migrate into those gaps quickly, making surface-level cleaning insufficient.

Allegheny County winters also play a role. Cold temperatures slow the natural breakdown of organic material, which can obscure the true extent of contamination if a situation goes unaddressed for days. Conversely, summer humidity in the Pittsburgh region accelerates microbial activity. In Penn Hills, where many homes have partially finished basements with limited ventilation, that combination creates conditions where thorough remediation matters more than a fast visual clean.

Older plumbing and drainage configurations in some of the borough's hillside properties can also complicate wastewater disposal during cleanup, requiring careful planning before work begins.
