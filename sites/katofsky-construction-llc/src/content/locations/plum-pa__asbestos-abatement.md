## Why Plum Properties See Asbestos Concerns

Allegheny County's eastern suburbs developed rapidly after World War II, and Plum was no exception. Builders in that era used asbestos-containing materials across nearly every trade: HVAC duct wrap, boiler insulation, vinyl floor tiles (especially the 9x9-inch variety), textured "popcorn" ceilings, and the paper backing on older drywall. Homes that have never had a major gut renovation are likely to have at least one of these materials intact.

The borough's climate adds another layer. Western Pennsylvania's freeze-thaw cycles stress older plumbing and mechanical systems, which means pipe insulation and boiler jackets, two common asbestos sources, get disturbed during repairs more often than in milder climates. A cracked pipe in a 1960s basement can expose deteriorating pipe wrap the moment a plumber cuts in. That is when an asbestos inspection matters most: before the repair crew starts, not after.

## Our Asbestos Abatement Process in Plum

Every project starts with a thorough inspection and bulk sampling of suspect materials. Samples go to an accredited laboratory, and we do not begin removal until results are confirmed. This step protects you legally and documents the scope for your insurance carrier or real estate transaction.

Once abatement is authorized, the work area is isolated with negative air pressure containment. Certified technicians wet the material to suppress fiber release, remove it in sealed, labeled bags, and transport it to a licensed disposal facility. Air clearance testing confirms the space is safe before containment comes down. The Pennsylvania Department of Environmental Protection sets the regulatory framework for asbestos work in the commonwealth, and all disposal follows those requirements.

For popcorn ceiling asbestos removal specifically, we assess the ceiling condition before deciding on full removal versus encapsulation. In Plum homes where the ceiling is intact and not being disturbed by a renovation, encapsulation can be a compliant option. If you are opening up the space for a remodel, removal is the right path.
