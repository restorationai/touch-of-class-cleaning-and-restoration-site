## Why Bethel Park Properties See Lead Paint Issues

Bethel Park developed rapidly in the postwar decades, with the bulk of its single-family housing built between the late 1940s and the early 1970s. That construction window falls squarely in the era when lead-based paint was the industry standard for both interior and exterior applications. Exterior trim, window sashes, porch floors, and door frames received the heaviest coats because lead added durability against weather. In southwestern Pennsylvania's climate, where freeze-thaw cycles run from November through March, that exterior paint has been contracting and expanding for fifty-plus years. The result is chalking, flaking, and micro-fractures that release lead dust long before the paint looks visibly deteriorated.

Interior surfaces tell a different story. Layers of subsequent paint often encapsulate the original lead coat, which sounds reassuring until a contractor sands a windowsill, a plumber cuts through a wall, or a homeowner strips woodwork for a remodel. Disturbance of any kind turns stable encapsulated paint into an airborne and surface-contact hazard. That is why lead paint testing before any renovation is not just a precaution in homes of this era, it is the responsible starting point.

## Our Lead Paint Abatement Process in Bethel Park

Every project begins with a thorough assessment. We identify suspect surfaces, document their condition, and determine whether the material is intact enough for encapsulation or requires full removal. When removal is the right call, we establish containment using heavy-gauge poly sheeting, negative air pressure, and HEPA filtration to prevent cross-contamination to unaffected rooms. Workers wear full personal protective equipment throughout.

For exterior work on Bethel Park homes, we pay close attention to soil around the foundation perimeter. Decades of paint chalking and flaking from siding and trim can deposit lead in the top few inches of soil along the drip line, a detail that matters especially on properties where children play near the house. Soil testing and disposal are part of a complete abatement scope when exterior surfaces are involved.

Once abatement is complete, clearance testing confirms that lead dust levels meet federal standards before containment comes down and the space is returned to normal use. We document the entire process with photographs and written records, which your insurer, your buyer's lender, or your local code office may require.
