Furniture, clothing, documents, and keepsakes absorb smoke odor and moisture faster than the structure itself, and in a borough where many homes predate the mid-twentieth century, those contents are frequently irreplaceable. ## Why Carnegie Properties See Contents Damage More Than You'd Expect

The housing stock throughout Carnegie skews older, with a significant share of homes built between the 1920s and 1950s. Those homes were constructed with plaster walls, horsehair insulation, and wood-framed interiors that hold moisture and smoke particulate differently than modern construction. When a pipe bursts behind a plaster wall or a kitchen fire sends soot through a drafty floor plan, the smoke and water don't stay where they started. They migrate into closets, saturate upholstered furniture, and settle into fabric, paper, and wood grain within hours.

Chartiers Creek has a documented history of flooding during heavy rain events, and homes close to the creek corridor are particularly vulnerable to basement flooding that wicks upward into finished living spaces. Contents sitting on the floor or in lower cabinets are often the first casualties, and the window between "wet but salvageable" and "a total loss" is short. Mold colonization on porous materials can begin within 24 to 48 hours of initial exposure, which is why the speed of pack-out matters as much as the restoration work itself.

## Our Contents Restoration and Storage Process in Carnegie

The process begins with a systematic inventory and pack-out. Every item is photographed, catalogued, and assigned a condition rating before it leaves the property. This documentation matters for insurance claims and gives you a clear record of what was removed and where it is at every stage.

From there, contents go to a climate-controlled facility where the appropriate restoration method is matched to the material. Hard goods, electronics, and furniture with structural integrity are cleaned using ultrasonic equipment, dry-ice blasting, or specialized surface treatments depending on the type and extent of damage. Textiles go through ozone or hydroxyl treatment to neutralize smoke odor at the molecular level rather than masking it. Documents and photographs, which are common in Carnegie's older homes where family records span generations, receive priority handling and can often be stabilized and dried even when they appear severely damaged.

Items that cannot be restored are documented in detail to support your insurance claim. Nothing is discarded without your sign-off.

## Carnegie Insurance Coordination

Contents restoration claims have more moving parts than structural claims. Carriers want itemized documentation, condition assessments, and restoration-versus-replacement cost comparisons for each item. The pack-out inventory Katofsky Construction creates from the start is built to satisfy those requirements. Adjusters receive a line-by-line record with photographs rather than a summary estimate, which typically speeds up the approval process and reduces back-and-forth.

If your carrier requires a third-party contents valuation or has a preferred vendor list, that information is worth gathering early. The documentation we provide is formatted to work with most major carriers' claim workflows.

## Local Note

Carnegie's older homes frequently have unfinished or partially finished basements with stone or brick foundation walls rather than poured concrete. Those walls are porous, and during a flood event they allow groundwater to seep in slowly rather than rushing in all at once. The result is that contents stored in basement areas are sometimes damp for days before a homeowner notices, because the water level never rises dramatically. By the time the problem is visible, mold is often already establishing on cardboard boxes, fabric bins, and wood furniture legs. If your basement has that older stone-wall construction, it is worth checking stored contents after any significant rain event, not just after an obvious flood.
