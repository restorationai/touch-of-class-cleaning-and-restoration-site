## Why McCandless Properties See Roofing Deterioration Faster Than Expected

The Pittsburgh region's weather is genuinely hard on roofing systems, and McCandless's elevated terrain north of the city amplifies that. Homes here can receive more accumulated snowpack than lower-elevation neighborhoods closer to downtown, and the repeated freeze-thaw cycles through January and February are particularly damaging to asphalt shingles that have already lost flexibility with age.

Much of McCandless's residential housing stock dates to the post-war suburban expansion of the 1950s through 1970s, with a second wave of construction through the 1990s. Homes from those earlier decades often have roof decking and underlayment that was never designed to last this long. When the decking itself has softened or delaminated, a surface-level repair only delays the inevitable. A full replacement that addresses the deck gives you a clean substrate and a roof that performs the way the manufacturer's warranty actually assumes.

The township's tree canopy is another factor. Mature oaks and maples that shade yards in summer drop debris across rooflines all fall, and that organic material holds moisture against shingles and in valleys, accelerating granule loss and algae growth.

## Our Roofing Installation and Replacement Process in McCandless

Every project starts with a thorough inspection before any materials are ordered. We check the ridge, valleys, flashing around chimneys and penetrations, and the condition of the decking itself. In older McCandless homes, it's common to find multiple shingle layers already in place, which adds weight and can mask underlying deck damage. Our assessment tells you exactly what you're working with before we talk about scope or budget.

Once the project is scoped, the process moves in a clear sequence:

- **Tear-off and deck inspection**: Existing shingles and underlayment are removed, and the deck is inspected board by board. Soft or delaminated sections are replaced before anything goes back on.
- **Underlayment and ice-and-water barrier**: In a climate like McCandless's, proper ice-and-water shield installation at the eaves and in valleys is not optional. This is the layer that protects the deck when ice dams form.
- **Shingle installation**: We work with quality asphalt shingle products rated for this region's temperature range, installed to manufacturer specifications so the warranty is valid.
- **Flashing and ridge work**: Chimney flashing, step flashing at dormers, and ridge cap are completed before the job is signed off.
- **Cleanup and final walkthrough**: Debris is removed, the property is walked for fasteners and material scraps, and you're shown the completed work before we leave.

## Local Note

One thing that comes up specifically in McCandless's older ranch and split-level homes from the 1960s is low-slope roof sections over additions and attached garages. These sections are often covered with modified bitumen or an aging built-up roof rather than standard shingles, and they're frequently overlooked during a general inspection because they're not visible from the street. Water that infiltrates a low-slope section can travel horizontally inside the structure before it shows up as a ceiling stain, making the source hard to trace. When we inspect a McCandless property, we treat every roof plane as its own system and don't assume the visible pitched sections tell the whole story.
