## Why Latrobe Properties See Lead Paint Issues

Westmoreland County's housing stock skews old. Latrobe developed rapidly between roughly 1890 and 1950 to house workers tied to the region's industrial economy, and the building methods of that era relied heavily on oil-based lead paint for durability on exterior wood, interior trim, windows, and doors. Lead paint was not banned from residential use until 1978, so any home built before that year carries some risk, and homes built before 1940 carry substantially more.

The local climate adds pressure. Latrobe winters bring freeze-thaw cycles that expand and contract wood siding repeatedly, causing paint to crack, chip, and peel at the surface. That mechanical weathering is one of the most common ways lead dust enters a home's interior and accumulates on windowsills, floors, and in the soil directly below exterior walls. Spring rains then wash loose paint chips into yard soil, which matters especially in homes where young children play outside.

Renovation is the other trigger. Pennsylvania follows the EPA's Renovation, Repair, and Painting rule, which requires certified contractors to use lead-safe work practices on pre-1978 homes. If you are updating a kitchen, replacing windows, or refinishing trim in an older Latrobe property, disturbing intact lead paint without proper containment creates an exposure risk for your family and a compliance issue for the project.

## Our Lead Paint Abatement Process in Latrobe

Abatement is not the same as painting over a problem. The process begins with testing to confirm the presence and concentration of lead in suspect surfaces. Certified testing uses XRF analysis or paint chip sampling sent to an accredited laboratory. Results determine which surfaces require full removal, encapsulation, or enclosure.

Once the scope is defined, the crew establishes containment: plastic sheeting seals doorways and HVAC vents, negative air pressure units with HEPA filtration prevent dust migration to unaffected rooms, and all workers wear appropriate respiratory protection. Removal methods depend on the surface. Chemical strippers, HEPA-vacuum-equipped power tools, and hand scraping each have appropriate applications, and the choice matters because dry sanding or open-flame burning are prohibited methods under EPA rules.

Waste from abatement is regulated solid waste in Pennsylvania. Debris is bagged, labeled, and disposed of through licensed channels, not in standard construction dumpsters. After removal, a clearance examination by a certified inspector confirms that dust levels meet the EPA's post-abatement clearance standards before containment comes down and the space is returned to normal use.

## Local Note

One pattern that comes up repeatedly in Latrobe's older housing stock: exterior porch columns and porch floor boards are often the highest-concentration lead surfaces on a property, not the interior walls. Porch surfaces were repainted frequently over the decades because they took the most weather exposure, which means the paint layer count can be surprisingly deep. When those columns or boards are disturbed for repair or replacement, the dust generation is significant.
