## Why Lower Burrell's Housing Stock Makes Lead Paint a Real Concern

Westmoreland County's building pattern mirrors what you see across the Pittsburgh metro: a concentrated wave of construction from the 1920s through the early 1960s, followed by slower suburban growth. In Lower Burrell, that means a significant share of the housing inventory predates 1978, the federal cutoff year after which lead-based paint was banned for residential use. The older the home, the more layers of paint tend to exist, and lead was commonly used in primer coats beneath later finishes, not just as the topcoat. That matters because surface testing alone can miss buried lead layers that become exposed the moment you sand a window sill or cut through a door frame.

The climate here adds another variable. Allegheny valley winters are wet, and freeze-thaw cycling is hard on exterior painted surfaces. Peeling, chalking, and cracking paint on soffits, porch columns, and window casings is common on homes that have not had exterior maintenance in several years. Deteriorated paint is the condition that triggers mandatory lead-safe work practices under EPA rules, and in a housing stock this age, it is not a rare edge case.

## Our Lead Paint Abatement Process in Lower Burrell

The process starts with testing, not assumptions. Before any physical work begins, surfaces in the affected areas are tested to confirm the presence and concentration of lead. Results from that testing determine the appropriate response: encapsulation, component replacement, or full abatement, depending on the condition and location of the material.

For abatement work, containment comes first. Plastic sheeting seals off the work area from the rest of the living space, and negative air pressure prevents dust migration. Workers use HEPA-filter vacuums throughout the process, and all debris, including paint chips, dust, and disposable protective materials, is packaged and disposed of according to Pennsylvania DEP and EPA requirements. Nothing gets bagged and thrown in a standard dumpster.

After the physical work is complete, a post-abatement clearance inspection verifies that lead dust levels in the work area fall within acceptable limits before the space is returned to occupants. That clearance step is not optional, and it is not something the abatement contractor performs on their own work. Katofsky Construction LLC holds EPA RRP certification, which means the team performing your project is trained and credentialed to work on pre-1978 housing under the federal Renovation, Repair, and Painting rule.

## Working with Pennsylvania's Lead Paint Regulations

Pennsylvania follows federal EPA RRP rules for renovation work in pre-1978 homes and child-occupied facilities, and Westmoreland County properties are subject to the same requirements as anywhere else in the state. If you are a landlord in Lower Burrell and your property was built before 1978, you have disclosure obligations to tenants, and any renovation work that disturbs painted surfaces above certain thresholds must be performed by an RRP-certified firm. Failing to use a certified contractor is not just a compliance risk, it can create liability exposure if a tenant or future buyer raises a lead-related health concern.

For property sales, buyers increasingly request lead paint inspections as a condition of purchase, particularly on older homes. Having documentation of a completed abatement, including the clearance report, can simplify that process considerably.
