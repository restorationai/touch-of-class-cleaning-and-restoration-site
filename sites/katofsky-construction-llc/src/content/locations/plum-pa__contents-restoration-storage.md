Plum Borough sits in a part of Allegheny County where wet winters, clay-heavy soil, and a housing stock that spans several decades of construction all combine to put residential belongings at real risk after a water loss, fire, or mold event. When a finished basement floods or smoke works its way through an older split-level, the contents inside often absorb damage faster than the structure itself. Getting those items out, documented, cleaned, and stored correctly is a separate discipline from drying walls, and it is one that requires a methodical hand from the first hour on-site.

## Why Plum Properties See Contents Damage

Plum's geography plays a direct role in how losses unfold. The borough is laced with small tributaries that drain into Plum Creek, and during heavy spring thaws or sustained rain events, basements in lower-lying sections of the community can take on water before a homeowner has any warning. Clay-dense subsoil, common throughout this part of Allegheny County, does not drain quickly, which means moisture lingers under slabs and around foundation walls longer than it would in sandier terrain.

The housing stock adds another layer. Many Plum homes built in the 1960s through the 1980s feature finished lower levels with wood-paneled walls, carpet over concrete, and built-in cabinetry, all of which trap moisture and hold odor. Upholstered furniture, area rugs, electronics, clothing, and family documents stored in those spaces are the first casualties when water arrives. Smoke from a kitchen or electrical fire travels the same way, settling into porous materials on every floor.

## Our Contents Restoration Process in Plum

The process begins with a room-by-room inventory before anything is moved. Every item is photographed, logged by category and condition, and assigned a disposition: on-site cleaning, pack-out for off-site restoration, or documented as a total loss for your insurance file. This inventory is not a formality; it is the record that supports your claim and ensures nothing gets misplaced during the restoration.

Items that can be cleaned and dried on-site are treated immediately using ultrasonic cleaning, ozone treatment, or thermal fogging depending on the type of contamination. Textiles, documents, and electronics require off-site handling in a controlled environment. Furniture and larger items are wrapped, padded, and transported to a climate-controlled storage facility where they remain secure until your home is ready to receive them. Throughout the process, the inventory log stays live so you know exactly where each item is at any point.

Katofsky Construction LLC is an IICRC Certified Firm, and the team holds IICRC credentials in water damage restoration and applied microbial remediation. Those standards govern how contents are handled when mold is a factor, which matters in Plum homes where a slow leak behind a wall can go undetected long enough for colonization to begin on stored items.

## Local Note

One pattern that shows up repeatedly in Plum homes from the 1970s is the combination of drop ceilings over finished basement spaces and wall-to-wall carpet installed directly on concrete. When water enters, the ceiling tiles wick moisture upward while the carpet traps it below, and the items stored in between, shelving units, holiday decorations, file boxes, exercise equipment, sit in a sustained humid environment. By the time a homeowner notices, the damage to contents is often more extensive than the visible water line suggests. Pulling those contents out early and getting them into dry, conditioned storage is almost always the decision that limits the total loss.
