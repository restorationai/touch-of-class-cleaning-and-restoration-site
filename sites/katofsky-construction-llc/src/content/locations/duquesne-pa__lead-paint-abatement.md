Lead paint abatement in Duquesne almost always starts with the same conversation: a homeowner pulling permits for a kitchen remodel or window replacement discovers the trim or sill paint predates 1978, and now the project can't move forward until the lead risk is addressed. In a city built up during the steel-mill boom along the Monongahela, that conversation comes up more often than in newer suburbs, and it changes how the whole job gets scoped.

## Why Duquesne Properties See Lead Paint Issues

Most of Duquesne's housing stock was built to shelter workers from the Duquesne Works and the surrounding mills, which means a large share of the city's rowhouses, duplexes, and single-family frame homes went up well before the 1978 federal lead paint ban. Layers of old oil-based paint on porches, window sashes, and exterior trim were common, and repainting over decades without full stripping means original lead-based coats are often still sitting underneath. The river valley's humidity swings also take a toll: paint on wood that sits close to the Monongahela tends to chalk and flake faster than paint on drier, higher ground, which generates more airborne lead dust from ordinary weathering, not just renovation.

## Our Lead Paint Abatement Process in Duquesne

We start with testing, not assumptions. XRF or lab-sample testing confirms whether the paint at the renovation site actually contains lead before anyone opens a wall or pulls trim, since not every pre-1978 home in Duquesne has it on every surface. Once lead is confirmed, we isolate the work area with poly sheeting and negative air containment, use HEPA-filtered vacuums and wet-scraping methods instead of dry sanding, and bag debris as lead-contaminated waste for proper disposal. Friction surfaces like double-hung window sashes, common in the city's older frame housing, get special attention because repeated opening and closing grinds old paint into fine dust long after a remodel is finished. Final clearance involves a visual check and surface wipe sampling before we sign off on the area as safe to reoccupy.

## Pennsylvania RRP Compliance and Permitting

Any contractor disturbing more than a small amount of painted surface in a pre-1978 home falls under the federal Renovation, Repair, and Painting (RRP) rule, and Allegheny County building departments expect that paperwork in order before final inspection on renovation permits. We follow RRP lead-safe work practices on every job that touches old painted surfaces in Duquesne, documenting containment, cleanup, and verification so the file is ready if a homeowner's permit reviewer or insurer asks for it. For rental properties, this documentation also matters if a tenant dispute or a Pennsylvania Department of Health inquiry ever comes up, so we keep records rather than relying on a verbal assurance that the work was done safely.

## Local note

One thing that catches homeowners off guard in Duquesne: porches and exterior trim facing the river tend to have more paint layers, sometimes six or more coats stacked over seventy-plus years, because repainting was cheaper than stripping for most of the city's history. That stack makes XRF readings less reliable near the surface and more likely to need a confirming paint chip sample, which adds a day to testing on exterior work but prevents a false negative that would let contaminated dust spread during the remodel.
