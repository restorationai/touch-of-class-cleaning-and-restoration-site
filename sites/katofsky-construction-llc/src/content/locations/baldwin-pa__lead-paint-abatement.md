## Why Baldwin Properties See Lead Paint Issues

Allegheny County's older boroughs accumulated decades of residential construction before lead paint was banned for residential use in 1978. In Baldwin, the bulk of the single-family housing stock predates that cutoff by a wide margin. Homes built in the 1940s and 1950s commonly have multiple paint layers on window sashes, door frames, porch trim, and exterior siding, each coat potentially containing lead at concentrations well above current safety thresholds.

The climate here adds another layer of complexity. Pittsburgh-area winters bring freeze-thaw cycles that stress painted surfaces, causing paint to crack, chalk, and peel. On older window frames especially, friction surfaces grind lead paint into fine dust every time a window is opened or closed. That dust settles on sills, floors, and soil directly below exterior windows, creating ongoing exposure pathways that a simple coat of encapsulant paint cannot reliably address long-term.

Rental properties in Baldwin also face Pennsylvania's lead disclosure requirements and, for units housing young children, additional pressure from pediatric screening programs coordinated through Allegheny County Health Department. A positive blood-lead result in a child can trigger a formal inspection and remediation order, turning a deferred maintenance issue into a compliance emergency.

## Our Lead Paint Abatement Process in Baldwin

Every project begins with confirmed testing rather than assumptions. Lead paint testing uses XRF (X-ray fluorescence) analysis or paint chip sampling to identify which components are affected and at what concentrations. That data drives the scope: full abatement (complete removal), encapsulation (where conditions and regulations permit), or component replacement for items like deteriorated windows where removal is more cost-effective than repair.

For removal work, the crew establishes containment using poly sheeting and negative air pressure to prevent dust migration into living areas. HEPA vacuums and wet methods control dust at the source during scraping, grinding, or component removal. All regulated waste is packaged and disposed of according to Pennsylvania Department of Environmental Protection requirements, with documentation provided to the property owner.

Post-abatement clearance testing is the final step before containment comes down. A certified inspector collects wipe samples from floors, window sills, and window troughs to verify that lead dust levels meet EPA clearance standards. Work does not close out until clearance is confirmed in writing.

Katofsky Construction LLC carries EPA RRP certification, which is required for any firm disturbing more than six square feet of lead paint in a pre-1978 interior or more than 20 square feet on an exterior.
