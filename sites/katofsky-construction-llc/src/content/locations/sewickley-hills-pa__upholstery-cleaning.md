## Why Sewickley Hills Homes See Upholstery Issues

Allegheny County's humid summers and damp shoulder seasons mean fabric furniture in this part of the region holds onto moisture longer than it would in a drier climate. Hillside lots common throughout Sewickley Hills often mean homes with walkout basements or lower-level family rooms, both of which run cooler and damper than upper floors, which is exactly where a lot of sectional sofas and recliners end up. Add in wood-burning fireplaces, popular in the area's wooded properties, and you get a second culprit: fine soot particles that settle into upholstery weave and produce a smoky odor long after the fire's out. Pet hair and dander compound both issues, since trapped moisture gives odor-causing bacteria something to feed on.

## Our Upholstery Cleaning Process

We start by identifying the fabric or leather type before any cleaning solution touches the piece, since a microfiber sectional and a leather club chair need completely different approaches. For fabric upholstery, we run a hot water extraction cycle calibrated to lift soil and moisture without oversaturating the cushion fill, which matters more in a humid climate where over-wetting can trigger mildew growth days later. Leather pieces get a pH-balanced cleaner followed by a conditioning treatment that restores oils lost to dry winter heat and humid summers alike. Every job starts with a hidden-area spot test, because older furniture and antique reupholstery jobs common in this area sometimes use dyes or fill materials that react differently than what's on a manufacturer's care tag.

One thing that catches new residents off guard: hillside homes in this area often have less airflow around lower-level furniture than homes on flatter lots, since retaining walls and wooded lot lines block the cross-breeze that helps upholstery dry faster after a routine cleaning.
