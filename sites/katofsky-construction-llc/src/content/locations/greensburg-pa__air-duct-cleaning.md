Greensburg winters are no joke. The Laurel Highlands push cold, damp air through the Westmoreland County corridor from November through March, and every heating cycle pulls that moisture through ductwork that, in many of this city's older neighborhoods, has been in place for decades. ## Why Greensburg Homes See Duct Buildup Faster Than You'd Expect

Greensburg sits in a region where coal-era construction is still the dominant housing stock. Homes built between the 1920s and the 1960s frequently have original duct runs that were sized and sealed with materials that simply weren't designed for modern HVAC systems. Flex duct added during later remodels often sags or kinks, creating low points where debris collects and moisture lingers. The city's position in a river valley also means humidity levels during spring thaw can spike quickly, and that moisture finds its way into supply and return lines before the system has a chance to dry out.

Older homes in Greensburg also commonly used asbestos-wrapped duct insulation, particularly on basement trunk lines. Before any cleaning work begins in a pre-1980 property, that material needs to be identified. Katofsky Construction's team is IICRC certified and trained to recognize suspect materials and pause work appropriately rather than disturb something that requires a licensed abatement contractor.

## Our Air Duct Cleaning Process in Greensburg

The process starts with a camera inspection of the main trunk and branch runs. This step matters more than most homeowners expect: it tells us whether there's biological growth, pest debris, construction dust from a renovation, or simply years of accumulated particulate. We don't quote a scope of work until we've seen what's actually inside.

Once the inspection is complete, we connect high-powered negative-pressure equipment to the main trunk line, creating a contained vacuum environment throughout the system. Technicians then work register by register with rotary brush tools, dislodging debris and pulling it toward the collection unit rather than into the living space. Supply lines, return lines, and the air handler cabinet are all addressed. If sanitization is warranted based on what the inspection revealed, we apply an EPA-registered antimicrobial agent to interior duct surfaces after cleaning is complete.

Dryer vent cleaning is part of the same visit when requested. In Greensburg's tighter older homes where laundry rooms are often tucked into interior spaces with long vent runs, lint accumulation is a genuine fire risk, and the vent path is frequently more convoluted than in newer construction.

## Local Note: What Greensburg's Heating Season Means for Duct Timing

One thing that comes up repeatedly in this part of Westmoreland County: homeowners schedule duct cleaning in October thinking they're getting ahead of heating season, only to find the system has already been running for two or three weeks by then. Greensburg's shoulder season is short. The furnace often comes on for the first time in late September, and that first extended run pushes whatever has accumulated over the summer directly into the living space. If you're planning a cleaning before heating season, early September is the better target window in this climate. Conversely, late spring, after pollen season peaks and before the air conditioning load begins, is the second-best window for getting a clean baseline before summer humidity cycles through the system.

## Equipment and Methods

The negative-pressure collection units we use are truck-mounted or high-capacity portable systems capable of handling the longer duct runs common in Greensburg's two-story and split-level homes. Rotary contact cleaning, rather than air-whip-only methods, is the standard here because it physically dislodges compacted debris rather than simply agitating it. For registers and grilles, we remove and clean them separately rather than cleaning around them in place. The air handler coil and blower compartment are inspected and cleaned as part of the service when accessible, since a clean duct system connected to a fouled air handler delivers limited benefit.

If your home has a humidifier integrated into the HVAC system, which is common in Greensburg given the dry indoor air during heating season, we inspect the humidifier pad and water panel as part of the visit and note any conditions that warrant attention.
