Mount Lebanon's steep, tree-lined streets and century-old housing stock put roofs through a particular kind of punishment. The borough sits in a pocket of Allegheny County where winter freeze-thaw cycles hit hard, ice dams form along older eave lines, and mature oaks and maples drop debris that accelerates shingle granule loss year over year. When a roof reaches the end of its service life here, the question isn't just which shingles to choose, it's how to handle the structural quirks and aesthetic standards that come with homes built across several distinct architectural eras.

## Why Mount Lebanon Properties See Roofing Issues Earlier Than Expected

Many homes in Mount Lebanon were constructed between the 1920s and the 1960s, and the roof decks on those houses often used skip sheathing or thinner board lumber rather than modern plywood panels. That older substrate can develop soft spots, cupping, or nail-pull long before the shingles above it fail visibly. By the time you notice lifted tabs or missing flashing around a chimney, the deck beneath may already need partial replacement.

The borough's elevation and tree canopy compound the issue. Neighborhoods closer to the hilltops experience more wind-driven rain than lower-lying parts of Pittsburgh, and heavy leaf accumulation in valleys and gutters keeps moisture against the roof surface well into late fall. That sustained contact degrades underlayment faster than the manufacturer's rated lifespan assumes.

Mount Lebanon also enforces permit requirements for full roof replacements, and inspectors here pay close attention to flashing details, drip edge installation, and proper ventilation, all areas where a rushed or underqualified crew tends to cut corners.

## Our Roofing Installation and Replacement Process in Mount Lebanon

Every project starts with a full roof inspection that goes beyond the surface layer. We check decking condition, ridge and soffit ventilation, existing flashing at all penetrations, and the condition of gutters and fascia boards that tie into the roofline. On older Mount Lebanon homes, we frequently find that original copper or galvanized flashing has outlasted two or three shingle generations and simply needs reseating, or that it has finally corroded through and needs full replacement.

Once the scope is clear, we pull the required permit from the municipality before any tear-off begins. Tear-off is complete down to the deck, not layered over existing shingles, because building code in Allegheny County limits the number of shingle layers allowed and because a clean deck lets us spot any soft or damaged sections before they become a structural problem.

New underlayment goes down first, followed by ice-and-water shield along all eaves and valleys, especially important given Mount Lebanon's winter conditions. We then install the chosen shingle product, ridge cap, and all flashing before final inspection. Cleanup includes magnet-rolling the yard and driveway for stray fasteners.

## Local Note

One pattern we see consistently on Mount Lebanon homes built before 1950 is that the original roof framing used true-dimension lumber with shallower rafter depths than modern construction. That affects attic ventilation calculations: the net free area required for proper airflow is harder to achieve without adding baffles or adjusting soffit venting, and inadequate ventilation is one of the leading causes of premature shingle failure in this housing stock. When we spec a replacement on a home from that era, ventilation gets evaluated as carefully as the shingle selection itself.
