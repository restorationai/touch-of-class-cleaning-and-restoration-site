## Restoration emergencies common in Pleasant Hills

Pleasant Hills sits in the hilly South Hills terrain south of Pittsburgh, and that topography shapes the kind of damage we see here. Homes built into slopes often have basements or partial basements that take on water when heavy rain saturates clay-heavy soil faster than it can drain, especially during spring thaw and the sudden summer thunderstorms typical of this part of Allegheny County. Freeze-thaw cycles through a Pittsburgh winter stress older plumbing and foundation joints, which is a frequent cause of pipe failures and slow basement leaks that go unnoticed until flooring or drywall is already compromised. Wind and ice from winter storms also bring down limbs onto roofs and siding on these wooded hillside lots, which is why storm damage and emergency board-up calls cluster here during and after bad weather.

## Building stock, site conditions, and permits in Pleasant Hills

Much of Pleasant Hills was built up in the postwar decades as Pittsburgh's South Hills suburbs expanded, which means a large share of the housing stock is wood-frame construction on basement or partial-basement foundations, with some homes built directly into hillsides. Plumbing from that era commonly includes galvanized supply lines and cast iron drains, both of which are well past their expected service life in many homes and prone to pinhole leaks and slow failures that cause gradual, hidden water damage rather than a single dramatic event. Homes from this period may also contain asbestos-containing materials in flooring, insulation, or pipe wrap, and lead paint if undisturbed since original construction, both of which affect how a restoration scope is planned and handled. Structural repair or rebuild work in Pleasant Hills falls under Pennsylvania's Uniform Construction Code, with permits issued through the borough; the hillside lots common here also mean site grading and foundation drainage are frequently part of the conversation when a basement has flooded more than once.
