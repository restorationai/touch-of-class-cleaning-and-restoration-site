## Services we provide in Ross Township

### Water damage restoration

Water damage in Ross Township frequently starts below grade. A cracked block foundation wall after a wet spring, a failed sump during a storm cell, or a burst pipe in an unheated crawlspace can saturate wall cavities and subfloor assemblies faster than a shop vac can manage. The work involves extracting standing water, placing industrial drying equipment, and monitoring moisture levels in walls and flooring until readings confirm the structure is dry, not just dry at the surface.

### Mold remediation

Mold becomes a concern when water intrusion goes undetected or is only partially dried. In Ross Township's older homes, water behind plaster or inside block wall cavities can read dry at the surface while the substrate stays damp for weeks. Remediation involves containing the affected area, removing compromised materials, and treating surfaces according to established industry protocols. Katofsky Construction LLC does not provide medical advice, and any health concerns should go to a qualified physician.

### Fire and smoke damage restoration

Smoke residue from even a contained kitchen fire travels through HVAC systems and settles into porous materials throughout a home. In the ranch-style and bi-level homes common across Ross Township, open floor plans mean smoke moves quickly from one room to the next. Restoration work addresses odor neutralization, soot cleaning on surfaces, and content handling, not just the area where flames were present.

### Biohazard cleanup

Biohazard situations require specialized protective equipment, proper containment, and disposal that meets Pennsylvania Department of Environmental Protection guidelines. This service is available to Ross Township homeowners, landlords, and property managers who need a safe, discreet response.

## Building stock, site conditions, and permits in Ross Township

The majority of Ross Township's residential construction dates from the 1940s through the 1970s, with a meaningful share of homes built in the postwar boom of the late 1940s and 1950s. That era produced full basements with poured concrete or concrete block foundations, plaster-over-lath interior walls, and galvanized steel supply piping that is now well past its service life in many homes. Galvanized pipe corrodes from the inside out, reducing flow and eventually failing at joints. Homes of this vintage also frequently contain lead-based paint and, in original floor tiles or pipe insulation, potentially asbestos-containing materials. Any restoration scope that disturbs those materials requires proper testing and handling protocols before work proceeds.

Allegheny County's clay-heavy soils retain water rather than draining it, which keeps hydrostatic pressure against basement walls long after a rain event ends. Ross Township's varied topography means some lots sit at the base of slopes where surface runoff concentrates. Permits for structural repair or rebuild in Ross Township are issued through the township's own building department, and work that affects structural elements, electrical systems, or plumbing typically requires a permit and inspection before walls are closed.
