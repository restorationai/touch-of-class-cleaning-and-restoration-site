## What Should You Look for in a Contents Restoration Storage Company?

The right contents restoration company does more than move boxes. Here is what separates a professional pack-out from a problematic one:

**IICRC certification.** The [IICRC](https://www.iicrc.org) sets the industry standards for cleaning and restoration. Certified firms follow documented procedures for moisture control, deodorization, and chain-of-custody tracking. Non-certified companies may still do good work, but you have no third-party standard to hold them to.

**Itemized inventory with photos.** Every item that leaves your home should be photographed, logged, and assigned a condition rating before it goes into storage. This protects you during the insurance claim and ensures nothing gets lost or damaged in transit.

**Off-site climate-controlled storage.** Your belongings should not sit in a warehouse that floods or overheats. Ask where the storage facility is located and whether it is climate-controlled.

**Cleaning methods matched to the damage type.** Smoke-damaged soft goods need ozone or hydroxyl treatment, not just a wipe-down. Water-damaged electronics need professional drying before anyone powers them on. A company that treats all contents the same way is cutting corners.

**Insurance documentation support.** A good contents company generates the documentation your adjuster needs: itemized lists, condition reports, cleaning logs. This speeds up your claim and reduces disputes over replacement versus restoration value.

## Does Insurance Cover Contents Restoration and Storage?

Most standard homeowners policies cover contents restoration and storage when the loss is a covered peril, such as a fire, burst pipe, or storm. The coverage typically falls under your personal property coverage (Coverage C) and sometimes under additional living expenses (Coverage D) if you are displaced.

The key documentation your adjuster will want:

- A complete itemized inventory of everything packed out, with pre-loss condition noted
- Photos of damaged items before cleaning begins
- Receipts or estimates for cleaning and storage costs
- A written scope from the restoration company

One important note: contents restoration is often more cost-effective than replacement, and insurers generally prefer it. A professional company will generate a restoration estimate and compare it to the actual cash value or replacement cost of the item. If restoration costs more than replacement, the item is typically declared a total loss and replaced.

## How to Choose: A Quick Decision Framework

If you are comparing companies right now, here is a simple way to narrow it down:

1. **Is the company IICRC certified?** If yes, they follow a documented standard. If no, ask what standard they follow instead.
2. **Do they handle the full scope of your loss?** A contents-only company may leave you coordinating separately with a general contractor, a fire restoration crew, and a board-up service. A full-service firm handles all of it.
3. **Are they available now?** Contents damage gets worse with time. Smoke odor sets into soft goods within 48-72 hours. Water-damaged items can begin to mold within 24-48 hours. You want a company that can start the pack-out today, not next week.
4. **Do they carry the right license and insurance?** In Pennsylvania, contractors working on residential properties should be licensed and insured. Verify before anyone enters your home.
5. **What do their reviews actually say?** Volume matters, but so does content. Look for reviews that mention specific services, communication, and insurance claim support.
