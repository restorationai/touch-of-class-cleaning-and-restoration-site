## What Renovations, Remodels and General Contracting actually involves

A kitchen that hasn't been touched since 1987. A bathroom where the tile grout has given up entirely. A basement that flooded last spring and now needs to be rebuilt the right way before anyone finishes it. These are the projects where a general contractor either earns your trust or costs you money. Renovations and remodels cover a wide range of work, from cosmetic updates to full structural rebuilds, and the difference between a smooth project and a frustrating one almost always comes down to how well the scope was defined before the first nail was pulled.

At Katofsky Construction LLC, general contracting work spans kitchen remodels, bathroom remodels, basement finishing, room additions, and post-damage rebuilds following water, fire, or mold remediation. Because the company holds IICRC certifications in water damage restoration and mold remediation, post-damage rebuild scopes are handled with an understanding of what the remediation crew actually did, not just what the summary report says.

Timelines vary significantly by project type. A bathroom remodel in a Pittsburgh row home typically runs two to four weeks once materials are on-site. A kitchen gut-and-rebuild runs four to eight weeks depending on cabinet lead times and whether the project touches plumbing or electrical. Post-damage rebuilds add a permitting and inspection layer that can extend the schedule, but they also tend to be the projects where cutting corners has the most visible consequences later.

## Our process

1. **Scope walkthrough and written estimate.** Before any work is scheduled, a detailed scope of work is developed on-site. This is not a ballpark figure over the phone. Every line item, material selection, and subcontractor coordination point is documented so there are no surprises mid-project.

2. **Permits and inspections.** In Allegheny County and the City of Pittsburgh, most structural, electrical, plumbing, and mechanical work requires a permit. Katofsky Construction LLC pulls the appropriate permits and coordinates inspections so the work is code-compliant and the home's resale record stays clean.

3. **Demolition and rough work.** Existing finishes come out, and the structural, mechanical, and rough-in work happens first. This is the phase where hidden problems, old plumbing, knob-and-tube wiring, or moisture-damaged framing, get identified and addressed before they are buried behind new drywall.

4. **Finish work and material installation.** Drywall, flooring, cabinetry, tile, fixtures, and trim are installed in the correct sequence so trades do not undo each other's work. Sequencing is one of the most common places where less-experienced contractors lose time and money on a job.

5. **Final walkthrough and punch list.** Before the project is closed out, a formal walkthrough identifies anything that needs adjustment. The punch list is completed before final payment, not after.

## What separates a good renovation response from a bad one

The most common failure in residential remodeling is an incomplete scope. A contractor who quotes a kitchen remodel without accounting for the electrical panel upgrade the new appliances require, or a bathroom remodel without verifying the existing drain line slope, is setting up a change-order conversation that will happen at the worst possible time.

In post-damage rebuilds, the stakes are higher. A rebuild that begins before the structure has been properly dried and cleared creates a situation where new materials are installed over a moisture problem that has not been resolved. Katofsky Construction LLC's background in IICRC-certified remediation means rebuild scopes are not started until the underlying loss has been properly addressed.

Insurance adjusters reviewing post-damage rebuild claims look for line-item documentation that matches the scope of the loss. Vague invoices or lump-sum estimates create delays and disputes. Detailed, trade-specific documentation moves claims forward.

Permitting is another dividing line. Unpermitted work can create problems when a home is sold, when an insurance claim is filed, or when a subsequent contractor has to work around something that was never inspected. Every project Katofsky Construction LLC takes on that requires a permit gets one.

## Seasonal and regional considerations

Pittsburgh's climate creates specific timing considerations for renovation projects. Exterior work, including additions, rooflines, and any project that opens the building envelope, is best scheduled outside of the November-through-March window when freeze-thaw cycles complicate waterproofing and concrete work. Interior remodels can proceed year-round, but basement projects in neighborhoods with older clay-tile drainage systems should account for spring wet season timing.

Pittsburgh's housing stock skews older. Many homes in Squirrel Hill, Shadyside, Lawrenceville, Mt. Lebanon, and the South Hills were built before 1960, which means remodels regularly surface asbestos-containing materials, lead paint, and outdated electrical systems. Identifying these conditions during the scope phase, rather than mid-demolition, keeps the project on schedule.
