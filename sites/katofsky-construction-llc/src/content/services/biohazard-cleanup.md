When something happens in a home or property that leaves behind blood, bodily fluids, or other infectious material, the instinct is to clean it up and move on. That instinct can make things worse. Pathogens like bloodborne viruses and bacteria are not visible to the naked eye, they do not stay where you can see them, and household cleaners do not neutralize them. Biohazard remediation is a regulated process, not a deeper version of ordinary cleaning, and the difference matters for your health, your property, and your insurance claim.

## What biohazard cleanup actually involves

Biohazard cleanup covers situations where human blood, bodily fluids, sharps and needle waste, or other infectious material has contaminated a surface, structure, or space. This includes unattended deaths, trauma scenes, hoarding conditions with sanitation concerns, and properties where needle or sharps waste has accumulated.

The work is technical and follows a specific sequence. Crews arrive in full personal protective equipment, establish containment to prevent cross-contamination to unaffected areas, remove and properly package all biologically contaminated material, and then treat surfaces with EPA-registered hospital-grade disinfectants and enzymatic cleaners that break down organic matter at a molecular level. Porous materials, including carpet, drywall, and subflooring, frequently require removal because they cannot be reliably disinfected once saturated. All regulated biohazardous waste is transported and disposed of under a documented waste manifest, as required by Pennsylvania law.

Timeline varies by the size of the affected area and the depth of contamination, but most residential scenes are addressed within one to two days of work beginning. Crews work discreetly, and vehicles are unmarked to protect your privacy.

## Our process

1. **Scene assessment and PPE staging.** Before any cleaning begins, the crew walks the affected area in full PPE, including respirators and Tyvek suits, to identify all contaminated surfaces, map the extent of spread, and determine what materials require removal versus disinfection. Contamination is often wider than it appears at first look.

2. **Containment and access control.** Plastic sheeting and negative air pressure are used where needed to isolate the work area from the rest of the property. This prevents cross-contamination during removal and protects occupants who may still be in other parts of the building.

3. **Biohazardous material removal.** All contaminated soft goods, porous building materials, and sharps or needle waste are removed, double-bagged in certified biohazard containers, and labeled per OSHA and Pennsylvania Department of Environmental Protection requirements. Nothing regulated leaves the site without documentation.

4. **Enzymatic treatment and disinfection.** Hard surfaces and any remaining structural materials are treated with EPA-registered enzymatic cleaners that break down organic compounds, followed by a hospital-grade disinfectant application. This step is not optional, and it is not something a general cleaning crew is equipped to perform safely.

5. **Waste manifest and documentation.** Every load of regulated biohazardous waste is tracked on a written manifest from pickup through licensed disposal. That documentation is part of the file we provide for your insurance claim and for your own records.

## What separates a good biohazard response from a bad one

The most common failure in biohazard cleanup is incomplete removal of contaminated material. Blood and bodily fluids wick into subflooring, wall cavities, and insulation quickly. A crew that cleans only what is visible at the surface can leave behind contamination that continues to pose a health risk and produces odor over time. Proper scope assessment requires probing beneath finished surfaces, not just treating what is visible.

A second common problem is improper waste handling. In Pennsylvania, biohazardous waste is regulated, and transport requires proper containment and documentation. Crews that bag waste in ordinary trash bags and dispose of it in standard dumpsters create liability for the property owner, not just themselves.

Insurance adjusters reviewing biohazard claims look for a documented scope of work, itemized removal and treatment records, and a waste manifest. Without that paperwork, carriers frequently dispute or reduce claims. Katofsky Construction LLC provides a written scope before work begins and complete documentation throughout.
