## Why La Mesa Properties See Water Damage Differently

## Our Water Damage Restoration Process in La Mesa

Every job starts with thermal imaging and moisture mapping, not a visual walkthrough. In La Mesa's older slab homes, that distinction matters — we've found moisture plumes extending six to eight feet from a visible water source, hidden beneath tile and original hardwood. Once we know the full scope, extraction comes first: truck-mounted and portable units pull standing water from flooring, subfloor cavities, and wall cavities before any drying equipment is placed.

Structural drying in La Mesa's climate is generally efficient — low ambient humidity helps — but slab construction slows the process because concrete releases moisture slowly and in all directions. We use desiccant and refrigerant dehumidifiers in combination, positioned based on the moisture map rather than room count, and we monitor readings every 24 hours with calibrated meters. Drying typically runs three to five days for a contained event; larger intrusions or those involving crawl spaces can run seven to ten.

Throughout the process, we document every moisture reading, equipment placement, and daily log entry in a format compatible with most insurance carriers' requirements — which simplifies the claim process considerably.
