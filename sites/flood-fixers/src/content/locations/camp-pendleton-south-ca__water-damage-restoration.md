## Why Camp Pendleton South Properties See Water Damage Issues

Most water intrusion calls we'd expect from this area trace back to a handful of causes: aging supply lines in multi-unit housing stock, washing machine hose failures, water heater ruptures, and roof or flashing leaks during the wetter winter months. The coastal humidity means relative moisture in framing and subfloor materials rarely drops to a true baseline dry, which is exactly why unaddressed leaks here tend to turn into secondary damage, warped flooring and soft drywall, faster than homeowners expect. Slab-on-grade foundations, common in this part of North County, also mean water from a slab leak has nowhere to go but up into flooring and baseboards, which is a different mitigation problem than a raised foundation would present.

## Our Water Damage Restoration Process in Camp Pendleton South

We start with water extraction using truck-mounted and portable pumps to pull standing water before it migrates further under flooring or into wall cavities. From there we moisture-map the affected rooms with meters and thermal imaging so drying equipment goes only where it's needed, not blanket-placed across a whole unit. Structural drying follows the IICRC S500 standard: air movers and dehumidifiers run on a monitored schedule, with daily moisture readings logged until framing, subfloor, and drywall readings return to normal range. In shared-wall housing, we also check the adjoining unit's baseboards and flooring, since water doesn't respect a property line.
