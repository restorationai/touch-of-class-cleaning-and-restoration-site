## Why Pala Properties See Water Damage Issues

Much of Pala sits in unincorporated San Diego County, where many homes and small ranch properties still run on private well systems, septic tanks, and older galvanized or copper supply lines installed decades before current plumbing codes. Hot, dry inland valley summers cause soil to contract and shift, which puts stress on buried supply lines and slab foundations, and that stress tends to show up as slow leaks under flooring rather than dramatic bursts. When the rainy season does arrive, properties on sloped or low-lying lots can see fast runoff pooling against foundations faster than the ground can absorb it, especially on lots with gravel or dirt driveways instead of graded drainage.

## Our Water Damage Restoration Process in Pala

We start with extraction, pulling standing water from floors, carpet pad, and crawlspaces with truck-mounted and portable extraction units. From there we map moisture with meters and thermal imaging to find out how far water has traveled under flooring and behind baseboards, which matters in rural properties where crawlspaces and raised foundations are common and water can travel further than it appears on the surface. Structural drying follows, using air movers and dehumidifiers set to targets based on the material (wood subfloor, drywall, plaster) and dried in line with the IICRC S500 standard for water damage restoration. On properties with well or septic systems, we also check that return groundwater or septic backup isn't the actual source before we close out drying, since treating symptoms without finding the real source means the job comes back.
