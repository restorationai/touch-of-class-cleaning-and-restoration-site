## Why Poway Homes Present Unique Renovation Challenges

Most of Poway's residential stock was built between 1975 and 1995, a window when builders relied heavily on polybutylene supply lines, single-pane aluminum-frame windows, and popcorn ceilings that often contain asbestos-era texture compounds. When a renovation project opens up a wall or ceiling in a home built during that era, surprises are common — and the scope of work can shift quickly. The region's Mediterranean climate also matters: long dry summers followed by concentrated winter rain cycles put stress on flat or low-slope rooflines, and it's not unusual for a bathroom remodel to reveal water intrusion damage along an exterior wall that's been silently wicking moisture for a season or two.

Poway also sits in a high fire-hazard severity zone, which means any addition or significant remodel triggers current California Building Code requirements around ignition-resistant construction — ember-resistant vents, tempered glass windows on exterior exposures, and Class A roofing materials. A general contractor working here needs to know those requirements before pulling a permit, not after the inspector flags it.
