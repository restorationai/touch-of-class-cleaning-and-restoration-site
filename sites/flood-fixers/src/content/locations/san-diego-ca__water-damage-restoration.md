## Why San Diego Properties See Water Damage Issues

San Diego sits in a semi-arid Mediterranean climate, which means most homes were built without the drainage redundancy you'd find in wetter regions. Flat or low-slope roofs common in mid-century Ranch and Spanish Revival homes in neighborhoods like Kensington and College Area weren't designed for the atmospheric river events that now hit the region every few winters — storms that can dump two to three inches of rain in a few hours on soil that hasn't absorbed significant moisture in months. Hardpan clay soil in many inland ZIP codes sheds water instead of absorbing it, pushing runoff toward foundations and through crawl spaces.

Indoor plumbing failures are equally common. Homes built before 1980 — and there are tens of thousands of them across San Diego County — frequently still carry original galvanized steel supply lines that corrode from the inside out. You won't see the rust until a fitting blows and water is running behind your kitchen cabinets. Slab foundations, standard in most post-war construction here, mean that a slow leak under a concrete floor can saturate the slab and wick moisture upward into flooring and baseboards for weeks before anyone notices.

## Our Water Damage Restoration Process in San Diego

Every job starts with a full moisture mapping inspection — thermal imaging and calibrated moisture meters across walls, floors, and ceilings — before a single piece of equipment is placed. In San Diego homes with stucco exteriors, moisture often migrates laterally inside the wall cavity farther than the visible damage suggests, so we map aggressively before we cut.

Once the scope is confirmed, water extraction comes first. Truck-mounted extractors pull standing water and deep-seated moisture from carpet, pad, and subfloor faster than portable units. Structural drying follows immediately: industrial desiccant and refrigerant dehumidifiers, air movers calibrated to the room volume, and daily moisture readings to track drying progress against the IICRC S500 standard. In San Diego's low-humidity conditions, drying timelines can be shorter than in coastal Pacific Northwest markets — but only if equipment placement is correct from day one. Cutting corners on air mover positioning because "it's dry here anyway" is how jobs get closed prematurely and mold colonizes three weeks later.

Demo and reconstruction are handled in-house. We don't hand you off to a second contractor after mitigation is done.

Most standard homeowner policies in California cover sudden and accidental water discharge — a burst pipe, a failed appliance supply line, an overflow. What they typically don't cover is gradual leakage or maintenance-related deterioration, which is a common friction point on San Diego claims involving those older galvanized lines. One pattern we see repeatedly in San Diego's older beach-adjacent neighborhoods — Ocean Beach, Mission Hills, and parts of North Park — is that original hardwood floors were installed directly over concrete slabs with no vapor barrier, a common practice before the 1970s.
