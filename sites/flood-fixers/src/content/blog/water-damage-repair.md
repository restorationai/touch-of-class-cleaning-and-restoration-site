## How to Compare Water Damage Repair Companies

Choosing the right company when water is actively damaging your home comes down to four factors. First, verify IICRC certification. The [IICRC](https://www.iicrc.org) is the industry's primary credentialing body; certified technicians follow documented drying protocols, which matters both for outcomes and for insurance documentation. Second, confirm 24/7 availability. Water damage that sits overnight is water damage that grows. A company that doesn't answer after hours is not a real emergency option. Third, check licensing and insurance. California requires contractors to be licensed through the [Contractors State License Board (CSLB)](https://www.cslb.ca.gov). Ask for the license number and verify it. Fourth, ask about insurance claim support. The best restoration companies provide detailed moisture logs, photo documentation, and written scopes that adjusters can work from directly.

For a deeper look at what to ask before you hire, see [How To Choose a Restoration Company](/blog/choosing-a-restoration-company/).

## What Water Damage Repair Actually Involves

Water damage repair is not just drying out the wet stuff. A properly scoped job follows a defined sequence: emergency extraction, structural drying, moisture verification, and then repair or reconstruction of damaged materials. Skipping or rushing any phase leads to hidden moisture pockets that produce mold weeks later.

Extraction removes standing water using truck-mounted or portable extractors. Structural drying uses high-velocity air movers and LGR (low-grain refrigerant) dehumidifiers to pull moisture out of walls, subfloors, and framing. Drying is validated with moisture meters and thermal imaging, not by feel or appearance. Only after readings return to pre-loss baselines does the repair phase begin: replacing drywall, flooring, insulation, and any structural elements the water compromised.

## What to Do While You Wait for the Crew

If you've called and help is on the way, a few steps protect your home and your claim. Stop the water source if you can (shut off the main supply valve or the appliance valve). Move valuables, documents, and electronics out of the wet area. Take photos and video of all visible damage before anything is moved or dried. Do not run your HVAC system over a wet area, as it can spread contamination. Do not use a household vacuum on standing water.
