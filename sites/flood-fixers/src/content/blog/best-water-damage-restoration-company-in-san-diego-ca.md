## What Should You Look for in a Water Damage Restoration Company?

The right restoration company has three things: proper certification, 24/7 availability, and the ability to handle your insurance documentation. Here is what each of those means in practice.

**IICRC Certification.** The Institute of Inspection, Cleaning and Restoration Certification sets the technical standards for water damage work. An IICRC-certified company follows the S500 standard, which governs how water is classified (Category 1 clean water through Category 3 black water), how drying targets are set, and how moisture readings are documented. Without this framework, there is no objective standard for when a structure is actually dry. That matters for your health and for your insurance claim.

**24/7 Availability.** Water damage does not wait for business hours. The longer saturated materials sit, the more secondary damage accumulates: swollen framing, delaminated subfloor, and mold growth that turns a straightforward mitigation into a remediation project. A company that can deploy a crew the same night the loss occurs limits that cascade.

**Insurance Documentation.** Most water damage losses in San Diego involve a homeowners insurance claim. The restoration company you hire should be fluent in that process: moisture mapping with calibrated meters, photo documentation at each phase, written scopes that match the language adjusters expect, and direct communication with your carrier if needed. A company that hands you a one-page invoice and calls it done is not set up to protect your claim. For more on what your policy likely covers, see [Does Homeowners Insurance Cover Water Damage?](/blog/does-homeowners-insurance-cover-water-damage/)

**Reconstruction Capability.** After mitigation (drying and removal of damaged materials), most losses require some level of rebuild: drywall, flooring, cabinetry, or structural framing. If your restoration company does not offer reconstruction, you will need a second contractor. That gap creates delays, handoff disputes, and potential coverage complications. A company that carries general contracting alongside restoration can take the project from initial extraction through finished rebuild.

## How to Decide Quickly When Water Is Actively Spreading

If water is still moving, your first call is not to a restoration company. Shut off the water supply first (or call a plumber if the source is unclear), then call restoration. If there is any electrical hazard, leave the area and call 911 before anything else.

Once the source is controlled, call a restoration company immediately. Do not wait to contact your insurance carrier first: most policies require prompt mitigation, and delaying action to get adjuster approval can create a coverage dispute. Start mitigation, document everything with photos and video, then notify your carrier. The restoration company can communicate directly with your adjuster from that point forward.
