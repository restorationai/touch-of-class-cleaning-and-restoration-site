## What Renovations, Remodels and General Contracting actually involves

You've just finished a water damage or fire claim, the mitigation crew has packed up, and you're standing in a gutted kitchen or a bathroom stripped down to the studs — and now the real question hits: who puts it all back together? That gap between restoration and a finished, livable space is exactly where Flood Fixers operates. Whether the project starts with post-damage rebuild or a long-planned kitchen remodel, the work involves coordinating licensed trades, pulling the right permits, sequencing subcontractors so nothing has to be torn out twice, and delivering a finished product that matches or improves what was there before.

Home renovation services in San Diego range from cosmetic refreshes — new cabinet faces, LVP flooring, fresh tile — to full structural reconfigurations that require architectural drawings and city plan check approval. A kitchen remodel typically runs 6–12 weeks from permit approval to punch list, depending on whether load-bearing walls are involved. A bathroom remodel with a full wet-area rebuild averages 3–5 weeks. Post-damage rebuilds often compress that timeline because scope-of-work documentation already exists from the insurance adjuster's estimate, but they add their own complexity: matching discontinued materials, satisfying both the homeowner and the carrier, and passing inspections on work that has to meet current code even if the original construction didn't.

Flood Fixers holds a California contractor's license and carries full general liability and workers' compensation, which matters when a subcontractor is cutting into a wall in a 1960s Kensington bungalow that may contain lead paint or asbestos-containing texture coat.

## Our process

1. **Scope development and pre-construction walkthrough.** Before a single number goes on paper, a project manager walks the space with you. For post-damage rebuilds, this means reconciling the insurance adjuster's Xactimate line items against actual field conditions — adjusters work from photos and software; we work from the actual wall cavity. For elective remodels, this is where wish lists get stress-tested against load-bearing realities, plumbing chase locations, and budget.

2. **Permitting and trade coordination.** Most structural, electrical, and plumbing work in San Diego requires a permit from the City's Development Services Department or the applicable jurisdiction (Chula Vista, El Cajon, Santee, and others have their own permit offices). We handle the application, respond to plan check corrections, and schedule inspections so the project doesn't stall waiting on a city inspector while a plumber sits idle.

3. **Demolition and rough-in.** Selective demolition removes only what needs to go. Framing, rough electrical, rough plumbing, and HVAC rough-in all happen before any wall surface closes up — and each phase gets inspected before the next begins. Skipping or rushing inspections is the single most common mistake on insurance-funded rebuilds; it creates a concealed defect that surfaces at resale.

4. **Finish work and material installation.** Drywall, texture matching (critical in older San Diego homes with skip-trowel or knockdown finishes), tile, cabinetry, countertops, fixtures, flooring, and paint. Material lead times are confirmed before demolition starts, not after — a 6-week cabinet backorder discovered mid-project is avoidable with proper pre-ordering.

5. **Final inspection, punch list, and closeout.** A project isn't done when the last tile is set. We walk the finished space with you, document every item that needs correction, and don't consider the job closed until those items are resolved and the permit is finaled with the city. You receive copies of all permits and inspection sign-offs for your records.

## What separates a good renovation response from a bad one

The most common failure point in post-damage general contracting is scope creep that nobody documented. An insurance carrier approves a scope; a contractor starts work and discovers additional damage; nobody writes a supplement; the homeowner gets a change-order bill at the end that the carrier won't pay. Good operators submit written supplements with photo documentation before proceeding with out-of-scope work — not after.

On elective remodels, the equivalent failure is an underbid that looks attractive at signing but generates constant change orders. Legitimate general contractors price complete scopes, including allowances for materials, and explain what those allowances cover. A kitchen remodel bid that omits permit fees, demo disposal, or electrical panel upgrades to support new appliance loads isn't a lower price — it's an incomplete price.

For older San Diego housing stock — the post-war tract homes in Allied Gardens, the craftsman bungalows in North Park, the mid-century ranches in Clairemont — lead-based paint and asbestos-containing materials are a real consideration. Disturbing them without proper testing and containment creates liability for the contractor and a health hazard for the occupants. Ask any general contractor bidding your project whether they've tested for hazardous materials and what their abatement protocol is.

Insurance adjusters reviewing rebuild invoices look for line-item specificity: labor hours broken out by trade, material quantities that match the measured square footage, and code-upgrade line items called out separately from like-for-like replacement. Vague invoices get disputed; detailed ones get paid.

## Seasonal and regional considerations

San Diego's mild climate means exterior work rarely gets weathered out, but it creates its own scheduling pressure: the region's construction labor market runs hot year-round, and permit offices in the City of San Diego routinely run 4–8 weeks on over-the-counter plan check for anything beyond simple permits. Starting the permitting process before demolition — not after — is the difference between a project that flows and one that stalls in a partially finished state for two months.

Santa Ana wind events in fall and early winter drive a spike in fire damage rebuilds across East County and the inland valleys. If your project follows a fire loss, expect material and subcontractor availability to tighten during those windows.
