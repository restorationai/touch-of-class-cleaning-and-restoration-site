**Flood damage in Richardson?** Call now for emergency service before standing water spreads past the room where it started. Floodwater behaves differently than a burst pipe or an overflowing appliance: it often carries silt, runoff, and whatever the water picked up on its way into your home, and it rarely stays contained to one room. The longer it sits against drywall, baseboards, and subfloor, the more material has to come out rather than just dry.

## What flood damage restoration actually involves

Flood cleanup starts with identifying what kind of water you're dealing with. Water that entered from outside, through a foundation crack, a window well, or storm runoff, is classified as Category 3 (black water) because it's assumed to carry contaminants from soil, lawn chemicals, and runoff debris. That classification changes the entire approach: porous materials that touched the water, carpet padding, baseboards, drywall below the flood line, often can't be dried in place and have to be removed rather than salvaged. Equipment on a flood job typically includes submersible or truck-mounted extraction pumps for standing water, air movers and low-grain-refrigerant dehumidifiers for structural drying, and moisture meters to track what's happening inside walls and under flooring where you can't see it. A single flooded room might dry in a few days; a flooded first floor with wet wall cavities can take a week or more of monitored drying before rebuild work starts.

## Our process

1. **Contamination and safety assessment.** Before anything else, we confirm the water category and check for safety hazards like saturated electrical outlets or compromised flooring before crews or homeowners re-enter affected rooms.
2. **Water extraction.** Standing water comes out first, using pumps for depth and extraction wands for carpet and flooring, to stop the water from wicking further into walls and subfloor.
3. **Removal of contaminated, unsalvageable materials.** Flood-affected drywall, insulation, and carpet padding typically get cut and removed to the waterline rather than dried, since porous materials that contacted Category 3 water generally can't be fully decontaminated.
4. **Structural drying with moisture mapping.** Air movers and dehumidifiers are placed based on room size and material type, and moisture readings are logged daily so drying isn't called finished based on guesswork.
5. **Cleaning and antimicrobial application.** Remaining structural surfaces are cleaned and treated before any rebuild work begins, reducing the conditions that let mold take hold in wall cavities that stayed damp.

## What separates a good flood damage response from a bad one

The biggest mistake on flood jobs is treating floodwater like clean water. Drying carpet padding or leaving wet insulation inside a wall because the drywall "looks fine" from the room side is one of the most common corners cut, and it's exactly where mold shows up six weeks later. A thorough response documents moisture levels room by room before any demolition, because that documentation is what an adjuster needs to support the scope of work. Good flood response also accounts for what's hidden: water that entered through a foundation crack often tracks sideways under flooring long after the visible puddle is gone, and skipping moisture mapping under cabinets, along baseboards, and inside closets is how homeowners end up with a second, smaller problem months after the first one was supposedly fixed.

## What does flood damage restoration cost?

Costs vary widely based on how much water came in, how long it sat, how many rooms were affected, and whether structural materials need to be removed and rebuilt. These are typical industry ranges, not a quote, every loss is different, and Touch of Class Cleaning and Restoration provides a written scope of work before any work begins so you know what's included.

| Scenario | Typical range |
|---|---|
| Single room, shallow water, quick extraction | $1,500 - $4,000 |
| Multiple rooms, flooring and baseboard removal | $4,000 - $9,000 |
| Flooded first floor with drywall removal | $8,000 - $18,000 |
| Basement or crawlspace flooding with contents loss | $5,000 - $12,000 |
| Whole-home flood with structural drying and rebuild prep | $18,000 - $40,000+ |

Standard homeowners insurance generally does not cover damage from external flooding, runoff, or overflowing creeks; that typically requires a separate flood policy through the NFIP or a private flood carrier, while water damage from a burst pipe or appliance failure is usually a homeowners claim. Knowing which policy applies affects how the claim is filed, which is part of why documentation matters from the first visit.

## Seasonal & regional considerations

Richardson sits in an area with heavy clay soil that drains slowly, and the North Texas spring and early summer storm season regularly brings fast-moving thunderstorms that drop a lot of rain in a short window. Flat terrain and older drainage infrastructure in some neighborhoods mean runoff can back up against foundations faster than it can be routed away, and homes near creeks or low-lying lots see the most repeat flood risk. Flood damage calls tend to cluster around these storm events rather than being spread evenly through the year.
