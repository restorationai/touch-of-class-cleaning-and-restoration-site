## Why Allen Properties See Water Damage Issues

Much of Allen's housing stock was built during the city's major growth years from the 1990s through the 2010s, which means post-tension slab foundations are far more common here than pier-and-beam construction. Slab foundations hide plumbing runs beneath the concrete, so a slow leak can travel sideways under the slab for weeks before it ever shows up as a damp spot on tile or carpet. Add in the expansive clay soil common across Collin County, which swells after heavy rain and contracts hard during August heat, and you get seasonal slab movement that stresses pipe joints in ways homeowners rarely see coming. Summer thunderstorms and the occasional hailstorm also put roofs and window flashing to the test, and a damaged roof penetration can push water into attic insulation and drywall long before it's visible from the street.

## Our Water Damage Restoration Process in Allen

We start with moisture mapping, not guesswork, because in slab-foundation homes the source of a leak is often several feet from where water actually surfaces. That means checking under cabinetry, behind baseboards, and along interior wall cavities before we commit to a drying plan. Once the source is controlled, we move into water extraction, pulling standing water from flooring and padding, then set up air movers and dehumidifiers sized to the affected square footage. Drywall and baseboards that have wicked water are cut back to sound material when saturation is too deep to dry in place, a call we make based on moisture readings rather than visual inspection alone. We document every step with photos and readings for your insurance file, and we monitor drying progress daily until materials read dry per the IICRC S500 standard rather than just dry to the touch.
