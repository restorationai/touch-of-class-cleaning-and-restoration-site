Between slab foundations, clay-heavy soil, and a climate that swings from drought to downpour in the same week, standing water here moves fast through walls, subfloors, and HVAC returns before most homeowners even notice the smell of wet drywall.

## Why Dallas Properties See Water Damage Issues

Much of Dallas sits on Blackland Prairie clay, soil that swells when it's saturated and shrinks hard when it dries out. That movement stresses slab foundations and the plumbing running through or under them, which is why so many of our water losses here trace back to a cracked slab line or a shifted supply pipe rather than an obvious leak. Add in a humid subtropical climate with intense spring thunderstorms and the occasional frozen pipe burst during a hard winter snap, and you get a city where water intrusion shows up through more entry points than most. Homes built from the 1950s through the 1970s, a big share of the local housing stock, often still have original cast iron or galvanized plumbing that's well past its intended service life, which raises the odds of a slow leak turning into a real water cleanup job.

## Our Water Damage Restoration Process in Dallas

Water extraction starts the moment our crew is on site, pulling standing water with truck-mount and portable extractors before it has a chance to wick further into baseboards and subfloor. On slab homes, that often means pulling carpet pad early since pad on slab holds moisture against the concrete and slows everything down if it's left in place. Structural drying follows using air movers and dehumidifiers set to actual psychrometric readings, not guesswork, with moisture content checked daily in drywall, framing, and flooring until materials are back to a dry standard. Because Dallas summers run hot and humid for months at a time, we pay close attention to ambient humidity inside the home during drying. Open windows or a system that's not holding its setpoint can undo two days of progress in an afternoon.

## Local note

One thing that trips up out-of-town crews working in Dallas: clay soil doesn't just affect the foundation, it affects how long a slab actually takes to read dry. A moisture meter can show an acceptable reading on the surface while the clay beneath is still holding water against the underside of the slab, especially after a prolonged leak. We factor that into how long we keep drying equipment running rather than pulling it the moment surface readings look good, because a slab that reads dry too early is often a callback waiting to happen.
