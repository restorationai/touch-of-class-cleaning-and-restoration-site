## Why Plano Properties See Water Damage Issues

Much of Plano sits on expansive clay soils common across North Texas, the kind that swell during wet spells and shrink hard during the summer drought cycle. That movement stresses slab foundations and the plumbing lines running beneath them, which is why slab leaks show up so often in Plano service calls. Add in sudden, heavy thunderstorms that can drop more rain in an hour than the ground can absorb, and you get a second common pattern: water backing up against foundations or finding its way in through grade-level window wells and garage thresholds. Homes built during Plano's major growth years in the 1970s through the 1990s also tend to have original cast-iron or early PVC supply lines that are now reaching the age where failures become more frequent.

## Our Water Damage Restoration Process in Plano

Every call starts with a moisture assessment, not a guess. We use penetrating and non-penetrating moisture meters to map how far water has traveled behind baseboards, under flooring, and into wall cavities, since surface-dry drywall in a Plano home can still be hiding a saturated slab edge underneath. Extraction comes next with truck-mounted or portable units depending on access, followed by structural drying set up and monitored per the IICRC S500 standard, which gives us a benchmark for how many air movers and dehumidifiers a given room actually needs rather than overloading a space and running up unnecessary equipment time. We log moisture readings daily until materials hit dry standard, then move into any repair work the loss requires.

## Local note

Many Plano homes, like much of North Texas, are built on concrete slab foundations with plumbing lines routed underneath the slab rather than through an accessible crawlspace. That means a slab leak can run for days or weeks before anyone notices, slowly saturating subfloor insulation and wicking up into baseboards and cabinet toe-kicks long before carpet or flooring feels wet to the touch. If a Plano homeowner notices a warm spot on the floor, a spike in the water bill, or a faint mildew smell with no visible source, it's worth having the slab checked before the damage reaches the framing.
