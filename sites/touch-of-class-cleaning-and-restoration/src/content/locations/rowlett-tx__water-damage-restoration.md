## Why Rowlett Properties See Water Damage Issues

Rowlett sits on a peninsula into Lake Ray Hubbard, and most of the housing stock from the 1970s through the 2000s was built on concrete slab foundations over the expansive Blackland Prairie clay common across North Texas. That clay swells when it's saturated and shrinks when it dries, and the constant movement stresses slab joints and the plumbing lines running through them. A slow slab leak can go unnoticed for weeks before it surfaces as warped flooring or a musty smell near an interior wall. Add in the area's heavy spring storms and the occasional remnant of a Gulf system pushing through Rockwall and Dallas counties, and gutters, grading, and window flashing all get tested hard. Homes closer to the lake also deal with higher ambient humidity, which slows natural evaporation and gives standing water more time to work into subfloor and drywall before anyone notices.

## Our Water Damage Restoration Process in Rowlett

The first visit is about finding where the water actually went, not just where it showed up. We pull baseboards and check behind drywall with moisture meters, because in a slab home water from a kitchen or bathroom leak often travels sideways under flooring before it surfaces somewhere else entirely. Extraction comes next, using truck-mounted or portable pumps depending on how much water is standing and how accessible the area is. Then we set up air movers and dehumidifiers and monitor moisture content daily, drying structural materials in line with the IICRC S500 standard rather than guessing by feel or smell. In Rowlett specifically, we pay close attention to flooring over slab, since tile and engineered wood installed directly on concrete hold moisture longer than homes with a raised foundation and crawl space, which changes how long the drying phase actually takes.
