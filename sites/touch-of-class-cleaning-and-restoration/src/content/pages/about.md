Touch of Class Cleaning and Restoration started in 2025 with a simple premise: homeowners dealing with water, smoke, or storm damage need a crew that shows up, explains what's actually happening to their house, and gets the work done right the first time. Restoration isn't a side business bolted onto carpet cleaning or handyman work here. It's the focus, because a property in distress doesn't have time for a contractor who's learning as they go.

## What we do

Most calls start the same way: someone finds standing water in a hallway, smells smoke damage that won't air out, or opens a closet to find something growing where it shouldn't be. Our job is to show up, assess what's actually happening in the walls and under the flooring (not just what's visible), and put a plan in place before more of the structure is compromised. A lot of the work happens alongside insurance adjusters: documenting the loss with photos and moisture readings, itemizing what's affected, and making sure the paperwork matches what we find on site. Homeowners are usually stressed when they call. Part of the job is slowing down enough to explain what's next.

## Our certifications and licensure

Restoration work is governed by real technical standards, not guesswork. The IICRC S500 water damage standard, for example, sets out how structures should be dried, monitored, and verified before a job is considered complete, and we structure our drying and documentation process around it. Pre-1978 homes carry additional considerations around lead-safe work practices under EPA guidelines, which shapes how we handle demolition and debris in older properties. We're upfront with homeowners about what our current licensing and credentialing covers for their specific job, and happy to answer those questions directly on a call.

## Where we work

We're based in Richardson and work across the surrounding North Texas communities. This part of the Dallas metro sits on expansive clay soil that shifts with the region's wet-dry cycles, which means slab foundations here see real movement and plumbing lines take stress that older cast-iron and copper systems weren't built for. Summer storms roll through fast and hard, and a lot of the housing stock dates from different build eras, each with its own quirks when water or fire gets into the structure. Knowing the area's construction patterns matters as much as knowing the drying equipment.
