## What does fire damage restoration actually involve?

Fire damage restoration is the full process of cleaning, drying, deodorizing, and rebuilding a property after a fire. It covers four distinct types of damage: structural char and heat damage, soot and smoke residue on surfaces and inside HVAC systems, water damage from firefighting hoses and sprinklers, and odor that penetrates drywall, insulation, and wood framing.

Most homeowners picture the charred walls and assume that is the main problem. In practice, smoke and water are often more destructive than the fire itself. Soot is acidic. Left on metal fixtures, appliances, or electronics for more than 72 hours, it begins to corrode. Water from suppression soaks into subfloors and wall cavities, creating ideal conditions for mold growth within 24 to 48 hours, according to [IICRC S500 standards](https://www.iicrc.org). A restoration crew addresses all four damage types in sequence, not just the visible burn area.

The scope of work on a typical Sunrise Manor residential loss includes:

- Emergency board-up and roof tarping to secure the structure
- Water extraction and structural drying using LGR dehumidifiers and air movers
- Dry-ice blasting or chemical sponge cleaning of soot from framing and surfaces
- HEPA air scrubbing to remove airborne particulates
- Thermal fogging or hydroxyl treatment for smoke odor
- Contents pack-out and restoration for salvageable belongings
- Reconstruction of damaged structural elements, drywall, flooring, and finishes

## What should you do in the first 24 hours after a fire in Sunrise Manor?

In the first 24 hours, your three priorities are safety, documentation, and mitigation. Do not re-enter the property until the fire department has cleared it. Once cleared, photograph every room before touching anything, including ceilings, floors, and the exterior. That documentation protects your insurance claim.

Call your insurance company to open a claim, then call a restoration company. The order matters: your insurer needs to know a loss occurred, but you do not need their approval before starting emergency mitigation. Most policies require you to take reasonable steps to prevent further damage, which means boarding up broken windows and extracting standing water right away, not waiting for an adjuster.

Here is what the first 24 hours should look like:

1. Confirm fire department clearance before entry
2. Photograph all damage from every angle before disturbing anything
3. Call your insurance company to open the claim
4. Call a certified restoration company for emergency board-up and water extraction
5. Secure temporary housing if the structure is uninhabitable
6. Retrieve essential documents and medications if safe to do so

## How does insurance cover fire damage restoration in Nevada?

Most standard homeowners insurance policies in Nevada cover fire damage restoration, including the water and smoke damage that comes with it. Your dwelling coverage pays for structural repairs. Your personal property coverage pays for damaged contents. Additional living expenses coverage pays for temporary housing while your home is being restored.

The [Nevada Division of Insurance](https://doi.nevada.gov) requires insurers to acknowledge a claim within 10 working days and accept or deny it within 30 working days of receiving proof of loss. In practice, an adjuster will schedule an inspection within a few days of your call.

A few things that affect your claim outcome:

- **Documentation quality.** Photos and video taken before any cleanup begins are the single most important factor. Adjusters rely on this evidence to scope the loss.
- **Written scope of work.** A restoration company should provide a line-item estimate using Xactimate, the industry-standard estimating platform. This gives the adjuster a verifiable scope to work from.
- **Proof of mitigation.** If you delayed calling a restoration company and secondary damage (mold, further structural deterioration) occurred, the insurer may dispute those costs. Acting quickly protects the full claim.
- **Policy exclusions.** Arson, vacancy clauses, and certain types of intentional damage are excluded. Accidental fires, electrical fires, and appliance fires are almost always covered.

## What does the fire damage restoration process look like from start to finish?

The restoration process follows a defined sequence. Understanding each phase helps you set realistic expectations and ask the right questions when reviewing a contractor's scope.

**Phase 1: Emergency stabilization (Day 1-2).** The crew boards up windows and doors, tarps any roof penetrations, and extracts standing water. This stops further damage and makes the structure safe to work in.

**Phase 2: Assessment and scoping (Day 2-4).** A project manager walks the entire structure, documents damage with photos and moisture readings, and produces a written scope of work. This is the document your insurance adjuster will review.

**Phase 3: Demolition and cleaning (Week 1-2).** Charred and unsalvageable materials are removed. Soot is cleaned from framing, subfloors, and surfaces using dry-chemical sponges, HEPA vacuums, and, on heavy char, dry-ice blasting. Contents are inventoried and either cleaned on-site or packed out for off-site restoration and storage.

**Phase 4: Drying and deodorization (Week 1-3).** Even after water extraction, structural materials hold moisture. LGR dehumidifiers and air movers run continuously, with moisture readings logged daily until the structure reaches dry standard per IICRC S500. Thermal foggers or hydroxyl generators run simultaneously to neutralize smoke odor at the molecular level.

**Phase 5: Reconstruction (Week 2-8).** Once the structure is clean and dry, rebuilding begins. New drywall, insulation, flooring, paint, and fixtures are installed to pre-loss condition. The timeline depends on the extent of structural damage and permit requirements from Clark County.
