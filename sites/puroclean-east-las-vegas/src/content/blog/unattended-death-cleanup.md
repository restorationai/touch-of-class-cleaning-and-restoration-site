## Who cleans up after an unattended death?

A licensed biohazard remediation company handles unattended death cleanup, not a regular cleaning crew, not a property manager, and not family members. When a person is not discovered for hours, days, or longer, biological materials can affect surfaces, air quality, and structural materials in ways that require specialized training, equipment, and disposal protocols.

The [CDC's guidance on bloodborne pathogen exposure](https://www.cdc.gov/niosh/topics/bbp/) makes clear why this work requires professional handling: bloodborne pathogens including hepatitis B, hepatitis C, and HIV can survive on surfaces and in materials long after a death. Proper remediation follows OSHA 29 CFR 1910.1030 standards for bloodborne pathogen control.

## What should you NOT do before the crew arrives?

Do not enter the space to clean, remove items, or air it out on your own. This is the single most important instruction for families.

Here is why each of these matters:

- **Do not clean anything yourself.** Biological materials at an unattended death scene are classified as biohazardous waste under Nevada law. Improper handling can expose you to serious pathogens and create liability.
- **Do not remove personal belongings from the affected area.** Items that appear undamaged may have absorbed odors or contaminants. The remediation crew will assess what can be safely cleaned and what must be discarded.
- **Do not use fans or open windows to "air out" the space.** This can spread airborne particles to unaffected areas of the home and complicate the remediation.
- **Do not use consumer odor-masking sprays.** These do not neutralize biological odors and can interfere with the crew's ability to locate all affected materials.
- **Do not discard anything before the insurance adjuster has documented the loss.** Premature disposal can reduce or void your claim.

If the scene is in a rental property, notify the landlord or property manager after law enforcement has cleared the scene, but before any cleaning begins.

## How does unattended death cleanup actually work?

Biohazard remediation at an unattended death scene follows a structured process that typically takes one to three days for a single-room scene, and longer for larger or more complex situations.

**Step 1: Scene assessment.** A trained technician walks the property in full PPE, including a sealed yellow Tyvek suit, full-face respirator, nitrile gloves, and taped wrists and boots. They identify all affected surfaces, materials, and HVAC pathways. You will receive a written scope of work before anything begins.

**Step 2: Containment.** Affected areas are sealed with poly sheeting and placed under negative air pressure using HEPA-filtered air scrubbers. This prevents cross-contamination to the rest of the property.

**Step 3: Removal of biohazardous materials.** Porous materials that cannot be decontaminated, such as carpet, padding, drywall, and certain furniture, are removed and bagged in certified biohazard containers for disposal through a licensed medical waste hauler. This step is performed with zero graphic exposure to family members.

**Step 4: Cleaning and disinfection.** All hard surfaces are cleaned with EPA-registered disinfectants rated for bloodborne pathogen elimination. This includes subfloor, wall framing, and any structural materials that were exposed.

**Step 5: Odor remediation.** Persistent biological odor is treated with hydroxyl generators or thermal fogging, depending on the materials involved. This is not masking, it is molecular neutralization.

**Step 6: Clearance verification.** A final inspection confirms the space meets health and safety standards before the property is returned to the family or landlord.

Throughout every step, the crew works discreetly. Vehicles are parked without drawing attention. No details about the scene are shared with neighbors or bystanders. Families are not required to be present during the work.

## Does insurance cover unattended death cleanup?

Homeowners and renters insurance policies often cover biohazard cleanup after an unattended death, though coverage varies by policy and carrier. Here is the general pattern:

**What is typically covered:** The cost of professional biohazard remediation, including labor, materials removal, and disposal, is often covered under the "additional living expenses" or "property damage" section of a homeowners policy. Some policies have a specific biohazard or trauma cleanup rider.

**What affects coverage:** The relationship between the deceased and the policyholder, the cause of death, and whether the property is owner-occupied or a rental all affect how a claim is handled. Rental property owners typically file through their landlord policy.

**What to do:** Call your insurance carrier's claims line after law enforcement has cleared the scene and before remediation begins. Ask specifically about biohazard cleanup coverage. A good remediation company will document the scope of work in a format your adjuster can use directly, which reduces back-and-forth and protects your claim.

**What if there is no insurance?** Some states have victim assistance programs that help cover cleanup costs in cases involving a crime. Nevada's [Victims of Crime Program](https://nvcourts.gov) may be a resource depending on the circumstances. The remediation company can often refer you to the right contact.

## How long does unattended death cleanup take?

Most single-room unattended death scenes in a standard Las Vegas home are remediated within one to three days. Larger scenes, multi-room involvement, or cases where the discovery was significantly delayed can extend the timeline to four or five days.

Factors that affect the timeline:

- **Size of the affected area.** A scene confined to one room is faster than one that has spread to adjacent flooring or HVAC.
- **Type of materials involved.** Concrete subfloor is faster to decontaminate than wood framing or plaster walls.
- **Extent of structural removal needed.** If drywall or flooring must be removed and replaced, reconstruction adds time after remediation is complete.
- **Property access.** If the property requires coordination with a landlord, estate attorney, or law enforcement evidence hold, scheduling may add a day.

The remediation company should give you a realistic timeline estimate after the initial assessment, in writing, before work begins.
