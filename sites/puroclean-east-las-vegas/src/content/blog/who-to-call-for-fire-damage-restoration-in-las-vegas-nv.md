## Who Else Do I Need to Call, and When?

Fire damage involves several parties, and calling them in the right sequence protects both your property and your claim.

**1. The fire department**, They go first, always. Do not re-enter the structure until they have cleared it as safe. In Las Vegas, that clearance comes from Clark County Fire Department or Las Vegas Fire and Rescue depending on your address.

**2. Your insurance company**, Call your insurer after the fire department clears the scene and after you've contacted a restoration company. Many homeowners call the insurer first, which is fine, but do not wait for an adjuster to arrive before starting mitigation. Most policies require prompt action to prevent further damage. Delaying mitigation can give the insurer grounds to reduce your claim.

**3. A licensed plumber or electrician**, If the fire involved your electrical panel, gas lines, or plumbing, a licensed trade contractor needs to sign off before power or water is restored. Your restoration crew can coordinate this, but the licensed trade work is separate.

**4. Your mortgage lender**, If you have a mortgage, your lender may need to be listed on the insurance check and may have requirements about how restoration funds are disbursed. Call them after you've notified your insurer.

**Who NOT to call first:** Do not call a general contractor before a restoration company. Restoration and reconstruction are different scopes. Restoration comes first, stabilization, drying, soot removal, odor treatment, contents pack-out. Reconstruction (rebuilding walls, replacing flooring, repainting) comes after. A contractor who skips the restoration phase and goes straight to rebuild often leaves hidden smoke damage and odor that resurfaces months later.

## What Happens When You Call a Fire Restoration Company?

The first phone call takes about five minutes. Have the property address, a brief description of what burned and where, and your insurance policy number ready if you have it. The crew coordinator will ask whether the fire department has cleared the scene and whether the structure has power and water.

Here's what a certified crew does on arrival:

1. **Safety assessment**, Structural integrity check, hazardous material identification (asbestos in older Las Vegas homes is not uncommon), and confirmation that utilities are safe.
2. **Scope documentation**, Photos, moisture readings, and a written damage inventory. This documentation is the foundation of your insurance claim. A thorough scope protects you from underpayment.
3. **Emergency stabilization**, Board-up of broken windows and doors, roof tarping if needed, and containment to prevent soot from spreading to unaffected areas.
4. **Contents assessment and pack-out**, Salvageable contents are inventoried, packed, and transported to a climate-controlled facility for cleaning and storage. This protects your belongings while the structure is being restored. See our [contents restoration and pack-out service](/services/contents-restoration-pack-out/) for how that process works.
5. **Soot and smoke removal**, Dry-chemical sponging of walls and ceilings, HEPA vacuuming of ductwork, and thermal fogging or hydroxyl treatment for odor. This is skilled work, wrong cleaning methods (wet-wiping dry soot, for example) permanently set stains.
6. **Structural drying**, Fire suppression water creates secondary water damage. LGR dehumidifiers and air movers run until moisture readings return to pre-loss baselines, verified with calibrated meters.

For a detailed breakdown of each restoration phase, see [The Fire Damage Restoration Process, Explained](/blog/fire-damage-restoration-process/).

## What Should I Do Before the Crew Arrives?

If the fire department has cleared the scene and it is safe to be near the property, a few steps help protect your claim and your belongings.

- **Do not enter if there is any structural uncertainty.** Ceilings weakened by heat and water can collapse without warning.
- **Take photos from the exterior** before anything is moved or cleaned. Your phone camera is fine.
- **Do not run the HVAC system.** It will circulate soot through every duct and deposit it in rooms that were not affected by the fire.
- **Do not wipe soot surfaces with a wet cloth.** Wet-wiping dry soot smears and sets it permanently into porous materials.
- **Locate your insurance policy documents**, the declarations page, your policy number, and your insurer's claims line.
- **Secure pets and family** at a safe location. Smoke residue is a respiratory hazard, especially for children, elderly family members, and animals.
