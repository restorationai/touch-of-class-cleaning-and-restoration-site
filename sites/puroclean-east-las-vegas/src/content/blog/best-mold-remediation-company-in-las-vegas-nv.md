## How to Compare Mold Remediation Companies in Las Vegas

The right mold remediation company does three things well: proper containment, documented clearance, and clean insurance paperwork. Here is how to evaluate any company on this list, or any company you find on your own.

**Containment and negative air pressure.** Mold spores spread when disturbed. A company that skips poly-sheeting containment and negative air machines is spreading the problem while they work. Ask directly: "Do you run negative air pressure during remediation?" If they hesitate, that's your answer.

**IICRC S520 certification.** The [IICRC](https://www.iicrc.org) publishes the S520 Standard for Professional Mold Remediation. Ask whether the technicians on-site, not just the company owner, hold current IICRC certification. A certified company with uncertified field crews is a gap worth asking about.

**Written scope before work begins.** Mold remediation costs vary widely depending on the square footage affected, the materials involved (drywall vs. framing vs. HVAC), and whether the moisture source has been fixed. Any reputable company provides a written scope of work before a single tool comes out. Verbal estimates with no documentation create disputes at the worst possible time.

**Clearance testing.** Remediation is not complete when the visible mold is gone. A post-remediation verification (PRV), typically an air sample or surface swab sent to an independent lab, confirms the spore count is back to an acceptable level. Ask whether clearance testing is included or offered as an add-on, and whether it is performed by an independent party.

**Insurance documentation.** If you're filing a claim, your adjuster will want moisture readings, photos, a written scope, and a remediation report. Ask the company whether they provide all of this and whether they communicate directly with adjusters. This step alone can determine whether your claim is approved or denied. ## What Mold Remediation Actually Costs in Las Vegas

Mold remediation in Las Vegas typically runs $1,500 to $6,000 for a contained single-room job with clean materials. Larger losses, multiple rooms, affected framing, HVAC contamination, can run $10,000 or more. The [EPA's mold guidance](https://www.epa.gov/mold) suggests that any affected area larger than 10 square feet warrants professional remediation rather than a DIY attempt.

Homeowners insurance covers mold remediation when the mold results from a covered peril, a burst pipe, appliance overflow, or storm intrusion, and when the claim is filed promptly. Mold from a slow, long-running leak that the homeowner should have caught is frequently denied. Document the moisture source, get a written scope, and call your adjuster before work begins if possible. PuroClean of East Las Vegas handles that documentation process as part of every job.
