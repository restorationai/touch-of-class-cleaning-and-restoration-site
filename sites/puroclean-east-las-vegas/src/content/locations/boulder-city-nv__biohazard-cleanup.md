## Why Boulder City Properties Present Unique Biohazard Considerations

Boulder City's housing stock tells a story. Many homes in the Historic District and surrounding streets were built during or shortly after the construction of Hoover Dam, which means structures from the 1930s and 1940s are not uncommon. These older homes often feature porous concrete subfloors, original hardwood, and plaster-and-lath walls — materials that absorb biological contamination differently than modern construction. Porous surfaces can harbor pathogens well below what's visible to the eye, which is why surface-level cleaning is never sufficient. Proper biohazard remediation here means testing beyond the stain, treating the substrate, and in some cases removing and disposing of affected materials in accordance with Nevada state regulations for biohazardous waste.

The desert climate adds another layer. Boulder City's low humidity can cause biological fluids to dry and concentrate rapidly, which may actually make contamination harder to identify visually while leaving microbial risk intact. Heat accelerates decomposition and odor penetration into porous materials, particularly in homes that may not have central air conditioning running continuously.

## Our Biohazard Cleanup Process in Boulder City

Every job begins with a careful assessment — not a visual scan, but a methodical evaluation of all surfaces, materials, and airflow patterns in the affected space. We use ATP testing and other detection tools to identify contamination that isn't visible. From there, the process follows a structured sequence:

- **Containment**: Affected areas are isolated to prevent cross-contamination to other parts of the home or building.
- **Personal protective equipment and regulatory compliance**: All technicians work in full PPE. Biohazardous waste is packaged, labeled, and transported for disposal in compliance with Nevada Administrative Code Chapter 444, which governs infectious waste management.
- **Surface and substrate treatment**: We treat not just the surface but the layers beneath — subfloor, wall cavity, or concrete — depending on what absorbed contamination.
- **Odor neutralization**: Enzymatic treatments and hydroxyl or ozone technology address odor at the molecular level, not just at the surface.
- **Final clearance verification**: We document the remediated space before closing out the job, providing records that can support insurance claims or future property transactions.

For properties in ZIP code 89005, our team coordinates directly with local waste disposal contacts familiar with Clark County requirements, which streamlines the back end of the job.
