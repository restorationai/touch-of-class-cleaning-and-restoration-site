## Why Whitney Properties See Burst and Frozen Pipe Problems

The Las Vegas Valley's desert reputation works against homeowners here. Because hard freezes feel like anomalies, many Whitney properties — particularly the single-story ranches and garden-style condos built through the 1980s and 1990s — have supply lines routed through exterior walls, unheated garages, and crawl spaces with minimal insulation. When a cold snap pushes overnight lows below 28°F for several consecutive hours, those lines are vulnerable.

Compounding the risk: the sandy, expansive soil common across this part of Clark County shifts seasonally. Underground supply lines that have experienced ground movement over decades develop micro-fractures that a freeze-thaw cycle can open into full ruptures. Homes near Boulder Highway and the Winterwood area, where lot grading sometimes channels runoff toward foundations, can see this pattern accelerate. Older polybutylene plumbing — installed widely in Nevada tract construction through the mid-1990s before the material fell out of code favor — is still present in a meaningful portion of Whitney's housing stock. Polybutylene becomes brittle with age and is particularly susceptible to freeze damage. If your home was built before 1995 and hasn't had a full repipe, that's worth knowing before the next cold night.

## Our Burst and Frozen Pipe Process in Whitney

The first priority on any burst pipe call is stopping the water. If the main is still running when we arrive, we locate and confirm shutoff before anything else. From there, the work breaks into three phases that overlap rather than run in sequence.

**Source and scope.** We use moisture meters and thermal imaging to trace where water traveled — not just where it pooled. Water follows the path of least resistance: it moves under flooring, wicks up drywall, and collects inside wall cavities before it becomes visible at the surface. Mapping the full wet zone before we start extraction prevents secondary damage from being missed.

**Extraction and controlled drying.** Standing water comes out with truck-mounted or portable extraction equipment. Structural drying follows, using industrial air movers and dehumidifiers calibrated to the square footage and material types in the affected area. In Whitney's dry desert air, ambient humidity is low — which helps drying times — but interior materials like engineered wood flooring and cabinetry still need to be monitored daily with moisture readings, not just dried by feel.

**Documentation.** Every affected area is photographed and moisture-mapped at intake and throughout the drying cycle. That documentation matters when you file a claim with your homeowner's carrier.
