## Our Basement Flood Cleanup Process in Whitney

The first step on every job is a moisture mapping survey using thermal imaging and calibrated moisture meters. Water in a Whitney basement rarely stays where it entered — it wicks laterally through concrete block, travels under tile adhesive, and saturates the bottom plates of interior walls before it becomes visible at the surface. Knowing where the moisture actually is shapes every decision that follows.

Once the scope is mapped, we extract standing water with truck-mounted and portable extraction units, then set a drying system calibrated to the conditions: low-grain refrigerant dehumidifiers and high-velocity air movers positioned to create a drying chamber around affected materials. In the desert, ambient humidity is low, which helps — but finished basements with spray foam insulation or vapor barriers trap moisture in ways that require targeted equipment placement rather than simply opening windows.

We monitor drying progress with daily readings and adjust equipment until structural materials reach dry standard. Nothing is closed in or rebuilt until the numbers confirm it.
