## Why North Las Vegas Properties See Burst Pipe Issues

The Mojave climate creates a counterintuitive vulnerability. Because hard freezes are rare, builders in North Las Vegas — particularly in tracts developed rapidly through the 1990s and early 2000s in areas like Eldorado and Cheyenne Ranch — often routed supply lines through exterior walls, unheated garages, and attic spaces without the insulation depth standard in colder climates. The assumption was reasonable at the time. But when an Arctic air mass pushes temperatures below freezing for consecutive nights, those under-insulated lines are exposed in ways homeowners don't anticipate.

Soil conditions compound the problem. The caliche-heavy ground common across the valley limits how deeply utility lines are buried in some older sections of North Las Vegas, leaving service lines closer to the surface and more susceptible to temperature swings. A pipe that survives a single cold night may develop a hairline fracture that doesn't fully fail until water pressure cycles the next morning.

Homes near Nellis Air Force Base and in the 89030 and 89081 ZIP codes include housing stock from multiple eras — some dating to the mid-20th century with galvanized or early copper plumbing that has already been stressed by decades of hard water mineral buildup. When those pipes freeze, they don't always burst cleanly. They can split along existing weak points, releasing water into wall cavities that show no visible damage at the surface for hours.

## Our Burst & Frozen Pipe Process in North Las Vegas

When we arrive, the first priority is confirming the water source is controlled — either the homeowner has shut the main or we help locate the shutoff. From there, the process is methodical:

**Moisture mapping** comes before any demolition. Thermal imaging and calibrated moisture meters let us trace where water has migrated behind walls and under flooring without opening every surface. In slab-on-grade homes common throughout North Las Vegas, this step is especially important because water under a concrete slab behaves differently than water over a crawl space — it tends to wick outward and upward through grout lines and expansion joints rather than pooling visibly.

**Controlled demo and extraction** removes only what the moisture data supports. Saturated drywall, wet insulation, and compromised flooring come out. Structural materials that read wet but are salvageable get dried in place using desiccant or refrigerant dehumidifiers and directed airflow.

**Drying validation** uses daily readings to confirm that wall cavities, subfloor assemblies, and framing are returning to acceptable moisture levels before any reconstruction begins. We document every reading — that documentation matters when an insurance adjuster reviews the claim.

One pattern we see in North Las Vegas that catches homeowners off guard: stucco exteriors, nearly universal in the valley, can mask water intrusion from a burst pipe for longer than wood-frame siding would.
