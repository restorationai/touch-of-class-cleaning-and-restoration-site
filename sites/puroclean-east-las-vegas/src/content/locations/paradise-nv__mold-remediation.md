Paradise, NV sits in one of the driest climates in the country, yet mold turns up with surprising regularity here — inside high-rise condos a block off the Strip, in aging rental units near UNLV, and in single-family homes throughout Paradise Palms. The reason isn't rain. It's the combination of overworked HVAC systems fighting 115-degree summers, plumbing that runs through concrete slabs, and buildings that were never designed with humidity control in mind. When a slow leak or a condensation problem goes unnoticed for even 48 to 72 hours, mold colonies can establish themselves behind drywall before a tenant or owner ever notices a smell.

The housing stock in Paradise is unusually varied. Along the 89109 corridor near the Las Vegas Convention Center and Allegiant Stadium, you have dense mid-century commercial conversions and hotel-adjacent residential units with shared HVAC risers — systems that recirculate air across dozens of units and can spread spores quickly when one unit has an active moisture problem. Further south, in the neighborhoods around Harry Reid International Airport and the 89119 zip code, you find 1970s and 1980s ranch-style construction with original ductwork that sweats heavily during the transition between summer heat and aggressive air conditioning.

Paradise Palms, one of the area's most architecturally distinctive mid-century neighborhoods, presents its own challenge: flat or low-slope roofs that were stylish in 1960 but are prone to membrane failures that let water pool and wick into ceilings and wall cavities. Owners often don't discover the intrusion until a musty odor shows up in a bedroom months after the original event.

The University District near UNLV adds another layer — high tenant turnover in rental properties means small leaks under sinks or around shower pans go unreported for months. By the time a property manager schedules an inspection, the mold growth is often extensive enough to require professional containment and remediation rather than a simple surface cleaning.

## Our Mold Remediation Process in Paradise

Every job begins with a thorough visual inspection and moisture mapping using thermal imaging and calibrated moisture meters. In Paradise's climate, this step matters more than it might elsewhere — because humidity is so low outdoors, building materials dry unevenly. Drywall facing an exterior wall may read dry while the cavity behind it still holds moisture against the framing. Skipping this step is how mold comes back.

Once the affected area is identified, we establish physical containment using negative air pressure and poly barriers to prevent spores from migrating to unaffected rooms or shared HVAC systems — a critical step in multi-unit buildings near the Strip or in condo associations. Affected porous materials (drywall, insulation, wood framing) that cannot be restored are removed and bagged for disposal per Nevada disposal guidelines. Remaining structural surfaces are treated with EPA-registered antimicrobial agents and HEPA-vacuumed. We do not consider a job complete until post-remediation moisture readings confirm the environment is dry enough to prevent recurrence.

## Paradise Insurance & HOA Coordination

Many properties in Paradise — particularly condos and townhomes near the Strip corridor and the University District — are governed by HOA agreements that specify how remediation work must be documented and who is responsible for common-wall repairs. We photograph affected materials, generate moisture logs, and produce written scope-of-work documentation that satisfies both insurance adjusters and HOA management companies. If your carrier requires an independent industrial hygienist to issue an air-clearance certificate before repairs begin or after remediation is complete, we can coordinate that testing so it doesn't stall your timeline.

Most major carriers cover mold remediation when it stems from a sudden and accidental water loss — a burst pipe, an appliance failure, an HVAC overflow. We document the loss origin carefully because that distinction matters for claim approval.

## Local Note

One thing that catches property owners off guard in Paradise Palms and other mid-century neighborhoods: the original interior walls in many of these homes used a sand-finish plaster over metal lath rather than modern drywall. Plaster itself resists mold better than paper-faced gypsum, but the wood lath strips sometimes used behind it — and the organic debris that accumulates in wall cavities over 50-plus years — can harbor significant mold growth even when the plaster face looks clean. If your home was built before 1975 and you're seeing discoloration at baseboard level or smelling mustiness after any water event, the source may be deeper in the wall assembly than a surface inspection would suggest.
