The Las Vegas Valley's caliche-heavy soil doesn't absorb groundwater the way clay-rich soils do in wetter climates — it deflects it. During the monsoon microbursts that roll through southern Nevada between July and September, that deflection pushes water toward foundations and window wells rather than away from them. ## Our Basement Flood Cleanup Process in Spring Valley

The first step on any flooded lower level is source control — stopping inflow before extraction begins. Once the water source is isolated, the affected space is documented with moisture mapping: thermal imaging and calibrated meters identify what's wet behind walls and under flooring, not just what's visibly saturated at the surface. Water behind stucco or behind the concrete block walls common in older Spring Valley construction can read dry at the surface while the substrate stays saturated for days.

Extraction follows, using truck-mounted and portable units sized to the space. Structural drying then begins with a calculated placement of air movers and refrigerant or desiccant dehumidifiers — the number of machines, their placement, and the target drying goals are driven by the square footage, material types, and ambient conditions specific to the job. In the Las Vegas Valley's low-humidity climate, ambient air can actually assist drying if managed correctly, but that advantage disappears the moment HVAC systems push humid air into the space from a still-wet condensate line.

Moisture readings are logged daily. No materials are cleared or rebuilt until readings return to baseline for the material type — a standard that protects against mold colonization, which can begin in as little as 24 to 48 hours in a warm, enclosed space.
