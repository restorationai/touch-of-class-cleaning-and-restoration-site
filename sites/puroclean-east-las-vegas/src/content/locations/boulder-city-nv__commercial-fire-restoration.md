## Why Boulder City Commercial Properties Face Distinct Fire Damage Challenges

The building stock in Boulder City's Historic District — roughly the blocks surrounding Nevada Way and Arizona Street — was constructed under Depression-era federal standards that predate modern fire-resistive assemblies. Lath-and-plaster interior walls, single-pane windows, and uninsulated roof cavities mean fire and smoke travel unpredictably. A kitchen fire in a restaurant on the main corridor can push smoke into adjacent tenant spaces through gaps that a modern building's fire-rated assemblies would have contained.

Boulder City also sits at roughly 2,500 feet in elevation on the edge of the Mojave, which affects restoration in practical ways. The low relative humidity accelerates surface drying but can lock smoke residue into porous materials before crews have a chance to clean them properly. Soot that looks dry on the surface of an older brick or stucco wall may still be chemically active underneath, continuing to etch and discolor if not treated with the right neutralizing agents. Commercial property owners in Del Prado and along the Highway 93 business corridor should not assume that "dry" means "clean."

## Our Commercial Fire Restoration Process in Boulder City

Every commercial fire loss starts with a structured assessment — not a visual walkthrough, but a systematic inspection that maps smoke migration, identifies odor sources, and documents structural compromise. For Boulder City properties, that assessment pays particular attention to older mechanical systems. Many buildings in the 89005 ZIP code still have ductwork and HVAC configurations that were retrofitted decades after original construction, creating pathways for smoke to reach areas that appear untouched.

Once the scope is established, the work moves in a defined sequence: emergency board-up and tarping to secure the structure, controlled demolition of unsalvageable materials, dry and wet chemical cleaning of smoke-affected surfaces, thermal fogging or hydroxyl treatment for embedded odor, and final reconstruction to pre-loss condition. For occupied multi-tenant buildings — a common situation along Boulder City's compact commercial blocks — containment barriers and phased scheduling help keep unaffected tenants operational during the process.

Documentation runs throughout every phase. Photographs, moisture readings, air quality notes, and material inventories are compiled into a loss file that supports the insurance claim and satisfies any city permitting requirements for structural repairs.

One thing that catches out-of-area contractors working in Boulder City's Historic District: the older masonry on many commercial buildings was laid with lime mortar, not modern Portland cement.
