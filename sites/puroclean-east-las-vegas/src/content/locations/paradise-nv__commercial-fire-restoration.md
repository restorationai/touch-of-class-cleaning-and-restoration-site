## Our Commercial Fire Restoration Process in Paradise

Every commercial fire loss is a layered problem: structural damage, smoke and soot contamination, water damage from suppression systems, and odor that can make a space unusable even after visible debris is removed. The sequence matters.

The first priority after a property is released by the fire marshal is emergency stabilization — boarding openings, tarping roof damage if present, and isolating the HVAC system so it doesn't continue circulating contaminated air. From there, a structured assessment documents affected zones by category: areas with direct char and heat damage, areas with heavy smoke and soot deposition, and areas with secondary water damage from sprinklers or hose lines.

Soot chemistry varies by what burned. Synthetic materials common in commercial interiors — carpet, vinyl flooring, foam seating, cable insulation — produce a wet, smearing soot that bonds aggressively to hard surfaces and requires specific chemical neutralization before it can be wiped without spreading. Protein-based residues from kitchen fires behave differently still, leaving a thin, nearly invisible film that carries intense odor. Identifying what burned informs which cleaning agents and methods apply to each surface.

Odor control in large commercial spaces typically requires thermal fogging or hydroxyl generation rather than surface-only treatments, because smoke penetrates into wall cavities, ductwork, and subfloor assemblies that can't be reached by wiping alone.

## Coordinating with Insurers and Clark County for Commercial Claims

Commercial fire claims in Paradise tend to be more documentation-intensive than residential ones. Carriers want itemized scope-of-loss reports, photo documentation by zone, and moisture or air-quality readings where applicable. Business interruption coverage — a component of many commercial policies — requires clear timelines: when work began, what was completed at each phase, and when the space was cleared for reoccupancy.

We produce the documentation carriers ask for as a standard part of the job, not as an add-on. That includes photo logs, drying or cleaning logs where applicable, and a written scope that aligns with the adjuster's line-item review process. For properties near the Las Vegas Convention Center or Allegiant Stadium — where event-driven revenue loss is real and measurable — having a tight, well-documented timeline can directly support a business interruption claim.

## Local Note: HVAC Smoke Migration in High-Density Paradise Buildings

One pattern that shows up repeatedly in Paradise's denser commercial corridors — particularly in the hotel-adjacent mixed-use buildings and the retail strips near the airport — is smoke migration through shared HVAC infrastructure that wasn't immediately obvious at the time of loss. A fire in a ground-floor restaurant kitchen can deposit detectable soot in third-floor office spaces served by the same air handler. When a building's HVAC system was running at the time of the fire, a thorough scope should include air handler inspection and duct swabbing in zones that appear unaffected.
