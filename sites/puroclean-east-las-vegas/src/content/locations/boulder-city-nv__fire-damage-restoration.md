## Why Boulder City Properties Face Distinct Fire Damage Challenges

The same dry heat that makes the Mojave beautiful creates specific post-fire risks. After a structural fire, exposed framing and subflooring in Boulder City's arid climate can begin to warp and crack within days as interior moisture from firefighting efforts evaporates unevenly. Meanwhile, smoke residue — particularly the oily, protein-based smoke common in kitchen fires — bonds more aggressively to hot surfaces, meaning the high ambient temperatures here can bake soot into porous materials before a restoration crew ever arrives.

Older homes in the Del Prado neighborhood and surrounding streets often have original stucco exteriors and interior plaster walls. Plaster holds smoke particulates deep in its pores, and surface wiping alone will not eliminate odor. Full odor neutralization in these homes typically requires thermal fogging or hydroxyl treatment after the physical cleaning phase — a step that can be skipped in newer construction but rarely should be in pre-1960 Boulder City homes.

The city's position adjacent to Lake Mead National Recreation Area also means that air quality concerns after a fire extend beyond the structure itself. Ash and char residue can contain heavy metals and combustion byproducts, and responsible disposal must follow Clark County hazardous waste guidelines rather than standard construction debris protocols.

## Our Fire Damage Restoration Process in Boulder City

Every fire loss is different, but the sequence of work follows a disciplined path. The first step on-site is a thorough damage assessment — documenting structural integrity, identifying smoke migration paths through HVAC systems and wall cavities, and cataloging salvageable contents before anything is moved. Photographs and written documentation are generated at this stage for both the property owner and the insurance carrier.

Demolition is selective. After structural cleaning, the focus shifts to odor elimination. Smoke penetrates HVAC ductwork, insulation, and subfloor cavities — areas that are invisible but will continue releasing odor for months if untreated. Hydroxyl generators run continuously during this phase, breaking down odor molecules at the molecular level rather than masking them. Final air quality verification confirms the space is ready for reconstruction.
