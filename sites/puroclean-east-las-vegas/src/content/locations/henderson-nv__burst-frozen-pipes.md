## Why Henderson Properties See Burst and Frozen Pipe Issues

The Las Vegas Valley averages only a handful of nights per year that dip below freezing, but that infrequency is part of the problem. Builders in Southern Nevada have historically insulated for heat, not cold — attic plumbing runs that would be buried deep in a Minnesota home are left exposed to ambient temperatures that can plunge into the mid-20s during a December or January cold snap. When that happens in ZIP codes like 89052 or 89012, the calls start coming in fast.

Slab construction adds another layer of complexity. A significant share of Henderson's housing stock — particularly in Seven Hills and MacDonald Ranch — sits on post-tension concrete slabs. A supply line embedded in or running beneath that slab can freeze, crack, and leak for days before a homeowner notices a warm spot on the tile floor or an unexplained spike on their water bill. By the time the damage is visible, moisture has often already migrated into adjacent wall cavities.

Newer appliance connections are another recurring source. The fast-growing neighborhoods near Inspirada and the southern edge of Henderson have seen a surge of smart-home installations — refrigerators with ice makers, tankless water heaters, and whole-home filtration systems — each with flexible supply lines that can fail under freeze-thaw stress or simple age.

## Local Note

One pattern we see in Henderson's post-tension slab neighborhoods: homeowners and even some plumbers will shut off the water, dry the visible surface, and assume the problem is resolved — only to find mold colonies forming inside a wall cavity four to six weeks later.
