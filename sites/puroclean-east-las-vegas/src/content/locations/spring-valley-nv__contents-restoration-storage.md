## Our Contents Restoration & Storage Process in Spring Valley

The process starts with a room-by-room inventory before anything is moved. Every item is photographed, catalogued, and assessed for restorability. This documentation matters for insurance claims and gives you a clear picture of what is being taken offsite and why.

Items that can be restored are packed out and transported to a secure, climate-controlled facility — critical in a city where summer temperatures routinely exceed 110°F and an unventilated storage unit can destroy what water damage started. At the facility, individual items go through the appropriate restoration track: ultrasonic cleaning for hard goods and non-porous surfaces, ozone or hydroxyl treatment for odor-affected soft goods, freeze-drying for water-saturated documents and photographs, and specialized cleaning for electronics.

Items that are restored are held in storage until your home or property is fully dried, repaired, and ready. Nothing is returned to a space that isn't ready to receive it — returning contents to a structure that still has elevated moisture is one of the most common ways secondary mold damage happens.

## Equipment & Methods We Use for Contents Restoration

Ultrasonic cleaning tanks use high-frequency sound waves to remove soot, smoke residue, and contamination from hard surfaces without abrasive scrubbing — effective for items that couldn't survive conventional cleaning. Ozone chambers neutralize smoke and biological odors at the molecular level, which is particularly relevant after fire or sewage losses where odor can outlast visible contamination. For documents, photographs, and books, freeze-drying (vacuum freeze-drying, specifically) halts further water damage and allows controlled drying that preserves paper fiber integrity.

The facility environment is temperature and humidity controlled, which matters in Spring Valley's climate.
