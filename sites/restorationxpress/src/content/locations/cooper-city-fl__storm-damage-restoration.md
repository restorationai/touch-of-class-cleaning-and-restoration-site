## Why Cooper City Properties See Serious Storm Damage

Cooper City's housing stock is heavily concentrated in planned communities built from the late 1970s through the mid-1990s. Neighborhoods like Rock Creek and Embassy Lakes were developed quickly during Broward's growth boom, which means a large share of homes share similar roof construction — original 3-tab shingles or early-generation tile systems that are now 30 to 45 years old. When a squall line or tropical system pushes through, those aging roof assemblies are the first to fail. A single lifted shingle or cracked tile can admit hundreds of gallons of water into an attic before the storm even clears.

Beyond the rooflines, Cooper City's mature tree canopy — one of the things residents love about neighborhoods near Brian Piccolo Park — becomes a liability in high winds. Laurel oaks and ficus trees that line residential streets in the 33026 and 33328 ZIP codes routinely drop limbs onto roofs, pool enclosures, and screened lanais. Impact damage from falling trees is often the entry point for water intrusion that continues long after the tree has been removed.

The city's flat topography also means surface water has nowhere to go quickly. After a heavy storm, standing water pools against foundations, seeps under slab edges, and saturates the sandy fill soil that underlies most of Cooper City's residential lots. That saturated soil holds moisture against concrete block walls and can push water into garages and lower-level living spaces even when there is no visible roof damage.

## Our Storm Damage Restoration Process in Cooper City

Every storm damage job starts with a thorough inspection — not just the obvious breach point, but the full envelope of the structure. We use moisture meters and thermal imaging to map where water has traveled inside wall cavities, under flooring, and into ceiling assemblies, because storm water rarely stops at the point of entry.

Once we understand the full scope, we move to controlled demolition of saturated materials. Wet drywall, soaked insulation, and damaged flooring are removed to clean, dry substrate before drying equipment is placed. Industrial air movers and refrigerant dehumidifiers run in a configuration calibrated to the affected square footage — not a one-size setup. We document moisture readings throughout the drying cycle so there is a clear, dated record of the structure returning to acceptable moisture levels.

For homes with roof damage, we coordinate temporary weatherproofing — tarping or board-up — before interior work begins. There is no point running drying equipment in a structure that is still open to the sky.
