## Why Hollywood Properties See Mold Issues

Hollywood sits at sea level along a coastline where relative humidity rarely dips below 70 percent, even in winter. That baseline moisture load means building materials — drywall, wood framing, ceiling tiles, carpet backing — stay closer to their saturation threshold than in drier climates. When any water intrusion event occurs, mold has very little margin to overcome before it takes hold.

The older beachside bungalows near Hollywood Beach and the streets just inland from the Broadwalk are a particular concern. Many of these homes were built in the 1950s and 1960s with construction practices that assumed natural ventilation rather than sealed, air-conditioned envelopes. When those homes were later retrofitted with central air, the vapor dynamics changed: cooled interior surfaces now create condensation points inside wall cavities, especially on north-facing exterior walls. Crawl spaces and attic decking in these properties frequently show mold growth that has been quietly spreading for years before a homeowner notices a musty odor or a discolored ceiling corner.

Highland Gardens, with its dense concentration of mid-century concrete block homes, presents a different challenge. Concrete block resists structural water damage better than wood-framed construction, but it is porous — it absorbs humidity and releases it slowly, keeping interior surfaces damp long after a visible leak has been repaired. Mold remediation in these homes requires extended drying cycles and careful post-remediation verification.

## Our Mold Remediation Process in Hollywood

Every job starts with a thorough visual inspection and moisture mapping using thermal imaging and calibrated moisture meters. This step matters more in Hollywood than in many markets because the source of moisture is often hidden — a slow slab leak, a failed pan under a second-floor AC air handler, or a compromised roof membrane that only leaks when wind-driven rain hits a specific angle. Treating visible mold without finding and correcting the moisture source guarantees a return call.

Once the source is identified and stopped, the affected area is isolated with physical containment barriers and placed under negative air pressure using HEPA-filtered air scrubbers. This prevents spores disturbed during remediation from migrating to unaffected rooms. Contaminated porous materials — drywall, insulation, subflooring — are removed, bagged, and disposed of per Florida Department of Health guidelines. Non-porous surfaces are cleaned with EPA-registered antimicrobial agents, then dried to documented moisture levels before any reconstruction begins.

Final clearance is verified through post-remediation visual inspection and, when warranted by the scope of the job or a homeowner's request, third-party air sampling by an independent industrial hygienist. That independent verification matters for insurance documentation and for homeowners who want objective confirmation before closing walls back up.
