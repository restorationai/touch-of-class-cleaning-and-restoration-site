## Why Hollywood Properties See Flood Damage Differently

The housing stock along Hollywood Beach and through Hollywood Lakes tells a specific story. Many of these homes were built in the 1950s and 1960s, when slab-on-grade construction was standard and vapor barriers were minimal or absent. When floodwater intrudes — whether from a tropical system, a failed water heater, or a backed-up municipal line — it doesn't just sit on tile floors. It travels laterally under the slab, saturates the fill material beneath, and migrates up through grout lines and baseboards long after the visible water is gone.

Coastal humidity compounds the problem. ## Our Flood Damage Restoration Process in Hollywood

Every job starts with a moisture mapping inspection — thermal imaging and calibrated moisture meters on walls, floors, and ceilings — before a single piece of equipment is placed. This step matters more in Hollywood than in many markets because the combination of concrete block construction, terrazzo, and coastal humidity means moisture readings can be misleading if you only check surface materials.

Once the scope is documented, standing water is extracted using truck-mounted and portable units, and affected materials are assessed for salvageability. In older Hollywood homes, this often means evaluating original wood cabinetry, plaster-over-block walls, and built-in features that a homeowner reasonably wants to preserve. Where materials can be dried in place, we establish a drying system — commercial-grade dehumidifiers, air movers, and where necessary, injectidry panels for wall cavities — and monitor daily with logged readings until the structure reaches its target moisture content.

Demolition, when required, is scoped to what the moisture data supports, not a blanket tearout. That documentation also becomes the foundation of your insurance claim file.
