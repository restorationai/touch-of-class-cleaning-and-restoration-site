## Why Plantation Properties See Smoke Damage Issues

Plantation's housing stock spans several decades and construction styles, and that variety shapes how smoke behaves after a fire. Homes built in the 1970s and 1980s — a large share of the inventory in ZIP codes 33317 and 33322 — often have textured popcorn ceilings and older HVAC ductwork that trap fine soot particles efficiently. Once smoke enters a return air duct, it can distribute residue to every room in the house within minutes, turning a contained kitchen fire into a whole-home odor problem.

## Our Smoke Damage Restoration Process in Plantation

Every job starts with a room-by-room assessment that maps soot type — dry smoke, wet smoke, or protein residue — because each requires a different cleaning chemistry. Dry smoke from fast-burning fires leaves a powdery residue that brushes off surfaces but embeds in grout and fabric. Wet smoke from slow, smoldering fires leaves a sticky, pungent film that standard household cleaners can't cut. Protein residue from kitchen fires is nearly invisible but produces an intense odor that intensifies when surfaces are heated — a common complaint from Plantation homeowners who think the smell is gone until they run the oven again.

After assessment, the process moves through containment, HEPA vacuuming of loose particulate, chemical sponge wiping of hard surfaces, and thermal fogging or hydroxyl generation for odor neutralization. In Plantation's climate, hydroxyl generators are often preferable to ozone treatments because they can run safely while occupants are present and don't require the extended evacuation periods that ozone demands — a practical consideration when families are already displaced.

Content packing and off-site cleaning is available for furniture, artwork, and clothing affected by smoke residue. Items from homes near Volunteer Park or the Westfield Broward Mall corridor are brought to our Davie facility for ultrasonic cleaning and deodorization before being returned.
