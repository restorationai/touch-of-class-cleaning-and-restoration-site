## Why Plantation Properties Face Particular Fire Damage Challenges

Plantation's housing stock tells a specific story. Many homes in the Jacaranda and Lauderdale West communities were built between the late 1960s and early 1980s, a period when aluminum wiring was common in South Florida residential construction. That wiring ages poorly — connections loosen, arcing occurs, and electrical fires in these homes can smolder inside walls for hours before breaking through. Unlike a kitchen grease fire that's contained to one room, an electrical fire in a 1975 ranch-style home can distribute smoke and soot through the entire HVAC system before anyone smells it.

Plantation's subtropical climate adds another layer. High ambient humidity — regularly above 75% even in the dry season — means that soot particles absorb moisture quickly after a fire. Wet soot is acidic. Left on metal fixtures, appliances, or tile grout for more than 48 to 72 hours, it begins etching surfaces that might otherwise have been salvageable. The window for saving contents and finishes is genuinely shorter here than it would be in a drier climate.

## Our Fire Damage Restoration Process in Plantation

The first step on any fire job is a room-by-room soot and smoke assessment — not a quick walkthrough, but a methodical mapping of char zones, smoke migration paths, and odor penetration depth. In Plantation homes with central air, smoke frequently travels through return vents and coats the interior of ductwork. We document all of it before a single item is moved, because insurance documentation starts at the scene.

From there, the process moves through controlled demolition of unsalvageable materials, dry and wet soot cleaning calibrated to surface type (flat paint versus semi-gloss, drywall versus concrete block), and hydroxyl or thermal fogging for odor neutralization. Structural drying runs concurrently if firefighting water is present — which it almost always is. We coordinate with your adjuster throughout, providing line-item documentation that matches the scope they need to process your claim.
