## Why Davie Properties See Mold Issues

Davie's geography puts it in a tricky spot. The western neighborhoods — the ranch and equestrian corridors that stretch out toward Robbins Preserve — sit on land that was historically wetland and pasture. Soil drainage is slower here than in coastal Broward communities, and after a heavy rain, moisture wicks upward through slab foundations and into the lower courses of drywall. Homes in Pine Island Ridge and Rolling Hills, many built in the 1970s and 1980s, frequently have original fiberglass batt insulation inside exterior walls — a material that holds moisture without showing it, giving mold exactly the dark, damp, food-rich environment it needs.

The town's subtropical climate compounds the problem year-round. Unlike northern states where mold season is roughly tied to spring thaw, Davie properties in ZIP codes like 33325 and 33328 can develop active mold growth in January just as easily as August. Air conditioning systems that run almost continuously also create condensation points along supply ducts and in poorly ventilated attic spaces — a pattern we see repeatedly in the neighborhoods surrounding Nova Southeastern University, where older rental stock and converted single-family homes often have aging HVAC equipment.

## Our Mold Remediation Process in Davie

Every remediation starts with a thorough assessment — not a visual scan, but a moisture mapping of the affected area using thermal imaging and calibrated moisture meters. Mold in Davie homes almost always has a moisture source that hasn't been fully addressed, and treating the visible growth without finding that source guarantees a return visit.

Once the scope is confirmed, we establish containment. Negative air pressure chambers using HEPA-filtered air scrubbers isolate the work zone so that spores disturbed during removal don't migrate to clean areas of the home. Affected drywall, insulation, and porous materials are removed, bagged, and disposed of per Florida Department of Health guidelines. Structural framing and hard surfaces are treated with EPA-registered antimicrobial agents. Drying equipment — commercial-grade dehumidifiers and air movers — then runs until moisture readings return to acceptable baselines for Davie's ambient humidity levels, which are higher than the national standard tables assume.

For homes with finished ceilings or tile floors over concrete, we use non-invasive moisture probes before cutting into any surface — a step that saves significant reconstruction cost in cases where the spread is more limited than the surface staining suggests.
