## Why Pembroke Pines Properties See Water Damage Issues

The city's rapid suburban build-out — much of it completed in the 1980s and 1990s — left thousands of homes with aging polybutylene or CPVC supply lines that are now well past their expected service life. Beyond aging plumbing, Pembroke Pines experiences some of the most intense convective rainfall in South Florida. ## Our Water Damage Restoration Process in Pembroke Pines

When we arrive at a Pembroke Pines property, the first step is moisture mapping — not just the visible wet area, but the full extent of saturation using thermal imaging and calibrated moisture meters. CBS construction holds water differently than wood-frame: the block itself can wick moisture laterally, meaning a wet wall in your hallway may trace back to a soaked exterior block course twenty feet away.

Once the scope is documented, water extraction begins with truck-mounted or portable extraction units sized to the loss. For slab-on-grade homes — the dominant construction type in communities like Grand Palms — we pay close attention to the gap between the slab and baseplate, where water migrates and hides. Structural drying follows using commercial-grade desiccant or refrigerant dehumidifiers and high-velocity air movers positioned to create a controlled drying chamber. Drying logs are recorded daily, which matters both for insurance documentation and for confirming the structure has reached acceptable moisture levels before any reconstruction begins.

In Pembroke Pines' older CBS neighborhoods — particularly those built in the late 1980s along the eastern corridors — original tile adhesive was often applied directly to the slab with a thick mortar bed.
