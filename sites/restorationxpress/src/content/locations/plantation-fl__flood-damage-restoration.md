## Our Flood Damage Restoration Process in Plantation

Every flood job starts with a moisture mapping inspection — not a visual walk-through, but a systematic reading of wall cavities, subfloor assemblies, and ceiling planes using thermal imaging and calibrated moisture meters. In Plantation's older concrete-block construction, moisture hides behind tile and stucco longer than it does in wood-frame homes, so the inspection phase isn't a formality; it determines the full scope of extraction and drying needed.

After mapping, standing water is removed with truck-mounted or portable extraction units, followed by the placement of commercial-grade desiccant dehumidifiers and high-velocity air movers. Drying timelines vary — a single-story slab home in Lauderdale West with contained water in one room may dry in three to four days, while a two-story home in Plantation Isles with water in multiple cavities can take seven to ten days to reach acceptable moisture levels. We monitor readings daily and adjust equipment placement based on the numbers, not a fixed schedule.

Where building materials cannot be dried in place — saturated drywall, swollen baseboards, buckled laminate — controlled demolition removes only what cannot be saved. The goal is always to preserve as much original material as possible while eliminating anything that would trap moisture and create conditions for secondary damage.
