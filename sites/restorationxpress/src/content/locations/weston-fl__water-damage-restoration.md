## Why Weston Properties Are Particularly Vulnerable to Water Damage

Weston sits on land that was drained Everglades marsh, which means the water table across ZIP codes like 33326 and 33331 sits unusually close to the surface. After a sustained rain event, that saturated soil has nowhere to push excess moisture except laterally — toward foundations, slab edges, and the weep screeds on stucco exteriors. Homes in communities like The Ridges and Savanna were built to strict South Florida building codes, but even code-compliant construction isn't immune to the cumulative stress of 25-plus years of wet-dry cycles, hurricane seasons, and the relentless humidity that keeps relative indoor moisture elevated year-round.

Beyond storm intrusion, the most common sources of water damage in Weston are internal: supply line failures behind refrigerators and washing machines, slow leaks at water heater connections, and — especially in two-story homes — second-floor bathroom supply lines that fail while the family is away. In a sealed, air-conditioned house, a slow leak behind a vanity can run undetected for weeks, quietly feeding mold colonization that typically begins within 24 to 48 hours of initial saturation.

## Our Water Damage Restoration Process in Weston

The first step on any job is moisture mapping — using thermal imaging cameras and calibrated moisture meters to trace exactly how far water has traveled beyond the visible wet area. In Weston's newer construction, water often wicks further than expected through the dense concrete block walls common in this region. We document every affected material and reading before a single piece of equipment is placed, which matters both for accurate drying and for the insurance documentation your adjuster will need.

Once the scope is defined, standing water is extracted using truck-mounted and portable extraction units. Structural drying follows with industrial-grade desiccant or refrigerant dehumidifiers and high-velocity air movers positioned to create directed airflow through wall cavities and under flooring. Drying timelines in Weston's climate — where outdoor dew points regularly exceed 70°F — run longer than in drier regions; we monitor psychrometric readings daily and adjust equipment placement until materials reach the dry standard required by the IICRC S500 protocol. We don't pull equipment based on a calendar; we pull it based on the numbers.
