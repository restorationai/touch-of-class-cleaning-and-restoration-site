## Why Weston Properties Are Vulnerable to Flood Damage

Weston was developed largely on drained wetland, and the soil beneath its subdivisions retains moisture in ways that older, elevated coastal cities do not. During heavy rain events — common from June through October — the area's canal and retention pond system can reach capacity quickly. When that happens, water migrates toward foundations rather than away from them. Homes in gated communities like The Ridges and Country Isles often feature slab-on-grade construction with minimal elevation above grade, which leaves very little buffer between the exterior water table and interior flooring systems.

The building stock throughout Weston's ZIP codes 33326 and 33331 skews toward 1990s and early 2000s construction, which means homes frequently have OSB subfloor panels, moisture-sensitive cabinetry, and spray-textured drywall — all materials that absorb standing water rapidly and begin supporting mold colonization within 24 to 72 hours if not extracted and dried properly. Waiting even a day to begin mitigation is rarely a neutral choice.

## Our Flood Damage Restoration Process in Weston

When we arrive at a Weston property, the first priority is a thorough moisture assessment — not just the visible wet areas, but the wall cavities, subfloor voids, and any space beneath built-in cabinetry where water migrates invisibly. We use thermal imaging and calibrated moisture meters to map the full extent of saturation before any equipment is placed.

Extraction comes next. For tile-over-slab floors common in Weston living areas, truck-mounted extraction pulls standing water efficiently. For rooms with engineered hardwood or laminate — which trap water in the click-lock seams — we assess whether drying in place is viable or whether controlled demolition of flooring is the faster, safer path. Drying is performed with commercial-grade desiccant dehumidifiers and high-velocity air movers positioned to create the airflow patterns the IICRC S500 standard calls for, with daily moisture readings to track progress and adjust equipment placement.

Once structural materials reach dry standard, we document everything — photographs, moisture logs, equipment placement records — before any reconstruction begins. That documentation matters both for insurance claims and for HOA compliance sign-off.
