## Why Weston Properties See Concentrated Storm Damage

Weston's housing stock is almost entirely built on a master-planned grid developed through the 1990s and 2000s, which means the construction details are consistent — and so are the failure points. Concrete block exteriors hold up well to wind, but the transitions between rooflines, second-story additions, and screened enclosures are where water finds its way in. Flat or low-slope roof sections on pool decks and covered lanais are especially vulnerable: a 3-inch-per-hour rainfall event — common during Atlantic hurricane season — overwhelms scuppers and forces water backward under flashing.

The region's shallow, sandy soil also matters. Weston is built on filled wetlands, and the water table sits close to the surface across much of the city. After a major storm, groundwater infiltration through slab seams and garage floors can add several inches of standing water to what fell from the sky. Homeowners who pump out a flooded garage and assume the job is done often find moisture readings still spiking two days later because the slab itself is wicking from below.

## Our Storm Damage Restoration Process in Weston

Every job starts with a documented scope — photographs, moisture mapping with calibrated meters, and a written assessment that your insurance adjuster and HOA architectural review board can both use. In Weston's gated communities, that documentation step isn't optional; many HOA covenants require pre-approval for exterior repairs, and having a clear damage report in hand before the first board meeting saves weeks.

From there, the process follows a defined sequence:

- **Emergency board-up and tarping** — exposed roof decks and broken windows are sealed to stop additional water intrusion before structural drying begins.
- **Water extraction and structural drying** — commercial desiccant dehumidifiers and high-velocity air movers run continuously, with daily moisture readings to confirm the drying curve is tracking correctly per IICRC S500 standards.
- **Debris removal and tree damage cleanup** — downed trees and wind-driven debris are cleared and hauled, including root-ball extraction where root heave has cracked driveways or pool decks.
- **Reconstruction** — framing, drywall, stucco patching, roofing, and finish work completed to Florida Building Code, with permits pulled where required by Broward County.

For larger losses in communities like The Ridges or Savanna, we coordinate directly with the HOA management company so exterior color matches, material specs, and contractor access schedules are handled before work begins — not after a violation notice arrives.

## Weston Insurance & HOA Coordination

Broward County homeowners' insurance claims after a named hurricane often move slowly because every adjuster in South Florida is working the same event simultaneously. We photograph and document affected materials in detail — roofing, insulation, drywall, flooring, cabinetry — so your claim file is complete before the adjuster's first visit. That documentation typically shortens the back-and-forth and reduces the chance of a partial denial based on insufficient evidence.

For HOA communities, we're familiar with the distinction between what the association's master policy covers (common elements, exterior envelope) and what your individual HO-6 policy covers (interior finishes, personal property). Getting that line drawn correctly at the start prevents gaps in coverage and duplicate repair scopes.
