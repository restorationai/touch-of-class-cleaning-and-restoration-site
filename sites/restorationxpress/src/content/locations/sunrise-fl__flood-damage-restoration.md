## Why Sunrise Properties See Flood Damage Issues

Sunrise's geography does most of the explaining. The city's western ZIP codes — 33323, 33325, and 33326 — border the conservation areas feeding into the Everglades, and the ground here drains poorly even under normal conditions. During the June-through-October wet season, a single afternoon storm can saturate the soil so completely that water has no choice but to pool against foundations and find its way inside.

The housing stock adds another layer of complexity. Many homes in Sunrise Golf Village and the Sunset Strip corridor were built in the 1970s and early 1980s, when slab construction was standard and vapor barriers were minimal by today's codes. Those slabs can wick moisture upward for days after a flood event, keeping relative humidity elevated long after visible water is removed. Carpet and vinyl flooring installed directly over concrete — common in that era — trap that moisture underneath, creating conditions where microbial growth can begin within 48 hours if drying isn't actively managed.

## Our Flood Damage Restoration Process in Sunrise

Every flood job starts with a moisture mapping inspection — not a visual walkthrough, but a systematic reading with thermal imaging cameras and calibrated moisture meters across every affected surface. In Sunrise homes, we pay particular attention to the slab interface and any interior walls that share a plane with the exterior, because those are the areas that stay wet longest given the local climate.

Once we have a complete moisture picture, we extract standing water using truck-mounted and portable units, then establish a drying system calibrated to the actual square footage and material types in the home. Dehumidifiers are sized to the vapor load, not just the room count. We set drying goals based on the IICRC S500 standard and return daily to log readings and adjust equipment placement. Documentation is continuous — moisture logs, photos, and drying reports — because your insurance adjuster will want that record.

For homes where flooring or drywall has absorbed water past the point of in-place drying, we perform targeted demolition: removing only what cannot be dried, preserving what can. This matters both for cost containment and for Broward County permitting, which requires documentation when structural materials are removed.
