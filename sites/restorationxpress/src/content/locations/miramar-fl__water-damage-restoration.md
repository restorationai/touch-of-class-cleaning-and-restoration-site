## Why Miramar Properties Face Distinct Water Damage Risks

Miramar's geography creates a split personality when it comes to water intrusion. The newer master-planned communities west of I-75 — Sunset Lakes, Vizcaya, and the developments pushing toward the Everglades buffer — were built on fill soil that drains poorly and can shift under slab foundations during heavy saturation events. A slow slab leak in these homes often goes undetected for weeks because the finished flooring shows no visible staining until the subfloor is already compromised.

On the eastern side, older concrete block homes built in the 1960s and 1970s near ZIP code 33023 present a different challenge. Many have original cast-iron or galvanized supply lines that are well past their service life, and a sudden pipe burst in one of these houses can push hundreds of gallons through terrazzo floors and into interior wall cavities before a homeowner realizes the water heater closet is flooding. Terrazzo is dense and non-porous on the surface, but the sand-and-cement base beneath it acts like a sponge — water migrates laterally under the slab rather than pooling visibly, which means the wet zone is almost always larger than it looks.

Broward County's building code also requires documentation of moisture readings before any structural repairs can proceed on permitted work, so skipping a professional drying phase isn't just a health risk — it can stall a reconstruction permit.

## Our Water Damage Restoration Process in Miramar

When we arrive at a Miramar property, the first step is a full moisture mapping of the affected area using thermal imaging and calibrated moisture meters. Water doesn't travel in straight lines — it follows framing cavities, HVAC chases, and the path of least resistance through flooring assemblies. We document every reading before we move a single piece of equipment.

Water extraction comes next. For standing water, we use truck-mounted extractors capable of removing hundreds of gallons quickly. For water that has wicked into carpet padding, engineered wood, or the terrazzo base common in eastern Miramar homes, we follow with targeted low-grain refrigerant dehumidifiers and high-velocity air movers positioned to create a drying vortex across the wet materials. Drying typically takes three to five days depending on material density and ambient humidity — we check readings daily and adjust equipment placement rather than setting it and disappearing.

Once materials reach target moisture levels, we provide a written drying log. That documentation matters for your insurance adjuster and, if reconstruction requires a permit, for the county inspector.
