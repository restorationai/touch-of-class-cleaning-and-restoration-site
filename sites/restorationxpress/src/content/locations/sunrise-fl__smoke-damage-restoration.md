## Our Smoke Damage Restoration Process in Sunrise

Every job begins with a structured scope assessment — not a quick walkthrough, but a room-by-room evaluation that documents smoke migration patterns, identifies affected materials, and photographs all damage before anything is touched. That documentation matters for insurance claims and for making sure no secondary damage is missed.

From there, the process follows a defined sequence:

- **Content pack-out**: Salvageable contents — furniture, clothing, electronics — are inventoried and moved to a clean staging area or off-site facility for individual cleaning and deodorization.
- **Dry residue removal**: Loose soot and smoke particles are removed with dry chemical sponges before any wet cleaning begins. Applying water or cleaning solution to dry soot first smears it deeper into porous surfaces.
- **Wet cleaning and deodorization**: Walls, ceilings, and hard surfaces are cleaned with appropriate agents matched to the residue type — protein residue from kitchen fires behaves differently than the heavy black soot from a structural fire.
- **HVAC and duct cleaning**: Given how aggressively South Florida homes recirculate air, duct cleaning is rarely optional after a fire. Smoke-contaminated ductwork will re-deposit odor into a freshly cleaned home every time the system runs.
- **Thermal fogging or hydroxyl treatment**: Odor molecules that have penetrated porous materials require more than surface cleaning. Thermal fogging or hydroxyl generators reach into wall cavities, subflooring gaps, and furniture fabric where surface cleaners cannot.
- **Final air quality check**: Before the job closes, the space is assessed to confirm odor levels are within acceptable range.
