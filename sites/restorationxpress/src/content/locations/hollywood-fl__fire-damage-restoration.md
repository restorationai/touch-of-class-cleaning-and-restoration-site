When a kitchen fire tears through a Hollywood Lakes bungalow or smoke from an electrical fault saturates the walls of a Emerald Hills ranch home, the clock starts immediately — not just on the visible char, but on the secondary damage that follows. South Florida's coastal humidity accelerates the corrosion of metal fixtures and the absorption of smoke odor into porous surfaces faster than in drier climates, which means fire damage in Hollywood, FL demands a response calibrated to this environment, not a generic playbook.

## Why Hollywood Properties Are Especially Vulnerable After a Fire

Hollywood's housing stock tells a specific story. A significant portion of the city's residential neighborhoods — particularly the older blocks near Hollywood Beach and the streets fanning out from ArtsPark at Young Circle — were built between the 1950s and 1970s. Those homes often feature original wood-frame construction, jalousie windows, and plaster-over-lath walls. After a fire, plaster absorbs smoke particulates deeply and releases them slowly, which means odor can resurface weeks after visible soot is cleaned if the remediation process doesn't account for it.

The coastal air compounds the problem. Salt-laden humidity causes metal components — HVAC ductwork, electrical conduit, door hardware — to begin oxidizing within 24 to 48 hours of fire exposure. Smoke residue mixed with that moisture creates an acidic film that etches glass, pits chrome, and discolors grout. In ZIP code 33019 and neighboring beachside addresses, we factor marine-environment corrosion into our damage assessment from the first walkthrough.

## Our Fire Damage Restoration Process in Hollywood

The first step on any Hollywood fire job is a structured scope assessment — not a quick visual scan, but a systematic evaluation of every affected material: char depth on framing, smoke penetration into insulation, HVAC contamination, and moisture readings, because water from suppression efforts introduces a mold risk that's amplified by Florida's baseline humidity.

From there, the process moves through these concrete phases:

- **Board-up and structural stabilization** — open walls, compromised rooflines, and broken windows are secured before any cleaning begins.
- **Dry-out** — suppression water is extracted and affected cavities are dried using commercial dehumidifiers and air movers before smoke work starts. Skipping this step in South Florida's climate invites mold colonization within 48 to 72 hours.
- **Soot and smoke residue removal** — different residue types (wet smoke, dry smoke, protein-based smoke from kitchen fires) require different cleaning agents and techniques. Protein smoke — common in cooking fires — is nearly invisible but leaves a powerful odor and a greasy film on every surface in the room.
- **Odor neutralization** — thermal fogging and hydroxyl generation reach into wall cavities and ductwork where surface wiping cannot.
- **Structural repair and reconstruction** — drywall, flooring, cabinetry, and trim are restored to pre-loss condition.
