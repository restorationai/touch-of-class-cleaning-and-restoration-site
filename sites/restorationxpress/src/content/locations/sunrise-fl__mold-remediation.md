## Why Sunrise Properties See Mold Issues

Sunrise's geography creates conditions that accelerate mold growth in ways that don't apply to drier parts of South Florida. The city's western neighborhoods — particularly those near the Sawgrass International Corporate Park corridor and the communities bordering Markham Park — sit on low-lying land with high water tables. After a heavy rain event, groundwater can rise close enough to a slab foundation to push humidity into crawl spaces and first-floor drywall without any visible flooding at all.

The housing stock compounds the problem. A large share of Sunrise's residential neighborhoods were built in the 1970s and 1980s, when vapor barriers were minimal and HVAC systems were oversized by today's standards. Oversized AC units short-cycle — they cool the air quickly but don't run long enough to pull humidity out. The result is indoor relative humidity that routinely climbs above 60%, the threshold at which common mold species like *Cladosporium* and *Stachybotrys* (black mold) can establish colonies within two to three days on paper-faced drywall, ceiling tile, and wood framing.

## Our Mold Remediation Process in Sunrise

Every job starts with a visual inspection and moisture mapping. Technicians use thermal imaging cameras and pin-type moisture meters to find wet material that isn't visible from the surface — behind baseboards, inside wall cavities, above drop ceilings. That data shapes the containment plan before a single panel is removed.

Containment is built with 6-mil poly sheeting and negative air pressure machines exhausting through HEPA filtration to the exterior. This keeps disturbed spores from migrating into unaffected rooms — a step that matters especially in the open floor plans common in Sunrise Golf Village townhomes and similar attached-unit developments, where a kitchen wall cavity can connect directly to a shared air handler closet.

Demolition of unsalvageable material — saturated drywall, compromised insulation, affected wood framing — is followed by HEPA vacuuming of all exposed surfaces, then treatment with an EPA-registered antimicrobial agent. Structural drying runs until moisture readings return to baseline for the specific material type. The job isn't closed until clearance criteria are met, which in practice means a final moisture check and a visual confirmation that no visible growth remains.
