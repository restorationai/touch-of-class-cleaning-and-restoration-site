## Why Hollywood Properties Face a Harder Smoke Damage Problem

Hollywood's housing stock tells a story in its walls. Many neighborhoods — particularly those closest to the coast and the Intracoastal Waterway — contain homes built in the 1950s through 1970s, when builders used plaster-over-lath wall systems, jalousie windows, and terrazzo floors. Plaster is highly porous and absorbs smoke particles at a microscopic level that drywall simply doesn't match. Terrazzo, while durable, traps soot in its aggregate surface if not treated with the right alkaline cleaning agents quickly.

Beyond the building materials, Hollywood's subtropical humidity — routinely above 80% from May through October — accelerates the chemical reaction between smoke residue and moisture in the air. That reaction produces the persistent, acrid smell that lingers for months if surfaces aren't properly neutralized. Homes in the 33019 and 33020 ZIP codes, where beachside bungalows sit just blocks from the Hollywood Beach Broadwalk, are especially susceptible because salt air compounds the corrosive effect of smoke on metals, fabrics, and finishes.

## Our Smoke Damage Restoration Process in Hollywood

Smoke damage restoration isn't a single step — it's a sequenced process, and the sequence matters. Skipping ahead to repainting before neutralizing odor-causing smoke particles is one of the most common mistakes homeowners make after a fire, and it results in odor bleeding back through fresh paint within weeks.

Here's how the work unfolds on a typical Hollywood property:

**1. Assessment and air quality documentation.** We walk the structure room by room, mapping the smoke migration path. Smoke travels through HVAC systems, wall cavities, and under doors — areas that look unaffected often aren't.

**2. Content inventory and pack-out.** Salvageable belongings — furniture, clothing, documents — are catalogued and removed for off-site cleaning when the structure itself needs intensive treatment.

**3. Dry soot removal.** Using HEPA-filtered vacuums and dry chemical sponges, we lift loose soot from ceilings, walls, and surfaces before any wet cleaning begins. Wetting soot before dry-removing it drives particles deeper into porous materials.

**4. Chemical neutralization.** We apply alkaline cleaning solutions calibrated to the surface type — different chemistry for plaster versus drywall versus terrazzo versus wood cabinetry. This step breaks the bond between smoke acids and the substrate.

**5. Thermal fogging and hydroxyl treatment.** For persistent odor in older Hollywood homes with plaster walls and original wood framing, thermal fogging or hydroxyl generator treatment reaches into cavities and porous materials that surface cleaning can't access.

**6. HVAC decontamination.** Smoke particles circulate through ductwork and coat coil surfaces. We clean and treat the HVAC system before it's run again — running contaminated air through a home after restoration undoes the work.
