## Our Smoke Damage Restoration Process in Miramar

The first thing the crew does on arrival is a room-by-room scope — not just the fire room, but every space the HVAC system touched. Duct registers get sealed to prevent cross-contamination before cleaning begins. From there, the process moves in a deliberate sequence:

**1. Residue typing.** Not all smoke is the same. Protein smoke from a kitchen fire (grease, food) leaves an almost invisible but intensely odorous film. Synthetic smoke from burning plastics or furniture foam leaves a thick, oily black residue that smears if treated with the wrong chemistry. Wet smoke from a slow, smoldering fire produces the stickiest deposits. Identifying the residue type on-site determines which cleaning agents and methods go on which surfaces.

**2. Dry cleaning first.** Loose carbon particles are removed with dry chemical sponges before any wet cleaning begins. Applying water or solvent to unsettled soot drives it deeper into porous materials.

**3. Surface-specific wet cleaning.** Walls, ceilings, cabinets, and hard surfaces are cleaned with pH-matched solutions — alkaline for protein residues, solvent-based for synthetic. Miramar's textured popcorn and knockdown ceilings require low-pressure application to avoid surface damage.

**4. Odor neutralization.** Thermal fogging or hydroxyl generation reaches the same microscopic pathways smoke traveled. This step is not cosmetic — it chemically alters odor-causing molecules rather than masking them.

**5. HVAC and duct cleaning.** Given how smoke migrates through ductwork in South Florida's always-running AC systems, this step is non-negotiable on any whole-structure loss.
