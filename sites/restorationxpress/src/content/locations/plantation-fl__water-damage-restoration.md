## Why Plantation Properties See Water Damage Issues

Plantation's housing stock spans several decades and several very different construction eras, and that variety shapes how water damage plays out here. Homes built in the 1960s and 1970s — common throughout Jacaranda and Lauderdale West — often have original cast-iron or galvanized steel supply lines that corrode from the inside out. A slow pinhole leak behind a wall can saturate insulation and subfloor for weeks before a homeowner notices a soft spot or a musty odor.

Out in Plantation Acres, the picture is different. The large lots and mature canopy trees that make the neighborhood distinctive also mean more storm-related roof damage — a falling oak limb or wind-lifted shingle can open a roof deck to a passing squall within seconds. Because the lots are deep and drainage is handled by private swales rather than curb-and-gutter systems, standing water can pond against a foundation for hours after a storm, finding every crack and cold joint in a slab.

Broward County's flat topography compounds all of this. There is almost no natural grade to carry water away from a structure, so once water enters a home — through a failed sliding glass door seal, a backed-up AC condensate line, or an overflowing washing machine — it spreads horizontally across the slab and under baseboards faster than in hillier regions.

## Our Water Damage Restoration Process in Plantation

The first thing a RestorationXpress technician does on arrival is map the moisture — not just the visible wet area, but the full extent of saturation using thermal imaging and calibrated moisture meters. In Plantation's older concrete-block construction, water migrates inside hollow-core block walls and shows up in rooms adjacent to the original source. Missing that secondary saturation is one of the most common reasons a water job fails and mold appears weeks later.

Once the scope is documented, water extraction begins with truck-mounted or portable extraction units depending on access. Standing water comes out first, followed by deep extraction from carpet and pad. Structural drying then uses a calculated combination of commercial-grade desiccant or refrigerant dehumidifiers and high-velocity air movers positioned to create a drying chamber around wet materials. Readings are logged daily — typically over three to five days — until affected assemblies reach target moisture content for the local climate.

If affected materials include drywall, insulation, or flooring that cannot be dried in place, controlled demolition removes only what is necessary. In South Florida's humid environment, leaving partially saturated material behind to "dry on its own" is not a viable option.

## Local Note

One thing that catches homeowners off guard in Plantation Acres specifically: the mature ficus and strangler fig trees common on those large lots have aggressive root systems that can infiltrate older clay sewer laterals. A sewer backup that looks like a plumbing failure is sometimes a root intrusion problem, and the water involved is Category 3 — contaminated — which changes the remediation protocol entirely. Porous materials that might be dried in place after a clean-water pipe burst must be removed and discarded after a sewage event. If your backup happened near a large tree and the water had any odor or discoloration, let the technician know immediately so they can classify the loss correctly from the start.
