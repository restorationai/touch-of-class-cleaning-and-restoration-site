## Restoration emergencies common in Hollywood

Hollywood sits in one of the most moisture-aggressive environments in South Florida. The city's water table is shallow — sometimes just a few feet below grade — which means a slow leak under a slab or a backed-up drain can saturate a concrete subfloor before a homeowner notices anything wrong. Seasonal storms push through Broward County with enough intensity to overwhelm older drainage systems, and coastal neighborhoods like Hollywood Beach face a compounding problem: salt-laden humidity accelerates mold colonization on drywall and wood framing, often within 24 to 48 hours of a moisture event.

The housing stock adds another layer of complexity. Many homes in Highland Gardens and the streets surrounding ArtsPark at Young Circle were built in the 1950s through 1970s, when building codes didn't require the vapor barriers and hurricane-rated windows that newer construction demands. That era of construction is more vulnerable to wind-driven rain intrusion and more likely to contain materials — older drywall compounds, certain adhesives — that complicate remediation work. Emerald Hills, with its mix of mid-century ranch homes and newer infill construction, sees a similar pattern: older structures absorbing moisture in ways that newer builds simply don't.

## Building stock, site conditions, and permits in Hollywood

Most Hollywood houses from the postwar decades are concrete block with stucco on slab-on-grade — no basements, no crawlspaces. That matters because water in a slab home travels sideways through the slab and up into base plates, terrazzo, and block walls rather than draining away. Sandy soil over limestone drains quickly, but with the water table close to grade the ground stays wet, so a saturated slab gives up moisture slowly.

Plumbing vintage drives a lot of the losses: cast iron drain lines in mid-century homes corrode and belly from the inside, and galvanized supply lines fail at the threads. Pre-1978 homes can carry lead paint, and old floor tile and mastic may contain asbestos, which changes the demolition plan.

Structural repairs are permitted through the City of Hollywood's building department, under the Florida Building Code with Broward's high-velocity hurricane zone provisions. Flood-zone properties also face substantial-improvement limits.
