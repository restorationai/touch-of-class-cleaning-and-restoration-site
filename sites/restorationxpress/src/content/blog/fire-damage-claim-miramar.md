Policies cover sudden fire loss but routinely underpay when documentation is weak. The steps below walk you through the process from the first call to final settlement.

## What does homeowners insurance actually cover after a fire?

Most standard homeowners policies in Florida cover the structure, attached structures, and personal property damaged by fire, including smoke and water damage caused by firefighting efforts. Florida policies written under HO-3 or HO-5 forms also typically include Additional Living Expenses (ALE), which pays for a hotel or rental while your home is uninhabitable. What policies do NOT cover: fires caused by arson by the homeowner, vacant-property fires (if the home sat empty beyond the policy's vacancy clause, usually 30-60 days), and damage from deferred maintenance that contributed to the fire spreading.

Smoke damage is covered under the same fire peril, which matters because smoke often travels far beyond the burn area. Soot on walls in rooms that never caught flame, odor embedded in HVAC ductwork, and discoloration on contents in adjacent rooms are all legitimate line items on a fire claim. Document all of it.

## What should you do in the first 48 hours after a fire in Miramar?

The first 48 hours set the foundation for your entire claim. Move through these steps in order.

**1. Get the fire incident report.** Contact Miramar Fire Rescue (or whichever agency responded) and request the official incident report. Your insurer will ask for it. It documents the origin, cause, and extent of the fire, which protects you if the cause is ever disputed.

**2. Call your insurance company to open the claim.** Most carriers have 24-hour claim lines. Opening the claim starts the clock on ALE benefits if you're displaced. Have your policy number ready. You do NOT need to give a detailed recorded statement on the first call, and you are not required to accept a settlement offer at any point before you're ready.

**3. Document everything before anything moves.** Walk every room with your phone on video. Open closets, cabinets, and the garage. Photograph soot lines on walls, water damage from hose lines, melted fixtures, and damaged contents. If your home is in a newer western development near Sunset Lakes, the construction materials matter: document whether the framing, insulation, and drywall are original or recently updated, because replacement costs vary.

**4. Do not throw anything away.** Even damaged or destroyed items are inventory. Insurers require a contents list with descriptions, approximate ages, and replacement values. Discarded items cannot be claimed.

**5. Secure the property.** Your policy requires you to prevent further damage. Board broken windows, tarp roof damage, and lock accessible entry points. Keep receipts for everything you spend on emergency board-up and tarping, because those costs are reimbursable.

## How does the insurance adjuster process work?

After you file, your carrier assigns an adjuster. That adjuster works for the insurance company, not for you. Their job is to assess the loss accurately, but they are also working from a software estimate (Xactimate is the industry standard) that may not capture every line item specific to your property.

Here is what protects your claim:

- **Get your own scope of damages first.** A licensed restoration contractor can walk the property and produce a written scope before the adjuster visits. This gives you a documented baseline to compare against the carrier's estimate. Gaps between the two are negotiable, and having a professional scope in hand is the single most effective way to close those gaps.
- **Request a public adjuster if the loss is large.** Public adjusters work for you, not the carrier, and take a percentage of the final settlement. For significant losses, they often recover more than their fee.
- **Understand the depreciation math.** Most policies pay Actual Cash Value (ACV) first, which is replacement cost minus depreciation. Once repairs are completed, you submit receipts to recover the withheld depreciation (called the recoverable depreciation). If you don't complete repairs, you typically don't collect that second check. Know your policy type before agreeing to a settlement.

The [Florida Department of Financial Services](https://www.myfloridacfo.com) oversees insurance claim disputes in the state and provides a free mediation program for homeowners who disagree with their carrier's settlement offer.

## What does fire damage restoration actually involve?

Fire damage restoration is not just cleaning up ash. A properly scoped job covers several distinct phases, and understanding them helps you verify that your contractor's scope matches what the insurer is willing to pay.

**Emergency stabilization** comes first: board-up, tarping, and structural shoring if needed. This stops the loss from growing and satisfies your policy's duty-to-mitigate requirement.

**Demolition and debris removal** follows. Charred framing, burned drywall, and compromised insulation come out. In older Historic Miramar homes, this phase sometimes uncovers pre-existing conditions (older wiring, deteriorated plumbing) that are separate from the fire claim, so document carefully what was fire-related and what was pre-existing.

**Smoke and soot cleaning** is the most labor-intensive phase. Soot is acidic and continues to etch surfaces, corrode metals, and embed into porous materials the longer it sits. HEPA vacuuming, chemical sponge wiping, and thermal fogging or hydroxyl treatment address different types of smoke residue. The [IICRC S700 standard](https://www.iicrc.org) governs fire and smoke restoration practices and defines the documentation required for a properly closed job.

**Odor elimination** often requires multiple treatments. Ozone generators, hydroxyl generators, and thermal foggers each address different odor compounds. HVAC systems need to be cleaned separately because ductwork carries smoke throughout the home.

**Reconstruction** is the final phase: drywall, paint, flooring, cabinetry, and fixtures. This is where the recoverable depreciation from your policy becomes relevant, because reconstruction costs are what trigger that second payment.
