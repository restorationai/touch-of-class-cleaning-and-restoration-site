If water is actively flooding your home right now, stop reading and do three things: shut off the main water supply valve, cut power to any flooded rooms at the breaker panel, and move people and pets to dry ground. Once those three things are done, come back here — the next 24 hours will determine whether you're dealing with a straightforward cleanup or a months-long mold and structural repair.

## Understanding What's Actually Happening to Your Home

Water doesn't stay where it lands. Within minutes of a pipe burst or appliance failure, water wicks into drywall, travels under baseboards, and soaks into subfloor materials you can't see. Hardwood floors can begin to cup and buckle within hours. Drywall that feels only slightly damp on the surface may already be saturated an inch deep.

In South Florida's climate — high humidity, warm year-round temperatures — the window before mold begins to colonize wet materials is shorter than most homeowners expect. Mold spores can start establishing colonies on wet drywall and insulation in as little as 24 to 48 hours under the right conditions. That's not a scare tactic; it's the reason the first day matters so much.

The source of the water also changes what you're dealing with:

- **Clean water** (burst supply line, overflowing sink, rain intrusion): lowest contamination risk, but still needs to dry fast.
- **Gray water** (washing machine overflow, dishwasher backup, toilet overflow without solids): contains mild contaminants and requires more careful handling.
- **Black water** (sewage backup, floodwater from outside): biohazardous. Do not wade through it without protection, and do not attempt cleanup without professional equipment.

If you're not sure which category your situation falls into, treat it as gray water at minimum until you know more.

## The Immediate Steps: Hours 1 Through 6

Work through this list in order. Each step either limits damage or protects your ability to file an insurance claim later.

1. **Shut off the water source.** If a pipe burst, the main shutoff is usually near the water meter — in Davie, that's typically at the front of the property near the street, or under a utility box in the garage. If it's an appliance failure (water heater, washing machine), there's usually a dedicated shutoff valve directly behind or beneath the unit.

2. **Cut power to affected rooms.** Water and live electricity are an immediately life-threatening combination. Go to your breaker panel and switch off any circuits that serve the flooded area. If the panel itself is in a flooded space, call your utility company — FPL can cut power to the meter from outside.

3. **Document everything before you touch it.** Open your phone and take a slow video walkthrough of every affected room. Photograph water lines on walls, damaged furniture, ruined flooring. Your insurance adjuster will want this, and photos taken before cleanup are far more credible than photos taken after.

4. **Remove standing water if it's safe to do so.** A wet/dry shop vac can pull several gallons per minute from hard floors. Mops and towels work for smaller volumes. The goal isn't to get the floor bone dry — it's to remove bulk water so it stops spreading.

5. **Move salvageable belongings out of the wet zone.** Furniture left sitting in water will wick moisture up through legs and fabric. Move rugs, upholstered pieces, and valuables to a dry area. Place aluminum foil under furniture legs if you can't move a heavy piece — it slows the wicking and prevents rust stains on wet flooring.

6. **Increase airflow.** Open windows if outdoor humidity is lower than indoor humidity (check your phone's weather app — if it's below about 60% outside, open up). Run ceiling fans. If you have a portable dehumidifier, deploy it now. Do not run your HVAC system if the air handler or ducts may have been exposed to water — you risk spreading contamination.

7. **Call your insurance company.** Most homeowner policies require you to report a loss promptly. A quick call to open a claim doesn't commit you to anything, but delaying that call can complicate coverage later.

## What Not to Do in the First 24 Hours

Some common instincts make the situation worse.

- **Don't use a regular household vacuum.** Standard vacuums aren't designed for liquids and can electrocute you or destroy the motor.
- **Don't run the HVAC to "dry things out."** If the system pulled in contaminated air or moisture, running it circulates that through the whole house.
- **Don't pull up flooring immediately.** It's tempting, but flooring removal done incorrectly can disturb hidden mold, damage the subfloor further, or void your insurance claim if it looks like you've altered the scene before an adjuster visits. Document first, then discuss removal with a restoration professional.
- **Don't assume a ceiling that isn't dripping is fine.** If water came from above — a roof leak, an upstairs bathroom overflow — the ceiling cavity can hold gallons before it shows any visible sign. Press gently on a suspicious area; if it feels soft or spongy, there's water trapped above it.
- **Don't use propane heaters or high-heat drying methods indoors.** They can create condensation, drive moisture deeper into materials, or create fire and carbon monoxide hazards.

## When to Call a Professional

Some water damage situations are genuinely manageable as a DIY project — a small washing machine hose leak caught within an hour, for example, where the affected area is a few square feet of tile floor. Most are not.

Call a water damage restoration professional when:

- The affected area is larger than roughly 10 square feet, or water has spread to more than one room.
- The water source is gray or black water.
- Water has been sitting for more than a few hours before you discovered it.
- The leak is behind a wall, under flooring, or in a ceiling cavity — anywhere you can't directly see and dry.
- You can already smell a musty or earthy odor. That smell is microbial activity, and it means the clock on mold colonization has already started.
- The structure of the home is involved: swollen door frames, buckled flooring, soft spots in walls or ceilings.

A certified water damage team brings equipment that changes the math on drying time: truck-mounted extractors that remove far more water than a shop vac, industrial air movers and dehumidifiers that can dry a room in days rather than weeks, and moisture meters that confirm materials are actually dry rather than just surface-dry.

## What Comes After the First 24 Hours

Once immediate extraction is done, the restoration process shifts into a drying and monitoring phase that typically runs three to five days for a contained loss. Technicians place equipment, take daily moisture readings, and adjust airflow to hit target dryness levels in walls, floors, and structural cavities.

Only after materials test dry does the rebuild phase begin — replacing drywall, reinstalling flooring, repainting. Trying to skip the drying phase and jump straight to repairs is the most common reason homeowners end up with mold problems months later: the materials looked fine on the surface but were still holding moisture inside.
