## Why Fullerton Homes See Duct Buildup Faster Than You'd Expect

Fullerton's housing stock tells a story in its ductwork. A significant portion of the city's residential neighborhoods were developed in the postwar boom of the 1940s through 1960s, and many of those homes were later retrofitted with central HVAC systems that weren't part of the original design. That means ducts were routed through tight attic crawlspaces, around original framing, and through wall cavities that weren't built with airflow in mind. Irregular duct geometry creates turbulence zones where dust and debris accumulate faster than in purpose-built systems.

Fullerton also sits downwind of several major freeway corridors, and the particulate load from vehicle traffic is measurable in the region's air quality data. On high-AQI days — which Orange County residents track closely through the South Coast AQMD — that outdoor particulate matter gets pulled directly into return air intakes. Homes near the 57 or 91 corridors tend to see heavier duct fouling than properties farther from those routes. Add in the region's abundant jacaranda, eucalyptus, and pepper trees, which shed fine organic debris through much of the year, and you have a consistent source of biological material feeding into duct systems every time a door or window opens.

## Our Duct Cleaning Process, Calibrated to Fullerton Conditions

The process starts with a camera inspection of the main trunk lines and branch runs before any equipment is connected. In older Fullerton homes, this step frequently reveals flex duct sections that have partially collapsed, disconnected joints where conditioned air has been leaking into attic space, or duct lining that has begun to degrade. Cleaning a compromised duct system without documenting those conditions first doesn't solve the problem — it just moves debris around.

Once the system is mapped, we use negative-pressure containment to pull the duct system into a controlled vacuum state, then work each branch with agitation tools sized to the actual duct diameter. For homes with older fiberglass-lined ducts — common in Fullerton properties built in the 1970s and 1980s — we adjust brush pressure carefully to avoid abrading the liner, which would create a new contamination source rather than eliminating the old one. After mechanical cleaning, we apply an EPA-registered sanitizing agent to duct interiors where microbial growth is present or likely given the home's moisture history.

Dryer vent cleaning is handled as a separate scope but is often scheduled at the same time, particularly in homes where the dryer is vented through a long horizontal run — a layout common in Fullerton's ranch-style floor plans where the laundry room sits in the interior of the house.
