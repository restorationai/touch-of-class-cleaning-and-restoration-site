## Why Corona Properties See Sewage Backup Issues

Corona's growth pattern matters here. The city expanded rapidly in multiple waves — older neighborhoods closer to downtown feature homes built in the 1950s through 1970s with cast-iron or clay-tile sewer laterals that have had decades to crack, root-intrude, and corrode. Newer master-planned communities on the city's edges have modern PVC lines, but those developments sit on graded hillside terrain where soil settlement and shifting can stress connections at the cleanout and main tie-in points.

Corona also sits in a region where the Santa Ana winds drive soil desiccation cycles — the ground contracts during dry stretches and expands when seasonal rains arrive, and that movement stresses buried sewer laterals year after year. When a lateral finally fails, the backup typically surfaces at the lowest drain in the structure: a floor drain in a garage, a first-floor bathroom, or a laundry room. By the time a homeowner notices standing water with that distinctive sulfur-and-waste odor, the contamination event is already underway.

## Our Sewage Cleanup and Sanitization Process in Corona

The first thing a crew does on arrival is contain the affected zone — sewage contamination is classified as Category 3 (grossly contaminated) water under IICRC S500 standards, which means no unprotected access and no cross-contamination into clean areas of the home. From there, the process moves through defined stages:

**Extraction and removal.** Standing sewage is extracted with truck-mounted or portable units. Porous materials that have absorbed Category 3 water — drywall below the flood line, saturated carpet and pad, compromised subfloor sections — are removed and bagged for proper disposal. In Corona's older housing stock, this sometimes means encountering subfloor assemblies with multiple layers of flooring from different renovation eras, which affects both the scope of removal and the drying timeline.

**Sanitization.** After physical removal, all affected hard surfaces are treated with EPA-registered disinfectants. This step isn't optional or cosmetic — it addresses pathogens including bacteria and viruses that remain on surfaces even after the water is gone.

**Structural drying and verification.** Commercial-grade air movers and desiccant or refrigerant dehumidifiers run until moisture readings across wall cavities, subfloor, and framing return to pre-loss baselines. Drying is documented with calibrated meters throughout the process, not estimated by feel or appearance.
