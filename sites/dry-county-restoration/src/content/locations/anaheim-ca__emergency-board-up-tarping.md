## Why Anaheim Properties Face Distinct Board-Up and Tarping Risks

Orange County's Santa Ana wind season is the single biggest driver of emergency board-up and tarping calls across Anaheim. These offshore, downslope winds — dry, fast-moving, and capable of snapping mature trees and peeling aged roofing material — arrive with little warning and often peak overnight. A roof section that lifts during a Santa Ana event at 2 a.m. is fully exposed to whatever moisture follows, and in Anaheim's climate, even a light coastal marine layer can push enough humidity into an open structure to begin the conditions that favor mold colonization within 24 to 48 hours.

The housing stock adds another layer of complexity. Anaheim's residential neighborhoods include a substantial number of homes built in the 1950s and 1960s, many with original wood-frame construction, composition shingle roofing at the end of its service life, and single-pane aluminum windows that are far more fragile than modern glazing. Fire damage board-up on these older homes often reveals that adjacent windows or door frames have shifted over decades, meaning standard plywood sizing doesn't always seat cleanly — a detail that matters when you're trying to create a genuine weather seal, not just a visual barrier.

Commercial properties along the resort corridor and the Platinum Triangle face a different set of pressures: HOA covenants, property management chains of approval, and the reputational weight of a visibly damaged storefront in a high-foot-traffic area. ## Our Emergency Board-Up and Tarping Process in Anaheim

When you call, the first thing we do is ask the right questions: Is the structure accessible? Has the fire department or building department placed a placard? Is there active hazard — downed power lines, unstable framing, standing water near electrical? That intake shapes how we stage the response.

On arrival, we assess the full perimeter before touching anything. Visible openings are obvious; hidden ones — a soffit blown loose, a vent cap missing, a side door with a compromised frame — are where secondary intrusion happens. We photograph every opening and every material we place, which matters when your insurance adjuster is reviewing the claim.

For roof tarping, we use reinforced polyethylene tarps secured with wood battens and screws driven into structurally sound decking — not draped loosely and weighted with sandbags, which fail in wind. For window and door board-up, we use 3/4-inch CDX plywood cut to fit and secured with structural screws into the framing, not just the casing. On fire-damaged structures where framing integrity is in question, we work with what's sound and document what isn't.

All work is photographed before, during, and after installation. We provide a written scope to you and, if you've already contacted your carrier, we can coordinate directly with the adjuster.

## Local Note

Anaheim homes built in the postwar decades frequently have roof-to-wall connections that predate modern hurricane-strap requirements. During a Santa Ana wind event, it's not uncommon for a section of fascia or rake board to pull away and take roofing material with it in a strip — leaving a long, narrow opening rather than a single large hole. Tarping a strip loss requires a different approach than tarping a large puncture: the tarp needs to extend well past the damaged run on both ends and be secured at intervals that account for wind uplift along the full length, not just at the corners. It's a detail that matters in a climate where the next wind event may arrive before permanent repairs are scheduled.
