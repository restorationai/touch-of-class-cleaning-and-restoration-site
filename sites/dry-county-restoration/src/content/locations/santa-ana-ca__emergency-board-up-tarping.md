## Why Santa Ana Properties Face Unique Board-Up and Tarping Risks

Santa Ana's housing stock skews older. A significant share of residential properties were built between the 1940s and 1970s, and many feature wood-frame construction with original single-pane windows, clay or composition shingle roofing, and stucco exteriors that can crack and spall after fire exposure. When those older rooflines take wind or fire damage, the decking beneath is often thinner than modern code requires, which means a tarp installation needs to be anchored more carefully to avoid pulling fasteners through degraded sheathing.

The city also sits in a Santa Ana wind corridor — the same offshore, downslope wind pattern that drives wildfire spread across Southern California every fall and winter. During red-flag conditions, embers can travel well ahead of a fire front, igniting rooftop debris or landing in open attic vents on homes that appear untouched. A roof that looks intact from the street may have a compromised vent or a scorched rafter bay that needs tarping before the next weather event.

Vehicle break-ins and property crime in denser commercial corridors mean broken storefront glass and compromised roll-up doors are a recurring board-up need — not just post-fire, not just post-storm.

## Our Emergency Board-Up and Tarping Process in Santa Ana

When we arrive on-site, the first task is a rapid exterior assessment: what's open, what's structurally sound enough to work from, and what sequence of closure will stop further weather or intrusion exposure fastest. We photograph every affected opening before any material goes up — that documentation matters for your insurance claim.

For window and door openings, we use nominal ¾-inch plywood cut to fit and secured with structural screws into the surrounding framing, not just the window casing. On older stucco homes common throughout Santa Ana's residential neighborhoods, we take extra care to anchor into the structural framing behind the stucco skin rather than relying on the surface layer, which can be brittle on pre-1970s construction.

For roof tarping, we use 6-mil polyethylene or heavier woven poly tarps depending on the size of the breach and the weather forecast. Tarps are run over the ridge where possible and weighted or screwed at the perimeter so that Santa Ana wind gusts — which can exceed 50 mph during offshore events — don't lift them overnight. We do not staple tarps to deteriorated sheathing and call it done.

## Santa Ana Insurance and HOA Coordination

Most homeowner and commercial property policies cover emergency board-up and tarping as part of the mitigation benefit — the logic being that securing the structure prevents a larger covered loss. We document the work thoroughly: photos before, during, and after; a written scope of what was installed and where; and material and labor records formatted for adjuster review.

If your property is in a managed community or subject to HOA oversight, temporary board-up materials are generally treated as emergency protective measures rather than aesthetic modifications, which typically exempts them from the prior-approval process. That said, we recommend notifying your HOA or property manager as soon as the emergency is stabilized.

## Local Note

Santa Ana's older residential blocks — particularly those with original wood-frame construction and clay tile roofing — present a specific tarping challenge that doesn't come up as often in newer construction: clay tiles are brittle and can crack under foot traffic or tarp anchor points if a crew isn't working carefully around them. We work around existing tile rather than through it, using standoff boards to distribute weight and protect the tile field while still getting a weathertight seal over the damaged section. It's slower than walking the roof directly, but it avoids turning a wind-damaged section into a full reroof.
