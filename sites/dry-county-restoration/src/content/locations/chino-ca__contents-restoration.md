## Why Chino Properties Face Particular Contents Damage Challenges

Chino sits in a region where the soil is dense clay-heavy adobe in many areas, which affects how water drains away from foundations. Homes built during the 1950s through 1980s — a substantial portion of Chino's residential stock — often have slab-on-grade construction with copper or galvanized plumbing that has reached or passed its service life. When those lines fail, water doesn't travel far before it saturates flooring, cabinetry, and the personal property sitting on top of it.

The city also carries a legacy of agricultural and dairy land conversion into residential tracts, which means some neighborhoods sit atop soil with variable compaction. That can translate to foundation settling, cracked slabs, and plumbing stress that shows up as slow leaks long before a homeowner notices. By the time water damage is visible, contents on lower shelves, in garages, or in finished basements have often been wet for days.

Fire risk compounds the picture. The Santa Ana winds that funnel through the Cajon Pass and across the Inland Empire during fall and winter months push embers and drive wildfire smoke into structures even when a home isn't directly threatened. Smoke and soot from regional fires can infiltrate through HVAC systems and coat contents throughout a house — clothing, electronics, documents, and furniture — without a single flame touching the building.

## Our Contents Restoration and Storage Process in Chino

The process begins with a detailed inventory before anything leaves the property. Every item is photographed, logged, and assigned a condition category — this documentation matters both for your insurance claim and for making sure nothing is misplaced during transport. Items that can tolerate on-site cleaning are handled first; anything requiring deeper treatment is carefully packed and moved to a secure, climate-controlled storage facility.

Cleaning methods are matched to the material and the type of loss. Smoke-damaged wood furniture requires a different approach than upholstery that absorbed sewage-contaminated water. Ultrasonic cleaning works well for hard goods, electronics housings, and decorative items with intricate surfaces. Ozone and hydroxyl treatment addresses odor in soft goods. Documents and photographs damaged by water go through freeze-drying or air-drying protocols depending on the extent of saturation.

Once restored items pass inspection, they're returned and placed back in the home — not just dropped at the door. The return phase includes a walkthrough so you can confirm every piece is accounted for and in acceptable condition before the job closes.

## Chino Insurance Coordination for Contents Claims

Contents claims are often the most disputed part of a property loss. Carriers want line-item documentation: what was damaged, what it was worth, and whether restoration is more cost-effective than replacement. The pack-out inventory Dry County Restoration produces is formatted to support that process — photographs, item descriptions, and condition notes that an adjuster can work from directly.

If your policy includes Additional Living Expenses (ALE) coverage, contents storage costs during remediation and reconstruction are typically reimbursable. Keeping the storage receipts and the timeline of the pack-out documented makes that reimbursement easier to claim.

## Local Note

Chino's older residential tracts — particularly homes built during the dairy-conversion boom of the 1960s and 1970s — frequently have detached garages and accessory structures that were converted into living space or storage rooms without permits. When a loss affects one of these structures, the contents inside may not be covered under a standard homeowner's policy the same way primary dwelling contents are. It's worth confirming with your adjuster before assuming everything in a converted garage or workshop is on the same coverage schedule as the main house. Knowing this ahead of time can change how the pack-out is prioritized.
