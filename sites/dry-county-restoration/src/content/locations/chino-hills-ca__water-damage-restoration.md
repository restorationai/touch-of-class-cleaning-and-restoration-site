## Why Chino Hills Properties See Water Damage Issues

Much of Chino Hills was developed in the late 1980s and through the 1990s, which means a large share of the housing stock is now carrying aging polybutylene or early CPVC plumbing that has reached or passed its expected service life. These materials become brittle over time, and the temperature swings common to the inland valleys — hot, dry summers followed by cooler, occasionally wet winters — accelerate that wear. A pipe that holds through a decade of dry summers can crack during the first cold snap of the season.

The city's hillside topography also matters. Properties built on graded lots can experience drainage that redirects surface water toward foundations rather than away from them, particularly after the region's periodic heavy rain events. Stucco exteriors, standard throughout Chino Hills, can mask slow intrusion for months before interior damage becomes visible. By the time you notice a soft spot in the drywall or a musty odor in a closet, the moisture has often been present long enough to threaten the structural sheathing behind the finish material.

## Our Water Damage Restoration Process in Chino Hills

Every job begins with a thorough moisture inspection using thermal imaging cameras and calibrated moisture meters. Water that is invisible at the surface — inside wall cavities, beneath tile set on a mortar bed, under engineered hardwood — shows up clearly with the right tools. We map the full extent of the loss before any drying equipment is placed, because equipment positioned without a moisture map often misses the wettest zones.

Once the scope is documented, we move to water extraction. Standing water is removed with truck-mounted or portable extraction units, and wet materials are assessed for salvageability. Structural drying follows the IICRC S500 standard: the number and placement of air movers and dehumidifiers is calculated based on the affected square footage, material types, and ambient conditions — not guesswork. Readings are logged daily so you and your insurance adjuster have a clear record of the drying progression.

If building materials cannot be dried in place — saturated insulation, compromised drywall, buckled subfloor — we handle controlled demolition and debris removal as part of the mitigation scope. Our IICRC ASD (Applied Structural Drying) certification means the crew making those calls has been trained specifically in drying science, not just general construction.

## Local Note

Homes built on Chino Hills' steeper graded lots often have split-level or daylight-basement configurations where the lower level is partially below the finished grade. These spaces can read dry at the slab surface while retaining significant moisture in the stem wall and lower framing — especially after a drainage event pushes water against the uphill foundation wall. If your lower level has any musty odor or efflorescence on the concrete, it is worth having a moisture inspection even if the floor feels dry underfoot. Catching that condition early is considerably less disruptive than addressing it after mold has established in the wall cavity.
