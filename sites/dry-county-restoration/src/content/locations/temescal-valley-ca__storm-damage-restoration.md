## Why Temescal Valley Properties See Storm Damage

Much of Temescal Valley's housing stock was built during the 2000s and 2010s expansion along the I-15 corridor, which means tile roofing, stucco exteriors, and engineered hillside lots are the norm rather than the exception. Newer construction generally handles wind load well, but hillside grading creates its own problem: water that should sheet away from a slab instead concentrates in swales and drainage easements that weren't sized for an unusually heavy storm cycle. We also see debris flow risk on properties downslope from hillsides that have burned in past wildfire seasons; scorched ground sheds rainwater far faster than vegetated slopes, which can send mud and runoff into yards and garages well after the storm itself has passed.

## Our Storm Damage Restoration Process in Temescal Valley

We start with a roof and envelope inspection to find where wind or impact opened the structure, not just where water is visibly pooling inside. On hillside lots we also check the grading and any drainage channels for blockage, since a clogged swale can keep feeding water into a structure for days after a storm clears. Once the property is stabilized, typically with tarping or board-up, we extract standing water and set drying equipment calibrated to the materials involved; stucco and tile construction holds moisture differently than wood-frame siding, which changes how long drying equipment needs to run. Throughout, we document everything with photos and moisture readings for your insurance file.

## Local note

One thing we watch closely in Temescal Valley: hillside lots below slopes that burned in a prior wildfire season carry an elevated debris flow risk during heavy rain, even years after the fire. Scorched soil loses the root structure that normally slows runoff, so a storm that would be a minor event on vegetated ground can send a surprising volume of mud and water downhill fast. If your property sits below open hillside, it's worth having that drainage path checked, not just the roof, after any significant storm.
