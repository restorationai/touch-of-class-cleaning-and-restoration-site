## Why Chino's Building Boom Creates Distinct Cleanup Challenges

Chino sits at the western edge of San Bernardino County, where decades of dairy-farm conversion have given way to dense residential subdivisions and mixed-use corridors. That land history matters for construction cleanup: former agricultural parcels often carry silty, clay-heavy soil that tracks onto slabs during framing and finish work. When that soil dries in Chino's low-humidity air, it pulverizes into a fine dust that behaves differently from the drywall compound dust most cleaners plan for — it's heavier, settles deeper into carpet fibers and subfloor gaps, and can leave a chalky film on cabinetry if wiped rather than vacuumed first.

Newer tract developments in the area are also built to tight tolerances with open-concept floor plans, which means construction debris and dust travel farther through the structure than in compartmentalized older homes. Specialty surfaces — quartz countertops, large-format porcelain tile, engineered hardwood — are standard spec in many Chino builds, and each one requires a different cleaning protocol to avoid scratching or etching during the final clean.

## Our Post-Construction Cleaning Process in Chino

Every post-construction or specialty clean follows a deliberate sequence, because order matters as much as effort.

**Rough clean first.** Before any fine cleaning begins, the crew removes bulk debris — cut lumber, packaging, caulk tubes, tape scraps — and vacuums all surfaces top to bottom using HEPA-filtered equipment. Blowing debris around with a leaf blower or shop vac without HEPA filtration just redistributes fine particulate.

**Surface-by-surface detail work.** Windows are cleaned in two passes: a razor-blade scrape for paint overspray and adhesive labels, then a streak-free wash. Cabinet interiors are wiped, then vacuumed again, because sawdust settles back in during the wipe stage. Tile grout lines are scrubbed before sealer is applied — grout haze left behind by tile setters is common in Chino's new construction and requires an acid-based remover applied carefully to avoid damaging adjacent surfaces.

**HVAC and vent protection.** Construction dust loads HVAC filters and duct openings fast. We inspect and replace temporary filter covers, and we flag any duct contamination to the property owner before the system runs — a step that's easy to skip but prevents the heating and cooling system from redistributing fine particulate through a freshly cleaned space.

**Final walk-through.** Every room is inspected under raking light, which catches residue on glass, smears on painted trim, and footprints on polished floors that overhead lighting misses entirely.
