## Why Ontario Carpets Take a Harder Hit

The Inland Empire's climate is the primary driver. Ontario averages well under 15 inches of rain per year, and the dry stretches between storms mean dust, pollen, and fine desert sand work their way deep into pile rather than being rinsed toward the surface. When rain does arrive — often in short, intense bursts between November and March — residents track wet, clay-heavy soil from driveways and sidewalks across thresholds before mats have a chance to do their job. That combination of dry-season grit and wet-season mud cycling through carpet creates a compaction problem that shortens fiber life and locks in odors.

Ontario also sits in a region with significant commercial and light-industrial activity. Employees and visitors tracking in from warehouse floors, logistics yards, and distribution facilities bring in a different soil profile than a purely residential neighborhood — petroleum residue, rubber particulate, and compacted fiber from packaging materials all behave differently under a cleaning wand than ordinary household dirt.

## Our Carpet Cleaning Process in Ontario

Every job starts with a fiber and soil assessment. Carpet construction varies widely across Ontario's housing stock — from older loop-pile installations in mid-century tract homes to modern cut-pile and frieze in newer master-planned developments — and the cleaning chemistry and dwell time need to match the fiber, not just the stain.

From there, the process follows a consistent sequence:

1. **Pre-vacuum and dry soil removal** — pulling out the abrasive particulate that hot water alone would push deeper into the backing.
2. **Pre-treatment and dwell** — pH-balanced solutions applied to high-traffic lanes, pet areas, and visible stains, with dwell time calibrated to soil type and fiber sensitivity.
3. **Hot water extraction** — truck-mounted equipment generating the water temperature and suction pressure needed to flush residue from the base of the pile, not just the surface.
4. **Spot treatment** — targeted work on any stains that didn't fully release in the main pass, using enzyme-based or oxidizing agents matched to the stain chemistry.
5. **Grooming and speed-dry** — pile is groomed directionally and airflow is set to accelerate drying, important in Ontario's low-humidity environment where fast drying is achievable but still requires active management to prevent wicking.

Dry County Restoration is an IICRC Certified Firm, and the technicians on your job carry individual IICRC credentials — that means the cleaning protocols we follow are grounded in industry standards, not improvised on-site.
