## Why Fontana Properties See Water Damage Issues

Fontana sits in the Inland Empire's high-heat, low-humidity belt, and that climate shapes how water damage actually happens here. Summers routinely push past 100 degrees, which means air conditioning systems run almost constantly from May through September. Condensate drain lines on those units clog with algae and mineral scale over time, and when they back up, the overflow often runs into an attic, a hallway closet, or along an interior wall where nobody notices until the drywall bubbles. Add to that the slab-on-grade construction common in Fontana's 1980s and 1990s tract developments, and you get a second pattern: a failed supply line or water heater leak doesn't drain away through a crawlspace, it pools against the slab and wicks into baseboards, cabinet toe-kicks, and carpet pad before anyone sees standing water on the surface. Newer construction near the city's logistics and warehouse corridors brings its own issues too, including large commercial roof drains and fire suppression lines that, when they fail, move a lot of water fast.

## Our Water Damage Restoration Process in Fontana

The first thing our crew does on arrival is find out where the water actually is, not just where it's visible. On slab foundations that means moisture mapping with penetrating meters across tile, laminate, and carpeted areas, since a leak can travel several feet under flooring before it ever shows as a stain. Extraction comes next, pulling standing water with truck-mounted or portable extraction units depending on how much square footage is affected. Structural drying follows the IICRC S500 standard: we set air movers and dehumidifiers based on the actual moisture readings in the walls and subfloor, not a flat timeline, because a slab that's held water for two days behaves differently than one caught within hours. In homes where flooring has to come up to dry the slab underneath, we document everything with photos before removal so the claim and the rebuild stay straightforward.
