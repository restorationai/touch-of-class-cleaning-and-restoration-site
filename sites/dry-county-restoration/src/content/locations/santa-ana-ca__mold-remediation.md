## Why Santa Ana Properties See Mold Issues

Santa Ana's housing stock skews older. Much of the city's residential fabric was built between the 1920s and the 1960s, when vapor barriers weren't standard practice and wall cavities were left open to whatever moisture the soil and climate delivered. Stucco exteriors — nearly universal in this part of Orange County — are durable, but hairline cracks from settlement or seismic movement let moisture wick inward. Once water is behind stucco, it has nowhere to go except into the wood framing and insulation, where mold can begin colonizing within 24 to 48 hours of sustained saturation.

Santa Ana also sits in a basin where the water table is relatively shallow in lower-elevation neighborhoods. Slab foundations that were poured without modern moisture barriers can transmit ground moisture upward, keeping concrete and the flooring above it perpetually damp — a condition that shows up as musty odors long before visible growth appears. Older plumbing, including galvanized supply lines still present in some pre-1960s homes, corrodes from the inside out and can leak slowly inside walls for months before a homeowner notices.

## Our Mold Remediation Process in Santa Ana

Every job starts with a thorough assessment — not a visual scan, but moisture mapping with calibrated meters and thermal imaging to find what's wet behind surfaces that look dry. Mold remediation without finding the moisture source is a temporary fix; the growth returns.

Once the scope is defined, we establish containment using negative air pressure and 6-mil poly barriers to prevent spores from migrating to unaffected rooms during removal. Affected porous materials — drywall, insulation, sections of subfloor — are removed, bagged, and disposed of per California's handling requirements. Non-porous surfaces are HEPA-vacuumed and treated with EPA-registered antimicrobial agents.

Drying comes next. In Santa Ana's climate, ambient humidity can slow the drying of structural cavities even in summer, so we use industrial dehumidifiers and air movers calibrated to the conditions on-site, not a one-size schedule. We monitor moisture readings daily until the structure meets the IICRC S520 standard before any rebuild begins.

## Local Note

In Santa Ana's older neighborhoods, many homes were built with a construction method that used wood lath beneath plaster walls rather than modern drywall. Plaster-and-lath assemblies hold moisture differently: the surface can read dry on a pin meter while the lath behind it remains saturated and actively supporting mold growth. If your home was built before roughly 1960 and you're seeing musty odors without obvious visible mold, that wall assembly is the first place we investigate — and it's one reason we don't stop at a surface reading.
