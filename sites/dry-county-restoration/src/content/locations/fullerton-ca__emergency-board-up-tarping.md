## Why Fullerton Properties Face Distinct Board-Up and Tarping Challenges

Fullerton's housing stock spans more than a century. The older Craftsman and Spanish Colonial Revival homes near downtown — many built between the 1910s and 1940s — often have wood-frame construction with original single-pane windows, clay tile roofs, and wide overhanging eaves. Clay tile is durable under normal conditions, but when fire or impact breaks a section loose, the irregular substrate underneath is far harder to tarp cleanly than a standard asphalt-shingle deck. The tiles that remain can shift during tarping, creating gaps that let water channel directly into the attic cavity.

Post-war tract homes in the central and eastern parts of the city present a different challenge: flat or low-slope roofs with built-up membrane systems that age and crack under the region's intense UV exposure. When those membranes fail during a storm event, water infiltrates quickly and silently — often pooling above drop ceilings before anyone notices. Board-up and tarping on these structures requires weighted ballast and mechanical anchoring rather than simple nail-and-staple methods, because the roofing material itself may not hold a fastener reliably.

The Santa Ana wind pattern — dry, fast-moving offshore winds that typically peak in fall and early winter — is the single biggest accelerant of secondary damage in this area. A fire that's extinguished by 10 p.m. can have its exposed framing dried out and its ash residue redistributed by 2 a.m. if the structure isn't sealed. Securing openings the same night is not a preference here; it's a practical necessity.

## Our Emergency Board-Up and Tarping Process in Fullerton

When a call comes in, the first step is a rapid site assessment to identify every breach: broken windows, kicked-in doors, fire-damaged wall sections, missing or displaced roofing material. We photograph and document each opening before any material goes up — that documentation becomes part of the insurance claim file and establishes a baseline for the scope of structural damage.

For window and door openings, we use exterior-grade plywood cut and fitted to the frame, secured with structural screws rather than nails where the framing allows. On historic or architecturally detailed homes — a category that applies to a meaningful portion of Fullerton's older residential neighborhoods — we take care to avoid fastening into original millwork or decorative trim that would be expensive to restore.

For roof breaches, we deploy heavy-duty polyethylene tarps with a minimum 6-mil thickness, anchored with wood battens screwed into solid decking and weighted at the perimeter. On clay tile roofs, we work around intact tile sections rather than over them, which takes more time but prevents the tarp from creating a funnel that directs water into the structure rather than away from it.

## Local Note

Fullerton's older downtown-adjacent neighborhoods include a number of properties with original clay tile roofs that have never been re-roofed — the tiles are intact but the underlayment beneath them is decades past its rated lifespan. When fire or impact breaks through the tile surface on one of these roofs, the underlayment often crumbles on contact, leaving no secondary moisture barrier at all. Tarping over this condition requires treating the entire exposed section as a total barrier failure, not just covering the visible hole — otherwise water wicks laterally under the tarp edge and into the attic along the dry, brittle felt. It's a detail that matters on these specific homes and that a crew unfamiliar with Fullerton's older housing stock might miss on a night call.
