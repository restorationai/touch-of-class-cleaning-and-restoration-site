## Why Chino Properties Need Reconstruction After Damage

Chino sits in the Inland Empire's inland heat belt, where summer temperatures regularly push past 100 degrees and Santa Ana wind events raise wildfire exposure along the foothill approaches to the east. Those same dry, windy conditions that drive fire risk also stress roofing and exterior finishes year-round, and when a structure fire, roof failure, or burst supply line does enough damage to require framing or drywall replacement, reconstruction has to account for both the original building era and current San Bernardino County code requirements. Older ranch-style homes near the city's historic core often have older electrical and plumbing runs that get exposed once wall cavities open up, which can add scope to a rebuild that started as a simple fire or water repair.

## Our Reconstruction Process in Chino

We start every reconstruction job with a scope walk that separates structural work (framing, subfloor, load-bearing repairs) from finish work (drywall, paint, flooring, trim), because insurance carriers and permitting offices treat those categories differently. Demolition and debris removal come next, followed by rough-in inspection on anything touching electrical, plumbing, or structural framing before we close walls back up. Our crews hold IICRC certification across water (WRT) and structural drying (ASD) disciplines, which matters on reconstruction jobs that start as water or fire losses, since drying and mitigation have to be verified complete before rebuild framing goes back in. Final finish work, from drywall texture matching to flooring and paint, gets scheduled once inspections clear.

## Chino Insurance & Permitting Coordination

Reconstruction claims typically involve more back-and-forth with an adjuster than a straightforward mitigation job, since the carrier wants documentation tying the rebuild scope directly to the covered loss. We photograph and log damage before demolition, keep moisture and structural readings on file, and submit itemized estimates that separate code-upgrade costs from like-kind-and-quality repair, which is often where disputes happen on older Chino properties. Permits for structural reconstruction in Chino go through the city's building division, and any job touching load-bearing framing, electrical panels, or more than a defined percentage of roof structure will need inspection sign-off before we close up walls or finish surfaces.

## Local note

A detail that catches homeowners off guard: several of Chino's older ranch homes, particularly those built before stucco cladding became standard, used board-and-batten or lap siding under later stucco retrofits. When fire or water damage forces us to open an exterior wall on one of these properties, we sometimes find the original siding still underneath, which changes how the wall assembly gets rebuilt and can affect whether the repair qualifies as like-kind-and-quality under the insurance policy. We flag this during the initial scope walk so there are no surprises once demolition starts.
