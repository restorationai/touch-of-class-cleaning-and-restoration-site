## Why Riverside Properties Face Particular Fire Damage Challenges

Riverside's housing stock spans more than a century of construction, and that range matters when a fire hits. Older homes in the historic core of the city — many built between the 1910s and 1950s — were framed with old-growth lumber that is denser and more resinous than modern dimensional lumber. That wood chars differently, holds smoke odor longer, and can mask structural compromise beneath a surface that looks intact. Homes from that era also commonly have knob-and-tube or early Romex wiring, which fire investigators and structural engineers flag as a priority review item after any fire event.

At the same time, Riverside's newer tract developments — built rapidly during the 1980s and 1990s expansion — often feature open floor plans with vaulted ceilings and lightweight truss systems. Those trusses can lose structural integrity faster under fire load than traditional rafter framing, which affects how safely a crew can enter and begin work. Regardless of construction era, the Inland Empire's low humidity accelerates smoke residue bonding to porous surfaces: tile grout, unsealed concrete, and stucco exteriors all absorb soot faster here than in coastal markets where ambient moisture slows the process.

## Our Fire Damage Restoration Process in Riverside

The first priority after fire is securing the structure — board-up, roof tarping, and temporary fencing if the property is compromised. That happens the same day we arrive, before any cleaning or drying work begins. Once the site is safe and the fire marshal has cleared re-entry, the process moves through several distinct phases.

**Documentation and scope:** Every affected surface is photographed and measured. Smoke migration is mapped using thermal imaging, which often reveals residue in areas that look clean to the eye — inside HVAC returns, behind electrical panels, and in attic insulation. This documentation drives the insurance claim and sets the remediation scope.

**Demolition of unsalvageable materials:** Charred drywall, insulation, and flooring that cannot be cleaned to pre-loss condition are removed and disposed of per California regulations. Structural members are assessed; sistering or temporary shoring is noted in the scope before any cosmetic work begins.

**Smoke and soot cleaning:** Dry sponge cleaning for loose soot, followed by chemical sponge and HEPA vacuuming for bonded residue. Protein smoke — common in kitchen fires — requires enzymatic cleaners and extended dwell time because it leaves an almost invisible film that produces persistent odor. Ozone and hydroxyl treatments address odor in the air volume after surfaces are clean.

**Deodorization and air quality:** Thermal fogging reaches into wall cavities and duct systems where surface cleaning cannot. HVAC systems are cleaned or sealed before the home is reoccupied.

**Reconstruction coordination:** Dry County Restoration holds Contractor License #1083555 and manages structural repairs, drywall, painting, and finish work — one point of contact from emergency response through final walkthrough.

## Local Note

Riverside's older residential neighborhoods near the University Avenue corridor and the downtown historic district frequently have original plaster-on-lath interior walls rather than drywall. After a fire, plaster walls present a specific challenge: they can absorb smoke odor into the lath substrate even when the plaster face appears intact, and aggressive cleaning can fracture the plaster bond. A crew that defaults to standard drywall protocols will either under-clean and leave odor, or over-clean and create unnecessary demolition. The right call — clean or remove — depends on a close read of the plaster condition, the depth of smoke penetration, and whether the historic character of the home is a factor in the restoration scope.
