## Why Temescal Valley Properties See Smoke Damage Issues

Much of Temescal Valley's housing stock went up during the 1990s and 2000s building boom, which means tile roofs, attic gable vents, and HVAC systems designed for airflow rather than smoke sealing. When Santa Ana winds push wildfire smoke down from the surrounding hillsides, that fine particulate finds every gap in the building envelope. ## Our Smoke Damage Restoration Process in Temescal Valley

We start with a walk-through to separate surface soot from deep residue, since wildfire smoke behaves differently than a structure fire. Protein smoke from a kitchen fire leaves a thin, greasy film that's often worse than it looks; wildfire ash is drier and travels further through the house on airflow alone. From there we HEPA vacuum and dry-sponge hard surfaces, run thermal fogging or ozone treatment where odor has penetrated porous materials, and pull and seal ductwork if the HVAC system recirculated smoke through the house. Drywall, insulation, and textiles that absorbed odor past the point of cleaning get removed rather than masked, since covering an odor source in a tile-roofed attic space just means it resurfaces when the weather warms.
