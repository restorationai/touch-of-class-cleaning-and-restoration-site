## Why Anaheim Homes Accumulate Duct Debris Faster Than You'd Expect

Anaheim's housing stock spans a wide range of construction eras. Tracts built in the 1950s and 1960s — common in the older residential corridors west of the 57 freeway — often have original sheet-metal ductwork with joints that were sealed with mastic or cloth tape. Over decades, that tape degrades, gaps open, and attic air (which in a hot Anaheim summer can exceed 140°F) gets pulled into the supply side of the system. That attic air carries insulation fibers, dust, and whatever has accumulated in the crawl space or attic floor.

Newer construction isn't immune either. Flex duct, which became standard in tract homes built from the 1980s onward, is prone to kinking and sagging — both of which create low points where dust settles and moisture can briefly collect after the system cycles off. When a Anaheim home goes several years without duct cleaning, that settled material becomes a substrate for mold spores and bacteria, particularly in rooms where humidity spikes (bathrooms with poor exhaust, kitchens without range hoods vented to the exterior).

## Our Air Duct Cleaning Process in Anaheim

The process starts with a camera inspection of the main trunk lines and as many branch runs as are accessible. This tells us whether we're dealing with simple dust accumulation, debris from a renovation, pest intrusion, or early microbial growth — each of which calls for a different approach.

For standard residential cleaning, we use negative-air pressure equipment: a high-powered vacuum unit is connected to the main return, creating negative pressure throughout the system while rotary brush agitation loosens debris from duct walls. The dislodged material travels toward the vacuum rather than into the living space. Supply registers are cleaned individually, and the air handler cabinet — the section most homeowners forget — is inspected and wiped down before the system is restarted.

When a visual inspection or customer history suggests microbial contamination, we apply an EPA-registered sanitizing agent to interior duct surfaces after the mechanical cleaning is complete. We do not apply any coating or sealant without first discussing it with the homeowner; some products marketed as duct sealants are not appropriate for occupied residential spaces.

Dryer vent cleaning is offered alongside duct cleaning and is worth scheduling at the same time. Anaheim's older ranch-style homes frequently have dryer vents that run long horizontal distances through interior walls before exiting — configurations that trap lint efficiently and are a documented fire risk.

## Local Note

Anaheim has a notable concentration of homes built during the postwar boom of the 1950s that used early fiberglass duct board — a rigid, fiber-faced insulation product that was factory-formed into duct sections. This material is still functional when intact, but it develops surface erosion over time, and aggressive rotary brushing can damage the liner and release fibers into the airstream. When we encounter duct board in older Anaheim homes, we switch to a gentler contact-vacuum method and flag any sections showing delamination for the homeowner's awareness. It's a detail that matters in this city's older housing stock and one that a crew unfamiliar with the area's construction era might miss.
