## Why Ontario Properties See Mold Issues

Ontario's housing stock spans several decades and construction styles, and each era carries its own mold vulnerability. Homes built in the postwar expansion of the 1950s and 1960s — common in older residential pockets throughout the city — often have original plaster-and-lath walls with minimal vapor barriers. When a slow plumbing leak or a failed roof flashing lets moisture in, it wicks deep into that assembly and stays there. The surface may feel dry to the touch within days, but the lath and framing behind it can remain saturated long enough for mold colonies to establish.

More recent construction isn't immune. Tract homes built during the 1990s and 2000s building boom across Ontario frequently used paper-faced drywall and OSB sheathing — materials that mold colonizes quickly when moisture intrusion goes unaddressed. The region's clay-heavy soils also slow surface drainage after heavy rain, which can push groundwater against slab edges and foundation stem walls, creating chronic low-level moisture at the base of interior walls.

Ontario's position in the San Bernardino Valley means it collects warm, humid air that funnels in from the south and southwest during late summer. Air conditioning systems that run hard through those months generate significant condensation, and when ductwork or air handler drain pans aren't maintained, that moisture becomes a slow drip inside wall and ceiling cavities — exactly the dark, damp environment mold needs.

## Our Mold Remediation Process in Ontario

Every job starts with a thorough inspection using moisture meters and thermal imaging to map where water has traveled — not just where mold is visible. Mold is a symptom; the moisture source is the problem, and addressing one without the other guarantees a return visit.

Once the scope is clear, the crew establishes negative-pressure containment using 6-mil poly barriers and HEPA-filtered air scrubbers. This keeps spores from migrating to unaffected rooms during the removal process — critical in occupied homes. Affected porous materials (drywall, insulation, wood framing where necessary) are removed, bagged, and disposed of per California Department of Public Health guidelines. Non-porous surfaces are HEPA-vacuumed and treated with EPA-registered antimicrobial agents.

Structural drying follows remediation. The team holds IICRC AMRT (Applied Microbial Remediation Technician) and ASD (Applied Structural Drying) certifications, which means the drying phase is documented and calibrated — not guesswork. Psychrometric readings are logged daily until materials reach target moisture content. When the remediation scope warrants it, we coordinate with an independent industrial hygienist for post-clearance air sampling before containment comes down.

## Local Note

Ontario properties built during the 1980s and early 1990s sometimes used EIFS (exterior insulation and finish systems) — the synthetic stucco product that was popular across the Inland Empire before its moisture-trapping tendencies became widely understood. When EIFS cladding develops even a hairline crack at a window corner or penetration, water enters and has almost nowhere to drain. The foam insulation board behind the finish layer stays wet indefinitely, and mold growth in that assembly is typically well advanced by the time interior symptoms appear. If your home has a textured exterior that was installed before the mid-1990s and you're seeing mold near windows or exterior walls, that cladding is worth examining as part of the inspection.
