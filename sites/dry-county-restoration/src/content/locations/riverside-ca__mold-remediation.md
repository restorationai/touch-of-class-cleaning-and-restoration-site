## Why Riverside Properties See Mold Problems

Riverside's housing stock spans more than a century, and that range matters for mold risk. Craftsman bungalows and Spanish Colonial Revival homes built before World War II — common in the older residential corridors near downtown — were often constructed with minimal vapor barriers and rely on wood-lathe framing that holds moisture far longer than modern OSB or drywall. When a slow plumbing leak or a failed roof flashing goes undetected for even a week or two, the interior framing in those older walls can reach the moisture content levels where Cladosporium, Penicillium, and Stachybotrys (commonly called black mold) begin to colonize.

The region's clay-heavy soils also contribute. After heavy rainfall — increasingly intense during La Niña winters — water doesn't drain quickly from the ground around foundations. Homes with slab-on-grade construction can experience moisture wicking upward through concrete, particularly in rooms where flooring was installed without an adequate moisture barrier. Crawl-space homes face the same problem from below, where standing water after a storm can elevate ambient humidity inside the crawl space to levels that sustain mold growth for weeks.

## Our Mold Remediation Process in Riverside

Every project starts with containment, not cleaning. Before any disturbed mold spores can migrate to unaffected rooms, the crew establishes negative air pressure in the work zone using HEPA-filtered air scrubbers. This step is non-negotiable — skipping it turns a localized problem into a whole-house problem.

From there, the process follows a structured sequence:

- **Moisture mapping** — thermal imaging and calibrated moisture meters locate the full extent of saturation, including areas behind tile, inside wall cavities, and beneath flooring. In Riverside's older plaster-wall homes, surface readings can look acceptable while the wood lath behind them stays wet.
- **Controlled demolition** — only material that cannot be effectively remediated is removed. Porous materials like drywall, insulation, and carpet padding that test above acceptable thresholds are bagged and disposed of per California guidelines.
- **HEPA vacuuming and antimicrobial treatment** — structural surfaces are HEPA-vacuumed, then treated with EPA-registered antimicrobial agents appropriate to the substrate.
- **Drying and verification** — after remediation, affected areas are dried to manufacturer and IICRC S520 standard thresholds before reconstruction begins.
- **Post-remediation verification** — clearance testing by an independent industrial hygienist confirms spore counts are within acceptable limits before the containment comes down.
