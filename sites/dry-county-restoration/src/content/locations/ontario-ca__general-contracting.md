Ontario sits at the crossroads of the Inland Empire's rapid growth and its older residential fabric — a combination that makes renovation and remodeling work here genuinely different from a straightforward suburban buildout. Homes that predate the city's post-war expansion often carry original plumbing, knob-and-tube-era electrical panels, and stucco exteriors that respond poorly to moisture intrusion from the Santa Ana winds and occasional atmospheric river events. Dry County Restoration handles the full arc of renovation and general contracting in Ontario, from post-damage rebuilds to planned kitchen and bathroom remodels, with a crew that understands what's behind these walls before the first cut is made.

## Why Ontario's Building Stock Shapes Every Remodel

Ontario's residential landscape spans roughly a century of construction. Neighborhoods developed in the mid-20th century frequently feature concrete slab foundations with embedded copper supply lines — a configuration that looks solid until a pinhole leak has been wicking moisture into the slab for months before anyone notices. When a remodel uncovers that kind of hidden damage, the scope changes fast. The region's clay-heavy soils also shift seasonally, which can stress slab edges and cause hairline cracks that complicate flooring and tile work if they're not addressed structurally before finish materials go down.

The Inland Empire's dry heat accelerates wood shrinkage and paint failure on exterior surfaces, while the occasional heavy rain — concentrated in a short winter window — can overwhelm older drainage and push water into crawl spaces or against foundations. A renovation plan that doesn't account for those climate swings tends to need repairs within a few years. That's a pattern worth interrupting at the design stage.

## Our Renovation and General Contracting Process in Ontario

Every project starts with a scope walk — not a sales pitch. We look at what's actually there: the condition of existing framing, the age and capacity of the electrical panel, whether the plumbing is copper, galvanized, or CPVC, and how the subfloor is holding up. For post-damage rebuilds, that walk is informed by the remediation documentation, so we're not guessing at what was affected.

From there, the process moves through permitting, material selection, and phased construction in a sequence that keeps the home livable where possible. Ontario falls under San Bernardino County building authority for unincorporated parcels and the City of Ontario's Building and Safety Division for incorporated areas — knowing which jurisdiction applies before pulling permits saves weeks. Inspections are scheduled to align with trade sequencing so framing, rough electrical, and rough plumbing aren't waiting on each other.

Kitchen and bathroom remodels get particular attention to ventilation — Ontario's interior climate means inadequate exhaust in a bathroom creates a reliable mold-growth environment within a season or two. We spec ventilation to actual room volume, not just the minimum the code requires.

## Coordinating with Insurance Carriers on Post-Damage Rebuilds

When a renovation follows a covered loss — fire, water, or storm damage — the rebuild scope has to align with the insurance carrier's estimate. That alignment is easier when the contractor has been involved since the remediation phase, which is exactly how Dry County Restoration is structured. We document existing conditions, photograph affected materials, and communicate directly with adjusters so the rebuild scope doesn't get trimmed because of paperwork gaps.

For homeowners navigating a claim while also trying to upgrade finishes during the rebuild, we help separate what the carrier covers from what the homeowner is electing to upgrade — a distinction that matters for both the claim and the final invoice.

## Local Note

Ontario's older residential corridors — particularly homes built during the postwar tract-housing era — frequently used a stucco-over-wire-lath exterior system that was common throughout the Inland Empire. When water gets behind that assembly, either from a failing roof-to-wall transition or from a window that's been re-caulked over a failed original seal, the damage can be extensive before it's visible from inside. During any exterior-adjacent remodel, we probe those assemblies before closing them back up. Skipping that step is how a bathroom remodel turns into a full exterior wall replacement six months later.
