## Why Norco Properties Face Distinct Fire Damage Challenges

Norco sits in western Riverside County at an elevation that channels the Santa Ana winds with particular force during fall and winter. Those offshore wind events drop relative humidity into the single digits and push embers across properties with the kind of fuel load — dry grass, straw, wood-rail fencing — that accelerates char and smoke spread well beyond the point of ignition. Even a fire that starts and ends in one room can leave smoke residue throughout a structure when a Santa Ana event is pulling air through every gap in the building envelope.

The housing stock adds another layer. Many Norco homes were built in the 1960s through the 1980s, when wood-framed construction with minimal fire blocking was standard. Older framing cavities allow smoke and heat to travel vertically and horizontally through walls long after the visible flame is out. Attic spaces in ranch-style homes on larger lots often span the full footprint of the structure, meaning smoke contamination in the attic can be as severe as in the living areas — and it's the last place a homeowner thinks to check.

## Our Fire Damage Restoration Process in Norco

The first thing a crew does on arrival is a full structural assessment — not just the burn area, but every space smoke could have reached. In Norco's single-story ranch homes, that means pulling attic access and checking insulation, rafters, and any stored materials above the ceiling plane. Smoke follows air, and air in a ranch-style home moves freely from eave to eave.

From there, the process moves through several defined stages:

- **Emergency board-up and tarping** to secure the structure against weather and unauthorized entry, especially critical on larger lots where the property may not be immediately visible from the street.
- **Smoke and soot removal** from all affected surfaces, using dry sponging on porous materials and chemical sponges on finished surfaces — the method depends on the type of soot (wet, dry, or protein-based) and the substrate.
- **Odor neutralization** using hydroxyl generators and thermal fogging, which penetrate wall cavities and HVAC ductwork where smoke odor embeds itself.
- **Structural drying and stabilization** if firefighting water has saturated framing, insulation, or flooring — a common secondary damage issue that gets missed when the focus stays on the fire itself.
- **Controlled demolition and debris removal** of materials that cannot be restored, followed by documentation for your insurance claim.

All drying and remediation work follows IICRC standards, and the team holds current IICRC certification as a firm.
