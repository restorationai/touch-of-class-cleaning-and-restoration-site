## Why Chino Hills Properties See Mold Issues

Chino Hills was largely developed from the late 1980s through the 2000s, which means the dominant housing stock is wood-framed construction with OSB sheathing, paper-faced drywall, and engineered lumber — materials that absorb moisture readily and provide an ideal cellulose food source for mold colonies. Unlike older masonry or plaster construction, these materials can show surface mold within days of saturation and harbor hidden growth behind vapor barriers long before a musty odor becomes obvious.

The city's position along the Chino Hills State Park corridor also matters. Properties backing up to the park-adjacent hillsides can experience soil moisture intrusion through slab perimeters and stem walls during and after the rainy season, particularly when El Niño patterns push above-average precipitation into San Bernardino County. Crawl space and subfloor mold is a recurring pattern in homes on sloped lots where grading directs runoff toward the foundation rather than away from it.

HVAC systems are another consistent source. Chino Hills summers push air conditioning systems to run long cycles, generating condensate that drains through lines prone to algae blockage. A clogged condensate line can overflow into wall cavities or ceiling assemblies quietly for weeks before anyone notices discoloration on drywall or a persistent musty smell in a bedroom.

## Our Mold Remediation Process in Chino Hills

Every remediation starts with containment — not cleaning. Before a single spore-bearing surface is disturbed, the affected area is isolated with 6-mil poly sheeting and negative air pressure is established using HEPA-filtered air scrubbers. This step is non-negotiable because disturbing mold without containment aerosolizes spores into living areas and HVAC return pathways.

From there, the process follows the IICRC S520 standard for mold remediation:

- **Assessment and moisture mapping** — thermal imaging and moisture meters locate the full extent of saturation, including hidden pockets behind cabinets, inside wall cavities, and beneath flooring.
- **Controlled demolition** — affected drywall, insulation, and flooring materials are removed to clean, unaffected substrate. Material is double-bagged and disposed of per California regulations.
- **HEPA vacuuming and antimicrobial treatment** — structural surfaces are vacuumed and treated. No encapsulant is applied over active mold growth.
- **Structural drying** — if the underlying moisture source has been corrected, drying equipment is deployed to bring framing and substrate to target moisture content before any rebuild begins.
- **Clearance verification** — post-remediation air and surface sampling confirms the work area meets clearance criteria before containment is removed.
