## Why Santa Ana Properties See Duct Issues

The Santa Ana winds are the most obvious culprit, but they're not the only one. When those offshore gusts arrive — typically October through February — they carry fine clay-rich soil particles that infiltrate homes through gaps around windows, doors, and return-air grilles. Older housing stock in Santa Ana, much of it built in the postwar decades of the 1950s and 1960s, often has original ductwork that was never designed to be easily sealed or accessed. Flex duct added during later renovations can sag and pool debris at low points. The result is a system that circulates the same trapped dust, pollen, and particulate matter every time the thermostat calls for cooling.

Santa Ana also sits in an air basin the South Coast AQMD monitors closely for PM2.5 and ozone. On high-pollution days, residents close windows and run HVAC systems longer — which means the duct system is the primary filter for everything in the indoor air. A dirty duct system on a Spare the Air day isn't just an odor problem; it's a comfort and air quality problem.

## Our Air Duct Cleaning Process in Santa Ana

The process starts with a camera inspection of the main trunk lines and branch runs before any equipment is connected. This matters in Santa Ana's older housing stock because original galvanized ducts sometimes have loose joints or deteriorated insulation lining that a blind cleaning could dislodge and spread. Once we've confirmed duct integrity, we connect a high-powered negative-pressure vacuum to the main trunk and work section by section with agitation tools — dislodging debris toward the collection point rather than pushing it deeper into the system.

For systems with visible biological growth or persistent odor after mechanical cleaning, we offer EPA-registered duct sanitization applied as a fine mist after the debris removal is complete. Dryer vent cleaning is handled as a separate line item — we run a rotary brush through the full length of the exhaust run and verify airflow at the exterior termination, which is especially important in Santa Ana's warmer climate where lint-clogged dryer vents are a genuine fire risk during high-heat months.

## Local Note

Santa Ana has a significant share of mid-century homes that were retrofitted with central HVAC systems in the 1970s and 1980s — often using flexible duct material that's now 40-plus years old. That older flex duct is prone to interior liner deterioration: the metallic inner layer can flake, and the fiberglass insulation batting around it can compress and collapse at bends. Before committing to a standard cleaning on a home of that era, it's worth asking the technician to camera-inspect the flex runs first. In some cases, cleaning a severely degraded flex section does more harm than good, and replacement is the better call — something we'll tell you honestly before we start the job.

If you're in a newer construction or a recently renovated property, the picture is different: modern duct board and sealed metal trunk systems respond well to routine cleaning and can go several years between services when combined with quality filtration.
