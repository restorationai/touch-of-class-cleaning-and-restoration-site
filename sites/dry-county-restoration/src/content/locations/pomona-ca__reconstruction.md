**Rebuilding after fire, water, or storm damage in Pomona takes more than a fast framing crew.** Dry County Restoration manages post-damage reconstruction in Pomona from the structural drying and demolition phase through final paint and trim, so property owners deal with one licensed team instead of juggling separate contractors for every stage of the rebuild.

## Why Pomona Properties Need Specialized Reconstruction

Pomona's housing stock spans several eras, from older bungalows near the city core to mid-century tract homes built during the Inland Valley's postwar growth and newer construction further out toward the foothills. Older homes often hide original lath-and-plaster walls, undersized electrical panels, or cast iron plumbing behind newer finishes, which changes how a reconstruction crew approaches demolition and what gets flagged for upgrade during permitting. The region's clay-heavy soil also shifts with seasonal moisture swings, which can translate into cracked slabs or settled foundations that complicate rebuild work after a water loss. Add in the dry Santa Ana wind pattern that raises wildfire exposure near the hillside areas east of the city, and reconstruction here has to account for both water-driven and fire-driven damage patterns in the same service area.

## Our Reconstruction Services Process in Pomona

We start every reconstruction project with a damage assessment that separates what can be dried, cleaned, or repaired from what has to come out entirely. For fire damage reconstruction, that means testing framing members for structural integrity before any rebuild begins, not just clearing visible char. For water-related rebuilds, we document moisture readings in subfloor and wall cavities so the reconstruction scope matches what the materials actually absorbed, rather than guessing at how far damage traveled. Once demolition is complete, our crews handle framing, drywall, flooring, and finish work, coordinating inspections with the city's permitting process along the way so the job closes out cleanly instead of stalling on paperwork. Because our team carries IICRC training across water, structural drying, and mold disciplines, the same crew that mitigated the original loss can often carry the project straight into rebuild without a second company re-assessing the damage from scratch.

## Pomona Insurance Coordination

Most reconstruction jobs in Pomona start as an insurance claim, and the gap between what mitigation covers and what rebuild actually costs is where projects get delayed. We document the original damage, the demolition scope, and the materials going back in with enough detail that adjusters can match the rebuild estimate to what the loss actually required. For older Pomona properties, that documentation matters even more, since carriers sometimes push back on code-required upgrades, like bringing electrical or plumbing up to current standard, that weren't part of the original construction. We flag those items early so the conversation with the adjuster happens before drywall goes back up, not after.

## Local note

A detail that catches a lot of Pomona homeowners off guard: properties built before the 1970s in this area frequently have undersized electrical service, sometimes 60-amp panels, that won't support the circuit load required for a modern kitchen or bathroom rebuild. When a fire or water loss forces a full reconstruction of those spaces, the permitting process typically requires the panel to be upgraded as part of the job, even if the panel itself wasn't damaged. We check this during the initial walkthrough so it's priced into the estimate from day one instead of surfacing as a surprise mid-project.
