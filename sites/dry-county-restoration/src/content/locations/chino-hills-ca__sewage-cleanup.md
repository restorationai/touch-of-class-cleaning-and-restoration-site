## Why Chino Hills Properties See Sewage Backup Issues

The topography that makes Chino Hills scenic is the same reason its sewer system works harder than those in neighboring flatland cities. Lateral lines running downhill through expansive clay soils are prone to root intrusion from the mature oaks and ornamental trees common in the city's hillside tracts. When roots crack a lateral or sediment bridges a low spot in an aging line, the backup doesn't trickle — it surges.

Chino Hills was largely developed in waves from the late 1970s through the early 2000s, meaning a significant portion of the housing stock is now old enough that original ABS plastic drain lines are showing stress fractures, and wax seals and cleanout caps are past their service life. Homes on steeper grades also experience higher pressure on fittings during heavy rain events, when groundwater infiltrates cracked laterals and overwhelms the line's capacity. The result is sewage appearing at the lowest fixture in the house — often a ground-floor toilet, floor drain, or utility sink — with little warning.

Septic systems are less common here than in the unincorporated county areas to the east, but they do exist on larger parcels toward the city's rural edges. A failing septic tank or a saturated drain field after a wet winter can push effluent toward the structure just as aggressively as a municipal backup.

## Our Sewage Cleanup and Sanitization Process in Chino Hills

Raw sewage is classified as Category 3 water — the most contaminated type — regardless of whether it originates from a municipal sewer or a private septic system. The IICRC S500 standard governs how that material is handled, and our IICRC-certified team follows that protocol on every job.

The process moves in a defined sequence:

1. **Containment** — we isolate the affected area to prevent cross-contamination to adjacent rooms, using physical barriers and negative air pressure where the layout requires it.
2. **Extraction** — truck-mounted extraction equipment removes standing sewage and saturated debris. Porous materials — drywall, insulation, carpet, subfloor — that have contacted Category 3 water are removed rather than dried in place, because drying does not render them safe.
3. **Surface cleaning and disinfection** — structural surfaces are cleaned with EPA-registered disinfectants rated for sewage pathogens. This step is not optional and cannot be shortened.
4. **Structural drying** — commercial desiccant and refrigerant dehumidifiers, along with high-velocity air movers, bring framing and sheathing to target moisture content before any reconstruction begins. Our IICRC ASD-certified technicians monitor readings daily.
5. **Clearance documentation** — moisture readings and a written scope are provided to the homeowner and, when applicable, the insurance carrier.

In Chino Hills developments built on graded hillside pads — particularly those where the original rough grading compacted the soil unevenly — settling over the decades can create low points in underground drain lines that weren't part of the original design.
