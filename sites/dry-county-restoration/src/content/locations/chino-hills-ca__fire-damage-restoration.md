## Why Chino Hills Properties Are Particularly Vulnerable to Fire Damage

Chino Hills is classified as a Very High Fire Hazard Severity Zone by the California Department of Forestry and Fire Protection — a designation that shapes everything from brush-clearance ordinances to the way homeowners insurance is written here. The city's rolling terrain means that fires can crown over ridgelines and expose homes on multiple sides simultaneously, leaving behind a complex mix of structural char, smoke penetration, and heat-warped materials that varies dramatically from room to room.

The housing stock adds another layer of complexity. Much of Chino Hills was developed in the 1980s and 1990s, when wood-frame construction with stucco exteriors was the dominant build type. That era of construction often included open-truss attic systems that allow smoke to migrate freely from one end of a home to the other, depositing oily soot on insulation, HVAC ductwork, and framing members that aren't visible from the living space. A room that looks lightly affected at eye level may have heavily contaminated attic space directly above it.

## Our Fire Damage Restoration Process in Chino Hills

Every fire loss is different, but the sequence of decisions matters. When we arrive at a Chino Hills property, the first priority is a safety and structural assessment — confirming that the roof deck, load-bearing walls, and floor systems are stable before any crew member enters a compromised area. We document the full scope with photographs and moisture readings before a single item is moved, because that documentation is what your insurance adjuster will rely on.

From there, the process moves through board-up and tarping of any breached openings (critical in Chino Hills, where dry Santa Ana conditions can push additional particulate into an open structure overnight), debris removal, and the beginning of smoke and soot cleaning. Smoke residue behaves differently depending on what burned: synthetic materials leave a wet, smearing soot that requires chemical sponges and specific cleaning agents, while wood and paper fires leave a dry soot that can be vacuumed first. We calibrate the cleaning method to the residue type rather than using a one-size approach.

Odor control follows cleaning. Thermal fogging and hydroxyl generation are both in our equipment inventory, and the choice depends on the structure's ventilation and occupancy status. HEPA air scrubbers run continuously through the affected areas until air quality readings confirm particulate levels are within acceptable range.

## Local Note

Homes built in Chino Hills during the late 1980s and early 1990s frequently have HVAC systems with ducting that runs through unconditioned attic space. After a fire — even one contained to a single room — smoke particulates can be drawn into the return air pathway and deposited throughout the duct network. If the HVAC system runs before the ducts are inspected and cleaned, it redistributes soot and odor to every room in the house. We flag this on every Chino Hills fire loss and coordinate duct inspection before any system is restarted, because missing that step means the odor problem comes back within days of the homeowner moving back in.
