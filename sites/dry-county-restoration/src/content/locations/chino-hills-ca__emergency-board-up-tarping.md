## Why Chino Hills Properties Face Distinct Board-Up and Tarping Risks

Chino Hills was developed rapidly through the late 1980s and 1990s, which means a large portion of the city's housing stock shares similar construction characteristics: wood-frame stucco exteriors, tile roofs over lightweight trusses, and attached garages that are often the most vulnerable point of entry after a collision or fire. The hills themselves create microclimates. Board-up and tarping in this context isn't just about covering a broken window — it's about sealing the structure against ember intrusion and wind-driven debris while the fire investigation or insurance inspection is still pending.

## Our Emergency Board-Up and Tarping Process in Chino Hills

When a call comes in, the first priority is a rapid site assessment to identify every breach — not just the obvious one. A broken window from a vehicle impact often means a compromised door frame as well. Fire damage to a soffit can leave the attic open to weather even if the main walls look intact. The crew walks the full perimeter before any material goes up.

For window and door openings, we cut and install structural-grade plywood, secured with screws into the framing rather than adhesive or staples, so the barrier holds against wind load. For roof damage, we use reinforced polyethylene tarps rated for UV exposure and wind, weighted and fastened with batten boards rather than simple tie-downs — a method that matters on the exposed ridgelines common to Chino Hills's hillside lots, where a poorly anchored tarp becomes a sail by morning.

We document every opening, every material used, and every fastener point with photographs before we leave. That documentation goes directly to your insurance adjuster and is formatted to align with standard carrier requirements for emergency mitigation claims.
