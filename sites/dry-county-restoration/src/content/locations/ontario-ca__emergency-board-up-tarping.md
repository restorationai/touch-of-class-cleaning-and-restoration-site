## Our Emergency Board-Up and Tarping Process in Ontario

When we arrive on site, the first step is a rapid exterior assessment — identifying every breach point before any material goes up. A visible broken front window is rarely the only opening; fire and wind damage frequently compromise soffit vents, attic access panels, and garage door tracks that aren't obvious from the street.

For window and door openings, we use 5/8-inch OSB secured with structural screws into the framing, not just the window trim. In Ontario's wind environment, a board fastened only to trim can pull free in a single strong gust. For roof damage, we use reinforced polyethylene tarps with a minimum 6-mil thickness, weighted with sandbags and anchored with cap nails driven into undamaged decking — never stapled to compromised material. Ridge-to-eave coverage is standard; partial tarps that leave exposed seams are a liability when a Pacific storm system moves through the Inland Empire.

If the structure has fire damage, we photograph every breach point before boarding, creating a documented record that feeds directly into the insurance claim file.
