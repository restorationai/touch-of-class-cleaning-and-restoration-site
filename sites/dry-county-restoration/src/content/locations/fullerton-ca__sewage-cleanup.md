## Why Fullerton Properties See Sewage Backup Issues

Fullerton's residential neighborhoods include a significant share of homes built between the 1940s and 1960s, many of which still have original clay or Orangeburg sewer laterals connecting the house to the city main. These materials degrade over time — clay joints shift with soil movement, and Orangeburg (a compressed wood-fiber pipe once common in postwar construction) softens and collapses as it ages. Add Fullerton's mature urban tree canopy, and root intrusion becomes a near-constant pressure on those older lines.

The city also sits in an area where the soil transitions between sandy alluvial fill near the flatlands and denser hillside soils toward the Coyote Hills area to the south. That variation in soil composition affects how lateral pipes settle and shift over decades. A line that was installed level in 1958 may now have low spots where solids accumulate and eventually cause a blockage — and when that blockage gives way, the backup can be sudden and significant.

Septic systems are less common in Fullerton's core residential areas, but properties on larger lots near the city's edges occasionally have older systems that haven't been transitioned to city sewer. Septic overflow on those properties carries its own set of cleanup and permitting considerations.

## Our Sewage Cleanup and Sanitization Process in Fullerton

Sewage cleanup is not the same as water damage cleanup — the contamination level demands a stricter protocol. Our IICRC-certified team follows a defined sequence that treats the job as a biohazard event from the moment we arrive.

**Containment and safety assessment.** We establish a containment boundary to prevent cross-contamination to unaffected areas of the home. Crew members work in appropriate PPE throughout.

**Extraction and solid removal.** Standing sewage and solid waste are extracted and removed from the structure. This includes material that has wicked into flooring, subfloor, and wall cavities — not just what's visible on the surface.

**Demolition of unsalvageable materials.** Porous materials — drywall, insulation, carpet, pad — that have absorbed Category 3 water are typically removed rather than dried in place. Attempting to dry sewage-saturated drywall is not a safe or effective approach, and in Fullerton's older homes where wall cavities may contain original insulation, we often find contamination has traveled further than the visible damage suggests.

**Hospital-grade disinfection and deodorization.** After extraction and demo, all affected surfaces are treated with EPA-registered disinfectants. We address subfloor, framing, and concrete slab surfaces as needed. Odor neutralization is part of this step — not a final spray at the end, but an integrated part of the sanitization process.

**Drying and documentation.** Once the space is clean and sanitized, we dry the structure to IICRC S500 standards and document moisture readings throughout. That documentation matters for insurance claims and for confirming the space is safe before reconstruction begins.

## Fullerton Insurance & HOA Coordination

Sewage backups are frequently covered under homeowners insurance as a sudden and accidental loss, though coverage depends on your specific policy and whether you carry sewer backup endorsement coverage — a separate rider that many Fullerton homeowners don't realize they need until after an event. We document the loss thoroughly: photos, moisture readings, affected material logs, and a written scope of work. That documentation is what your adjuster needs to process the claim efficiently.

For Fullerton properties in HOA-governed communities, the question of who is responsible — the homeowner or the association — often depends on where the blockage occurred. A clog in the shared main line is typically the HOA's responsibility; a clog in the lateral between your home and the main is usually the homeowner's. We can help you document the source of the backup, which matters when that conversation with your HOA board or management company happens.
