## Why Silverado Properties See Fire Damage Issues

Silverado sits in a wildland-urban interface zone, tucked into the Santa Ana Mountains with dense chaparral right up against property lines. That terrain means two different fire exposures: interior fires (electrical, cooking, heating) in homes that are often older cabin-style construction with wood siding and exposed beams, and exterior exposure from wildfire embers during Santa Ana wind events. Wood-frame construction with minimal firebreak clearance absorbs smoke odor into exposed structural members in a way that drywall-and-stucco homes don't. Many properties also rely on propane heating or wood stoves, which adds a secondary fire-risk pattern we see less often in more urbanized parts of the county.

## Our Fire Damage Restoration Process in Silverado

We start with a damage assessment that separates char and structural loss from smoke and soot migration, since canyon homes with open-beam ceilings and knotty pine interiors tend to show heavier odor penetration than drywall construction even when the visible char is limited to one room. Soot removal comes next, using dry sponging and HEPA vacuuming on porous wood surfaces before any wet cleaning, because wetting soot-covered wood too early can drive residue deeper into the grain. Odor control follows with thermal fogging or ozone treatment depending on the material, and we document everything with photos and moisture or soot readings for the insurance file. Reconstruction, when needed, is scheduled once the structure has passed clearance.
