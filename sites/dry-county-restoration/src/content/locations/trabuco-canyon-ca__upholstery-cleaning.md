## Why Trabuco Canyon Upholstery Soils Differently

Trabuco Canyon sits in the unincorporated foothills of Orange County, and that geography matters for fabric care. Homes tucked against open space or bordering equestrian trails deal with windborne dust and dander that settles into upholstery even when doors and windows stay shut. Many properties in the canyon also rely on well water or septic systems rather than municipal utilities, which can mean harder water reaching the home and, occasionally, mineral residue that shows up as dulling on lighter fabric after a DIY spot-clean gone sideways. And because the canyon sees real fire-season smoke drift some years, ash particulate can work into cushion foam and fiber long before a homeowner notices the smell.

## Our Upholstery Cleaning Process

We start by identifying the fabric or leather type, since canyon homes run the gamut from rustic leather sectionals to lighter linen-blend pieces that show soiling fast. A dry pre-vacuum pulls loose dust and dander out before any moisture touches the piece, which matters more here than in a typical suburban job given how much fine particulate these homes accumulate between cleanings. From there we use hot water extraction for most fabric upholstery, calibrated to a lower moisture setting than a standard carpet job so cushions don't stay damp in canyon humidity swings. Leather gets a separate conditioning step rather than extraction, since saturating leather in a dry climate can crack the finish over time. We finish with controlled air movement to bring pieces back to a safe moisture level before furniture goes back into use, following IICRC-referenced drying practices throughout.
