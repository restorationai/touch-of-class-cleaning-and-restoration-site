## Why Silverado Properties See Smoke Damage Issues

Silverado's canyon geography means smoke doesn't just come from a fire on your own lot. Wind-driven wildfire smoke from the surrounding hillsides can blanket a neighborhood for days, working its way through attic vents, window seams, and HVAC returns long before any flame is visible from the road. Many homes in the area are set back on sloped lots with older wood-frame construction and open-air crawlspaces, which gives smoke more surface area to cling to than a typical tract-home interior. Ash and soot also mix with canyon dust kicked up by Santa Ana winds, so what looks like a light gray film is often a stubborn combination of both.

## Our Smoke Damage Restoration Process in Silverado

We start with an assessment of how deep the smoke has traveled, not just what's visible on walls and ceilings. In canyon homes, that usually means checking attic insulation, HVAC ductwork, and any crawlspace venting before touching interior surfaces. From there we use HEPA air scrubbers and negative air machines to pull particulate out of the air while we work, since re-circulating smoke-laden air through an open house in a breezy canyon location undoes progress fast. Surfaces get cleaned with agitation and specialized smoke-residue solvents matched to the material, wood trim and cabinetry get treated differently than drywall or stucco. Thermal fogging and ozone or hydroxyl treatment handle the odor that clings to soft goods and wall cavities after visible soot is gone. We document every step with photos for the insurance file as we go, not after the fact.
