Chino Hills sits on a ridge between the Pomona Valley and the Chino Valley, and that geography shapes nearly every renovation project here. The hillside lots, clay-heavy soils, and HOA-governed planned communities that define so much of the city mean that a kitchen remodel or post-damage rebuild involves more planning layers than a comparable project in a flat, unincorporated suburb. Dry County Restoration holds Contractor License #1083555 and has been coordinating renovations, remodels, and general contracting work across the Inland Empire since 2020 — including the particular demands that come with building in Chino Hills.

## Why Chino Hills Homes Present Unique Renovation Challenges

Much of Chino Hills was developed rapidly through the 1980s and 1990s as master-planned tracts, which means large portions of the housing stock share the same construction era and the same potential weak points. Stucco exteriors over wood-frame walls were standard, and after decades of the region's hot, dry summers followed by occasional heavy El Niño winters, that stucco can develop hairline cracks that allow moisture intrusion behind the surface — damage that often doesn't show up until a bathroom remodel or kitchen renovation opens the wall cavity.

The clay-expansive soils common throughout the hills also mean that slab movement is a real consideration before any flooring, tile, or cabinetry project. A renovation that skips a slab assessment in this geology can end up with cracked tile grout within a season. We factor soil conditions into scope conversations before a single cabinet is ordered.

Chino Hills also sits in a high fire-hazard severity zone designated by CAL FIRE, which affects both the materials permitted for exterior renovations and the insurance landscape for homeowners undertaking post-damage rebuilds. Understanding those material restrictions upfront prevents costly change orders mid-project.

## Our Renovation and General Contracting Process in Chino Hills

Every project starts with a detailed walkthrough — not a quick estimate, but a room-by-room assessment that documents existing conditions, notes any moisture or structural anomalies behind finishes, and identifies permit requirements before demolition begins. Chino Hills falls under San Bernardino County's Building and Safety Division for permitting, and the county's review timelines and inspection scheduling are something we build into every project schedule from day one.

For post-damage rebuilds — homes that experienced water damage, fire, or mold and need reconstruction after remediation — we coordinate the transition from the mitigation phase directly into the rebuild phase. Because Dry County Restoration handles both sides of that process, there is no gap where a homeowner is left managing two separate contractors and two separate scopes of work.

Kitchen and bathroom remodels follow a sequenced trade schedule: demo, rough plumbing and electrical, inspections, insulation, drywall, tile, cabinetry, fixtures, and final finish work. Skipping or compressing that sequence to save time is where projects go sideways — especially in a city where HOA architectural review can require documentation at multiple stages.

## Chino Hills HOA Coordination

A significant share of Chino Hills is governed by homeowners associations, many of which have architectural review committees that must approve exterior changes, roofline alterations, window replacements, and sometimes even front-yard hardscape modifications. Interior remodels that don't change the exterior footprint typically bypass HOA review, but any project touching the outside of the structure — including post-fire or post-water-damage rebuilds that alter siding, roofing, or windows — usually requires submitted plans and written approval before work begins.

We prepare the documentation packages these committees typically request: scope descriptions, material specifications, and color or finish samples where required. Getting that submission right the first time matters because re-review cycles add weeks to a project timeline.

For homeowners working with an insurance carrier on a covered loss, we document the damage scope, photograph affected materials, and communicate directly with adjusters to align the approved repair scope with what the rebuild actually requires. That coordination keeps the project from stalling at the billing stage.

## Local Note

Chino Hills homes built on the steeper ridge lots — particularly those with daylight basements or split-level foundations — frequently have drainage systems that were designed to handle the original grading but have shifted as the clay soil has expanded and contracted over the years. Before any renovation that touches a lower-level space, it is worth having the perimeter drainage inspected. We have seen finished basement conversions interrupted mid-project by drainage issues that were invisible until the existing slab was opened. Catching that condition in the assessment phase costs far less than discovering it after new flooring is already down.
