## Why Silverado Properties See Water Damage Issues

The canyon setting itself is a factor. Homes tucked against hillsides or built close to the creek bed deal with runoff during winter storms, and septic systems common throughout the canyon can back up or saturate surrounding soil after heavy rain, adding a contamination layer to what might otherwise be a straightforward water intrusion. Well water systems, when a pressure tank or line fails, can dump water steadily for hours before anyone notices, especially in detached structures or lower levels that aren't used daily. Combine that with wood-frame construction from an earlier building era and you get a pattern we see often: slow leaks that go unnoticed until flooring cups or a musty smell shows up in a crawlspace or closet.

## Our Water Damage Restoration Process in Silverado

We start with extraction, pulling standing water with truck-mounted or portable units depending on vehicle access up the canyon roads, then move to moisture mapping. In older Silverado homes this step takes longer than average because we're checking behind plaster or beadboard paneling that wasn't designed with inspection ports in mind, and we're often checking crawlspace framing that sits closer to grade than code would allow today. Once we know where the water traveled, we set air movers and dehumidifiers calibrated to the room's actual materials and humidity load, not a generic drying schedule. We track moisture content daily against the IICRC S500 standard until readings confirm the structure is dry, not just dry to the touch.
