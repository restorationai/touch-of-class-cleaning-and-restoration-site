## Why Santa Ana Properties Face Distinct Fire Damage Challenges

Santa Ana's housing stock is one of the most varied in Orange County. The city's older residential corridors — many built between the 1920s and the 1960s — feature wood-lath plaster walls, knob-and-tube or early Romex wiring, and balloon-frame construction. In a fire, balloon framing is particularly unforgiving: because the wall cavities run continuously from the foundation to the roof without fire blocking, smoke and heat can travel vertically through the structure faster than in platform-frame homes built after the mid-century code changes. That means a fire that looks contained to one room may have deposited soot and odor-causing particles two floors away.

Santa Ana also sits in one of the densest urban cores in the county, with many properties on narrow lots and attached or semi-attached structures. Smoke migration to adjacent units is a real concern, and restoration scopes often need to account for shared walls, common attic spaces, and neighboring properties — not just the unit of origin.

## Our Fire Damage Restoration Process in Santa Ana

Every fire loss is different, but the sequence of work follows a logic that protects the structure and the people inside it.

**Stabilization first.** Before cleaning begins, the structure has to be safe to work in. That means boarding windows and doors if the fire compromised them, tarping any roof damage, and assessing structural integrity. We coordinate with the local fire marshal's clearance before re-entry when required.

**Soot and smoke mapping.** Soot is not uniform. Wet soot from a grease fire behaves differently than the dry, powdery residue from a fast-burning wood fire, and protein residue from cooking fires is nearly invisible but creates persistent odor. Our IICRC-certified technicians identify the soot type before selecting cleaning chemistry — the wrong approach can permanently set staining into surfaces.

**Content and structure cleaning.** Salvageable contents are inventoried and packed out for off-site cleaning. Structural surfaces — walls, ceilings, framing, subfloor — are cleaned using methods matched to the substrate. In older Santa Ana homes with original plaster, we work carefully: aggressive mechanical cleaning can damage a surface that is otherwise restorable.

**Odor neutralization.** Thermal fogging and hydroxyl generation reach into the same cavities that smoke traveled through. In homes with ducted HVAC systems, the ductwork itself often needs cleaning — smoke particles settle in supply and return runs and re-circulate every time the system runs.

**Moisture management.** Fire suppression water is a secondary loss that runs alongside the fire damage. We dry affected areas concurrently with smoke remediation so that mold does not establish in the weeks after the fire.

## Local Note

Santa Ana's older residential neighborhoods contain a significant number of homes with original single-pane steel-frame windows — a detail that matters after a fire. Steel frames conduct heat efficiently, and in a fire event the glass often fails while the frame itself warps but stays in place.
