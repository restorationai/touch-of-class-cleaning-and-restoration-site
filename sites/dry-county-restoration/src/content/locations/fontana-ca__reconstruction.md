Reconstruction Services from Dry County Restoration picks up where demolition and mitigation leave off, turning a gutted structure back into a livable, code-compliant home or building.

## Why Fontana Properties See Reconstruction Issues

Fontana's rapid build-out during the Inland Empire's growth boom means a lot of homes share the same framing, stucco, and roofing materials installed within a tight few decades, so when fire or water damage hits one property, it often hits a whole tract the same way. Many of these neighborhoods also sit near the wildland-urban interface along the foothills north of the city, where Santa Ana wind events can push fire into residential blocks with little warning. On the water-damage side, the valley's clay and alluvial soils expand and contract with the area's wet winters and dry summers, which stresses foundations and slab-on-grade construction that's common in the newer subdivisions. Add in the seismic backdrop of the broader San Bernardino Valley, close to the San Andreas Fault system, and you get a building stock that needs reconstruction work done with an eye toward how the structure will actually move and settle afterward, not just how it looks on move-in day.

## Our Reconstruction Services Process in Fontana

We start with a structural assessment that documents what mitigation and demolition already removed, then scope the rebuild against current code requirements enforced through the City of Fontana's building and safety division. Framing repairs come first, matched to the home's original construction where the structure allows it, followed by rough electrical, plumbing, and insulation work before drywall goes back up. Stucco and exterior finish matching gets particular attention in Fontana, since so many tract-built exteriors use a limited palette of textures and colors that are easy to mismatch if a crew isn't paying attention. We close out with a final walkthrough and documentation package your insurer or property manager can use for claim resolution.

## Local note

Fontana homes built during the 1990s tract boom frequently sit on expansive clay soils that shift seasonally, and it's common for cosmetic cracking to reappear within the first year after a rebuild if slab and foundation grading weren't rechecked before drywall closed everything up. We build that check into the process here rather than treating it as an afterthought, because a rebuild that looks clean at handoff but cracks again by next summer isn't actually finished.
