## Our Storm Damage Restoration Process in Chino

When a storm call comes in, the first priority is stopping ongoing exposure. That means emergency tarping of any breached roof section, boarding windows or doors that have failed, and identifying where water has already entered the structure. Thermal imaging helps locate moisture that has migrated behind stucco or drywall without visible surface signs — a common finding in Chino homes where stucco cladding can mask saturation in the sheathing beneath.

Once the structure is stabilized, the scope of damage is documented photographically and with moisture readings at multiple wall depths. Structural drying follows IICRC S500 and S520 standards, using commercial-grade desiccant dehumidifiers and high-velocity air movers calibrated to the material type and ambient conditions. Chino's typically low relative humidity in non-storm periods is actually an asset during drying — once the exterior is sealed, the dry inland air accelerates moisture extraction compared to coastal conditions.

Tree impact damage — a frequent result of the region's mature eucalyptus and palm trees giving way in wind events — is assessed for structural compromise before any interior work begins. If rafters, trusses, or load-bearing walls are involved, the scope is documented for the structural repair phase.
