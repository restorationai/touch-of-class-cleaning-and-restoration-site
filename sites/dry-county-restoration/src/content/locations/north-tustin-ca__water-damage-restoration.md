## Why North Tustin Properties See Water Damage Issues

A large share of North Tustin's housing stock dates to the postwar ranch-style building era, when copper and galvanized supply lines were standard and slab foundations were poured without the moisture barriers used in homes built after the 1980s. Decades later, those original pipes are a common source of slow leaks that go undetected under flooring for weeks. The area's hillside and foothill lots along the eastern edge of the county also mean drainage doesn't always behave the way it does on flat valley ground: runoff from a hard winter rain can pool against a downhill foundation wall long after the storm has passed, pushing moisture into crawlspaces and lower-level framing. Because North Tustin is unincorporated, there's no dedicated city stormwater department managing local drainage infrastructure the way an incorporated city would, which puts more responsibility on individual property grading and gutter systems.

## Our Water Damage Restoration Process in North Tustin

The first visit focuses on extraction and moisture mapping, not demolition. We pull standing water with truck-mount extractors, then use moisture meters and thermal imaging to find out how far water has traveled under flooring and behind baseboards, which matters more in a slab-foundation ranch home than in a home with a raised foundation and accessible subfloor. Structural drying follows the IICRC S500 standard: air movers and dehumidifiers are placed based on the drying chamber we've mapped out, not a one-size-fits-all setup, and we monitor daily until materials read dry rather than just surface-dry. Where plaster or lath construction is still present in older sections of a home, we account for the slower release rate of moisture from those materials before closing out the job.

## North Tustin Insurance & County Permitting Coordination

Because North Tustin falls under County of Orange jurisdiction rather than a separate municipal building department, any repair work that goes beyond drying and into structural reconstruction, like replacing drywall, flooring, or framing, gets permitted through the county rather than a city office. We document the loss with photos and moisture readings as we go so that paperwork lines up with what an adjuster expects to see, and we coordinate the permitting step directly with the county when reconstruction is part of the job rather than leaving that step for the homeowner to sort out afterward.
