## Why Fullerton Properties See Storm Damage Differently Than Newer Suburbs

Fullerton's housing stock spans nearly a century of construction, and that range matters when a storm rolls through. Craftsman bungalows and Spanish Colonial Revival homes built in the 1920s and 1930s — common in the older residential corridors near downtown — often have original wood-framed roofs with skip sheathing rather than modern plywood decking. When wind-driven rain gets under a clay tile or composition shingle, that skip sheathing soaks up moisture quickly and can stay wet long after the surface looks dry. Stucco exteriors, another fixture of mid-century Fullerton construction, can mask wall-cavity saturation entirely: the surface feels firm while the wood lath and framing behind it are already beginning to support mold growth.

Orange County's wet season is short but can be intense. Atmospheric river events — the kind that dropped several inches across the county in recent winters — overwhelm older storm drains and can push water back through foundation vents or low-profile garage doors. In flatter sections of the city where grade drainage is minimal, that water has nowhere to go quickly.

## Our Storm Damage Restoration Process in Fullerton

Every storm loss is different, but the sequence of decisions matters. When we arrive, the first priority is safety — downed power lines, compromised structural members, and gas line exposure from uprooted trees all have to be identified before any restoration work begins. We coordinate with Southern California Edison and SoCalGas when utility shutoffs are needed; trying to dry a structure while a live hazard is present wastes time and creates risk.

Once the site is safe, we document everything photographically and with moisture mapping before a single piece of material is moved. That documentation is what your insurance adjuster needs to process the claim accurately. From there, the process moves through debris removal, temporary weatherproofing (tarping, board-up, or emergency roof covering), structural drying with commercial desiccant and refrigerant dehumidifiers, and then controlled demolition of materials that cannot be saved — wet insulation, saturated drywall, or compromised sheathing.

As an IICRC Certified Firm with technicians holding Water Restoration Technician (WRT) and Applied Structural Drying (ASD) credentials, we dry to the IICRC S500 standard rather than stopping when surfaces feel dry to the touch.

## Fullerton Insurance and HOA Coordination

Storm damage claims in California are governed by the same Department of Insurance framework as the rest of the state, but local factors shape how claims play out. Older Fullerton homes sometimes carry policies with older replacement-cost schedules that undervalue clay tile roofing or original hardwood flooring — materials that are genuinely expensive to source and install today. ## Local Note

Fullerton's mature tree canopy — particularly the large Canary Island palms and California pepper trees planted along residential streets in older parts of the city — becomes a significant liability in high-wind events. These trees have extensive root systems that can heave sidewalks and undermine foundation drainage over decades. When a large tree falls against a structure, the root ball pulling up often damages underground drainage lines at the same time the above-ground impact damages the roof or wall. We scope both the visible damage and the below-grade drainage path before closing out a storm loss, because a repaired roof over a broken drain line is a mold problem waiting to happen.
