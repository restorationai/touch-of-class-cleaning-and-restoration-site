## Why Chino Properties Are Particularly Vulnerable After a Loss

Chino's housing stock spans several decades and building eras. The city's older residential neighborhoods — many developed in the 1960s and 1970s — feature wood-frame construction with stucco exteriors that can look intact from the street while concealing compromised sheathing underneath. After a fire, stucco often holds its shape even when the underlying framing has burned away, which means a structure can appear more stable than it actually is. That matters for board-up work: securing an opening in a fire-damaged stucco wall requires assessing what the fasteners are actually anchoring into, not just what's visible on the surface.

Chino also borders active agricultural and industrial zones to the south and east, and wind patterns across those open areas accelerate during Santa Ana events. A tarp installed without proper ballasting and edge-sealing can become a sail within hours during those conditions — something a crew unfamiliar with Inland Empire wind behavior may not anticipate.

## Our Emergency Board-Up and Tarping Process in Chino

When you call, we gather basic information about the structure — single-family home, multi-unit, commercial — and the nature of the opening: fire-damaged wall, broken storefront glass, failed roof section, or vehicle impact. That lets us load the right materials before we leave Corona.

On arrival, the first step is a rapid structural assessment. We identify load-bearing elements near the breach, check for active hazards (smoldering material, compromised framing, downed utility lines), and photograph everything before touching the structure. Documentation matters because it becomes part of your insurance claim file.

For window and door openings, we use OSB panels cut to fit and secured with structural screws into solid framing — not just into drywall or stucco. For roof breaches, we use heavy-duty polyethylene tarps with cap nailing and weighted edges, extended well past the damaged area so wind-driven rain cannot work under the edges. On pitched roofs common in Chino's tract neighborhoods, we anchor tarps to ridge lines where possible rather than relying solely on perimeter fastening.
