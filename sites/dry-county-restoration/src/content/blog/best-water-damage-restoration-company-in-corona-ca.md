## What to Look for When Choosing a Water Damage Company in Corona

The most important credentials to verify before you hire any water damage restoration company are IICRC certification, a California contractor license, and proof of insurance. Those three things protect you if the work is done wrong or if a worker is injured on your property.

**IICRC certification** means the technicians were trained to a published standard. For water damage, the relevant certifications are WRT (Water Damage Restoration Technician) and ASD (Applied Structural Drying). A company can claim IICRC without holding current credentials, so ask for the certification number and verify it at [iicrc.org](https://www.iicrc.org).

**California contractor license** is required for any restoration work that touches the structure of a building. You can verify a license number in seconds at the California Contractors State License Board (CSLB) website. If a company cannot provide a license number, that is a hard stop.

**24/7 availability** matters because water damage compounds fast. The [IICRC S500](https://www.iicrc.org) defines drying timelines in hours, not days. Mold can begin colonizing wet materials within 24 to 48 hours under the right conditions. A company that cannot respond until Monday morning on a Friday-night loss is not the right call for an active water emergency.

**Insurance documentation support** is a practical differentiator. Restoration companies that work regularly with insurance claims know how to write a scope of work, document moisture readings, and communicate with adjusters. That saves you hours of back-and-forth and reduces the risk of a denied or underpaid claim. ## What Happens During a Water Damage Restoration Job in Corona?

A professional water damage restoration job follows a defined sequence. Understanding the phases helps you know what to expect and what to ask about.

1. **Emergency contact and dispatch.** You call. A technician confirms the loss type, asks about standing water and the water source, and dispatches a crew.
2. **Inspection and moisture mapping.** On arrival, the crew uses thermal imaging cameras and moisture meters to map the extent of the damage, including hidden moisture inside walls and under flooring.
3. **Water extraction.** Truck-mounted or portable extractors remove standing water. This is the fastest phase and the most critical for limiting damage.
4. **Drying setup.** Blue axial air movers and LGR dehumidifiers are staged to create airflow across wet surfaces and pull moisture from the air. Drying targets are set based on the material type and initial moisture readings.
5. **Monitoring.** A technician returns daily (or as required by the drying plan) to log moisture readings and adjust equipment placement. Documentation goes to the insurance file.
6. **Demolition if needed.** Saturated drywall, flooring, and insulation that cannot be dried in place are removed. The [EPA recommends](https://www.epa.gov/mold) removing porous materials that have been wet for more than 48 hours if mold is suspected.
7. **Clearance and rebuild.** Once materials reach dry standard, equipment is removed and the rebuild phase begins. A contractor handles repairs, drywall, flooring, and paint.
