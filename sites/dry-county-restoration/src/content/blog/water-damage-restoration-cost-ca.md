## How Much Does Water Damage Restoration Cost in California?

In California, water damage restoration typically costs $1,500 to $15,000 for a single-family home, with the statewide average landing around $3,500 to $5,500 for a mid-size loss. Small, clean-water jobs (a dishwasher line, a supply hose) on the lower end of that range can sometimes come in under $2,000 if caught within the first few hours. Large losses involving multiple rooms, contaminated water, or delayed discovery push past $10,000 quickly.

Here is what specific scenarios typically cost across California:

## What Factors Move the Cost Up or Down?

Four things drive the final number more than anything else: the category of water, the class of loss, the materials affected, and how fast you responded.

**Water category** is the single biggest cost driver. The [IICRC S500 standard](https://www.iicrc.org) defines three categories:

- **Category 1** (clean water from a supply line or appliance) is the least expensive to remediate. No special PPE, no biocide treatments, lower disposal costs.
- **Category 2** (gray water from washing machines, dishwashers, toilet overflows without feces) requires antimicrobial treatment and more protective equipment.
- **Category 3** (black water: sewage, floodwater, or any water that has sat long enough to grow bacteria) carries the highest remediation cost. Full containment, sealed Tyvek PPE, biocide application, and controlled demolition of porous materials are standard.

**Class of loss** describes how deeply water has penetrated. Class 1 (surface wet only) dries fast. Class 4 (water absorbed into concrete, hardwood, or plaster) requires specialty drying techniques and significantly more equipment-days.

**Materials affected** change the rebuild cost dramatically. Wet carpet and pad: relatively cheap to remove and replace. Wet engineered hardwood: often unsalvageable. Wet drywall with insulation behind it: must come out to prevent mold. Wet cabinetry, tile backer, or subfloor: costs climb fast.

**Response time** is the variable homeowners control. The [EPA notes](https://www.epa.gov/mold) that mold can begin growing on wet materials within 24 to 48 hours. A job called in at hour two is a drying job. The same job called in at day three is a drying job plus mold remediation, which can double the total cost.

Other factors that affect the final number in California specifically:

- **Labor rates**: Southern California labor runs 10-20% higher than the state's rural averages.
- **Permits**: Structural repairs in Corona and surrounding Riverside County cities may require a permit for drywall replacement or electrical work touched by water.
- **Contents**: Furniture, electronics, and personal property moved, dried, or stored add to the total. See [contents restoration](/services/contents-restoration/) if you have salvageable items.
- **Asbestos or lead testing**: Homes built before 1980 in California may require testing before demolition of drywall or flooring, adding $300 to $800 to the scope.

## What Does Homeowners Insurance Typically Cover?

Most standard homeowners policies cover sudden and accidental water damage but exclude gradual leaks and flood events. That distinction matters enormously for your claim.

Covered under a typical HO-3 policy:
- Burst pipe that failed suddenly
- Appliance supply line failure (washing machine, dishwasher, refrigerator ice maker)
- Accidental overflow from a tub or sink
- Water damage that results from a covered peril (a storm breaks a window, rain enters)

Not covered under a typical HO-3 policy:
- Gradual leaks that built up over weeks or months (the insurer calls this a maintenance issue)
- Sewer or drain backup (requires a separate endorsement, common and inexpensive to add)
- Flood from outside the home (requires a separate [NFIP or private flood policy](https://www.fema.gov/flood-insurance))
- Mold that resulted from a leak you knew about and did not address

The documentation you provide in the first 48 hours protects your claim. Photograph everything before extraction begins. Keep the failed component (the burst hose, the cracked fitting). A professional restoration company should provide a written moisture map, equipment logs, and a daily drying report. ## What Does the Water Damage Restoration Process Look Like?

Understanding the phases helps you evaluate any estimate you receive. A legitimate restoration scope will include all of these steps.

1. **Emergency contact and assessment**: A certified technician inspects the loss, identifies the water category and class, documents moisture readings with a calibrated meter, and scopes the affected area.
2. **Water extraction**: Truck-mounted or portable extractors remove standing water. This step happens first and fast. Every hour of standing water increases saturation depth.
3. **Controlled demolition (if needed)**: Wet drywall, insulation, flooring, and cabinetry that cannot be dried in place are removed to expose the structural cavity. This is called a "flood cut" and is standard practice, not upselling.
4. **Structural drying**: Blue axial air movers and LGR dehumidifiers are placed according to a drying plan. Equipment counts and placement follow the IICRC S500 standard. Daily moisture readings confirm progress. Average drying time is 3 to 5 days for a Class 2 loss.
5. **Antimicrobial treatment**: Applied to any surface that had Category 2 or 3 contact, and to any area where drying was delayed.
6. **Final clearance moisture readings**: Before equipment is removed, technicians confirm all readings are within acceptable dry standards.
7. **Rebuild**: Once the structure is dry and cleared, reconstruction begins. Dry County Restoration handles the rebuild phase through [general contracting](/services/general-contracting/) so you are not managing two separate contractors.

## What Should You Do Right Now?

If water is still entering the structure, stop the source first. Shut the main water supply valve or the valve at the failed fixture. If the source is a sewer line or you cannot identify it, call a plumber and a restoration company at the same time.

Once the source is stopped:
- Move valuables and electronics out of the wet area if it is safe to do so.
- Do not run a residential shop vac on contaminated water (Category 2 or 3). That spreads pathogens.
- Do not turn on ceiling fans or HVAC to "dry it out" before a moisture assessment. That can spread contamination and push humid air into wall cavities.
