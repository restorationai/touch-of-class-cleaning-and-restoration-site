**TL;DR:** Water damage restoration cost depends on the water category (clean, gray, or sewage), how many rooms are affected, the materials involved, and how long the water sat before extraction started. The single biggest factor you control is how fast you act. Most standard homeowners policies cover sudden, accidental losses after your deductible; gradual leaks and flood events are typically excluded. Dry County Restoration provides a written scope of work before any work begins, so you know exactly what you are paying for.

If you just found standing water in your home and you're trying to figure out what this is going to cost before you call anyone, this guide explains what actually drives the number, what insurance typically absorbs versus what lands on you, and why a written scope, not a phone quote, is the only way to get an accurate figure for your loss.

## How Is Water Damage Restoration Cost Determined?

There is no flat rate for water damage restoration, because no two losses are the same. The [IICRC S500 Standard for Professional Water Damage Restoration](https://www.iicrc.org/page/IICRCS500) classifies each loss by category and class, and both directly affect the scope of work. Restoration companies price from that scope, so the faster you stop the water and start extraction, the smaller the scope tends to be. A clean-water loss caught within hours is a straightforward drying job; a Category 3 event that sat for two days before anyone noticed involves demolition, antimicrobial treatment, and far more equipment.

## What Does Water Category Mean for Cost?

Water category is the industry's way of describing how contaminated the water is, and it is the single biggest factor in your restoration bill. Category 1 (clean water) is the least involved to remediate. Category 3 (sewage or floodwater, also called black water) is the most involved because it requires full PPE, antimicrobial treatment, and often complete removal of any porous material the water touched.

Note that Category 1 water becomes Category 2 after 24-48 hours, and Category 2 becomes Category 3 after another 24-48 hours. Bacterial growth accelerates quickly in Southern California's warm climate. A supply line that burst on a Friday and wasn't found until Monday is no longer a clean-water job.

## Is Water Damage Priced Per Square Foot?

Some of the scope scales with the affected area — extraction and structural drying cover a defined footprint — but square footage alone doesn't set the price. Whether demolition is needed to access wet framing or insulation matters more, and finishing work (drywall, flooring, paint) is scoped separately once the structure is dry. This is why a technician measures moisture and maps the affected area before writing a scope, rather than multiplying a rate by the room size.

## What Factors Move the Number Up or Down?

Several variables can push your final bill significantly higher or lower.

**Response time** is the most controllable factor. Mold can begin colonizing wet materials within 24-72 hours under warm, humid conditions. A job that starts within hours of the loss typically requires less demolition than one that starts three days later. If you have standing water right now, the first step is extraction, not research. See our [burst pipe emergency checklist](/blog/burst-pipe-emergency-checklist/) for what to do in the first hour.

**Class of loss** (not to be confused with Category) describes how deeply water has penetrated materials. IICRC S500 defines Classes 1 through 4. Class 1 means minimal absorption, usually hard surfaces only. Class 4 involves specialty drying situations like hardwood floors, concrete, or plaster walls that require low-grain refrigerant (LGR) dehumidifiers and extended dry times. Class 4 jobs take longer and require more equipment.

**Affected materials** matter because not everything can be dried in place. Wet insulation, particleboard cabinets, and laminate flooring almost always have to come out. Solid hardwood, concrete, and tile can often be dried and saved. The restoration company's moisture readings and thermal imaging scans determine what stays and what goes.

**Building age and code upgrades** can add scope in older homes. If a wall opened for drying reveals knob-and-tube wiring or asbestos-containing materials, the work changes. California code may require upgrades before work can be closed up.

**Equipment days** add up. A typical residential drying job runs 3-5 days. Each day that air movers and LGR dehumidifiers run on-site is part of the scope, and extended dry times on Class 3 or 4 losses run longer.

## What Does Insurance Usually Cover?

Most standard California homeowners policies (HO-3 form) cover sudden and accidental water damage from internal sources. If a pipe bursts, an appliance fails, or a water heater ruptures, you are generally covered after your deductible. What is typically excluded: gradual leaks (a slow drip under the sink that caused damage over months), flooding from outside the home, and sewer backup unless you added that endorsement.

For a detailed breakdown of what policies actually pay, see our guide on [does homeowners insurance cover water damage](/blog/does-homeowners-insurance-cover-water-damage/).

Here is how the claim typically flows:

1. You report the loss to your insurer and receive a claim number.
2. A restoration company begins mitigation immediately. Most policies require you to mitigate promptly to prevent further damage.
3. The restoration company documents everything: moisture readings, photos, equipment logs, and a written scope of work.
4. The adjuster reviews the scope and issues a payment based on your policy's actual cash value (ACV) or replacement cost value (RCV) terms.
5. You pay your deductible; insurance covers the rest up to your policy limit.

The documentation step is where claims get disputed. A restoration company that uses industry-standard estimating software (Xactimate is the most common) and provides daily moisture logs gives your adjuster the data needed to process the claim cleanly. That documentation also protects you if the adjuster questions the scope.

One important note: your deductible applies before insurance pays anything. Knowing your deductible before you call helps you understand what portion of a smaller loss you may absorb yourself.

## What Happens During Water Damage Restoration?

Understanding the process helps you evaluate the scope you are given and ask the right questions.

1. **Emergency extraction.** Truck-mounted or portable extractors remove standing water. This is the fastest phase, often completed in 1-3 hours.
2. **Moisture mapping.** Technicians use thermal imaging cameras and calibrated moisture meters to find water that has migrated behind walls, under flooring, and into subfloor assemblies. You cannot dry what you have not found.
3. **Controlled demolition (when needed).** Wet drywall, insulation, and flooring that cannot be dried in place are removed. This is called "flood cuts" when drywall is cut at a horizontal line to expose the wet framing behind it.
4. **Drying system setup.** Blue axial air movers are positioned to create high-velocity airflow across wet surfaces. LGR dehumidifiers pull moisture from the air and lower the vapor pressure so wet materials release moisture faster. Containment may be used to isolate the drying zone.
5. **Daily monitoring.** Technicians return each day to record moisture readings and adjust equipment placement. Drying is complete when all readings reach target levels per the IICRC S500 standard, typically 3-5 days.
6. **Antimicrobial treatment.** Applied to any surfaces that were exposed to Category 2 or 3 water, or that were wet long enough to be at risk for mold growth.
7. **Final documentation.** A drying report with all moisture readings, equipment logs, and photos is compiled. This is your claim documentation.

If mold is discovered during the process, that becomes a separate scope. You can read more about what that involves in our guide on [black mold vs regular mold](/blog/black-mold-vs-regular-mold/), and our [mold remediation service](/services/mold-remediation/) covers what professional remediation looks like.

## What Should You Do Right Now?

If you have an active water loss, the most expensive thing you can do is wait. Every hour of additional saturation increases the likelihood of demolition, extended drying, and mold growth. The steps that protect both your home and your claim are the same: stop the water source, extract standing water if you can, document with photos before anything is moved, and call a restoration company.
