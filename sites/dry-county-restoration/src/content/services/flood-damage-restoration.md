Flood damage is different from a burst supply line or an overflowing appliance. It usually means water has entered the structure from outside: storm runoff backing up through a window well, a slab crack letting groundwater rise under a finished floor, or a storm drain that couldn't keep pace with a fast-moving system. That water often carries silt, lawn chemicals, or sewage from overwhelmed municipal lines, which changes how it has to be handled from the first pump cycle.

## What Flood Damage Restoration actually involves

A flood response starts with categorizing the water. Clean rainwater that entered through a roof breach is treated differently than water that backed up through a floor drain or carried silt in from a yard. Once the category is set, crews bring in submersible pumps for standing water over two inches, followed by truck-mounted extraction for carpet, pad, and saturated flooring. Anything porous that sat in contaminated water, baseboards, drywall below the flood line, insulation, carpet pad, usually comes out rather than being dried in place. What remains gets dried with a combination of air movers and low-grain-refrigerant dehumidifiers, with moisture content logged daily until the structure reads dry per IICRC S500 guidelines. Depending on how much water intruded and how long it sat before the call came in, drying can run several days to over a week.

## Our process

1. **Water categorization and contamination check.** We test for silt, sewage backflow, and chemical contamination before deciding what can be dried in place versus what has to be removed.
2. **Extraction and muck-out.** Pumps and extraction units pull standing water first; mud, debris, and silt left behind by floodwater get shoveled and wet-vacced out before drying equipment goes in.
3. **Selective demolition of contaminated materials.** Drywall, insulation, and flooring that absorbed Category 2 or 3 water are removed to the flood line, not just dried, to prevent odor and microbial growth later.
4. **Structural drying with antimicrobial treatment.** Remaining framing and subfloor get treated and dried under monitored conditions, with daily moisture readings documented for the file.
5. **Moisture verification and reconstruction handoff.** Once materials read dry on a moisture meter, we document final readings and scope what needs to be rebuilt, from baseboards to subfloor.

## What separates a good flood response from a bad one

The biggest mistake we see is treating a flood like a simple water leak: drying everything in place without checking whether the water carried contamination. Floodwater that backed up through a drain or crossed a yard before entering the house is rarely clean, even if it looks clear. Drying contaminated drywall instead of cutting it out just traps the problem behind a dry-looking wall.

The second mistake is incomplete moisture mapping. Floodwater travels along baseboards, under cabinet toe-kicks, and into wall cavities well past where the water line is visible. Adjusters reviewing a flood claim want to see moisture readings taken at multiple points per room, not a single reading used to justify the whole job. As a licensed (#1083555), IICRC-certified firm, we document category, affected materials, and daily drying logs so the claim file matches what actually happened at the property.

## What does Flood Damage Restoration cost?

Flood damage pricing depends on how much square footage was affected, how contaminated the water was, and how long the water sat before cleanup started. These are typical industry ranges, not a quote; every loss is different, and we provide a written scope before any work begins. Homeowners insurance typically covers sudden flood-adjacent losses like storm-driven water intrusion or a plumbing-related backup, but standalone flood insurance (through NFIP or a private flood policy) is usually required for rising groundwater or overland flooding, so it's worth confirming which policy applies before assuming coverage.
