## What emergency water removal & cleanup actually involves

Emergency water removal is the physical extraction of standing and trapped water before it migrates further into a structure, paired with the drying work that follows. This is different from a routine cleanup: the priority in the first hours is pulling bulk water out with truck-mounted or portable extraction units, identifying how far the water traveled under flooring and behind baseboards, and stopping active intrusion if the source hasn't been shut off yet. Standing water removal on hard-surface flooring is relatively fast. Water that has gotten under laminate, into carpet pad, or down into a slab through expansion joints takes longer to locate and extract, and it's usually where the real damage is hiding.

Once bulk water is gone, the job shifts into structural drying: moisture mapping with meters, placement of air movers and dehumidifiers sized to the affected square footage, and daily monitoring until readings return to a dry standard. For clean water losses (a supply line, a dishwasher hose) this is usually a straightforward dry-out. For water that's been sitting, or that came from a source with any contamination, the cleanup requires antimicrobial treatment of affected materials before drying begins.

## Our process

1. **Source control and safety check.** Before anything else, we confirm the water source is stopped and check for electrical hazards in flooded areas.
2. **Bulk water extraction.** Truck-mounted or submersible pumps and extraction wands pull standing water from flooring, carpet, and any pooled areas, including crawlspaces where applicable.
3. **Moisture mapping.** Moisture meters and thermal imaging locate water that migrated into walls, under cabinetry, or beneath flooring that looks dry on the surface.
4. **Containment and categorization.** Water is classified by contamination level (clean, gray, or black) per IICRC S500 guidelines, which determines whether materials can be dried in place or need to be removed.
5. **Structural drying and monitoring.** Air movers and dehumidifiers are set based on the drying chamber size, with moisture readings logged daily until the structure hits dry standard.

## What separates a good emergency water removal response from a bad one

The most common mistake in water cleanup is treating it as a surface problem: mopping up visible water and running a couple of fans without ever checking what's underneath. Carpet pad holds water long after the carpet face feels dry. Subfloor under vinyl plank or laminate can stay saturated for days if it isn't pulled back and dried directly. Baseboards and the bottom few inches of drywall wick water upward and need to be checked with a moisture meter, not a visual glance, because drywall that looks fine can still be holding enough moisture to support mold growth within 24 to 48 hours.

Adjusters reviewing a water claim look for documented moisture readings at the start of the job and at each follow-up visit, photos of the affected areas before extraction and demolition, and a clear equipment placement log. Jobs that get disputed are usually the ones with no documentation trail, where it's not clear how wet things actually were or how long drying equipment ran. We document categorization, moisture readings, and equipment placement from day one specifically because that record is what supports the claim.
