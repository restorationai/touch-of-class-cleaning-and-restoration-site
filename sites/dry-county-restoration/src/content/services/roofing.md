A roof that's shedding granules into the gutters, showing daylight through the attic decking, or has shingles curling at the edges isn't a patch job anymore. Once underlayment fails or decking starts to soften from years of trapped moisture, repeated repairs stop making financial sense and a full tear-off and replacement becomes the more reliable path. ## What Roofing Installation and Replacement actually involves

A full roof replacement starts below the surface, not with the shingles themselves. The existing roofing material comes off down to the decking, which gets inspected board by board for rot, delamination, or insect damage before anything new goes down. From there it's a layered system: synthetic or felt underlayment, ice and water shield in valleys and around penetrations, new flashing at chimneys and walls, balanced attic ventilation, and finally the roofing material itself, whether that's asphalt shingle, tile, or a low-slope membrane. Most single-family residential replacements in Corona run two to four days depending on roof size, pitch, and material, with tile reroofs typically taking longer because of the weight and handling involved.

## Our process

1. **Roof inspection and written scope.** We walk the roof and attic, document existing damage with photos, check for decking softness, and confirm ventilation intake and exhaust are adequate for the home.
2. **Tear-off and decking assessment.** Old material comes off in sections to limit exposure, and any rotted or delaminated decking gets flagged and priced for replacement before new underlayment goes down.
3. **Underlayment, flashing, and ventilation installation.** This is where leaks actually get prevented: ice and water shield in valleys, step flashing at walls, and properly sized vents so the attic doesn't trap heat and moisture.
4. **Roofing material installation.** Shingles, tile, or membrane go down to manufacturer specifications, including correct nailing pattern and exposure, which matters for both wind resistance and warranty validity.
5. **Final walkthrough and documentation.** We review the completed roof with the homeowner, confirm gutters and downspouts are clear of debris, and provide warranty paperwork and photos for insurance or resale records.

## What separates a good roofing replacement from a bad one

Most roof failures that show up years later trace back to flashing, not the shingles. Crews that rush skip step flashing at wall intersections, reuse old flashing that's already corroded, or skimp on ice and water shield in valleys where water concentrates. Ventilation is the other quiet failure point: a roof with too much exhaust and not enough intake pulls conditioned air out of the house and can actually shorten shingle life from the underside. Insurance adjusters evaluating wind or hail claims look closely at whether decking replacement was documented, whether the underlayment matches current code, and whether the nailing pattern on the old roof shows signs of under-driven or overdriven nails, which affects whether the damage is wear-related or storm-related.

## Seasonal and regional considerations

Corona's summer heat and intense UV exposure age asphalt shingles faster than they would in a milder climate, and the Santa Ana winds that move through the Inland Empire each fall can lift shingles that are already past their service life. Wildfire exposure in the surrounding foothills is also worth factoring into material choice, since Class A fire-rated roofing offers better protection during ember-driven fire events than lower-rated assemblies. Rainfall here is infrequent but concentrated, so a roof with marginal flashing or underlayment often shows no problems for months until a storm finally finds the weak point.
