## What Smoke Damage Restoration actually involves

Smoke damage restoration is a different discipline from fire damage repair, even though the two often overlap on the same job. Fire repair deals with structural loss: burned framing, melted wiring, collapsed drywall. Smoke damage restoration deals with everything the fire touched but didn't destroy, which is usually a much larger footprint. That means identifying soot type (dry protein soot from a kitchen fire behaves differently than wet synthetic soot from burning plastics or foam), neutralizing the smell at the source rather than masking it, and cleaning or replacing porous materials that have absorbed odor molecules. Equipment typically includes HEPA air scrubbers, thermal foggers, ozone or hydroxyl generators depending on the severity, and dry-chemical sponges for delicate surfaces. A single-room smoke event with HVAC involvement can take two to four days from initial cleaning through final deodorization; whole-house jobs run longer, especially when soft contents need to be pulled, cleaned offsite, and returned.

## Our process

1. **Soot characterization.** We identify whether residue is dry, wet, or protein-based, since the wrong cleaning agent can smear soot deeper into a surface instead of lifting it.
2. **Containment and pre-cleaning.** We seal off affected zones to stop soot migration through air handlers and open doorways before any cleaning begins.
3. **Surface and content cleaning.** Hard surfaces, HVAC components, and salvageable contents are cleaned with method-matched techniques, dry sponging, wet wiping, or ultrasonic cleaning for smaller items.
4. **Thermal fogging and odor neutralization.** Heat-based fogging drives deodorizing agents into the same pores and fibers the smoke penetrated, rather than leaving a surface-level air freshener effect.
5. **Air quality verification.** We run air scrubbers through final stages and confirm odor and residue levels before calling a space clear.

## What separates a good Smoke Damage Restoration response from a bad one

The most common mistake is treating smoke damage like a cleaning job instead of a chemistry problem. Soot is acidic and corrosive, and generic all-purpose cleaners can set a stain permanently instead of removing it, especially on painted drywall and unsealed grout. Another frequent miss is ignoring the HVAC system entirely; if the air handler ran during or shortly after the fire, soot gets pulled through ductwork and redistributed throughout the house every time the system cycles, which means a clean-looking living room can still smell like smoke a week later. Adjusters also pay close attention to documentation of pre-existing versus fire-related damage, photo logs of affected rooms, and whether contents were properly inventoried before cleaning or pack-out. An IICRC Certified Firm follows documented protocols for each of these steps, which matters both for the quality of the work and for how cleanly the claim moves through your carrier.

## Seasonal & regional considerations

Wildfire smoke is a recurring concern across Riverside County, particularly during late summer and fall when Santa Ana winds push smoke and ash from regional fires into the Corona area even when the fire itself is miles away. That exterior smoke can infiltrate homes through attic vents, window seams, and HVAC intake, leaving a fine ash layer and lingering odor without any flame ever touching the property. Homes with older or poorly sealed ductwork tend to show heavier odor retention after these events, which is worth checking even if you didn't have a fire of your own.
