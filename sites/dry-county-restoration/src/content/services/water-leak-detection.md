A water bill that jumped for no reason, a warm spot on the slab, a musty smell behind the baseboard that won't go away: these are the quiet signals of a leak that hasn't announced itself yet. By the time water damage becomes visible, it's often been running behind a wall or under a foundation for weeks. Water leak detection finds the source before it turns into a structural repair, and it does it without tearing the house apart to look.

## What water leak detection actually involves

Finding a hidden leak is a diagnostic process, not a demolition project. Our technicians use thermal imaging cameras to spot temperature differentials in walls and flooring, acoustic listening equipment to pick up the sound of pressurized water moving through pipe or escaping a fitting, and moisture meters to confirm readings at the surface before anyone opens anything up. For slab leak detection, we add electromagnetic pipe locators and pressure testing to isolate which line is compromised, since a concrete slab doesn't give up its secrets easily. Most inspections run one to three hours depending on the size of the home and how many plumbing zones need to be checked, and the goal at every step is to locate the leak with the smallest possible footprint of disruption.

## Our process

1. **Intake and symptom review.** We start with what you've noticed: a spike in the water bill, a damp patch on carpet, a sound of running water with every fixture off, a musty smell with no visible source.
2. **Non-invasive scanning.** Thermal imaging and acoustic sensors scan suspect areas first, walls, slab, ceiling, before any cutting or probing happens.
3. **Isolation and pressure testing.** Plumbing zones are isolated one at a time to narrow down which line, fixture, or fitting is losing pressure.
4. **Pinpointing the source.** For slab leaks and leaks inside walls, we use tracer methods and electromagnetic locators to mark the exact point of failure, not just the general area.
5. **Documented findings and handoff.** You get a written report with the location, the likely cause, and photos or thermal images, so your plumber or our restoration team can move straight to repair without guesswork.

## What separates a good water leak detection response from a bad one

The most common mistake is skipping the non-invasive scan and going straight to opening drywall or jackhammering slab based on a guess. That costs homeowners money in unnecessary repair and still might miss the actual source if there's more than one failure point, which happens more often than people expect with aging galvanized or polybutylene plumbing. A thorough inspection checks for secondary leaks and distinguishes a plumbing leak from condensation, irrigation intrusion, or a roof leak tracking down through framing, since all four can produce a similar damp spot on a ceiling or wall.

Insurance adjusters generally want documented moisture readings, thermal images, and a clear statement of cause before approving repair or related water damage work, because coverage often depends on whether the loss was sudden and accidental versus a long-term, unmaintained leak. A detection report that pinpoints location and likely timeline gives the adjuster what they need to make that call quickly instead of sending someone back out for a second look.

## What does water leak detection cost?

Costs vary with how hard the leak is to find and whether it's a single fixture or a slab leak requiring electromagnetic locating and pressure testing across multiple zones. Every property and plumbing layout is different, which is why Dry County Restoration provides a written scope before any work begins. Homeowners insurance sometimes covers the cost of detection when it's tied to a covered water loss, though policies vary on whether they pay for locating the leak versus only the resulting damage, so it's worth checking your specific policy language.

## Seasonal & regional considerations

Corona and the surrounding Inland Empire sit on clay-heavy soils that expand and contract with seasonal moisture swings, and that movement puts stress on slab plumbing in the slab-on-grade homes that make up much of the local housing stock, especially properties built before the 1990s on copper or galvanized supply lines. Hot, dry summers followed by winter rain accelerate that soil movement, which is a common contributor to slab leaks here. Irrigation systems and landscaping lines running close to foundations can also mask a plumbing leak or get mistaken for one, which is another reason a proper scan matters before anyone starts digging.
