## Why Taft Properties See More Water Damage Than You'd Expect

Taft's identity as a West Side oil town shaped its housing stock in ways that matter when a pipe fails. The workers' cottages built throughout Ford City and South Taft between the 1920s and 1950s were constructed with galvanized steel plumbing that has a functional lifespan of roughly 40–70 years — meaning much of it is well past due. Galvanized pipe corrodes from the inside out, narrowing the bore until pressure spikes cause a sudden failure rather than a slow drip you'd catch early. Those same homes typically have undersized floor drains and no subfloor vapor barrier, so when water escapes, it travels horizontally under flooring before it ever shows up as a wet spot on the surface.

The wall furnaces common in these homes add another wrinkle: the chase cavities that house gas lines and flue vents create hidden pathways that carry water from a second-floor leak straight down to the foundation without saturating any visible drywall along the way. By the time a homeowner notices a problem, the structural framing has often been wet for days.

## Our Water Damage Restoration Process in Taft

Every job starts with thermal imaging and moisture mapping — not a visual inspection alone. In pre-1950 plaster-and-lath construction, water wicks into wall cavities differently than it does in modern drywall, and a moisture meter reading at the surface can significantly underestimate saturation depth. We map the full affected area before any equipment is placed.

Extraction comes next. Truck-mounted extraction units pull standing water from hard floors and subfloor assemblies; weighted extraction tools work carpet and pad. Once the bulk water is removed, we set drying systems calibrated to the actual cubic footage and material types — not a generic "one fan per room" approach. Drying is documented daily against the IICRC S500 standard so there's a clear record for your insurance adjuster.
