## Our Soot Removal Process in Delano

Every job starts with a room-by-room soot mapping — we use a dry chemical sponge test on walls and ceilings to identify the migration path before touching anything. In Delano's older plaster-wall homes, this step matters more than most people expect: plaster is alkaline and reacts differently to acidic soot residue than modern drywall does, so the cleaning chemistry has to be matched to the substrate, not just the soot type.

Once the scope is mapped, the process moves in a defined sequence:

1. **Dry removal first** — dry sponging and HEPA vacuuming to lift loose carbon particles before any wet cleaning introduces moisture that could drive residue deeper into porous surfaces.
2. **Surface-specific wet cleaning** — alkaline cleaners for protein soot (common in kitchen fires), dry-cleaning solvents for oily petroleum-based residue, and pH-neutral solutions for painted plaster walls where aggressive chemistry would lift the finish.
3. **Odor neutralization** — thermal fogging or hydroxyl generation to break down odor-causing volatile organic compounds embedded in wall cavities and soft furnishings. In Delano's summer heat, VOCs off-gas faster, which means odor treatment can't be skipped even on what looks like a minor smoke event.
4. **HVAC and duct inspection** — soot in the duct system is the most common reason a "cleaned" home still smells like smoke weeks later. We inspect and, where indicated, clean supply and return ducts before the system is turned back on.
5. **Post-cleaning verification** — visual inspection and, when indicated, air quality sampling to confirm particulate levels are within acceptable ranges before the space is reoccupied.
