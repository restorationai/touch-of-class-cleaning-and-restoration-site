## Our Flood Damage Restoration Process in Rosedale

Every job starts with a full moisture survey — thermal camera, pin and pinless meters, and a written baseline reading at every affected assembly. That document becomes the drying log that your insurance adjuster will want and that IICRC S500 drying protocols require.

Extraction comes next. For the finished interiors common in Rosedale — LVP over concrete, engineered hardwood over OSB subfloor, tile on a mortar bed — the extraction method matters as much as the equipment. Weighted extraction heads pull residual moisture from flooring systems that a standard wand won't reach. Once standing water and surface saturation are addressed, we set a calculated drying system: low-grain refrigerant dehumidifiers, high-velocity air movers positioned to create a directed airflow pattern, and, where wall cavities are wet, injectidry panels that dry framing from the inside without requiring full drywall demolition.

Daily monitoring visits track the drying curve against psychrometric targets. We don't pull equipment on a calendar — we pull it when the readings confirm the assembly has reached equilibrium. That discipline matters for matching finishes: drywall hung over framing that isn't fully dry will telegraph seams and nail pops within a season.

Once the structure is dry and cleared, reconstruction begins — matching existing paint sheens, replicating cabinet door styles, sourcing the same flooring SKUs where available. Rosedale homeowners expect the finished product to look like the loss never happened, and that standard drives every material decision we make.
