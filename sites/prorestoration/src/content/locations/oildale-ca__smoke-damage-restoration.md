## Our Smoke Damage Restoration Process in Oildale

The first step on any Oildale job is a room-by-room assessment that maps smoke migration — not just the room of origin. In older cottages, smoke travels through wall cavities and attic spaces that were never sealed to modern standards, meaning a fire in a back bedroom can leave detectable odor in the living room and kitchen. Thermal imaging helps locate residue that isn't visible to the naked eye.

From there, the process moves through four concrete phases:

- **Dry soot removal** using HEPA-filtered vacuums and dry chemical sponges before any wet cleaning begins — applying moisture to dry soot drives it deeper into porous materials.
- **Surface cleaning** with alkaline and enzymatic agents calibrated to the specific surface — different chemistry for original plaster versus painted drywall versus unfinished wood.
- **Odor neutralization** using thermal fogging and hydroxyl generators, which are particularly important in homes with swamp coolers, since the ductwork acts as a reservoir for smoke odor.
- **Structural assessment** for materials that cannot be cleaned to an acceptable standard and need controlled removal and replacement.

All work follows IICRC standards. As an EPA Lead-Safe Certified Firm and CSLB-licensed contractor (#960566), the team is equipped to handle the lead paint and asbestos-containing materials that appear regularly in pre-1960 Oildale homes during any fire-related demolition.
