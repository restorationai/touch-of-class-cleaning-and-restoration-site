## Our Soot Removal Process in Taft

Every soot job starts with a room-by-room assessment to categorize the residue type. Protein soot from a kitchen fire behaves differently than the thick, wet smoke left by a burning synthetic material — protein residue is nearly invisible but intensely odorous and requires enzymatic cleaners, while petroleum-based soot from burning plastics or old wiring insulation is oily and demands dry-chemical sponging before any wet cleaning begins.

For the plaster walls common in pre-1950 Taft cottages, we use dry chemical sponges first to lift loose carbon without grinding it deeper into the porous surface. We follow with pH-balanced wet cleaning agents calibrated to the alkalinity of old lime plaster. HEPA-filtered negative air machines run throughout to capture airborne particulates and prevent cross-contamination to rooms that weren't directly affected. Structural cavities — the wall voids and subfloor gaps that old cottages have in abundance — get inspected with a thermal camera to confirm smoke hasn't deposited residue inside framing bays where it would continue off-gassing.

Contents are inventoried, photographed, and either cleaned on-site or packed out to our Bakersfield facility depending on the extent of saturation. Soft goods — curtains, upholstered furniture, clothing — go through ozone or hydroxyl treatment for odor neutralization rather than masking.
