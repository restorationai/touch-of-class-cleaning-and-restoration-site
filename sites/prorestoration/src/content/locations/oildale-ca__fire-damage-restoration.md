## Our Fire Damage Restoration Process in Oildale

A fire loss has a sequence, and skipping steps creates problems months later. Here is how the work unfolds on an Oildale property:

**Emergency stabilization** — Board-up, roof tarping, and utility isolation happen first. In the Airport Drive corridor and along North Chester Avenue, properties sometimes sit close to neighboring structures; containment barriers go up immediately to prevent cross-contamination of smoke odor to adjacent units.

**Structural assessment** — Older Oildale homes often have mixed construction: original 1940s framing with 1970s additions. A thorough scope identifies which materials can be restored versus which must be removed, and flags any suspect materials for testing before crews begin demolition.

**Soot and smoke removal** — Dry-chemical soot from fast-burning fires and wet, oily soot from smoldering fires require different cleaning chemistry. HEPA vacuuming, dry-ice blasting on exposed framing, and thermal fogging for odor penetration are matched to what the fire actually produced.

**Structural drying and deodorization** — Even after a fire, water damage from suppression efforts is significant. Industrial desiccant dehumidifiers and high-velocity air movers dry the structure to IICRC S500 standards before any reconstruction begins.

**Reconstruction** — Licensed under CSLB (#960566), the team handles framing, drywall, insulation, and finish work so the property returns to pre-loss condition under a single contractor.
