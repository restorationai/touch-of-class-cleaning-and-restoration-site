## Why Delano Properties Are Especially Vulnerable to Smoke Damage

Delano's housing stock is split between two very different eras. The older bungalows and craftsman-style homes along the Randolph Street corridor and in the core of Downtown Delano were built with plaster-and-lath construction, wood subfloors, and minimal insulation — materials that absorb smoke particulate deeply and hold odor compounds long after visible soot is wiped away. Smoke doesn't just coat surfaces in these homes; it migrates through gaps in original window framing, into wall cavities, and underneath hardwood floors where it's invisible until the smell comes back weeks later.

On the west side of town, the 2000s-era subdivisions present a different challenge. Tract homes built with engineered lumber, synthetic cabinetry, and spray-foam insulation produce a different smoke chemistry when they burn — one that includes off-gassing from melted plastics and adhesives. That smoke is more acidic, and it etches metal fixtures and electronics faster than wood smoke alone.

Delano's Central Valley climate adds another variable. Summer temperatures regularly push above 100°F, which accelerates the oxidation of soot particles and drives odor compounds deeper into porous materials. A fire that happens in July needs faster intervention than the same fire in January — the heat essentially bakes the residue in.

## Our Smoke Damage Restoration Process in Delano

When we arrive at a Delano property, the first step is a structured assessment — not a visual walk-through, but a systematic inspection using thermal imaging and air sampling to map where smoke has traveled beyond the fire room. In older homes near Cesar Chavez Park, that often means checking the attic space, which in pre-1960 construction is frequently connected to wall cavities through unsealed top plates.

From there, the process moves through these stages:

- **Dry soot removal** using HEPA-filtered vacuums and dry chemical sponges before any wet cleaning, because introducing moisture to dry soot can smear and set it permanently.
- **Chemical cleaning** of hard surfaces with pH-matched cleaners — alkaline for protein-based smoke residue (common in kitchen fires), acidic formulations for synthetic-material smoke.
- **Thermal fogging or hydroxyl generation** to neutralize odor molecules embedded in soft materials, wall cavities, and HVAC ductwork. Hydroxyl generators are preferred in occupied or partially occupied structures because they're safe to run while contents are still present.
- **Duct cleaning and HVAC decontamination**, which is non-negotiable in Delano's climate — a contaminated HVAC system will redistribute smoke odor every time the air conditioning runs, and in a Central Valley summer, that system runs constantly.
- **Structural drying and controlled ventilation** to flush residual off-gassing from treated materials.

All work is documented photographically and in written scope reports formatted for insurance carriers.
