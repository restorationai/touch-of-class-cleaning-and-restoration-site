## Our Odor Removal and Deodorization Process in Arvin

Effective deodorization is a sequence, not a single treatment. The process starts with a thorough source assessment — identifying whether the odor originates from char and smoke residue, microbial growth, sewage intrusion, or some combination. Skipping this step and jumping straight to ozone or hydroxyl treatment is a common mistake that leaves the underlying source intact.

Once the source is confirmed, the workflow typically follows this path:

1. **Physical removal of contaminated materials** — charred wood, saturated insulation, or mold-colonized drywall that cannot be cleaned must come out before any deodorization equipment is deployed.
2. **Thermal fogging** — a solvent-based or water-based fog is heated and introduced into the space, penetrating the same pathways smoke traveled, neutralizing odor molecules bonded to surfaces inside wall cavities and under flooring.
3. **Hydroxyl deodorization** — hydroxyl generators produce hydroxyl radicals that break down odor-causing compounds at a molecular level and are safe to run in occupied or partially occupied spaces, which matters in Arvin's densely housed neighborhoods.
4. **Ozone treatment** — used in unoccupied spaces for severe smoke or biological odors; ozone at effective concentrations requires full evacuation and careful post-treatment airing.
5. **Final air quality verification** — confirming odor levels have returned to acceptable baselines before the space is cleared for full reoccupancy.

For homes near the Bear Mountain Boulevard corridor with original wood-paneled interiors, we extend the thermal fogging dwell time, because wood grain holds smoke particulates in ways that painted drywall does not.

## Equipment and Methods We Use for Odor Removal

Not every odor problem calls for the same tool. Hydroxyl generators are the preferred first-line method when occupants or pets cannot be fully relocated — they work continuously without requiring the space to be sealed and vacated. Thermal fogging is particularly effective after kitchen fires and in homes with older, textured wall surfaces common in Arvin's pre-1970s construction. Ozone treatment remains the most aggressive option for severe, set-in smoke odor or trauma-scene biological odors, but it requires strict safety protocols and is never deployed in occupied spaces.

The IICRC-certified team at ProRestoration Services selects and sequences these methods based on the specific odor type, the building materials present, and occupancy constraints — not a one-size approach.
