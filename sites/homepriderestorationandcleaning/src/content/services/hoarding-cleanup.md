When clutter has built up over months or years, the problem usually isn't visible from the street. It shows up in blocked exits, floors that haven't been seen in a long time, pest activity, and air quality that makes breathing inside the home uncomfortable. Families often reach a point where the volume of belongings has outpaced what anyone can sort through alone, and that's when a structured, judgment-free cleanup plan becomes necessary rather than optional.

## What Hoarding Cleanup actually involves

Hoarding cleanup is slower and more deliberate than a standard deep clean. It typically starts with sorting, not hauling: separating keepsakes and documents from trash, recyclables, and materials that need to be discarded due to contamination or pest damage. Depending on how long the accumulation has been building, crews may encounter structural issues hidden under the clutter, damaged flooring, compromised subfloors, or electrical and plumbing fixtures that haven't been accessible in years. A single-room job might take a day. A whole-house job with years of accumulation can take several days of staged work, often in coordination with the homeowner or a family member who can make decisions about what stays.

Equipment varies by scope but commonly includes HEPA air scrubbers to manage dust and airborne particulate, PPE for crews working around biological contamination or pest waste, and commercial-grade trash removal and hauling capacity that a homeowner couldn't arrange on their own. Odor control and sanitizing treatments are applied where organic material has broken down into flooring or walls.

## Our process

1. **Private walkthrough and scope.** We assess the property room by room, note any structural or sanitation concerns, and build a written plan before anything is moved.
2. **Sorting and categorization.** Items are separated into keep, donate, and dispose categories, with the homeowner or an authorized family member involved in decisions wherever possible.
3. **Removal and haul-out.** Bulk debris, damaged furniture, and unsalvageable materials are removed and disposed of according to local waste regulations.
4. **Cleaning and sanitizing.** Exposed surfaces, flooring, and air are cleaned and treated to address odor, residue, and contamination left behind by the clutter.
5. **Final walkthrough.** We confirm the space is livable and usable again, and flag any repairs (flooring, drywall, pest treatment) the homeowner should schedule separately.

## What separates a good hoarding cleanup response from a bad one

A rushed crew treats the job as a trash-out: everything goes in a dumpster with no sorting, which often means important documents, medications, or sentimental items get discarded along with genuine debris. A better approach slows down at the sorting stage, even when it adds time, because that's where trust is either built or lost with the family.

The other common miss is stopping at "visibly clean." Odor and contamination can sit in subflooring, baseboards, and HVAC systems long after the surface clutter is gone, and skipping that layer means the smell or the pest issue comes back within weeks. Crews working under IICRC-informed cleaning standards treat the underlying surfaces, not just what's visible, and document conditions with photos at each stage, which matters if insurance or a property manager needs a record of the work performed.

## What does Hoarding Cleanup cost?

Costs depend heavily on the volume of belongings, the condition of the flooring and walls underneath, and whether biohazard or pest remediation is also needed. Every property is different, which is why Home Pride Restoration and Cleaning provides a written scope of work before any cleanup begins. Homeowners insurance sometimes covers the portion of cleanup tied to a covered peril, such as pest infestation or sanitation hazards, but it rarely covers general decluttering, so it's worth confirming coverage details with your carrier before work starts.

## Seasonal & regional considerations

Utah's dry climate limits some of the mold risk that humid regions deal with, but closed-up homes in Saratoga Springs still trap heat and dust during summer months, which can make prolonged cleanup work uncomfortable without proper ventilation. Winter jobs sometimes require extra care around frozen or unused plumbing that hasn't been checked in a long time, since clutter often hides leaks or shutoff valves that need attention before the space is usable again.
