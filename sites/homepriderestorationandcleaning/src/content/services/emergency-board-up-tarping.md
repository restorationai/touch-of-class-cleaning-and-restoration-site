**Storm-damaged roof or a shattered window in Saratoga Springs?** Call now for emergency board-up and tarping before wind, rain, or trespassers turn a contained problem into a total loss.

A missing shingle section, a tree limb through the roof deck, or a window blown out by hail doesn't stay a small problem for long. Once the building envelope is breached, every hour of exposure lets wind-driven rain soak insulation, drywall, and framing that were otherwise untouched. Board-up and tarping stops that chain reaction. It's not a repair, it's a stabilization measure that buys time for your adjuster to inspect the loss and your contractor to scope the real fix.

## What Emergency Board-Up and Tarping actually involves

This service covers two related but distinct jobs. Tarping protects roof damage, usually from wind, hail, fallen branches, or fire that burned through decking, using heavy-gauge reinforced poly sheeting anchored with furring strips or sandbags so it survives wind gusts rather than tearing loose and flapping holes into itself. Board-up covers broken windows, kicked-in doors, or fire-damaged openings with exterior-grade plywood, cut to size and fastened into the framing, not just the trim, so it actually resists forced entry and weather.

Both are temporary by design. They're meant to hold until permanent repairs or reconstruction can happen, and they're usually installed within hours of the call rather than scheduled days out, because every additional hour of exposure adds to the eventual drying or reconstruction scope.

## Our process

1. **Hazard check first.** Downed power lines, unstable roof framing, and sharp debris get identified before anyone climbs a ladder or steps onto a damaged roof section.
2. **Measure and scope the opening.** Every broken window, door, or roof section gets measured so the board or tarp actually covers the full damaged area, not just the visible hole.
3. **Install tarping with proper anchoring.** Tarps are run past the damaged edge onto sound roofing material and secured with furring strips, not just nails through the tarp itself, which tears out in the first windstorm.
4. **Board openings into the framing.** Plywood is fastened to structural framing members, not stapled to trim or sheetrock, so it holds against weather and deters entry.
5. **Photo-document everything.** Pre- and post-board-up photos go into the file your insurance adjuster will ask for when the claim moves to the repair estimate stage.

## What separates a good board-up and tarping response from a bad one

The most common failure point is tarp anchoring. A tarp stapled only at the edges will catch wind like a sail and tear away within days, sometimes making the opening worse than before it was covered. A correctly installed tarp extends well past the damaged area, is secured to solid roof deck on all sides, and is checked for proper drainage so pooled water doesn't collect in the sag.

On the board-up side, the mistake is fastening into trim or stucco instead of framing. It looks secure for a photo but won't hold against wind load or a determined intruder. Adjusters reviewing a fire or storm claim also look for whether the board-up was done promptly, since a delayed response that allowed additional rain or smoke damage to spread can affect how the secondary damage gets categorized on the claim.
