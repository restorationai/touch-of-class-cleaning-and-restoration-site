**Water pooling under baseboards or a sump pump that just quit?** Every hour standing water sits on drywall, subfloor, or carpet pad, it pushes further into the material and closer to the point where it has to be replaced instead of dried. Call now for emergency water removal and cleanup in Saratoga Springs before the water finds its way into wall cavities or under cabinetry where you can't see it working.

## What emergency water removal actually involves

Emergency water removal and cleanup is the physical extraction of standing water and the structural drying that follows, not just running a wet vac over visible puddles. A real extraction job uses truck-mounted or portable extraction units pulling hundreds of gallons out of carpet, pad, and hard flooring, followed by moisture mapping with a penetrating meter to find out how far the water traveled under baseboards, behind drywall, and into subfloor. Depending on how much water intruded and what it touched, drying equipment (air movers, low-grain refrigerant dehumidifiers) typically stays in place for three to five days, with daily moisture checks to confirm materials are actually drying rather than just reading dry on the surface.

## Our process

1. **Source and category assessment** - identify where the water came from (supply line, appliance, roof, groundwater) and classify it as Category 1, 2, or 3, since that determines what can be dried versus what has to be removed.
2. **Standing water extraction** - pull bulk water from flooring, carpet, and pad using extraction equipment sized to the job, prioritizing rooms where water is actively spreading to adjoining spaces.
3. **Moisture mapping** - use meters and sometimes thermal imaging to trace water migration into subfloor, baseboards, and wall cavities that look dry but aren't.
4. **Containment and drying setup** - place air movers and dehumidifiers based on the moisture map, and isolate Category 2 or 3 water areas to keep contamination from spreading to unaffected rooms.
5. **Monitoring and documentation** - return for moisture readings until materials hit dry standard, with photos and logs that support the insurance claim.

## What separates a good water removal response from a bad one

The most common mistake is treating visible wet carpet as the whole job and skipping what's underneath it. Pad almost always needs to come out even when carpet can be saved, and subfloor that reads damp on a meter but looks fine to the eye still needs air movement or it becomes a mold problem in days, not weeks. A second mistake is under-sizing equipment, too few air movers or dehumidifiers for the square footage means drying stretches on longer than it should, which adjusters notice and sometimes push back on. Good technicians document moisture readings daily and photograph affected materials before removal, because that paper trail is what gets a claim approved without a fight. They also know the difference between a Category 1 clean-water loss and a Category 2 or 3 situation, which changes whether materials get dried in place or removed entirely.

## What does emergency water removal and cleanup cost?

Costs vary with how much water intruded, what category it falls into, and how many building materials were affected before the water was caught. Every loss gets a written scope before work begins so there are no surprises once drying equipment is already running.
