## Why Benjamin Properties See Mold Growth

Much of Benjamin's housing stock sits on rural and semi-rural lots, many with crawlspace foundations rather than full basements, and quite a few built decades ago before vapor barriers were standard practice. Combine that with Utah County's irrigation canal network running close to residential lots and a water table that rises noticeably during spring runoff, and you get crawlspaces that stay damp well past the point where wood framing and subfloor insulation start supporting mold colonies. Homes near agricultural fields also deal with higher ambient humidity during watering season, which shows up as condensation on windows and, over time, as spore counts that run higher than what we'd expect in a drier, more urban part of the county.

## Our Mold Inspection and Testing Process in Benjamin

We start with a visual inspection of the areas most likely to be affected given Benjamin's construction patterns: crawlspace framing, sill plates, HVAC returns, and any spot where plumbing runs near an exterior wall. From there we use a moisture meter to check subsurface readings in wood and drywall, because surface-dry materials can still be saturated underneath, especially in older framing that's absorbed moisture slowly over multiple wet seasons. If visible growth or elevated moisture readings point to a problem, we collect air samples and, where appropriate, surface or tape-lift samples, and send them to an independent lab for analysis so the results aren't coming from the same company recommending the remediation. Our technicians are IICRC certified, and we walk through the lab report with you in plain language rather than handing over a page of spore counts and a bill.
