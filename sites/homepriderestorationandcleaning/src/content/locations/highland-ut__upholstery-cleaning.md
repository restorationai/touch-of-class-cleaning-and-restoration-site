## Why Highland Furniture Wears Differently

Highland sits at a higher elevation than most of Utah County, which means more direct UV exposure through south- and west-facing windows. That accelerates fiber breakdown and fading on fabric upholstery faster than in shadier, lower-elevation neighborhoods. Add in the area's harder municipal water, and you get mineral deposits that settle into fabric fibers whenever a spill or spot-clean job gets blotted with tap water instead of treated solution. Because a lot of Highland properties sit on larger lots with horses, dogs, or barn cats in the mix, we also see more embedded hair and dander worked deep into cushion seams than we typically find on smaller in-town lots.

## Our Upholstery Cleaning Process in Highland

We start every job with a fiber and dye test, which matters more here than in humid climates because Highland's dry air makes some fabrics more brittle and prone to color bleed under aggressive agitation. For fabric sofas and sectionals, we use a low-moisture steam extraction method that pulls embedded soil and dander out of the padding without oversaturating it, since dry mountain air means we want to control moisture precisely rather than rely on ambient humidity to help dry things out. Leather upholstery gets a different approach entirely: a pH-balanced cleaner followed by a conditioning treatment, because Highland's dry climate pulls moisture out of leather faster than in coastal or valley-floor areas, leading to premature cracking if it's never conditioned. Hard water spotting gets pretreated separately before the main clean, since standard shampoo alone won't break down mineral buildup.
