## Why Vineyard Properties See Water Damage Issues

Most homes in Vineyard were built within the last decade, which means slab-on-grade foundations, engineered hardwood or luxury vinyl plank flooring, and PEX plumbing are far more common here than the copper and basement construction you'd see in Provo or Orem. Slab foundations sit close to a water table that's influenced by Utah Lake just to the west, so when a pipe bursts or an appliance supply line lets go, water has fewer places to drain on its own and tends to spread laterally under flooring rather than pooling in a basement. Winters along the lake also bring sharp freeze-thaw swings, and unheated garages or exterior hose bibs in newer construction are a recurring source of frozen and split pipes once temperatures drop.

## Our Water Extraction Process in Vineyard

We start by identifying the water category and shutting off the source, then move into extraction with truck-mounted or portable units depending on how the home is laid out. In slab-built Vineyard homes with LVP or engineered wood, we pull flooring sections where water has migrated beneath the surface rather than assuming the slab itself will dry on its own, since trapped moisture under flooring can sit for weeks and feed mold growth. Moisture mapping with penetrating meters tells us where the slab and wall base are still holding water, and we set drying equipment to IICRC S500 standard targets rather than pulling it on a fixed schedule. For newer stucco or fiber-cement exteriors common in Vineyard's recent subdivisions, we also check wall cavities near window penetrations, since those assemblies can trap water differently than older lap siding.
