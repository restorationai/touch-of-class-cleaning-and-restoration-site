## Why Highland Properties See Water Damage Issues

Highland sits at the base of the Wasatch Range in Utah County, and that location shapes how water damage actually happens here. Most of the housing stock went up from the late 1980s through the 2000s, which means a lot of finished walkout basements built into sloped lots, plus the expansive clay soils common throughout the benches of Utah County. Clay swells when it's wet and shrinks when it dries, and that movement stresses foundation walls and slab penetrations over time, opening up small cracks that let groundwater in during spring runoff. Add in the secondary pressurized irrigation systems that most Highland neighborhoods rely on for lawns, and you've got another pressure-fed line that can fail at a valve or fitting with surprisingly little warning. We also see winter freeze-thaw cycles crack exposed hose bibs and unheated crawlspace plumbing, something that catches homeowners off guard because Highland's dry climate most of the year makes people forget how hard a cold snap can hit a pipe that isn't insulated.

## Our Water Damage Restoration Process in Highland

We start with extraction, pulling standing water with truck-mounted and portable units before it has a chance to wick further into drywall, baseboards, and subfloor. In Highland's finished basements, that often means pulling back carpet and pad quickly, since padding holds water like a sponge and will keep feeding moisture back into the carpet and slab even after the surface looks dry. From there we map moisture with meters and thermal imaging to find out how far water traveled behind walls and under flooring, which matters a lot in homes with engineered hardwood or LVP over concrete, both common in newer Highland construction. Structural drying follows the IICRC S500 standard, with air movers and dehumidifiers placed and adjusted over several days rather than left running blind, and we document readings at each visit so the drying is verifiable, not just assumed. Once materials test dry, we handle any demo and reconstruction needed, from baseboard and drywall to flooring, so you're not coordinating a separate contractor.

## Local note

One thing that trips up a lot of Highland homeowners: because so many lots here are graded for walkout basements on sloped ground, water from a slab leak or a failed irrigation line tends to migrate toward the low side of the foundation rather than pooling evenly, so the visible wet spot on the carpet is often not directly above the source. We routinely find the actual entry point several feet uphill from where the damage first shows, which is why moisture mapping before demo matters more in Highland's walkout-style homes than in a flat-lot house.
