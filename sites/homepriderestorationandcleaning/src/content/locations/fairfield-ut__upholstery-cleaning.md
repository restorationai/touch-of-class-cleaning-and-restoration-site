## Why Fairfield Furniture Wears Differently

Most homes out here are newer construction on acreage, with big south- and west-facing windows to take in the valley views. That's great for light, but it means upholstery fabric fades and dries out faster than in a shaded, in-town lot. Add in Cedar Valley's low humidity and frequent wind, and dust works its way deep into weave fibers rather than sitting on the surface, which is why a quick vacuum often doesn't touch what's actually embedded. Households running well water also see more mineral deposits on fabric after spills or pet accidents, since hard water leaves behind residue that plain water extraction won't fully lift.

## Our Upholstery Cleaning Process, Adjusted for the Climate

We start with a dry-soil removal pass, pulling out the embedded grit before any moisture touches the fabric, since wetting dusty upholstery first just turns it to paste in the fibers. From there we test fabric type and colorfastness, then choose between hot water extraction for cotton and synthetic blends or a low-moisture cleaning method for fabrics that don't hold up well to heavy saturation in a dry climate, where over-wetting can leave a slower-drying ring than it would in a humid region. Leather gets a separate conditioning step, since Fairfield's dry air pulls moisture out of hide faster than most cleaners expect, and skipping conditioner here is how leather starts cracking within a year. We finish with light, fast-drying extraction so the piece is usable again in hours, not days.
