## Why Lindon Properties See Water Damage Issues

Lindon sits close to Utah Lake, and that proximity means a naturally higher water table in the lower-lying parts of the city, especially after a wet spring runoff year. Combine that with the heavy clay soil common throughout Utah Valley, and you get foundations that don't drain quickly once water gets underneath them. Add pressurized secondary irrigation lines, which many Lindon homes rely on for lawns and landscaping, and a cracked valve or fitting can push a surprising amount of water into a crawlspace before anyone notices. Winter brings its own pattern: freeze-thaw cycles along the Wasatch Front stress supply lines and hose bibs, and a pipe that splits in late January often isn't discovered until a thaw a few days later, by which time water has already traveled into wall cavities and subfloor.

## Our Water Damage Restoration Process in Lindon

We start with extraction, pulling standing water with truck-mount and portable units sized to the loss, then move into moisture mapping. In homes with engineered hardwood or laminate over concrete, which is common in Lindon's newer developments, we check subfloor moisture content carefully before deciding whether flooring can be saved or needs to come up. Structural drying follows IICRC S500 guidelines, with air movers and dehumidifiers placed based on psychrometric readings rather than guesswork. We monitor daily, because Utah's dry climate can create a false sense that materials are drying faster than they actually are at the stud and subfloor level. Once moisture content hits an acceptable range throughout the affected materials, we document the final readings for your records and for the insurance file.
