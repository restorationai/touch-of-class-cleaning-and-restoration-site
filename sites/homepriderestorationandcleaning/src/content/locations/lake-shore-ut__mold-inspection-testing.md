**Noticing a musty smell in a Lake Shore basement or a damp patch near the crawlspace that won't go away?** A mold inspection and testing visit gives you lab-backed answers instead of guesswork, which matters in a town built along the edge of Utah Lake where groundwater sits closer to the surface than most buyers expect. We combine moisture mapping with air and surface sampling so you know whether what you're smelling is a cleaning problem or a remediation job.

## Why Lake Shore Homes See Hidden Mold Growth

Lake Shore's position on the eastern edge of Utah Lake means many properties, especially those on lower lots near the water, deal with a higher water table than homes up in the benches toward Payson or Spanish Fork. That groundwater pressure pushes moisture into block foundations and crawlspaces even when there's no visible leak. Add in Utah County's wide seasonal swing, dry summer heat followed by irrigation season and winter snowmelt, and you get wall cavities and subfloor framing that go through repeated wet-dry cycles. Homes still running evaporative (swamp) coolers, common throughout this part of the valley, also push extra humidity into attic spaces during the hottest months, which is a pattern we check for specifically when we inspect.

## Our Mold Inspection and Testing Process in Lake Shore

We start with a visual walkthrough and moisture mapping using a penetrating meter, paying close attention to the sill plate and lower wall framing in homes near the lakeshore where capillary moisture is most likely to show up first. From there we take air samples in the suspect area and a baseline sample outside or in an unaffected room, since comparing the two is what actually tells you whether spore counts are elevated rather than just present, which they always are to some degree. If we see visible growth or a hidden cavity we're uncertain about, we'll pull a tape or swab sample directly off the material. Everything gets sent to an independent, accredited lab, and we walk you through the results in plain language rather than handing you a report full of spore genus names and no context.

## Equipment & Methods We Use for Mold Inspection

Beyond the air pump and sample cassettes, we use a moisture meter to flag framing that reads wet even when the surface looks dry, and a thermal camera to spot temperature differentials that often point to moisture migrating behind drywall or under flooring. In crawlspaces, which are common under older Lake Shore homes built before municipal sewer expansion reached this stretch of the county, we also check for standing water or poor vapor barrier coverage, since a damp crawlspace is one of the most common sources of a musty smell that shows up two floors away. Our technicians are IICRC trained, and we follow the IICRC S520 standard for how samples are collected and documented so the report holds up if you need it for an insurance claim or a home sale negotiation.

## Local note

One thing we watch for specifically in Lake Shore: homes closer to the lakeshore road often have sump pumps installed in the crawlspace or basement that were added after construction rather than designed in from the start. When those pumps fail or get undersized for a wet spring, the crawlspace can hold standing moisture for weeks before anyone notices a smell upstairs, which is exactly the lag time mold needs to establish a colony on untreated framing.
