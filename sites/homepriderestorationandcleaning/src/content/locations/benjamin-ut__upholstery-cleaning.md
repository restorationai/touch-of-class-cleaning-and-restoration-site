## Why Benjamin Homes See Upholstery Wear and Buildup

Benjamin sits in the agricultural belt of Utah County, south of Spanish Fork, where irrigation ditches and open fields mean fine soil dust rides the wind into homes on a near-daily basis during the dry months. That dust settles into upholstery fibers and, combined with the area's hard well water, creates a gritty buildup that ordinary vacuuming spreads around rather than removes. Many properties out here also run on private wells rather than municipal water, and the mineral content in that water leaves behind a dulling residue if a sofa is ever spot-cleaned with tap water instead of properly treated solution. Add in pets, working dogs, and the occasional barn cat that's decided the couch is theirs, and fabric upholstery in Benjamin tends to need a different cleaning chemistry than upholstery in a tighter, more urban neighborhood.

## Our Upholstery Cleaning Process in Benjamin

We start every job with a fabric and fiber assessment, because a hay-dust-coated microfiber sectional and a leather recliner near a wood stove need entirely different treatment. For fabric upholstery, we pre-treat ground-in soil, then use hot water extraction with a pH-balanced solution formulated to avoid the mineral streaking that plain well water can cause. Leather upholstery gets cleaned and conditioned separately, since the wrong moisture level on leather can crack it faster in Utah's dry climate. Drying time matters too: Benjamin's low humidity is actually an advantage here, upholstery dries noticeably faster than it would in a coastal climate, usually within a few hours with airflow, so furniture is back in use the same day in most cases.
