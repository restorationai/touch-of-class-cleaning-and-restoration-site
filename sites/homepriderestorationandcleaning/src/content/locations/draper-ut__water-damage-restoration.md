## Why Draper Properties See Water Damage Issues

Draper sits where the Salt Lake Valley narrows against the Traverse Mountains, and that geography shapes how water moves through a property here. Homes built on the benches and foothills often deal with clay-heavy soil that drains slowly after a heavy spring runoff or a sprinkler line break, which means water can pool against foundations longer than it would in sandier soil down the valley. Meanwhile, the newer subdivisions built during Draper's growth boom over the past two decades rely heavily on PEX plumbing and tankless or hybrid water heaters, and while that reduces some of the old galvanized-pipe failures we still see in older Utah homes, it introduces its own failure points at fittings, connectors, and pressure regulators. Winter freeze-thaw cycles along the bench areas also stress exterior hose bibs and irrigation backflow preventers, which is a common source of basement and crawlspace water intrusion we get called out for between late fall and early spring.

## Our Water Damage Restoration Process in Draper

The first visit is about stopping the loss and mapping where the water actually went, not just where it's visible. We pull moisture readings from drywall, subfloor, and framing using penetrating and non-penetrating meters, because water from a slab leak or a second-floor bathroom overflow travels along joists and behind baseboards well past the obvious stain. Extraction comes next: truck-mounted or portable extraction units depending on access, since some of Draper's hillside lots have driveways and staging areas that limit where we can park equipment. Structural drying follows the IICRC S500 standard, with air movers and desiccant or refrigerant dehumidifiers sized to the affected square footage and the specific building materials involved, since newer engineered flooring and older solid hardwood dry at very different rates. We re-check moisture daily until materials are back to a dry standard, document everything with photos and readings for your records, and only then move into repairs if reconstruction is needed.

## Draper Insurance & HOA Coordination

Draper is split between Salt Lake County and Utah County, which occasionally matters for permitting on larger reconstruction jobs depending on which side of the city a property falls on. We coordinate directly with the relevant building department when a job requires permits, and we handle documentation the way most major insurance carriers expect: moisture logs, photos at each visit, and a clear scope of work before repairs begin. A number of Draper's newer subdivisions are HOA-governed, and some HOAs require pre-approval or specific contractor insurance minimums before exterior work or shared-wall repairs can start; we're licensed (RC-25-0737) and carry the coverage most HOA boards ask to see, and we'll get you a copy of it the same day if your board needs it on file.

One thing that catches a lot of Draper homeowners off guard: properties on the upper benches near the foothills often have their main water shutoff in a basement utility closet or an exterior valve box buried under landscaping rock rather than near the street, which can cost precious minutes during an active leak.
