Light-colored performance fabrics, popular in newer Vineyard builds, show this soiling early even when the cushions still look structurally new.

## Our Upholstery Cleaning Process for Vineyard Fabrics

We start every job with a fabric identification and pH test, since a lot of the furniture in Vineyard's newer homes uses synthetic blends and performance weaves that behave differently than traditional cotton or linen. Leather upholstery gets a separate conditioning step to prevent cracking, which matters here because forced-air heating in winter and dry summer heat both pull moisture out of hides faster than in more humid climates. For fabric sofas and sectionals, we use low-moisture steam extraction calibrated to the fiber type, followed by a controlled drying pass so cushions aren't left damp in a closed-up house. Pet odor and stain pretreatment is common on service calls here, given how many Vineyard households moved in with young families and pets around the same time the neighborhoods filled in.

## Local note

Because so much of Vineyard was built on former industrial land near the lake, we sometimes see a fine grayish dust settle into upholstery fibers that homeowners assume is just normal dirt. It's usually wind-blown sediment from the lakebed and surrounding unfinished lots, and it sits differently in fabric than household dust, so a standard vacuum pass often leaves it embedded rather than lifting it out. We adjust our pre-vacuuming and agitation step accordingly on Vineyard jobs so that sediment gets pulled before the wet extraction stage instead of getting pushed deeper into the cushion fill.

## What to Expect for Leather vs. Fabric Pieces

Leather sectionals, increasingly common in Vineyard's newer builds, need a different approach than the fabric sofas still holding on from starter furniture sets. We test conditioning products on an inconspicuous panel first, since some protective leather coatings applied at the factory react differently to cleaning solutions. Fabric pieces, especially the tightly woven performance textiles sold as "kid and pet friendly," often look clean on the surface while trapping odor underneath; our extraction step is built to pull from the cushion core, not just the surface fibers, which is where most of the smell actually lives.
