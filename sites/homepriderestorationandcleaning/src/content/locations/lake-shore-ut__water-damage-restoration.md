## Why Lake Shore Properties See Water Damage Issues

Most of Lake Shore's housing stock sits on the flat, low-lying ground west of Highway 68, where the soil is heavy with lake-bottom clay. That clay doesn't drain, it holds water against foundations long after a storm passes, and it pushes moisture laterally into crawlspaces and basement walls. Combine that with Utah Lake's seasonal level swings, which rise noticeably during spring runoff from the surrounding mountains, and you get a pattern we see again and again: basements that were bone dry in August taking on seepage by May. Older homes with original cast iron or galvanized supply lines add a second failure point, since corrosion inside those pipes tends to accelerate in soil with high moisture content.

## Our Water Damage Restoration Process in Lake Shore

We start every job with extraction, pulling standing water off floors and out of carpet pad with truck-mounted or portable extraction units, because every hour water sits is an hour it's wicking further into drywall, baseboard, and subfloor. From there we map moisture with meters and thermal imaging to find where water traveled, not just where it's visible, which matters in Lake Shore because clay-heavy soil can push moisture sideways under slab foundations well past the original leak point. Structural drying follows the IICRC S500 standard: air movers and dehumidifiers sized to the affected square footage, placed to create the airflow pattern that pulls moisture out of framing and subfloor instead of just drying the surface. We monitor daily with moisture readings until materials hit dry standard, then document that readout for your file, your insurer, or both.
