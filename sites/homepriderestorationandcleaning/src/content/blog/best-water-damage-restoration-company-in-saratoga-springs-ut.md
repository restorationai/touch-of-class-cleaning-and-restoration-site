## What to Look for When Choosing a Water Damage Company in Saratoga Springs

Not all restoration companies are equal, and in a water emergency, the wrong choice can cost you thousands in avoidable secondary damage. Here are the criteria that actually matter.

**IICRC Certification.** The IICRC (Institute of Inspection, Cleaning and Restoration Certification) sets the industry standard for water damage restoration. An IICRC-certified firm follows documented drying protocols, uses calibrated moisture meters, and produces the kind of drying logs that insurance adjusters need to process claims. Ask specifically: is the company IICRC certified, or just the technician who shows up?

**24/7 Availability.** Water damage does not wait for business hours. A company that can't respond at night or on weekends is not a true emergency restoration provider. Confirm actual after-hours response before you need it.

**Licensing.** Utah requires contractor licensing for restoration work. A licensed company carries the insurance and accountability that protects you if something goes wrong on the job.

**Insurance documentation experience.** The best restoration companies in Saratoga Springs don't just dry your home - they document the loss in a format your insurance carrier can use. That means moisture readings, drying logs, photo documentation, and a written scope of work before any demolition begins. If a company can't explain how they handle insurance documentation, that's a red flag.

**Local knowledge.** Saratoga Springs sits at roughly 4,500 feet elevation near Utah Lake, and the area's newer housing stock - much of it built in the 2000s and 2010s - often features finished basements, engineered wood flooring, and spray-foam insulation, all of which respond differently to water intrusion than older construction. A company that has worked in this specific housing market understands those nuances.

## What Happens When You Call a Water Damage Restoration Company?

Knowing what to expect helps you make a faster, calmer decision when water is still spreading.

1. **Emergency call and triage.** You describe the situation. The dispatcher asks about the water source (clean supply line, appliance, sewer, or storm), the affected area, and whether the water source is stopped. They dispatch a crew.

2. **Arrival and assessment.** The crew arrives with moisture meters, thermal imaging cameras, and extraction equipment. They map the affected area and classify the water: Category 1 (clean water from a supply line), Category 2 (gray water from appliances or toilets), or Category 3 (black water from sewage or flooding). Category affects the required drying protocol and PPE.

3. **Water extraction.** Truck-mounted or portable extractors remove standing water. This step is fast - most extraction is complete within the first hour or two.

4. **Structural drying setup.** LGR (low-grain refrigerant) dehumidifiers and high-velocity air movers are placed according to a calculated drying plan. The goal is to bring affected materials to their pre-loss moisture content, documented with daily readings.

5. **Monitoring and documentation.** The crew returns daily to check moisture readings and adjust equipment. Every reading is logged. This documentation is what your insurance adjuster will review.

6. **Clearance and scope.** Once materials reach target moisture levels, the crew documents clearance. If any materials (drywall, flooring, insulation) can't be dried in place, a written scope for removal and reconstruction is provided before any demolition begins.
