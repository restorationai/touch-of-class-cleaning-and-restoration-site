## What to Look for When Choosing a Water Damage Restoration Company in Midland

The right company for a water loss does more than extract water. Here is what matters when you are making a fast decision:

**Licensing and insurance.** In Texas, restoration contractors should carry general liability insurance and workers' compensation. Ask for proof before work begins. ACS Enterprise is licensed and insured.

**Moisture documentation.** A professional crew uses calibrated moisture meters and thermal imaging to map the extent of the damage before and after drying. This documentation is what your insurance adjuster needs to approve the claim. Without it, you are guessing at whether the structure is actually dry.

**Written scope before work starts.** Any reputable company provides a written scope of work before extraction begins. This protects you from scope creep and gives your insurer a clear record.

**Insurance claim experience.** Water damage claims are complex. A company that communicates directly with adjusters, provides moisture logs, and submits a proper scope speeds up your settlement. Ask specifically whether they handle insurance documentation.

**Equipment on the truck.** Commercial drying requires truck-mounted or trailer-mounted extraction units, low-grain refrigerant (LGR) dehumidifiers, and high-velocity air movers. Ask what equipment they bring. A shop vac and a box fan is not a drying system.

## What to Do Right Now If You Have Active Water Damage

Stop the source first. If a pipe is still flowing, shut off the main water supply. If the water involves an appliance, turn off the supply valve behind it. Then call a restoration company. Our [burst pipe emergency checklist](/blog/burst-pipe-emergency-checklist/) walks through the first steps.

Do not wait to call your insurance company. Most homeowners policies cover sudden, accidental water damage but exclude gradual leaks. The sooner you report and document, the better your claim position. See our plain-English guide on [whether homeowners insurance covers water damage](/blog/does-homeowners-insurance-cover-water-damage/) before you call your adjuster.

Time matters. The [EPA recommends](https://www.epa.gov/mold) drying water-damaged materials within 24 to 48 hours to prevent mold growth. In Midland's summer heat, that window can be shorter. Every hour of standing water adds to the structural drying time and the risk of secondary damage.
