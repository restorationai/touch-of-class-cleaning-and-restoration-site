## The Short Answer: It Depends on *How* the Water Got In

Homeowners insurance covers water damage in some situations and not others, and the line between covered and denied often comes down to a single word: **sudden**. If water entered your home suddenly and accidentally (a pipe burst, a washing machine hose failed, a toilet overflowed), most standard HO-3 policies will pay for the resulting damage. If water crept in slowly over weeks or months, a pinhole leak under the sink, a deteriorating wax ring, a roof that needed replacing, insurers typically call that a maintenance issue and deny the claim. Knowing which category your loss falls into before you call your adjuster can save you significant time and frustration.

---

## What Standard Homeowners Insurance Usually Covers

Most HO-3 policies (the most common policy type in Texas) cover what the industry calls "sudden and accidental" discharge of water. Common examples:

- A supply line to your refrigerator or washing machine bursts
- A pipe freezes and splits during an unusual cold snap
- A toilet supply valve fails and floods the bathroom
- An HVAC condensate drain backs up and overflows into the ceiling
- A neighbor's upstairs unit leaks into your space (in condo situations)

When one of these events happens, your dwelling coverage typically pays to repair the structure, drywall, flooring, cabinetry, and your personal property coverage handles damaged belongings. You'll pay your deductible first, then the carrier covers the rest up to your policy limits.

**One important nuance for West Texas homeowners:** Midland's climate is arid, but the region does get hard freezes. Pipes in exterior walls, garages, and crawl spaces can freeze when temperatures drop unexpectedly. Freeze damage is generally covered, but your insurer may ask whether you took reasonable precautions, keeping heat on, drip-running faucets during a hard freeze, insulating exposed pipes.

---

## What Homeowners Insurance Almost Never Covers

Understanding the exclusions is just as important as knowing the coverages. These categories are routinely denied:

**Gradual damage and neglect.** If an adjuster finds evidence that a leak existed for a long time before you reported it, staining patterns, mold growth behind walls, soft subfloor, the carrier will likely argue the damage resulted from a maintenance failure, not a sudden event. That determination can void coverage even if you genuinely didn't know about the leak.

**Flooding from outside the home.** This is the big one. Standard homeowners policies do not cover flood damage, water that enters from the ground up, storm surge, or overland flow from heavy rain. In Midland and the broader Permian Basin, flash flooding after intense thunderstorms is a real risk. Separate flood insurance through the National Flood Insurance Program (NFIP) or a private carrier is the only way to cover that exposure.

**Sewer and drain backup.** If a municipal sewer line backs up into your home through a floor drain or toilet, that's typically excluded from a base policy. Many carriers offer a sewer backup rider for a modest additional premium, worth asking about if you haven't already.

**Seepage and groundwater.** Water that seeps through foundation walls or a slab is generally excluded. This is distinct from a pipe that breaks inside the slab, which may be covered, but the distinction matters and adjusters look carefully at the evidence.

## The Steps That Protect Your Claim (and Your Home)

If you've just discovered water damage, the sequence of actions you take in the first few hours matters both for your home's condition and for your insurance claim.

1. **Stop the source if you can.** For a burst pipe or failed supply line, turn off the main water shutoff. For an appliance leak, shut off the valve behind or beneath the unit. If you don't know where your main shutoff is, find it now, before an emergency.

2. **Document everything before touching it.** Take photos and video of standing water, wet materials, and any visible damage. Capture the source of the water if it's visible. This documentation is your evidence if the carrier questions the cause or extent of the loss.

3. **Call your insurance company.** Report the loss promptly. Most policies require "timely" reporting, and delays can complicate your claim. Ask specifically what your policy covers, what your deductible is, and whether the carrier has a preferred vendor list.

4. **Begin mitigation, but keep records.** You have a duty under most policies to prevent further damage. That means extracting standing water, moving wet belongings out of the affected area, and placing fans if you have them. Save receipts for any equipment you rent or supplies you buy.

5. **Don't discard damaged materials until the adjuster has seen them.** Wet carpet, damaged drywall, and ruined flooring are evidence. Photograph everything before removal, and if possible, keep samples.

---

## What NOT to Do After a Water Loss

- **Don't assume the surface tells the whole story.** Hardwood floors can look fine and still have standing water underneath. Drywall can feel dry to the touch while the insulation behind it holds moisture for weeks. Without moisture meters and thermal imaging, you're guessing.

- **Don't run ceiling fans over wet drywall without knowing what's above.** If water is pooling in a ceiling cavity, a fan can accelerate mold growth in the trapped space rather than drying it out.

- **Don't sign anything from a restoration company before your adjuster has been involved.** Assignment of Benefits agreements can affect your claim. Read carefully before signing.

- **Don't assume the job is done when things look dry.** Mold can begin colonizing wet building materials within 24 to 48 hours of a water event. Materials that appear dry at the surface, plywood subfloor, wall framing, insulation, can still hold enough moisture to sustain mold growth for weeks.

---

## When the Damage Is More Than a DIY Fix

Some water losses are manageable with a shop vac and a few fans. Many are not. Signs that you need professional water damage restoration:

- Water has been sitting for more than a few hours
- The affected area is larger than roughly 10 square feet
- Water has reached wall cavities, subfloor, or ceiling assemblies
- You can smell a musty or earthy odor, mold may already be present
- The water source was a toilet, sewage line, or anything that may carry contaminants
- You're dealing with a finished basement, hardwood floors, or materials that require controlled drying

Professional restoration crews use calibrated drying equipment, industrial dehumidifiers, air movers, and moisture mapping tools, to dry a structure to a measurable standard rather than to a visual guess. That documentation also matters to your insurance carrier when it comes time to settle the claim.
