When a pipe bursts, you have minutes, not hours, before water migrates into walls, subfloor, and insulation. Turn off your main water shutoff valve immediately, then cut power to any circuits in the affected area at your breaker box. Once the water is stopped and the electricity is safe, you can start documenting and drying. This checklist walks you through every step in order, from the first 60 seconds to the weeks of recovery that follow.

## Step 1, Stop the Water and Make the Space Safe

Your main shutoff valve is the single most important thing to find *before* a pipe ever bursts. In most Midland homes, it sits near the water meter at the street, or inside the home near the utility room or garage. If you have never located yours, do it today.

**Sequential steps for the first five minutes:**

1. Turn the main shutoff valve clockwise until it stops. Water flow should cease within seconds.
2. Open a faucet on the lowest floor of the house to relieve pressure still sitting in the lines.
3. Go to your electrical panel and shut off the breakers for any room where water is present or directly above. Water and live circuits are the fastest way a pipe burst becomes a life-safety emergency.
4. If the burst is near your water heater, turn the heater to its pilot or vacation setting, running a water heater with no supply water can damage the tank.
5. Call your water utility's emergency line if you cannot locate or turn the shutoff, or if the valve itself is broken. In Midland, the water service is managed through the City of Midland Utilities department.

Do not re-enter a room with standing water until you have confirmed the electricity is off.

## Step 2, Contain and Document Before You Touch Anything

Once the water is stopped and the space is electrically safe, resist the urge to start ripping up carpet or pulling drywall. A few minutes of documentation now protects your insurance claim later.

- **Photograph everything.** Shoot wide angles of each affected room, then close-ups of the burst pipe itself, the water line on the walls, and any damaged belongings.
- **Video the source.** A short walkthrough video showing where the pipe failed and how far water traveled is more useful to an adjuster than still photos alone.
- **Note the time.** Write down when you discovered the loss and when you shut off the water. Insurers use this to assess response time and secondary damage.
- **Move salvageable items out of the wet zone.** Furniture, rugs, and electronics that are not yet soaked should come out now. Items sitting in water for more than an hour begin absorbing moisture into their structure.

If you have a wet/dry shop vac, start pulling standing water off hard floors. Every gallon you remove now is less moisture available to wick into the subfloor.

## Step 3, What NOT to Do After a Pipe Bursts

Some of the most common instincts after a pipe burst make the damage worse or create new hazards.

**Do not use a standard household vacuum.** Wet/dry vacs are built for water extraction. A regular vacuum is not, and using one risks electrocution and motor damage.

**Do not run your HVAC system to dry the space.** It feels logical, but forcing warm or cool air through a wet structure without proper airflow management can push moisture deeper into wall cavities and spread mold spores if any have already begun to colonize. Mold can begin establishing within 24 to 48 hours in a warm, wet environment, a common condition in West Texas summers.

**Do not assume the damage stops at the visible wet area.** Water follows the path of least resistance. A burst pipe in a second-floor bathroom can travel down inside the wall cavity and pool on the first-floor ceiling or inside a closed soffit before you see a single stain. If the leak ran for more than a few minutes before you caught it, the affected area is almost certainly larger than it looks.

**Do not discard damaged materials before your adjuster or a restoration contractor has seen them.** Torn-out drywall and cut flooring are evidence. Throwing them away before documentation can complicate your claim.

**Do not use electric fans or heaters in a room with standing water still present.** Clear the standing water first.

## Step 4, When to Call a Water Damage Professional

Some pipe bursts are genuinely manageable as a DIY cleanup, a slow drip from a supply line to a bathroom sink that was caught in under a minute, on a tile floor, with no wall penetration. Most are not.

Call a water damage restoration professional when:

- Water has been running for more than a few minutes before you found it
- The affected area crosses more than one room or one floor
- Water has reached drywall, insulation, hardwood, or engineered flooring
- You can smell a musty or earthy odor, that is microbial activity already beginning
- The burst occurred in a wall, ceiling, or under a slab
- You have any doubt about the extent of the moisture

Professional restoration crews use moisture meters and thermal imaging cameras to map exactly where water has traveled, including inside walls that feel dry to the touch. Plaster walls common in older Midland homes can read dry at the surface while the lath and framing behind them stay saturated for weeks. Without that equipment, you are guessing.

## Step 5, The Recovery Process (What Comes After the Emergency)

Stopping the water is the beginning, not the end. Full recovery from a pipe burst typically moves through these phases:

1. **Extraction**, Removing all standing water using truck-mounted or portable extraction equipment.
2. **Drying**, Industrial air movers and dehumidifiers run continuously, often for three to five days depending on materials and ambient conditions. Midland's low humidity can help drying times, but materials like OSB subfloor and dense insulation hold moisture longer than the air around them suggests.
3. **Moisture monitoring**, Daily readings with a moisture meter track whether materials are trending toward dry. Drying is not complete until readings reach acceptable baselines for the specific material type.
4. **Demolition of unsalvageable materials**, Saturated drywall, insulation, and flooring that cannot be dried in place are removed. This is not optional, wet materials left inside a wall become a long-term mold problem.
5. **Mold assessment**, If the water sat for more than 24 to 48 hours before drying began, a mold assessment may be warranted before reconstruction starts.
6. **Reconstruction**, New drywall, insulation, flooring, and paint bring the space back to its pre-loss condition.

Insurance typically covers sudden and accidental water damage from a burst pipe. Keep all receipts, contractor invoices, and documentation from every phase, your adjuster will need them.
