A sewage backup or major flood affecting multiple rooms can push $5,000 to $15,000 or more. The biggest cost drivers are the category of water (clean vs. gray vs. sewage), how long it sat before cleanup started, and how many materials need to come out.

If you just found water in your home and you're trying to figure out what this is going to cost before you call anyone, you're in the right place. Midland's climate is dry most of the year, but when a pipe bursts behind a wall or a summer storm backs up a drain, the damage can spread fast through drywall, flooring, and framing before it's visible. The numbers below reflect real industry ranges for Texas jobs, not national averages that don't account for West Texas conditions.

## How Much Does Water Damage Restoration Cost in Texas?

In Texas, water damage restoration costs an average of $3,200 to $4,500 for a mid-size residential job, with a typical range of $1,500 to $8,000. Jobs involving sewage, structural materials, or long-term moisture exposure regularly exceed $10,000. The cost per square foot for water extraction and drying alone runs $3.75 to $7.00 in most Texas markets.

The table below breaks down typical costs by scenario:

| Scenario | Typical Cost Range |
|---|---|
| Single room, clean water (Cat 1), caught quickly | $1,200 - $3,500 |
| Multiple rooms, clean or gray water (Cat 1-2) | $3,000 - $7,500 |
| Sewage backup or black water (Cat 3), single area | $4,000 - $9,500 |
| Sewage or flood, multiple rooms + structural damage | $8,000 - $20,000+ |
| Crawl space or subfloor water intrusion | $2,500 - $6,000 |
| Appliance leak (dishwasher, washing machine) caught same day | $900 - $2,500 |
| Storm-related flooding with standing water | $5,000 - $15,000+ |

These are industry-typical ranges. Every loss is different, and a written scope from a licensed restoration company is the only way to get an accurate number for your specific situation.

## What Factors Drive the Final Cost?

Four things move the number more than anything else: the category of water, how long the water sat, how many materials were affected, and whether the structure itself is compromised.

**Water category** is the single biggest factor. The [IICRC S500 standard](https://www.iicrc.org) classifies water losses into three categories. Category 1 (clean water from a supply line or appliance) is the cheapest to remediate. Category 2 (gray water from a washing machine overflow or toilet with no solids) requires more protective equipment and disposal protocols. Category 3 (black water, including sewage, flood water from outside, or any water that has sat long enough to become contaminated) requires full PPE, antimicrobial treatment, and disposal of porous materials that cannot be safely dried in place.

**Response time** matters enormously. Water spreads through drywall, insulation, and subfloor materials within hours. The [EPA recommends](https://www.epa.gov/mold/mold-cleanup-your-home) beginning drying within 24 to 48 hours to prevent mold growth. A job that starts at hour 2 versus hour 48 can be the difference between drying in place and tearing out walls. For more on that timeline, see [How Long Does Water Damage Restoration Actually Take?](/blog/how-long-does-water-damage-restoration-take/)

**Materials affected** determine both labor and replacement costs. Hardwood flooring, cabinetry, and plaster cost more to restore than vinyl plank and painted drywall. Insulation almost always has to be replaced once wet. Subfloor damage adds significant cost because it requires demo before drying can happen underneath.

**Structural involvement** (wet framing, compromised load-bearing elements, or foundation seepage) adds both complexity and cost. These situations require moisture readings at the structural level and sometimes a longer drying cycle before any rebuild can begin.

## What Does the Restoration Process Actually Involve?

Restoration crews follow a defined sequence. Understanding each phase helps you evaluate a scope of work before signing anything.

1. **Inspection and moisture mapping.** A technician uses a moisture meter and sometimes a thermal camera to find all affected areas, including hidden moisture behind walls and under floors. This step defines the scope.
2. **Water extraction.** Truck-mounted or portable extractors pull standing water and saturated material. This is where the clock really starts.
3. **Controlled demolition (if needed).** Wet drywall below the flood line, saturated insulation, and compromised flooring are removed. This is not optional for Category 2 or 3 losses.
4. **Structural drying.** Industrial air movers and LGR (low-grain refrigerant) dehumidifiers run continuously, typically for 3 to 5 days. Technicians take daily moisture readings to confirm drying progress.
5. **Antimicrobial treatment.** Applied to any surface that was exposed to gray or black water, and to any area at risk for mold. For Category 3 losses, this is mandatory.
6. **Final moisture verification.** Readings must confirm materials have returned to acceptable moisture content before the drying phase closes. This documentation protects your insurance claim.
7. **Rebuild and restoration.** Replacing drywall, flooring, insulation, and finishes. This phase is sometimes handled by a separate contractor; some restoration companies handle both.

## Will Homeowners Insurance Cover Water Damage Restoration in Texas?

Most standard homeowners policies in Texas cover sudden, accidental water damage but exclude gradual leaks and flooding from outside. The distinction matters a lot.

A burst pipe that happens overnight and is discovered in the morning: typically covered. A slow leak under a sink that has been dripping for months and finally caused visible damage: typically denied as a maintenance issue. Flood water that entered through a door or foundation during a storm: not covered by standard homeowners insurance (requires a separate [NFIP flood policy](https://www.fema.gov/flood-insurance) or private flood coverage).

For Texas homeowners, the most common covered losses are pipe bursts, appliance failures, and HVAC condensate line overflows. The most common denials are gradual leaks, roof maintenance failures, and any water that entered from outside the structure.

Documentation is what protects a claim. A licensed restoration company should provide a written moisture map, daily drying logs, and a detailed scope of work. That paper trail is what adjusters rely on when evaluating the claim. For a deeper look at what policies actually cover, see [Does Homeowners Insurance Cover Water Damage?](/blog/does-homeowners-insurance-cover-water-damage/)

Your deductible applies before insurance pays anything. In Texas, deductibles on homeowners policies commonly run $1,000 to $2,500 for water losses, though wind/hail deductibles are often percentage-based and separate.
