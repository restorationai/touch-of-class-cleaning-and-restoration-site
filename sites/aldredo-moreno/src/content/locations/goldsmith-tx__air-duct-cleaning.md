Out in Goldsmith, the West Texas desert doesn't just bake the ground, it works its way into everything, including the ductwork running through your home or business. The combination of fine Permian Basin caliche dust, relentless summer heat, and the kind of dry winters that crack caulk and gap duct seams means the air circulating through your vents carries a heavier particulate load than most people realize. ## Why Goldsmith Properties See Duct Issues

Ectorcounty sits in one of the dustiest corridors in the continental United States. The soil here, a mix of caliche, sandy loam, and fine gypsum particles, becomes airborne with almost any wind event. During the frequent spring dust storms that roll across the Permian Basin, particulate matter infiltrates return-air grilles, coats evaporator coils, and settles deep into flex duct runs. Over a season or two, that accumulation restricts airflow, forces your HVAC system to work harder, and circulates allergens every time the blower kicks on.

Many homes in and around Goldsmith were built during the mid-twentieth-century oil boom, when duct systems were often fabricated from sheet metal with fiber-glass lined interiors. That lining degrades over decades, and when it does, it sheds fibers directly into the airstream. Older systems also tend to have more mechanical joints, more places for dust to collect and for conditioned air to leak before it reaches the room you're trying to cool.

Dryer vents are a separate but related concern. In a climate where outdoor temperatures can exceed 100°F for weeks at a stretch, a partially blocked dryer vent doesn't just reduce efficiency, it creates a genuine fire risk. Lint accumulation combined with extreme ambient heat is a combination worth taking seriously.

## Our Air Duct Cleaning Process in Goldsmith

The process starts before we open a single register. We inspect the accessible portions of your duct system, supply and return trunks, flex runs, boots, and the air handler cabinet, to identify any sections with visible damage, disconnected joints, or heavy biological growth before we begin agitation. Disturbing a severely compromised duct without first understanding its condition can spread contamination rather than remove it.

Once we have a clear picture, we use high-powered negative-pressure equipment to place the entire duct system under vacuum. Rotating brush heads and compressed-air whips work through each supply and return line, breaking loose settled dust, debris, and biological material and pulling it into a contained collection unit rather than back into your living space. For systems with fiberglass-lined interiors, we adjust our agitation method to avoid further liner damage.

If sanitization is warranted, particularly in systems where moisture intrusion has allowed microbial growth to take hold, we apply an EPA-registered sanitizing agent to duct interiors after mechanical cleaning. We also clean the air handler cabinet, evaporator coil face, and blower wheel when accessible, because a clean duct connected to a dirty air handler is only half the job.

Dryer vent cleaning follows a similar negative-pressure approach, with a rotary brush run through the full length of the exhaust duct from the termination cap back to the dryer connection.

## Equipment & Methods We Use for Air Duct Cleaning

The equipment that matters most for duct cleaning in a dusty, arid environment is the collection side of the system, specifically, the filtration on the vacuum unit. We use truck-mounted or high-capacity portable negative-pressure machines equipped with HEPA filtration so that what we pull out of your ducts doesn't re-enter your home through the equipment exhaust. In a region where outdoor air quality can already be compromised by blowing dust, adding to the indoor particulate load during a cleaning job is not acceptable.

For flex duct systems, common in residential construction across Ector County, we use soft-bristle agitation tools that clean thoroughly without tearing the inner liner. Sheet metal trunk lines get more aggressive brush treatment where appropriate.
