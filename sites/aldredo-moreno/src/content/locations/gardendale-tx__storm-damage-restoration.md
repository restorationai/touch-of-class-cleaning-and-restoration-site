## Why Gardendale Properties Are Vulnerable After Severe Weather

The geology and climate of this part of Ector County create a specific set of problems after a storm. Caliche-heavy soil drains poorly when saturated, which means standing water can pool against foundations and work its way into crawl spaces or slab seams long after the rain stops. Gardendale sits in a region where wind gusts during thunderstorm outflows routinely exceed 60 mph, enough to lift poorly fastened roof decking, push rain horizontally under ridge caps, and drive debris through windows and soffit vents.

Homes built during the oil-boom construction cycles common to this part of West Texas often used materials and framing techniques that were cost-effective at the time but leave less margin against sustained wind loading. Older composition shingles, for instance, can look intact from the ground while the adhesive strip has failed along entire rows, a condition that won't show up until the next hard rain soaks the decking underneath. That hidden damage is exactly the kind of thing a post-storm inspection is designed to catch before it becomes a mold or structural problem.

## Our Storm Damage Restoration Process in Gardendale

The first priority after any severe weather event is stopping new damage from entering the structure. That means emergency tarping of breached roofs, board-up of broken windows and doors, and a full perimeter walk to identify points where water has already infiltrated. Once the structure is stabilized, the work shifts to documentation, photographs, moisture mapping, and a written scope that can be handed directly to an insurance adjuster.

From there, the process moves to debris removal and material assessment. Wind-driven tree limbs and roofing material need to come off before any structural evaluation is meaningful. Interior work, drying wet insulation, removing saturated drywall, treating any surfaces where moisture has been sitting long enough to support microbial growth, follows a systematic drying protocol that uses readings rather than guesswork to confirm when materials are genuinely dry, not just dry at the surface. Reconstruction and finish work close out the job once the structure passes that threshold.

One pattern worth knowing in this part of West Texas: flat or low-slope roofing, common on both residential additions and commercial structures throughout the Gardendale area, tends to accumulate wind-blown debris in the drain channels and scuppers.
