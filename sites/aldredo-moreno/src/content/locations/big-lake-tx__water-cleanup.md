## Why Big Lake Properties See Water Damage Issues

Reagan County's climate runs between extremes: long stretches of heat and low humidity punctuated by flash flooding events that can drop several inches of rain in a matter of hours. That contrast is hard on plumbing. Pipes that bake under a slab all summer and then experience a sudden cold snap in January are prone to cracking, and Big Lake does see freezing temperatures, sometimes without much warning. Older homes in town, many built during the mid-20th century oil boom, often have galvanized supply lines that have corroded from the inside out over decades. You may not know there is a problem until a joint gives way and water is running under your floors.

The caliche-heavy soil under many Big Lake lots also creates a drainage situation that works against homeowners. Water that pools against a foundation during a storm has nowhere to go quickly, and hydrostatic pressure can push moisture through block or slab, especially in homes where the original waterproofing has aged out. Standing water in a garage, utility room, or crawl space is not just a surface problem here; it is often a sign that water has already wicked into wall cavities and subfloor material.

## Our Emergency Water Cleanup Process in Big Lake

When we arrive at a Big Lake property, the first priority is understanding where the water came from and where it has gone. We use moisture meters and thermal imaging to map saturation behind walls and under flooring before we move equipment, because pulling up the wrong materials first can slow the whole job down. Once we have a clear picture, we extract standing water with truck-mounted and portable units, then set industrial air movers and dehumidifiers calibrated to the ambient conditions.

West Texas air is dry, which helps drying times in some respects, but interior humidity inside a water-damaged home can climb fast once doors and windows are closed for equipment staging. We monitor readings throughout the drying phase and adjust equipment placement based on daily moisture logs, not a fixed schedule. When sewage is involved, such as a backed-up line or a toilet overflow, we treat the affected area as a contaminated zone and follow appropriate containment and disinfection steps before any drying equipment goes in.

ACS does not handle mold remediation or structural rebuilds. If the loss has progressed to visible mold growth or if framing and drywall need replacement, we will tell you plainly and point you to the right specialist for that work.
