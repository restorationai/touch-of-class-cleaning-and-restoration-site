## Why McCamey Properties See Water Damage Issues

The climate here is semi-arid, which creates a false sense of security. McCamey averages relatively little annual rainfall, but when storms do arrive, they tend to arrive hard and fast. The caliche and clay soil profile common throughout Upton County sheds water rather than absorbing it, so even a moderate downpour can sheet directly toward a home's foundation or push through a window well. Slab-on-grade construction, which dominates the housing stock across McCamey, offers no crawl space buffer. Water that gets under the slab or wicks into the concrete edge has a direct path to flooring adhesives, baseboards, and wall cavities.

Older homes in McCamey, many built during the oil boom decades of the mid-twentieth century, often have galvanized supply lines that have been corroding quietly for years. A pinhole leak inside a wall can saturate insulation and framing long before a homeowner notices a stain on the drywall. By the time the surface shows damage, the structural material behind it may already be compromised.

## Our Water Damage Restoration Process in McCamey

The first step on any job is a thorough moisture assessment. Standing water is obvious, but the water you cannot see is what drives long-term damage. Technicians use thermal imaging and calibrated moisture meters to map saturation behind walls, under flooring, and along the slab perimeter before a single piece of equipment is placed.

Once the scope is mapped, water extraction begins. Truck-mounted and portable extractors pull free-standing water from carpets, tile, and hard-surface floors. For sewage-related losses, the cleanup protocol changes: contaminated materials are handled and disposed of separately, and affected surfaces are treated before drying begins.

Structural drying follows extraction. Industrial air movers and dehumidifiers are positioned according to the moisture map, not just placed near visible wet spots. Drying logs are kept throughout the process so there is a documented record of daily moisture readings, equipment placement, and drying progress. That documentation matters when an insurance adjuster reviews the claim.

One thing worth knowing about water losses in McCamey specifically: the combination of hard West Texas water and older galvanized or copper supply lines means that when a pipe does fail, the water coming out often carries mineral sediment and rust particulate.
