## Why Greenwood Properties See Water Cleanup Issues

The Permian Basin's climate is deceptively dry on average, but Greenwood and the surrounding Midland County area are no strangers to intense, short-duration rainfall events that overwhelm drainage. When several inches fall in a matter of hours on hardpan caliche, the ground sheds water rather than absorbing it, and that runoff finds its way into garages, crawl spaces, and low-lying entry points. Older ranch-style homes common to this part of West Texas often have slab-on-grade construction with minimal elevation above grade, which means even modest pooling at the foundation can push water beneath flooring systems.

Indoor sources are just as common. The same arid conditions that crack soil also stress supply lines and water heater connections, fittings that expand and contract through years of temperature swings eventually fail, sometimes slowly and silently inside a wall cavity. By the time the damage is visible at the baseboard, the framing behind it may already be saturated.

## Our Water Cleanup Process in Greenwood

Effective water cleanup in this region starts with understanding what you can't see. Surface water is removed first using truck-mounted extraction equipment, but the real work is confirming moisture levels inside wall assemblies, under flooring, and in cabinet toe-kicks using thermal imaging and calibrated moisture meters. In West Texas homes with spray-foam insulation, increasingly common in newer builds around Midland County, moisture can be trapped in pockets that read dry at the surface while staying wet inches deeper.

Once extraction is complete, drying equipment is placed strategically: high-velocity air movers to disrupt the boundary layer of moisture at material surfaces, and refrigerant or desiccant dehumidifiers to pull that vapor out of the air. Greenwood's naturally low ambient humidity is actually an asset during the drying phase, when outdoor conditions allow, controlled ventilation can accelerate the process. Moisture readings are logged at each visit so the drying curve is documented, not guessed.

If flooring or drywall has absorbed water beyond recoverable thresholds, selective demolition removes only what needs to go, not entire rooms. Decisions about what to save versus remove are based on moisture readings, not assumptions.
