## Why West Odessa Properties Are Especially Vulnerable to Storm Damage

The Permian Basin's climate is unforgiving in ways that aren't always obvious until a storm has already done its work. West Odessa receives a significant share of its annual rainfall in intense, short-duration bursts rather than steady soaking rains. That pattern matters because the caliche-heavy soil common to this part of Ector County has poor drainage capacity. When two inches of rain falls in forty minutes, water doesn't percolate, it pools against foundations, flows under doors, and backs up through low-lying yard grades into garages and living spaces.

The region's housing stock adds another layer of complexity. Many West Odessa homes were built during the oil boom decades of the 1970s and 1980s using construction methods and roofing materials that were standard then but are now well past their designed service life. Aging composition shingles, original wood fascia, and single-pane windows are common, and they don't hold up well against large hail or sustained wind. A storm that causes cosmetic damage to a newer home can cause structural penetration on a property built forty or fifty years ago.

## Our Storm Damage Restoration Process in West Odessa

Every storm loss is different, but the sequence of work follows a clear logic. The first step is a thorough exterior and interior assessment, roof decking, attic insulation, exterior walls, windows, and any areas where wind-driven rain found an entry point. We photograph and document everything before a single piece of material is removed, because that documentation is what your insurance adjuster needs to process the claim accurately.

Once the scope is established, emergency tarping and board-up happens immediately to stop ongoing water intrusion. Standing water and saturated materials come out next, because the timeline for secondary damage in West Odessa's summer heat is short. In temperatures that routinely exceed 100 degrees Fahrenheit, wet insulation and wet wall cavities can begin supporting mold growth within 24 to 48 hours. Drying equipment goes in as soon as the structure is secure, and we monitor moisture readings daily until the building reaches acceptable levels. Structural repairs and finish work follow once the envelope is dry and stable.
