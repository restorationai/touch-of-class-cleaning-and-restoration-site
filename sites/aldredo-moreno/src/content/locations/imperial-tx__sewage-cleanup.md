Imperial sits in the heart of the Permian Basin, where the same caliche-heavy soil that makes drilling country so productive also creates real headaches for residential plumbing. Caliche layers shift and compact unevenly, and when a sewer line cracks or a septic system backs up under that kind of ground pressure, raw sewage does not stay put. It migrates toward foundations, seeps into crawl spaces, and surfaces in places you would not expect. ACS Enterprise responds to sewage backup calls throughout Imperial and the surrounding Midland County area, handling the removal, disinfection, and structural drying that a sewage event demands.

## Why Imperial Properties See Sewage Backup Issues

The geology of far West Texas is not kind to buried infrastructure. Caliche soil expands slightly when it absorbs moisture and contracts during dry spells, and Imperial sees both extremes. That cycle of swelling and shrinking puts lateral stress on older clay or cast-iron sewer lines, gradually shifting joints until a crack opens. When a crack opens, two things happen: groundwater infiltrates the line during wet periods, overloading the system, and sewage escapes the line during dry periods when pressure builds from inside.

Septic systems are common on properties outside of any municipal sewer connection in this part of Midland County. A septic tank that has not been pumped on schedule, or a drain field that has absorbed years of caliche-laden runoff, can fail suddenly rather than gradually. The result is sewage surfacing in a yard, backing up through floor drains, or pushing up through a toilet at the worst possible moment.

Older ranch-style homes common to the Imperial area were often built with plumbing that connects directly to a septic system with minimal cleanout access. That makes a backup harder to diagnose and harder to clear without professional equipment.

## Our Sewage Cleanup and Sanitization Process in Imperial

Sewage is classified as Category 3 water, the most contaminated type, because it carries bacteria, viruses, and parasites that survive on surfaces long after the visible mess is gone. The cleanup process reflects that reality.

When the crew arrives, the first step is stopping the source. If the backup is still active, isolating the affected drain or shutting off water supply to the fixture prevents additional contamination from spreading. From there, the process moves through these stages:

**Containment and removal.** Standing sewage is extracted using truck-mounted or portable extraction units. Porous materials that have absorbed contaminated water, including carpet, pad, drywall, and insulation below the flood line, are removed and bagged for disposal. In Imperial's dry climate, sewage can begin to concentrate and crust on surfaces quickly once the liquid is gone, which makes prompt removal important before residue hardens.

**Disinfection and sanitization.** Every surface the sewage contacted is treated with EPA-registered disinfectants appropriate for Category 3 contamination. This includes subfloor sheathing, concrete slab surfaces, wall cavities, and any HVAC components that may have drawn contaminated air through the system.

**Structural drying.** Even after sewage is removed, moisture remains in building materials. Air movers and dehumidifiers run until moisture readings return to baseline. In Imperial's low-humidity environment, drying times can be shorter than in coastal climates, but the slab-on-grade construction common to the area means that concrete subfloors need careful monitoring because they hold moisture longer than wood framing.

**Clearance verification.** Before work is closed out, surfaces are tested to confirm contamination levels are within safe limits. Documentation of the process is provided for insurance purposes.

## Imperial Insurance Coordination

Sewage backup coverage is not automatic on a standard homeowner's policy. Many carriers in Texas require a separate sewer backup endorsement, and whether a loss is covered often depends on the cause: a municipal line surcharge, a private lateral failure, or a septic system event may each be treated differently by the same policy.

ACS Enterprise documents the loss thoroughly, including photographs of affected areas, moisture readings, and a written scope of work, so that your adjuster has what they need to evaluate the claim.
