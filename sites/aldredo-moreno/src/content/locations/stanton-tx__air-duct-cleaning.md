Stanton sits in the heart of Martin County, where West Texas wind carries fine caliche dust and cotton-gin particulates across the Permian Basin flats for much of the year. That dust doesn't stay outside, it works into return-air grilles, coats evaporator coils, and builds up inside ductwork until your HVAC system is essentially pumping a slow-motion dust storm through every room. ACS Enterprise provides professional air duct cleaning for Stanton homeowners and property managers, clearing out the accumulation that West Texas conditions make almost inevitable.

## Why Stanton Properties See Duct Buildup Faster Than Most

The climate here is the main driver. Stanton averages low annual humidity but high wind events, and the surrounding agricultural land, cotton fields, feedlots, and bare caliche flats, generates a specific mix of fine particulates that standard HVAC filters struggle to capture completely. When a norther rolls through Martin County and the wind picks up, that dust infiltrates homes through gaps around doors, windows, and duct connections, then circulates through the air handler.

The housing stock adds another layer. A significant portion of Stanton's residential properties were built during the mid-twentieth-century oil-patch expansion, when duct systems were often sized for older, less efficient equipment and installed with mastic joints that dry out and gap over time. Gapped joints pull unconditioned attic air, loaded with insulation fibers and dust, directly into the supply stream. Homes with original sheet-metal ductwork from that era can accumulate debris in ways that newer flex-duct systems handle differently.

Dryer vents are a separate but related concern. In homes where laundry rooms share an interior wall with the HVAC closet, lint migration into the air handler cabinet is more common than most homeowners realize, and a partially blocked dryer vent is a genuine fire risk regardless of how new the appliance is.

## Our Air Duct Cleaning Process in Stanton

The work starts with a visual inspection of the accessible duct system, supply registers, return grilles, the air handler cabinet, and any visible flex connections in the attic or crawl space. In older Stanton homes, that inspection often turns up disconnected duct sections or collapsed flex runs that reduce airflow before cleaning even begins; we document those findings so you know exactly what you're dealing with.

Cleaning uses negative-air pressure: a high-powered vacuum collection unit attaches to the main trunk line and holds the system under continuous suction while rotating brush heads and compressed-air whips dislodge debris from branch runs. Dislodged material travels toward the collection unit rather than back into the living space. Supply and return sides are cleaned separately, because the contamination profile on each side differs.

If the inspection reveals microbial growth, visible mold or a persistent musty odor that survives cleaning, we offer EPA-registered duct sanitization applied as a fine mist after mechanical cleaning. We describe what we find; we don't recommend sanitization as a default add-on when the system doesn't need it.

Dryer vent cleaning follows the same negative-pressure principle, clearing lint from the full run from the dryer cabinet to the exterior termination cap. In Stanton's single-story ranch-style homes, that run is often short and straightforward; in split-level or two-story additions, it can involve longer horizontal runs that accumulate lint faster.
