## Why Imperial Properties See Duct Buildup Faster Than Average

The Permian Basin's combination of low humidity, persistent wind, and fine caliche dust creates a unique loading pattern inside residential and light-commercial ductwork. Unlike humid climates where moisture binds particles together and they settle, the dry air here keeps particulate suspended longer. That means more of it gets drawn into return vents and deposited on duct walls, coil surfaces, and blower components.

Homes in Imperial that were built during the oil-boom construction waves common to West Texas often feature ductwork installed quickly and without the tight sealing standards that became standard in later decades. Gaps at joints and poorly secured flex duct runs allow soil-level air, including the fine silica and caliche dust that is everywhere in this part of Midland County, to infiltrate the system between the insulation and the conditioned space. The result is buildup that goes beyond ordinary household dust.

Dryer vents in these same properties are worth noting. When exterior vent covers become clogged with the fibrous lint-and-dust combination common in this climate, dryers run longer, use more energy, and in worst cases create a fire hazard. ACS includes dryer vent cleaning as part of its service scope for Imperial customers who want the full system addressed in one visit.

## Our Air Duct Cleaning Process in Imperial

The process starts before any equipment goes into the duct. A technician walks the property to identify the supply and return layout, note any visible damage or disconnected runs, and check the condition of registers and grilles. In West Texas homes, it is not unusual to find registers that have been painted over multiple times or grilles that are partially blocked by furniture placement, both of which affect airflow and cleaning access.

Once the system is mapped, negative pressure is established using a high-powered vacuum collection unit connected to the main trunk line. This draws loosened debris toward the collection point rather than into the living space. Rotary brush agitation tools then work through each supply and return branch, dislodging compacted dust, lint, and debris from duct walls. For systems with flexible duct, the approach is adjusted to avoid tearing the inner liner.

Air duct sanitization is available as an add-on for customers whose systems show signs of biological growth or persistent odor. This step applies an EPA-registered sanitizing agent to interior duct surfaces after mechanical cleaning is complete. It does not replace cleaning and is not a substitute for addressing moisture sources, but it can reduce microbial load in systems that have been exposed to humidity events or rodent activity, both of which occur in Permian Basin properties.
