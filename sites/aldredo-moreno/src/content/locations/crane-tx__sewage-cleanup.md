## Why Crane Properties See Sewage Backup Issues

Crane County's soil profile is a significant factor. Caliche layers that sit close to the surface resist drainage, which means water and waste that should percolate away from a septic system or a failed sewer lateral instead pool and back-pressure into the home. Many residences in Crane were built during earlier oil booms, and the cast-iron or Orangeburg sewer lines from those eras are well past their service life. Orangeburg pipe, a fiber-and-pitch material common in mid-century construction, collapses inward as it ages, creating partial blockages that eventually become full backups. A slow drain that homeowners ignore for weeks can become a sewage overflow event overnight.

The region's temperature swings also matter. West Texas summers push ground temperatures high enough to accelerate the breakdown of organic waste in a backed-up line, which intensifies odor and bacterial load by the time a crew arrives on site. Winter cold snaps can cause shallow lines to shift, cracking joints and letting soil intrude. Both conditions mean the contamination profile in a Crane sewage event is often more aggressive than what you would find in a climate-moderated city.

## Our Sewage Cleanup and Sanitization Process in Crane

The first priority on any raw sewage call is containment. Before extraction equipment is staged, the affected area is assessed for the category and class of contamination. Raw sewage is Category 3 water, the most serious classification, and everything it contacts must be treated as contaminated regardless of how it looks on the surface.

Extraction removes standing waste and saturated material. Porous materials such as carpet, pad, drywall, and insulation that have direct contact with sewage are removed rather than dried in place. Attempting to dry Category 3 material is not a remediation strategy. Once bulk material is out, the structural surfaces that remain are cleaned, disinfected with EPA-registered antimicrobial agents, and dried to documented moisture levels. Air scrubbers with HEPA filtration run throughout the process to address airborne particulates and odor. Final sanitization is applied before any reconstruction work begins.

In Crane's older housing stock, crews often encounter subfloor assemblies that have held moisture longer than the visible damage suggests. A slab that reads dry at the surface can conceal saturation in the fill material beneath it. Thermal imaging and moisture meters are used to map the actual boundary of contamination, not just the visible boundary.
