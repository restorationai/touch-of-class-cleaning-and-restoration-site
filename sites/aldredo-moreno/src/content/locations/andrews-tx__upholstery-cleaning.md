## Why Andrews Homes See Upholstery Buildup

Andrews sits in the heart of the Permian Basin, where oilfield activity and ranch dust combine with some of the driest air in the state. That dryness is a double-edged sword for furniture. Low humidity keeps mildew from being much of a concern, but it also means airborne dust never really settles, it just recirculates through HVAC systems and lands on sofas, recliners, and dining chairs in a fine gray film. Homes near active pad sites or along unpaved county roads tend to see it worse, and households with a family member working in the oilfield often deal with an added layer: crude residue and machine grease transferred from work clothes onto the nearest recliner after a long shift. That combination of mineral grit and petroleum-based staining is something we plan for before a single cleaning pass starts.

## Our Upholstery Cleaning Process for Andrews Fabrics and Leather

We start every job with a dry pre-vacuum pass, not optional here the way it might be elsewhere, because caliche dust is abrasive and wet cleaning over ungroomed grit can grind fine scratches into both fabric weave and leather finish. Once the loose soil is lifted, we test for colorfastness and fiber type before choosing between low-moisture steam extraction for fabric pieces and pH-balanced cleaning with conditioning for leather. West Texas's arid climate is actually an advantage at this stage: dry times that would stretch to a full day in a humid region often finish in a few hours here, which means furniture goes back into use faster. For oilfield grease or petroleum-based stains, we apply a targeted pre-treatment before the main cleaning pass rather than relying on steam alone to lift it.
