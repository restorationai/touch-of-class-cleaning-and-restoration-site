## Why Andrews Properties See Repeated Storm Damage

The geology and climate of Andrews County create a specific pattern of storm damage that differs from what you'd find in wetter parts of Texas. The caliche-dominant soil drains poorly during heavy rain events, water that can't percolate fast enough pools against foundations and migrates into crawl spaces and lower-level rooms. At the same time, the region's wide-open geography gives thunderstorms and occasional tornadoes a long runway, meaning wind speeds are often higher by the time a system reaches Andrews than they were when it formed.

Roofing in this part of West Texas takes a particular beating. Asphalt shingles exposed to intense UV and heat cycles year-round are often already brittle before a hail event strikes. When hail hits a roof that's been baking at 150°F all summer, granule loss is severe, and the underlying felt or synthetic underlayment can crack rather than flex. That means water intrusion during the next rain is almost guaranteed, sometimes through dozens of small punctures that are invisible from the ground.

Wind-driven debris is another consistent factor. In a landscape with limited tree cover, it's fencing panels, equipment yard materials, and loose metal roofing from outbuildings that become projectiles. Damage to siding, windows, and exterior walls from that kind of debris requires a different assessment approach than fallen-tree damage would.

## Our Storm Damage Restoration Process in Andrews

The first priority after any storm event is stopping additional water or wind from entering the structure. That means emergency tarping of compromised roof sections, boarding broken windows, and securing any breached wall openings before a full assessment begins. Skipping this step, or delaying it, turns a roof claim into a mold claim within days in West Texas heat.

Once the structure is stabilized, we document the full scope of damage with photos and written notes calibrated to what insurance adjusters need to process a claim. In storm losses, the difference between a fully paid claim and a partial settlement often comes down to how thoroughly the initial documentation captures hidden damage, soaked insulation above a ceiling, moisture behind exterior sheathing, or subfloor saturation that doesn't show on the surface.

Drying equipment goes in as soon as affected materials are identified. In Andrews' low-humidity environment, drying times for structural materials can be faster than in coastal Texas, but that same dry air can mask moisture readings if equipment is placed incorrectly. We take sequential moisture readings across multiple days rather than pulling equipment based on a single measurement.

Reconstruction follows once materials are confirmed dry and structurally sound: roofing, siding, drywall, insulation, flooring, and any interior finishes affected by the event.
