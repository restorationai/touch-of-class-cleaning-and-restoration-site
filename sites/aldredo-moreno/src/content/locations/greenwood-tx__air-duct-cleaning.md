Greenwood, TX sits in the Permian Basin, where summer heat regularly pushes past 100°F and the region's notoriously fine, alkaline dust, the same caliche-laced particulate that coats windshields and window sills, finds its way into return air vents with every gust of West Texas wind. That combination of extreme heat cycling and persistent airborne grit means HVAC systems here work harder, run longer, and accumulate debris faster than systems in more temperate parts of the state. If your home or business in Greenwood hasn't had its ductwork inspected in the last few years, what's circulating through your vents may surprise you.

## Why Greenwood Properties See Duct Buildup Faster Than You'd Expect

The Permian Basin's climate creates a specific set of conditions that accelerate duct contamination. Cooling season in this part of Midland County stretches from late April well into October, meaning your air handler runs nearly continuously for half the year. That sustained airflow pulls dust, pet dander, and outdoor particulate through the filter and into the duct system, and West Texas dust is unusually fine, which means it passes through lower-MERV filters more readily than coastal or humid-climate dust would.

Greenwood's housing stock adds another layer. Many homes in the area were built during the oil-boom construction waves of the 1970s and 1980s, when flex duct and fiberglass-lined sheet metal were the standard. That interior lining degrades over time, especially under the thermal stress of 110°F attic temperatures, and degraded liner sheds fibers into the airstream. Older duct joints sealed with mastic or duct tape, not the metal-foil type, also tend to separate in extreme heat, creating gaps where unconditioned attic air (and whatever is in it) gets drawn into the supply side.

## Our Air Duct Cleaning Process in Greenwood

Every job starts with a visual inspection of the accessible ductwork, registers, and air handler cabinet. We use a camera scope on trunk lines when the geometry allows, so you can see the condition of the interior before any equipment runs. This matters in Greenwood specifically because attic duct runs here often show heat-related liner separation that changes the cleaning approach, a section of collapsed or detached flex duct needs to be repaired or replaced before negative-pressure cleaning, not after.

Once the system is assessed, we attach a truck-mounted or high-powered portable vacuum to the main trunk and work each branch line and register systematically, using compressed-air whips to dislodge debris from the duct walls and drive it toward the collection point. Supply and return sides are cleaned separately to avoid cross-contamination. If sanitization is requested, we apply an EPA-registered antimicrobial fogger after the mechanical cleaning is complete, not before, which is a sequencing error that reduces effectiveness.

Dryer vent cleaning follows a similar inspection-first approach. In single-story Greenwood homes with long horizontal dryer vent runs, common in ranch-style layouts, lint accumulation near bends is a genuine fire risk, and we clear the full run rather than just the accessible section near the appliance.
