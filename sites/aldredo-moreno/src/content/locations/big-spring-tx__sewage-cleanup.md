## Why Big Spring Properties Experience Sewage Backups

The geology beneath Big Spring is unforgiving to plumbing. Caliche layers, the dense, calcium-rich hardpan common across West Texas, don't absorb water the way looser soils do. When a sewer line cracks or a septic tank overflows, effluent has nowhere to migrate except back toward the structure or laterally along the pipe trench. That means a backup that might drain away harmlessly in a sandier region can pool against a foundation here for hours.

Big Spring's housing stock adds another layer of complexity. Many residential neighborhoods contain homes built in the 1950s through 1970s, an era when cast-iron and Orangeburg sewer pipes were standard. Both materials degrade over decades, cast iron corrodes from the inside out, and Orangeburg (a tar-and-paper composite) softens and collapses under sustained moisture. A line that's held for 60 years can fail without warning, sending raw sewage into a bathroom, utility room, or crawl space with little notice.

The city's municipal sewer system also faces capacity pressure during the heavy rain events that occasionally sweep through Howard County. A slow-moving storm cell can dump several inches of rain in a short window, overwhelming lift stations and causing sanitary sewer overflows that back up into connected structures, particularly in lower-lying areas of town.

## Our Sewage Cleanup and Sanitization Process in Big Spring

Sewage is classified as Category 3 water, the most contaminated category in the IICRC's water damage framework, which means standard water extraction protocols aren't sufficient. Every sewage job requires full containment, personal protective equipment, and EPA-registered disinfectants rated for bacteriological and viral pathogens.

The process at a Big Spring property typically moves through these stages:

**Containment and assessment.** The affected area is isolated to prevent cross-contamination to clean spaces. Moisture meters and thermal imaging identify how far effluent has traveled, especially important on slab-on-grade construction, where sewage can migrate beneath flooring without surfacing visibly.

**Extraction and removal.** Standing sewage is extracted using truck-mounted or portable units. Porous materials, drywall, insulation, carpet, and subflooring, that have contacted raw sewage are removed and bagged for disposal per Texas Commission on Environmental Quality (TCEQ) guidelines for biohazardous waste.

**Disinfection and deodorization.** Hard surfaces, concrete, and structural framing are treated with EPA-registered antimicrobial agents. Odor from sewage isn't cosmetic, hydrogen sulfide and ammonia compounds penetrate wood framing and masonry, and they require targeted chemical treatment, not just ventilation.

**Drying and monitoring.** After sanitization, commercial drying equipment, air movers, dehumidifiers, brings affected structural materials to acceptable moisture levels. In Big Spring's dry climate, ambient conditions can actually accelerate surface drying, but that can be misleading: concrete slabs and wall cavities retain moisture longer than surface readings suggest, and monitoring continues until readings are stable.

## Working with Insurance on Sewage Claims in Big Spring

Sewage backup coverage is not automatic under a standard homeowner's policy, it typically requires a separate sewer backup rider. That distinction matters when you're filing a claim in Texas, because carriers will ask whether the source was an internal blockage (sometimes covered under the base policy) or a municipal sewer overflow (usually requiring the rider).

ACS Enterprise documents the loss thoroughly: photographs of affected materials, moisture readings, a written scope of work, and itemized removal records. That documentation supports your adjuster's review and reduces back-and-forth during the claims process. If you're unsure what your policy covers, your carrier's claims line can clarify before work begins, getting that answer early prevents disputes over scope later.

## Local Note

One thing that catches out-of-area contractors working in Big Spring: the caliche hardpan beneath many properties holds moisture in a counterintuitive way. After a sewage overflow soaks into a dirt-floor crawl space or an unpaved utility area, the surface can appear dry within a day or two because the top layer desiccates quickly in West Texas heat. The saturated caliche layer underneath, however, continues off-gassing hydrogen sulfide and supporting bacterial activity for much longer. Checking only surface conditions and calling the job complete is a mistake, subsurface moisture and air quality both need to confirm clearance before containment comes down.
