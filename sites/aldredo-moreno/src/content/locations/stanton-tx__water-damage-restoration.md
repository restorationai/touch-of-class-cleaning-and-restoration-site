When that water finds its way into a home, through a foundation seam, a failed water heater, or a supply line that finally gave out, it moves fast and hides longer than most homeowners expect. ## Why Stanton Properties See Water Damage Issues

The same caliche and clay soils that make Martin County farmland productive also create drainage headaches around foundations. During the intense, short-duration storms common to West Texas, water pools against exterior walls before it has a chance to run off. Older homes in Stanton, many built during the mid-20th century oil-field expansion, often have pier-and-beam or shallow slab foundations that were never designed with today's storm intensity in mind. Gaps around utility penetrations, aging cast-iron or galvanized supply lines, and evaporative cooler pans that overflow during monsoon-season humidity swings are among the most common entry points we see in this part of the Permian Basin.

Well water is also a factor here in a way it isn't in larger metro areas. Properties on private wells sometimes run higher mineral content, which accelerates corrosion in older copper and galvanized lines. A pinhole leak behind drywall can go unnoticed for weeks in a low-traffic utility room, and by the time a homeowner notices a soft spot in the floor or a musty smell in a hallway, the damage has already spread well beyond the visible stain.

## Our Water Damage Restoration Process in Stanton

Every water damage job starts with moisture mapping, not a visual scan, but readings taken with calibrated meters at the wall cavity, subfloor, and ceiling plane. In West Texas homes with adobe-style stucco exteriors or thick interior plaster, surface readings can look acceptable while the substrate holds significantly more moisture. We document the full moisture picture before any drying equipment is placed.

From there, the process moves through water extraction, targeted structural drying, and monitoring:

- **Water removal and extraction**: Standing water is extracted using truck-mounted or portable units, depending on access. Crawl spaces under pier-and-beam homes require different equipment positioning than open slab areas.
- **Structural drying**: Industrial air movers and dehumidifiers are placed based on the moisture map, not just wherever they fit. In Martin County's low-humidity climate, ambient conditions can actually help drying, but only if equipment placement takes advantage of them correctly.
- **Daily monitoring**: Moisture readings are logged each day. Equipment is adjusted or repositioned as materials dry unevenly, which is common in older homes with mixed wall construction.
- **Controlled demolition when needed**: Saturated drywall, insulation, or subfloor sections that won't dry in place are removed to prevent secondary issues from developing in enclosed cavities.

## Stanton Insurance Coordination

Water damage claims in Martin County tend to move through standard homeowner's policy channels, but the documentation requirements matter. Carriers want to see moisture readings, affected square footage, and a clear timeline of when the loss occurred versus when it was discovered. We photograph and document throughout the job, not just at the start, because adjusters sometimes request mid-drying evidence to confirm that materials were actually wet, not just discolored.

If your policy includes a deductible that concerns you, ask your adjuster specifically about coverage for secondary damage. In Texas, a sudden and accidental discharge from a supply line is typically a covered peril, while slow leaks discovered late may be treated differently depending on your policy language.

## Local Note

One thing that comes up specifically in Stanton and the surrounding Martin County area: evaporative coolers, swamp coolers, are still common here because the climate is dry enough for them to work well most of the year. But during the late-summer monsoon pattern, when humidity climbs and the coolers run continuously, the drain pans and water lines on rooftop units can overflow or develop slow drips into the ceiling below. It's a water source that often gets overlooked when a homeowner is trying to figure out where moisture is coming from, because the cooler is working fine, it's just moving more water than the pan was designed to handle at high humidity. If you're seeing ceiling stains or soft drywall near a rooftop cooler, that's worth checking before assuming the roof itself is the problem.
