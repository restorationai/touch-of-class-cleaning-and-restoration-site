Big Lake sits in Reagan County, deep in the Permian Basin, where the soil is a dense caliche-heavy clay that drains poorly and can shift under foundations during the region's occasional heavy rains. When a sewer line backs up or a septic system overflows here, that standing sewage has nowhere to go fast, and it can saturate subfloor materials and wall cavities before the smell even reaches the next room. ACS Enterprise responds to sewage backup calls across Big Lake and the surrounding Reagan County area, handling the extraction, disinfection, and structural drying that turns a hazardous situation back into a livable space.

## Why Big Lake Properties See Sewage Backup Problems

The Permian Basin's boom-and-bust population cycles have left Big Lake with a housing stock that spans several decades of construction, and older homes in particular tend to have aging cast-iron or clay-tile sewer laterals that corrode, root-intrude, or simply collapse under the weight of caliche soil that expands when wet. A single hard rain after a dry stretch can saturate that clay layer and put enough hydrostatic pressure on a lateral to push sewage back through floor drains and toilets.

Septic systems are also common on the outskirts of Big Lake, where city sewer connections are not available. Septic tanks that have not been pumped on schedule, or drain fields that have become compacted, are among the most frequent sources of sewage overflow calls in this part of West Texas. The problem tends to surface quickly in summer when heat accelerates bacterial activity inside the tank.

## Our Sewage Cleanup and Sanitization Process in Big Lake

Raw sewage is classified as Category 3 water, the most contaminated class, because it carries bacteria, viruses, and parasites that can cause serious illness. The cleanup process is not simply mopping and airing out. Here is what the work actually involves:

**Containment first.** Affected rooms are isolated to prevent cross-contamination to clean areas of the home. This is especially important in older Big Lake homes where open floor plans or shared HVAC returns can spread airborne pathogens.

**Extraction and removal.** Standing sewage is extracted using truck-mounted or portable units. Porous materials that cannot be adequately disinfected, including saturated carpet, pad, and drywall below the flood line, are removed and bagged for disposal according to Texas Commission on Environmental Quality guidelines.

**Disinfection and sanitization.** Hard surfaces, subfloor sheathing, and framing are treated with EPA-registered disinfectants. This step is applied, allowed to dwell, and then wiped or rinsed, not simply sprayed and left.

**Structural drying.** Industrial air movers and dehumidifiers run until moisture readings in the subfloor and wall cavities return to acceptable levels. In Reagan County's low-humidity climate, drying times can be shorter than in coastal Texas, but caliche subsoils under slab foundations can hold residual moisture longer than the surface suggests.

**Post-cleanup verification.** Moisture readings are documented before equipment is removed. If anything reads elevated, drying continues.
