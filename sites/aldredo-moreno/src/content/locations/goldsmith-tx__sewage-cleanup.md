## Why Goldsmith Properties See Sewage Backup Problems

Ector County's geology plays a direct role in sewer and septic failures that homeowners here deal with more often than residents of wetter regions might expect. Caliche layers near the surface resist water movement, which means that during the brief but intense West Texas rain events that push through this area, ground saturation can spike quickly and overwhelm septic drain fields that otherwise function fine in dry conditions. When the soil can't absorb effluent fast enough, pressure builds back toward the tank and into the structure.

Older homes in Goldsmith, many built during the mid-century oil boom, often have cast-iron or Orangeburg sewer laterals that have been degrading for decades. These materials crack, root-intrude, and collapse in ways that PVC pipe does not. A slow drain that seems like a minor nuisance can be the first sign that a lateral is failing and that a full sewage backup is weeks or months away. The flat topography around Goldsmith also means there is very little natural grade to help sewage move away from structures; lines that lose even a small amount of slope due to soil shift can begin holding solids and backing up regularly.

## Our Sewage Cleanup and Sanitization Process in Goldsmith

Raw sewage is classified as Category 3 water, the most contaminated classification in the industry, and the cleanup process reflects that. When the ACS Enterprise team arrives at a Goldsmith property, the first priority is stopping ongoing exposure: identifying the source, isolating affected areas, and making sure no one re-enters contaminated zones without appropriate protection.

Extraction comes next. Standing sewage is removed using truck-mounted or portable extraction equipment, depending on site access. In Goldsmith's single-story ranch-style homes, a common footprint in this part of Ector County, sewage backups frequently affect utility rooms, hallways, and slab-level bathrooms simultaneously, so the extraction phase often covers more square footage than the initial call suggests.

After bulk removal, affected materials are assessed. Porous materials like drywall, insulation, and carpet that have contacted Category 3 water typically cannot be restored and are removed. Hard surfaces, concrete slab, ceramic tile, metal fixtures, are cleaned with EPA-registered disinfectants and allowed to dry under controlled conditions. Air scrubbers with HEPA filtration run throughout the process to reduce airborne pathogen load. A final sanitization pass addresses residual contamination before any reconstruction begins.
