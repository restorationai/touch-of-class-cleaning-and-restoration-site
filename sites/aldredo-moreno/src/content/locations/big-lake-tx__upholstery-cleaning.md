## Why Big Lake Furniture Wears Differently

Big Lake sits in dry, wind-exposed ranch and oil-field country, and that shows up in the furniture before it shows up anywhere else in the house. Fine caliche and mesquite dust settle into fabric weave and never fully vacuum out on their own. Add in the area's hard, mineral-heavy well and municipal water, and you get a second problem: DIY spot cleaning with a rented machine often leaves mineral rings behind as the water evaporates, which look worse than the original stain. Leather furniture has its own issue here. Low humidity and long stretches of sun through west-facing windows dry out natural oils faster than in wetter parts of the state, and leather that isn't conditioned regularly starts to crack at the seams and armrests within a few years.

## Our Upholstery Cleaning Process in Big Lake

We start by identifying the fabric or leather type, since a hot-water extraction pass that's perfectly safe on a poly-blend sectional can shrink or water-spot certain natural fibers. For fabric pieces, we pre-treat ground-in soil and oil-field dust, then use low-moisture steam extraction so the piece isn't left damp for days in a house that already runs dry, which can otherwise cause fibers to stiffen unevenly as they cure. For leather sofas and recliners, common in Big Lake homes given the ranching and oil-industry presence, we clean with a leather-safe solution and follow with a conditioner that replaces oils the dry climate strips out. We finish with a grooming pass on fabric pile to even out the nap and pull the piece back to something close to its original texture.
