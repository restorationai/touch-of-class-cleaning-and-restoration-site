## Why Garden City Homes See Different Upholstery Wear

Garden City sits in Glasscock County, where sandy, wind-exposed soil and ranching operations put a steady layer of fine dust into every home, even with windows shut most of the year. That dust settles into upholstery fibers and, combined with low humidity, acts almost like sandpaper on fabric weave over time, which is why couches in this part of West Texas often look dull and feel stiff long before they show visible stains. Leather furniture has the opposite problem: the same dry air that keeps dust airborne also strips natural oils out of hide, leading to cracking around seat cushions and armrests years earlier than leather would crack in a humid coastal city. Homes with well water can also see mineral deposits transfer onto light-colored fabric from hands and clothing, leaving faint gray or rust-toned marks that regular household cleaners tend to smear rather than lift.

## Our Upholstery Cleaning Process for West Texas Conditions

We start with a dry-soil extraction pass, pulling as much of that caliche dust and grit out of the weave as possible before any moisture is introduced. Fabric pieces get a pH-balanced pre-treatment suited to the fiber type, followed by hot water extraction that's calibrated lighter than what we'd use in a humid climate, since Garden City's dry air means fabric can hold heat and moisture differently than upholstery in wetter regions. Leather pieces get a separate conditioning step rather than a straight steam treatment, since steam alone can accelerate the drying and cracking that's already a known issue in local leather furniture. We finish with a grooming pass to reset the nap on fabric and a light conditioning wipe on leather so the piece isn't left tacky or over-oiled.
