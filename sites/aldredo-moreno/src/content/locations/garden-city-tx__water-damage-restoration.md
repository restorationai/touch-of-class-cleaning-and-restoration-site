## Why Garden City Properties See Water Damage Issues

The same geology that makes Glasscock County productive oil country creates real challenges for residential plumbing and foundations. Caliche and clay soils expand when wet and contract sharply during dry spells, and Garden City sees both extremes. That movement stresses underground supply lines and sewer laterals over time, and a pipe that holds through a decade of dry summers may finally give during a hard freeze in January or a rare heavy rain event in spring.

Most of the housing stock in and around Garden City was built for ranching and agricultural families, which means pier-and-beam construction is common alongside slab foundations. Pier-and-beam homes are particularly vulnerable to water intrusion because the crawl space beneath the floor acts as a collection basin. Standing water under a wood subfloor can begin promoting mold colonization within 24 to 48 hours, and the damage is often invisible from inside the house until floors start to soften or buckle.

Well water is the norm for many properties in Glasscock County, and pressure tank failures or waterline breaks at the wellhead can release hundreds of gallons before a homeowner notices a pressure drop. That kind of slow, hidden release soaks insulation, wall cavities, and subfloor materials long before it shows up as a wet spot on the carpet.

## Our Water Damage Restoration Process in Garden City

Every job starts with moisture mapping. Before any equipment is placed, the crew uses thermal imaging cameras and calibrated moisture meters to find exactly where water has traveled, including inside wall cavities, beneath flooring, and along ceiling joists. In a slab-on-grade home, this step is critical because water wicks laterally under vinyl plank or tile and can saturate a wide area while the surface still feels dry underfoot.

Once the scope is documented, standing water is removed with truck-mounted or portable extraction units. After bulk water is out, the drying phase begins using industrial air movers and refrigerant or desiccant dehumidifiers sized to the affected square footage. Readings are logged daily so drying progress is measurable and documentable, which matters when an insurance adjuster reviews the claim file.

If building materials cannot be dried in place within acceptable moisture thresholds, controlled demolition removes only what is necessary: a section of drywall, a strip of flooring, or insulation from a wall cavity. The goal is to protect the structure while minimizing the scope of rebuild work.

In Glasscock County, many rural properties rely on septic systems rather than municipal sewer, and a water damage event that involves a sewage backup or a drain field failure is a different category of loss than a clean-water pipe break.
