## Why Goldsmith Properties Are Vulnerable to Storm Damage

Goldsmith sits in the heart of the Permian Basin, where the flat, open terrain offers almost no natural windbreak. That geography matters when a line of thunderstorms rolls through: wind speeds that might be moderated by tree cover or topography elsewhere arrive here largely unimpeded. Hail is a particular threat, the region sees some of the highest hail frequency in Texas, and stones large enough to crack asphalt shingles, dent metal roofing, and shatter skylights are not unusual during spring and early summer storm seasons.

The local housing stock adds another layer of complexity. Many homes and commercial structures in Goldsmith were built during oil-boom cycles, using materials and construction methods common to their era. Older wood-framed structures with original roofing can absorb wind uplift differently than newer builds, and once the envelope is breached, interior materials, insulation, drywall, wood sheathing, begin absorbing moisture within hours. The caliche-heavy soil common to this part of Ector County also drains slowly after heavy rain, which means water tends to pool against foundations and push toward crawl spaces and slab edges rather than dispersing quickly.

## Our Storm Damage Restoration Process in Goldsmith

Every storm loss is different, but the sequence of work follows a consistent logic: stop further damage first, document thoroughly, then restore.

**Stabilization and tarping.** When wind or hail has opened a roof or broken out windows, the first priority is preventing additional water intrusion. We cover breached areas with heavy-duty tarps or board-up materials before rain has a chance to compound interior damage.

**Moisture mapping and documentation.** Water that enters through a storm breach doesn't stay where it lands. We use moisture meters and thermal imaging to trace where water has migrated, behind walls, under flooring, into insulation cavities, and document every affected area with photographs and written measurements. This documentation is the foundation of a defensible insurance claim.

**Debris removal and structural assessment.** Downed tree limbs, displaced roofing materials, and broken glass are cleared safely. We assess structural components for compromise before any interior work begins.

**Drying and dehumidification.** Once the structure is secured, commercial drying equipment is deployed to bring moisture levels back to baseline. In the Permian Basin's low-humidity climate, ambient air can assist drying, but materials like OSB sheathing and blown insulation still require active equipment to dry fully, and we monitor readings daily until clearance is reached.

**Repairs and reconstruction.** Roofing, siding, windows, drywall, and flooring are repaired or replaced to match pre-loss condition, coordinated with your insurance adjuster's scope.

## Local Note

One thing that catches property owners off guard in this part of Ector County: after a storm with significant hail, the damage to a roof can look minor from the ground, a few dings on gutters, some granule loss visible in the downspout. But metal roofing common on agricultural outbuildings and some residential structures in the Goldsmith area can develop micro-fractures at fastener points that don't leak immediately. By the next significant rain, water is tracking along the fastener shaft and appearing as a ceiling stain well away from the actual entry point. If you've had hail and notice any interior staining in the weeks following a storm, it's worth having the roof inspected before assuming the structure came through clean.
