## Why Greenwood Properties See Water Damage Issues

The geology and climate of this part of West Texas set up a particular pattern of water intrusion that homeowners here recognize. Caliche layers close to the surface don't absorb rainfall, they redirect it. During heavy rain events, that means water moves laterally toward structures rather than percolating down. Slab-on-grade construction, which dominates the housing stock in this part of Midland County, leaves little buffer between the foundation and standing water.

Inside the home, the dry West Texas air creates a false sense of security. Surfaces dry quickly, sometimes within hours, but that evaporation pulls moisture deeper into wall cavities, subfloor assemblies, and insulation. A pipe leak that looks minor at the surface can have been feeding moisture into a wall for days before a homeowner notices a soft spot in the drywall or a musty odor near a baseboard. Catching that hidden saturation early is what separates a two-day drying project from a full wall-cavity rebuild.

## Our Water Damage Restoration Process in Greenwood

Every water damage job starts with moisture mapping, not a visual inspection, but readings taken with penetrating and non-penetrating meters across the full affected area. In Greenwood homes, where slab construction means water often migrates along the concrete before wicking up into wall framing, that mapping frequently extends further than the visible wet zone suggests.

Once the scope is established, extraction comes first. Standing water and surface saturation are removed with truck-mounted and portable extraction units before any drying equipment is placed. Skipping straight to air movers without thorough extraction is a common shortcut that extends drying time significantly, the equipment ends up working against residual bulk water rather than evaporating bound moisture from materials.

After extraction, the drying phase uses a calculated combination of commercial-grade desiccant or refrigerant dehumidifiers and high-velocity air movers. The ratio and placement are adjusted based on daily moisture readings, not a fixed schedule. In Greenwood's low-humidity climate, ambient conditions can accelerate surface drying while masking slower moisture release from denser materials like OSB sheathing or concrete block. Daily monitoring catches that gap before it causes secondary damage.
