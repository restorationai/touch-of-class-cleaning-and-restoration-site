## Why Andrews Carpets Take a Harder Hit

The Permian Basin's climate is punishing on interior textiles. Andrews sees hot, dry summers with frequent dust events, and when the wind picks up across the open range, fine particulate matter, including caliche, silica, and petroleum-adjacent dust from nearby oilfield activity, settles into carpet pile faster than it can be removed by routine cleaning. That same dry air causes carpet fibers to become brittle and static-prone, which actually attracts and holds more airborne debris.

Winter brings its own challenge. Left too long, that residue degrades the adhesive layer between the pile and the backing, shortening the life of an otherwise healthy carpet.

Homes and commercial properties in Andrews also tend to run central HVAC systems hard for most of the year. Recirculated air means dust and allergens cycle through the home repeatedly, and carpet acts as the primary filter, catching what the system misses. ## Our Carpet Cleaning Process in Andrews

Because of the specific soil composition around Andrews, we adjust our pre-treatment chemistry before we ever run a wand across your floor. Caliche and silica-based soils are alkaline, and a neutral or acidic pre-spray does very little to break them down. We use an alkaline pre-conditioner calibrated to West Texas soil profiles, applied with enough dwell time to actually emulsify what's bonded to the fibers, not just wet the surface.

From there, the process moves through hot-water extraction at temperatures and pressures suited to your carpet's fiber type. Nylon and polyester, the most common choices in Andrews residential construction, respond well to high-heat extraction. Wool or natural blends, less common here but present in some older homes, require cooler water and gentler agitation to avoid shrinkage.

For commercial carpet cleaning in Andrews, whether that's an oilfield office, a medical clinic, or a retail space, we treat high-traffic lanes separately. Those areas accumulate a compressed, oxidized soil layer that requires mechanical agitation with a counter-rotating brush before extraction will reach it. Skipping that step is why commercial carpets often look clean right after a service and dingy again within days.

Stain removal follows extraction. Petroleum-based stains, not uncommon in a working oilfield town, require a solvent-based spotter before any water-based cleaning. Applying water first sets those stains permanently.
