Big Lake sits in the heart of Reagan County, where the West Texas sky can turn from clear to violent with little warning. Thunderstorms rolling off the Permian Basin carry straight-line winds, large hail, and flash flooding that can strip roofing, push water under exterior doors, and leave standing water inside homes within minutes. When that happens, the clock starts immediately, saturated insulation, swollen door frames, and waterlogged flooring all worsen the longer they sit.

## Why Big Lake Properties See Storm Damage Issues

Reagan County's flat, semi-arid terrain gives storms almost nothing to slow them down. Wind gusts during severe weather events regularly exceed what older construction was built to handle, and Big Lake has a mix of housing stock that reflects decades of oil-field boom-and-bust cycles. Homes built during earlier boom periods often feature wood-frame construction with minimal roof bracing, making them more vulnerable to uplift damage from strong gusts. Hail here is not a rare event, the region sits within one of the most active hail corridors in the country, and a single storm can puncture roofing membranes, crack skylights, and leave thousands of small entry points for water to follow.

The clay-heavy soils common across this part of West Texas also complicate drainage. When rain falls faster than the ground can absorb it, water sheets across yards and toward foundations. Homes with older or shallow foundation perimeters can see water intrusion at the base of exterior walls within the first hour of a heavy storm.

## Our Storm Damage Restoration Process in Big Lake

When ACS Enterprise arrives after a storm, the first priority is a full exterior and interior assessment before any material is moved or removed. That means walking the roofline, checking soffits and fascia for wind damage, and tracing any water entry point from the outside in. Inside, moisture meters and thermal imaging identify where water has traveled behind walls or under flooring, water that looks contained at the surface often isn't.

Once the scope is clear, the process moves to water extraction and controlled drying. Industrial air movers and dehumidifiers are positioned based on the structure's layout, not a generic formula. In West Texas, the outdoor air is often dry enough that strategic ventilation can assist the drying process, but that only works when outdoor humidity is genuinely lower than indoor readings, something the crew confirms with monitoring equipment before opening the structure.

Debris removal runs alongside drying. Storm-deposited material, damaged insulation, and compromised building components are cleared and documented for insurance purposes. ACS handles the water damage and cleanup side of the work. For structural repairs or rebuild scope, we can point you toward the right contractor once the water mitigation is complete.

## Local Note

One thing that catches Big Lake homeowners off guard after a major storm is how quickly the dry West Texas air can create a false sense of recovery. Surfaces dry out fast here, and a wall that feels dry to the touch within a day or two of a storm may still hold moisture in the wall cavity or behind baseboards. That surface dryness can mask ongoing saturation that leads to odor and material degradation over the following weeks. Moisture meter readings taken at multiple depths, not just at the surface, are the only reliable way to confirm a structure is actually dry, and that step should not be skipped just because the air outside is arid.
