## Why Greenwood Properties Face Distinct Storm Damage Challenges

The Permian Basin sits in a corridor where cold fronts collide with warm Gulf moisture, producing some of the most intense hail events in the country. Greenwood properties are exposed to that same pattern, and the local building stock reflects it, many homes in the area feature wood-framed construction with composition shingle roofing that can take a single large-hail event and go from serviceable to compromised in minutes. Hailstones that bruise or crack shingles don't always create an immediate visible leak, but they fracture the granule layer that protects the mat underneath. The next rain event, even a mild one, finds those weak points.

High-wind events compound the problem. Straight-line winds common to this part of Texas can lift fascia, peel soffit panels, and drive rain horizontally into gaps that normally shed water without issue. Flat or low-slope roof sections on commercial outbuildings and some residential additions are particularly vulnerable, since they rely on membrane integrity that wind stress can compromise at seams and penetrations.

Tree damage is a separate category worth noting. The area's caliche-heavy soils limit how deeply root systems anchor, meaning trees stressed by drought, a recurring condition in this part of West Texas, are more likely to topple or drop major limbs under wind load than the same species would in wetter regions.

## Our Storm Damage Restoration Process in Greenwood

When a storm damage call comes in from Greenwood, the first priority is stopping ongoing exposure. That means tarping breached rooflines, boarding compromised windows or doors, and identifying any points where water has already entered the structure. This emergency stabilization phase happens before any detailed assessment, because a roof open to the sky during a second storm event can turn a manageable claim into a gut-renovation.

Once the structure is stabilized, the team conducts a full moisture mapping survey using thermal imaging and calibrated moisture meters. Water that enters through a roof breach doesn't stay at the ceiling, it follows framing members, runs along top plates, and can reach wall cavities two or three rooms away from the visible damage. Finding the full extent of intrusion before drying begins is what separates a complete restoration from one that leaves hidden moisture to feed mold growth weeks later.

Debris removal, structural drying, and material replacement are sequenced based on what the inspection reveals. Exterior repairs, roofing, siding, window replacement, are coordinated with the interior drying timeline so the building envelope is closed before finish work begins.

Hail and wind damage claims are among the most common residential insurance losses in this part of Texas, and carriers have seen enough of them that documentation standards are high. ## Local Note

Caliche soil throughout Midland County drains poorly when it's saturated, which is rare, but storm events that drop significant rainfall in a short window can overwhelm it. When that happens in Greenwood, water that would normally sheet away from a foundation instead ponds and finds any low point in the structure's perimeter. If your property took on water through a foundation wall or floor slab during a storm event, that's a separate moisture intrusion pathway from the roof breach, and it needs to be assessed and dried independently, not assumed to be resolved once the roof is repaired.
