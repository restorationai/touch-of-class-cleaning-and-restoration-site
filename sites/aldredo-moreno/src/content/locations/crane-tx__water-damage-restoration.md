## Why Crane Properties See Water Damage Issues

The West Texas climate is deceptive. Crane averages sparse annual rainfall, but when storms do arrive, they tend to arrive hard and fast, overwhelming drainage that was never designed for high-volume runoff. Flash events can push water through garage doors, window wells, and any foundation crack that has widened in the summer heat. Equally common are internal failures: the intense temperature swings between summer highs and winter cold snaps stress plumbing joints and water heater connections, and a fitting that held through a dozen mild winters can split during a hard freeze.

Crane's housing stock leans toward mid-century construction, and many of those homes were built on slab foundations with copper or galvanized supply lines that are now well past their design life. When galvanized pipe corrodes from the inside and finally lets go, the water doesn't just wet the floor. It travels under vinyl flooring and into the concrete slab's edge, where moisture meters at the surface can read nearly dry while the perimeter stays saturated for days.

## Our Water Damage Restoration Process in Crane

When we arrive on a Crane property, the first step is a full moisture assessment, not just a visual scan. We use thermal imaging alongside pin and pinless moisture meters to map where water has migrated, including areas behind baseboards and inside wall cavities that look untouched from the outside.

Once we understand the full scope, we move to water extraction. Standing water comes out first with truck-mounted or portable extraction units, followed by targeted drying with commercial-grade air movers and refrigerant or desiccant dehumidifiers sized to the square footage and the ambient conditions. West Texas summer heat actually accelerates evaporation in open areas, but it also raises the humidity load on equipment, so we monitor drying progress with daily readings and adjust equipment placement accordingly. Drying logs are kept throughout the job, which matters when you file a claim and your carrier asks for documentation.

If flooring, drywall, or insulation has absorbed enough moisture that it cannot be dried in place within a safe window, we remove it cleanly and document the material for your adjuster. Nothing gets torn out that doesn't need to go.

## Local Note

One thing worth knowing about older Crane homes: the interior partition walls in mid-century construction here often used a thicker plaster-over-lath assembly rather than modern drywall. Plaster is denser and holds moisture longer than gypsum board, but it also doesn't crumble and lose structural integrity as quickly. That means we sometimes have a wider drying window before removal becomes necessary, but it also means surface moisture readings can be misleading. A wall that feels dry to the touch and reads low on a surface meter can still have saturated lath behind it. We account for this by taking deep-wall readings and extending drying times when the construction type warrants it.
