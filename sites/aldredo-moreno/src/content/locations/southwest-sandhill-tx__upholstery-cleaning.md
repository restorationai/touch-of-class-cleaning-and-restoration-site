## Why Southwest Sandhill Homes See Upholstery Wear and Soiling

West Texas dust doesn't stay outside. It rides in on clothes, shoes, and pets, and it settles into upholstery fibers where regular vacuuming only lifts the surface layer. Combine that with the region's hard municipal water, which leaves behind mineral residue any time a stain gets treated with plain water or an over-the-counter spray, and you get fabric that looks gray and feels stiff even in homes that are cleaned regularly. Leather furniture has its own issue here: the same low humidity that keeps mold from being much of a concern also dries out hides faster, so leather upholstery cleaning in this part of Texas is as much about conditioning as it is about lifting soil.

## Our Upholstery Cleaning Process for West Texas Homes

We start by identifying the fabric type, natural fiber, synthetic blend, or leather, because each responds differently to heat and moisture. For fabric upholstery, we pre-treat ground-in dust and oil residue, then use hot water extraction to pull soil and mineral buildup out of the weave rather than just pushing it around. Furniture steam cleaning works well for most sofas and sectionals common in Sandhill-area homes, but we adjust water temperature and dwell time for anything with a delicate or vintage weave. Leather pieces get a separate process entirely: a pH-balanced cleaner followed by a conditioner that replaces the oils West Texas air pulls out of hides over time. Because humidity here is low, drying time after extraction is usually faster than what you'd see in a coastal or East Texas city, which means less time with damp cushions and less risk of the mineral ring-around-the-cushion look that shows up when water sits too long.
