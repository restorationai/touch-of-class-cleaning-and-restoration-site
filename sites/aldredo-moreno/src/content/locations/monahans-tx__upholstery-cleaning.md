## Why Monahans Homes See Extra Wear on Upholstery

Monahans sits in the Permian Basin, and that means fine, sandy soil and oilfield dust that rides in on shoes, pets, and open windows during spring wind events. That grit works its way down into the weave of fabric upholstery and acts like sandpaper every time someone sits down, which is why cushions in this area often show wear faster than the fabric's rated lifespan would suggest. Add in the region's hard water, common across most Ward County wells and municipal supply, and you get mineral deposits that dull fabric color and leave stiff, dry patches on leather that isn't conditioned regularly. The low humidity that keeps West Texas summers bearable indoors also pulls natural oils out of leather upholstery faster than in more humid climates, which is part of why cracking and stiffening shows up sooner here than it might on the same sofa in a coastal city.

## Our Upholstery Cleaning Process for Monahans Conditions

We start with a dry pre-vacuum using an upholstery attachment strong enough to lift embedded sand and dust out of the weave before any moisture goes on the fabric, since wetting down gritty upholstery first just grinds the particles deeper into the fibers. From there we test for colorfastness and fabric type, then move to hot water extraction for most fabric pieces or a low-moisture cleaning method when the fabric or frame can't handle heavy saturation. Because local tap water tends to run hard, we use a pH-balanced solution designed to lift mineral residue rather than add to it, which matters on light-colored sofas where hard water spotting is already a known issue. Leather upholstery gets a separate cleaning and conditioning step, since West Texas dryness means skipping conditioner can leave leather brittle within a matter of months rather than years.

## Local note

One thing that catches new upholstery clients off guard out here: furniture that sat near a window or patio door during a dust event often looks fine on the surface but still has a layer of fine caliche-colored dust settled deep in the cushion fill, not just the fabric face. A quick surface wipe or home vacuum misses it, and it resurfaces as a dingy gray cast after the first proper cleaning pulls it out. We flag this for customers up front so the color change after cleaning doesn't come as a surprise, it's the dust finally leaving, not a sign anything went wrong with the fabric.
