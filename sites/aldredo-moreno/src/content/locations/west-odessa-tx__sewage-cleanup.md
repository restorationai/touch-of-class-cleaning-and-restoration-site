## Why West Odessa Properties Experience Sewage Backups

The caliche-heavy soil across this part of Ector County is dense and poorly draining. During the infrequent but intense rain events that move through the Permian Basin, municipal sewer systems can become temporarily overwhelmed, pushing sewage back through the lowest fixtures in a home. That same soil also causes lateral pipe movement over time, creating bellies and offsets in older lines that collect grease, debris, and root intrusion.

Homes built in West Odessa during the oil boom years of the 1970s and 1980s often have cast iron or Orangeburg sewer laterals that are well past their service life. These materials corrode from the inside out, and the first sign of failure is frequently a slow drain, followed by a full backup. Septic systems on the western edges of the area face similar stress: caliche restricts percolation in drain fields, which can push effluent back toward the tank and eventually into the structure.

## Our Sewage Cleanup and Sanitization Process in West Odessa

The first priority on any raw sewage job is containment. Sewage does not stay where it lands, it wicks into subfloor materials, travels under vinyl plank, and soaks into the base of drywall within hours. Before any extraction begins, the affected zone is isolated to prevent cross-contamination to clean areas of the home.

Extraction removes standing sewage and saturated materials. In West Odessa's slab-on-grade homes, which are common throughout the area, there is no crawl space beneath the floor, so any sewage that has migrated under flooring must be accessed by removing the surface material and treating the concrete slab directly. The slab is then cleaned with EPA-registered disinfectants rated for Category 3 water, and air scrubbers with HEPA filtration run continuously to capture airborne particulates.

After the physical cleanup, the space is treated with hospital-grade antimicrobial agents applied to every affected surface, including wall cavities if sewage has wicked up into framing. Drying equipment stays in place until moisture readings confirm the structure has returned to normal levels for this climate. In West Odessa's low-humidity environment, ambient drying conditions are often favorable, but slab moisture can linger longer than surface readings suggest, so monitoring continues through the full drying cycle.
