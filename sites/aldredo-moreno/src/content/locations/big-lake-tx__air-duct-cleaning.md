Big Lake sits in the heart of the Permian Basin, where West Texas wind carries fine caliche dust and sand year-round. That grit doesn't just coat your windowsills, it works its way into return vents and settles deep inside ductwork, building up layer by layer between cooling seasons. If your system has been running through a few Reagan County summers without a professional cleaning, the air moving through your home is likely carrying more than you'd want to breathe.

## Why Big Lake Properties See Duct Buildup Faster Than Most

The climate here is a particular challenge for HVAC systems. Big Lake averages low annual rainfall, high winds, and extended stretches of dry heat that keep dust suspended in the air for hours after a storm passes. Caliche soil is powdery when dry, and when the wind picks up across open ranchland, that particulate matter gets pulled straight into any home's air intake. Homes that run their systems heavily from May through September, which is most of them, cycle that dust through the ductwork repeatedly.

Older construction in the area adds another layer of complexity. Homes built in the 1960s and 1970s often used flex duct that has become brittle over time, or metal duct with joints that have loosened and collect debris at the seams. Gaps at those joints don't just let conditioned air escape, they let attic dust and insulation fibers migrate into the supply stream. That's a different problem than a newer sealed system, and it requires a more careful approach during cleaning to avoid dislodging material into living spaces.

## Our Air Duct Cleaning Process in Big Lake

When an ACS Enterprise crew arrives at a Big Lake property, the first step is a visual inspection of the accessible ductwork and the air handler. We want to know what's in the system before we start moving air through it, whether that's dust cake, pest debris, or deteriorating insulation fragments near the plenum.

From there, we use high-powered negative pressure equipment to put the duct system under vacuum before agitating debris with rotary brushes. This sequence matters: agitate first and you push contaminants into the living area; vacuum first and you contain them. Supply and return runs are cleaned separately, and registers are removed, cleaned, and reseated. If dryer vent cleaning is also on the list, we run that as a separate line item, a clogged dryer vent is a fire risk that deserves its own attention, not a quick add-on.

For homes where dust or biological buildup is significant, we offer air duct sanitization after the mechanical cleaning is complete. This step applies an EPA-registered disinfectant to interior duct surfaces and is especially relevant for systems that have had moisture intrusion or have been sitting idle in a vacant property.

## Equipment and Methods We Use

The equipment we bring to Big Lake jobs is truck-mounted or trailer-mounted negative pressure machinery, not the portable consumer-grade units that some services use. The difference in suction capacity is significant when you're dealing with long duct runs in a ranch-style home or a commercial property with extensive distribution. Rotary contact cleaning tools are matched to the duct diameter, round flex, rectangular metal, and board duct each respond differently, and using the wrong brush size can damage liner material.

We also carry inspection cameras for systems where the homeowner has concerns about specific sections of ductwork, or where we see signs of pest activity near the air handler. Knowing what's in the system before and after cleaning is part of doing the job right.

## Local Note

One thing that comes up specifically in Big Lake and the surrounding Reagan County area: many homes here have evaporative coolers or dual systems that were converted to refrigerated air at some point. When a swamp cooler is decommissioned and the home is tied into a new refrigerated system, the old duct connections are sometimes capped rather than removed. Those dead-end sections can become collection points for dust, rodent nesting material, and standing debris that never gets cycled through the filter. If your home went through that kind of conversion and the ductwork hasn't been inspected since, it's worth having those sections checked before assuming the system is clean.
