When water does appear inside a West Texas home, whether from a supply line that cracked under the temperature swings between a cold January night and a warm afternoon, or from a washing machine connection that finally gave out, it moves fast through slab-on-grade construction and finds every low point before you have a chance to react. ## Why Imperial Properties See Water Damage Issues

The Permian Basin's climate creates a specific set of conditions that most coastal restoration guides never mention. Imperial and the broader Ward and Crane County corridor experience dramatic temperature swings, sometimes dropping below freezing on winter nights and climbing well above 90 degrees by afternoon in late spring. That thermal cycling stresses supply lines, particularly the older galvanized and polybutylene plumbing found in homes built during the mid-20th century oil boom. When those lines fail, they tend to fail fast, and the water has nowhere obvious to go on a flat slab foundation.

The caliche-heavy soil common across this part of West Texas also complicates drainage. Caliche layers resist absorption, which means water from a roof leak or exterior intrusion during a rare but intense thunderstorm pools at the foundation line rather than dispersing into the ground. That standing water can wick under a slab or work into a crawl space before it becomes visible inside the structure.

## Our Water Damage Restoration Process in Imperial

Every job starts with a thorough moisture assessment. In slab-on-grade homes, which are the dominant construction type across this region, water can travel laterally under flooring and register as dry at the surface while the subfloor material stays saturated. Thermal imaging and calibrated moisture meters let the crew map exactly where the water has migrated before any drying equipment is placed.

Once the scope is clear, standing water is extracted using truck-mounted and portable extraction units. Wet flooring materials, baseboards, and any compromised drywall are removed to the point where the structure can actually dry, not just appear dry. Industrial air movers and dehumidifiers are then positioned based on the room layout and the specific materials involved. Readings are logged daily so drying progress is documented, which matters when an insurance adjuster reviews the claim.

ACS does not perform mold remediation or structural reconstruction. If the loss has progressed to a point where either of those services is needed, the team can point you toward the appropriate specialist before closing out the water damage side.

## Imperial Insurance Coordination

Most standard homeowner policies cover sudden and accidental water losses, which is the category that covers burst pipes, appliance failures, and similar events. ACS documents the damage with photographs, moisture readings, and a written scope so the file your adjuster receives is complete. That documentation also protects you if a question arises later about what materials were affected and why they were removed. Gradual leaks and long-term seepage are handled differently by carriers, so it is worth reviewing your policy language before assuming coverage.

## Local Note

One thing worth knowing about homes in this part of the Permian Basin: the combination of hard well water and older supply lines means mineral buildup inside pipes is common, and a slow pinhole leak can go undetected for weeks behind a wall before it shows up as a stain or a soft spot in flooring. By the time the damage is visible, the moisture has often already reached the wall cavity insulation. In Imperial-area homes with this pattern, the drying timeline is typically longer than the surface damage suggests, because fiberglass batt insulation holds water without showing it. Pulling that insulation is usually the right call, not an upsell.
