## Why Monahans Properties Are Vulnerable to Water Damage

The Permian Basin's boom-and-bust construction cycles left Monahans with a housing stock that spans several decades of building standards. Many homes built during earlier oil booms were constructed on slab foundations using materials and plumbing fittings that were standard then but are now well past their expected service life. Galvanized steel supply lines and cast-iron drain stacks corrode from the inside out, and the first sign of a problem is often water staining on a ceiling or a soft spot in a floor rather than a visible leak.

The local soil compounds the problem. Caliche, the calcium carbonate-rich layer common throughout Ward County, does not drain freely. When a slab develops a slow leak beneath the surface, water has nowhere to go quickly. It migrates laterally under the foundation, saturating the soil and eventually wicking upward through the concrete into flooring and wall cavities. By the time a homeowner notices moisture at the baseboard, the area beneath the slab may have been wet for weeks.

Summer temperature extremes add another layer of risk. When outdoor temperatures push past 100 degrees for extended stretches, HVAC systems run continuously. Condensate drain lines clog, evaporator coils ice over, and overflow pans fill faster than most homeowners expect. A condensate overflow inside an attic air handler can saturate blown-in insulation and the ceiling drywall below before the system trips a float switch, if one is installed at all.

## Our Water Damage Restoration Process in Monahans

Every water damage job begins with a thorough moisture assessment using thermal imaging and calibrated meters. In Monahans, where slab leaks are common, that assessment extends below the visible surface. We check floor coverings, wall cavities, and adjacent rooms because water under a slab does not stay in one place.

Once the scope is mapped, water removal begins. Standing water is extracted first using truck-mounted and portable extraction units. Wet flooring materials, saturated insulation, and compromised drywall are documented and, where necessary, removed to expose the structural materials underneath. Leaving wet building materials in place traps moisture and creates conditions where secondary damage accelerates.

Structural drying follows extraction. We place commercial-grade desiccant or refrigerant dehumidifiers and high-velocity air movers in a calculated configuration based on the affected square footage and material types. In Monahans, the low ambient humidity outdoors can actually assist drying when conditions allow, but interior drying equipment is still essential because the goal is controlled evaporation, not simply opening windows. Moisture readings are logged daily until all structural materials reach acceptable levels, and that documentation becomes part of the file we provide to your insurance carrier.

## Local Note

One pattern worth knowing in Monahans: homes with original terrazzo or saltillo tile floors, which appear in a number of properties built during the mid-century oil boom, behave differently during slab leak events than homes with vinyl or carpet. The tile itself does not absorb water, but the mortar bed beneath it does, and that mortar bed can hold significant moisture long after the surface feels dry to the touch. Moisture meters read the tile surface and give a false-dry reading unless the probe reaches the substrate. We use penetrating probes and thermal imaging specifically to catch this, because a missed wet mortar bed under tile will grow mold against the underside of the tile long before anyone sees a visible sign.

If your home has original tile floors and you have had any plumbing work done in the past year, it is worth having a moisture check done even if you have not noticed a problem yet.
