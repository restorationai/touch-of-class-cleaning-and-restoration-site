## Why Greenwood Properties See Sewage Backup Issues

The caliche and clay-heavy soils common throughout this part of West Texas don't drain the way sandy soils do. When heavy rain events hit, the kind that roll through the Permian Basin in late spring and early fall, ground saturation can overwhelm older septic drain fields and push effluent back toward the home. Municipal sewer laterals in established Greenwood neighborhoods can also experience backpressure during those same storm events, sending sewage up through floor drains, toilets, and washing machine standpipes before a homeowner realizes what's happening.

Homes built in the 1970s and 1980s, which make up a significant portion of the housing stock in this part of Midland County, often have cast-iron drain lines that have had decades to corrode, scale, and partially collapse. A partial blockage that's been manageable for years can become a full backup without much warning. The combination of aging pipe materials and soil conditions that shift with the heat cycles of a West Texas summer creates conditions where sewage events are less a matter of "if" and more a matter of "when."

## Our Sewage Cleanup and Sanitization Process in Greenwood

Raw sewage is classified as Category 3 water, the most contaminated classification in the industry, which means the response protocol is more involved than a standard water loss. The process starts with containment: limiting the spread of contaminated material before any extraction begins. From there, the affected area is documented thoroughly with photos and moisture readings, both for your records and for any insurance claim that follows.

Extraction of standing sewage and saturated materials comes next. In West Texas homes with slab-on-grade construction, which is common throughout this region, there's no crawl space to ventilate, so any sewage that has wicked into flooring materials or penetrated the slab edge requires careful attention. After bulk removal, affected surfaces are treated with EPA-registered disinfectants appropriate for biological contamination. Porous materials that cannot be adequately disinfected, certain flooring types, drywall that has absorbed sewage, are removed and disposed of per applicable standards. Drying equipment is then deployed to bring structural moisture levels back to baseline before any reconstruction begins.
