Standing water is only the beginning. Within the first hour, water migrates through drywall seams, wicks into insulation, and travels along subfloor joists far beyond the visible wet zone. By 24 hours, wood framing begins to swell. By 48 to 72 hours, conditions are ripe for mold colonization. Water damage restoration is a race against those timelines, and the work that happens in the first few hours determines how much of your home can be saved and how long the drying process will take.

## What water damage restoration actually involves

Restoration isn't cleanup, it's a controlled drying process guided by moisture readings, not by how things look or smell. Water that soaked into a wall cavity on Monday can still be saturating the bottom plate on Friday while the surface feels dry to the touch. That's why professional water mitigation relies on thermal imaging cameras, penetrating moisture meters, and calibrated psychrometric readings (temperature, relative humidity, and dew point) to map the true extent of the loss.

Equipment matters as much as process. Industrial-grade truck-mounted or portable extractors remove standing water and pull moisture from carpet padding and subfloor assemblies far more effectively than wet-vacs. Low-grain refrigerant (LGR) dehumidifiers and high-velocity air movers create a controlled drying environment that accelerates evaporation from structural materials. In Category 2 or Category 3 losses, water that carries contaminants, sewage, or has been standing long enough to degrade, affected materials may need to be removed rather than dried in place, and containment is established to protect unaffected areas of the home.

Timeline varies by category and construction type. A clean-water loss in a single room with no wall cavity involvement can reach drying goals in three to five days. A sewage backup that saturated multiple rooms and wicked into wall framing may require ten days or more of active drying, plus controlled demolition to expose saturated cavities.

## Our process

1. **Damage assessment and moisture mapping.** Before any equipment is placed, the full wet zone is identified using moisture meters and thermal imaging. This establishes a baseline, every reading is documented so drying progress can be tracked objectively, not by guesswork.

2. **Water extraction.** Standing water and surface moisture are removed first. For carpet and pad, weighted extraction tools press deep into the fiber to pull water that air movers alone cannot reach. For hard flooring with water trapped in the subfloor below, floor mat extraction systems are used to draw moisture up through the surface.

3. **Controlled demolition (when required).** Wet insulation does not dry, it holds moisture and creates a mold-friendly environment behind walls. When moisture readings confirm that wall cavities, insulation, or flooring assemblies are saturated beyond what in-place drying can address, targeted removal is performed. Flood cuts are made at a height that exposes wet framing while preserving as much material as possible.

4. **Structural drying with daily monitoring.** Air movers and dehumidifiers are positioned according to the moisture map. Readings are taken and recorded daily. Equipment placement is adjusted as the drying front moves. Drying is not complete until all affected materials reach their target moisture content, not when the equipment has run for a set number of days.

5. **Final documentation and clearance.** A closing moisture report documents that all affected areas have reached acceptable levels. This report is part of the claim file and is what insurance adjusters use to verify that drying was completed properly before reconstruction begins.

## What separates a good water damage response from a bad one

The most common failure in water mitigation is under-scoping the loss. Water follows gravity and capillary action, it moves through wall assemblies, under flooring, and into adjacent rooms in ways that aren't visible from the surface. Operators who rely on visual inspection alone routinely miss saturated wall cavities, wet subfloor sheathing under vinyl plank, and moisture that has traveled along plumbing chases to rooms far from the source.

A second common problem is premature equipment removal. Drying takes as long as the materials require. Pulling dehumidifiers early to save rental days leaves residual moisture in framing that fuels mold growth behind the finished walls, a problem that won't surface until weeks or months later, often after reconstruction is complete.

Insurance adjusters look for daily moisture logs, psychrometric data, and a clearly documented scope that ties the equipment placed to the readings taken. A claim file without that documentation is harder to close and more likely to result in disputes over what was actually dried.

For Category 2 and Category 3 losses, containment and proper handling of contaminated materials are non-negotiable. Water from a sewage backup or a long-standing leak carries pathogens that require appropriate PPE, containment barriers, and disposal, not just extraction and drying.

## What does water damage restoration cost?

Typical costs run from under $2,000 for a small, clean-water loss caught quickly to well over $10,000 for a multi-room loss involving contaminated water, structural drying, and controlled demolition. Every loss is different, and ACS Enterprise provides a written scope before work begins so there are no surprises. Most standard homeowners insurance policies cover sudden and accidental water damage, including the cost of water extraction, structural drying, and controlled demolition, though coverage for the source of the leak itself (a failed appliance or broken pipe) varies by policy.

## Seasonal and regional considerations

Midland's climate creates a specific set of water damage patterns. The region's clay-heavy soils expand and shift with moisture cycles, which puts stress on slab foundations and the plumbing embedded in them, slab leaks are among the more common loss sources in West Texas homes. Summer thunderstorms can deliver heavy rain in short bursts that overwhelm gutters and window seals on older homes. During winter cold snaps, pipes in exterior walls and unconditioned spaces, garages, attics, crawl spaces, are vulnerable to freezing and bursting when temperatures drop sharply overnight, as they do across the Permian Basin. Catching a freeze-related pipe burst early, before water has time to spread through multiple rooms, is one of the most significant factors in keeping a claim manageable.
