## What commercial restoration actually involves

Commercial losses behave differently than residential ones. A single-story office suite might have water traveling under raised flooring into server rooms. A retail space might have water wicking up through VCT tile and into gypsum board behind fixtures that can't be moved without a planned shutdown. Multi-tenant buildings add shared walls, shared HVAC returns, and property manager coordination on top of the physical drying work.

The work itself is extraction with truck-mount or portable pumps depending on access, placement of air movers and dehumidifiers sized to the affected square footage, and moisture monitoring across drywall, subfloor, and any specialty flooring until readings return to a dry standard. Sewage backups in commercial kitchens or restrooms get handled as contaminated water, with affected porous materials removed rather than dried in place. Timelines run from a few days for a contained office leak to a week or more for a larger retail or warehouse footprint with layered flooring and ceiling damage.

## Our process

1. **Scope and document the loss.** We walk the affected areas with you or your property manager, photograph damage, and note which systems (electrical, HVAC, plumbing) are involved before any equipment goes down.
2. **Extract standing water and contain the affected zone.** Commercial spaces often need partial containment so unaffected areas of the business can keep operating during the drying phase.
3. **Set drying equipment and begin moisture mapping.** Air movers, dehumidifiers, and moisture meters go in, and we track readings in affected materials daily rather than guessing when drying is complete.
4. **Coordinate around business hours where possible.** For retail and office tenants, we discuss which phases of the work can happen before or after peak hours so foot traffic and operations are disrupted as little as the loss allows.
5. **Final walkthrough and documentation.** Before we close out the job, we confirm dry readings and hand over documentation your insurance carrier or property management company will want on file.

## What separates a good commercial restoration response from a bad one

The most common mistake on commercial jobs is drying to "looks dry" instead of drying to a verified moisture reading. Carpet tile, VCT, and commercial-grade subfloor can all read dry at the surface while moisture is still trapped underneath, and that gap is exactly where secondary problems start. A second common miss is treating a sewage backup in a commercial restroom the same as a clean water leak. Category 3 water from a backed-up line requires different containment and disposal of affected porous materials, not just drying fans pointed at the mess.

Adjusters reviewing commercial claims generally want moisture logs showing day-over-day readings, photos of the affected area before equipment placement, and a clear note on which materials were dried versus removed and why. Operators who skip that documentation often end up with disputed claims later, which costs the property owner time and money on top of the original loss.

## What does commercial restoration cost?

Commercial water damage costs vary widely depending on square footage, flooring type, and whether contamination is involved. These are typical industry ranges, not a quote: every loss is different, and ACS Enterprise provides a written scope of work before any work begins.

| Scenario | Typical range |
|---|---|
| Single office, clean water, carpet only | $1,500 - $4,000 |
| Retail floor, water under VCT or tile | $3,000 - $8,000 |

| Commercial sewage backup, restroom or kitchen | $4,000 - $12,000 |

Most commercial property insurance policies cover sudden and accidental water damage, including the cost of extraction, drying, and replacement of damaged materials, though coverage for the original source of the leak (like a failed appliance or aging pipe) can vary by policy.

## Seasonal & regional considerations

West Texas swings hard between extreme summer heat and winter freezes, and both create commercial water loss patterns. Summer monsoon storms can overwhelm flat commercial roofs and parking lot drainage faster than older storm systems were designed to handle. Winter cold snaps put exposed or poorly insulated commercial plumbing, especially in strip malls and older office buildings, at risk of a freeze-and-burst event right as temperatures rebound.
