**Flood damage in Midland?** Call now for emergency service. Floodwater from a storm surge, overflowing arroyo, or sewer backup carries silt, chemicals, and bacteria that a standard water leak never does, and it starts breaking down drywall and subfloor within hours of contact. The longer contaminated water sits against framing and flooring, the more material has to come out rather than simply dry, so the window to limit demolition is short.

## What flood damage restoration actually involves

Flood damage restoration is a different job from drying out a bathroom leak. Floodwater that enters a home from outside, whether from a street-level flash flood, a retention area overflow, or a sewer line surcharging during heavy rain, is classified as Category 3 water under the IICRC S500 standard. That classification changes almost everything about the response: porous materials that touched the water (carpet pad, baseboards, drywall up to the flood line, insulation) typically can't be cleaned and dried in place, they have to be removed and disposed of as part of the cleanup. The work involves pumping or extracting standing water, assessing how far contamination tracked up walls and under flooring, controlled removal of affected porous materials, and then drying the remaining structure with air movers and dehumidifiers while monitoring moisture content in framing and subfloor until it reads at a safe baseline.

## Our process

1. **Water categorization and safety check.** Before any equipment goes in, we identify the water source and confirm whether it's carrying sewage, chemicals, or groundwater contamination, which determines what PPE and containment the job requires.
2. **Extraction.** Standing water is pumped and extracted from floors, crawlspaces, and low points first, since every hour it sits increases how much material has to be removed rather than dried.
3. **Selective demolition of contaminated materials.** Flood-soaked drywall, baseboards, insulation, and carpet pad that can't be sanitized are cut out and bagged for disposal, typically at a consistent flood line height around the room.
4. **Antimicrobial application and structural drying.** Remaining framing, subfloor, and slab are treated and then dried with air movers and dehumidifiers, with daily moisture readings logged against a drying goal.
5. **Final moisture verification.** We don't call a flood job finished on a calendar schedule, we call it finished when moisture meters confirm studs, subfloor, and slab have returned to a dry standard.

## What separates a good flood response from a bad one

The most common mistake on flood jobs is trying to dry materials that should have been removed. Drywall that stayed wet for more than 24 to 48 hours, or that touched contaminated water, can trap bacteria behind a surface that looks dry to the touch, which is exactly what an insurance adjuster or a future buyer's inspector will flag. A rushed crew will also skip documenting the flood line and moisture readings room by room, which makes it harder to support the claim later if the insurer questions why certain materials were removed. Good flood restoration documents water categorization, photographs the extent of contamination before demolition, and logs moisture readings throughout drying, not just at the start and end. That paper trail is also what protects the homeowner if mold grows behind work that wasn't actually dry when it was closed back up.

## What does flood damage restoration cost?

Costs vary with how much water entered the home, how long it sat, and how much demolition the contamination requires, since Category 3 flood water almost always costs more to remediate than a clean water loss of the same size. ACS Enterprise provides a written scope of the affected areas and materials before any work begins, because every flood loss behaves differently once walls and flooring are opened up. Homeowners insurance often excludes groundwater and overland flooding under a standard policy, so coverage frequently depends on whether a separate flood policy or an endorsement is in place, which is worth confirming with your agent before work starts.

## Seasonal and regional considerations

Midland's flat terrain and clay-heavy soil don't absorb heavy rain quickly, which is why a strong summer thunderstorm can put standing water into homes and businesses faster than residents expect, even without a named storm event. Flash flooding here tends to cluster in the late summer monsoon window, when intense, short-duration storms overwhelm street drainage before it can move water away from slab foundations. Because so much of the area's housing stock sits on slab-on-grade construction, floodwater that reaches the exterior often finds its way in at the lowest point of the slab, which is why fast extraction matters just as much here as it does in homes with crawlspaces or basements.
