**Water damage emergency in Midland?** Call now for emergency service. Most homeowners who search for reconstruction services after a burst pipe or roof leak are actually looking for two different jobs. The first is water removal and structural drying, which has to happen immediately. The second is the framing, drywall, flooring, and finish work that comes after the structure is verified dry. ACS Enterprise handles the first part, the water damage mitigation and cleanup, and works directly with your insurance adjuster and the licensed reconstruction contractor who picks up the rebuild once drying is complete.

## What happens after water damage: mitigation vs. reconstruction

Water damage mitigation is the work that stops a loss from getting worse: pulling standing water, removing saturated carpet pad, pulling baseboards to dry wall cavities, and running equipment until moisture content in the wood, drywall, and subfloor returns to a normal range. This typically runs a few days to a week depending on how much water intruded and what it touched. Post-damage reconstruction, the framing repairs, drywall hanging, texture, paint, flooring, and trim, is a separate trade that requires a general contracting license and its own permitting process in most jurisdictions. ACS Enterprise does not perform that rebuild work. What we do is make sure the structure is genuinely dry and documented before anyone starts putting new materials back in, which protects the rebuild from trapped moisture, warping, and callbacks down the line.

## Our process

1. **Scope of work and moisture mapping.** We document every affected material, take baseline moisture readings, and categorize the water (clean, gray, or black) because that determines how materials are handled.
2. **Extraction.** Standing water comes out of flooring, carpet, and pad first, since porous materials left wet for more than a day or two start breaking down and odor sets in.
3. **Structural drying and monitoring.** Air movers and dehumidifiers go in, and we log moisture readings daily against dry standard, not by how the surface looks or feels.
4. **Documentation and handoff.** Drying logs, photos, and moisture reports get compiled for your insurance adjuster and for whoever takes on the rebuild.
5. **Referral for reconstruction.** Once the structure hits dry standard, we can point you toward a licensed reconstruction contractor for the framing, drywall, and finish work. We stay out of that scope.

## What separates a good water damage response from a bad one

The most common mistake is calling a space dry because the surface feels dry to the touch. Drywall, subfloor, and framing can hold moisture well below the surface for days, and if reconstruction materials go in over that, mold and warping show up weeks later, usually after the rebuild crew has already finished. Adjusters look closely at moisture logs, dated photos, and whether readings were taken behind baseboards and inside wall cavities, not just on open floor. A rushed job skips carpet pad removal, under-dries cabinetry toe-kicks, or stops monitoring the moment the air mover sounds quiet, which isn't the same as the material actually reading dry. A careful crew tracks numbers daily until materials match the dry standard of an unaffected area in the same structure, and hands over a paper trail that supports both the insurance claim and the reconstruction contractor's scope.

## What does water damage mitigation cost?

Typical industry costs for the mitigation phase vary by how much water intruded, what category it is, and how many rooms are affected. Every loss is different, and ACS Enterprise provides a written scope before any work begins so there are no surprises once drying starts. Homeowners insurance typically covers mitigation costs for sudden, covered losses like a burst pipe, though gradual leaks or neglected maintenance are often excluded, and reconstruction costs are scoped and billed separately by the rebuild contractor.

## Seasonal & regional considerations

Midland's slab foundations and caliche soil mean plumbing runs under concrete in a lot of older homes, so a slab leak often shows up as a warm spot on the floor or a spike in the water bill before anyone sees standing water. West Texas also swings hard between extremes: summer thunderstorms can flood a property in under an hour, and winter freeze events have caused widespread pipe bursts in homes and pipes that weren't built for sustained hard freezes. Either way, the mitigation clock starts the moment water hits a material, regardless of the season.
