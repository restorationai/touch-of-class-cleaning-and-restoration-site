A dryer vent is easy to forget because it is out of sight, running from behind the dryer through the wall and out to an exterior hood. But every load of laundry pushes lint into that duct, and over time it packs down until the airflow the dryer depends on is choked off. The result is longer drying times, a hotter laundry room, higher energy bills, and, at the far end of the problem, a real fire hazard. Dryer vent cleaning clears the full length of that exhaust run so the system works the way it was designed to and the fire fuel is removed.

## What dryer vent cleaning actually involves

Cleaning a dryer vent is not the same as pulling the lint screen after a load. The lint screen catches a fraction of what comes off your clothes; the rest travels into the duct and settles along its length, especially at bends, transitions, and the exterior hood where the flapper cap sits. A proper cleaning clears the entire run, from the connection behind the dryer to the vent cap outside, not just the section you can reach.

The layout of the vent matters. A short, straight run to an outside wall stays cleaner longer and is quick to service. A long run, one with several elbows, or a duct that travels up through a roof collects lint faster and needs more thorough work to clear completely. The exterior hood is its own checkpoint: flapper caps stick, screens clog, and in this part of West Texas birds and pests will nest in an open or damaged vent, blocking it entirely. Part of the job is confirming the exhaust is actually reaching the outside with good flow.

## Our process

1. **Inspection and airflow check.** The vent run is traced from the dryer to the exterior hood, its length and layout are noted, and the current airflow at the outside vent is checked so there is a before-and-after picture of the improvement.

2. **Clearing the duct.** The dryer is pulled out and disconnected, and the full length of the duct is cleaned with rotary brushing and airflow so packed lint is removed from the elbows and transitions, not just the straight sections.

3. **Exterior hood and cap.** The outside vent hood, flapper, and any screen are cleared and checked for damage, pest nesting, or a stuck cap that would keep the system from exhausting properly.

4. **Reconnection and verification.** The duct is reconnected securely with the correct clamps, the dryer is set back in place, and airflow is confirmed at the exterior vent so you know the exhaust is moving freely.

5. **Walkthrough.** You get a straight account of what was cleared, the condition of the ducting and exterior hood, and a realistic interval for the next cleaning based on your vent layout and how often the dryer runs.

## What separates a good dryer vent job from a bad one

The most common shortcut is cleaning only the accessible length behind the dryer and calling it done. The lint that matters most is often packed in the elbows and out at the exterior cap, where it does the most to restrict airflow. Clearing only the first few feet leaves the restriction, and the drying problem, in place.

The second is skipping the airflow verification. The point of the service is that hot, moist air leaves the house freely again. Confirming good flow at the exterior vent, and checking that the flapper and screen are not the real blockage, is what proves the job did what it was supposed to. Reconnecting the duct with proper clamps rather than tape also matters, because a loose connection dumps lint-laden air into the wall cavity instead of outside.

## Seasonal and regional considerations

Around Midland, Odessa, and the wider Permian Basin, blowing dust and fine grit are a constant, and they collect at exterior vent hoods and flapper caps along with the lint from inside. That makes the outdoor end of the vent worth checking on a regular schedule, not just the ductwork. Heavy laundry seasons and homes running the dryer daily build lint faster, so an annual cleaning is a reasonable baseline for most households here, with more frequent service for long runs and high-use homes.
