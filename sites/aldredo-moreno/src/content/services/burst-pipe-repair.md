## What burst pipe cleanup and repair actually involves

A burst pipe is a plumbing emergency first and a water damage emergency second, and the two jobs are handled by different trades. ACS Enterprise manages the water side: shutting off or confirming the water source is isolated, extracting standing water, pulling baseboards or cutting access where water has tracked behind walls, and setting drying equipment to bring flooring, drywall, and framing back to a normal moisture range. If the pipe itself still needs to be cut out and replaced or soldered, that repair is plumbing work, and we coordinate with your plumber or point you to one so the water is stopped at the source while we handle everything the water touched.

Depending on how long the pipe was leaking before it was found, you could be dealing with a fresh, clean-water loss or a slower leak that's had days to soak into subfloor and wall cavities. Copper pipe splits from freeze expansion behave differently than a slow pinhole leak in PEX or galvanized line, and the cleanup scope follows the water, not the pipe material.

## Our process

1. **Source confirmation and water shutoff check.** Before anything else, we confirm water is actually off at the source, not just slowed, because a pipe that's still weeping under pressure will undo drying work before it starts.
2. **Extraction.** Standing water is pulled from flooring, carpet pad, and any low points, using extraction equipment sized to the volume of the loss.
3. **Material assessment and controlled demolition.** Baseboards, drywall, and flooring that can't dry in place are opened or removed in a targeted way, usually just enough to expose saturated framing and insulation without over-demoing material that will dry fine.
4. **Drying and moisture monitoring.** Air movers and dehumidifiers are set based on the room's size and the materials affected, and moisture content in wood framing and subfloor is checked over several days until readings return to a stable, dry range, consistent with IICRC S500 drying guidelines.
5. **Final verification and documentation.** Once moisture readings confirm the structure is dry, we document the readings and the work performed for your records and your insurance file.

## What separates a good burst pipe response from a bad one

The most common mistake on a burst pipe job is drying the surface and calling it done while the wall cavity or subfloor underneath is still wet. Drywall can feel dry to the touch within a day while the framing behind it stays saturated for a week, and that gap is exactly where hidden mold growth and delamination start weeks later. A thorough response uses a moisture meter on framing and subfloor, not just a visual check, and keeps drying equipment running until the readings say so, not until the water is no longer visible.

Insurance adjusters reviewing a pipe break claim look for a few specific things: documented moisture readings at the start and end of drying, photos of the affected materials before any demolition, and a clear timeline of when the leak was discovered versus how long it may have been running. A claim with thin documentation is more likely to get pushed back on scope or duration, so we log readings and photos as part of the job, not as an afterthought.

## Seasonal & regional considerations

West Texas winters bring hard freezes that catch homes with exposed pipes, crawlspace lines, or hose bibs that weren't insulated, and a quick overnight freeze followed by a thaw is one of the more common ways a pipe splits in Midland. The region's clay-heavy soil also means slab foundations shift slightly over time, which can stress older copper or galvanized supply lines running under the slab and lead to slow pinhole leaks rather than a dramatic burst. Either way, the sooner the leak is found, the smaller the drying scope.
