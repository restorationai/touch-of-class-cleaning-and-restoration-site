**Smoke damage rarely shows up alone.** By the time a fire is out, the materials in that room have usually taken on water too, from hose lines, sprinkler heads, or saturated insulation and drywall that soaked it up during the suppression effort. Here's the scope clarification worth knowing upfront: ACS Enterprise handles the water damage and cleanup side of a fire loss in Midland. We don't perform smoke damage restoration, soot removal, or odor remediation ourselves, and we won't tell you otherwise. What we can do is get ahead of the water intrusion fast and point you toward a licensed fire and smoke specialist for the rest.

## What a fire loss actually involves

A structure fire or even a contained kitchen fire typically leaves two separate problems behind. One is smoke and soot residue, which clings to porous surfaces, migrates through HVAC systems, and needs specialized cleaning like thermal fogging or soda blasting that a dedicated fire restoration contractor is equipped for. The other is water: pooled on flooring, trapped under carpet pad, wicking up drywall from suppression runoff. That second problem is time-sensitive. Wet drywall and insulation left untouched for a day or two start supporting mold growth, and carpet pad soaked with ash-contaminated water needs to come out, not just dry in place.

## Where ACS fits into a fire loss

Our part of the job is the water. That means extracting standing water from suppression activity, pulling wet carpet and pad, running air movers and dehumidifiers to bring structural moisture back to a normal range, and hauling out fire-damaged debris and contents that need to leave the property before a specialist can start on walls and ceilings. We also clean and disinfect areas affected by runoff, since fire suppression water often carries ash and debris that behaves closer to contaminated water than clean water.

## Our process when a fire loss includes water damage

1. **Phone intake and scope separation.** We ask what happened: was it a fire department response, a contained appliance fire, a chimney issue? We identify what's water damage (our scope) versus soot and smoke residue (not our scope).
2. **Water extraction and debris removal.** We remove standing water, saturated materials beyond saving, and fire debris that's blocking access or holding moisture.
3. **Structural drying.** Air movers and dehumidifiers go to work on the water-affected areas, monitored with moisture meters until readings return to baseline for the material type.
4. **Documentation for your insurance file.** We photograph and log the water damage specifically, separate from any soot or fire documentation your adjuster or fire restoration contractor will handle.
5. **Referral for the smoke and soot portion.** We'll point you toward a fire and smoke restoration specialist for odor treatment, soot cleaning, and any rebuild work, so that piece gets handled by someone certified for it.

## What separates a good response from a bad one

The biggest mistake in a mixed fire and water loss is letting one contractor claim they'll handle everything when they're only equipped for part of it. Smoke odor treatment, protein versus synthetic soot cleanup, and ozone or hydroxyl treatment require specific training and equipment that a water-focused crew doesn't carry, and vice versa: a fire restoration contractor who isn't running moisture meters on the water-affected studs can miss trapped saturation that shows up as a mold problem weeks later. Adjusters generally expect to see two distinct scopes of work on a fire claim that involved water, documented separately, with clear handoffs between contractors. Getting that separation right from day one keeps the claim cleaner and keeps you from paying twice for overlapping work.
