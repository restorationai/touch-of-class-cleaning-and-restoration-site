Standing water doesn't stay still. Within the first hour it migrates, soaking into drywall, wicking up baseboards, pooling beneath hardwood floors where it sits invisible until the subfloor starts to buckle. By 24 hours, saturated insulation has lost most of its R-value and wet framing has begun the slow process that ends in mold colonization. Water cleanup isn't just removing what you can see; it's tracking down what the water touched on its way through your home and drying it completely before the damage compounds.

## What water cleanup actually involves

The visible puddle is the easy part. The real work is what happens after extraction, finding moisture that has migrated behind walls, under flooring, and into ceiling cavities. Professional water cleanup uses thermal imaging cameras to locate cold, wet pockets that a moisture meter alone can miss, then places industrial desiccant or refrigerant dehumidifiers and high-velocity air movers in a calculated pattern to drive evaporation from the inside out.

The category of water matters as much as the volume. Clean water from a burst supply line (Category 1) can be dried in place if addressed quickly. Gray water from an overflowing washing machine or dishwasher (Category 2) requires antimicrobial treatment of affected materials. Black water from sewage backups or floodwater (Category 3) means affected porous materials, drywall, insulation, carpet, typically have to come out entirely, not just dry out. Confusing these categories is one of the most common and costly mistakes in water damage response.

Drying time depends on materials, ambient conditions, and how long the water was present before work began. A single room with clean water and prompt response can reach drying goals in two to four days. A Category 2 or 3 loss with saturated structural framing may take seven to ten days or more, with daily moisture readings to confirm progress.

## Our process

1. **Inspection and moisture mapping.** Before any equipment is placed, the full extent of the loss is documented, photos, moisture readings at every affected surface, and thermal imaging where wall or ceiling cavities may be holding water. This documentation also becomes the foundation of any insurance claim.

2. **Standing water extraction.** Truck-mounted or portable extraction units remove standing water from floors and carpeting. Carpet and pad are evaluated, pad almost always requires removal because it holds water like a sponge and cannot be dried effectively in place.

3. **Controlled demolition (when required).** Wet drywall below the flood line, saturated insulation, and Category 2 or 3 affected materials are removed to expose the structural framing for drying. Cuts are made at standard heights to simplify reinstallation. Nothing is removed that doesn't need to be.

4. **Structural drying with daily monitoring.** Industrial dehumidifiers and air movers are positioned per drying calculations, not just placed at random. Moisture readings are logged every 24 hours at each monitoring point. Equipment is adjusted as materials dry to maintain efficient evaporation rates.

5. **Final clearance and scope handoff.** When all monitored materials reach target moisture levels, the drying phase is complete. A written drying log documents every reading from day one to clearance, the record your adjuster needs and the baseline your reconstruction contractor uses to scope repairs.

## What separates a good water cleanup response from a bad one

The most common failure in water cleanup is stopping at the surface. A floor that feels dry underfoot can still read 20–30% moisture content in the subfloor beneath it. Drywall that looks intact can have wet insulation and saturated framing behind it. Operators who rely on visual inspection rather than meters and thermal imaging consistently leave moisture behind, and that moisture becomes a mold problem in 48 to 72 hours.

A second common problem is under-equipping the job. One dehumidifier in a 1,200-square-foot loss is not a drying system, it's a gesture. Proper equipment placement is calculated based on the cubic footage of the drying chamber, the materials present, and the ambient humidity. In West Texas, low outdoor humidity can actually work in your favor when outside air is used strategically, but that calculation still has to be made deliberately.

Insurance adjusters look for a drying log with dated moisture readings at labeled monitoring points. Without it, there's no way to verify that drying was completed, and claims can be disputed or underpaid. A documented, systematic approach protects both the homeowner and the scope of covered repairs.

## Seasonal and regional considerations

Midland's climate creates a specific water damage pattern worth knowing. The region's clay-heavy soils expand and contract significantly with moisture, which stresses slab foundations and the plumbing that runs through them. Slab leaks are a recurring source of slow, hidden water intrusion in area homes, often undetected until flooring or baseboards show visible damage. Summer thunderstorms can dump rainfall faster than drainage systems handle it, sending water through window wells, garage doors, and low-lying entries. And while hard freezes are less frequent here than in northern Texas, they do occur, and when they do, supply lines in exterior walls and uninsulated garage spaces are the first to go.
