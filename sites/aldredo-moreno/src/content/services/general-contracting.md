If your search started with "general contractor" or "kitchen remodel" after a pipe burst or a slow leak ruined your flooring, there's an important distinction to make before you call anyone: the water has to come out and the structure has to dry before any rebuild makes sense. ACS Enterprise handles that first half, the extraction, drying, and cleanup that happens before a contractor ever picks up a hammer. We don't frame walls, hang cabinets, or run a remodel crew, but we make sure the space you hand off to a builder is actually dry, documented, and ready to work in.

## What this actually involves

Water damage mitigation is the technical work that determines whether your eventual remodel holds up. That means pulling standing water with truck-mounted extraction equipment, setting air movers and dehumidifiers to bring wood framing and subfloor back to a stable moisture content, and tracking that progress daily with a moisture meter rather than guessing by feel or smell. Depending on how saturated the materials are, drying typically runs several days. If the water source was a sewage backup or a flooded crawlspace, there's also a sanitizing step before drying even starts, since porous materials that stay wet and contaminated will keep causing problems long after the surface looks fine. What we don't do is the finish work after that, the drywall, flooring, cabinetry, or paint. For that stage, we can point you to a licensed general contractor who specializes in post-damage rebuild, but that scope sits outside what ACS Enterprise quotes or performs.

## Our process

1. **Moisture assessment.** We walk the affected area with a moisture meter and thermal imaging to find out how far the water traveled, including under flooring and inside wall cavities where it isn't visible.
2. **Extraction.** Standing water comes out first, using pumps and extraction wands matched to the volume and the flooring type involved.
3. **Drying and monitoring.** Air movers and dehumidifiers go in, and we log moisture readings over the following days until the materials hit a dry standard, not just a dry appearance.
4. **Containment, if needed.** For Category 2 or 3 water (gray or black water from appliances, sewage, or outside flooding), we isolate the affected area to keep contamination from spreading to dry sections of the home.
5. **Documentation and handoff.** We provide photos, moisture logs, and a written scope of the mitigation work completed, which is the paperwork a rebuild contractor and your insurance adjuster will both want to see.

## What separates a good mitigation response from a bad one

The most common mistake is stopping too early. A wall or subfloor can read dry at the surface while the material underneath is still holding moisture, and if drying equipment gets pulled before that resolves, mold and warping show up weeks later, usually after the drywall is already back up. Adjusters know this, which is why they look for daily moisture logs rather than a single before-and-after reading. Another frequent gap is skipping containment on contaminated water, treating a sewage backup the same as a clean supply-line leak. The cleanup method has to match the water category, not just the volume. Good mitigation also means not touching the rebuild scope, handing off clean documentation to your contractor instead of guessing at what needs replacing.

## What does water damage cleanup cost?

Costs vary with the size of the affected area, the water category, and how long the material sat wet before anyone noticed. Every loss is different, which is why ACS Enterprise provides a written scope before any work begins rather than a flat quote over the phone. Most homeowners insurance policies cover sudden and accidental water damage, such as a burst pipe or appliance failure, but typically exclude gradual leaks or flooding from an outside water source unless you carry separate flood coverage.

| Scenario | Typical range |
|---|---|
| Single room, clean water, caught early | $1,200 - $3,500 |
| Multi-room water intrusion, clean water | $3,500 - $8,000 |
| Sewage backup, single room | $4,000 - $9,000 |
| Flooded crawlspace or basement | $5,000 - $12,000 |
| Saturated subfloor requiring extended drying | $6,000 - $15,000 |

## Seasonal & regional considerations

Midland sees sharp temperature swings between fall and winter, and exposed or poorly insulated pipe runs in older homes are prone to freezing and splitting when a cold front moves through fast. Those breaks often happen overnight or while a property sits vacant, so the water has time to spread before anyone finds it. Slab foundations, common across the area, also mean water from a burst line can travel under flooring before it ever reaches a visible wall, which is part of why moisture mapping matters more here than a quick visual check.
