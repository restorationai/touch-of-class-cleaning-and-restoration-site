## Restoration emergencies common in Big Spring

Big Spring sits in the southern reaches of the High Plains, where the climate swings hard between extremes. Winter cold snaps drop temperatures fast enough to freeze supply lines in older homes before owners have time to react, and the thaw that follows can send water into walls, cabinets, and flooring before anyone notices the damage. Spring and early summer bring the threat of severe thunderstorms and hail, which can compromise roofing and drive water through any gap in the envelope. When that water sits, even for a day or two, it works into insulation, subfloor, and wall cavities. The region's low average humidity is deceptive: interior moisture trapped behind drywall or under carpet does not evaporate on its own, and without proper drying equipment it lingers long enough to create serious secondary problems.

Sewage backups are another recurring issue. Much of Big Spring's residential housing was built between the 1940s and the 1980s, a period that produced predominantly slab-on-grade construction with brick veneer exteriors.
