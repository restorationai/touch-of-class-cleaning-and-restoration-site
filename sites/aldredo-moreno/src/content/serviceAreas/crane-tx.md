## Restoration emergencies common in Crane

Crane sits in the Permian Basin, where the climate swings between extended dry spells and short, intense rain events that the hard caliche soil cannot absorb quickly. When a thunderstorm drops an inch of rain in under an hour, water moves across driveways, yards, and parking areas before drainage infrastructure can catch up, and it finds any gap in a foundation, door threshold, or roof flashing. Winters in this part of West Texas are mild most years, but a hard freeze arrives without much warning and can split supply lines in attics or exterior walls that were never insulated for sustained cold. The combination of aging housing stock, hard ground that sheds water rather than absorbing it, and temperature swings that stress plumbing creates a steady pattern of water intrusion calls across the county.

### Water damage restoration and cleanup

Water damage in Crane frequently starts small and spreads fast. ### Sewage cleanup

Crane's older sewer laterals can root-intrude or back up during heavy rain events when ground saturation pushes capacity. ACS handles the full cleanup scope and documents everything for the insurance file.

### Storm damage cleanup

## Building stock, site conditions, and permits in Crane

Most of Crane's residential housing was built during the mid-twentieth century oil boom, which means slab-on-grade construction dominates. Slab foundations eliminate crawlspace moisture concerns but create a different problem: water that gets under a slab from a slab-leak or perimeter intrusion has nowhere to drain and can stay trapped beneath flooring for weeks. Wall assemblies from that era often used plaster over wood lath rather than drywall, and plaster reads dry at the surface long before the lath behind it is actually dry. Plumbing in homes of that vintage is frequently galvanized steel on the supply side, which corrodes from the inside out and can fail without visible exterior warning. Caliche soil drains poorly and does not absorb water evenly, which means foundation edges stay wet longer after a rain event than the surface suggests. Permits for structural repair and rebuild work in Crane fall under the City of Crane and Crane County jurisdiction; ACS handles the water damage and cleanup side and can point you to the right licensed contractor for any permitted rebuild scope.
