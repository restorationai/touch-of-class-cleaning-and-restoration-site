## Restoration emergencies common in Monahans

Monahans sits in the Permian Basin at roughly 2,600 feet elevation, where the climate swings hard between extremes. Summers push well past 100°F, and winters bring periodic hard freezes that can drop overnight temperatures below 20°F. That freeze-thaw cycle is the most common driver of sudden water losses: supply lines in exterior walls and under-insulated crawl spaces fail when temperatures plunge, often overnight, and the damage is already spreading by the time a homeowner wakes up.

The region also sees intense, fast-moving thunderstorms, particularly in late spring and early summer, that can drop several inches of rain in a short window. Monahans soil is a mix of deep sand and caliche hardpan. Heavy rain that cannot soak through the caliche layer sheets across lots and drives water against foundations and through low-clearance entry points. Flat or gently sloping lots common in older parts of the city offer little natural drainage relief, which means standing water can linger against a structure long after the storm passes.

Sewage backups are a related concern. When storm runoff overwhelms older municipal lines, it can reverse pressure through floor drains and cleanouts in lower-level bathrooms and utility rooms.
