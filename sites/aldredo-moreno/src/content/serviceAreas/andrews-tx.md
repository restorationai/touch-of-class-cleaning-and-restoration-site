## Restoration emergencies common in Andrews

Andrews sits in the southern Permian Basin, where the climate is semi-arid but not immune to the sudden, intense rainfall events that roll through West Texas in late summer and early fall. Caliche-heavy soil does not absorb water quickly, so even a moderate downpour can sheet across yards, driveways, and parking lots and find its way into garages, crawl spaces, and around slab edges before it has anywhere to drain. The same dry climate that makes Andrews feel mild most of the year also means plumbing systems go years without a stress test, and when a hard freeze does arrive, supply lines in exterior walls and uninsulated spaces fail fast. Flat commercial roofs common on the oil-field service buildings around town are also vulnerable to ponding after any significant rain event.

## Services we provide in Andrews

### Water damage restoration

Water in Andrews homes tends to follow predictable paths: under slab foundations where soil movement has opened gaps, into wall cavities behind older plaster or drywall, and beneath vinyl or tile flooring where it sits undetected. ACS extracts standing water, sets drying equipment, and monitors moisture readings until the structure reaches acceptable levels. We document the loss throughout so your insurance carrier has what it needs.

### Sewage cleanup

Sewage backups are more common in Andrews than most homeowners expect. ## Building stock, site conditions, and permits in Andrews

Most of Andrews's residential housing was built between the 1950s and the 1980s, when the Permian Basin oil economy drove rapid growth. That era produced predominantly slab-on-grade construction with brick veneer exteriors, low-pitched roofs, and interior plumbing in galvanized steel or early copper. Galvanized supply lines from that period are well past their service life and can fail at fittings or corrode through walls with little warning. Homes built before the mid-1970s may also contain asbestos-containing materials in floor tile, mastic, and pipe insulation, which affects how a restoration scope is planned and what disturbance is permissible before testing.

The soil beneath Andrews is predominantly caliche and sandy loam, which drains poorly in concentrated events and can shift under slab edges as moisture content changes seasonally. This movement opens pathways for water intrusion at the slab perimeter that are not always obvious until flooring or baseboards show staining.
