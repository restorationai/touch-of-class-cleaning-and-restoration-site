## Restoration emergencies common in Andrews

Andrews sits in the southern Permian Basin, where the climate creates a specific set of conditions that drive restoration calls. Summer thunderstorms arrive fast and dump water on caliche-heavy soil that sheds runoff rather than absorbing it, water finds its way through foundation seams, window wells, and low-grade entries before a homeowner realizes what's happening. Winter freezes are infrequent but sharp: when overnight temperatures drop into the teens, supply lines in exterior walls and uninsulated crawl spaces can burst within hours. The resulting water loss is often hidden inside wall cavities and under flooring, reading dry at the surface while saturated materials continue to deteriorate underneath.

Wind is a year-round factor here. Sustained gusts and occasional severe storms strip roofing materials and push wind-driven rain into attic spaces and wall assemblies. Once moisture is inside an insulated cavity in West Texas heat, mold colonization can begin within 24 to 48 hours, faster than most homeowners expect.

## Services we provide in Andrews

**Water damage mitigation**, Whether the source is a failed water heater, a storm-driven roof penetration, or a slab leak, standing water in an Andrews home needs to be extracted and the structure dried before secondary damage sets in. We use thermal imaging and moisture meters to find water that has migrated beyond the visible wet area, then set drying equipment calibrated to the low-humidity West Texas air.

**Fire and smoke restoration**, Cooking fires and electrical failures are the most common ignition sources in residential properties. Smoke residue travels through HVAC systems and settles on surfaces far from the burn point. Effective cleanup means addressing the odor at the molecular level, not masking it, that includes ductwork, cabinetry interiors, and wall cavities where smoke penetrated.

**Mold remediation**, After any water intrusion that goes undetected for more than a day or two, mold testing and remediation may be necessary before repairs can begin. We follow established containment and removal protocols and document the work for insurance purposes.

**Structural repairs and reconstruction**, Once mitigation is complete, damaged drywall, flooring, cabinetry, and framing need to be rebuilt. Handling both phases under one contractor simplifies the insurance claim and eliminates the gap that often delays a project when mitigation and rebuild are managed separately.

## Building stock, site conditions, and permits in Andrews

Most of Andrews's residential housing was built between the 1950s and the 1980s, during the oil-driven growth that shaped much of the Permian Basin. Construction in that era typically means slab-on-grade foundations, wood-frame exterior walls, and original plumbing in galvanized steel, a material that corrodes from the inside out and can fail without visible warning. Homes built before the mid-1980s may also contain asbestos-containing materials in floor tile, pipe insulation, or textured ceilings, which affects how a restoration scope is written and what abatement steps precede drywall removal.

The soil beneath Andrews is predominantly caliche and sandy loam. Caliche does not expand and contract the way clay-heavy soils do, but it sheds water quickly, directing runoff toward foundations rather than letting it percolate. Structural repairs and any work that opens exterior walls or affects the foundation typically require a permit through the City of Andrews. Texas follows the International Residential Code with state amendments; your contractor should pull the appropriate permits before reconstruction begins.

West Texas's low average humidity is an advantage during structural drying, evaporation rates are favorable, but it can also mask moisture readings if equipment placement is not precise.
