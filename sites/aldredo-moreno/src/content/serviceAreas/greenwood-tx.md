## Restoration emergencies common in Greenwood

Greenwood sits in the Permian Basin, where the climate swings hard in both directions. Summers bake the soil into a cracked, contracting layer that pulls away from slab foundations and lets water migrate in unexpected directions when a supply line finally gives out. Winters bring the occasional hard freeze, the kind that splits uninsulated pipes in exterior walls or in the crawl spaces beneath older ranch-style homes. When temperatures drop fast and then rebound, the thaw can push water into wall cavities before a homeowner even notices the pressure drop.

Wind-driven rain during spring storm season is another consistent driver of loss in this part of Midland County. Flat rooflines common to mid-century construction here don't shed water the way a steeper pitch does, and a compromised roof membrane after a hail event can let moisture work into attic insulation and framing for days before it shows up as a ceiling stain. The region's low average humidity means surface materials can feel dry to the touch while moisture readings inside wall assemblies remain elevated, which is exactly the condition that allows mold to colonize within 24 to 48 hours of a water intrusion.

Most residential construction in the Greenwood area dates from the mid-twentieth century onward, with a significant portion of the housing stock built as ranch-style, single-story homes on slab-on-grade foundations.
