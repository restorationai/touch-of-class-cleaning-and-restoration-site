ACS Enterprise started in 2019 with a straightforward purpose: give property owners in the Permian Basin a restoration company that shows up, does the work correctly, and handles the paperwork without leaving the homeowner to figure it out alone. Water damage does not wait for a convenient moment, and the company was built around that reality from the beginning.

## What we do

Most of the calls that come in follow a recognizable pattern: a pipe failed overnight, a water heater let go, a storm pushed water through a roof or foundation, or a drain backed up and left sewage where it had no business being. The homeowner is standing in the middle of it, not sure whether to call a plumber, a contractor, or their insurance agent first. That is exactly the situation ACS Enterprise was set up to handle.

The work itself involves extracting standing water, drying structural materials to documented moisture levels, cleaning and sanitizing affected surfaces, and coordinating directly with insurance carriers so the claim moves forward without the property owner carrying the full financial weight up front. Carpet cleaning, air duct cleaning, tile and grout cleaning, and junk and debris removal round out the services, because water losses rarely leave just one thing to deal with. If a loss turns out to need mold remediation, fire restoration, or structural rebuild work, ACS will say so plainly and point you toward the right specialist rather than pretend the scope fits when it does not.

## Where we work

ACS Enterprise is based in Midland, TX and serves the broader Permian Basin region, including Odessa and the surrounding communities across Midland and Ector counties. The area presents its own set of property challenges: caliche-heavy soil that sheds water laterally rather than absorbing it, slab-on-grade construction that is nearly universal in the region and leaves little margin when moisture migrates under a foundation, and periodic heavy rain events that arrive fast on ground that cannot drain quickly. Older neighborhoods in both Midland and Odessa carry plumbing that has been in the ground for decades, and supply-line failures in those homes tend to affect larger areas before anyone notices. Knowing the local housing stock and how water behaves in it is part of doing this work well.
