## Why Los Alamos Properties Are Vulnerable to Fire Damage

Los Alamos is a small, historically rooted town, and much of its residential and commercial building stock reflects that. Many homes were built in the mid-twentieth century with wood-lath construction, older electrical panels, and crawl space configurations that allow smoke to migrate into wall cavities and subfloor systems quickly. The downtown corridor along Bell Street includes structures that predate modern fire-stopping requirements — meaning a fire that starts in one section of a building can push smoke laterally through framing bays before occupants realize the extent of the spread.

The regional climate compounds the problem. Santa Barbara County's low-humidity autumn and winter periods — often dropping below 20% relative humidity — cause smoke odor compounds to bind tightly to porous materials like plaster, exposed wood, and older insulation. That bonding makes superficial cleaning ineffective; without thermal fogging or hydroxyl treatment, the odor resurfaces within weeks.

Wildland-urban interface conditions also matter here. Properties on the edges of Los Alamos are surrounded by dry grass and chaparral, and even a contained structure fire can deposit ash and char particles across neighboring rooflines and HVAC intakes, creating secondary contamination that requires its own assessment.

## Our Fire Damage Restoration Process in Los Alamos

The first priority after the fire department clears a scene is stabilization — boarding windows, tarping compromised roof sections, and securing the structure against weather and unauthorized entry. Los Alamos properties often have detached outbuildings, horse facilities, or large lot footprints, and each structure on the parcel needs its own damage assessment before scope-of-work decisions are made.

From there, the process moves through several distinct phases:

- **Soot and char removal**: Dry chemical sponging of ceilings and walls before any wet cleaning, because introducing moisture to dry soot smears it deeper into porous surfaces.
- **Odor neutralization**: Thermal fogging and hydroxyl generation to reach smoke compounds inside wall cavities, ductwork, and subfloor spaces — areas that a surface wipe-down never touches.
- **Content inventory and pack-out**: Salvageable furnishings, clothing, and personal items are documented, packed, and transported to a controlled facility for cleaning and deodorization.
- **Structural drying and material removal**: Fire suppression water is often left behind after a department response. Affected drywall, insulation, and flooring are removed to prevent secondary mold colonization — which can begin within 48 to 72 hours in a compromised structure.
- **Reconstruction coordination**: Once the structure is clean, dry, and documented, rebuild work begins — framing, drywall, painting, flooring, and finish carpentry — so the property can return to pre-loss condition.

Older Los Alamos homes — particularly those built before the 1970s along the rural parcels north of town — frequently have unpainted interior adobe or plaster walls that absorb smoke odor compounds at a much higher rate than modern drywall. Restoration work in these homes requires pH-adjusted cleaning solutions and longer odor-treatment cycles than a comparable fire loss in a newer tract home.
