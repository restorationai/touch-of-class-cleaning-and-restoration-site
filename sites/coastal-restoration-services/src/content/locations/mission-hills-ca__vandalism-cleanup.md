## Why Mission Hills Properties See Vandalism Damage Differently

The housing stock throughout Mission Hills leans heavily toward post-war construction — think 1950s and 1960s stucco-clad single-family homes and low-rise apartment complexes built on concrete masonry unit foundations. That era of stucco was typically applied in a three-coat system over wire lath, and it is highly porous. Aerosol paint and marker ink wick into the surface rather than sitting on top of it, which means a simple pressure wash often spreads pigment deeper rather than removing it. The same porosity issue shows up on the concrete block walls and pilasters common along commercial stretches in the area.

Broken glass cleanup — from smashed windows, kicked-in doors, or shattered storefronts — carries its own regional wrinkle. Older aluminum-frame sliding windows, still common in Mission Hills rentals and small commercial buildings, tend to shatter into larger, irregular shards rather than the pebbled fragments of tempered glass. Safe removal requires methodical hand-picking and magnet sweeping before any vacuuming, because fragments embed in door tracks and threshold gaps and can cause injuries days after the initial cleanup.

## Our Vandalism Cleanup Process in Mission Hills

Every job starts with a full damage assessment — photographing all affected surfaces, cataloguing broken materials, and identifying any secondary hazards like exposed wiring from a forced entry or biohazard concerns if the vandalism involved bodily fluids or drug paraphernalia. That documentation matters for insurance claims and, in some cases, police reports.

For graffiti removal on Mission Hills stucco and masonry, we use a layered approach: chemical dwell agents matched to the substrate, low-pressure application to avoid driving pigment deeper, and mechanical agitation only where the surface can tolerate it. On painted stucco, we often find that color-matching and repainting a panel section is more reliable than attempting a full chemical strip — and it leaves a cleaner result. For glass and debris cleanup, we follow a sweep-then-vacuum-then-magnet protocol, and we bag and dispose of all materials per local waste handling requirements.

When forced entry has compromised a door or window frame, we coordinate board-up and temporary securing of the opening before we leave the site, so the property is not left exposed while permanent repairs are scheduled.

## Local Note

One pattern we see repeatedly in Mission Hills: the decorative split-face block walls and pilasters common along residential streets in the area are especially difficult to restore after tagging because the irregular surface texture traps pigment in dozens of tiny recesses. Chemical agents that work well on flat stucco often miss the shadowed faces of split-face block entirely. On these surfaces, we use a combination of extended dwell time and a soft rotary brush rather than a pressure wand — it takes longer, but it's the only method that actually clears pigment from the full texture without eroding the block face.
