## Why Cambria Properties Are Especially Vulnerable After Structural Damage

Cambria's building stock skews older, with a significant share of homes built in the 1960s and 1970s using materials and construction methods that don't shed water the way modern assemblies do. Tongue-and-groove board siding, single-pane wood windows, and shallow roof pitches common in the village's hillside neighborhoods are functional in dry conditions but become liabilities the moment a breach opens. A broken window isn't just a security gap — in a coastal environment, it's an invitation for windborne salt spray to begin corroding metal fasteners, staining interior finishes, and driving moisture into wall cavities that may already be carrying elevated humidity from the marine layer.

The wildfire risk adds another dimension. The chaparral and pine forest pressing up against Cambria's east side creates ember-cast exposure during wind events. Fire damage board-up after a structure fire or a near-miss ember strike needs to account for both the immediate opening and the residual heat stress on surrounding framing — boards and fasteners placed into thermally compromised wood require closer spacing and backing support to hold.

## Our Board-Up and Tarping Process in Cambria

When we arrive on-site, the first step is a rapid structural read — identifying every breach, assessing load-bearing condition, and flagging any areas where the damage has compromised the surface we're anchoring into. In Cambria's older homes, that often means probing for soft or charred framing before driving a single fastener.

For broken window board-up, we use exterior-grade plywood cut to overlap the frame by at least two inches on each side, secured with structural screws rather than nails where framing condition allows. For roof tarping, we use reinforced poly tarps rated for coastal UV and wind exposure, weighted and anchored with lumber battens rather than just sandbags — the marine wind off the Pacific can run sustained at 25–35 mph during storm events, and a tarp held only at its edges will fail. We lap the tarp over the ridge when possible so water sheds away from the breach rather than pooling at it.

Fire damage board-up adds a documentation layer: we photograph every opening before and after boarding, note the condition of surrounding materials, and flag anything that suggests the fire investigation or insurance adjuster will need unobstructed access to specific areas before we close them.
