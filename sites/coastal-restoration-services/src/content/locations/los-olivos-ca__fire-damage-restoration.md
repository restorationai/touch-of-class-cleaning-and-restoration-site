When smoke has worked its way into the walls of a wine-country cottage or a ranch-style home along Grand Avenue, the clock starts immediately: soot is acidic, and within 72 hours it begins etching painted surfaces, corroding metal fixtures, and permanently staining porous stone and brick. ## Why Los Olivos Properties Face Distinct Fire Damage Challenges

The building stock in Los Olivos leans older and more rural than you might expect for a Santa Barbara County community. Many homes were built in the mid-20th century with wood-frame construction, open-rafter ceilings, and natural materials — adobe plaster, rough-sawn timber, clay tile — that absorb smoke and soot deeply and unevenly. Unlike modern drywall, which can often be cut out and replaced cleanly, adobe and plaster walls hold odor compounds in their matrix. A surface wipe-down is not enough; the material itself has to be treated or, in severe cases, removed.

The valley's low humidity for much of the year also means smoke residue dries and bonds to surfaces faster than in coastal climates. By the time a homeowner calls after a fire, the window for easier cleaning has often already closed. Acting within the first 24 to 48 hours makes a measurable difference in what can be restored versus what has to be replaced.

The surrounding wildland interface adds another layer. Los Olivos is surrounded by oak woodland and chaparral, and homes on the rural edges of town carry real exposure to wind-driven embers during Red Flag events. Structural fire damage from a wildfire encroachment typically involves the exterior envelope — siding, eaves, attic insulation, roof decking — in addition to interior smoke infiltration, which requires a different sequencing of work than a contained interior fire.

## Our Fire Damage Restoration Process in Los Olivos

Every job begins with a thorough damage assessment before any cleaning or demolition starts. We photograph and document every affected surface and material — both for the scope of work and for your insurance carrier's records. From there, the process follows a defined sequence:

**Structural stabilization and board-up.** If the fire compromised windows, doors, or roof sections, we secure the structure against weather and unauthorized entry before anything else.

**Smoke and soot removal.** Dry soot is vacuumed with HEPA equipment before any wet cleaning begins — introducing moisture to dry soot spreads it. We use dry chemical sponges on delicate surfaces and alkaline cleaners on harder materials, matching the method to the residue type and surface.

**Odor neutralization.** Smoke odor is not a surface problem — it's a penetration problem. We use thermal fogging and hydroxyl generation to reach the same microscopic channels the smoke traveled through, neutralizing odor compounds rather than masking them.

**Structural drying and content handling.** Water from firefighting efforts is often as damaging as the fire itself. We deploy commercial-grade dehumidifiers and air movers to dry affected framing and subfloor before reconstruction begins.

**Reconstruction coordination.** Once the affected areas are clean, dry, and documented, we move into repair and rebuild — patching, reframing, drywall, painting, and finish work — so you are dealing with one contractor through the entire process.

## Local Note

One thing that comes up repeatedly on Los Olivos fire jobs is the presence of older clay tile roofing over skip-sheathing — a construction method common in California ranch and Spanish Colonial homes built before the 1970s. When a fire burns through the attic of one of these homes, the skip-sheathing (spaced boards rather than solid plywood decking) allows smoke to migrate laterally across a much wider area than a modern sheathed roof would. Homeowners are sometimes surprised to find smoke damage in rooms far from the fire's origin. We account for this during the initial assessment so the scope of cleaning reflects where the smoke actually traveled, not just where the flames were visible.
