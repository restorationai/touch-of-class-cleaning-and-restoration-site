Pismo Beach sits where the Pacific doesn't just shape the view — it shapes what happens inside your walls. The marine layer that rolls in off San Luis Obispo Bay most mornings keeps relative humidity elevated year-round, and that persistent moisture gives odors somewhere to live. Smoke residue from a kitchen fire, pet dander ground into coastal-cottage carpeting, or the musty aftermath of a slow plumbing leak can all bond to porous surfaces faster here than in drier inland climates. Coastal Restoration Services Inc works with Pismo Beach homeowners and property managers to identify exactly what's causing the smell and eliminate it at the molecular level — not mask it.

## Why Pismo Beach Properties Hold Odors Longer

The same salt air that makes Pismo Beach desirable accelerates the way odor compounds embed themselves in building materials. Sodium chloride particles in the air act as a mild hygroscopic agent, meaning walls, upholstery, and subflooring absorb and retain slightly more ambient moisture than they would fifty miles inland. When a fire, flood, or biological event introduces odor-causing compounds into that environment, those compounds don't just sit on the surface — they migrate into damp drywall paper, into the grain of wood trim, and into the fibers of insulation batts.

Pismo Beach's housing stock adds another layer of complexity. A significant portion of the city's residential properties are older beach cottages and mid-century bungalows, many with original wood-panel interiors and minimal vapor barriers. These materials are highly absorbent. A smoke event in a 1960s beach cottage on the west side of town will typically require more aggressive treatment than the same fire in a newer construction home, because the uncoated wood has been soaking up decades of coastal air — and now smoke — like a sponge.

## Our Deodorization Process, Calibrated for Coastal Conditions

Effective odor removal isn't a single step. It's a sequence, and the sequence matters especially in a humid coastal environment where skipping a step means the odor returns with the next marine layer.

**Source removal first.** No deodorization technology eliminates an odor that's still generating. We locate and remove charred material, saturated insulation, or contaminated contents before any treatment begins.

**Thermal fogging** penetrates the same pathways that smoke traveled during a fire. The fog particles are small enough to reach inside wall cavities, under baseboards, and into HVAC ducting — the places a spray bottle never reaches. In older Pismo Beach homes with open-framing cavities, this step is particularly important.

**Hydroxyl generation** is our preferred ongoing treatment for occupied or semi-occupied spaces. Unlike ozone, hydroxyl generators are safe to run while residents or pets remain in adjacent areas, which matters for vacation rentals and owner-occupied homes that can't be fully vacated. Hydroxyl radicals break odor molecules apart at the chemical bond — they don't cover the smell, they destroy the compound causing it.

**Ozone treatment** is reserved for unoccupied spaces where a higher-concentration, faster-acting approach is warranted — typically after a severe smoke event or a long-term pet odor situation. The space must remain unoccupied during treatment and be properly ventilated afterward.

**Final air quality verification** closes the job. We don't rely on the "sniff test." Readings confirm that airborne volatile organic compound levels have returned to baseline before we sign off.

Our team holds IICRC certification, and all deodorization work follows current IICRC S520 and S500 standards for source removal and structural drying — relevant because moisture control and odor control are rarely separate problems in a coastal climate.
