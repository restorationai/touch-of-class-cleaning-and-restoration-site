## Why Morro Bay Properties Are Vulnerable to Storm Damage

The geography here works against homeowners during severe weather. Morro Bay's position along Highway 1 puts it directly in the path of onshore flow, and the low-lying areas near the estuary and back bay can collect runoff faster than storm drains handle it. Many homes in the older residential sections of town were built in the 1950s through 1970s, when coastal wind-load standards were considerably more lenient than current California Building Code requirements. That means roofing systems, fascia boards, and older single-pane windows are more likely to fail under sustained gusts than newer construction would be.

Salt air accelerates corrosion on metal flashing, gutters, and roof fasteners — so a storm that might cause minor damage inland can compromise roofing assemblies here that were already weakened by years of marine exposure. Mature Monterey cypress and eucalyptus trees, common throughout the area, have shallow root systems in the sandy coastal soils, making them prone to uprooting during saturated-ground wind events. When a tree comes down on a roof or fence line, the damage window for water intrusion opens immediately.

## Our Storm Damage Restoration Process in Morro Bay

The first priority after any storm event is stopping additional loss. That starts with a thorough exterior assessment — roof, siding, windows, and any breached openings — followed by emergency tarping or board-up to weatherproof the structure before the next weather system arrives. Along the Morro Bay coastline, that window between storms can be short, especially from November through March.

Once the structure is secured, the interior assessment begins. We use thermal imaging and moisture meters to map water intrusion that isn't visible to the eye — saturated wall cavities, wet insulation behind drywall, and moisture wicking up through subfloor materials. Wet materials that aren't identified and dried within 24 to 48 hours create conditions where mold colonization can begin, which turns a storm claim into a significantly more complex remediation.

Debris removal — downed branches, displaced roofing material, damaged siding — is documented photographically before anything is moved. That documentation matters when your insurance adjuster reviews the claim. We then move into structural drying using industrial air movers and dehumidifiers calibrated for the ambient humidity levels typical of a coastal California environment, where outdoor relative humidity rarely drops low enough to assist natural drying.
