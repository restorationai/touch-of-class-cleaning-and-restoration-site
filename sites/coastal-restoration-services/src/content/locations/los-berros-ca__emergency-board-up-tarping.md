## Why Los Berros Properties Face Elevated Board-Up and Tarping Needs

The terrain around Los Berros creates a specific set of vulnerabilities that don't show up in inland communities. The coastal fog cycle — dense marine layer most mornings, drying winds by afternoon — means an unsecured opening cycles between wet and dry conditions multiple times in a single day. Moisture that enters through a broken window or fire-damaged wall cavity at dawn can begin supporting mold colonization within 24 to 48 hours if the opening isn't sealed. That timeline is compressed here compared to drier inland valleys.

The hillside vegetation common to this part of San Luis Obispo County also means wildfire is a recurring threat rather than a once-in-a-generation event. Fire damage board-up in this area frequently involves charred roof decking, melted fascia, and compromised attic vents — all of which need temporary closure before smoke residue and ash can migrate further into the living space. Older ranch-style homes on larger rural parcels, typical of the Los Berros corridor, often have wide roof overhangs and exposed rafter tails that require custom-cut plywood rather than standard panel sizing.

## Our Emergency Board-Up and Tarping Process in Los Berros

When we arrive on-site, the first step is a rapid perimeter walk to identify every breach — not just the obvious ones. Fire events routinely compromise soffit vents, gable ends, and crawlspace access panels that are easy to miss in the initial adrenaline of a loss. We photograph and document each opening before any material goes up, which matters later when your insurance adjuster reviews the scope.

For roof tarping, we use reinforced polyethylene tarps rated for high-UV coastal exposure, secured with weighted battens or screwed lumber rather than simple rope ties that the afternoon onshore wind can loosen overnight. On pitched roofs with fire or wind damage, we ridge-cap the tarp and extend it past the drip edge to prevent water from wicking back under the membrane — a detail that matters when the marine layer deposits condensation even without measurable rainfall.

Broken window board-up uses nominal ½-inch or ¾-inch OSB cut to overlap the frame by at least two inches on each side, fastened into the structural framing rather than just the window casing. For occupied or partially occupied structures, we can install polycarbonate panel systems that allow light transmission while maintaining security — useful when a property needs to remain livable during the claims process.

## Local Note

One thing we've learned working in the Los Berros area and the broader coastal San Luis Obispo corridor: the combination of marine-layer moisture and fire ash creates a particularly corrosive surface condition on exposed metal fasteners and flashing. When we install temporary tarping over a fire-damaged roof in this microclimate, we use hot-dipped galvanized or stainless fasteners rather than standard zinc-plated screws — the latter can begin to rust and stain the underlying roofing material within a week of marine fog exposure, complicating the eventual permanent repair. It's a small detail, but it's the kind of thing that shows up in the final reconstruction estimate if it's ignored at the board-up stage.
