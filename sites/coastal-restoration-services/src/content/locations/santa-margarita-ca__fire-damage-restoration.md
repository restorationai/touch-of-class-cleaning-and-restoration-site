Santa Margarita's position in the inland foothills of San Luis Obispo County means it sits squarely in the zone where coastal marine influence fades and dry, easterly wind events take over. Older wood-frame construction also tends to have more uninsulated cavities where smoke odor can settle and persist long after visible soot is removed.

## Our Fire Damage Restoration Process in Santa Margarita

Every fire loss is different, but the sequence of work follows a disciplined order that prevents secondary damage from compounding the original loss.

The first step on-site is a thorough assessment — not just of the rooms with visible char and soot, but of adjacent spaces, the attic, crawlspace, and HVAC distribution system. Smoke migrates under pressure, and the areas that look clean are often where odor problems originate weeks later if they're skipped.

Once the scope is documented and photographed for your insurance carrier, structural stabilization comes next: boarding windows, tarping any compromised roof sections, and securing the building envelope against weather intrusion. San Luis Obispo County can see rapid temperature swings between day and night, and an open structure is vulnerable to secondary moisture damage even in a dry climate.

From there, the process moves to controlled demolition of unsalvageable materials, dry and wet soot removal using HEPA-filtered vacuums and appropriate chemical sponges matched to the soot type, and thermal fogging or hydroxyl treatment for odor neutralization deep in wall cavities and structural members. Ozone and hydroxyl generators are staged based on occupancy status and the specific chemistry of the smoke residue present. Final reconstruction — framing, drywall, insulation, painting — closes out the project so you're not managing two separate contractors.
