## Why Vandenberg AFB Properties See Distinct Storm Damage Patterns

The geography of Vandenberg AFB creates conditions that amplify storm damage in ways that aren't obvious until you've worked here. The base occupies a broad coastal terrace where wind accelerates as it comes off the ocean, and there's very little natural windbreak before it hits structures. During strong Pacific fronts, sustained winds can strip roofing material, drive rain horizontally into window frames and exterior wall assemblies, and topple mature eucalyptus and Monterey cypress trees that have shallow root systems in the sandy coastal soils.

Those fallen trees are a recurring problem. The same sandy, well-drained soils that make the base's terrain distinctive also mean large trees don't anchor as deeply as they would in clay-heavy inland soils. A tree that looks healthy can uproot in a 50 mph gust and land on a roof, a fence line, or a vehicle. Tree damage cleanup in this environment often means coordinating debris removal before structural assessment can even begin.

Salt air compounds the damage timeline. Exposed metal fasteners, flashing, and HVAC components corrode faster here than they would 20 miles inland, so storm damage that might be cosmetic elsewhere can accelerate into a structural problem if it isn't addressed within days.

## Our Storm Damage Restoration Process at Vandenberg AFB

Every storm damage job starts with a thorough exterior and interior assessment — we're looking at the roof deck, attic cavity, wall cavities, and any below-grade spaces where water may have migrated. On the Central Coast, storm water intrusion often travels farther than it appears on the surface; a compromised ridge cap can mean wet insulation two rooms away from the visible stain on the ceiling.

Once the scope is documented with photos and moisture readings, we move into debris removal and emergency weatherproofing. Tarping and board-up work happens fast because the Central Coast storm season doesn't always give you a dry window between systems. We use commercial-grade tarps rated for coastal wind loads, not the lightweight polyethylene that fails in the next squall.

Drying and structural repair follow a sequenced process: extract standing water if present, deploy desiccant or refrigerant dehumidifiers appropriate to the space, monitor moisture levels in framing and sheathing daily, and only close up wall cavities once readings confirm the material is within acceptable range. Rushing that step is how mold colonizes behind a freshly painted wall.

## Local Note: Coastal Soil and Foundation Drainage

One thing that catches property owners off guard after heavy storms at Vandenberg AFB is subsurface water migration. The sandy coastal soils drain quickly on the surface, which can make it look like the ground has dried out within a day or two of a storm.
