## Why Lompoc Properties See Water Damage Issues

Lompoc's housing stock tells a story in the water damage calls we receive. A significant portion of homes in the city were built between the 1940s and 1970s — an era when galvanized steel supply lines were standard. Those pipes corrode from the inside out, and by the time a pinhole leak becomes visible, water has often been tracking behind drywall or under subfloor for weeks. Older homes in this range also tend to have minimal vapor barriers under the crawl space, which means ground moisture wicks upward and compounds any active leak.

Lompoc's agricultural heritage also shaped its drainage patterns. Many residential lots in and around the city sit on clay-heavy soils that don't absorb water quickly. During the rainy season — typically November through March — saturated ground pushes water toward foundations rather than away from them. Homes with older or shallow French drains are especially vulnerable to water intrusion along the base of exterior walls and through garage slabs.

## Our Water Damage Restoration Process in Lompoc

Every water damage job starts with a thorough moisture assessment before any equipment goes in. We use thermal imaging cameras and calibrated moisture meters to map exactly where water has traveled — including inside wall assemblies, under flooring, and into ceiling cavities. In Lompoc's older homes, this step is especially important because water often migrates farther than the visible damage suggests.

Once the scope is documented, water extraction begins using truck-mounted and portable extraction units sized to the loss. Standing water under flooring or in crawl spaces requires different tooling than surface flooding, and we carry both. After extraction, we set a drying system — commercial-grade desiccant or refrigerant dehumidifiers paired with high-velocity air movers — calibrated to the square footage and material types present. Drying typically runs three to five days, with daily moisture readings to confirm the structure is tracking toward its dry standard. We don't pull equipment early because a moisture reading looks close; we pull it when the numbers confirm it.

Documentation runs throughout the entire process: photos, moisture logs, and equipment placement records. That file goes to your insurance adjuster.

## Local Note

One thing we've learned working in Lompoc specifically: homes with original hardwood floors — common in properties built before 1960 — behave differently under drying conditions than modern engineered wood or LVP. Solid hardwood expands when wet and can buckle if dehumidification is pushed too aggressively. We slow the drying rate on those floors intentionally and monitor cupping daily. It takes longer, but it's the difference between a floor that flattens back out and one that has to be replaced. If you have original hardwood and water has reached it, mention that when you call — it affects how we set up the drying system from day one.
