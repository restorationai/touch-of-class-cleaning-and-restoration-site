## Why Templeton Properties See Water Damage Issues

The Templeton area is a mix of older ranch-style homes, newer subdivisions on the valley edges, and agricultural properties with aging infrastructure. Many homes built in the 1970s and 1980s still have galvanized steel supply lines that corrode from the inside out, often showing no visible warning before they fail. When they do fail, water spreads quickly through subfloor assemblies and into wall cavities before it ever reaches a drain.

The region's Mediterranean climate adds another layer of risk. Extended dry summers followed by concentrated winter rain events mean that roof penetrations, window flashing, and foundation perimeter seals that held up through a dry season can fail suddenly when the first heavy rains arrive. Crawl spaces under older Templeton homes are particularly vulnerable: without adequate vapor barriers or drainage, groundwater intrusion during wet winters can saturate floor joists and create conditions where moisture lingers long after the rain stops.

## Our Water Cleanup Process in Templeton

Every job starts with a thorough moisture assessment using thermal imaging cameras and calibrated moisture meters. Water visible on the surface is rarely the full picture. Behind baseboards, under vinyl plank flooring, and inside wall cavities, moisture readings often tell a different story than what you can see or touch.

Once the scope is mapped, standing water is extracted using truck-mounted and portable extraction units. After bulk water is removed, the drying phase begins with industrial air movers and desiccant or refrigerant dehumidifiers positioned according to the IICRC S500 standard for structural drying. Moisture readings are logged at each visit so the drying curve is documented, not estimated. This documentation matters when you file a claim: adjusters want to see a drying log, not a verbal summary.

For homes with hardwood floors or plaster-and-lath wall construction, which appear in some of Templeton's older properties, the drying timeline is longer than it would be in a newer home with drywall and engineered flooring. Plaster releases absorbed moisture slowly, and hardwood subfloors can hold water in the wood grain well after the surface feels dry to the touch. Our WRT- and ASD-certified technicians adjust equipment placement and drying targets to account for the specific materials in each structure.
