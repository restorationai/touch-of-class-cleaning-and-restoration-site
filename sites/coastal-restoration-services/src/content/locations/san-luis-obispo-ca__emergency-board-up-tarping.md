San Luis Obispo sits at the intersection of coastal fog, dry-season Santa Ana wind events, and a housing stock that ranges from 1920s Craftsman bungalows to mid-century stucco—a combination that can turn a broken window, a fire, or a storm-lifted roof into a cascading problem within hours. When a structure is left open to the elements, secondary damage compounds fast: fog-driven moisture seeps into exposed framing overnight, and the region's warm afternoon temperatures accelerate mold colonization in as little as 24 to 48 hours. Getting plywood over openings and poly tarps secured to the roof deck is the single most effective way to stop that clock.

## Why San Luis Obispo Properties Are Vulnerable After Structural Damage

The Central Coast's climate is mild by most measures, but it creates specific risks for damaged structures. Marine layer rolls in most mornings from April through August, pushing relative humidity well above 80 percent before noon. A home with a fire-damaged wall or a broken window left open through a single overnight fog cycle can absorb enough ambient moisture to require full structural drying on top of whatever fire or impact damage already existed.

Beyond climate, the city's building stock matters. A significant share of residential properties in San Luis Obispo were built before 1960, when balloon-frame construction was common. In these homes, wall cavities run continuously from the foundation to the roof line with no fire blocking—meaning an opening at one level exposes the entire cavity system to weather. Tarping and boarding these structures requires careful attention to where moisture can travel, not just where the visible damage is.

Wildfire smoke is a separate concern. When fires burn in the hills east of the city or in the Los Padres National Forest, ash and particulate settle on rooflines and collect in open cavities. Boarding and tarping promptly limits how deeply those contaminants penetrate before remediation begins.

## Our Emergency Board-Up and Tarping Process in San Luis Obispo

Every job starts with a documented site assessment—photos, measurements, and a written scope—before a single sheet of plywood goes up. That documentation matters for insurance claims and for any subsequent permits the city may require for repair work.

For window and door openings, we cut plywood to fit flush with the frame, secure it with structural screws rather than nails, and seal the perimeter with weatherproof tape to block wind-driven moisture. Broken glass is cleared and disposed of on-site. For larger breaches—fire-damaged walls, vehicle impacts, collapsed sections—we frame temporary supports where needed before sheathing.

Roof tarping follows the IICRC and industry standard approach of running tarps from the ridge down and securing them with weighted battens or cap strips rather than staples alone. Staple-only tarps pull free in the first significant wind event; battened tarps hold through the kind of offshore gusts that accelerate down the grades west of the Santa Lucia Range. We use 6-mil reinforced poly for standard coverage and 10-mil woven poly on roofs with significant structural exposure.

## Local Note

One pattern that comes up repeatedly on older properties in San Luis Obispo: the original roof decking on pre-1950 homes is often 1x6 skip sheathing rather than continuous plywood. When a section of roofing is lost or damaged, that open-gap decking lets wind-driven rain pass almost unimpeded into the attic. A standard tarp laid over skip sheathing without a solid substrate beneath it can pool and sag rather than shed water. On these roofs, we lay a layer of 1-inch foam board or plywood scraps across the gap areas before the tarp goes down—it takes an extra 20 minutes but prevents the tarp from becoming a water-collection basin that makes interior damage worse.
