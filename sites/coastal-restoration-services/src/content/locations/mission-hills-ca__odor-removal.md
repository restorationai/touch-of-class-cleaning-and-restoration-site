Mission Hills sits in the northwestern San Fernando Valley, where warm, dry Santa Ana wind events can lock smoke and chemical odors deep into building materials for weeks after the source is gone. Whether a kitchen fire left char smell baked into the drywall of a mid-century ranch home, a sewage backup soaked into subfloor framing, or a tenant's tobacco habit saturated every surface of a rental unit, odors in this climate don't simply air out — they need active, targeted treatment to break down at the molecular level.

## Why Mission Hills Properties Hold Odors Longer

The housing stock in Mission Hills skews heavily toward homes built between the late 1950s and early 1980s. Many of these properties feature original plaster-over-lath walls, hardwood subfloors, and older HVAC ductwork — all porous materials that absorb odor compounds far more aggressively than modern construction. When smoke or sewage gases infiltrate a plaster wall, the odor molecules bind to the calcium in the substrate and resist simple surface cleaning.

The regional climate compounds the problem. The San Fernando Valley's temperature swings — cool marine-influenced mornings giving way to dry afternoon heat — cause building materials to expand and contract daily. That cycling draws odor-laden air deeper into wall cavities and flooring gaps. During Santa Ana conditions, when relative humidity can drop below 10%, volatile organic compounds off-gas more readily from contaminated materials, spreading the smell through the entire structure even after the source has been removed.

## Our Odor Removal and Deodorization Process in Mission Hills

Effective deodorization is not a single-step spray-and-leave service. The process begins with a thorough source identification walk-through — locating every material that has absorbed the contaminant, including inside HVAC registers, behind baseboards, and inside wall cavities if necessary. Skipping source removal and jumping straight to masking agents is the single most common reason odors return within weeks.

Once contaminated materials are either cleaned or removed, the treatment phase is matched to the odor type and the building's construction:

- **Thermal fogging** — a solvent-based deodorizer is vaporized and dispersed as a fine fog that follows the same pathways smoke traveled, reaching inside wall cavities, ductwork, and under flooring. It's particularly effective in the older, more porous construction common in Mission Hills.
- **Hydroxyl deodorization** — hydroxyl generators produce reactive molecules that oxidize odor compounds in the air and on surfaces without requiring occupants to vacate. This is the preferred method when pets, plants, or sensitive materials are present.
- **Ozone treatment** — high-concentration ozone is highly effective on stubborn smoke and biological odors in unoccupied spaces. Because ozone at treatment concentrations is harmful to occupants, the space must remain vacant during the process and be ventilated thoroughly before re-entry.
- **Enzyme and oxidizing agents** — applied directly to porous surfaces like concrete, wood framing, and grout for biological odors (urine, sewage, decomposition).

All drying and deodorization work follows IICRC standards, ensuring the process is documented and defensible for insurance purposes.

## Local Note: Older Ductwork Is Often the Hidden Culprit

In Mission Hills homes built before 1975, the original forced-air ductwork is frequently constructed from duct board — a fiberglass-lined cardboard product — rather than sheet metal. Duct board is extremely porous and absorbs smoke, mold spores, and biological odors readily. When a fire or sewage event occurs, the HVAC system can distribute contaminated air through the duct board lining and then continue off-gassing for months. During every odor assessment in this part of the Valley, the duct system gets inspected directly — not just the living spaces — because treating the rooms without addressing the ducts almost always results in odor recurrence within 30 to 60 days.
