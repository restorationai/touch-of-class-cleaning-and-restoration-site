## Our Board-Up and Tarping Process in Atascadero

When a call comes in from an Atascadero address, the first step is a rapid structural assessment on arrival — before a single sheet of plywood goes up. Crews identify load-bearing points, check for active hazards like broken glass or unstable debris, and photograph every affected opening. This documentation matters because it becomes part of the insurance claim file.

For broken window board-ups, we use minimum ½-inch CDX plywood cut to overlap the frame by at least two inches on each side, secured with exterior-grade screws rather than nails, which hold better in the dry, expanding-and-contracting wood common in Atascadero's temperature swings. For roof tarping, we use 6-mil polyethylene or heavier woven poly tarps depending on the size of the breach, with 2×4 battens screwed through the tarp into the roof deck to prevent wind uplift — not just weighted edges that can shift overnight.

Fire damage board-up requires an additional step: coordinating with the local fire authority or code enforcement if the structure has been red-tagged. We carry the documentation to work on posted properties and can communicate directly with the Atascadero Fire Department or SLO County building officials so the work proceeds without delays.

Atascadero's older wood-frame homes — particularly those built in the 1960s and 1970s on the city's west side — frequently used redwood framing, which is more resistant to rot than pine but can be brittle and prone to splitting when fasteners are driven into dry, aged wood.
