Los Olivos sits in the Santa Ynez Valley where the coastal marine layer pushes inland through the San Marcos Pass corridor, keeping humidity elevated well into summer mornings and slowing the natural evaporation that normally helps a wet structure begin drying on its own. When a supply line fails under a farmhouse sink or a water heater lets go overnight, that ambient moisture means standing water lingers longer and saturates deeper into subfloor assemblies than it would in a drier inland climate. Coastal Restoration Services Inc responds to emergency water cleanup calls throughout Los Olivos and the surrounding Santa Ynez Valley, bringing the drying science and equipment needed for this specific environment.

## Why Los Olivos Properties Face Particular Water Damage Risks

Much of the residential and agricultural building stock in and around Los Olivos dates to mid-twentieth century construction or earlier, when pier-and-post foundations and tongue-and-groove subfloor sheathing were standard. That framing holds water differently than modern OSB: the wood is often denser and slower to release moisture, but it is also more prone to cupping and delamination once saturation sets in. Older galvanized supply lines, common in homes built before copper became the regional standard, corrode from the inside out and can fail with little visible warning. The combination of aging plumbing and a foundation style that allows ground moisture to migrate upward creates conditions where a single plumbing event can affect not just the finished floor but the structural framing beneath it.

The valley's clay-heavy soils also matter. After heavy winter rainfall, the ground around foundations stays saturated for weeks, and any crack in a stem wall or an improperly graded yard can direct that water toward a crawl space rather than away from it. Flood water cleanup in this area frequently involves crawl space extraction as well as interior living space, because the two moisture sources compound each other.

## Our Water Cleanup Process in Los Olivos

The first step on any water damage call is containment and documentation. The crew photographs and meters the affected area before moving anything, establishing a moisture baseline that supports your insurance claim and guides the drying plan. Thermal imaging helps locate water that has wicked behind baseboards or into wall cavities without showing at the surface, which is especially common in plaster-over-lath construction still found in older Los Olivos homes.

Extraction comes next. Truck-mounted and portable extraction units pull standing water from hard floors, carpet, and pad. Once bulk water is removed, the focus shifts to structural drying: commercial-grade desiccant and refrigerant dehumidifiers are placed to address the valley's elevated ambient humidity, and high-velocity air movers are positioned to accelerate evaporation from framing and subfloor materials. Moisture readings are logged daily against drying targets set per the IICRC S500 standard, so the process is documented and defensible rather than based on guesswork.

Certifications held by the team include IICRC Firm Certification, Water Damage Restoration Technician (WRT), and Applied Structural Drying (ASD), which means the drying plan is built on tested methodology, not approximation.
