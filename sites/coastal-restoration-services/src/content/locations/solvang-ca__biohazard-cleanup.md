## Why Solvang Properties Present Unique Biohazard Considerations

Solvang sits in the Santa Ynez Valley, where the climate swings between dry summer heat and cool, sometimes damp winters. That seasonal moisture — combined with the age of much of the residential housing stock in and around town — means that when a biohazard incident occurs, affected materials often include older subfloor assemblies, plaster or lath-and-plaster wall systems, and unfinished crawl spaces that have never been sealed to modern standards. Porous, aged materials absorb biological contamination more deeply than newer construction, which affects how thoroughly the remediation must be carried out and how long the process takes.

The valley's agricultural heritage also means some properties on the outskirts of Solvang include outbuildings, detached casitas, or converted structures that weren't originally designed as living spaces. These can present access and ventilation challenges during cleanup that a standard residential job wouldn't require.

## Our Biohazard Cleanup Process in Solvang

Every job begins with a contained, private assessment. A technician walks the affected area, identifies all impacted surfaces and materials, and outlines a remediation scope before any work begins — so you know exactly what will happen and in what order.

The actual remediation follows a structured sequence: containment of the affected zone using physical barriers, removal of contaminated porous materials that cannot be decontaminated in place, treatment of hard surfaces with EPA-registered hospital-grade disinfectants, and final verification before containment is removed. All biohazardous waste is packaged and transported in compliance with California Department of Public Health regulations governing infectious and biological waste — this is not a step that can be improvised, and it's one reason this work requires trained technicians rather than a general cleaning crew.

For properties with older construction — the kind common in Solvang's residential neighborhoods — we probe beneath surface materials when contamination may have wicked into subfloor or wall cavities. Skipping that step on an older home is how a remediated-looking room develops an odor problem weeks later.
