That climate swing — intense heat followed by humidity that creeps back in — means odors that seem to disappear in July can return with a vengeance by November. Coastal Restoration Services Inc brings professional-grade deodorization equipment and IICRC-certified technicians to Callender properties when surface sprays and open windows have already failed.

## Why Callender Properties Hold Odors Longer Than You'd Expect

The Sacramento Valley's temperature extremes do something specific to older wood-frame homes common in this part of California: they cause framing, subfloor, and wall cavities to expand and contract repeatedly over the years, opening micro-gaps where smoke residue, mold off-gassing, and biological odors settle in. Once odor molecules migrate into those voids, no amount of ventilation pulls them back out. You can paint over smoke-stained drywall, but the aldehydes and acrolein compounds from a kitchen fire or a cigarette-saturated bedroom will continue to volatilize through the new finish for months.

Callender's proximity to wildfire corridors adds another layer. Smoke from regional fires doesn't just drift through open windows — it infiltrates HVAC systems, attic insulation, and crawl spaces, leaving a fine layer of char particulate that smells faint at first and intensifies when the heat kicks on the following season. By the time a homeowner calls, the odor source is rarely where they think it is.

## Our Odor Removal and Deodorization Process in Callender

Every job starts with a systematic source survey. We don't apply deodorizing chemistry to a space we haven't mapped. Technicians use moisture meters, thermal imaging, and direct sampling to locate where odor compounds are concentrated — under flooring, inside wall cavities, in ductwork — before selecting a treatment method.

For smoke odor removal following a fire or wildfire infiltration event, **thermal fogging** is often the right tool. The fogging solution is heated into a dry vapor that follows the same pathways smoke originally traveled, bonding with odor molecules at the molecular level rather than masking them. For spaces where occupants or pets will return quickly, **hydroxyl deodorization** is a safer active-treatment option — hydroxyl generators produce the same oxidizing radicals the sun generates outdoors, breaking down odor compounds without requiring the space to be vacated for extended periods.

In cases involving severe biological contamination or long-term cigarette saturation, **ozone treatment** delivers the deepest penetration, but the space must remain unoccupied during treatment and properly ventilated afterward. We determine which method — or which combination — fits the specific contamination profile and the practical constraints of the property.

## Equipment Calibrated to the Conditions Here

Northern California's low-humidity summers mean that ozone and hydroxyl equipment reach effective concentration levels faster than they would in a coastal marine environment. That's useful — treatment cycles can sometimes be shorter. But it also means technicians need to monitor concentration levels more carefully to avoid over-treating materials like leather furniture or natural-fiber rugs that can degrade with prolonged oxidizer exposure.

For wildfire smoke infiltration into HVAC systems — a recurring issue for properties in this region — we pair duct cleaning with an in-line hydroxyl or ozone pass through the duct network before sealing and restarting the system. Running the HVAC without addressing the ductwork first simply redistributes the problem.

## Local Note

One pattern we see repeatedly on properties in this part of California: homes with evaporative (swamp) coolers rather than central air conditioning. Evaporative coolers draw outside air directly into the living space with minimal filtration, which means during a wildfire smoke event, the cooler itself becomes a primary odor source — the pads, the reservoir, and the housing all absorb smoke compounds. Replacing the pads and deodorizing the cooler housing is a step that gets skipped when a restoration crew isn't familiar with how common this equipment is in the Central Valley and foothills. We account for it as part of every smoke odor assessment in Callender.
