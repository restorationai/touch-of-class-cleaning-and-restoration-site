## Why Mission Hills Properties See Water Damage Issues

The San Fernando Valley's climate runs hot and dry for most of the year, which leads many homeowners to underestimate their plumbing risk — but that dry heat is part of the problem. Soil in this part of Los Angeles County contracts significantly during drought stretches, then swells when seasonal rains arrive. That ground movement puts stress on older supply lines and slab penetrations, and Mission Hills has a substantial share of homes built in the 1950s through 1970s that were never replumbed from their original galvanized or early copper runs.

When those pipes fail — whether from a pinhole leak behind a bathroom wall or a full supply-line rupture under a kitchen slab — water migrates fast through the concrete and into flooring assemblies. Hardwood and engineered wood floors common in mid-century remodels trap moisture underneath, and the subfloor can be saturated while the surface still looks dry. Stucco exteriors, standard throughout the neighborhood, can also absorb and hold rainwater intrusion at window frames and roof-wall intersections, creating hidden moisture pockets that don't surface until drywall starts to stain or buckle.

## Our Water Damage Restoration Process in Mission Hills

Every job starts with a moisture mapping inspection — thermal imaging and calibrated moisture meters across walls, floors, and ceilings to locate water that isn't visible. This step matters especially in Mission Hills' older homes, where original plaster-and-lath construction in pre-1960s sections of the neighborhood holds moisture differently than modern drywall. Plaster releases water slowly, so readings that look acceptable on day one can still show elevated moisture on day three without proper drying equipment in place.

After mapping, standing water is removed using truck-mounted extraction units. Structural drying follows with industrial-grade desiccant or refrigerant dehumidifiers and high-velocity air movers positioned to create a controlled drying chamber in the affected space. Drying logs are recorded daily — moisture readings, equipment settings, ambient temperature and humidity — because insurance carriers and any subsequent contractors need that documentation to validate the scope of work. Once materials reach target dryness levels, affected areas are treated and cleared before any reconstruction begins.
