## Why Morro Bay Properties See Mold Issues

The oceanfront climate here is unlike anything inland San Luis Obispo County deals with. Salt-laden air accelerates the degradation of caulking, window glazing, and exterior paint — the same micro-gaps that let moisture into wall cavities. Many homes along the waterfront and in older residential pockets of Morro Bay were built in the 1950s through 1970s, before modern vapor barriers were standard. Those homes often have wood-framed walls with no interior moisture management layer, meaning a single season of condensation can saturate insulation and start mold growth that isn't visible until a renovation or inspection uncovers it.

Crawl spaces are a particular concern in this area. The combination of cool ground temperatures and warm interior air creates a condensation cycle that keeps crawl space framing damp for months at a time. When that framing shows elevated moisture readings — anything above 19 percent wood moisture content is a threshold worth taking seriously — mold abatement becomes a structural conversation, not just a surface cleaning job.

## Our Mold Remediation Process in Morro Bay

Every mold remediation job starts with a thorough assessment before any physical work begins. That means moisture mapping with a thermal camera and pin-type meters to identify hidden wet zones, followed by air sampling if the extent of contamination isn't visually clear. Understanding where the moisture source is — and confirming it's been stopped — is the foundation of any remediation that actually holds.

Once the scope is established, containment goes up. Negative air pressure barriers isolate the work area so disturbed spores don't migrate into living spaces during removal. Affected porous materials — drywall, insulation, compromised wood framing — are removed, bagged, and disposed of per California Department of Public Health guidelines. Non-porous surfaces are HEPA-vacuumed and treated with an EPA-registered antimicrobial agent. Air scrubbers with HEPA filtration run continuously throughout the process and for a period after physical work is complete to capture airborne particulates.

Before any reconstruction begins, we verify that the affected area has returned to normal moisture levels and that air quality readings are within acceptable ranges. In coastal environments like Morro Bay, that drying phase can take longer than it would in a drier inland climate — we don't shortcut it.
