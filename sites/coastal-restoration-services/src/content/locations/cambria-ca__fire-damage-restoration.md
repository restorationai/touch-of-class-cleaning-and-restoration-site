## Why Cambria Properties Face Distinct Fire Damage Challenges

Cambria's housing stock skews older and more eclectic than most Central Coast communities. The East Village and West Village areas are dense with wood-frame cottages, many built in the 1950s through 1970s, that feature tongue-and-groove pine ceilings, cedar siding, and open-beam construction — materials that absorb smoke and soot at a far higher rate than modern drywall and fiber-cement exteriors. Char and smoke residue wick into exposed wood grain quickly, and if cleaning is delayed even by a few days, the acidic compounds in smoke begin permanently etching surfaces and corroding metal fixtures.

The coastal humidity that defines Cambria's climate adds a second layer of complexity. Smoke particles are hygroscopic — they attract moisture — and in a marine environment where relative humidity routinely climbs above 80 percent overnight, smoke-damaged materials can begin to support secondary mold growth within 48 to 72 hours of the fire event. That means fire cleanup here isn't a single-phase project; it requires moisture monitoring alongside soot removal from the first day on site.

San Luis Obispo County's wildland-urban interface designation for much of the Cambria area also means that structure fires can coincide with active vegetation fires nearby, complicating access and requiring coordination with Cal Fire and the Cambria Community Services District before restoration crews can safely enter.

## Our Fire Damage Restoration Process in Cambria

Every fire loss is different, but the sequence matters. When a crew from Coastal Restoration Services Inc arrives at a Cambria property, the first priority is a safety and structural assessment — confirming that the roof deck, load-bearing walls, and floor joists are stable enough to work in. In older cottages common to the East Village, this step occasionally reveals fire damage that has traveled through wall cavities or attic spaces far beyond the visible burn zone.

Once the structure is cleared, the process moves through these stages:

- **Emergency board-up and tarping** to secure openings and protect exposed framing from the marine air
- **Dry soot and debris removal** using HEPA-filtered vacuums and dry-chemical sponges before any wet cleaning begins — applying water to dry soot spreads it and drives it deeper into porous surfaces
- **Structural cleaning and deodorization**, including thermal fogging and hydroxyl treatment for smoke odor embedded in wood framing and insulation
- **Moisture mapping** to catch any water introduced by fire suppression efforts, which in Cambria's humid climate can accelerate mold colonization
- **Controlled demolition and rebuild coordination** for materials that cannot be restored — we document everything for your insurance claim before anything is removed

Throughout the project, we photograph and inventory affected materials to support your adjuster's scope of loss.

## Local Note

Cambria's older wood-frame cottages — particularly those in the East Village built before 1975 — frequently have uninsulated attic spaces with open-rafter construction. Smoke from a kitchen or utility-room fire rises and pools in these attics, saturating raw wood that was never sealed or painted. Standard odor treatments applied only to the living space below will not eliminate the smell; the attic cavity needs to be treated independently, and in some cases the rafters require encapsulation with an odor-blocking primer before drywall or tongue-and-groove replacement goes back up. It's a step that gets skipped when crews aren't familiar with this building type, and homeowners notice the smoke smell returning months later.
