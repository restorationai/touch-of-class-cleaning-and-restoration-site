## Our Fire Damage Restoration Process in Edna

Every fire loss is different, but the sequence matters. The first step on any Edna job is a thorough structural assessment — documenting which materials are salvageable and which need to come out. Smoke travels through HVAC systems, wall penetrations, and attic spaces long before it becomes visible, so the inspection goes well beyond the obvious burn zone.

Once the scope is clear, the process moves through these stages:

- **Emergency board-up and tarping** to secure the structure against weather and unauthorized entry while the full restoration plan is developed.
- **Controlled demolition** of unsalvageable materials, including charred framing, melted insulation, and smoke-saturated drywall.
- **Dry ice blasting or chemical sponge cleaning** of structural surfaces, depending on material type and the depth of soot penetration.
- **Thermal fogging and hydroxyl generation** to neutralize odor compounds embedded in framing, subfloor, and HVAC ductwork — not just mask them.
- **Reconstruction** coordinated with local permitting requirements in San Luis Obispo County, so rebuilt sections meet current code rather than simply matching what was there before.

Documentation runs through every phase. Photos, moisture readings, and material inventories are compiled into a loss report formatted for insurance carrier review.

## Local Note: Smoke Behavior in Edna Valley's Agricultural Structures

One pattern that comes up repeatedly in this part of San Luis Obispo County: fires that originate in or spread to agricultural outbuildings — hay storage, equipment sheds, older barns — produce a specific category of smoke residue that differs from a typical house fire. Burning agricultural materials, treated lumber, and stored chemicals generate protein-based and chemical smoke that requires different cleaning chemistry than standard dry or wet smoke. Crews who haven't worked rural San Luis Obispo County properties sometimes treat these structures with the same approach as a suburban kitchen fire and end up with persistent odor months later.
