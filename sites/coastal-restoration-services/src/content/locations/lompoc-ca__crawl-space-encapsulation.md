Lompoc sits in a coastal valley where marine air from the Pacific funnels inland through the Santa Ynez River corridor, keeping relative humidity elevated even on sunny afternoons. That persistent moisture finds its way into unprotected crawl spaces, where bare soil and inadequate venting create the exact conditions that accelerate wood rot, attract termites, and allow mold to colonize floor joists within weeks of a wet season. Crawl space encapsulation seals that environment off permanently — and in Lompoc's climate, it's less a luxury upgrade than a long-term structural investment.

## Why Lompoc Properties Are Especially Vulnerable to Crawl Space Moisture

The Santa Ynez Valley's geography means Lompoc receives coastal fog intrusion well into late morning for much of the year. Underneath homes, that translates to ground-level relative humidity that can hover above 70% even when the surface soil looks dry. Older homes throughout the city — many built in the postwar boom years when vapor barriers weren't standard practice — sit over bare dirt crawl spaces with minimal cross-ventilation. Ground moisture evaporates upward, condenses on cold floor framing, and slowly degrades the wood from below.

Beyond humidity, Lompoc's soil composition in lower-lying areas near the river plain tends toward clay-heavy profiles that retain water long after rain events. Clay soil doesn't drain quickly, so crawl spaces in those zones stay damp for weeks after a storm. Homeowners often don't notice the problem until they smell mustiness through the subfloor or feel soft spots in hardwood floors — both signs that moisture has already been working on the structure for months.

## Our Crawl Space Encapsulation Process in Lompoc

Every encapsulation project starts with a thorough inspection of the crawl space — measuring moisture levels in the framing, checking existing vents, and identifying any standing water or active drainage issues. In Lompoc, that inspection step often turns up deteriorated fiberglass batt insulation hanging between joists, which holds moisture like a sponge and needs to be removed before any barrier goes down.

Once the space is cleared and dried, we install a heavy-duty polyethylene vapor barrier — typically 20-mil reinforced material — across the entire ground surface and up the foundation walls, sealed at seams and penetrations with moisture-resistant tape. Depending on the crawl space's natural airflow, we may also recommend closing off existing foundation vents and adding a mechanical dehumidifier to maintain the sealed environment year-round. In Lompoc's climate, a sealed and conditioned crawl space consistently outperforms a vented one because the marine air coming through open vents introduces more humidity than it removes during the cooler months.

The process typically takes one to two days for a standard single-family home, and the difference in subfloor humidity is measurable within the first week.

## Equipment and Methods We Use for Crawl Space Encapsulation

The barrier material matters more than most homeowners realize. Thin 6-mil sheeting — still sold at big-box stores — punctures easily during routine crawl space access and degrades within a few years under the alkaline conditions common in Central Coast soils. The reinforced 20-mil liner we use is rated for foot traffic and resists tearing at penetration points like plumbing cleanouts and HVAC supports.

For homes where existing moisture has already reached the framing, we bring in commercial-grade desiccant dehumidifiers and air movers to dry the structural wood to below 19% moisture content before sealing — the threshold at which wood-destroying fungi cannot sustain growth. We measure with calibrated pin-type moisture meters at multiple joist locations, not just at the access hatch, so the reading reflects conditions throughout the space rather than just the easiest spot to reach.
