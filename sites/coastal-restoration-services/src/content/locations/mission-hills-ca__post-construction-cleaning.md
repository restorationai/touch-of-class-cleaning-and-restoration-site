Mission Hills sits in the northern San Fernando Valley, where construction activity has been steady — from kitchen gut-renovations in mid-century ranch homes to full ADU builds in backyards that back up against the 118 Freeway corridor. When that work wraps, what's left behind isn't just sawdust and scrap lumber. It's a fine silica-laden construction dust that settles into every horizontal surface, HVAC register, and cabinet hinge — and in the dry, warm climate of this part of Los Angeles County, that dust doesn't stay put. It migrates. A final clean here isn't a quick sweep; it's a methodical, multi-phase process that returns a newly built or renovated space to genuinely livable condition.

## Why Mission Hills Properties Require a Different Approach to Post-Construction Cleanup

The housing stock in Mission Hills skews heavily toward homes built between the 1950s and 1970s — single-story and split-level ranches with original hardwood floors, plaster-over-lath walls, and older ductwork that was never designed to filter construction particulate. When a contractor opens up walls or sands drywall compound in one of these homes, the dust doesn't stay in the work zone. It travels through gaps in original door frames, settles into textured acoustic ceilings, and coats the inside of return-air ducts that may not have been cleaned in decades.

That combination — older building materials plus the region's low humidity and warm temperatures — means fine dust dries and bonds to surfaces faster than it would in a coastal or higher-elevation environment. Waiting even a few days after construction wraps before scheduling the final clean can make the job measurably harder. Surfaces that could have been wiped clean in one pass require light agitation and repeat cleaning when dust has had time to cure onto painted trim or cabinet interiors.

## Our Post-Construction and Specialty Cleaning Process in Mission Hills

Every post-construction clean begins with a walkthrough to identify what trades were on-site, what materials were used, and what the general contractor has already removed. From there, the process moves in a deliberate sequence — because cleaning out of order means re-contaminating surfaces you've already finished.

**Phase 1 — Rough debris removal.** Any remaining construction material, packaging, tape, and visible debris is bagged and removed before any surface cleaning begins. This keeps abrasive particles from being dragged across finished floors.

**Phase 2 — Top-down dry cleaning.** Starting at ceiling level and working down, we dry-wipe light fixtures, ceiling fans, window sills, door tops, and cabinet exteriors. HEPA-filtered vacuums handle textured surfaces and registers where dust has packed in.

**Phase 3 — Detail and specialty surfaces.** This is where post-construction cleaning diverges from a standard deep clean. Grout lines in new tile work, the inside of new cabinetry, window tracks, and hardware all get individual attention. Paint overspray on glass is removed with appropriate tools that won't scratch the surface.

**Phase 4 — Final wet clean and floor finish.** Hard floors are cleaned based on material — new hardwood gets a different treatment than freshly grouted tile. We check for grout haze, adhesive residue, and any protective film that contractors leave on surfaces and forget to remove.

**Phase 5 — Verification pass.** A second walkthrough under strong lighting catches anything the first pass missed. On larger renovation projects, this step often surfaces a handful of overlooked areas — the inside of a new medicine cabinet, the top of a refrigerator that was moved back into place before cleaning finished.

## Specialty Cleaning Scenarios Common in Mission Hills Renovations

Beyond standard post-construction final cleans, the renovation patterns in Mission Hills generate a few recurring specialty cleaning needs worth naming directly.

**Smoke and odor remediation after kitchen remodels.** Older homes in this area often have original range hoods that vented into wall cavities rather than to the exterior. When contractors open those walls, decades of grease-laden residue can be disturbed and redistributed. A standard post-construction clean won't address that — it requires targeted degreasing and odor neutralization.

**HVAC and duct cleaning coordination.** Because the ductwork in many Mission Hills homes predates modern filtration standards, we strongly recommend coordinating a duct cleaning alongside any post-construction final clean that involved significant drywall work. Construction dust that enters the system will recirculate for months if it isn't removed.

**Pressure washing and exterior cleanup.** ADU and addition projects leave concrete splatter, stucco overspray, and paint on driveways and exterior hardscape. We handle exterior post-construction cleanup as part of a complete scope or as a standalone service.

One pattern that comes up repeatedly in Mission Hills is the combination of original plaster walls and newer drywall additions in the same home — a common result of phased renovations over the decades.
