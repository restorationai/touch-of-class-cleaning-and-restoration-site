## Why Los Osos Properties See Persistent Odor Problems

The San Luis Obispo County coast doesn't dry out the way inland California does. Los Osos regularly experiences overnight relative humidity in the 80–90% range, and homes here — many of them built in the 1960s and 1970s on the Los Osos Valley floor — were constructed with wood-framed walls and minimal vapor barriers that were never designed for sustained coastal moisture exposure. Over decades, that wood absorbs odor-causing compounds from smoke, pets, mold growth, and sewage events, and the damp air keeps those compounds active and volatile.

Fire and smoke odors are especially stubborn here. When smoke particles settle into redwood siding, cedar closet interiors, or older fiberglass insulation batts, the cool, humid air slows the off-gassing process — which means the smell lingers weeks longer than it would in a drier climate. The same principle applies to biohazard and sewage odors: the coastal microclimate creates near-ideal conditions for odor-producing bacteria to remain active long after the visible mess has been cleaned.

## Our Odor Removal and Deodorization Process in Los Osos

Effective odor elimination in this environment requires more than one method, because different odor sources respond to different chemistry. The process typically moves through several stages calibrated to what the property has been through.

The first step is source removal — any saturated material that cannot be deodorized must come out. Drywall, insulation, or subflooring that has absorbed sewage or heavy smoke residue will re-emit odors indefinitely if left in place. Once the source is addressed, we move to **thermal fogging**, which uses a petroleum-based or water-based deodorizing solvent heated into a fine fog that penetrates the same pathways smoke traveled — into wall cavities, under cabinets, into HVAC ductwork. Because the fog mimics smoke's behavior, it reaches surfaces that sprays and wipes cannot.

For ongoing or biological odors — pet urine, mold, decomposition — **hydroxyl generator treatment** is often the right tool. Hydroxyl radicals break down odor molecules at the molecular level without requiring occupants to vacate for extended periods, which matters in a tight-knit community like Los Osos where families may have limited temporary housing options. For severe smoke cases in unoccupied spaces, **ozone treatment** is deployed: ozone is a powerful oxidizer that destroys smoke and mold odors at the source, but the space must remain unoccupied during treatment and properly ventilated afterward. Our IICRC-certified team determines which combination of methods fits the specific loss.

After treatment, we verify results with calibrated air sampling and a structured walkthrough rather than a subjective sniff test, so there's a documented record of the outcome.

## Equipment & Methods We Use for Odor Removal

The equipment mix matters as much as the chemistry. For Los Osos jobs, the coastal humidity means we often run **commercial-grade desiccant or refrigerant dehumidifiers in parallel with deodorization equipment** — if ambient moisture stays high during ozone or hydroxyl treatment, the process takes significantly longer and the results are less stable. Drying the air first creates the conditions where deodorization actually works.

We also use **air scrubbers with activated carbon filtration** in occupied or partially occupied spaces. These units pull airborne odor compounds and particulates out of circulation continuously, which is especially useful during the days following a smoke or sewage event when off-gassing from structural materials is at its peak.

For HVAC systems — a common odor distribution pathway in homes throughout Los Osos — we treat supply and return ductwork directly rather than relying on whole-room fogging to penetrate the system.
