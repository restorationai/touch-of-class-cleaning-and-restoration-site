## Why Santa Maria Properties Face Unique Board-Up and Tarping Challenges

Santa Maria's building stock spans a wide range of eras. Older homes throughout the city — many built during the post-WWII agricultural boom when the region's strawberry and broccoli fields drew a surge of new residents — often feature original wood-framed windows with single-pane glass and aging exterior trim that shatters or splinters more extensively in a fire or impact event. That means board-up work here frequently involves irregular opening sizes that don't conform to standard sheet dimensions, requiring on-site cuts and custom framing to achieve a secure, weather-tight seal.

The region's wind patterns add another layer of complexity. Diablo winds — the dry, offshore gusts that push through the Santa Ynez Mountains and accelerate across the valley floor — can arrive with little warning and lift an improperly secured tarp off a damaged roof in minutes. A tarp that fails at 2 a.m. is a tarp that lets rain or dew into exposed rafters all night. Proper ballasting, ridge-cap anchoring, and mechanical fastening (not just sandbags) are non-negotiable on Santa Maria roofs.

## Our Emergency Board-Up and Tarping Process in Santa Maria

For roof tarping, we use minimum 6-mil reinforced polyethylene sheeting, run it over the ridge and down both sides of the damaged section, and anchor it with 2×4 cap boards screwed through the tarp into the decking — a method that holds under sustained wind rather than just dead weight. For window and door board-up, we use 5/8-inch plywood panels secured with structural screws into the surrounding framing, not just the window casing, so the panel can't be pushed inward.

Photographic documentation happens throughout — wide shots, close-ups of fastener placement, and images of the damage condition before we touch anything. Those photos matter when your insurance adjuster needs to establish the scope of the original loss versus what happened after the fact.
