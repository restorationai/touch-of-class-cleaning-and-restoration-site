## Why Avila Beach Properties Are Especially Vulnerable After Structural Damage

The housing stock along this stretch of the Central Coast tends toward older wood-frame construction — beach cottages, bungalows, and small multi-unit buildings that were built before modern moisture-resistant sheathing became standard. Many of these structures have cedar or redwood siding that looks solid until a breach lets in standing water, at which point the wood can begin to delaminate within days rather than weeks. The marine layer that rolls in off the Pacific most mornings keeps relative humidity elevated even when it isn't raining, which means an unprotected opening doesn't need a rainstorm to cause secondary damage — overnight condensation alone can soak exposed insulation and subfloor material.

Fire damage compounds this problem in a specific way along the coast: smoke residue absorbs moisture from the marine air, and an unboarded structure after a fire can develop a secondary water intrusion problem before the fire investigation is even complete. Getting plywood over openings and heavy-duty poly tarps over compromised roofing isn't just a security measure here — it's active moisture management.

## Our Board-Up and Tarping Process in Avila Beach

When we arrive at a property in Avila Beach, the first step is a rapid exterior assessment to identify every breach — broken windows, kicked-in doors, missing roof sections, and any soffit or fascia damage that could funnel water into the wall cavity. We photograph and document each opening before we cover it, which matters later when you're working through an insurance claim.

For window and door openings, we use minimum ½-inch CDX plywood cut to overlap the frame, secured with structural screws rather than nails — nails back out in coastal humidity cycles. Edges are sealed with weatherproof tape to block wind-driven moisture. For roof damage, we use reinforced polyethylene tarps rated for UV exposure and wind load, anchored with lumber battens and screwed into solid decking rather than draped loosely over the ridge. A tarp that lifts in a Pacific gust does nothing. We weight and anchor every edge.

If there is any indication of structural compromise — a rafter that shifted, a load-bearing wall that took impact — we flag it clearly in our documentation and recommend an engineer review before interior access.
