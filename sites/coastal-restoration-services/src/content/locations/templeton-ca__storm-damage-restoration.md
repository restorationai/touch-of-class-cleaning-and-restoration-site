## Why Templeton Properties See Storm Damage Differently

The geology and climate of this stretch of SLO County create a specific storm damage profile. Templeton's hillside parcels — many of them on expansive clay soils — are prone to rapid surface runoff when storms arrive faster than the ground can absorb. That runoff doesn't just pool in yards; it migrates under slab foundations and into crawl spaces, wicking upward into subfloor framing within hours. The region also sits in a wind corridor that accelerates through the valley during strong low-pressure systems, meaning tree strikes and partial roof failures are common even in storms that don't make regional news.

The local housing stock adds another layer of complexity. Templeton has a mix of 1970s and 1980s ranch-style homes with wood-shake or aging composition roofing, alongside newer construction on the rural edges of town. Older shake roofs can shed shingles or split along the ridge in high winds, and once the roof deck is exposed, interior damage from a single night of rain can be extensive — soaked insulation, stained drywall, and the beginning of mold colonization within 24 to 48 hours if materials aren't dried promptly.

## Our Storm Damage Restoration Process in Templeton

When we arrive at a Templeton property after a storm event, the first priority is stabilization — not paperwork. That means tarping any open roof sections, boarding compromised windows or doors, and identifying where water has already entered the structure. We use thermal imaging cameras to trace moisture migration behind walls and under flooring, which matters here because Templeton's clay-heavy soils hold water and can continue feeding moisture into a foundation long after the storm has passed.

Once the structure is stabilized and moisture mapping is complete, we move into extraction and drying. Industrial air movers and desiccant dehumidifiers are positioned based on the moisture readings, not just placed generically in wet rooms. We monitor drying progress with daily moisture readings and adjust equipment placement as materials dry unevenly — a process that typically runs three to five days depending on the extent of saturation. Debris removal, structural assessment, and coordination with roofing and framing contractors follow once the structure is confirmed dry.
