## Why Pismo Beach Properties See Mold Issues

The same ocean air that makes Pismo Beach one of the most livable spots on the Central Coast is also its biggest mold driver. Relative humidity along this coastline regularly climbs above 80 percent overnight, even in summer. That ambient moisture seeps into wall cavities through micro-gaps in older stucco, around aging window frames, and through unventilated crawl spaces that sit close to grade — a common feature in the beach cottages and bungalow-style homes scattered throughout the city.

Many properties here were built in the 1950s through the 1970s, when vapor barriers in crawl spaces were either minimal or nonexistent. Fiberglass batt insulation in those spaces absorbs coastal humidity like a sponge, and once mold colonizes the paper facing on that insulation, it spreads quietly for months before a homeowner notices a musty smell or visible growth on a wall. Stucco exteriors — standard on most Pismo Beach homes — can trap moisture behind the finish coat when the weep screed at the base becomes blocked by soil or hardscape, creating a hidden reservoir against the sheathing.

Flood events matter here too. King tides and storm surge can push water into ground-level garages and crawl spaces along the lower-elevation streets near the waterfront. Even a few inches of intrusion that dries improperly sets the stage for mold growth behind baseboards and under flooring within days.

## Our Mold Remediation Process in Pismo Beach

Every job starts with a thorough visual inspection and moisture mapping using thermal imaging and calibrated moisture meters. This matters especially in Pismo Beach because the source of moisture is rarely just one thing — it's often a combination of a slow leak and chronic ambient humidity, and addressing only one means the mold comes back.

Once the moisture source is identified and controlled, the affected area is isolated with negative-air containment barriers. HEPA-filtered air scrubbers run continuously throughout the work area, capturing airborne spores before they can migrate to unaffected rooms. Contaminated materials — drywall, insulation, wood framing — are removed, double-bagged, and disposed of per California Department of Public Health guidelines.

After physical removal, affected structural surfaces are cleaned and treated. Drying equipment — commercial dehumidifiers and high-velocity air movers — runs until moisture readings in wood and drywall return to acceptable baselines. In Pismo Beach's coastal climate, that drying phase often runs longer than it would in an inland environment because the outdoor air being drawn in carries its own moisture load. We account for that in how we set up the drying system, using closed-drying configurations when outdoor dew points are high.

Final clearance involves a post-remediation visual inspection and, where warranted, air sampling coordinated with an independent industrial hygienist — particularly important for larger jobs or any property where occupants have respiratory sensitivities.
