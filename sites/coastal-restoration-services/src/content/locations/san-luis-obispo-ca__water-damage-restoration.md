## Why San Luis Obispo Properties See Water Damage Issues

The city's housing stock tells the story. Blocks near the historic downtown core include bungalows and craftsman-style homes built in the 1920s through 1950s, many with original plaster-and-lath wall systems, redwood subfloors, and pier-and-beam foundations that leave crawlspaces exposed to ground moisture year-round. Those materials absorb water differently than modern OSB and drywall — saturation happens fast, but the release is slow and uneven.

SLO's Mediterranean climate adds a counterintuitive wrinkle. The dry summers give homeowners a false sense of security about plumbing and roofing systems that have been baking and contracting for months. Then the first significant atmospheric river of the season — often arriving in October or November before anyone has thought about gutters or sump pumps — hits those same systems hard. Cracked supply lines, overwhelmed French drains, and roof penetrations that looked fine in August are common culprits behind the water damage calls we receive every fall.

The hillside neighborhoods above town present their own challenge. Slopes accelerate runoff, and homes built into cut-and-fill lots can experience water intrusion through foundation walls even when no plumbing has failed at all — it's simply the hillside draining through the concrete.

## Our Water Damage Restoration Process in San Luis Obispo

Every job starts with a thorough moisture assessment using thermal imaging cameras and calibrated moisture meters. We map the full extent of saturation before a single piece of equipment is placed — this matters especially in older SLO homes where water can wick laterally through plaster or travel along redwood framing members well beyond the visible damage zone.

From there, the process moves through four concrete phases:

**Water extraction** — truck-mounted and portable extractors pull standing water from flooring, subfloor assemblies, and crawlspaces. For pier-and-beam homes, we access the crawlspace directly and extract pooled water before it saturates the sill plates.

**Structural drying** — commercial-grade desiccant and refrigerant dehumidifiers, paired with high-velocity air movers, are positioned according to a drying plan calibrated to the room's square footage, material types, and ambient humidity readings. In SLO's coastal air, we typically run dehumidification longer than inland jobs of comparable size.

**Monitoring** — we return on a documented schedule to log moisture readings at every measurement point. Drying is not complete until readings return to regional equilibrium — not just until the floor feels dry underfoot.

**Documentation** — every reading, every equipment placement, and every photo is logged in a format compatible with most major insurance carriers' claim requirements.
