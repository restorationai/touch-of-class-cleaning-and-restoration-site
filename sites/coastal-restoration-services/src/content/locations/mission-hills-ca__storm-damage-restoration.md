## Why Mission Hills Properties Are Particularly Vulnerable to Storm Damage

The housing stock across Mission Hills skews heavily toward mid-century construction — ranch-style homes and low-slope stucco buildings put up between the 1950s and 1970s. Those flat and near-flat roofs were never designed with the kind of drainage margins that modern building codes require. When debris clogs the scuppers and water ponds even a few inches, it finds every aged seam and crack. Once it gets through the roof assembly, it travels fast through the interior framing because older homes in this part of the Valley often lack the moisture barriers that newer construction takes for granted.

The soil profile compounds the problem. Much of Mission Hills sits on expansive clay that becomes saturated quickly during heavy rain, reducing its ability to absorb additional runoff. That saturated soil pushes water toward foundations and through slab cracks, and it destabilizes root systems — which is why tree-on-structure damage is a recurring pattern here after any storm with sustained winds above 40 mph. The combination of aging rooflines, clay soil, and mature landscaping creates a damage profile that differs meaningfully from coastal communities to the south or the newer tract developments further into the Valley.

## Our Storm Damage Restoration Process in Mission Hills

The first priority on any storm call is stopping the water pathway. Our crew begins with an exterior assessment — documenting roof breaches, broken windows, and compromised siding — and deploys emergency tarping or board-up immediately so rain can't continue entering while interior work is underway. We photograph every affected surface before touching anything, which matters enormously when an insurance adjuster reviews the claim later.

Once the structure is secured, we move to moisture mapping. Industrial moisture meters and thermal imaging cameras reveal where water has traveled inside wall cavities and beneath flooring — damage that isn't visible to the eye but will produce mold colonization within 24 to 72 hours if left unaddressed. We extract standing water, set commercial-grade desiccant dehumidifiers and high-velocity air movers calibrated to the square footage and material types present, and monitor drying progress on a documented schedule. Structural repairs — framing, drywall, roofing, stucco patching — follow once materials reach target moisture levels, so we're not sealing wet assemblies.
