## Why Templeton Properties Are Especially Vulnerable After a Loss

Templeton's housing stock skews older and more rural than the coastal cities to the west. Many homes along the Highway 101 corridor and out toward the rolling ranch parcels east of town were built in the 1960s through 1980s with wood-frame construction, single-pane windows, and rooflines that weren't engineered for the kind of debris loading a fast-moving wind event produces. When a fire, vehicle impact, or windstorm compromises one of those structures, the openings left behind are large and irregular — not the standard 36-inch doorframe a box of pre-cut plywood handles easily.

The region also sits in a fire-prone interface zone. Cal Fire's San Luis Obispo Unit responds to vegetation fires in this area regularly during the late-summer and fall dry season, and even a fire that doesn't consume a structure can shatter windows from radiant heat, melt vent screens, and leave roof assemblies partially exposed. Board-up after a fire isn't just about keeping rain out — it's about keeping smoke odor from re-entering through open cavities and preventing further contamination of interior surfaces that still need professional cleaning.

## Our Board-Up and Tarping Process in Templeton

Every job starts with a rapid exterior assessment — we walk the full perimeter before touching anything, documenting every breach with photographs that become part of your insurance claim file. For broken windows and door openings, we use 3/4-inch CDX plywood cut to fit and secured with structural screws rather than nails, which holds better in wind and is easier for contractors to remove cleanly during the repair phase. Frames are protected with foam backer tape so the plywood doesn't grind against existing trim.

For roof damage, we use reinforced polyethylene tarps rated for UV exposure and secured with 2x4 battens screwed through the tarp and into the decking — not bungee cords or sandbags, which fail in the first real wind gust. On steeper-pitch roofs common to older Templeton ranch-style homes, we use fall-protection anchoring before anyone steps onto the surface. The tarp installation is documented with before-and-after photos and GPS-tagged, which most insurance adjusters now expect as part of a properly submitted emergency services claim.
