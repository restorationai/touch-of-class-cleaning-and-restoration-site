## Why Mission Hills Properties Face Elevated Board-Up and Tarping Needs

The housing stock across Mission Hills skews heavily toward mid-century construction — ranch-style homes and Spanish-revival stucco built from the 1950s through the 1970s. That era of construction typically used single-pane jalousie or aluminum-frame windows that are more fragile under pressure and more expensive to source as replacements today. When one of those windows breaks during a fire or a forced entry, the opening is often irregular in shape, which means standard plywood panels require custom cutting on-site rather than off-the-shelf sizing.

The Santa Ana wind events that roll through the San Fernando Valley each fall and winter add another layer of urgency. Roof tiles on older Mission Hills homes can dislodge under sustained gusts, leaving underlayment exposed to whatever weather follows. A tarp applied correctly — anchored with weighted battens rather than just stapled at the edges — can hold through a second wind event. One applied carelessly will peel back and allow water into the attic cavity, where it will reach insulation and framing within hours.

Fire damage board-up is a particular concern here because the hillside streets adjacent to Mission Hills see brush fire activity during dry seasons. A structure that has sustained fire damage to a wall or soffit needs boarding that accounts for the structural integrity of the surrounding framing — you cannot anchor plywood to charred wood and expect it to hold.

## Our Emergency Board-Up and Tarping Process in Mission Hills

When we arrive at a Mission Hills property, the first step is a rapid structural assessment before any material goes up. We check that anchor points for plywood or OSB panels are load-bearing, not decorative trim or compromised framing. For broken window board-up, we measure each opening individually, cut panels to fit flush against the exterior surface, and secure them with through-bolts where the wall construction allows — not just surface screws that can be pried out.

For roof tarping, we use 6-mil or heavier polyethylene sheeting depending on the size of the exposed area, weighted and secured at the ridge and eaves. On the low-slope or flat roof sections common on some Mission Hills ranch homes, we pay particular attention to drainage paths so the tarp does not pool water and add load to an already-stressed roof deck.

Every opening we secure gets photographed before and after — wide shots for context, close-ups for the insurance adjuster. That documentation package goes to the property owner the same day.
