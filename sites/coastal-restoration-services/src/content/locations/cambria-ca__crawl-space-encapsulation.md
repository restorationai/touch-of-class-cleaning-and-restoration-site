Cambria sits on a narrow coastal shelf where the Santa Lucia Range meets the Pacific, and that geography creates a crawl space environment unlike almost anywhere else on the Central Coast. Marine fog rolls in most mornings, humidity rarely drops below 60 percent even in summer, and the sandy-loam soils beneath many homes hold moisture long after a rain. Without a proper vapor barrier and sealed encapsulation system, that persistent ground moisture migrates upward into floor joists, insulation, and subfloor sheathing — quietly rotting wood and feeding mold colonies before a homeowner ever notices a soft spot underfoot. If you've been putting off crawl space work on your Cambria property, the coastal climate is actively working against you.

## Why Cambria Properties Are Especially Vulnerable to Crawl Space Moisture

Cambria's building stock skews older. A significant share of homes in town were built in the 1950s through 1970s, when crawl space ventilation was the standard approach — the idea being that cross-ventilation would carry moisture away. On an inland site that might work reasonably well. On the Central Coast, where prevailing onshore winds push humid marine air directly into those vents, ventilation can actually worsen conditions. Crawl spaces that were "code-compliant" decades ago are now chronically damp by modern standards.

The town's proximity to Moonstone Beach and the marine terrace geology also means many lots have minimal slope away from the foundation. Rainwater — even the modest amounts San Luis Obispo County receives — can pool against stem walls and seep under the home rather than draining away. Combine that with the clay-heavy subsoils found in parts of Cambria, which drain slowly, and you have the conditions for persistent ground moisture year-round.

## Our Crawl Space Encapsulation Process in Cambria

Encapsulation in a coastal environment requires a few specific calibrations compared to inland work. Here's how a typical project unfolds:

**Assessment and moisture mapping.** Before any material goes in, we measure wood moisture content in the joists and subfloor, check for active mold or pest damage, and document standing water or efflorescence on the stem walls. In Cambria homes, we pay particular attention to the north-facing crawl space quadrants, which see the least sun and tend to stay wettest.

**Debris removal and prep.** Old, deteriorated fiberglass batt insulation — common in homes built before the 1980s — gets removed. Soggy insulation is not insulation; it's a moisture reservoir and a mold substrate.

**Liner installation.** We install a reinforced polyethylene vapor barrier across the entire ground surface, running it up the stem walls and sealing it to the foundation with adhesive and mechanical fasteners. For Cambria properties where ground moisture readings are elevated, we use a 20-mil liner rather than the minimum 6-mil product — the thicker material resists puncture during future access and provides meaningfully better vapor resistance over the long term.

**Sealing vents and penetrations.** In a true encapsulation, foundation vents are closed and sealed. All pipe and beam penetrations through the liner are taped and sealed to eliminate bypass pathways.

**Dehumidification.** A crawl space dehumidifier sized to the square footage of the space is installed and plumbed to drain automatically. This is the active component that keeps relative humidity in the crawl space below the 55 percent threshold where mold growth stalls.

**Final documentation.** We photograph the completed installation and provide a written report — useful if you ever sell the property or need to demonstrate the work to an insurer.
