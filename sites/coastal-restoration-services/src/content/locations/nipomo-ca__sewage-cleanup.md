## Why Nipomo Properties Experience Sewage Backup Issues

Nipomo's development pattern is a patchwork of older ranch homes on private septic systems, newer subdivisions tied to the South San Luis Obispo County Sanitation District, and rural parcels where lateral lines can run 50 feet or more before reaching a tank or main. That variety creates several distinct failure points.

Private septic systems in the area are particularly vulnerable during the wet season — typically November through March — when the water table rises and drain fields lose their absorption capacity. A system that functions fine in August can back up into a home's lowest fixtures by January. Grease buildup, root intrusion from the mature eucalyptus and oak trees common on older Nipomo lots, and aging clay-tile laterals all compound the risk. In newer tract developments, shared sewer mains can experience surcharge events when heavy rain overwhelms the system, pushing raw sewage back through floor drains and toilets.

## Our Sewage Cleanup and Sanitization Process

Raw sewage is classified as Category 3 water — the most contaminated type — which means the work follows a stricter protocol than a clean-water pipe burst. Here's how a typical job unfolds:

**Containment first.** Affected areas are isolated with plastic sheeting and negative air pressure where the layout allows, preventing cross-contamination to unaffected living spaces.

**Extraction and removal.** Standing sewage is extracted with truck-mounted or portable units. Porous materials — drywall below the flood line, saturated insulation, carpet and pad — are bagged, documented, and removed. In Nipomo crawl spaces, which are common in the area's ranch-style homes, this often means removing contaminated vapor barrier and treating the soil surface directly.

**Disinfection.** Hard surfaces, framing, and concrete are treated with EPA-registered antimicrobials rated for Category 3 contamination. This step is not optional or cosmetic — sewage carries pathogens including E. coli, hepatitis A, and norovirus that persist on surfaces after the water is gone.

**Drying and monitoring.** After sanitization, structural drying begins using commercial dehumidifiers and air movers. Moisture readings are logged daily until materials reach target levels, creating a documented drying record that matters for insurance claims.

**Final inspection.** Before closing out the job, surfaces are re-tested and the work area is cleared for re-occupancy.
