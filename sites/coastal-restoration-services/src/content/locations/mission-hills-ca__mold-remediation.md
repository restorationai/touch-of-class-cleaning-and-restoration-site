## Why Mission Hills Properties See Mold Issues

The housing stock in Mission Hills skews older — a significant share of homes were built between the 1950s and 1970s, when vapor barriers were minimal or nonexistent and bathroom ventilation was often a single small exhaust fan venting into the attic rather than outside. That design pattern means moisture generated by daily showers or cooking has been accumulating in wall cavities and attic sheathing for decades. When a slow supply-line leak or a roof intrusion adds liquid water to an already-humid cavity, mold can begin colonizing porous materials — drywall paper, wood framing, insulation — within 24 to 48 hours.

The region also sits in a seismically active corridor. Small cracks in stucco exteriors and around window frames — the kind that appear gradually after minor ground movement — let coastal fog and seasonal rain wick into wall assemblies. Because the damage is incremental and invisible from the street, many Mission Hills homeowners don't discover it until they're renovating a bathroom or replacing flooring.

## Our Mold Remediation Process in Mission Hills

Every job starts with a thorough visual inspection and moisture mapping using thermal imaging and calibrated moisture meters. We're looking for the moisture source first — because remediating mold without fixing the underlying water intrusion is a short-term fix that will fail. Once the source is identified and controlled, the process moves through these stages:

**Containment** — Polyethylene sheeting and negative air pressure isolate the affected area so spores aren't redistributed to clean rooms during the work. In occupied homes, this step protects the rest of the living space.

**Removal of compromised materials** — Drywall, insulation, and wood framing that have absorbed mold beyond surface level are carefully removed and double-bagged for disposal per California Department of Public Health guidelines. We don't sand or wire-brush mold on porous substrates — that aerosolizes spores.

**HEPA vacuuming and antimicrobial treatment** — All remaining surfaces in the containment zone are HEPA-vacuumed, then treated with an EPA-registered antimicrobial agent. We allow proper dwell time before wiping — a step that's sometimes skipped when crews are rushing.

**Drying and clearance** — Commercial dehumidifiers and air movers bring structural moisture readings down to acceptable levels. We document final moisture readings before closing containment, giving you a written record that's useful for insurance claims and future property disclosures.
