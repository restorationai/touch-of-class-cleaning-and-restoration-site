## Why Vandenberg Village Properties Are Prone to Crawl Space Moisture Problems

The Central Coast marine layer is relentless from roughly May through September. Ground-level relative humidity in this area regularly climbs above 80 percent overnight, and the sandy, decomposed-granite soils common throughout the Lompoc Valley don't hold a consistent moisture barrier on their own. Older crawl spaces in this community were typically built with thin poly sheeting — or none at all — and passive foundation vents that were designed for a drier inland climate standard. When cool, humid Pacific air cycles through those vents at night and meets the warmer soil surface, condensation forms on floor joists, vapor barriers, and HVAC ductwork running through the crawl.

Homes built in the 1960s through the early 1980s — which make up a large portion of the residential stock in Vandenberg Village — frequently used untreated wood framing in the crawl space. After decades of seasonal humidity swings, that wood is often showing early-stage fungal growth or wood rot even when there has never been a plumbing leak. A full encapsulation system addresses the root cause rather than treating the symptom.

## Our Crawl Space Encapsulation Process in Vandenberg Village

Every encapsulation project starts with a thorough crawl space inspection — measuring moisture levels in the soil and wood framing, checking for standing water or drainage issues, and documenting any existing vapor barrier, insulation, or pest activity. In this area, we pay particular attention to HVAC ductwork that passes through the crawl, because condensation on uninsulated flex duct is one of the most common — and most overlooked — moisture sources in local homes.

Once the inspection is complete, the process moves through these stages:

- **Debris and old vapor barrier removal** — deteriorated poly sheeting, insulation batts that have fallen from the joists, and any organic debris are removed and disposed of properly.
- **Drainage and grading correction** — if low spots in the crawl are pooling water, we address grade or install a perimeter drain channel before sealing anything in.
- **20-mil reinforced encapsulation membrane** — a heavy-duty, puncture-resistant liner is sealed to the foundation walls and piers, eliminating ground moisture vapor at the source.
- **Rim joist insulation and air sealing** — the band joist is one of the biggest thermal and moisture bridges in a crawl space; we insulate and seal it with closed-cell spray foam or rigid board.
- **Conditioned air or dehumidification** — depending on the home's HVAC configuration, we either condition the crawl space with a supply duct or install a dedicated crawl space dehumidifier sized for the square footage.

The result is a crawl space that stays dry, reduces HVAC load, and protects the structural framing from the humidity cycles that are simply part of living on California's Central Coast.

## What Makes Encapsulation Different Here vs. Inland California

Contractors who primarily work in the Inland Empire or the Central Valley are used to crawl spaces where the main threat is summer heat and occasional rain intrusion. In Vandenberg Village, the moisture threat is year-round and comes primarily from the air, not from rain events. That changes the encapsulation design. A system that relies heavily on passive venting — which is still code-compliant in some California jurisdictions under certain conditions — will underperform here because the outside air being vented in is often more humid than the crawl space itself during summer nights.

For homes in this area, we typically recommend a sealed, conditioned crawl space rather than a vented one, and we size dehumidification equipment based on the specific square footage and soil conditions rather than using a one-size-fits-all unit.
