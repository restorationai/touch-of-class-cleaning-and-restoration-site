## Why Vandenberg Village Properties Develop Persistent Odors

The housing stock in Vandenberg Village skews toward homes built in the 1960s and 1970s, a period when builders used particleboard underlayment, cellulose insulation, and single-pane aluminum-frame windows — all materials that absorb and hold odor-causing compounds far longer than modern construction. When smoke, pet waste, or mold-generated VOCs (volatile organic compounds) work their way into particleboard subfloors, the material acts almost like a sponge; surface cleaning alone never reaches the source.

The area's proximity to Vandenberg Space Force Base also means a significant portion of local residents are renters or transitional occupants, which increases the likelihood of layered odors — pet dander from multiple tenants, tobacco residue from prior occupants sealed under fresh paint, or cleaning-product buildup masking a deeper biological source. Identifying what's actually generating the smell, rather than just treating the surface, is where professional deodorization separates itself from a can of spray.

Santa Barbara County's marine layer keeps relative humidity elevated through much of the year, particularly in the morning hours. High ambient humidity slows the off-gassing of odor compounds, which means a smell that seems mild in the afternoon can become overwhelming overnight when the air cools and moisture rises. That cycle also accelerates mold colonization after any water intrusion — mold can establish within 24 to 48 hours in conditions like these.

## Our Odor Removal and Deodorization Process in Vandenberg Village

Every job starts with a source identification walk-through. We use thermal imaging and moisture meters alongside a sensory assessment to locate the actual origin of the odor — not just where it's strongest, but where the contamination is concentrated. Treating the symptom without finding the source is the most common reason a smell returns weeks after a cleaning.

Once the source is confirmed, we select the deodorization method based on the odor type, the materials involved, and the occupancy status of the property:

- **Thermal fogging** penetrates porous surfaces — wood framing, drywall, carpet backing — by converting a deodorizing agent into a fine fog that follows the same pathways smoke or odor molecules traveled. It's particularly effective in post-fire scenarios in Vandenberg Village's older ranch-style homes, where open attic framing and interior wall cavities give smoke a wide distribution path.
- **Hydroxyl generation** runs continuously in occupied or semi-occupied spaces. Hydroxyl generators produce the same oxidizing radicals the sun creates naturally, breaking down odor molecules at the molecular level without requiring occupants to vacate for extended periods.
- **Ozone treatment** is reserved for unoccupied spaces with severe, deeply embedded odors — heavy smoke damage, long-term pet contamination, or biohazard situations. Ozone is highly effective but requires full evacuation of people, pets, and plants during treatment and a proper airing-out period before re-entry.
- **Encapsulants and sealers** are applied to structural materials when odor compounds have penetrated below the surface layer and cannot be fully removed by air treatment alone.

All drying and deodorization work is performed to IICRC standards, which matters for documentation purposes if an insurance claim is involved.
