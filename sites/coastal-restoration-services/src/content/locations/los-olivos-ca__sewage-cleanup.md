## Why Los Olivos Properties See Sewage Backup Issues

The Santa Ynez Valley's geology works against homeowners in ways that aren't obvious until something goes wrong. The region's expansive clay soils shift seasonally — contracting in the dry summer months and swelling significantly after the winter rains arrive. That movement puts lateral stress on older clay-tile and cast-iron sewer laterals, the type of pipe that still runs beneath many of the older ranch properties and wine-country estates in the area. A hairline crack that opens in November can let tree roots infiltrate by February, and by spring you have a full blockage that sends raw sewage back through floor drains, laundry connections, or the lowest toilet in the house.

Properties on private septic systems — which are common throughout unincorporated Santa Barbara County, including much of the Los Olivos area — face a different but related problem. Septic drain fields that were sized for a single-family residence in the 1970s are sometimes now serving expanded footprints or higher-occupancy uses like vacation rentals and tasting room event spaces. An overloaded or saturated drain field can push effluent back toward the tank and eventually into the structure, especially after a wet winter when the water table rises and the soil loses its absorption capacity.

## Our Sewage Cleanup and Sanitization Process in Los Olivos

Sewage is classified as Category 3 water — the most contaminated type — and the cleanup protocol reflects that. When we arrive at a Los Olivos property, the first priority is stopping ongoing contamination: confirming the source is isolated, whether that means coordinating with a plumber on the sewer lateral or having the septic system pumped before we begin extraction.

Once the source is controlled, we extract all standing effluent using truck-mounted or portable extraction equipment, then remove any porous materials — carpet, pad, drywall, insulation — that absorbed sewage and cannot be safely restored. This is not optional; porous materials that have contacted Category 3 water harbor pathogens including E. coli, hepatitis A, and norovirus, and no amount of surface drying makes them safe to leave in place.

After removal, all affected hard surfaces — concrete, tile, wood framing, wall cavities — are treated with EPA-registered disinfectants appropriate for the specific pathogens associated with sewage exposure. We then set drying equipment (air movers, desiccant or refrigerant dehumidifiers calibrated to the valley's ambient humidity) and monitor moisture readings daily until structural materials reach target dry standards. A final sanitization pass closes out the remediation phase before any reconstruction begins.
