## Our Crawl Space Encapsulation Process in Vandenberg AFB

Every encapsulation project starts with a thorough inspection of the crawl space — clearance height, existing vapor barriers, insulation condition, wood moisture content, and any signs of standing water or biological growth. In this coastal environment, we pay particular attention to the rim joist area, where cold exterior air meets warm interior framing and condensation is most likely to form.

Once the assessment is complete, we remove deteriorated insulation and any failed vapor barrier material before preparing the soil surface. We install a heavy-duty reinforced polyethylene liner — typically 20-mil — across the ground and up the foundation walls, sealing seams with specialized tape and mechanically fastening the material to the foundation perimeter. Rim joists are insulated and air-sealed with closed-cell spray foam, which also resists the vapor drive common in coastal climates. Depending on the crawl space's ventilation configuration, we may recommend transitioning from passive foundation vents to a conditioned or sealed system with a dedicated dehumidifier to actively manage relative humidity year-round. In the damp coastal air near Vandenberg AFB, passive venting often introduces more moisture than it removes.
