## Why Los Alamos Properties Face Distinct Board-Up and Tarping Needs

Los Alamos is a small, historically rooted town in northern Santa Barbara County, and the building stock reflects that. Many homes and commercial structures along Bell Street and the surrounding blocks were built in an era when roof assemblies used lighter sheathing and narrower rafter spacing than modern code requires. When fire damage scorches a rafter bay or a wind event lifts flashing, the underlying decking can be thinner and more fragile than what you'd find in a newer subdivision — which changes how tarping anchors are placed to avoid creating secondary damage.

The regional climate adds pressure. The Santa Ynez Valley experiences strong diablo-style wind events in fall and early winter that can drive embers or debris into already-compromised openings. A tarp installed without proper ballasting and perimeter securement won't survive a 40-mph gust overnight. Board-up panels on window openings need to account for the same wind loading, which means fastener depth and panel thickness matter more here than in calmer coastal microclimates closer to the water.

Wildfire is the other constant. Los Alamos is within the broader zone of Santa Barbara County that has seen repeated fire activity, and fire-damaged structures often need board-up within hours of suppression — both to meet insurance documentation requirements and to prevent unauthorized entry into structurally unstable buildings.

## Our Board-Up and Tarping Process for Los Alamos Structures

When we arrive on-site, the first step is a rapid structural assessment — identifying which openings are actively exposed to weather or entry and which load-bearing elements have been compromised. We photograph every affected area before touching anything, creating a timestamped record that supports your insurance claim.

For roof tarping, we use reinforced polyethylene tarps rated for high-wind conditions, secured with weighted edge battens and screwed perimeter boards rather than simple rope ties. On older roof assemblies common in Los Alamos, we use plywood buffer strips under fastener points to distribute load and avoid punching through deteriorated sheathing.

For broken window board-up and door openings, we cut OSB panels to fit, fasten them with structural screws into the surrounding framing (not just the window trim), and seal edges with foam backer where weather intrusion is a risk. Fire-damaged openings get a secondary assessment for smoke odor containment — an unsealed gap doesn't just let in rain, it lets smoke residue migrate into unaffected rooms.

Every job is documented with before-and-after photos and a written scope sheet you can hand directly to your adjuster.

## Local Note

Los Alamos has a number of older wood-frame commercial buildings along its main corridor that were constructed with unreinforced masonry parapets or decorative facade elements. After a fire or impact event, these parapets can be destabilized even when the main structure looks intact — a detail that isn't always obvious from street level. When we board up commercial properties in this part of town, we flag any parapet or facade instability in our documentation and recommend a structural engineer review before the building is re-entered, because a board-up that secures the windows doesn't make a loose parapet safe.
