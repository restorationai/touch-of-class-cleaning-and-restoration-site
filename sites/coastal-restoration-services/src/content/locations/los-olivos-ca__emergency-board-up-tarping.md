## Why Los Olivos Properties Face Distinct Board-Up and Tarping Risks

The Santa Ynez Valley's climate creates a specific vulnerability cycle. Summers here regularly push past 100°F, drying out wood framing and roofing materials until they become brittle. Then the Diablo and sundowner winds arrive — localized offshore gusts that can exceed 60 mph along the valley floor — and suddenly a weakened fascia board or a cracked ridge cap becomes a point of failure. After fire season, the same winds that fan flames can also strip away partially burned roofing materials overnight, turning a contained fire loss into an open-sky exposure.

The building stock in Los Olivos skews older. Many properties along Grand Avenue and the surrounding ranch parcels were built in the mid-20th century, when roofing standards and window glazing were less robust than current California Building Code requires. Older single-pane windows shatter more completely in a storm or impact event, leaving larger openings that need full plywood boarding rather than a simple patch. Roof decking from that era is often 1×6 skip sheathing rather than modern plywood — it's structurally sound when intact, but once a section is compromised, tarps must be anchored with extra care to avoid tearing through the gaps.

Wildfire smoke and ember cast is a year-round consideration in this part of Santa Barbara County. Even when a structure isn't directly burned, a nearby fire can break windows from radiant heat or leave soot-laden openings that accelerate interior contamination. Boarding up quickly limits the secondary smoke and ash intrusion that drives up restoration costs.

## Our Board-Up and Tarping Process in Los Olivos

When we arrive on-site, the first step is a rapid structural assessment — identifying every compromised opening before touching anything. On older Los Olivos ranch properties, that often means checking not just the obvious breach but also checking adjacent sections of roofline and outbuilding connections, which can share load paths with the main structure.

For window and door board-ups, we cut and fit ¾-inch exterior-grade plywood, secured with structural screws rather than nails, which reduces vibration loosening during high-wind events. Openings are measured and cut on-site; we don't use pre-cut panels that leave gaps at irregular window frames common in custom ranch homes.

For roof tarping, we use 6-mil reinforced poly tarps anchored with 2×4 cap boards screwed through the tarp into the roof deck — a method that holds under sustained wind loads rather than relying on sandbags alone. On skip-sheathed roofs, we locate solid nailing points at each rafter before anchoring, which takes longer but prevents the tarp from pulling free on the first windy night. Tarp edges are lapped and sealed at ridges and valleys to channel water away from the breach rather than into it.

Documentation runs parallel to every step: timestamped photos of each opening before and after securing, material measurements, and a written scope that your insurance adjuster can use directly.
