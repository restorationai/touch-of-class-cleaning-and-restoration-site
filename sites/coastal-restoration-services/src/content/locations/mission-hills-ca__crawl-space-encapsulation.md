Mission Hills sits in the northern San Fernando Valley, where warm, dry summers give way to cool, occasionally damp winters — and that seasonal moisture swing does quiet damage underneath homes that most owners never see. Crawl spaces in this part of Los Angeles County accumulate ground vapor, attract rodents, and develop mold colonies that can go unnoticed for years. Encapsulation seals that environment off before it starts affecting indoor air quality, structural wood, or your energy bills.

## Why Mission Hills Properties See Crawl Space Problems

The San Fernando Valley's soil is largely clay-heavy, which means it holds water near the surface after winter rains rather than draining quickly. That trapped moisture migrates upward through an unprotected crawl space floor and condenses on joists, insulation batts, and vapor barriers that were never designed to handle repeated wetting cycles. Many homes in Mission Hills were built in the 1950s and 1960s during the postwar suburban expansion of the Valley — construction standards from that era typically included only a thin polyethylene sheet on the crawl space floor, if anything at all. After six decades, those barriers have torn, shifted, or simply degraded. The result is a crawl space that functions more like a humidity reservoir than a protected subfloor cavity.

The region also sits close to the Santa Susana Mountains, and cold air drainage off those slopes on winter nights creates temperature differentials that encourage condensation on cool crawl space surfaces — a pattern that accelerates wood rot and mold growth even in years with below-average rainfall.

## Our Crawl Space Encapsulation Process in Mission Hills

Every encapsulation project starts with a thorough inspection of the existing crawl space conditions — access points, standing water or staining, insulation condition, wood moisture readings, and any signs of pest intrusion. In older Mission Hills homes, we frequently find original duct runs sagging through the crawl space, which complicates liner installation and sometimes requires coordination with an HVAC contractor before we can fully seal the floor.

Once the space is cleared and prepped, we install a reinforced polyethylene liner — typically 20-mil — across the entire floor and up the foundation walls, sealed at all seams with moisture-resistant tape and mechanically fastened at the perimeter. If the crawl space has a vented foundation (common in pre-1980 Valley construction), we assess whether converting to a conditioned, sealed crawl space makes sense given the home's HVAC configuration. A dehumidifier sized to the cubic footage is installed when humidity readings warrant it, with a drain line routed to daylight or a condensate pump. The finished space is documented with photos before and after so you have a clear record for insurance or future buyers.

## Equipment and Methods for Encapsulation in This Climate

The San Fernando Valley's low annual rainfall can create a false sense of security — homeowners assume a dry climate means a dry crawl space. In practice, the clay soils and overnight temperature swings mean vapor drive is a year-round issue, not just a rainy-season problem. We use commercial-grade dehumidification equipment rather than residential units, because undersized dehumidifiers cycle on and off without ever pulling the space down to the target 50–55% relative humidity range that discourages mold growth. Moisture meters and data loggers are placed during installation and checked at a follow-up visit to confirm the system is performing correctly under real conditions, not just on the day of installation.
