## Why Callender Properties Face Distinct Board-Up and Tarping Risks

The Sacramento Valley's climate creates a specific pattern of damage that board-up and tarping crews see repeatedly in this part of California. Summer fire seasons are the most obvious factor — a structure fire or a wildfire ember event can leave exterior walls and roof decking charred and structurally compromised, requiring immediate boarding to prevent collapse of weakened sections and to keep the loss boundary from expanding. But the transition seasons bring their own hazards. Late-fall wind events in the foothills routinely push gusts that crack older single-pane windows and dislodge roofing materials, and the rain that follows a dry summer hits bone-dry wood framing hard.

The housing stock in and around Callender skews toward older ranch-style and craftsman construction — homes where the roof sheathing may be original tongue-and-groove boards rather than modern plywood panels. That matters for tarping because the fastening points are different, the surface is less uniform, and a tarp that isn't anchored correctly on that substrate will lift in the next wind event and cause secondary damage. Crews who haven't worked this type of construction before sometimes underestimate how much additional ballasting and edge-sealing is required.

## Our Board-Up and Tarping Process in Callender

When we arrive at a Callender property, the first thing we do is a rapid perimeter assessment — every opening, every compromised roof section, every point where weather or an intruder could enter. We photograph everything before we touch it, which matters enormously for the insurance documentation that follows.

For window and door board-up, we use exterior-grade plywood cut to fit each opening and secured with structural screws rather than nails, which hold better against repeated wind pressure and are easier to remove cleanly when permanent repairs begin. For openings in fire-damaged walls, we assess whether the surrounding framing can support a standard board-up or whether temporary shoring is needed first.

Roof tarping on Callender properties follows a layered approach: we run the tarp from the ridge line down past the eave, secure the uphill edge under existing roofing material where the structure allows, and weight or screw the perimeter depending on pitch and substrate. On the older tongue-and-groove sheathing common here, we use additional batten strips across the tarp surface to distribute load and prevent the material from flapping and tearing at anchor points during wind events.
