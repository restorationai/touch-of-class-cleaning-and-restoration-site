## Why Templeton Properties See Water Damage Issues

The Central Coast's wet season runs roughly November through March, and Templeton's position in the Santa Lucia Range foothills means it catches more rainfall than communities closer to the coast. That rainfall lands on adobe-heavy clay soil that doesn't absorb quickly — water pools against foundations, finds its way through crawl space vents, and saturates subfloor assemblies before a homeowner realizes there's a problem.

The housing stock adds complexity. Many Templeton homes were built in the 1970s and 1980s with galvanized steel supply lines that are now at or past their service life. Galvanized pipe corrodes from the inside out, narrowing over decades until a pressure spike — common when the local water system cycles after a dry spell — causes a pinhole leak or a full rupture inside a wall cavity. By the time discoloration appears on drywall, moisture has often been wicking into framing and insulation for days.

Newer construction on the hillside lots east of the town center presents a different challenge: stucco cladding that traps moisture when window flashing fails or roof-to-wall transitions crack under thermal cycling. Stucco systems can hold water against the sheathing for weeks, creating conditions that accelerate wood rot and mold colonization well before visible exterior damage appears.

## Our Water Damage Restoration Process in Templeton

Every job starts with a thorough moisture mapping inspection. We use thermal imaging and calibrated moisture meters to trace water migration beyond the obvious wet zone — because in Templeton's older ranch-style homes, water that enters a hallway bathroom often travels along the subfloor toward a bedroom or living area before it shows up on the surface.

Once the affected boundary is mapped, extraction comes first. We use truck-mounted and portable extraction units to pull standing water and saturated carpet or flooring material. For crawl space flooding — common in Templeton homes that sit on pier-and-beam foundations — we deploy submersible pumps and high-capacity air movers designed for low-clearance spaces.

Structural drying follows a calculated drying plan. Dehumidifiers are sized to the cubic footage of the affected area, and drying targets are set based on the material types present — engineered hardwood floors, for example, require tighter humidity control than concrete slab. We monitor moisture readings daily and adjust equipment placement until materials reach documented dry standard.

Content that can be moved — furniture, cabinetry, stored items — is inventoried, dried separately where possible, and documented for insurance purposes throughout the process.
