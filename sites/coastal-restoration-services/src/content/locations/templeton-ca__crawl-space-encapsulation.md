## Why Templeton Properties Are Prone to Crawl Space Moisture Problems

The Salinas River corridor and the surrounding Paso Robles wine country share a soil profile that holds moisture longer than it looks like it should. Clay-heavy subsoils common throughout this part of San Luis Obispo County expand when wet and contract when dry, which means the ground beneath a crawl space is never truly stable. That movement creates small gaps in older foundation stem walls, and those gaps let ground vapor climb steadily into the framing above.

Templeton's housing stock adds another layer of complexity. A significant portion of homes here were built in the 1970s and 1980s, before vapor barriers were a standard construction requirement in this climate zone. Many of those original polyethylene sheets — if they were installed at all — are now torn, displaced by decades of pest activity, or simply too thin to do meaningful work. Fiberglass batt insulation stapled between floor joists in that era absorbs moisture like a sponge and holds it directly against the wood it was meant to protect.

The agricultural surroundings matter too. Properties near vineyard land or horse pasture often have higher ambient humidity at ground level than properties in more urbanized areas. Irrigation runoff and seasonal creek activity can raise the effective moisture load under a crawl space well above what a homeowner would expect.

## Our Crawl Space Encapsulation Process in Templeton

Encapsulation is not a single product — it is a sequenced system, and the sequence matters. The work begins with a full inspection of the crawl space: measuring humidity levels with a calibrated hygrometer, probing wood members for moisture content, checking for standing water or evidence of past flooding, and documenting any existing vapor barrier condition.

If mold or wood rot is present, that remediation happens before any barrier goes down — covering a problem does not eliminate it. Once the space is clean and structurally sound, the process moves through these stages:

- **Ground cover installation**: A reinforced polyethylene liner, typically 20-mil, is laid across the entire floor and lapped up foundation walls, then sealed at seams and penetrations with foil tape rated for vapor control.
- **Wall and pier wrapping**: Foundation walls and support piers are wrapped and sealed to the floor liner, eliminating the gap where most vapor entry occurs.
- **Vent sealing**: In climates like Templeton's — where the temperature differential between day and night is significant — open foundation vents can actually draw humid outside air in during evening hours. Sealing vents and conditioning the space is often more effective than the ventilated approach older building codes assumed.
- **Drainage and dehumidification**: Where bulk water intrusion is a risk, a perimeter drain channel and sump system is integrated before the liner goes down. A crawl space dehumidifier sized to the square footage maintains relative humidity below 55% year-round.

The completed system is documented with photos and a moisture baseline reading so you have a record for insurance or resale purposes.
