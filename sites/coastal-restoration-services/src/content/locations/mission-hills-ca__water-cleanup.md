## Why Mission Hills Properties Face Distinct Water Damage Risks

Much of Mission Hills was developed during the postwar building boom of the late 1940s through the 1960s. Homes from that era were typically built on concrete slab foundations, which means there is no crawl space to inspect when water migrates under the floor. Water that enters through a slab crack, a failed supply line, or a slow-draining appliance can travel laterally beneath tile or hardwood for several feet before it shows at the surface. By the time a homeowner notices a soft spot or a musty odor, saturation may already extend well beyond the visible wet area.

The clay-dominant soil common throughout this part of the Valley also contributes to drainage problems. During heavy rain events, saturated ground can push moisture toward foundation perimeters and through any gap in the slab or stem wall. This is not unique to Mission Hills, but the density of older slab construction here means fewer homes have the perimeter drainage systems that newer builds typically include.

Gas and water service in this neighborhood runs through Los Angeles Department of Water and Power infrastructure. After a significant water loss, shutting off supply at the street-side meter is sometimes necessary, and homeowners should know where that shutoff is located before an emergency happens.

## How Emergency Water Cleanup Works in Mission Hills

The first step on any water damage job is containment and assessment, not immediately running equipment. A technician walks the affected area with a moisture meter and thermal imaging to map where water has traveled, including inside walls and under flooring where it is not visible. In slab-foundation homes, this mapping step is especially important because water follows the path of least resistance beneath the concrete, and extraction equipment needs to be positioned where the moisture actually is, not just where it looks wet.

Once the scope is documented, standing water is removed using truck-mounted or portable extraction units. Structural drying follows, using industrial air movers and dehumidifiers calibrated to the room volume and the materials involved. Drying targets are set according to the IICRC S500 standard, and moisture readings are logged daily so there is a clear record of progress. In Mission Hills homes with tile over slab, drying mats are often used to draw moisture through the tile and thinset without requiring full demolition.

If wall cavities are saturated, controlled demolition of baseboards or drywall sections may be necessary to allow airflow. Decisions about what to open and what to dry in place are made based on moisture readings, not assumptions.

## Coordinating with Insurance for Mission Hills Claims

Most standard homeowner policies in California cover sudden and accidental water losses, including burst pipes and appliance failures. Gradual leaks that developed over time are frequently excluded, which is why thorough documentation at the start of a job matters. Coastal Restoration Services Inc photographs affected materials, logs moisture readings, and prepares scope documentation that supports the claim process with most major carriers.

For Mission Hills homeowners who carry a policy through a California FAIR Plan or a surplus-lines carrier, the documentation process is the same, but the adjuster timeline can differ. Keeping a copy of the initial moisture map and photo set is useful regardless of which carrier is involved.
