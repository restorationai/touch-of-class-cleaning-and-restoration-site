## Our Emergency Board-Up and Tarping Process in Vandenberg AFB

When a call comes in, the first step is a rapid site assessment — cataloging every breach point before a single sheet of plywood goes up. For fire-damaged structures, that means checking for heat-weakened framing around window and door openings before workers apply lateral pressure boarding them closed. For storm-damaged roofs, we walk the deck carefully, probing for soft spots, before positioning tarps.

Boarding uses minimum ⅝-inch CDX plywood, cut to overlap the rough opening by at least four inches on each side and fastened into structural framing — not just into drywall or trim. In the coastal environment here, we use exterior-rated fasteners because standard zinc screws begin corroding within days in salt air, which matters if the board-up needs to hold for several weeks while insurance adjusters process the claim. Roof tarps are secured with weighted lumber battens and, where roof pitch and material allow, screwed through into decking rather than relying on weight alone — onshore gusts can pull a loosely anchored tarp free overnight.

Photographic documentation happens at every stage: before boarding, after boarding, and a final walkthrough. That documentation goes directly into the claim file and is formatted to meet the requirements most major carriers use when reviewing emergency mitigation invoices.
