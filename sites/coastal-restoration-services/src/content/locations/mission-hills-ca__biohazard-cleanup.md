## Why Mission Hills Properties Present Unique Biohazard Considerations

The housing stock in Mission Hills tells a story in layers. Many properties along the residential corridors near Sepulveda Boulevard were built in the 1950s and 1960s, when construction standards, ventilation designs, and flooring materials differed substantially from today. Porous concrete subfloors, older hardwood, and original grout lines in bathrooms can absorb biological material deeply — far deeper than a surface cleaning can reach. Standard household disinfectants do not neutralize pathogens at the level required by California Department of Public Health guidelines, and in enclosed spaces with limited airflow, contamination can persist long after the visible evidence is gone.

The density of multi-unit housing in parts of Mission Hills adds another layer of complexity. In apartment buildings, contamination in one unit can raise legitimate concerns about shared HVAC systems, adjacent wall cavities, and common-area exposure. Landlords and property managers in these situations face both a remediation challenge and a disclosure obligation under California law — which is why documentation matters as much as the cleanup itself.

## Our Biohazard Remediation Process in Mission Hills

Every job begins with a confidential assessment. A trained technician walks the affected space, identifies all areas of concern — including surfaces that may not be visibly affected but are within the contamination zone — and outlines a scope of work before anything is touched. This step protects you legally and ensures nothing is missed.

Containment comes next. We isolate the work area using physical barriers and negative air pressure where the layout of the property requires it, preventing cross-contamination to unaffected rooms. All biological materials, saturated building components, and disposable PPE are packaged and transported as regulated medical waste in compliance with California Code of Regulations Title 22 requirements — not placed in standard trash. Affected surfaces are then treated with hospital-grade EPA-registered disinfectants, followed by a verification step before containment is removed.

For properties where structural materials must be removed — flooring, drywall, subfloor sections — we coordinate the disposal and can document the scope for your insurance carrier or property management company.

## Local Note

One thing that comes up specifically in Mission Hills and the broader northwestern San Fernando Valley is the prevalence of properties with original 1950s and 1960s terrazzo or vinyl asbestos tile flooring. When biohazard remediation requires removing flooring in these homes, there is a real possibility that the tile or the mastic adhesive beneath it contains asbestos — a material that cannot simply be pulled up and discarded. Our team identifies this risk during the initial assessment and, when asbestos-containing materials are suspected, coordinates with a licensed California asbestos abatement contractor before flooring removal proceeds. Skipping this step is not just a regulatory violation; it creates a secondary hazard in a situation that already demands careful handling.
