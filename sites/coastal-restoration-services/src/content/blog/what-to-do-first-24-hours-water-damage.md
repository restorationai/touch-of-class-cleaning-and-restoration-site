If water is actively spreading through your home right now, stop the source first: find your main shutoff valve (usually near the water meter, in a garage, or under a sink) and turn it off. Then get people and pets out of any room with standing water near electrical outlets or panels. The decisions you make in the next few hours — what you move, what you leave, what you dry, what you document — will directly affect how much damage your home sustains and how smoothly your insurance claim goes. Here is exactly what to do.

## Step 1: Stop the Water and Make the Space Safe

Before anything else, cut off the source. If a pipe burst, turn off the main shutoff. If an appliance failed — a washing machine hose, a water heater, a dishwasher — shut off the dedicated supply valve behind or beneath that appliance. If the water is coming from a roof leak or a window during a storm, you cannot stop it at the source, but you can move valuables and place buckets to limit spread.

Once the water stops flowing, assess the room before walking into it:

- **Electrical hazard**: If there is standing water and any outlets, baseboards, or appliances are submerged or nearby, do not enter until the circuit breaker for that area is switched off. Water and live current are a fatal combination.
- **Structural hazard**: A ceiling that is sagging and discolored is holding water. Do not stand under it. Poke a small hole at the lowest point with a screwdriver to let it drain in a controlled stream rather than collapse.
- **Sewage contamination**: If the water came from an overflowing toilet, a backed-up drain, or a flooded crawl space, treat it as contaminated. Wear rubber gloves and boots before contact, and keep children and pets out entirely.

## Step 2: Document Everything Before You Touch It

Insurance adjusters work from evidence. Before you move a single piece of furniture or pull up a single rug, take photos and video of every affected room from multiple angles. Walk slowly. Capture:

- The water level on walls (a wet line will fade as it dries)
- Flooring — hardwood buckling, carpet soaked through, tile grout lines darkened
- Personal property in place: furniture, electronics, clothing, documents
- The source of the water if it is visible (the burst pipe, the failed appliance, the crack in the foundation)

Save these files to cloud storage immediately so they are not lost if your phone or computer is later damaged. Note the date and time on your documentation — insurers will ask.

Call your homeowner's insurance company as soon as the space is safe. Most policies require you to report a loss promptly. An agent will tell you whether your policy covers the cause of the loss (sudden pipe burst: typically yes; slow leak that went unnoticed for months: often excluded) and whether you need an adjuster on-site before any work begins.

## Step 3: Begin Drying — Every Hour Counts

Mold can begin colonizing wet organic material — drywall paper, wood framing, carpet padding — within 24 to 48 hours under warm, humid conditions. Along the Central Coast, where summer fog keeps indoor humidity elevated even without a leak, that window can be even shorter. The goal of the first 24 hours is to remove as much standing water and surface moisture as possible.

**What you can do yourself:**

1. Use a wet/dry shop vac to extract standing water from hard floors. Make multiple passes.
2. Pull up wet area rugs and move them outside to dry in the sun. Do not leave them on hardwood — they will trap moisture and accelerate warping.
3. Remove soaked items from the floor: cardboard boxes, books, clothing. Wet paper and fabric are mold food.
4. Open windows and interior doors to increase airflow — but only if outdoor humidity is lower than indoor humidity. On a foggy Central Coast morning, opening windows can make things worse.
5. Run every fan you own pointed at wet surfaces. Box fans on the floor aimed at baseboards are more effective than ceiling fans for drying floor-level moisture.
6. If you have a dehumidifier, run it continuously and empty the reservoir every few hours.

**What you cannot do yourself:** Moisture hides. Water travels along floor joists, wicks up inside wall cavities, and pools under vapor barriers in crawl spaces. A surface that looks dry to the eye can read 40% moisture content on a professional meter — wet enough to grow mold inside the wall while the paint looks fine. This is why professional water damage restoration uses thermal imaging cameras and calibrated moisture meters to map the full extent of saturation before drying equipment is placed.

## What NOT to Do in the First 24 Hours

Some instincts make sense but will cost you later:

- **Do not run a central HVAC system** to dry the space. Forced air through ducts can carry mold spores and contaminated particles to unaffected rooms.
- **Do not use a regular household vacuum** on standing water — it is not designed for liquid and creates an electrocution risk.
- **Do not throw away damaged items** before your insurance adjuster or a restoration crew has documented them. Discarding property before it is inventoried can complicate your claim.
- **Do not paint or caulk over wet surfaces.** Sealing moisture in accelerates mold growth and may void a warranty on any remediation work done later.
- **Do not assume it is dry because it feels dry.** Drywall and subfloor can feel dry to the touch while holding enough moisture in their core to sustain mold growth for weeks.

## When to Call a Water Damage Professional

Call a restoration company the same day if any of the following are true:

- The affected area is larger than roughly 10 square feet of flooring
- Water reached drywall, insulation, or a wood subfloor
- The source was sewage or a backed-up drain
- You can smell a musty or earthy odor (mold may already be present from a prior undetected leak)
- The water came from a roof or exterior and may have reached wall cavities
- You are filing an insurance claim and need documentation support

For smaller incidents — a single appliance leak caught within minutes, a contained spill on tile — thorough DIY drying with fans and a dehumidifier may be sufficient. But when in doubt, a moisture reading from a professional costs far less than tearing out a mold-colonized wall six weeks later.

## What Happens After the First 24 Hours

Professional water damage restoration typically unfolds in phases over several days to a couple of weeks, depending on the extent of saturation:

1. **Extraction and initial drying setup** — industrial air movers and commercial-grade dehumidifiers are placed throughout the affected area. These machines move far more air volume than household fans.
2. **Moisture monitoring** — a technician returns daily to read moisture levels in walls, floors, and ceilings and adjusts equipment placement until readings reach acceptable dry standards.
3. **Controlled demolition if needed** — sections of drywall or flooring that cannot be dried in place are removed to allow the structure behind them to dry. This is called a "flood cut" and is far less disruptive than it sounds.
4. **Mold prevention treatment** — antimicrobial applications to affected framing and subfloor before any reconstruction begins.
5. **Rebuild** — once the structure is verified dry, repairs begin: new drywall, flooring, paint, trim.

The total timeline for a typical water loss — a burst pipe affecting one or two rooms — is usually one to two weeks for drying and another one to three weeks for reconstruction, depending on material availability and permit requirements.
