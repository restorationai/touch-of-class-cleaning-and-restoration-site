That said, "first call" depends on what is happening right now:

- **Water still flowing from a pipe or appliance?** Call a licensed plumber to shut off or repair the source. A restoration crew handles extraction and drying, not plumbing repairs. Once the water is off, call the restoration company.
- **Electrical hazard?** If outlets, panels, or appliances are submerged or sparking, call 911 and do not enter the space. Wait for the utility to confirm it is safe.
- **Gas smell?** Leave the building, call SoCalGas at 1-800-427-2200 from outside, then 911 if needed. Do not re-enter until cleared.
- **Sewer backup or sewage visible?** This is Category 3 water, also called black water, which carries pathogens. Do not touch it. Call a restoration company that handles sewage cleanup directly.
- **Roof leak during a storm?** Tarping and board-up can happen concurrently with extraction. A full-service restoration company can coordinate both.

Once the immediate hazard is controlled, the restoration call comes next, before you call your insurance company. Here's why: the restoration crew documents the loss, and that documentation protects your claim.

## When Should I Call My Insurance Company?

Call your insurance company after mitigation has started, not before. Most adjusters want to see a documented scope of work, and the restoration company produces that. Calling the insurer first and waiting for approval before starting drying almost always makes the damage worse, and some policies have language that limits coverage when a policyholder delays mitigation.

What to have ready when you do call your insurer:

- The date and time you discovered the damage
- The source of the water (burst pipe, appliance failure, roof leak, storm, etc.)
- Photos and video you took before anyone moved anything
- The restoration company's name, license number, and the scope they provided

## What Happens When You Call a Restoration Company?

The first call is short. You describe what happened, the address, and whether the water source is controlled. The crew coordinator will ask a few triage questions: Is there standing water? What rooms are affected? Is there any visible mold or sewage? Are there contents (furniture, electronics, documents) still in the space?

From there, a crew is dispatched. When they arrive, here is the standard sequence under the [IICRC S500 standard](https://www.iicrc.org) for water damage restoration:

1. **Safety assessment.** The crew checks for electrical hazards, structural instability, and contamination category before entering.
2. **Moisture mapping.** Technicians use calibrated moisture meters and thermal imaging to find where water has migrated, including inside walls and under flooring. Water travels further than it looks.
3. **Water extraction.** Truck-mounted or portable extractors remove standing water. This is not the same as drying, which comes next.
4. **Drying system setup.** LGR (low-grain refrigerant) dehumidifiers and high-velocity air movers are placed according to a drying plan. The goal is to bring structural materials to their pre-loss moisture content, typically measured in wood moisture equivalents (WME).
5. **Daily monitoring.** Technicians return each day to log moisture readings and adjust equipment placement. Drying typically takes 3-5 days for a Category 1 (clean water) loss in a standard residential space.
6. **Documentation for your claim.** Moisture logs, photos, and a written scope of work go to your insurer. This is the paper trail that protects your settlement.

If mold is already visible, or if drying reveals hidden growth, a separate [mold remediation](/services/mold-remediation/) protocol under IICRC S520 applies. The two scopes are distinct.

## What to Have Ready Before the Crew Arrives

You do not need to do much, but a few things help:

- **Take photos and video right now**, before you move anything. Walk every affected room. Open cabinets under sinks. Lift area rugs. Document the waterline on walls.
- **Move small valuables and electronics** out of the wet area if it is safe to do so.
- **Locate your water shutoff** if you haven't already. For most Santa Maria homes, the main shutoff is near the meter at the street or on the side of the house.
- **Do not use fans or a shop vac** as a substitute for professional extraction. Fans can spread contamination and push moisture into wall cavities. A shop vac does not have the capacity to extract from subfloor or wall assemblies.
- **Do not run your HVAC system** if the air handler or ductwork may be wet. This spreads contamination and can damage the unit.
