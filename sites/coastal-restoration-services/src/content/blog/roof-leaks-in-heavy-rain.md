**TL;DR:** When a roof leaks during heavy rain, your first job is to limit interior damage, not fix the roof. Move belongings, catch water, document everything with photos and video, and call your insurer to open a claim. Most homeowners policies cover the interior water damage caused by a sudden roof leak, but they do not cover the roof repair itself unless a covered peril (like wind or hail) caused the opening. A licensed roofer handles the roof; a certified restoration company handles the water damage inside.

If rain is coming through your ceiling right now, you are dealing with two separate problems that need two separate contractors. The roof opening needs a roofer or an emergency tarp crew. The soaked drywall, wet insulation, and saturated subfloor need a water damage restoration team. Mixing those up, or waiting on either one, is where the real cost happens. Here is what to do, in order, before the storm even stops.

## What Should You Do the Moment a Roof Leak Starts?

The first 30 minutes determine how much damage you end up with. Move furniture and electronics out of the drip zone, lay down plastic sheeting, and put buckets under active drips. If water is pooling in a ceiling bubble, carefully puncture the lowest point with a screwdriver to drain it in a controlled stream rather than letting it collapse the drywall all at once.

Then document everything before you touch another thing. Walk the affected rooms with your phone camera rolling. Capture the ceiling stain, the active drip, any standing water on floors, and wet contents. That footage is your claim. The California Department of Insurance notes that thorough documentation at the time of loss is one of the most important steps a homeowner can take to protect a property claim. You can review their residential claims guidance at [insurance.ca.gov](https://www.insurance.ca.gov/01-consumers/105-type/95-guides/03-res/res-prop-claim.cfm).

After documentation, call your insurance company to open a claim. You do not need to know the full extent of damage yet. Opening the claim starts the clock on your coverage and puts the insurer on notice that mitigation costs are accumulating.

## Does Homeowners Insurance Cover Water Damage from a Roof Leak?

Most standard homeowners policies cover interior water damage from a roof leak when a sudden, covered event caused the roof opening. Wind, hail, a falling tree limb, and similar storm perils are typically covered. The water damage to your ceilings, insulation, drywall, flooring, and contents usually falls under your dwelling and personal property coverages.

What policies typically do NOT cover:

- **The roof repair itself**, unless the same storm event caused the damage and the policy includes roof coverage (check your declarations page carefully).
- **Gradual leaks.** If your roof had a slow drip for months that finally showed up as a stain, most insurers will deny that as a maintenance issue rather than a sudden loss.
- **Pre-existing deterioration.** An adjuster will look for evidence that the roof was already failing before the storm.

This is why the sequence matters. A sudden, heavy-rain event that opens a previously sound roof is a strong claim. A roof that was already soft and finally gave way during rain is a harder one. Document the storm (save weather reports, note the date and time), and document the condition of the area around the leak to show it was not a long-standing problem.

For more detail on how California homeowners policies handle water damage generally, see our post [Does Homeowners Insurance Cover Water Damage? A Plain-English Guide](/blog/does-homeowners-insurance-cover-water-damage/).

## How Do You Protect the Interior Until the Roof Is Fixed?

Emergency tarping is the bridge between the storm and a permanent roof repair. A heavy-duty polyethylene tarp secured over the damaged section stops additional water intrusion while you coordinate with a roofer. This is not a DIY job during or immediately after a storm, when roofs are wet and wind may still be present.

A professional [emergency board-up and tarping service](/services/emergency-board-up-tarping/) can deploy quickly, secure the tarp with battens or weighted edges so it holds through additional rain, and document the work for your insurance file. That documentation matters: your insurer expects you to take reasonable steps to prevent further damage after a loss. Failing to tarp a known opening can give an adjuster grounds to limit the claim for any subsequent water that enters.

Once the tarp is in place, the roof opening is the roofer's job. The restoration company takes over everything below the roof deck: wet insulation, soaked drywall, saturated subfloor, and any contents that absorbed moisture.

## How Long Does It Take to Dry Out Water Damage from a Roof Leak?

A properly equipped drying job typically takes 3 to 5 days for standard residential materials, though that window depends on how much water entered, what materials got wet, and how quickly drying equipment was deployed. Wet insulation, dense subfloor assemblies, and double-layer drywall take longer than a single wet ceiling panel.

The [IICRC S500 Standard for Professional Water Damage Restoration](https://www.iicrc.org/page/IICRCS500) defines the science behind structural drying: materials must reach documented moisture content targets before reconstruction begins. Drying to a number, not to a guess, is what prevents mold from growing inside a wall cavity after the drywall has been patched and painted.

A certified [water damage restoration](/services/water-damage-restoration/) crew uses industrial air movers (typically one per 50-100 square feet of affected area), low-grain refrigerant (LGR) dehumidifiers, and daily moisture meter readings to track progress. Those readings are logged and become part of the documentation your insurer receives. Rushing reconstruction before materials are dry is the most common reason a repaired room develops mold 3 to 6 weeks later.

## Does Home Insurance Cover Mold from a Roof Leak?

Mold coverage depends on whether the underlying water damage was covered and whether the mold developed because of a delay or a maintenance failure. If your roof leak was a covered sudden event and you called a restoration company promptly, any mold that developed as a direct result of that event is generally covered under your policy's mold endorsement or as part of the water damage claim.

If you waited weeks to address the wet materials, or if the insurer determines the leak was gradual, mold coverage becomes much harder to secure. The EPA recommends beginning mold remediation within 24 to 48 hours of a water event to prevent colonization. That window is real: visible mold growth can begin on wet drywall paper and insulation in as little as 48 hours under warm, humid conditions.

This is one reason the tarp-and-dry sequence matters beyond just the structure. Prompt action protects both the building and the claim.

## How Do You Make a Successful Roof Leak Insurance Claim?

A successful roof leak claim comes down to documentation, timing, and scope. Follow this sequence:

1. **Document the storm.** Screenshot local weather reports or NOAA data showing the date and intensity of the rain event.
2. **Photograph and video the damage immediately.** Ceiling stains, active drips, wet flooring, affected contents. Time-stamped.
3. **Open the claim the same day.** Call your insurer or use their app. Get a claim number.
4. **Mitigate immediately.** Tarp the roof opening, extract standing water, begin drying. Keep all receipts.
5. **Get a written scope from your restoration contractor.** A detailed line-item estimate (not a verbal quote) is what the adjuster works from. Make sure it covers extraction, drying, and any affected materials.
6. **Request a copy of the moisture logs.** Your restoration company should provide daily readings. These prove the drying was thorough and support any reconstruction costs in the claim.
7. **Do not reconstruct until drying is documented complete.** Patching wet drywall voids the drying record and can create a mold problem that is harder to claim later.

For [storm damage restoration](/services/storm-damage-restoration/) that includes insurance documentation support, a certified restoration company can work directly with your adjuster and provide the written scope and moisture logs the claim requires.

If the storm also damaged windows, doors, or left the structure exposed overnight, an emergency board-up can be added to the same claim scope. Keep those receipts separate and clearly labeled.
