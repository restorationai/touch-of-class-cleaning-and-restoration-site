## What to Look for in a Water Damage Restoration Company

The right water damage company is IICRC-certified, available when you need them, and can document the loss in a format your insurer accepts. Those three factors matter more than price when you are dealing with an active water loss.

**IICRC certification** is the baseline credential in this industry. The [IICRC S500 standard](https://www.iicrc.org) defines how water losses are categorized (Category 1 clean water through Category 3 black water), how drying targets are set, and what documentation a proper job requires. A company without IICRC-certified technicians may dry a space to the wrong moisture content, leaving conditions for mold growth behind walls.

**24/7 availability** matters because water damage compounds by the hour. The [EPA notes](https://www.epa.gov/mold/mold-course-chapter-2) that mold can begin developing within 24 to 48 hours of a moisture event. A company that cannot respond until Monday morning on a Saturday night pipe burst is not a real emergency option.

**Insurance documentation** is where many homeowners lose money on a claim. A good restoration company tracks moisture readings at every affected surface, documents drying progress daily, and produces a scope of work that matches what your adjuster needs to approve the claim. If your crew is not doing this, ask why.
