A burst pipe can release dozens of gallons of water per minute. The moment you hear the rush or spot the flood, shut off your home's main water supply valve, don't wait to find the source first. Once the water is off, you have a narrow window to limit structural damage, protect your belongings, and prevent the mold growth that can begin colonizing wet materials within 24 to 48 hours. This checklist walks you through every step, in order, so you're not guessing while water is spreading across your floor.

## Step-by-Step: What to Do the Moment a Pipe Bursts

Work through these steps as fast as you can. Sequence matters, doing them out of order (like cleaning up before cutting power) can turn a bad situation into a dangerous one.

1. **Shut off the main water supply valve.** In most Lakewood homes this is either in a utility closet, the garage, or near the water meter at the street. Turning it clockwise (righty-tighty) stops the flow. If you've never located yours, do it now, before you need it.
2. **Cut power to affected areas.** If water is pooling near outlets, baseboards, or your electrical panel, flip the breakers for those zones at your main panel. Don't step through standing water to reach a live outlet.
3. **Open faucets to drain remaining pressure.** After the main is off, open the lowest faucet in the house (a hose bib or basement utility sink) to bleed residual water from the pipes and reduce pressure on the break point.
4. **Document everything before you touch it.** Walk the affected area with your phone camera. Shoot wide angles of each room, close-ups of the pipe or fixture, and the waterline on walls and baseboards. Your insurance adjuster will need this, and it costs you nothing to capture it now.
5. **Move what you can, fast.** Rugs, furniture, electronics, and anything stored on the floor should come out of the wet zone. Porous materials absorb water quickly and become difficult to salvage after even a few hours of contact.
6. **Start air movement if you have fans.** Box fans pointed across wet hard floors can slow absorption while you wait for help. This is not a substitute for professional drying equipment, but it buys time.
7. **Call your insurance company.** Most homeowner policies cover sudden and accidental pipe bursts (as opposed to slow leaks from deferred maintenance). Report the claim early, carriers often have preferred restoration vendors and can connect you to resources quickly.

## What NOT to Do After a Burst Pipe

Some instincts that feel helpful in the moment can actually make the damage worse or create liability problems later.

- **Don't use a wet/dry shop vac as your primary extraction tool.** A shop vac can pull surface water, but it won't reach water that has wicked into subfloor, insulation, or wall cavities. You may think you've dried a room that is still saturated an inch below the surface, and that hidden moisture is exactly where mold starts.
- **Don't run the HVAC system to dry things out.** Forced air through a wet duct system can spread moisture and, if mold is already present, distribute spores through the house.
- **Don't use a standard household fan in a room with standing water near outlets.** Extension cords and plug-in fans near water are an electrocution risk.
- **Don't throw away damaged materials before your adjuster sees them.** Torn-out drywall, ruined flooring, and waterlogged insulation are evidence. Photograph everything, and ideally keep samples until the claim is settled.
- **Don't assume the damage is limited to what you can see.** Water behind plaster or drywall can read dry at the surface while the lath or OSB underneath stays saturated. If you pressed your hand against a wall and it felt cool and slightly soft, there's likely moisture behind it.

## When the Damage Is More Than You Can Handle Yourself

Some burst-pipe situations are manageable with a mop and a few fans. Many are not. Here's how to tell the difference.

**Call a water damage restoration professional if:**
- The water has been running for more than a few minutes before you caught it, or you don't know how long it ran
- Water has reached more than one room or traveled to a lower floor
- The affected area includes carpet over padding, hardwood flooring, or any finished basement space
- You see water staining or bubbling on ceilings (water is pooling above you)
- The pipe that burst is behind a wall and you can hear or feel moisture in the wall cavity
- It's been more than 24 hours since the event, at that point, mold risk is real and rising

Professional restoration crews use thermal imaging cameras and moisture meters to find water that has migrated beyond the visible damage zone. They also run commercial-grade dehumidifiers and air movers calibrated to the specific volume of wet material, equipment that pulls far more moisture per hour than anything available at a hardware store.

## The Longer Recovery: What Happens After the Water Is Gone

Stopping the water is step one. The recovery that follows can take days to weeks depending on the extent of saturation and the materials involved.

**Drying and monitoring (days 1–5 or longer):** Professional drying isn't a set-it-and-forget-it process. Technicians return to check moisture readings and adjust equipment placement as materials dry at different rates. Concrete slabs, for example, hold moisture far longer than drywall and require extended drying times.

**Demolition of unsalvageable materials:** Wet drywall, insulation, and some flooring types cannot be dried in place, they have to come out. This is called a "controlled demo" and it's a normal part of water damage restoration, not a sign that something went wrong. Removing saturated material quickly is what prevents mold from establishing.

**Mold testing and remediation (if needed):** If the water sat for more than 24–48 hours, or if you notice a musty smell after drying is complete, mold testing is worth considering. Visible mold growth after a water event is not uncommon and is treatable, but it needs to be addressed before reconstruction begins.

**Reconstruction:** Once materials are dry and the structure is clean, repairs begin, new drywall, flooring, paint, and any fixture work. Some restoration companies handle reconstruction in-house; others hand off to a general contractor at this stage.

If the burst was connected to an appliance, a washing machine supply line, a refrigerator ice maker line, or a dishwasher hose, the cleanup process is similar, but the source repair involves the appliance itself. Appliance leak cleanup follows the same drying and documentation steps, with the added step of confirming the appliance is repaired or replaced before reconstruction closes the walls.
