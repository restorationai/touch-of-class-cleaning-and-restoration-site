## Why Puyallup Properties See Asbestos Concerns

Pierce County's residential construction peaked in several distinct waves. The postwar boom of the late 1940s through the 1960s produced a large number of homes where asbestos-containing materials were standard, popcorn acoustic ceilings, vinyl floor tiles and their mastic, duct insulation, and textured drywall joint compound. A second wave of construction in the 1970s continued to use some of these materials before federal restrictions began tightening in the late 1970s and into the 1980s.

What makes Puyallup's situation distinct from newer suburban corridors is the concentration of that older inventory in and around the city's historic core. Homes within walking distance of downtown Puyallup, particularly those built before 1980, have a statistically higher likelihood of containing asbestos in at least one material category. Renovation projects, insurance-covered water losses, and fire damage repairs all create disturbance risk. Washington State requires asbestos surveys before demolition or significant renovation of pre-1980 structures, so the inspection step isn't optional, it's regulatory.

## Our Asbestos Abatement Process in Puyallup

Every project starts with a material assessment. A trained inspector collects bulk samples from suspect materials, ceiling texture, floor tile, pipe insulation, roofing felt, drywall compound, and sends them to an accredited laboratory. Results typically return within a few business days, though rush analysis is available when a water or fire loss is driving the timeline.

If the lab confirms asbestos-containing material above regulated thresholds, abatement follows a defined sequence:

- **Containment setup**: Poly sheeting and negative air pressure units isolate the work area from the rest of the structure. HVAC registers are sealed to prevent fiber migration.
- **Wet removal**: Regulated materials are wetted before disturbance to suppress airborne fibers. Workers wear full PPE, respirators, disposable coveralls, gloves.
- **Waste packaging and transport**: Removed material is double-bagged in labeled, leak-tight containers and transported to a licensed disposal facility. Washington requires specific manifest documentation for asbestos waste, and we handle that paperwork.
- **Air clearance**: Post-abatement air sampling confirms fiber levels are within acceptable limits before containment comes down. This is the step that allows reconstruction to proceed.

Frontline Fire & Flood holds EPA Lead-Safe certification and carries OSHA training across the crew, both relevant when abatement overlaps with lead paint in pre-1978 homes, which is common in Puyallup's older neighborhoods.
