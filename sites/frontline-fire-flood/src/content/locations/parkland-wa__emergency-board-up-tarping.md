Parkland sits in a rain shadow that still manages to collect more than its share of Pacific Northwest moisture, and when a fire, windstorm, or break-in leaves a structure open to the elements, that damp air moves fast. A broken window or a section of missing roof decking that might stay dry for a day in a drier climate can begin wicking moisture into wall cavities and attic insulation within hours here. Frontline Fire & Flood responds around the clock from our Lakewood base to board up openings and tarp compromised rooflines before the next rain event turns a bad day into a much larger restoration project.

## Why Parkland Properties Are Particularly Vulnerable After Structural Damage

Parkland's housing stock skews toward mid-century construction, ranch homes and split-levels built between the 1950s and 1980s that were framed with dimensional lumber and often feature original single-pane windows and aging composition shingle roofs. When fire or impact damage compromises those older rooflines, the underlying sheathing is frequently already at the edge of its service life, meaning water intrusion can degrade structural members faster than it would in newer construction. Siding on these homes is often original hardboard or T1-11, both of which swell and delaminate quickly when exposed to standing moisture.

The Pierce County area also sees regular wind events that can lift partially damaged roofing sections entirely, turning a localized fire loss or storm strike into a wide-open exposure. Boarding up and tarping immediately after the incident, not the next business day, is what keeps a contained loss from escalating into full structural remediation.

## Our Emergency Board-Up and Tarping Process in Parkland

When you call, we gather the basics over the phone: type of incident, approximate size of the opening, and whether the structure is occupied. That information lets the crew load the right materials before leaving Lakewood, plywood in the thicknesses most common for mid-century framing, commercial-grade poly tarps rated for sustained wind and rain, and the fastening systems that won't cause additional damage to older wood framing.

On-site, the sequence is straightforward but deliberate:

1. **Safety assessment first.** Fire-damaged structures and storm-struck rooflines can have compromised load paths. The crew walks the perimeter and identifies any areas where working at height or near the structure poses a hazard before any boards go up.
2. **Measure and cut to fit.** Openings are measured and plywood is cut on-site for a tight fit. Gaps around edges are where wind-driven rain enters; a properly fitted board eliminates that path.
3. **Tarp placement and anchoring.** Roof tarps are run over the ridge when possible and weighted or fastened at the eaves and rakes. A tarp that lifts in a wind gust provides almost no protection, we anchor for the conditions, not just for calm weather.
4. **Documentation for your claim.** Every opening is photographed before and after boarding. That documentation goes directly to your insurance adjuster.

## Insurance and Documentation in Pierce County

Most homeowner policies in Washington cover emergency protective measures, board-up and tarping are typically classified as "reasonable steps to prevent further damage" under standard policy language, which means your carrier expects you to act quickly and will generally reimburse the cost. We photograph every affected opening before and after work, note materials and dimensions, and produce a line-item invoice formatted the way most Pierce County adjusters prefer to receive it. If your adjuster has questions about scope or method, we are available to speak with them directly.

## Local Note

Older homes in Parkland frequently have attic spaces with minimal ventilation, a common construction pattern from the era when much of this area was built out. When a roof is breached by fire or storm damage, that enclosed attic can trap moisture-laden air against the underside of the sheathing for days before anyone notices. When we tarp a roof in this area, we note the attic ventilation situation and flag it in our documentation so the restoration team knows to check for condensation and early mold colonization before closing the attic back up. It is a small step that prevents a much larger problem downstream.
