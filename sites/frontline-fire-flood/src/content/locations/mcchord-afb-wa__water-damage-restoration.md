Whether you're in privatized base housing, a leased duplex near the flight line, or a property just outside the gate, water moving through a home on Puget Sound lowland soil behaves differently than it does further inland, and that changes how fast we need to move.

## Why McChord AFB Properties See Water Damage Issues

McChord AFB sits in the south Puget Sound lowlands, where winter rainfall is steady rather than intense but lasts for months at a time. Soils here stay saturated from fall through spring, so sump systems and perimeter drains that work fine in July can get overwhelmed by February. A lot of the housing stock in and around the base, including older military family units that predate privatization-era renovations, was built with crawlspace foundations rather than slabs. Crawlspaces trap moisture against floor joists and subfloor, and a slow plumbing leak or a cracked supply line under one of these homes can go unnoticed for weeks because there's no basement to flood and alert anyone. By the time a musty smell reaches the living room, the insulation and subfloor underneath have usually been wet far longer than the homeowner realizes.

## Our Water Damage Restoration Process in McChord AFB

We start with extraction, pulling standing water with truck-mounted or portable pumps depending on how the property is accessed, then move to moisture mapping. On crawlspace homes that's often the real job: we open access points, check joist bays and subfloor with moisture meters, and figure out how far the water traveled before we ever set a dehumidifier. From there we place air movers and commercial dehumidifiers sized to the affected square footage, and we monitor daily until readings confirm the structure is dry, not just dry to the touch. Drying times run longer in homes with older plaster or dense subflooring than in newer construction with standard drywall, so we set expectations up front rather than guessing at a finish date.

## McChord AFB Insurance & Housing Office Coordination

A lot of water damage work near the base isn't a straightforward homeowner's insurance claim. If you're in privatized military housing, repairs often route through the housing management company rather than a personal policy, and documentation requirements can differ from a typical homeowner claim. For properties with standard insurance, we photograph and document the loss the same way we would anywhere, moisture readings, affected materials, extraction logs, so the paperwork holds up whether it's going to a private carrier or a base housing office. If you're not sure who to call first, we can usually tell you based on how the property is managed.

## Local note

One thing that catches people off guard near McChord AFB: homes with crawlspace vapor barriers installed decades ago often have barriers that have torn or shifted over time, which means ground moisture is already working its way up into the structure before a pipe ever fails. When we're called out for an active leak in one of these homes, we check the vapor barrier condition as part of the inspection, because restoring the leak without addressing a failed barrier just means the crawlspace stays damp year-round.

## Equipment & Methods We Use for Water Damage

Structural drying in this area means accounting for ambient humidity that doesn't drop much in winter, so we don't rely on open windows or passive airflow. Low-grain refrigerant dehumidifiers paired with axial air movers do the real work, and for crawlspace jobs we sometimes run negative air to keep moisture from migrating up into living space while drying is underway. We follow IICRC S500 drying standards for documentation and verification, logging moisture content daily so the drying log itself becomes part of the insurance or housing office record.
