## Why Centralia Properties Are Especially Vulnerable to Storm Damage

The Chehalis River and its tributaries have a long history of overbank flooding during major storm events, and the valley's clay-heavy soils shed water quickly once they're saturated, meaning runoff reaches foundations fast. A significant portion of Centralia's residential housing stock dates to the early and mid-twentieth century, when roof sheathing was often solid board rather than plywood or OSB. That older material holds up well under normal rain but can delaminate or crack when wind-driven debris scores the surface, leaving underlayment and framing exposed to extended wet weather.

The tree canopy throughout Centralia is another factor. Mature Douglas fir and big-leaf maple are common in residential areas, and during high-wind events these trees shed large limbs, or come down entirely, onto roofs, fences, and utility lines. A tree strike that punches through a roof creates an immediate water intrusion pathway: even a few hours of rain through an open breach can saturate insulation, ceiling joists, and interior wall cavities before the storm has passed.

## Our Storm Damage Restoration Process in Centralia

The first priority after any storm strike is stopping ongoing intrusion. Our crews arrive with tarping and board-up materials to seal roof breaches and broken windows before any interior assessment begins, because drying a structure that's still taking on water is wasted effort. Once the envelope is secured, we use thermal imaging and moisture meters to map exactly where water has migrated, which in older Centralia homes often means tracing it through plaster ceilings, original hardwood subfloors, and wall cavities insulated with compressed cellulose.

From there, the process follows the IICRC S500 and S520 standards for water and structural drying: commercial-grade air movers, desiccant or refrigerant dehumidifiers sized to the affected area, and daily moisture readings logged for your insurance file. Debris removal, downed limbs, displaced roofing, damaged siding, is coordinated alongside the drying work so your property isn't left in a hazardous state while equipment runs. If structural repairs are needed after drying is complete, we document scope and coordinate with your carrier before work begins.

## Local Note

Centralia's older housing stock frequently includes original plaster-and-lath interior walls rather than modern drywall. When a roof breach or broken window allows rain to enter, plaster absorbs moisture more slowly than drywall, it may feel firm to the touch even when the lath behind it is saturated. Moisture meters calibrated for gypsum will give false-low readings on dense plaster, so our technicians use reference mode settings and probe into wall cavities rather than relying on surface readings alone. This matters because plaster that reads "dry" at the surface can still harbor enough residual moisture to support mold colonization within days if the structure isn't dried to the correct standard.
