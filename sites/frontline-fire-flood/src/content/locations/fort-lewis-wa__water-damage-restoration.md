## Why Fort Lewis Properties See Water Damage Issues

The South Puget Sound region gets long stretches of steady, low-intensity rain through fall and winter rather than isolated downpours, and that pattern matters for how water gets into a structure. Saturated ground around foundations keeps hydrostatic pressure elevated for weeks at a time, which pushes moisture through foundation cracks and slab penetrations that would stay dry in a drier climate. Add in the area's mix of older single-family homes and newer construction tied to the base's growth over the decades, and you get a wide range of plumbing vintages, galvanized supply lines in older houses, PEX in newer ones, each failing in different ways. A slow weep behind a wall can go unnoticed for days in a part of the house that doesn't get regular foot traffic, which is often how a small leak becomes a mold conversation.

## Our Water Damage Restoration Process in Fort Lewis

We start with extraction, pulling standing water with truck-mounted or portable units depending on how much volume we're dealing with and how accessible the space is. From there we inspect the building assemblies with moisture meters, not guesswork, because in this climate a surface that reads dry to the touch can still be holding water in the subfloor or framing. Structural drying follows the IICRC S500 standard: air movers positioned to create directional airflow across wet materials, dehumidifiers sized to the cubic footage of the affected area, and daily moisture readings logged until the materials return to a dry standard, not just a dry feeling. For homes with crawlspaces, which are common in this area's older housing stock, we check subfloor insulation and vapor barriers separately since trapped moisture there often outlasts what's visible in the living space.

## Local note

Because so much of this area sits on glacial till and compacted soils left over from the last ice age, drainage around foundations is slower than in sandier soils further south in the state. That means crawlspace moisture problems in Fort Lewis homes often trace back to ground saturation rather than a single plumbing failure, which is why we check exterior grading and drainage as part of the inspection, not just the interior source.
