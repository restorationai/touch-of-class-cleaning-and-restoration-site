## Why Puyallup Properties Take Storm Damage Hard

The Puyallup River valley's topography is the first factor. Storm cells that track inland from Puget Sound lose elevation quickly, and the resulting wind shear is unpredictable, gusts that barely register in Tacoma can topple mature trees in the foothills neighborhoods east of downtown Puyallup. That tree canopy, dense with second-growth conifers, is both a neighborhood asset and a liability: a saturated root ball in the valley's silty alluvial soil doesn't hold the way it would in drier, rockier ground, and a 60-foot fir can come down with far less wind than homeowners expect.

The housing stock compounds the risk. Puyallup has a significant number of homes built in the postwar decades, 1950s through 1970s, with roof decks sheathed in older board lumber rather than modern OSB panels. When that sheathing gets wet and stays wet, it doesn't just swell; it can delaminate or rot in ways that aren't visible from the attic hatch. Older vinyl and aluminum windows in that same era of construction are also more prone to wind-driven rain infiltration at the frame seals, sending water down interior wall cavities where it's invisible until drywall starts to buckle.

## Our Storm Damage Restoration Process in Puyallup

Every storm job starts with a full exterior and interior assessment before any work begins. We document wind damage, impact points from fallen branches or debris, and every water intrusion path, roof penetrations, compromised soffits, cracked foundation walls, and window frame failures. That documentation matters for your insurance claim and for making sure no hidden moisture pocket gets missed.

Once the assessment is complete, emergency tarping and board-up stops the weather from continuing to enter the structure. From there, the work shifts to water extraction and structural drying. We use thermal imaging cameras to locate moisture behind wall surfaces and under flooring, critical in Puyallup's older homes where plaster and lathe construction can hold water deep in the assembly while reading dry at the surface. Drying equipment is placed according to IICRC S500 and S520 standards, and moisture readings are logged at every visit until the structure reaches documented dry standard.

Tree and debris removal is coordinated as part of the scope when impact damage is involved, and we work directly with your adjuster to make sure the full scope, structural, mechanical, and contents, is captured before anything is closed out.
