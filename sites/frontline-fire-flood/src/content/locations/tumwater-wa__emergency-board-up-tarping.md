## Why Tumwater Properties Face Particular Exposure After a Loss

Thurston County's weather pattern is the first thing to understand. Tumwater averages well over 50 inches of rainfall annually, and the shoulder seasons, October through December and again in February and March, deliver sustained low-pressure systems that can dump inches of rain over 48-hour stretches. A fire-damaged roof or a window blown out by a windstorm doesn't get a grace period here the way it might in a drier climate. Water follows the path of least resistance, and a compromised envelope in this climate means saturated insulation, swelling framing, and the conditions mold needs to colonize, all within the first 24 to 48 hours.

The housing stock adds another layer. Much of Tumwater's residential fabric includes mid-century construction from the postwar boom, with older homes near the Deschutes River corridor built on crawl-space foundations rather than slabs. Those foundations are already working against groundwater pressure. When a roof breach or broken window lets rain in, water doesn't just pool on a concrete floor, it infiltrates subfloor assemblies and can reach the crawl space before a homeowner realizes the extent of the damage.

## Our Emergency Board-Up and Tarping Process in Tumwater

Every call starts with a rapid damage assessment on arrival. The crew identifies all breach points, not just the obvious ones like a fire-damaged dormer or a shattered sliding door, but secondary vulnerabilities like soffit damage, compromised flashing, and cracked window frames that a fire's heat warped without breaking. In Tumwater's climate, a partial seal is nearly as bad as no seal.

For board-up work, we use structural-grade plywood cut and fitted to opening dimensions, secured with methods that won't damage the surrounding frame further. On older wood-framed homes common to this area, we're careful about fastener placement, driving screws into deteriorated wood without proper backing can widen the breach. For roof tarping, we use heavy-duty polyethylene tarps weighted and anchored with battens and strapping rather than simply draped over the ridge, which is the difference between a tarp that holds through a three-day Pacific storm and one that peels off by morning.

Documentation runs parallel to the physical work. We photograph every breach point before and after securing it, which matters when your insurance adjuster needs to establish the scope of the original loss versus what was prevented by prompt mitigation.
