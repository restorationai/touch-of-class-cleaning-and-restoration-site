## Why Yelm Properties See Asbestos Concerns

Yelm's growth as a community accelerated in the postwar decades and again through the 1970s and 1980s, which means a significant portion of the local housing stock falls squarely within the construction windows when asbestos was most widely used. Popcorn ceilings applied before the late 1970s frequently contain chrysotile asbestos. Vinyl floor tiles and the black mastic adhesive beneath them, common in Yelm homes with original kitchen or basement flooring, are among the most frequently encountered asbestos-containing materials during remodels. Pipe insulation on older forced-air and hydronic systems, as well as the joint compound used on drywall seams through the mid-1980s, are other common sources.

Thurston County's wet winters also matter here. Moisture intrusion, whether from roof damage, plumbing failures, or the region's persistent seasonal rain, can disturb aging building materials. When water-damaged ceiling tiles or deteriorating floor underlayment are torn out without prior testing, intact asbestos fibers can become airborne. That intersection of older construction and a wet climate makes pre-disturbance testing especially important for Yelm properties.

## Our Asbestos Abatement Process in Yelm

Every project begins with a site assessment and bulk sampling of suspect materials. Samples go to an accredited laboratory; results typically return within a few business days, though rush turnaround is available when timelines are tight. If asbestos-containing materials are confirmed, we develop a scope of work aligned with Washington State Department of Labor & Industries regulations, which govern both notification requirements and disposal procedures for asbestos waste in this region.

Containment is established using negative air pressure enclosures and heavy-gauge poly sheeting to isolate the work area from the rest of the structure. Our team works in full personal protective equipment, and all removed material is wetted, double-bagged, and transported to a licensed disposal facility. After abatement, air clearance testing by an independent industrial hygienist confirms fiber levels have returned to acceptable thresholds before containment is removed and the space is released for re-occupancy or continued renovation. Washington State requires this clearance step, it is not optional, and we build it into every project timeline.
