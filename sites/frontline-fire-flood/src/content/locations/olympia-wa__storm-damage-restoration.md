## Why Olympia Properties See Distinctive Storm Damage

The geography here creates a specific kind of storm problem. The region sits in a rain shadow gap that funnels wind off the Sound, and the soil throughout much of Thurston County is a glacial till that drains poorly once it's saturated. When a storm delivers two or three inches of rain in a few hours, not unusual from October through March, that water has nowhere to go quickly. It pools against foundation walls, infiltrates crawl spaces, and wicks up into subfloor assemblies before the storm has even passed.

Olympia's housing stock adds another layer of complexity. The city has a substantial number of homes built in the 1940s through the 1960s, many with original cedar siding, single-pane windows, and older roofing systems that were never designed to shed the kind of sustained horizontal rain that comes with a Puget Sound windstorm. Roof decking on those homes is often skip-sheathed, spaced boards rather than continuous plywood, which means wind-driven rain can penetrate even without a visible breach. Attic spaces in these homes can hold significant moisture for days before a homeowner notices staining on interior ceilings.

## Our Storm Damage Restoration Process in Olympia

When a storm call comes in, the first priority is stopping ongoing intrusion before any drying work begins. That means tarping and boarding compromised roof sections, securing broken windows, and identifying every point where water entered the structure, not just the obvious ones. Water travels, and in older Olympia homes with plaster-and-lath interior walls, it can migrate horizontally inside wall cavities well beyond where the entry point suggests.

Once the structure is stabilized, the team conducts a full moisture mapping using thermal imaging and calibrated meters. This step matters more in Olympia than in drier climates because ambient outdoor humidity during storm season routinely runs above 85 percent, standard drying timelines need to be adjusted, and equipment placement has to account for the fact that the air outside isn't helping you dry anything. Structural drying follows the IICRC S500 standard, with daily monitoring to confirm that assemblies are tracking toward documented dry goals rather than just feeling dry to the touch.

Tree impact damage, a frequent call after Olympia windstorms, requires coordinating debris removal, structural assessment, and weatherproofing in the right sequence. The crew is OSHA trained and carries the certifications to handle the full scope: IICRC WRT, ASD, and FSRT credentials cover the water intrusion, structural drying, and any fire or smoke component if a downed line was involved.

## Olympia Insurance Coordination

Storm damage claims in Washington are generally covered under the dwelling portion of a standard homeowner's policy, but the documentation requirements matter. Carriers want to see moisture readings, photographs of affected materials before any removal, and a clear chain of custody for what was found and when. Frontline documents losses with that standard in mind, moisture logs, thermal images, and itemized scope notes, so the file you hand your adjuster is complete rather than something they have to send back for supplements.

If your policy includes a separate wind or storm deductible, knowing that number before work begins helps everyone set realistic expectations. The team can walk through the documented scope with you before submission.

## Local Note

One thing that catches homeowners off guard in Olympia: the Douglas fir and big-leaf maple trees that make the city's older neighborhoods beautiful are also some of the most damaging trees in a windstorm. Firs in particular develop shallow root systems in the glacial till soil common throughout the area, which means a sustained 50-mph gust can topple a tree that looked perfectly healthy the day before. When a root ball pulls up, it often takes a section of foundation drainage or a buried downspout line with it, damage that isn't visible until water starts appearing in the crawl space weeks later. If a tree came down on or near your home, it's worth having the area around the root zone inspected even if the tree itself missed the structure.
