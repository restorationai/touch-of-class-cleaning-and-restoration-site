## Why Centralia Properties Face Distinct Fire Damage Challenges

A significant portion of Centralia's residential housing stock dates to the early and mid-twentieth century, craftsman bungalows, wood-lap-sided farmhouses, and two-story colonials built before modern fire-retardant framing standards. Older balloon-frame construction, common in homes built before the 1950s, has no fire blocking between wall cavities from the sill plate to the roof line. When a fire starts in a lower level, smoke and heat travel vertically through those open cavities and deposit soot in attic spaces, wall interiors, and ceiling voids that show no visible char from the outside. A room that looks lightly affected at first inspection can harbor heavy odor contamination two floors away.

The region's climate adds another layer. Lewis County averages over 50 inches of rainfall annually, and relative humidity stays elevated for much of the year. After a fire is extinguished, firefighting water combines with that ambient moisture to create conditions where secondary damage, mold colonization can begin within 24 to 48 hours of a water intrusion event, becomes a real concern on top of the fire loss itself. Restoration here means managing two simultaneous threats, not just one.

## Our Fire Damage Restoration Process in Centralia

The first priority on any fire loss is structural stabilization and safety assessment, confirming load-bearing elements are intact, boarding or tarping openings, and establishing a safe perimeter before any cleaning work begins. From there, the process moves through several distinct phases:

**Smoke and soot mapping** comes before any cleaning. Different combustion materials, synthetic carpet, older oil-based paint, treated lumber, leave different residue types that require different chemical approaches. Protein smoke from kitchen fires, for example, leaves an almost invisible film that causes persistent odor if treated with the wrong agent.

**Content pack-out and inventory** protects salvageable belongings. Items are catalogued, transported to a controlled environment for cleaning, and stored while structural work continues.

**Structural cleaning and deodorization** uses HEPA-filtered negative air machines to capture airborne particulates while technicians work through affected cavities. Thermal fogging and hydroxyl generation address odor at the molecular level rather than masking it.

**Controlled demolition** removes materials that cannot be restored, char-damaged framing, smoke-saturated insulation, compromised drywall, and prepares the structure for reconstruction. Frontline holds an EPA Lead-Safe certification, which matters in Centralia's older housing stock where lead paint in pre-1978 homes must be handled under specific containment protocols before any demo work begins.

## Local Note

Centralia's older downtown-adjacent neighborhoods include homes with plaster-and-lath wall systems rather than modern drywall. After a fire, plaster absorbs smoke odor differently than gypsum board, it's denser and less porous on the surface, which can make a room smell nearly normal within days, only for the odor to return weeks later as the lath behind the plaster continues off-gassing. Crews working in these homes need to probe behind the plaster surface before signing off on odor clearance, not just assess what's detectable at the face of the wall. It's a step that gets skipped by teams unfamiliar with this construction type, and it's one of the more common reasons fire-damaged homes in older Lewis County communities end up with recurring odor complaints after restoration is supposedly complete.
