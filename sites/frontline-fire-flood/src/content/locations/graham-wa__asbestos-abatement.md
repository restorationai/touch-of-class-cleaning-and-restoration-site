## Why Graham Properties See Asbestos Concerns

Pierce County's unincorporated communities, including Graham, grew quickly in the 1950s through the early 1980s as families moved south along the SR-161 corridor. Builders of that era routinely used asbestos-containing materials because they were affordable, fire-resistant, and widely available. Common sources in Graham homes include:

- **Popcorn (acoustic) ceilings** applied before 1980, which can contain up to 10% chrysotile asbestos by weight
- **9-inch and 12-inch vinyl floor tiles** and the black mastic adhesive beneath them
- **Pipe and duct wrap insulation** in crawl spaces and utility rooms
- **Drywall joint compound** used through the mid-1970s
- **Roofing shingles and siding panels** on homes built before federal restrictions tightened

Pierce County's heavy seasonal rainfall also matters here. Moisture intrusion, common in crawl spaces on the clay-heavy soils found throughout this part of the county, can degrade pipe insulation and floor tile adhesive over time, increasing the likelihood that previously stable asbestos-containing material becomes friable without obvious visual warning signs.

## Our Asbestos Abatement Process in Graham

Abatement is not a single step; it is a regulated sequence. Washington State Department of Labor & Industries governs asbestos work under WAC 296-62-077, and Pierce County requires notification for projects above certain thresholds before any demolition or renovation begins. The process Frontline Fire & Flood follows:

1. **Inspection and bulk sampling**, A technician collects material samples from suspect areas. Samples are sent to an accredited laboratory; results typically return within a few business days.
2. **Project notification**, For qualifying projects, we file the required notification with the appropriate state and county agencies before work begins.
3. **Containment setup**, Affected areas are sealed with poly sheeting and placed under negative air pressure using HEPA-filtered air scrubbers so fibers cannot migrate to clean spaces.
4. **Controlled removal**, Material is wetted to suppress fiber release, carefully removed, and double-bagged in labeled, puncture-resistant containers per state requirements.
5. **Disposal at a licensed facility**, Asbestos waste is transported to a facility permitted to accept regulated hazardous material, not a general construction debris site.
6. **Air clearance testing**, After removal and cleanup, air samples are collected and analyzed. The space is not released for reoccupancy or renovation until clearance is confirmed.

Every phase is documented with photographs and chain-of-custody records, which matter both for your peace of mind and for any future buyer's disclosure requirements.

## Graham Insurance & Permitting Coordination

Homeowner's insurance coverage for asbestos abatement depends heavily on the cause of the disturbance. Damage from a covered peril, a burst pipe that soaks and destroys asbestos-wrapped plumbing, for example, may trigger coverage; pre-existing deterioration typically does not.
