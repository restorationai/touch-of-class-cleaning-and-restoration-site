## Why Burien's Housing Stock Makes Asbestos a Real Concern

Burien grew substantially during the 1950s and 1960s, when asbestos was still a preferred additive for fireproofing, insulation, and texture. That era of construction left a dense footprint of single-family homes, small apartment buildings, and commercial strip properties where asbestos-containing materials are not the exception, they're the baseline assumption until testing proves otherwise.

The Pacific Northwest's persistent moisture compounds the problem. Wet winters and the humidity that settles in crawl spaces and attics accelerate the degradation of older building materials. Pipe wrap that was once firmly bonded can become friable, meaning it crumbles and releases fibers, after years of condensation cycling. Attic insulation that looks intact from a distance may be compromised where roof decking has absorbed repeated rain intrusion. In Burien's older bungalows and ranch-style homes, these conditions are common enough that any pre-1980 remodel should begin with a professional inspection before any demolition.

## Our Asbestos Abatement Process in Burien

Every project starts with a thorough inspection and sample collection by trained personnel. Suspect materials are identified visually and confirmed through accredited laboratory analysis, you receive written results before any abatement work begins. That documentation also matters if you're coordinating with a real estate transaction or pulling a permit through King County.

Once testing confirms the presence of asbestos-containing materials, the work area is isolated with poly sheeting and negative air pressure to prevent fiber migration to unaffected parts of the home. Regulated waste is wetted, double-bagged in labeled containers, and transported to a licensed disposal facility, Washington State requires specific manifesting for asbestos waste, and that paperwork stays in your file. After removal, air clearance sampling confirms fiber levels are within acceptable limits before containment comes down. The team holds EPA Lead-Safe certification and OSHA training, which matters in older Burien homes where lead paint and asbestos frequently coexist in the same wall assembly.
