Bonney Lake sits on a plateau between two river valleys, and when a windstorm rolls through the South King County foothills or a house fire leaves a roof open to the Pacific Northwest sky, the window for preventing secondary damage is measured in hours, not days. Rain doesn't wait for business hours here, and neither does Frontline Fire & Flood. ## Why Bonney Lake Properties Are Particularly Vulnerable After Structural Damage

Bonney Lake's position along the Cascade foothills corridor means it catches weather systems that have already gathered moisture over Puget Sound before climbing in elevation. Annual rainfall here is meaningful, but it's the shoulder-season storms, late October through March, that do the most damage after a structural breach. A roof opening that might stay dry for two days in Eastern Washington can absorb several inches of rain in the same window here.

The housing stock adds another layer of complexity. Much of Bonney Lake developed rapidly during the 1990s and early 2000s, meaning a large share of homes use OSB (oriented strand board) roof sheathing and engineered lumber framing. Unlike older plank sheathing, OSB delaminates quickly when it gets wet and stays wet, once moisture penetrates a compromised roof deck, the clock on structural degradation starts immediately. A properly secured poly tarp, weighted and fastened to undamaged framing rather than just draped over the peak, can mean the difference between a roof repair and a full deck replacement.

The city's hillside topography also matters. Properties on steeper lots, common throughout Bonney Lake's residential developments, can experience accelerated water intrusion because rainwater follows the slope directly into any opening at the roofline or wall plane.

## Our Emergency Board-Up and Tarping Process in Bonney Lake

When you call Frontline Fire & Flood at **253-200-0503**, dispatch begins immediately, our team operates around the clock. The process on-site follows a deliberate sequence:

**Assessment first.** Before any material goes up, a crew member walks the perimeter to identify all breach points, not just the obvious ones. Fire damage in particular creates secondary openings where heat has warped framing or melted flashing, and those gaps are easy to miss in the initial scan.

**Tarping for roof damage.** We use heavy-duty polyethylene tarps secured with batten boards screwed into undamaged structural members, not stapled to damaged sheathing that may not hold. On steeper pitches common in Bonney Lake's hillside neighborhoods, we use additional anchor points to prevent wind lift, a standard flat-ground installation can fail in the gusts that funnel through the valley corridors here.

**Board-up for windows, doors, and wall openings.** We cut plywood to fit and fasten it in a way that resists forced entry as well as weather. After a fire, this matters: an unsecured structure attracts unauthorized entry quickly, which creates liability issues and can complicate insurance claims.

**Documentation throughout.** Every opening is photographed before and after securing. That documentation goes directly to your insurance carrier.
