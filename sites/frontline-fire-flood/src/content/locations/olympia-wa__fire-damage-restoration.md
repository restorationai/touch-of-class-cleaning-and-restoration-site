Once a structure has been opened up by heat and suppression water, that ambient moisture moves in fast, into charred framing, saturated insulation, and smoke-coated surfaces, turning what started as a fire loss into a compounding problem that requires both fire and smoke restoration and aggressive moisture management from the first hour on site. ## Why Olympia Properties See Distinct Fire Damage Challenges

Thurston County's housing stock spans a wide range of eras, and that range matters when a fire moves through a home. Neighborhoods closer to the original city core, areas developed in the early to mid-twentieth century, frequently feature balloon-frame construction, where wall cavities run continuously from the basement sill plate to the roof rafters with no fire blocking in between. In a balloon-frame home, smoke and heat travel vertically through those open cavities faster than in platform-frame construction, meaning fire damage can appear in the attic or upper floor even when the visible burn is confined to a ground-floor room.

Olympia's older residential areas also commonly have original fir or cedar framing, plaster-and-lath walls, and knob-and-tube or early aluminum wiring, all of which affect how a fire spreads, how smoke deposits, and what restoration methods are appropriate. Plaster walls, for example, hold soot differently than drywall: the porous surface traps fine particulate deeper into the material, which means chemical sponging and dry-ice blasting often outperform standard wiping on those substrates.

Beyond the building stock, Olympia's position as the state capital means some commercial and mixed-use properties are subject to Washington State Department of Enterprise Services oversight or historic preservation review, factors that can shape material choices and scope-of-work documentation during restoration.

## Our Fire Damage Restoration Process in Olympia

Every fire loss is different, but the sequence of decisions follows a disciplined framework. When we arrive, the first priority is structural safety assessment and utility isolation, confirming that gas has been shut off at the meter and that the electrical panel is safe to approach. In Puget Sound Energy territory, which serves most of Olympia, we coordinate directly with the utility when a meter pull or reconnect is needed as part of the restoration timeline.

From there, the process moves through:

- **Emergency board-up and tarping** to close openings left by broken windows, burned-through roofing, or suppression-water entry points, critical in Olympia's wet season, when an unprotected structure can absorb significant additional moisture within 24 hours.
- **Moisture mapping and extraction** of suppression water before it migrates into subfloor assemblies or wall cavities.
- **Controlled demolition** of unsalvageable materials, documented photographically for insurance purposes.
- **Smoke and soot removal** using HEPA vacuuming, dry-chemical sponging, and thermal fogging or hydroxyl treatment depending on the odor profile and occupancy timeline.
- **Structural drying** of any water-affected framing, verified with calibrated moisture meters before reconstruction begins.
- **Reconstruction coordination**, scoped to match pre-loss condition and current Washington State building code requirements.

EPA Lead-Safe certification is part of our credential set, relevant in Olympia's older housing stock, where disturbing painted surfaces during fire cleanup can trigger lead-safe work practice requirements under Washington State Department of Labor & Industries rules.

## Local Note

Olympia's frequent overcast and the way marine air settles into low-lying areas near Capitol Lake and the inlet means that even in summer, post-fire structures dry more slowly than they would in eastern Washington or a drier climate. We account for this by running dehumidification longer than national baseline drying standards might suggest, and by checking ambient relative humidity against outdoor conditions before we close out the drying phase. A structure that reads within acceptable moisture content on a dry afternoon can rebound overnight when marine air moves back in. That extra verification step matters in this climate.
