## Why Lacey Properties See Water Damage Differently

The Pacific Northwest's wet season runs long, and Lacey's position between the Capitol State Forest and the Puget Sound basin means the ground is often already saturated before a heavy rain event or a plumbing failure adds more water to the equation. Clay subsoils retain moisture, which slows drainage away from foundations and crawl spaces. Homes built in Lacey's older residential corridors frequently have vented crawl spaces rather than encapsulated ones, making them especially vulnerable to groundwater intrusion and vapor migration after a loss.

Newer construction in areas like the Hawks Prairie corridor tends to have concrete slab foundations, which carry their own set of complications, water wicks laterally under slab and can travel 10 to 15 feet from the source before surfacing, making the visible wet spot a poor indicator of actual scope. Regardless of construction era, the combination of a wet climate, slow-draining soils, and varied foundation types means water cleanup in Lacey requires careful moisture mapping before any drying equipment is placed.

## Our Water Cleanup Process in Lacey

Every job begins with a full inspection using thermal imaging and calibrated moisture meters, not a visual sweep. Water behind plaster, under LVP flooring, or inside wall cavities reads dry at the surface long before it actually is. Once we know where the water went, we extract standing water with truck-mounted and portable extraction units, then establish a drying system engineered to the actual readings, not a generic room count.

Structural drying in Lacey's climate requires attention to outdoor humidity levels. When ambient relative humidity is high, which it often is from October through April, simply opening windows does more harm than good. Our IICRC-certified technicians (Water Damage Restoration Technician and Applied Structural Drying credentials) set up negative-pressure drying chambers and use desiccant or refrigerant dehumidifiers matched to the psychrometric conditions of the specific loss. Daily monitoring logs track temperature, humidity, and material moisture content until every reading meets the IICRC S500 drying standard.

If building materials, drywall, insulation, subfloor, have absorbed water beyond the threshold for in-place drying, we document the scope for your insurance adjuster before any demolition begins. Nothing is removed without a record.

## Local Note

Lacey's growth over the past two decades has produced a large number of homes with engineered wood subfloor, OSB rather than traditional plywood. OSB is more moisture-sensitive than plywood and begins to swell and delaminate faster once it gets wet. In a crawl-space intrusion or a dishwasher leak that sits overnight, OSB subfloor can reach the point of structural compromise before the surface flooring shows any visible damage. When we respond to water calls in Lacey, we probe subfloor material type early in the inspection, because the drying timeline and the threshold for replacement are different than they would be in a home with plywood or concrete.
