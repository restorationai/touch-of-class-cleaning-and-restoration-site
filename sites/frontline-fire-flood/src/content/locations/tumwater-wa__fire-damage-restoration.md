## Why Tumwater Properties Present Specific Fire Damage Challenges

Tumwater's housing stock spans several distinct eras. The older neighborhoods near the original townsite include homes built in the mid-20th century, structures with wood-lath plaster walls, older balloon-frame construction, and plumbing and electrical systems that haven't always been updated to current code. In a fire, balloon framing is particularly unforgiving: the open vertical cavities between exterior walls allow flames and superheated gases to travel floor to floor with almost no resistance, meaning fire damage can extend well beyond the room of origin.

Beyond construction type, Tumwater's position in Thurston County means that many properties sit on soils with high clay content near low-lying areas. After a structure fire, firefighting water doesn't always drain quickly from crawl spaces and basements. That standing water, combined with smoke-saturated building materials, creates an environment where secondary mold colonization can begin in as little as 24 to 48 hours, turning a fire loss into a compound claim if restoration doesn't start promptly.

Local utility infrastructure is another factor. Puget Sound Energy serves most of Tumwater, and after a significant structural fire, PSE typically requires a safety inspection before power is restored. That inspection timeline affects when crews can safely run drying and dehumidification equipment on-site, and experienced restoration contractors plan around it rather than being surprised by it.

## Our Fire Damage Restoration Process in Tumwater

Every fire loss is different, but the sequence of decisions follows a consistent logic calibrated to the property and the local conditions.

**Emergency stabilization** comes first, boarding windows and doors, tarping the roof if the fire breached it, and controlling access to the structure. In Tumwater's rainy season, an unsecured roof opening can introduce enough additional moisture overnight to double the scope of structural drying needed.

**Assessment and documentation** follows immediately. Our team photographs and measures all affected areas, documents smoke and soot migration patterns, and identifies materials that can be restored versus those that require removal. This documentation is formatted for insurance carriers and serves as the foundation of your claim.

**Structural cleaning and deodorization** addresses the soot and smoke residue that penetrates porous surfaces, drywall, insulation, wood framing, and contents. We use dry chemical sponges for the initial pass on walls and ceilings, then apply hydroxyl or thermal fogging treatments to neutralize odor molecules embedded in the structure. In older Tumwater homes with plaster walls, this step requires more care: plaster is more alkaline than modern drywall, and aggressive chemical cleaners can cause surface damage that creates additional restoration work.

**Structural drying and moisture control** runs concurrently with cleaning. Firefighting water saturates materials that the fire itself may not have touched, and those materials need to be dried to IICRC S500 standards before any reconstruction begins.

**Reconstruction coordination** closes the project, framing, insulation, drywall, paint, and finish work brought back to pre-loss condition, managed under our Washington contractor license (#CCFRONTFF761CB).
