## Why Waller Properties See Water Damage Issues

Like much of western Washington, Waller sits under a weather pattern that delivers long stretches of steady rain rather than short, intense storms. That pattern matters for water damage because it means soil around foundations stays saturated for weeks at a time, which raises hydrostatic pressure against basement and crawl space walls even on properties with no obvious drainage problem. Older homes built with crawl space foundations, common throughout Washington towns settled before modern vapor barrier codes, are especially prone to slow, hidden moisture intrusion that homeowners don't notice until they smell it or see it surface through flooring. Add in aging galvanized or early-generation copper supply lines in houses from past decades, and a pinhole leak behind a wall can run for days before anyone catches it.

## Our Water Damage Restoration Process in Waller

We start with extraction, pulling standing water with truck-mounted or portable pumps depending on how much volume we're dealing with and how accessible the affected area is. From there we move to moisture mapping: thermal imaging and penetrating meters tell us exactly how far water traveled behind walls, under subfloor, and into insulation, because what looks dry on the surface is frequently still wet two inches deeper. Structural drying follows, using calibrated air movers and dehumidifiers set to pull moisture out at a rate the materials can actually release it, which for older plaster or dense old-growth lumber framing common in this area takes longer than it would in newer construction. We monitor daily with the same meters used at intake, and we don't call a job finished until readings confirm the structure is back to a normal moisture range, not just visually dry.
