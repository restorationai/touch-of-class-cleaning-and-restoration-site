## Why Tacoma Properties See Water Damage So Often

Tacoma sits on a glacially carved landscape where clay-heavy soils drain slowly and hydrostatic pressure builds against foundation walls every wet season. That geology, combined with average annual rainfall that arrives mostly as persistent drizzle rather than dramatic downpours, keeps ground saturation high from October through April. Homes in the Hilltop and North End neighborhoods, many of them built between the 1910s and 1940s, were constructed before modern vapor barriers and waterproof membrane systems existed. Crawl spaces in those older structures often have bare-earth floors and minimal ventilation, creating conditions where ambient humidity alone can push wood framing moisture content above the threshold where mold colonization begins, typically within 48 to 72 hours of a water intrusion event.

The city's older plumbing stock adds another layer of risk. Galvanized steel supply lines, still present in a meaningful share of pre-1960 housing, corrode from the inside out and can fail without visible warning. When they do, water moves fast through wall cavities and under flooring before anyone hears a drip.

## Our Water Damage Restoration Process in Tacoma

Every job starts with a thorough moisture assessment using thermal imaging and calibrated pin-and-pinless meters, not a visual inspection alone. Standing water is extracted first with truck-mounted vacuum units capable of handling the volume that a failed sump or a broken main can produce. Once bulk water is removed, the real work begins: identifying where moisture has migrated.

In Tacoma's older housing stock, that often means checking beneath fir hardwood floors, inside plaster-and-lath wall assemblies, and along rim joists in unfinished crawl spaces. Plaster releases absorbed moisture more slowly than modern drywall, so drying timelines in pre-WWII homes typically run longer, and we calibrate our drying system, commercial-grade desiccant or refrigerant dehumidifiers paired with high-velocity air movers, to the actual material, not a one-size schedule. Moisture readings are logged daily and shared with you and your adjuster throughout the drying period.

All work follows the IICRC S500 standard for water damage restoration. Because Frontline is also EPA Lead-Safe Certified, any work that disturbs painted surfaces in homes built before 1978, common throughout Tacoma's historic neighborhoods, is handled with the required containment and documentation protocols.

## Local Note

Tacoma's North End contains a significant concentration of homes with original fir subfloor over unconditioned crawl spaces, a construction detail that matters during drying. Fir is denser than modern engineered wood and holds moisture longer, but it also tolerates careful, controlled drying better than OSB or particleboard, which can swell and delaminate quickly. The practical difference: we run drying equipment longer in these homes, but the floors are often salvageable where a newer material might not be.
