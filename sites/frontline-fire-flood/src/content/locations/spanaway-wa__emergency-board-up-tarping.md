## Our Emergency Board-Up and Tarping Process in Spanaway

When a call comes in, the first priority is a rapid site assessment to identify every breach, not just the obvious one. Fire damage board-up and broken window board-up calls often have secondary openings: a melted vent screen, a door frame warped by heat, a skylight cracked by falling debris. Missing one means the structure is still exposed.

For window and door openings, we use 3/4-inch CDX plywood cut and fastened to the structural framing, not the trim, so the board stays in place under pressure. For roof openings, we use reinforced polyethylene tarps rated for high-wind applications, secured with weighted lumber battens and screwed through the tarp grommets into the decking, not draped over the ridge and weighted with sandbags, which is a temporary measure that fails in the first serious wind event.

On Spanaway's older homes with aging fascia and soffit boards, we assess the attachment surface before committing to a fastener pattern. Rotted wood will not hold a screw under load, and we adapt the approach, sometimes sister-framing a new nailing surface, rather than creating a false sense of security with hardware that will pull free.
