## Our Water Damage Restoration Process in University Place

The first thing the crew does on arrival is moisture mapping, not a visual scan, but a systematic read with thermal imaging and calibrated moisture meters across walls, subfloor assemblies, and ceiling cavities. Water follows the path of least resistance, and in a two-story home on a sloped lot, that path often ends somewhere unexpected.

Once the scope is documented, water extraction begins using truck-mounted and portable units sized to the loss. Standing water in a finished basement comes out first; then the focus shifts to trapped moisture in materials. Structural drying follows a calculated protocol aligned with the IICRC S500 standard: industrial air movers and desiccant or refrigerant dehumidifiers are positioned based on the psychrometric conditions in the space, not just placed and left. Daily monitoring adjusts the drying system as materials release moisture over time. Nothing is signed off until meter readings confirm the structure has returned to baseline moisture levels for this region's climate.

When flooring or wall materials need to come out to allow drying, the crew documents removal carefully, both for the insurance claim and because many University Place homes contain building materials that require EPA Lead-Safe handling protocols.
