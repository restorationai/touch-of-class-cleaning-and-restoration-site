## Our Emergency Board-Up and Tarping Process in Fife

When you call, a crew mobilizes from our Lakewood headquarters, a short run up I-5 or Pacific Highway South depending on traffic and time of day. On arrival, the first priority is a rapid damage assessment: identifying every breach point, evaluating structural stability before anyone works at elevation, and photographing the full scope for your insurance carrier.

Board-up work uses plywood cut and fitted to the opening, secured with screws rather than nails so it can be removed cleanly during reconstruction without additional damage to the frame. For fire-damaged structures, we pay particular attention to window frames and door jambs that may have warped, a board that looks flush can leave a gap if the frame has racked, so we check for that before signing off.

Roof tarping on Fife properties follows a similar discipline. Tarps are weighted and anchored at the ridge and rafter lines rather than simply draped and tied at the eaves, which is the difference between a tarp that holds through a Puget Sound windstorm and one that peels back by morning. Where roof sheathing is compromised, we add a layer of protection at the deck before the tarp goes down.

Every opening is documented, before and after, with photos and written notes that become part of the insurance claim package.
