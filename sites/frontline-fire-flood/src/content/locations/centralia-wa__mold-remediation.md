When moisture works its way into a crawl space, behind old plaster, or through a compromised roof, mold doesn't wait, under the right humidity and temperature, colonies can establish within 24 to 48 hours. ## Why Centralia Properties See Mold Issues

The geography here matters. Centralia occupies low-lying ground along the Chehalis River corridor, and Lewis County's rainfall totals rank among the higher figures in western Washington. That sustained moisture load, months of drizzle rather than dramatic downpours, keeps relative humidity elevated inside structures that weren't built with modern vapor barriers or mechanical ventilation.

Centralia's housing stock skews older. A significant share of the city's residential neighborhoods contain homes built between the 1910s and 1960s, many with full or partial basements, brick or wood-framed exterior walls, and crawl spaces that predate modern ground-cover vapor barriers. In homes of that era, rim joists and subfloor sheathing are often the first places mold colonizes after a plumbing leak or a wet winter season, and those areas are invisible to a homeowner doing a casual walk-through. Older homes in this part of Lewis County also frequently have single-pane windows and minimal attic ventilation, which drives condensation onto interior surfaces every time outdoor temperatures drop.

The Chehalis River flooding history adds another layer. Properties that have taken on any floodwater, even a shallow event that receded quickly, carry elevated risk for mold in wall cavities and beneath flooring, because floodwater carries organic material that feeds mold growth long after the visible water is gone.

## Our Mold Remediation Process in Centralia

Every job starts with a thorough inspection rather than an assumption. We use moisture meters, thermal imaging, and air sampling to map where moisture is elevated and where mold is actively growing versus where it has dried but left behind spores and staining. In Centralia's older wood-framed homes, that often means checking inside wall cavities at plumbing penetrations, inspecting crawl space joists and insulation, and testing attic sheathing near any area where the roof has a history of leaks.

Once we understand the scope, we establish physical containment, negative air pressure barriers with HEPA-filtered air scrubbers, to prevent spores from migrating to unaffected areas of the home during removal. Affected materials are removed, bagged, and disposed of per Washington State Department of Ecology guidelines. Structural surfaces that can be cleaned rather than removed are treated with antimicrobial agents appropriate for the substrate. We document every step with photographs, which matters when an insurance claim is involved.

Drying is not optional after remediation. Mold returns if the underlying moisture source isn't corrected and the structure isn't brought to acceptable moisture content. We verify drying with post-remediation readings before closing out the containment.

## Local Note

In Centralia's older downtown-adjacent residential blocks, many homes were built with true plaster-and-lath wall systems rather than drywall. Plaster reads differently on a moisture meter than modern gypsum board, the dense substrate can show acceptable surface readings while the wood lath behind it remains saturated and actively growing mold. We probe at multiple depths in these homes rather than relying on surface scans alone, and we factor in longer drying times because plaster releases absorbed moisture more slowly than drywall.
