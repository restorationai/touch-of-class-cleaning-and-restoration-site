## Why Bremerton Properties See Asbestos Concerns

Kitsap County's wet, marine climate accelerates the deterioration of building materials in ways that don't happen in drier inland regions. Persistent moisture works into older construction assemblies, causing pipe wrap to crack, vinyl floor backing to delaminate, and ceiling texture to crumble, all of which can disturb asbestos-containing materials (ACM) that were otherwise stable. Homes built between roughly 1940 and 1980 are the highest-risk category, and Bremerton has a dense concentration of them. The post-war housing push tied to Puget Sound Naval Shipyard brought rapid residential construction, and builders of that era had few alternatives to asbestos-laden products.

Popcorn ceilings are among the most common finds in mid-century Bremerton homes. Spray-applied acoustic texture was widely used through the late 1970s, and a significant portion of it contains chrysotile asbestos. The texture looks intact until it doesn't, water intrusion from a leaking roof or a plumbing failure overhead can cause it to sag and release fibers. Disturbing it without testing first is a regulatory violation under Washington State Department of Labor & Industries rules, and it creates a genuine health risk for anyone in the space.

## Our Asbestos Abatement Process in Bremerton

Every project starts with inspection and bulk sampling, physical collection of material specimens sent to an accredited laboratory. We do not guess based on appearance. Once results confirm the presence of ACM, the scope of work is defined: full removal, encapsulation, or a combination depending on the material type, its condition, and what the renovation or repair requires.

Containment is built before any disturbance begins. Negative air pressure is established using HEPA-filtered air scrubbers to prevent fiber migration into adjacent rooms. Workers wear full-face respirators and disposable protective suits. Removed material is wetted to suppress fibers, sealed in labeled poly bags, and transported to a licensed disposal facility, in Washington, that means a facility permitted to accept regulated asbestos waste under state and federal guidelines.

Final air clearance testing is performed after cleanup and before containment is removed. This step is not optional, it's the documented proof that fiber levels have returned to acceptable levels. Frontline Fire & Flood is EPA Lead-Safe Certified and OSHA trained, and our crews follow the IICRC framework for hazardous material handling throughout the process.
