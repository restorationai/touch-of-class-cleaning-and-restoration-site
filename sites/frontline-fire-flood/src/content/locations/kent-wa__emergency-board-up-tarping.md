Kent sits in the Green River Valley, where Pacific storms roll in off Puget Sound and stall against the Cascade foothills, dropping rain in sustained sheets that can turn a broken window or a fire-damaged roof into a cascading water problem within hours. When a structure is compromised, whether from a house fire, a windstorm, a vehicle impact, or a vandalism break-in, the window between the initial damage and secondary water intrusion is narrow. Frontline Fire & Flood responds 24/7 to board up openings and tarp damaged rooflines across Kent, stopping that clock before the interior becomes a mold and structural problem on top of whatever already happened.

## Why Kent Properties Are Particularly Vulnerable After Structural Damage

Kent's building stock spans a wide range of eras and construction types. The older residential neighborhoods west of the valley floor include homes built in the 1950s and 1960s with wood-framed walls, single-pane windows, and rooflines that were never designed with modern weather barriers in mind. When a fire burns through an attic or a windstorm peels back shingles, those structures have very little redundancy, the exterior envelope is the only line of defense, and once it's breached, interior materials absorb moisture fast.

The valley floor itself adds another layer of complexity. Kent's low-lying commercial corridors and industrial areas sit on alluvial soils with a relatively high water table, meaning that even a modest rainstorm can push water laterally into an already-compromised structure. A fire-damaged warehouse or a flood-affected commercial building with open wall cavities can take on ground-level moisture from multiple directions simultaneously. Board-up and tarping in this environment isn't just about keeping rain out from above, it's about sealing the structure against moisture pressure from the surrounding soil and drainage patterns as well.

## Our Emergency Board-Up and Tarping Process in Kent

When you call, the first question we ask is what type of damage created the opening, because that determines the boarding and tarping approach. A fire-damaged roof with compromised structural members gets tarped differently than a clean wind-damaged section where the decking is still sound. A broken storefront window in a commercial building needs different fastening than a residential window frame that's been warped by heat.

Our crew arrives with pre-cut plywood stock, heavy-duty polyethylene tarps rated for sustained Pacific Northwest rainfall, and the fastening hardware appropriate for the substrate, masonry anchors for concrete block commercial buildings, structural screws for wood-framed homes. We assess the opening, check the surrounding framing for load-bearing integrity before anyone goes on a roof, and install protection that's meant to hold through multiple storm cycles, not just the next 24 hours. Every installation is photographed systematically for your insurance claim file before and after.

For roof tarping specifically, we use ballasted and fastened systems rather than simple drape-and-weight methods. Kent's wind events, particularly the gap-effect gusts that funnel through the valley, can strip a loosely tarped roof overnight. A tarp that fails at 2 a.m. during a rainstorm creates exactly the secondary damage the initial response was supposed to prevent.

## Local Note

Kent's East Hill plateau, the elevated residential area rising east of the valley, has a concentration of homes built during the 1970s and 1980s with T1-11 plywood siding, a material that was common in that era and that region. T1-11 is grooved vertically, which means water tracks down the grooves and into any gap at window or door penetrations faster than it would on smooth siding. When a window is broken or a door frame is damaged on one of these homes, the siding itself can wick moisture into the wall cavity within a single rain event. Board-up on T1-11 structures requires careful attention to the perimeter seal, just covering the opening isn't enough if the siding channels water around the edges of the plywood patch.
