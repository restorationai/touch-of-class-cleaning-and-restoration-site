## Why Port Orchard Properties See Sewage Backup Issues

Kitsap County's mix of municipal sewer service and private septic systems creates two distinct failure points. Properties on the older sections of Bay Street and the hillside neighborhoods above the waterfront were often built before modern sewer infrastructure reached the area, meaning some still rely on aging septic tanks and drain fields. When those drain fields become saturated, which happens regularly during the wet season from October through March, sewage can surface in crawl spaces, back up through floor drains, or seep through foundation walls.

Properties connected to the municipal system face a different risk: the region's clay-heavy soils expand when wet, which puts lateral pressure on older clay-tile and cast-iron sewer lines. Root intrusion and pipe offset are common in homes built before the 1980s, and a single blockage can push raw sewage back through every low-lying fixture in the house. The problem is especially pronounced in homes with unfinished crawl spaces, where standing sewage can sit undetected long enough for secondary mold colonization to begin.

## Our Sewage Cleanup and Sanitization Process in Port Orchard

Sewage cleanup is classified as a Category 3 water loss, the most contaminated type, and the process reflects that. When we arrive, the first step is containment: isolating the affected area to prevent cross-contamination to unaffected rooms or HVAC systems. We extract all standing sewage and remove saturated materials, flooring, insulation, drywall, that cannot be adequately disinfected in place.

After bulk removal, every affected surface is treated with EPA-registered disinfectants rated for sewage contamination. We don't skip this step or abbreviate it based on how the space looks; pathogens are not visible. Structural drying follows, using commercial dehumidifiers and air movers calibrated to the ambient conditions, in Port Orchard's high-humidity coastal climate, drying times can run longer than they would in an inland or arid environment, and we monitor moisture readings daily until the structure reaches its target drying goal per the IICRC S500 standard. Throughout the process, we document affected materials, moisture readings, and remediation steps for your insurance file.
