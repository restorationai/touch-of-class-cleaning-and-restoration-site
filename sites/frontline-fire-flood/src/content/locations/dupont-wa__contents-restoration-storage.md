DuPont sits at the edge of Joint Base Lewis-McChord, where the damp maritime air rolling off Puget Sound keeps relative humidity elevated for much of the year, and when a pipe bursts or a kitchen fire chars your cabinets, that persistent moisture becomes an immediate threat to everything inside your home. Contents restoration and storage is the part of recovery most people don't think about until they're watching a crew carry their grandmother's china out the door: the careful cleaning, deodorizing, and climate-controlled storage of the belongings that make a house worth saving in the first place. Frontline Fire & Flood responds around the clock from its Lakewood base, handling the full arc of contents care so you can focus on the structure.

## Why DuPont Properties See Elevated Contents Risk

DuPont's housing stock is heavily influenced by the planned community development that reshaped the city in the 1990s, a mix of production-built single-family homes with open floor plans, vaulted ceilings, and engineered wood components that respond poorly to sustained moisture. When a supply line fails behind a wall or a roof leak goes unnoticed through a wet Pierce County winter, water doesn't just soak the drywall. It wicks into laminate flooring, saturates upholstered furniture, and migrates along subfloor sheathing into rooms that appear untouched. Smoke from a kitchen or garage fire travels the same path in reverse, coating soft goods in a fine layer of soot that oxidizes fabric fibers within hours if left untreated.

The proximity to JBLM also means a significant share of DuPont households are renters or frequent movers, families who may have stored items in garages or detached outbuildings that lack climate control. Those spaces are especially vulnerable to mold colonization after a water event, because condensation cycles between cool nights and warm afternoons create ideal conditions for spore growth within 24 to 48 hours of saturation.

## Our Contents Restoration & Storage Process in DuPont

When we arrive at a DuPont home, the first step is a room-by-room contents inventory before anything is moved. Every item is photographed, logged by category, and assessed for restorability, a distinction that matters enormously for insurance documentation. Salvageable items are packed using materials rated for long-term storage and transported to our climate-controlled facility.

At the facility, contents go through a triage process tailored to the loss type:

- **Water-damaged goods** are dried using desiccant chambers or air-drying racks, then assessed for secondary damage like mold or delamination.
- **Smoke and soot-affected items** are cleaned using ultrasonic technology for hard goods and ozone or hydroxyl treatment for soft goods and electronics, methods that neutralize odor at the molecular level rather than masking it.
- **Mold-affected contents** are handled by our IICRC AMRT-certified technicians under containment protocols, with items evaluated individually for safe restoration versus disposal.

Once the structure is cleared and rebuilt, contents are returned, unpacked, and placed back in their original rooms based on the inventory photos.

## DuPont Insurance & Coordination

Contents claims are often the most contested part of a property loss settlement. Carriers want itemized documentation; adjusters push back on replacement values for electronics, furniture, and collectibles. Our team produces a line-item inventory with photographs and condition notes at pack-out, the same format most major carriers require, and we communicate directly with your adjuster throughout the process. If your policy includes additional living expense (ALE) coverage, we can also help document the storage period so that cost is properly captured in the claim.
