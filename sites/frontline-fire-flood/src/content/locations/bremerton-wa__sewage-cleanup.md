## Why Bremerton Properties See Sewage Backup Issues

Bremerton's sewer system includes lines that date back to the mid-twentieth century, and the city's hilly topography means gravity-fed laterals in lower neighborhoods can experience backpressure during heavy rainfall events, a pattern the Pacific Northwest sees regularly from October through March. When storm runoff overwhelms combined or aging sewer mains, the path of least resistance is often up through floor drains, toilets, or washing machine standpipes.

Homes built before the 1970s, a significant share of Bremerton's residential stock, frequently have cast-iron or clay-tile sewer laterals. These materials are prone to root intrusion from the region's mature tree canopy and can develop offset joints over decades of soil movement. A partial blockage in a lateral can go undetected until a heavy-use day or a rain event tips it into a full backup. Properties near the waterfront or in lower-elevation areas of the city face the added challenge of a water table that rises seasonally, which can complicate drying after a sewage event if subfloor cavities have taken on moisture.

Septic systems are less common within Bremerton's city limits but do appear on larger or older parcels at the city's edges. A septic overflow carries the same Category 3 contamination risk as a municipal sewer backup and requires the same level of PPE, containment, and sanitization protocol.

## Our Sewage Cleanup and Sanitization Process in Bremerton

Every sewage job starts with containment, isolating the affected area to prevent cross-contamination to adjacent rooms or HVAC systems. Our IICRC-certified technicians arrive with full PPE, establish a decontamination zone, and begin extracting standing sewage using truck-mounted and portable extraction units capable of handling solid-laden water.

Once bulk extraction is complete, we remove and bag all unsalvageable porous materials, saturated drywall, subfloor sections, insulation, and flooring, following Washington State Department of Ecology guidelines for biohazardous waste disposal. Hard surfaces are then treated with EPA-registered disinfectants rated for Category 3 contamination, applied in multiple passes with appropriate dwell times. We don't skip the dwell time; contact-kill chemistry only works when the product stays wet on the surface long enough to neutralize pathogens.

Structural drying follows using commercial desiccant and refrigerant dehumidifiers alongside high-velocity air movers. In Bremerton's cooler, high-humidity climate, ambient conditions can slow evaporation significantly, our equipment is sized to overcome that, and we monitor moisture readings daily with calibrated meters until structural materials reach regional equilibrium moisture content. Documentation of readings is provided throughout, which your insurance adjuster will need.
