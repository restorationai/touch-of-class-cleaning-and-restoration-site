## Why DuPont Properties Are Particularly Vulnerable After a Fire

DuPont's residential development history is unlike most Pierce County cities. Much of the housing stock was built in concentrated phases tied to master-planned community development, which means large sections of the city share similar construction methods, insulation types, and HVAC configurations. Open-plan layouts and shared attic spaces, common in planned community builds, allow smoke to travel farther and faster than in compartmentalized older homes. By the time a fire is contained to one room, smoke odor and fine particulate may have already penetrated adjacent spaces, ductwork, and wall cavities.

The region's climate adds another layer. Western Washington's cool, damp air causes smoke particles to bond more stubbornly to porous surfaces like drywall, carpet backing, and wood framing. What looks like light smoke staining on the surface often means deeper penetration into the substrate, something that becomes apparent only when materials are tested or probed.

## Our Fire Damage Restoration Process in DuPont

Every fire loss is different, but the sequence matters. When Frontline Fire & Flood arrives, the first priority is a thorough scope, not just the burn zone, but every room that shares an air pathway with it. Smoke follows pressure differentials, and in DuPont's newer construction with tighter building envelopes, that can mean contamination in rooms that look completely untouched.

From there, the process moves through:

- **Emergency board-up and tarping** to secure the structure against the region's frequent rain events, which can compound fire damage quickly if the roof or walls are breached
- **Controlled demolition** of char-damaged and smoke-saturated materials that cannot be restored, documented in detail for your insurance claim
- **HEPA vacuuming and dry-chemical sponging** of soot from ceilings, walls, and structural members before any wet cleaning begins (wet-cleaning soot before dry removal drives it deeper into surfaces)
- **Thermal fogging and hydroxyl treatment** for smoke odor neutralization throughout affected spaces
- **Structural drying** if firefighting water is present, our IICRC-certified team holds credentials in both fire and smoke restoration (FSRT) and structural drying (ASD), so both problems are handled under one scope
- **Reconstruction coordination** once the structure is clean, dry, and cleared

All documentation, photos, moisture readings, material inventories, is formatted to meet insurance adjuster requirements from the first day on-site.
