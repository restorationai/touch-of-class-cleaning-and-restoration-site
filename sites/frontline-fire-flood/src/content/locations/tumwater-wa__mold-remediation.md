## Our Mold Remediation Process in Tumwater

Mold remediation isn't cleaning, it's a controlled removal and containment process, and the steps matter. When a crew arrives at a Tumwater property, the first priority is moisture mapping. Infrared cameras and penetrating moisture meters identify where water has been, not just where it is now. Mold follows water, and water in a Tumwater crawl space or behind a plaster wall can travel several feet from the original source before it stops.

Once the affected area is mapped, the crew establishes negative air pressure containment, plastic sheeting, zipper doors, and HEPA-filtered air scrubbers running continuously to prevent spores from migrating to unaffected rooms. Affected porous materials (drywall, insulation, subfloor sections) are removed, bagged, and disposed of per Washington State Department of Ecology guidelines. Structural framing that can be retained is treated with an EPA-registered antimicrobial agent and dried to the IICRC S520 standard before any encapsulation or rebuilding begins.

Frontline Fire & Flood holds IICRC AMRT (Applied Microbial Remediation Technician) certification, which means the process follows a defined standard, not a general cleaning protocol. The company is also EPA Lead-Safe Certified, which matters specifically in Tumwater's older housing stock where lead paint on window trim or door casings can be disturbed during mold-related demo work.

## Tumwater Insurance & Coordination

Mold claims in Washington are handled differently depending on whether the mold is tied to a sudden covered loss (a burst pipe, a storm-driven roof leak) or to long-term moisture intrusion.
