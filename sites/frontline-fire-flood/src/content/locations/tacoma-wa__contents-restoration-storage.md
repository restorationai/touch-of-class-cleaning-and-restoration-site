## Why Tacoma Properties See Contents Damage Differently

The housing stock across Tacoma skews older than many people expect. Craftsman bungalows, brick-clad foursquares, and mid-century ranchers make up a significant share of the city's residential inventory, and the contents inside them often reflect that history, hardwood furniture, wool rugs, upholstered pieces that have been in families for decades. These materials are more porous and more sensitive to humidity swings than modern composites, which means smoke residue and water damage penetrate deeper and faster.

Fire damage compounds this. Smoke from a kitchen fire in an older Tacoma home doesn't just coat surfaces, it migrates into wall cavities, settles into fabric, and bonds chemically with wood finishes. The acidic residue begins etching metals and degrading upholstery within hours. Getting contents out of the affected environment and into a controlled-climate facility is often the single most important step in determining what can be saved.
