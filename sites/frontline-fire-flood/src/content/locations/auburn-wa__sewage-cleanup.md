Raw sewage carries bacteria, viruses, and parasites that colonize porous materials within hours. ## Why Auburn Properties See Sewage Backup Issues

The Green River valley floor that runs through much of Auburn is underlain by alluvial soils, loose, water-bearing sediments deposited over centuries of river activity. During heavy Pacific Northwest rain events, that soil profile becomes saturated quickly, raising the local water table and putting lateral pressure on older clay-tile and cast-iron sewer laterals. When a lateral cracks or collapses under that pressure, the sewage has nowhere to go but back through the lowest fixture in the house, typically a basement floor drain, a ground-floor toilet, or a laundry standpipe.

Auburn also has a significant inventory of homes built in the 1950s through 1970s, many of them on the west side of the city closer to the Auburn Municipal Airport corridor and the older residential streets near downtown. The sewer laterals on those properties are approaching or past their functional lifespan. A lateral that looked fine on last year's inspection can fail during the first major storm of fall. Properties on larger lots further east, particularly those still on private septic systems, face a different but equally urgent risk: drain field saturation during the wet season that pushes effluent back toward the structure.

## Our Sewage Cleanup and Sanitization Process in Auburn

Sewage is classified as Category 3 water, the most contaminated class, and the cleanup protocol reflects that. When a crew arrives at an Auburn address, the first step is containment: sealing off affected areas to prevent cross-contamination to clean parts of the home, and establishing negative air pressure where the layout allows. No one enters the contaminated zone without full PPE.

Extraction comes next. Standing sewage is removed with truck-mounted extraction equipment, and any porous material that has absorbed Category 3 water, drywall, insulation, carpet, pad, particleboard subfloor, is assessed for removal. In Auburn's older homes, that often means encountering original hardwood subfloor beneath vinyl or carpet. Hardwood can sometimes be saved if extraction is rapid and the contamination exposure was brief; the crew makes that call on-site using moisture readings, not assumptions.

Once bulk material is removed, all affected surfaces are treated with EPA-registered disinfectants. This is not a single-pass wipe-down. Hard surfaces receive multiple applications with appropriate dwell times, and the space is allowed to off-gas before air scrubbers with HEPA filtration are run to capture any aerosolized particulates. Structural drying follows sanitization, not the other way around, because sealing contaminated moisture inside a wall cavity creates a mold problem on top of a sewage problem.

## Auburn Insurance and HOA Coordination

Most homeowner insurance policies cover sudden and accidental sewage backups, but the language matters, gradual seepage or a lateral that failed over months may be treated differently than an acute backup event. Frontline documents the loss thoroughly: moisture readings, photographs of affected materials before removal, and a written scope that a carrier's adjuster can follow. That documentation is handed to the homeowner and submitted directly to the insurer, keeping the claim process moving while the crew focuses on the property.

For Auburn properties in HOA-governed communities, particularly the newer planned developments on the city's north end, the crew can coordinate with property management on access, staging, and any required notification before work begins.
