## Why Seattle Properties See Mold Issues

Seattle's rainfall pattern is less about dramatic downpours and more about persistent drizzle that never fully dries out. That steady saturation creates two common entry points for mold: exterior envelope failures and condensation-driven moisture inside the building. Older homes throughout neighborhoods like Capitol Hill and Ballard were often built with minimal vapor barriers and single-pane windows, conditions that let warm interior air hit cold exterior walls and condense, feeding mold behind plaster and lathe long before it's visible from the living side.

Crawl spaces are a particular vulnerability in Seattle. The region's clay-heavy soils drain slowly, and many homes sit on pier-and-beam foundations where ground moisture migrates upward year-round. A crawl space that smells musty in February has often been quietly growing mold colonies since the previous October. By the time a homeowner notices a musty odor on the main floor, the subfloor framing may already be compromised.

Flat and low-slope roofs, common on mid-century modern and post-war bungalows, collect standing water and funnel it toward parapet walls and penetrations where flashing eventually fails. That failure rarely announces itself loudly. Water travels along roof sheathing and appears as a ceiling stain weeks or months after the breach.

## Our Mold Remediation Process in Seattle

Remediation starts with containment, not cleaning. Before any disturbed mold spores can migrate to unaffected areas, the work zone is sealed with polyethylene sheeting and placed under negative air pressure using HEPA-filtered air scrubbers. This step is non-negotiable regardless of colony size, a small visible patch often signals a larger hidden growth behind it.

Once containment is established, affected materials are assessed. Porous materials, drywall, insulation, wood framing, that are colonized beyond surface growth are removed and bagged for disposal per Washington State Department of Ecology guidelines. Non-porous surfaces are HEPA-vacuumed, wiped with EPA-registered antimicrobial solution, and allowed to dry to verified moisture levels before any reconstruction begins.

Moisture mapping runs throughout the entire process. We use thermal imaging and calibrated moisture meters to trace the water source that fed the mold in the first place. Remediating mold without correcting the moisture source is temporary at best, the colony returns.

Final clearance involves post-remediation air sampling to confirm spore counts have returned to normal background levels. In Seattle, where many properties are managed by HOAs or subject to tenant disclosure requirements, that documentation matters as much as the physical work.
