## Why Wollochet Properties See Water Damage Issues

Much of unincorporated Wollochet sits on sloped, wooded lots above the bay, which means homes built into the hillside often have daylight basements or crawl spaces positioned right where runoff collects. Pierce County's wet season pushes groundwater up against foundations for months at a stretch, and septic systems common to this part of the county can back up or saturate drain fields when the water table rises. Add older well and plumbing lines on properties that predate modern code updates, and a slow leak under a slab can go undetected for weeks before it surfaces as a soft spot in the flooring or a musty smell near the water heater closet. Waterfront and near-waterfront homes also deal with wind-driven rain intrusion around windows and decks that face the Sound, which shows up as hidden moisture in wall cavities long before paint or trim gives any visible sign.

## Our Water Damage Restoration Process in Wollochet

We start with moisture mapping, using meters to find where water has traveled, not just where it's pooled, since Wollochet's hillside lots often mean water migrates downslope inside wall cavities and under subfloors. Standing water comes out first with truck-mounted or portable extraction equipment, followed by targeted structural drying using air movers and dehumidifiers set to pull moisture out of framing, subfloor, and insulation without over-drying finishes that don't need it. For crawl space intrusion, which is common on the lower lots near the bay, we isolate and dry the space separately from the living area to keep humidity from migrating upward into the home. Every job is documented with psychrometric readings so drying is tracked against the IICRC S500 standard rather than guessed at, and we don't close out a job until materials read dry, not just damp-to-the-touch.
