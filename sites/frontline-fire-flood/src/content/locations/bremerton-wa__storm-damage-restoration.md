## Why Bremerton Properties See Storm Damage Differently

The Kitsap Peninsula averages well over 50 inches of rain annually, and Bremerton's waterfront topography means that rainfall doesn't always drain the way it does in flatter inland cities. Soil saturation happens quickly, and once the ground is at capacity, water finds the next available path, often through foundation walls, crawl space vents, or the gap between a roof deck and a failing flashing seal.

Bremerton's housing stock adds another layer of complexity. A significant portion of the city's residential neighborhoods were built in the 1940s and 1950s, when Naval Station Puget Sound was driving rapid construction. Homes from that era typically have original-growth fir framing, older roofing substrates, and in many cases, crawl spaces with minimal vapor barriers. When storm water intrudes into that kind of structure, it doesn't behave the way it does in a 2005 stick-frame house. The older wood is denser and slower to release moisture, which means standard drying timelines need to be extended and moisture mapping has to go deeper than surface readings.

High-wind events also tend to drive rain horizontally into window frames, older brick chimneys, and wood-framed walls that have lost their original caulk lines, entry points that aren't obvious until you're looking for them with an infrared camera.

## Our Storm Damage Restoration Process in Bremerton

The first priority on any storm damage call is stopping additional loss. That means emergency tarping of compromised rooflines, boarding openings where windows or doors have failed, and extracting any standing water before it migrates further into wall cavities or subfloor assemblies.

Once the structure is stabilized, the assessment phase begins. We use thermal imaging and calibrated moisture meters to trace where water has traveled, not just where it's visible. In Bremerton's older homes, water can wick several feet through original plaster and lath before it shows up on a surface reading. Every affected material gets documented with photographs and moisture readings, which feeds directly into the insurance claim file.

Drying equipment, industrial air movers, desiccant or refrigerant dehumidifiers, and where needed, injectidry systems for wall cavities, is set up based on the IICRC S500 standard for water damage and adjusted daily based on psychrometric readings. Tree impact damage that has exposed the structure to the elements gets addressed in coordination with the drying process, not after it, so the building envelope is closed as quickly as possible.

Final restoration, framing repairs, roofing, drywall, insulation, exterior siding, follows once the structure reads dry and the scope is agreed upon with your carrier.
