## Why Graham Properties See Sewage Backup Issues

The combination of rural lot sizes, aging infrastructure, and Pierce County's clay-heavy soils creates conditions that put Graham homes at elevated risk for sewage-related losses. Clay soil drains slowly, which means that during prolonged wet seasons, drain fields can become saturated and lose their ability to process effluent, a problem that often shows up first as slow drains or gurgling toilets before escalating to a full overflow inside the home.

Many properties in and around Graham were developed in the 1970s through the 1990s, when septic systems were sized to household standards that predate today's water usage patterns. A tank that was adequate for a family of three can struggle under the load of a larger household or a home with added bathrooms. Sewer line backups are also common in areas where tree root intrusion into older PVC or cast-iron laterals goes undetected until a blockage forces waste back through the lowest drain in the house, typically a floor drain, utility sink, or toilet on the ground floor or in a crawl space.

## Our Sewage Cleanup and Sanitization Process in Graham

Raw sewage removal is not a job that benefits from a wait-and-see approach. The process Frontline Fire & Flood follows is calibrated to the specific contamination category and the construction characteristics of the affected space.

**Containment and assessment** come first. Technicians establish containment barriers to prevent cross-contamination from spreading to unaffected rooms, then use moisture meters and thermal imaging to map how far the sewage has migrated, including into wall cavities, subfloor assemblies, and crawl spaces, which are common in Graham's ranch-style and split-level homes.

**Extraction and removal** follows. Standing sewage is extracted using truck-mounted units, and contaminated porous materials, drywall, insulation, carpet, and subfloor sections, are removed and bagged for proper disposal in accordance with Pierce County waste handling requirements. Non-porous surfaces are cleaned in multiple passes.

**Sanitization** uses EPA-registered antimicrobial agents applied to all affected surfaces. This step addresses pathogens present in Category 3 water, including bacteria and viruses that are not eliminated by drying alone.

**Structural drying** is the final phase before reconstruction can begin. Drying equipment is placed based on psychrometric readings, and moisture levels are logged daily until materials reach documented dry standards per the IICRC S500 protocol.

Homeowners insurance coverage for sewage and septic backup varies significantly by policy.
