## Why Puyallup Properties See Water Damage Differently

The Puyallup River floodplain shapes more than the scenery. Much of the city's residential development sits on alluvial soils that hold moisture long after a storm passes, which means crawl spaces and slab edges stay damp well into spring. That persistent ground moisture creates a baseline humidity that accelerates secondary damage once water gets inside a structure, mold colonization can begin within 24 to 48 hours in conditions like these, faster than in drier inland climates.

Puyallup's housing stock adds another layer of complexity. The city grew steadily through the mid-twentieth century, and many neighborhoods contain homes with original cast-iron or galvanized steel supply lines that have long since passed their service life. These pipes don't fail with warning, they corrode from the inside and rupture under normal operating pressure, often behind finished walls or beneath concrete slabs where the water spreads undetected for hours. Older homes may also have original plaster-and-lath wall assemblies rather than drywall; plaster absorbs water more slowly at the surface but holds it deep in the substrate, which means moisture readings at the face of the wall can look acceptable while the lath and framing behind it remain saturated.

## Our Water Damage Restoration Process in Puyallup

Every job starts with a thorough moisture assessment using thermal imaging and calibrated moisture meters, not a visual inspection alone. Water follows framing cavities, subfloor channels, and HVAC chases in ways that aren't visible, and finding those hidden migration paths early determines how many materials can be saved versus removed.

Once the scope is mapped, we extract standing water with truck-mounted and portable extraction units, then transition to structural drying. Drying in Puyallup requires equipment calibrated for the region's ambient humidity, standard drying targets used in arid climates aren't appropriate here. We set drying goals per the IICRC S500 standard and monitor daily with psychrometric readings, adjusting airflow and dehumidification as the structure responds. Documentation of each reading is kept throughout the project, which matters when an insurance adjuster needs to verify that drying was completed to standard before reconstruction begins.

For homes with crawl spaces, common throughout Puyallup's older residential areas, we inspect and treat the crawl as part of the affected zone, not as an afterthought. Wet insulation, saturated vapor barriers, and damp floor joists in a crawl space will re-introduce moisture into the living area above if left unaddressed.

Puyallup homes built in the postwar decades frequently used a combination of plaster ceilings and early drywall on walls, a hybrid assembly that behaves unpredictably under moisture.
