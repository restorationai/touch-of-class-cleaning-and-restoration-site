## Why Seattle Properties Are Especially Vulnerable After a Loss

Seattle averages roughly 150 days of measurable rainfall per year, and the Pacific storms that push through the region between October and March carry sustained winds that can lift a poorly secured tarp or widen a compromised roof section overnight. That climate pressure is compounded by the city's housing stock: a significant share of Seattle's residential neighborhoods, from the hillside Craftsmans in Beacon Hill to the older wood-frame construction common across the city's north end, were built before modern moisture barriers and sheathing standards became code. Older skip-sheathing on roofs, which was standard practice through much of the mid-20th century, leaves almost no redundancy when a section of roofing is lost. A missing shingle field on a newer home might tolerate a day of light rain; a gap in skip-sheathed decking on a 1930s bungalow can saturate ceiling joists within a single storm event.

Fire damage adds another layer of urgency. Smoke and heat weaken window frames and door jambs even when the glass and hardware look intact, and a structure that appears stable from the street may have compromised load-bearing members that make unsecured openings dangerous as well as weather-exposed.

## Our Emergency Board-Up and Tarping Process in Seattle

When a call comes in, the crew loads the vehicle with dimensional lumber, OSB panels, heavy-mil polyethylene tarps, and the fastening systems appropriate to the substrate, what anchors cleanly into a Douglas fir rafter doesn't work the same way on a steel lintel or a masonry parapet. On arrival, the first priority is a rapid safety assessment of the structure before anyone goes onto the roof or into a fire-affected interior. Once the perimeter is confirmed safe to work, crews address the largest or highest-risk openings first: an exposed roof deck before a broken ground-floor window, for instance, because water intrusion from above travels through multiple assemblies before it's visible.

Board-up work on fire-damaged structures is documented photographically at every stage, panel placement, fastener pattern, and the condition of the substrate behind each opening. That documentation matters when an insurance adjuster reviews the scope of emergency services, and it matters again when the reconstruction contractor needs to understand what was found underneath.
