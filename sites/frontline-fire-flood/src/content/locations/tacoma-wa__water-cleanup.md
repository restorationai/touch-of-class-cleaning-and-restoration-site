## Why Tacoma Properties See Water Damage Differently

Tacoma's housing stock skews older than most of Pierce County. A significant share of single-family homes in neighborhoods like North End and Proctor were built before 1960, which means original cast-iron or galvanized steel supply lines that have been quietly corroding for decades. When those lines fail, often at a fitting, often behind a wall, water tracks along floor joists and subfloor before it ever appears at the surface. By the time a homeowner notices a soft spot in the hardwood or a stain on the ceiling below, the moisture has already been migrating for hours.

Tacoma's soil profile and relatively high annual rainfall compound the problem. The city sits on a mix of glacial till and clay-heavy soils that drain slowly. During heavy rain events, that drainage resistance pushes water toward crawl spaces and basement perimeters. Homes with older or undersized drain tile systems are particularly vulnerable, crawl space flooding is one of the most common calls we take from Tacoma addresses.

## Our Water Cleanup Process in Tacoma

Every job starts with moisture mapping, not assumptions. We use thermal imaging cameras and calibrated moisture meters to trace water migration through walls, subfloors, and ceiling assemblies before a single piece of flooring comes up. That matters especially in older Tacoma construction, where water can travel several feet from the source along original fir subfloor boards before pooling.

From there, the process follows the IICRC S500 standard for water damage restoration:

- **Extraction**, truck-mounted and portable extractors pull standing water and saturated material moisture as fast as the substrate allows.
- **Structural drying**, commercial-grade desiccant and refrigerant dehumidifiers, paired with high-velocity air movers, are positioned based on psychrometric readings, not guesswork.
- **Monitoring**, we return daily to log moisture readings and adjust equipment placement. Drying is not a set-it-and-leave-it process.
- **Documentation**, moisture logs, photographs, and scope notes are formatted for insurance adjuster review from day one.

As an IICRC Certified Firm holding WRT and ASD credentials, the crew performing this work is trained specifically in water damage and structural drying, not general construction labor reassigned to a wet job.

Most standard homeowner policies in Washington cover sudden and accidental water discharge, a burst pipe, an appliance failure, an overflow, but they do not cover gradual leaks that a homeowner "knew or should have known" about. ## Local Note

In Tacoma's older craftsman and bungalow neighborhoods, particularly the blocks between 6th Avenue and North 21st where original fir lath-and-plaster walls are still common, water cleanup takes longer than the same square footage in a modern drywall home. Plaster is dense and absorbs moisture slowly, but it also releases it slowly. Surface readings can show acceptable levels while the lath behind the plaster is still holding significant moisture. We account for this by extending monitoring cycles and positioning dehumidifiers closer to wall cavities rather than relying on ambient room drying. Pulling plaster unnecessarily is expensive and rarely required if drying equipment is placed correctly from the start.
