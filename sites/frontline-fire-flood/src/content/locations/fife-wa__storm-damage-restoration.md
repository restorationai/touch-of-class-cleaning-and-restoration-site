The same low-lying industrial and residential land that made Fife a logistics hub also means that heavy rain events can overwhelm storm drains quickly, sending water against foundations and under slab-on-grade structures before most homeowners realize a storm has passed. ## Why Fife Properties See Elevated Storm Damage Risk

Fife's position in the Puyallup River valley means the water table sits close to the surface across much of the city. When a strong Pacific low drops several inches of rain in a short window, a pattern Pierce County sees repeatedly between October and March, that saturated soil has nowhere to absorb additional runoff. Sump pumps in crawl spaces and basements get overwhelmed, and surface water finds its way through any gap in a foundation wall or under a garage door.

The city's commercial corridor along Pacific Highway and the dense residential pockets nearby include a mix of construction eras. Older homes built on pier-and-beam foundations can shift when saturated soils move, opening gaps that let wind-driven rain penetrate wall cavities. Newer slab-on-grade construction in Fife's residential areas faces a different problem: water that pools against a slab has no crawl space to drain into, so it migrates through concrete and into flooring systems from below, often without any visible surface sign until flooring begins to buckle or a musty odor develops.

High-wind events compound the picture. Fife's flat terrain and proximity to the Port of Tacoma corridor means wind gusts during named Pacific storms can strip roofing materials, push over mature trees, and drive debris into windows and siding. Tree damage cleanup is frequently part of the same job as interior water intrusion, a fallen limb punches through a roof, and rain enters the attic before anyone can tarp the opening.

## Our Storm Damage Restoration Process in Fife

When Frontline Fire & Flood arrives at a Fife property after a storm, the first priority is stopping ongoing water entry. That means emergency tarping of roof penetrations, board-up of broken windows or doors, and extraction of any standing water, before any documentation or drying equipment is staged.

Once the structure is secured, our IICRC-certified technicians use thermal imaging cameras and moisture meters to map exactly where water has traveled. In Fife's older pier-and-beam homes, moisture frequently tracks along subfloor joists well beyond the visible wet area. In slab construction, we check under vinyl plank and laminate flooring for moisture trapped at the concrete interface. This mapping drives the drying plan, how many air movers, where dehumidifiers are positioned, and how long the drying cycle needs to run before materials reach acceptable moisture content per the IICRC S500 standard.

Structural drying is followed by damage assessment for reconstruction: compromised sheathing, damaged insulation, wind-lifted roofing, and any materials that cannot be dried in place and must be removed. We photograph and document every affected material for insurance purposes before anything is touched that doesn't need to come out.

## Fife Insurance Coordination for Storm Claims

Storm damage claims in Pierce County typically involve wind, hail, and water as separate line items on an adjuster's worksheet, and how those categories are documented affects what your carrier pays. Our team photographs affected materials in place, notes the sequence of damage (wind breach first, then water intrusion), and provides a written scope that aligns with the way adjusters read storm loss files. We bill most major carriers directly and work with your adjuster throughout the process so you are not managing two separate conversations.

EPA Lead-Safe certification matters on older Fife properties where storm damage disturbs painted surfaces, we follow required containment and disposal protocols, which protects both the homeowner and the claim.
