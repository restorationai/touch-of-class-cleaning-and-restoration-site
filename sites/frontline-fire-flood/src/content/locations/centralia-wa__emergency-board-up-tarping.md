## Why Centralia Properties Face Elevated Board-Up and Tarping Risk

Centralia's housing stock tells a story in lumber and brick. Much of the residential core was built in the early to mid-twentieth century, when balloon-frame construction was standard, a framing method where wall cavities run uninterrupted from the foundation sill to the roof line. That open cavity is efficient at moving fire and smoke vertically, and it is equally efficient at funneling wind-driven rain once an opening exists in the envelope. A broken window or a hole in the fascia that might stay relatively contained in a newer platform-frame home can expose a much larger interior volume in an older Centralia property.

The regional climate compounds the risk. Lewis County averages well over 50 inches of annual precipitation, and the Chehalis River basin, which Centralia sits within, is no stranger to atmospheric river events that deliver sustained, heavy rainfall over multiple days. A compromised roof that goes untarped overnight during one of those events is not a minor inconvenience; it is a water intrusion scenario that can saturate ceiling assemblies, run down interior walls, and pool beneath finished flooring before the rain stops.

Fire damage adds another layer. When a structure fire is extinguished, the building is often left with broken windows, missing roof sections, and door frames that no longer close, all of it exposed to whatever weather arrives next. Securing that structure immediately is not cosmetic; it is the first step in limiting the total scope of the restoration.

## Our Emergency Board-Up and Tarping Process in Centralia

When a call comes in from Centralia, the first task is a rapid exterior assessment, identifying every breach in the building envelope, from obvious holes to less visible damage like lifted flashing, cracked skylights, or fire-warped siding gaps. We document every opening with photographs before any material goes up, which matters later when the insurance adjuster is reviewing the claim.

Board-up work on residential properties uses plywood panels cut and fitted to window and door openings, secured in a way that resists both wind load and unauthorized entry. On older Centralia homes with non-standard window dimensions, common in pre-war Craftsman and vernacular farmhouse styles throughout Lewis County, standard sheet sizes rarely fit cleanly, so panels are field-cut on site. For roof damage, heavy-duty polyethylene tarps are anchored with battens and fastened to sound decking or ridge structure, not just draped and weighted. A tarp that lifts in a wind gust provides no protection.

We are IICRC certified and EPA Lead-Safe certified, which matters on older Centralia properties where disturbing painted surfaces during board-up work can trigger lead-safe handling requirements. We follow those protocols as a baseline, not as an add-on.

## Insurance Coordination for Centralia Claims

Most standard homeowners policies cover emergency board-up and tarping as a mitigation expense, the logic being that securing the structure limits the total claim. We document the damage and the scope of protective work in a format that supports the adjuster's review, and we can bill carriers directly on covered losses so the property owner is not managing out-of-pocket costs while the claim is pending. If your policy has a mitigation provision, the board-up work is typically part of the covered loss, not a separate line item you absorb.

## Local Note

Older homes in the Centralia area that have been re-roofed one or more times over the decades sometimes have layered roofing assemblies, original wood shake beneath a later composition layer, for example. When fire or storm damage exposes that layered structure, a standard tarp installation anchored to the outermost layer may not have the purchase it appears to. During our roof assessments on older Lewis County properties, we probe for delaminated or deteriorated underlayers before committing to anchor points, because a tarp that pulls free in the next wind event has done nothing to protect the home.
