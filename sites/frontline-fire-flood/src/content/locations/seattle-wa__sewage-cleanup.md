## Why Seattle Properties See Sewage Backup Problems

Seattle's sewer system is a patchwork of infrastructure built across several eras. Many neighborhoods, particularly those with housing stock from the early to mid-1900s, sit on combined sewer systems that carry both stormwater and sanitary waste in the same pipe. During the heavy, sustained rainfall that defines a Seattle winter, those systems can surcharge. When the volume of stormwater overwhelms the pipe capacity, sewage has nowhere to go but backward, up through floor drains, toilets, and sink connections at the lowest point of a structure.

Soil conditions compound the problem. Seattle's glacially deposited soils vary sharply across the city's hills and valleys. In lower-elevation areas and neighborhoods built on filled land near the waterfront, drainage is slower and groundwater sits closer to the surface. That means water and sewage that escape a broken or backed-up line don't dissipate, they pool, saturate subfloor assemblies, and wick into framing before the smell even registers upstairs.

Older cast-iron and clay-tile lateral lines, common in homes built before 1970, are also prone to root intrusion and joint failure. A cracked lateral is often the first domino: the line backs up, the homeowner plunges the toilet, and the pressure pushes Category 3 water, raw sewage, across a bathroom or laundry room floor.

## Our Sewage Cleanup and Sanitization Process in Seattle

Sewage is classified as Category 3 contaminated water, the most hazardous category recognized by the IICRC S500 standard. That classification drives every decision we make on-site.

When we arrive, the first priority is containment, stopping the source if it hasn't been stopped, and establishing a barrier between the affected area and the rest of the structure. Technicians suit up in full PPE before any material is touched. We then extract standing waste water using truck-mounted extraction equipment capable of handling solids, not just liquid.

After bulk removal, all porous materials that absorbed Category 3 water, drywall, insulation, subfloor sheathing, carpet, and pad, are removed and bagged for disposal per Washington State Department of Ecology guidelines. This is non-negotiable: porous materials contaminated by raw sewage cannot be dried in place and reused.

Sanitization follows demolition. We apply EPA-registered antimicrobial agents to all affected structural surfaces, including framing, concrete, and crawl space soil where applicable. Surfaces are treated, allowed to dwell, and then re-treated before any drying equipment is placed. Air scrubbers with HEPA filtration run continuously to capture airborne particulates and odor compounds throughout the drying phase.

Final clearance involves moisture verification across all structural assemblies using thermal imaging and calibrated moisture meters. We document readings at every stage, that documentation matters for your insurance claim and for any permit-required inspections that may follow.

Most homeowner policies in Washington cover sudden and accidental sewer backup losses, but coverage depends heavily on whether the homeowner added a sewer backup endorsement, a rider that is not included in standard policies by default.
