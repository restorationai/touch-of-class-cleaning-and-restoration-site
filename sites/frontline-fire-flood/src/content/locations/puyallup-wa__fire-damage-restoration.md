## Why Puyallup Properties Present Unique Fire Damage Challenges

Puyallup's housing stock spans a wide range of construction eras. The older neighborhoods closer to downtown, many with homes built in the mid-20th century, frequently feature original wood-lath plaster walls, single-pane windows, and older HVAC ductwork. After a fire, smoke travels through every gap in a structure, and in homes with uninsulated balloon-frame construction, that means soot can migrate from a kitchen fire all the way into attic cavities and crawl spaces before the flames are out. Plaster walls also absorb odor compounds differently than modern drywall: the surface may look cleanable, but the porous substrate underneath can off-gas smoke smell for months if the restoration isn't done correctly.

Puyallup's location in the Puyallup River valley also means elevated humidity throughout fall and winter. Wet char and smoke-saturated insulation left in place even briefly will begin to support mold growth, two problems compounding into one claim.

## Our Fire Damage Restoration Process in Puyallup

Every fire loss is different, but the sequence of decisions matters. When the crew arrives, the first priority is a thorough scope, not just the rooms with visible char, but the HVAC system, the attic, and any shared wall cavities where smoke traveled. In Puyallup's older homes, that investigation often turns up damage two or three rooms away from the origin point.

From there, the process moves through:

- **Board-up and weatherproofing**, broken windows and roof penetrations are sealed immediately. Given the rainfall typical in Pierce County from October through April, an unsecured structure can accumulate water damage within a single night.
- **Controlled demolition of unsalvageable materials**, charred framing, melted insulation, and smoke-saturated drywall are removed and documented for the insurance claim.
- **Dry ice blasting or chemical sponge cleaning** on structural surfaces, depending on material and char depth.
- **Thermal fogging and hydroxyl treatment** for odor, smoke odor in a closed Pacific Northwest home can be particularly persistent because the structure doesn't dry out naturally between rain events.
- **Air quality verification** before reconstruction begins.

The team holds IICRC FSRT (Fire and Smoke Restoration Technician) certification, and the work is documented to IICRC S700 standards, the format most carriers expect when reviewing a structural fire claim.

## Local Note

In Puyallup's older residential areas, many homes still have original cast-iron or galvanized steel heating ducts. After a fire, these ducts act as a distribution system for smoke particulate, the same forced air that once heated the home pushes soot into every register in the building. Before any HVAC system in one of these homes is turned back on post-fire, the duct network needs to be inspected and cleaned separately from the room-by-room restoration. Skipping that step and running the furnace to dry out the structure is one of the most common ways a contained fire loss becomes a whole-house odor problem.
