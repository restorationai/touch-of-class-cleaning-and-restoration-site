## Why Bonney Lake Homes Are Particularly Vulnerable to Fire Damage

Much of Bonney Lake's residential development occurred between the 1980s and the 2000s, a period when engineered wood products, LVL beams, OSB subfloor, I-joists, became the standard framing materials. These products perform well under normal conditions, but they degrade significantly faster in fire than traditional dimensional lumber. An I-joist that has been exposed to flame for even a few minutes can lose structural integrity well before it looks compromised from the outside. This matters during post-fire assessment: what appears to be a contained kitchen fire can involve structural members that need engineering review before the space is re-occupied.

Pierce County's wet winters also mean that by the time a fire is extinguished, the structure has absorbed substantial water from suppression efforts. In Bonney Lake's climate, that moisture doesn't dry out on its own, it migrates into wall cavities and subfloor assemblies and creates secondary mold risk within days if not addressed with commercial drying equipment.

## Our Fire Damage Restoration Process in Bonney Lake

Every fire loss is different, but the sequence of work follows a disciplined order that protects both the structure and the people inside it.

**Emergency stabilization** comes first, boarding windows, tarping the roof if the fire breached the sheathing, and securing the property against weather. In Bonney Lake, where fall and winter storms can push rain into an open structure within hours, this step is not optional.

**Damage assessment** follows. We document every affected material, charred framing, smoke-stained insulation, soot-coated HVAC ducts, with photographs and written scope. This documentation is the foundation of your insurance claim and the roadmap for reconstruction.

**Smoke and soot removal** is the most labor-intensive phase. Soot is acidic and continues to etch surfaces, metal fixtures, glass, finished wood, the longer it sits. We use dry chemical sponges, HEPA vacuuming, and thermal fogging to reach soot that has migrated into wall cavities and attic spaces. In homes with vaulted ceilings common throughout Bonney Lake neighborhoods, smoke stratifies at the peak and requires specific attention to prevent persistent odor.

**Structural drying** runs concurrently with cleanup when suppression water is present. We deploy commercial dehumidifiers and air movers calibrated to the square footage and monitor moisture readings daily until the structure meets dry standard per the IICRC S500.

**Reconstruction** closes out the project, framing repairs, drywall, insulation, finishes, coordinated with Pierce County permitting requirements as applicable.
