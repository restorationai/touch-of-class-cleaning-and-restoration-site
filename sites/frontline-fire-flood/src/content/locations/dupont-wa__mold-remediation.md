## Why DuPont Properties See Mold Issues

DuPont's location between Joint Base Lewis-McChord and the Nisqually River lowlands means the area experiences some of the most consistent ground-level moisture in Pierce County. Marine airflow from Puget Sound keeps relative humidity elevated through much of the year, and the town's topography, gently sloped toward wetland corridors, means drainage around foundations can stall rather than sheet away cleanly.

The master-planned neighborhoods that define most of modern DuPont were built primarily in the 1990s and 2000s, and many of those homes share a common construction detail: tightly sealed building envelopes designed for energy efficiency that, without adequate mechanical ventilation, trap interior moisture. When a dishwasher supply line weeps behind a cabinet for weeks or a window seal fails on a north-facing wall, that trapped humidity has nowhere to go. Mold colonization behind drywall can begin in as little as 24 to 48 hours under those conditions, long before a visible stain appears on the surface.

Older structures near DuPont's historic town center present a different challenge. Some of those buildings retain original wood framing that, once wet, holds moisture deep in the grain long after the surface reads dry on a basic moisture meter. Accurate assessment in those properties requires thermal imaging and deep-probe readings, not a quick surface scan.

## Our Mold Remediation Process in DuPont

Every job starts with containment, negative air pressure barriers that isolate the affected area so disturbed spores don't migrate to clean rooms while work is underway. That step matters especially in DuPont's attached and semi-detached homes, where shared wall cavities can carry contamination laterally if the work zone isn't sealed properly.

After containment, the process follows the IICRC S520 standard for mold remediation:

- **Assessment and moisture mapping**, thermal imaging and calibrated moisture meters identify the full extent of saturation, including hidden pockets behind cabinets, inside wall cavities, and beneath subfloor sheathing.
- **Controlled demolition**, porous materials that cannot be cleaned to clearance (drywall, insulation, compromised framing) are removed, bagged, and disposed of per Washington State Department of Ecology guidelines.
- **HEPA vacuuming and antimicrobial treatment**, structural surfaces are vacuumed with HEPA-filtered equipment, then treated with EPA-registered antimicrobial agents.
- **Structural drying**, industrial dehumidifiers and air movers run until moisture readings confirm the substrate is within normal range, preventing recurrence before reconstruction begins.
- **Clearance testing**, an independent air sample verifies spore counts have returned to acceptable levels before containment comes down.

Frontline holds IICRC AMRT (Applied Microbial Remediation Technician) certification, and the team is EPA Lead-Safe Certified, relevant in any DuPont property where older painted surfaces are disturbed during demolition.

## Local Note

DuPont's newer master-community homes are built on concrete slab foundations rather than the raised crawl spaces common in older Pierce County housing stock. Slab construction eliminates one major mold vector, the damp crawl space, but it concentrates risk at the slab perimeter and in any area where a plumbing penetration through the slab allows moisture to wick upward. If you notice a musty smell in a first-floor bathroom or laundry room with no visible source, the origin is often at or below the slab level, and it takes a combination of thermal imaging and core sampling to locate it accurately.
