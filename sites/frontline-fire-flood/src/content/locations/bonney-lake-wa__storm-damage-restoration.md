## Why Bonney Lake Properties See Significant Storm Damage

The terrain around Bonney Lake is not flat, and that matters enormously when severe weather arrives. Steep lots channel runoff directly toward structures, and the glacially deposited soils common to this part of Pierce County don't absorb heavy rainfall quickly. A single night of wind and rain can leave standing water against foundation walls, saturated crawl spaces, and roof decking exposed to the elements.

The housing stock adds another layer of complexity. Bonney Lake grew rapidly through the 1990s and 2000s, meaning a large share of the city's single-family homes feature engineered wood products, OSB sheathing, laminated beams, composite decking, that begin to swell and delaminate within hours of water intrusion. Unlike old-growth lumber, these materials don't forgive a slow response. Roof damage from fallen trees or wind-lifted shingles can allow water to penetrate several layers of structure before a homeowner realizes the interior is wet.

The region also sits in a zone where atmospheric rivers periodically deliver multi-day rainfall events, not just isolated afternoon storms. That sustained saturation is what separates a manageable cleanup from a full structural drying project.

## Our Storm Damage Restoration Process in Bonney Lake

Every storm loss is different, but the sequence that prevents further damage follows a consistent logic. When we arrive, the first priority is stabilizing the structure, boarding broken windows, tarping compromised roof sections, and removing standing water before it migrates into wall cavities or subfloor assemblies.

Once the immediate threat is controlled, we conduct a full moisture mapping of the affected areas using thermal imaging and calibrated moisture meters. Water that appears contained at the surface is often traveling laterally through insulation, along joists, and into adjacent rooms. In Bonney Lake's hillside homes, water entry through a compromised foundation or garage wall can track surprisingly far before it becomes visible.

Structural drying follows, using commercial-grade desiccant and refrigerant dehumidifiers alongside high-velocity air movers positioned to create the airflow patterns the IICRC S500 standard calls for. We monitor daily and adjust equipment placement as readings change. Debris removal, fallen limbs, damaged roofing materials, destroyed insulation, runs parallel to drying so the structure is ready for reconstruction as soon as the moisture levels confirm it.

Frontline Fire & Flood is an IICRC Certified Firm with technicians holding Water Restoration Technician and Applied Structural Drying credentials, and the team is EPA Lead-Safe Certified, relevant in Bonney Lake's older pockets where pre-1978 construction may be disturbed during storm repairs.
