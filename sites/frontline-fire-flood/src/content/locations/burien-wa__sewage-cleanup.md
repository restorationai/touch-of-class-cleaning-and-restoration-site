## Why Burien Properties See Sewage Backup Issues

The housing stock across Burien skews older, a significant portion of homes were built in the postwar decades when cast-iron and Orangeburg sewer laterals were standard. Orangeburg pipe, made from compressed wood pulp and pitch, was never designed to last more than 50 years, and many of those laterals are now failing quietly beneath front yards and driveways. When they collapse or root-intrude, the first sign is often a slow drain that suddenly becomes a full backup into the lowest fixture in the house, a floor drain, a basement toilet, or a utility sink.

Burien's topography adds another layer of risk. Properties on the lower slopes toward Puget Sound and along the drainage corridors that cut through the city are especially vulnerable during heavy rain events. When the municipal system surcharges, meaning stormwater overwhelms the combined or sanitary sewer capacity, pressure can reverse flow back through residential laterals. That's not a plumbing failure you can fix with a snake; it requires proper sewage removal, surface disinfection, and documentation for your insurance carrier.

## Our Sewage Cleanup and Sanitization Process in Burien

Sewage is classified as Category 3 water, the most contaminated classification under the IICRC S500 standard, which means every material it contacts must be treated as potentially infectious. The process Frontline uses in Burien homes follows a strict sequence:

**Containment first.** Before any extraction begins, affected areas are isolated with poly sheeting and negative air pressure to prevent cross-contamination to clean spaces. In older Burien homes with open-joist crawl spaces, this step is especially important because air moves freely between the crawl and living areas.

**Extraction and removal.** Standing sewage is extracted using truck-mounted units. Porous materials, carpet, pad, drywall below the flood line, insulation, that have contacted Category 3 water are removed rather than dried in place. There is no safe way to dry and restore sewage-saturated porous material.

**Disinfection and deodorization.** Hard surfaces, framing, concrete, and subfloor are treated with EPA-registered disinfectants. The odor compounds in raw sewage bind to surfaces, so disinfection and odor control happen together, not sequentially.

**Structural drying.** After sanitization, remaining structural materials are dried using commercial desiccant and refrigerant dehumidifiers paired with air movers. Moisture readings are logged daily until materials reach target values, a step that matters more in Burien's persistently humid winters than in drier climates.
