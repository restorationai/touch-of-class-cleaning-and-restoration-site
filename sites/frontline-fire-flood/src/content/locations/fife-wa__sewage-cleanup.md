## Why Fife Properties See Sewage Backup Issues

Fife's geography puts it in a low-lying flood plain between the Puyallup River and the Port of Tacoma. The water table here is shallow, sometimes only a few feet below grade, which means that during the extended wet season running from October through March, saturated soils have nowhere to drain. That hydraulic pressure forces water and sewage into the path of least resistance: aging clay or cast-iron lateral lines that connect older commercial and residential properties to the municipal system.

The city's industrial and commercial density along 54th Avenue E and the surrounding business corridors means many properties have large-diameter floor drains and grease traps that can backflow when the main line surges. Residential properties closer to the Puyallup River delta face a related but different problem: septic systems installed before the area connected to municipal sewer can fail when the drain field becomes waterlogged, sending partially treated effluent into yards and crawl spaces. Both scenarios require the same urgent response, containment, extraction, and full sanitization, but the source matters for documentation and insurance purposes.

## Our Sewage Cleanup and Sanitization Process in Fife

Every sewage job starts with source confirmation. Before extraction equipment rolls in, the crew identifies whether the backup is a municipal sewer surcharge, a failed lateral, a septic overflow, or a localized blockage. That distinction shapes the scope of work and what Pierce County Environmental Health may need to be notified about.

Once the source is controlled or isolated, the process moves through four stages:

**Containment and PPE setup**, affected areas are isolated with poly sheeting. All technicians work in full PPE consistent with OSHA standards for Category 3 water exposure.

**Extraction and bulk removal**, truck-mounted extraction units remove standing sewage. Saturated porous materials, carpet, pad, drywall, insulation, subfloor sheathing, are removed and bagged for disposal per Washington State Department of Ecology guidelines for biohazardous waste.

**Structural drying and antimicrobial treatment**, after bulk removal, remaining structural surfaces are treated with EPA-registered antimicrobial agents. IICRC-certified technicians monitor moisture readings in framing, concrete, and crawl space soil until readings return to pre-loss baselines. Drying equipment is placed and logged daily.

**Post-remediation verification**, surfaces are visually inspected and tested before containment comes down. Documentation is compiled for the insurance carrier and, where required, for the property owner's records with the county.
