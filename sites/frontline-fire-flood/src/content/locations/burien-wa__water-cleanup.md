## Why Burien Properties See Water Damage Differently

Burien's position along the eastern shoreline of Puget Sound gives it a climate that stays damp well into spring. Prolonged overcast and cool temperatures slow evaporation, which means water that soaks into subfloor assemblies or wall cavities lingers far longer than it would in a drier inland climate. That extended wet window is exactly when secondary damage accelerates.

The housing stock adds another layer of complexity. A significant portion of Burien's residential neighborhoods were built between the late 1940s and early 1970s. Homes from that era frequently have original galvanized steel supply lines that have been corroding from the inside out for decades, a slow pinhole leak can saturate insulation and floor sheathing for weeks before a homeowner notices a soft spot underfoot. Older crawl spaces in this area also tend to have minimal vapor barriers or none at all, so ground moisture compounds whatever has leaked from above.

Finally, Burien's topography creates natural drainage toward low-lying streets and yards. During heavy rain events, surface water can find its way through foundation vents and crawl space access points in ways that newer construction further east is engineered to prevent.

## Our Water Cleanup Process in Burien

Every water cleanup call starts with a thorough moisture assessment, not just of the visible wet area, but of adjacent assemblies. Water migrates. A dishwasher leak in a kitchen can travel under the subfloor and show up as elevated readings in a hallway or adjacent bathroom 10 feet away. We use thermal imaging and calibrated moisture meters to map the full extent of saturation before any drying equipment is placed.

Once the scope is documented, standing water is extracted with truck-mounted or portable extraction units, depending on access. In Burien's older split-level and multi-story homes, portable equipment often reaches areas a truck-mount hose cannot. After extraction, we establish a structured drying system, commercial-grade air movers and refrigerant dehumidifiers positioned to create airflow through wall cavities and under flooring rather than just across surfaces. Drying is monitored against IICRC S500 standards, with daily moisture readings logged until affected materials reach acceptable equilibrium.

If building materials cannot be dried in place, saturated particleboard subfloor is a common example in homes from this era, we document what needs to come out before any demolition begins, so your insurance adjuster has a clear record.

## Local Note

In Burien's older ranch-style and split-level homes, the original hardwood floors were often installed directly over board sheathing with no underlayment, a construction detail common through the 1960s. When water gets under those floors, it wicks along the boards and into the sheathing faster than it would through modern plywood-and-underlayment assemblies. The surface may feel barely damp while the sheathing below reads saturated on a moisture meter. We account for this when placing drying equipment, directing airflow downward through gaps between boards rather than just across the surface, otherwise drying times are significantly underestimated and the floor ends up cupped anyway.
