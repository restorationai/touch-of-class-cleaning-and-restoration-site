## Why Gig Harbor Properties See Sewage Backup More Often Than You'd Think

The peninsula's geography works against older plumbing in ways that aren't obvious until something fails. Much of Gig Harbor's residential development happened in waves, waterfront and hillside neighborhoods built decades apart, some on municipal sewer, others still on private septic. Where the two systems exist in close proximity, aging lateral lines running through the area's characteristic marine clay can shift, root-intrude, or simply collapse under the weight of saturated soil after a wet winter.

Gig Harbor averages well over 50 inches of rainfall annually, and the heaviest storms arrive between October and March, exactly when ground saturation is already at its peak. That combination puts pressure on both sewer mains and septic drain fields simultaneously. A sewer line backup during a heavy rain event isn't just a plumbing problem; it's a Category 3 water loss (raw sewage) that requires professional extraction, disposal, and sanitization under strict protocols. Attempting to clean it with household products doesn't neutralize the pathogens, it spreads them.

## Our Sewage Cleanup and Sanitization Process in Gig Harbor

Every sewage response starts with containment, not extraction. Before any equipment goes in, the affected area is isolated to prevent cross-contamination to adjacent rooms, particularly important in the crawl-space-heavy construction common throughout the peninsula, where an overflow can wick into floor cavities that aren't immediately visible.

From there, the process follows a defined sequence:

- **Extraction and removal**, standing sewage and saturated materials are removed and disposed of in accordance with Washington State Department of Ecology guidelines for biohazardous waste.
- **Structural assessment**, subfloor sheathing, wall cavities, and insulation are inspected and moisture-mapped. In Gig Harbor's older homes, tongue-and-groove subfloor common in mid-century construction holds contamination differently than OSB and often requires full removal rather than drying in place.
- **Antimicrobial treatment**, EPA-registered disinfectants are applied to all affected surfaces. This is not a one-pass wipe-down; contact time and concentration matter, and both are verified before moving on.
- **Structural drying**, once sanitized, affected cavities are dried to IICRC S500 standard using commercial desiccant and refrigerant dehumidifiers calibrated to the ambient humidity levels typical of a Puget Sound winter.
- **Post-remediation verification**, moisture readings and visual inspection confirm the space is dry and clean before any reconstruction begins.

## Local Note

Homes in Gig Harbor's older waterfront-adjacent neighborhoods often have crawl spaces that sit very close to the water table, and some were built before vapor barriers were standard practice. When a septic overflow or sewer backup reaches one of these crawl spaces, the contamination doesn't stay on the surface, it absorbs into the soil beneath the structure. That soil typically can't be dried; it has to be excavated and replaced, which changes the scope and timeline of the job significantly compared to a slab-on-grade or conditioned basement. If your home has an unencapsulated crawl space and you've had any sewage event touch that space, make sure whoever you call assesses the soil, not just the framing above it.
