## Why Spanaway Homes Present Specific Fire Damage Challenges

Pierce County's South End has a housing stock that spans several decades, and that range matters enormously in fire restoration. Homes built before the mid-1980s frequently contain materials, original cedar siding, older blown-in insulation, single-pane aluminum windows, that absorb smoke particulates deeply and off-gas long after the fire is out. The persistent damp of western Washington winters means that fire-damaged framing here rarely dries on its own; residual moisture from firefighting efforts gets trapped under smoke-stained surfaces and creates conditions where mold can colonize within days if materials aren't properly addressed.

Spanaway also sits in an area with a mix of on-slab and crawl-space foundations. A crawl-space home that sustains fire damage, even a contained kitchen or garage fire, can accumulate smoke and soot in the subfloor cavity in ways that aren't visible from above. That hidden contamination affects indoor air quality long after the visible damage is cleaned.

## Our Fire Damage Restoration Process in Spanaway

Every fire loss is different, but the sequence of work follows a disciplined order that our IICRC FSRT-certified technicians apply to every job.

**Emergency stabilization** comes first: boarding windows, tarping the roof if the structure is open to weather, and shutting off utilities if that hasn't already happened. In Spanaway's rainy season, which runs most of the year, an unsecured structure can absorb significant rain intrusion within hours of a fire, compounding the damage.

**Scope and documentation** follows immediately. We photograph and catalog every affected material before anything is moved or removed. This documentation is the foundation of your insurance claim and protects you if there are disputes about pre-loss condition.

**Soot and smoke removal** is where the technical work begins in earnest. Dry soot from fast-moving fires requires dry chemical sponges and HEPA vacuuming before any wet cleaning, introducing moisture too early smears soot deeper into porous surfaces. Wet smoke from slow, smoldering fires (common in upholstered furniture or insulation) requires different chemistry entirely. Our EPA Lead-Safe certified team also tests for lead paint before any sanding or demolition on homes built before 1978, a relevant consideration across much of Spanaway's older residential areas.

**Structural drying and deodorization** closes out the mitigation phase. Thermal fogging and hydroxyl generation address odor molecules that have penetrated framing and insulation; ozone is used only in unoccupied spaces and with appropriate clearance time. Moisture readings are logged daily until framing and subfloor materials meet the IICRC S700 standard for acceptable moisture content.
