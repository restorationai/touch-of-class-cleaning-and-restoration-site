Parkland's wet winters have a way of turning a single burst pipe or kitchen fire into a room-by-room recovery project, and when the structure is damaged, so is everything inside it. ## Why Parkland Properties See Contents Damage Differently

Parkland sits in the rain shadow's wet edge, west of the Cascades and close enough to the Puyallup River lowlands that sustained rainfall is the norm from October through April. Homes in this part of Pierce County, many built during the postwar expansion of the 1950s and 1960s, tend to have wood-framed construction with original hardwood floors, plaster-and-lath interior walls, and older HVAC systems that circulate smoke and odor through the house quickly after a fire. That combination matters for contents: smoke particles embed differently in porous plaster dust than in modern drywall, and the higher ambient humidity common here during the rainy season slows the off-gassing of odor from upholstered furniture and textiles.

Water losses carry a similar wrinkle. When a supply line fails behind a wall in an older Parkland home, moisture can wick laterally through original plaster before it ever shows on the surface. By the time a homeowner notices a soft spot in the floor or a musty smell, contents in adjacent rooms may already be affected, not just the room where the leak originated.

## Our Contents Restoration & Storage Process in Parkland

Every contents job starts with a room-by-room inventory before anything leaves the property. Each item is photographed, described, and assigned a condition rating. That documentation matters for two reasons: it supports your insurance claim, and it means nothing gets lost or confused during the cleaning and storage phase.

From there, the process branches based on what each item needs:

- **Pack-out**: Affected contents are carefully packed and transported to our secure facility in Lakewood, keeping them out of the damaged environment while structural work proceeds.
- **Cleaning and deodorization**: Textiles go through specialized laundering. Hard surfaces, furniture, electronics housings, cabinetry, are cleaned using methods matched to the material. Smoke-affected items receive ultrasonic or ozone treatment depending on the substrate. Our IICRC FSRT-certified process guides smoke and soot cleaning; our IICRC WRT certification covers water-damaged contents.
- **Climate-controlled storage**: Items are held in a monitored storage environment until your home is ready to receive them. Pierce County's damp winters make this step more than a formality, contents returned to a still-drying structure can re-absorb moisture and undo the cleaning work.
- **Pack-back**: Once structural drying and repairs are complete, we return and reinstall contents to their original rooms, cross-checking against the initial inventory.

## Parkland Insurance Coordination

Contents losses are frequently underdocumented on insurance claims, which means homeowners sometimes receive settlements that don't reflect the actual replacement cost of what was damaged. Our room-by-room inventory, condition photographs, and cleaning records give your adjuster a complete picture of the loss. We work directly with most major carriers and can communicate with your adjuster throughout the process so you're not translating between two parties.

If your policy includes additional living expenses (ALE) coverage, the pack-out and storage costs are often a covered line item, something worth confirming with your adjuster early in the claim.

## Local Note

Parkland's older housing stock, particularly homes built in the 1950s and early 1960s, often used oil-based paints and varnishes on interior woodwork and cabinetry. After a fire or smoke event, those finishes can reactivate and bleed odor compounds for weeks if the cleaning approach treats them the same as modern water-based finishes. Identifying original oil-finish woodwork during the initial inventory changes the cleaning protocol: solvent-compatible cleaning agents and extended deodorization cycles are needed to fully neutralize the odor rather than just masking it at the surface.
