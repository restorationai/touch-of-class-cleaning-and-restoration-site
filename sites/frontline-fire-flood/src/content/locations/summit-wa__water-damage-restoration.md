## Why Summit Properties See Water Damage Issues

Much of the housing stock in and around Summit sits on crawlspace foundations rather than slabs, which is typical across the South Sound. That space under the floor is exactly where water goes first after a supply line fails or a washing machine hose lets go, and it's also where it lingers longest. Pierce County's wet season runs long, and when ground saturation is already high, a plumbing leak or roof intrusion has nowhere easy to drain. Add in the number of homes still on original galvanized or early copper supply lines, and unincorporated-area properties on septic systems, and you get a pattern we see often: water damage that starts small and stays hidden in a crawlspace or behind a water heater closet until the smell or the sag in the flooring gives it away.

## Our Water Damage Restoration Process in Summit

We start with extraction, pulling standing water from flooring, crawlspaces, and wall cavities using truck-mounted and portable equipment depending on access. From there we map moisture with meters and thermal imaging to find out how far the water traveled, which matters more in crawlspace construction than it would in a home built on a slab, because water can run downhill under the house well past the point where it entered. Structural drying follows, set up and monitored to the IICRC S500 standard, with daily readings until materials are back to a dry standard rather than just "dry to the touch." If insulation, subfloor, or drywall has absorbed water past the point of reasonable drying, we remove and document it before rebuild.

## Equipment & Methods We Use for Water Damage

Crawlspace access points are often tighter and lower than a standard basement, so we use low-profile extraction wands and air movers sized for confined spaces rather than open-floor equipment. Dehumidifiers are set based on the cubic footage of the affected area and the vapor barrier condition underneath, since a torn or missing vapor barrier changes how fast ground moisture re-enters a dried space. We also pull samples for moisture content in wood framing using a penetrating meter, not just a surface scan, because surface-dry framing in a crawlspace can still be wet several inches in.

## Local note

Older Summit-area homes built with crawlspace vents instead of a sealed, conditioned crawlspace tend to dry slower after a water event because outside humidity is working against the drying equipment rather than with it. On those properties we typically run drying equipment longer and recheck moisture content more often before calling the space dry, rather than relying on a standard drying timeline that assumes a sealed crawlspace.
