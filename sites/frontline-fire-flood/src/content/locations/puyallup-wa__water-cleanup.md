When a supply line fails or a storm backs up a crawl space drain here, the water doesn't just pool; it moves into the substructure fast, especially in the ranch-style and split-level homes built across the valley during the 1960s and 1970s. ## Why Puyallup Properties See Water Damage Differently

The Puyallup River valley's clay-heavy soil is slow to drain, and that matters the moment water finds a path into your foundation or crawl space. Unlike sandy or loamy ground that absorbs overflow, compacted clay pushes hydrostatic pressure against stem walls and slab edges, a condition that shows up repeatedly in homes built before modern drainage codes required perimeter drain tile. Add to that the region's persistent winter rain cycles, where ground saturation can stay near capacity from November through March, and a single plumbing failure or roof breach can introduce far more water than the structure can naturally shed.

Older construction in the valley also tends to feature pier-and-beam foundations with vented crawl spaces. Those crawl spaces are efficient at collecting moisture and slow to dry without mechanical intervention. Vapor barriers in homes of that era were often thin polyethylene sheeting, or absent entirely, leaving floor joists and subfloor sheathing exposed to whatever the soil releases upward and whatever a leak delivers from above.

## Our Water Cleanup Process in Puyallup

When we arrive, the first priority is stopping ongoing intrusion, shutting off the water source, identifying secondary pathways, and assessing whether the crawl space or basement has taken on standing water. We use thermal imaging cameras alongside moisture meters to map saturation behind walls and under flooring before any materials are moved. This step matters especially in Puyallup's older housing stock, where plaster-over-lath wall systems can read nearly dry at the surface while the lath and framing behind them hold significant moisture.

Extraction comes next. Truck-mounted and portable extractors pull standing water from finished floors, carpeting, and crawl spaces. Once bulk water is removed, we deploy commercial desiccant and refrigerant dehumidifiers alongside high-velocity air movers, calibrated to the actual cubic footage and material types in your home, not a one-size estimate. Drying logs are recorded daily and available to you and your insurance adjuster throughout the job. We dry to IICRC S500 standard, which means documented moisture readings at goal, not a visual check.

## Puyallup Insurance Coordination

Most homeowner policies cover sudden and accidental water losses, a burst pipe, a failed appliance supply line, or storm-driven water entry through a compromised roof or window. What carriers scrutinize is the documentation: moisture readings at the time of loss, photographic evidence of affected materials, and a clear scope of work. We photograph and log everything from the initial walkthrough forward, and we communicate directly with your adjuster so the claim file is complete before any dispute arises. If your policy includes an additional living expense provision and the loss displaces your household, we can speak to that timeline as well.
