## Why Renton Properties See Contents Damage Differently

Renton's housing stock spans a wide range of eras and construction types. Older neighborhoods closer to downtown feature mid-century homes with original hardwood floors, plaster walls, and wood-framed cabinetry, materials that absorb smoke odor and water deeply and release them slowly. Newer construction in the hillside areas tends toward engineered wood and composite finishes that behave differently under heat and humidity stress. That variation matters when deciding how aggressively to treat a piece of furniture or whether a cabinet box can be restored on-site versus packed out for controlled drying.

The city also sits in a bowl geography that traps moisture after rain events, and King County's wet season runs long, from October well into April. During those months, ambient relative humidity indoors can stay elevated even in undamaged rooms, which means contents that aren't moved to a controlled environment can continue absorbing moisture long after the source loss is resolved.

## Our Contents Restoration & Storage Process in Renton

The first step is a room-by-room inventory. Every item is photographed, catalogued, and assessed before anything leaves the property. That documentation matters for insurance purposes and gives you a clear record of what was removed and in what condition.

Items that can be restored on-site, hard-surface furniture, metal fixtures, certain electronics, are treated with appropriate cleaning agents and dried using industrial air movers and dehumidifiers calibrated to the room conditions. Items that need more controlled work are packed out to a secure climate-controlled storage facility. There, contents go through ultrasonic cleaning, ozone or hydroxyl treatment for smoke and odor, freeze-drying for water-saturated documents and photographs, and structural drying for wood pieces.

Once restoration is complete and your property is cleared for re-occupancy, contents are returned, unpacked, and placed back in their original positions. The inventory log closes out at that point, with a final condition report provided for your records and your adjuster.
