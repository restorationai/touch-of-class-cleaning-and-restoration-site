## Why Fircrest Properties See Water Damage Issues

Fircrest is a small, mostly residential city surrounded by Tacoma, and a lot of its housing stock dates back to the post-war building boom, meaning original cast iron drain lines, galvanized supply pipe, and crawlspace foundations that were never engineered for modern plumbing fixtures. Galvanized pipe corrodes from the inside out, and by the time a pinhole leak shows up as a wet spot on the ceiling or a soft patch in the flooring, water has often been tracking through wall cavities or along floor joists for days. Add in the Puget Sound region's wet winters, and crawlspaces across the South Sound area stay damp enough that a single plumbing failure can push humidity into mold-friendly territory within 48 to 72 hours. Homes with finished basements, which are more common in Fircrest and neighboring University Place than in newer Pierce County subdivisions, carry extra risk because basement-level water damage is harder to spot early and harder to dry once it's set in.

## Our Water Damage Restoration Process in Fircrest

The first visit is about stopping the loss and mapping how far the water has traveled, not just where it's pooling. We use moisture meters and thermal imaging to trace water behind baseboards, under subfloor, and into wall cavities, since a mid-century Fircrest home with plaster or lath-and-plaster walls hides moisture differently than a newer drywall build. Extraction comes first, pulling standing water out of carpet, pad, and hardwood before it wicks further. Then we set air movers and dehumidifiers calibrated to the room's actual moisture load, not a generic equipment count, and track drying progress daily against the IICRC S500 standard until materials read dry rather than just dry to the touch. On older homes we also check for galvanized or cast iron pipe runs near the damaged area, since a repeat failure a few feet away from the original leak is common once one section of old plumbing has already given out.
