## Why Bremerton Properties Are Especially Vulnerable After Structural Damage

A large share of Bremerton's residential neighborhoods were built in the mid-20th century, many during the post-war naval expansion tied to Puget Sound Naval Shipyard. Homes from that era typically feature wood-framed construction with original single-pane windows, older roof sheathing, and in some cases, cedar shake or composition roofing that has been repaired and re-repaired over decades. When fire or storm damage breaches that envelope, the underlying framing absorbs moisture fast, and the marine climate here means there is almost never a dry window to buy time.

Bremerton also sits in a rain shadow gap that can shift quickly. A property that looks dry at 8 a.m. can be taking on wind-driven rain by noon. That unpredictability makes temporary weatherproofing, heavy-gauge polyethylene tarping, plywood boarding, and door-frame bracing, a genuine emergency measure rather than a precaution.

## Our Emergency Board-Up and Tarping Process in Bremerton

When you call, a crew mobilizes from our Lakewood base and heads across the Tacoma Narrows Bridge toward Bremerton. The route via SR-16 and SR-3 keeps us moving even during peak commute hours, and we communicate arrival time as soon as we're on the road.

On arrival, the crew assesses every breach point, not just the obvious one. Fire damage in particular tends to compromise multiple roof sections and soffit areas that aren't visible from the street. We document each opening with photographs before any material goes up, which matters for your insurance claim. Then:

- **Roof tarping**: We use reinforced polyethylene sheeting weighted and fastened to undamaged decking, not just draped over the peak. In Bremerton's wind conditions, an improperly anchored tarp can cause secondary damage within hours.
- **Window and door boarding**: Structural-grade plywood is cut to fit and secured with screws rather than nails, which holds better against sustained wind and discourages unauthorized entry.
- **Perimeter check**: We confirm that any compromised siding, garage doors, or crawl-space vents are also addressed, openings that are easy to miss and easy for moisture and pests to exploit.

All materials and methods align with IICRC standards for emergency services, and the crew holds IICRC FSRT (Fire & Smoke Restoration) and WRT (Water Restoration) certifications, so they understand what's happening inside the structure, not just on its surface.

## Bremerton Insurance Coordination

Most homeowner policies cover emergency board-up and tarping as part of the loss, it falls under "reasonable measures to protect the property from further damage," language that appears in nearly every standard policy. What matters to adjusters is documentation: photos of the breach before boarding, a written scope of what was secured and why, and an itemized materials list.

Frontline Fire & Flood produces that documentation as a standard part of every job, not as an add-on. We work directly with insurance carriers and can communicate with your adjuster so you're not playing phone-tag in the middle of an already stressful situation. If you have a mortgage, your lender may also require proof that the structure is secured before releasing claim funds, our written report satisfies that requirement.

## Local Note

Older homes in Bremerton's hillside neighborhoods, the areas that climb away from the waterfront toward the ridge lines, often have steep roof pitches that were designed to shed the region's heavy rainfall. That pitch is an asset in normal weather but a complication during emergency tarping: standard flat-tarp techniques don't hold on a 10/12 or steeper slope when wind hits from the water side. Our crews carry ridge-cap framing materials specifically for steep-pitch applications, which keeps the tarp seated through the kind of sustained southwest wind that Bremerton sees regularly in fall and winter storm systems.
