## Why DuPont Properties See Sewage Backup Issues

DuPont's development history is a little unusual for Pierce County. Much of the city's residential core was built in concentrated phases, first around the historic company town footprint, then in planned communities constructed in the 1990s and 2000s. That means you have two distinct plumbing generations sitting side by side: older cast-iron and clay-tile laterals that have had decades to corrode or root-intrude, and newer PVC systems that are generally more resilient but can still be overwhelmed by the volume of stormwater that funnels through the area during a heavy Puget Sound rain event.

Pierce County's wet season runs long, meaningful rainfall from October through April, and the water table in low-lying parts of DuPont rises accordingly. When the ground is already saturated, municipal sewer systems can experience surcharge conditions, pushing sewage backward through the path of least resistance: your floor drain, your toilet, your washing machine standpipe. Septic systems on the edges of the city face a related problem: drain fields that drain well in August can become sluggish or fail entirely when the soil is holding maximum moisture in January.

## Our Sewage Cleanup and Sanitization Process in DuPont

Raw sewage is classified as Category 3 water, the most contaminated classification in the IICRC S500 standard, and that classification drives every decision we make on-site. The process isn't simply pumping out the visible water and spraying a surface disinfectant.

When we arrive, the first priority is stopping ongoing exposure: ventilating the space, identifying the source, and containing the affected zone so contamination doesn't migrate on foot traffic into clean areas of the home. From there:

- **Extraction and bulk removal**, standing sewage is extracted with truck-mounted or portable units rated for Category 3 material. Porous materials that have absorbed sewage, carpet, pad, certain types of insulation, compromised drywall, are removed and bagged for disposal per Pierce County waste handling requirements.
- **Structural disinfection**, all affected hard surfaces are treated with EPA-registered disinfectants appropriate for pathogen reduction. This includes subfloor sheathing, concrete slab, framing members, and any HVAC components in the affected zone.
- **Structural drying**, after disinfection, industrial air movers and desiccant or refrigerant dehumidifiers run until moisture readings in framing and substrate materials return to acceptable baselines. In DuPont's climate, ambient humidity during the rainy season means drying takes longer than it would in a drier region, equipment stays on-site until the numbers confirm completion, not on a fixed-day schedule.
- **Post-remediation verification**, we document moisture readings and surface conditions before closing out the job, providing records that support your insurance claim and confirm the space is safe to rebuild.

Frontline Fire & Flood holds IICRC certification in Water Restoration (WRT), Applied Structural Drying (ASD), and Applied Microbial Remediation (AMRT), and is EPA Lead-Safe Certified, relevant in any DuPont home where older painted surfaces may be disturbed during material removal.

Sewage backup coverage is one of the more commonly misunderstood parts of a homeowner's policy. Standard HO-3 policies frequently exclude sewer backup unless a specific rider has been added, and many homeowners don't discover the gap until they're standing in an inch of contaminated water. We document the loss thoroughly: photographs of affected materials before removal, moisture mapping, and a written scope of work that gives your adjuster a clear picture of what happened and what it takes to remediate it correctly. DuPont's planned communities built in the late 1990s and early 2000s often feature homes with finished basements or daylight lower levels, a selling point that becomes a liability during a sewage event.
