## Why DuPont Properties See Elevated Storm Damage Risk

DuPont's development history creates a specific vulnerability profile. Much of the residential construction here dates to the late 1990s and early 2000s, an era when engineered wood products like OSB sheathing were standard. OSB is efficient and strong under normal conditions, but it swells and delaminates faster than older plywood when storm-driven rain finds a breach in the building envelope. A missing shingle or a cracked ridge cap that would be a minor issue in a dry climate becomes a structural concern here when three inches of rain can fall in 24 hours during a Puget Sound windstorm.

The mature tree canopy throughout DuPont, particularly the stands of fir and cedar that line residential streets, means tree-impact damage is a recurring pattern after severe weather. A fallen limb that punches through a roof deck exposes the interior to immediate water intrusion, and the clock starts immediately: mold can begin colonizing wet framing within 24 to 48 hours under the right temperature and humidity conditions. The Puget Sound lowland climate, with its mild temperatures and persistent moisture, provides exactly those conditions for most of the year.

## Our Storm Damage Restoration Process in DuPont

Every storm job starts with a full exterior and interior assessment before any material is moved or removed. We document the breach point, whether that's a tree-impact zone, a failed roof-to-wall connection, or a flooded crawl space, and photograph the full moisture migration path, not just the visible damage. This documentation matters for your insurance claim and for making sure we don't dry the surface while saturated framing stays wet underneath.

Once the scope is clear, the process moves in a defined sequence:

- **Emergency tarping and board-up** to stop active water intrusion
- **Standing water extraction** from affected interior spaces, including crawl spaces common in DuPont's residential builds
- **Structural drying** using commercial desiccant dehumidifiers and air movers, calibrated to the actual moisture readings in walls, subfloor, and framing, not a fixed number of days
- **Debris and damaged-material removal**, coordinated with your adjuster's scope
- **Reconstruction** of affected assemblies once drying logs confirm target moisture levels are reached

Our team holds IICRC certifications in Water Damage Restoration (WRT) and Structural Drying (ASD), and we dry to the IICRC S500 standard, a process that uses daily moisture mapping rather than visual inspection alone.

DuPont's newer subdivisions were built on land that was previously part of a large industrial and military installation, and the underlying soil in parts of town has a higher clay content than you'd find in older Pierce County neighborhoods.
