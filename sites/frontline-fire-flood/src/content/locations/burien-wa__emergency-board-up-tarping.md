## Why Burien Properties Face Elevated Exposure After a Loss

The housing stock throughout Burien skews older. Many single-family homes were built in the 1950s through the 1970s, when construction relied on single-pane windows, wood siding, and roof assemblies that are now decades past their design life. When a fire damages a soffit or a windstorm lifts a section of aging composition shingles, the underlying sheathing is often already softened by years of moisture cycling. That means a breach that looks minor on the surface can expose framing that absorbs rainwater quickly and dries slowly, a pattern that turns a board-up call into a mold-prevention call within 48 to 72 hours if the opening stays unprotected.

Burien also sits close to the flight paths and industrial corridors near Sea-Tac, which means vibration stress on older window glazing is a real, recurring factor. A fire in an adjacent unit or a vehicle impact can shatter windows that were already crazed or poorly sealed, leaving openings that need immediate closure before the next rain band moves through.

## Our Emergency Board-Up and Tarping Process in Burien

When a call comes in, the crew assesses the structure before any material goes up. That means walking the perimeter, checking roof load if tarping is needed, and identifying whether the framing around a damaged opening is sound enough to anchor plywood securely. On older Burien homes with wood window surrounds that have been painted over multiple times, the anchoring points sometimes need to shift to the structural framing rather than the casing, a detail that matters for both security and preventing additional cosmetic damage during installation.

For roof tarping after fire or storm damage, the crew uses weighted and secured poly tarps rated for Pacific Northwest conditions, not the lightweight sheeting that lifts in the first gust off the Sound. Ridge-to-eave runs are secured at the peak and weighted or fastened at the drip edge so water sheds away from the breach rather than pooling at the tarp edge and wicking back under. On steeper-pitch roofs common in some of Burien's hillside neighborhoods, fall protection protocols are part of every tarping job.

Board-up work follows a similar logic: plywood is cut to fit, not approximated, and fastened in a way that a property owner or adjuster can clearly see the breach has been closed, not just covered. Every opening is photographed before and after for the insurance file.

## Burien Insurance Coordination

Most homeowner policies cover emergency protective measures, board-up and tarping are typically classified as mitigation, not repair, which means they fall under the same claim as the underlying loss. Frontline documents the scope of work with photographs, measurements, and a written scope before and after installation. That documentation goes directly to the adjuster and is formatted to match what carriers expect for a mitigation line item. If your policy has a managed repair program or a preferred vendor list, ask your adjuster whether independent mitigation work is still covered, in most cases it is, and we can work alongside whatever contractor the carrier assigns for permanent repairs.

## Local Note

Burien's older ranch-style homes frequently have low-slope or nearly flat roof sections over additions and attached garages, a design common in mid-century King County construction. These sections drain poorly compared to a standard pitched roof, and a tarp that isn't lapped and weighted correctly will pond water directly over the breach rather than shedding it. Crews working in Burien learn quickly to treat these low-slope sections differently: the tarp run needs to extend well past the damaged area and be anchored so that standing water is directed toward the existing drip edge, not held against the opening.
