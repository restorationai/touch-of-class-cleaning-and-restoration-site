## Why Bremerton Properties Are Particularly Vulnerable After a Fire

Bremerton's housing stock tells a lot of the story. Much of the city's residential inventory was built during the mid-20th century naval expansion, when Puget Sound Naval Shipyard drove rapid construction across the hillside neighborhoods that climb away from the waterfront. Homes from that era commonly feature balloon-frame or platform-frame construction with minimal fire blocking between wall cavities, which means smoke and heat travel vertically through wall assemblies far more aggressively than in newer builds.

Older plaster-and-lath walls, common in homes built before the 1960s, absorb smoke odor compounds differently than modern drywall. The lath substrate holds volatile organic compounds deep in the material, which is why a surface wipe-down is never sufficient, odor resurfaces days later as temperatures fluctuate. Proper restoration requires thermal fogging or hydroxyl treatment to reach those embedded layers.

Kitsap County's rainy season also extends the risk window. A fire that damages roofing or exterior walls in November or February leaves the interior exposed to rainfall almost immediately. Water intrusion into fire-damaged framing accelerates mold colonization, typically within 24 to 72 hours under saturated conditions, so emergency tarping and board-up is not optional; it's the first line of defense before any cleaning begins.

## Our Fire Damage Restoration Process in Bremerton

The process starts with a thorough scope assessment, not a quick visual walk-through, but a methodical inspection of structural assemblies, HVAC pathways, attic spaces, and crawlspaces. Smoke follows air movement, and in a multi-story Bremerton home on a hillside lot, that can mean soot has traveled two floors away from the fire's origin point.

Once the scope is documented, the sequence runs:

- **Emergency stabilization**: board-up, roof tarping, and utility isolation to stop secondary damage.
- **Content pack-out**: salvageable belongings are catalogued, packed, and transported to a climate-controlled facility for cleaning and deodorization.
- **Structural cleaning**: HEPA-filtered negative air machines contain particulates while crews clean framing, subfloor, and wall cavities using dry-ice blasting or chemical sponge methods calibrated to the material.
- **Deodorization**: thermal fogging and hydroxyl generation address odor at the molecular level, not just at the surface.
- **Controlled demolition and rebuild coordination**: materials that cannot be restored are removed, documented for the insurance claim, and replaced under the same contractor relationship, no handoff gap between mitigation and reconstruction.

Every step is documented with photos and moisture readings, which feeds directly into the insurance claim file.

## Local Note: Crawlspace Smoke Migration in Older Bremerton Homes

Many of Bremerton's mid-century homes sit on pier-and-beam or partial-basement foundations with vented crawlspaces. After a fire, smoke particulates and odor compounds settle into crawlspace insulation and vapor barriers through HVAC returns and floor penetrations, a detail that gets missed when restoration crews focus only on the living space above. In Bremerton's damp climate, a contaminated crawlspace also becomes a moisture trap. Inspecting and treating below the subfloor is a standard part of every fire scope here, not an add-on.
