University Place sits on a west-facing bluff above the Narrows, and when a Pacific storm rolls in off Puget Sound, it doesn't just bring rain, it brings wind-driven debris, snapped branches, and the kind of sudden structural exposure that turns a broken window or lifted roof section into a cascading problem within hours. When fire, storm, or impact leaves your home or business open to the elements, emergency board-up and tarping is the first line of defense between a manageable repair and a gutted interior.

## Why University Place Properties Face Elevated Board-Up and Tarping Needs

The housing stock in University Place skews older than much of Pierce County. Many homes in the city's western and hillside corridors were built in the 1950s through 1970s, when roofing materials and window framing standards were substantially different from current code. Aging wood-framed windows are more likely to shatter or fail under impact. Original roof sheathing, often thinner and with fewer fasteners per square foot than modern construction, is more susceptible to uplift during high-wind events.

The bluff geography compounds this. Properties along the higher elevations catch sustained gusts that flatten out before reaching lower-lying Lakewood or Tacoma neighborhoods. A storm that causes minor branch damage elsewhere can strip flashing, crack skylights, or peel back ridge caps on University Place homes. Once a roof or wall is breached, the interior is exposed to the same moisture that makes this corner of the South Sound one of the wetter parts of western Washington, and mold colonization can begin in as little as 24 to 48 hours in an unprotected, humid structure.

## Our Emergency Board-Up and Tarping Process in University Place

When you call Frontline Fire & Flood at (253) 200-0503, we dispatch from our Lakewood headquarters around the clock. The crew arrives with pre-cut plywood panels, heavy-gauge polyethylene tarps, furring strips, and the fastening hardware needed to secure openings without causing additional damage to your structure.

For fire-damaged properties, the sequence matters: we assess structural stability before any crew member enters the affected area, then work outward, sealing window and door openings with plywood to deter both weather and unauthorized entry, then moving to the roof to tarp any burn-through or compromised sections. Tarps are secured with weighted battens and screw-fastened furring strips rather than nails alone, which reduces the risk of tarp failure in the next wind event.

For storm or impact damage, broken windows, garage doors off their tracks, roof sections lifted by a fallen tree, the priority is speed and coverage. We photograph every opening before and after boarding, which creates a documented record your insurance adjuster will reference when scoping the claim.
