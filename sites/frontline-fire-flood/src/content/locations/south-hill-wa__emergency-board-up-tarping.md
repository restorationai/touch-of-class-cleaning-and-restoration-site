## Why South Hill Properties Face Elevated Board-Up and Tarping Needs

Much of South Hill developed rapidly through the 1980s and 1990s, meaning a significant share of the housing stock is now 30 to 40 years old, old enough for roof decking to have absorbed years of moisture cycling, but not old enough to carry the structural redundancy of mid-century construction. When a windstorm peels back aging composition shingles or a house fire vents through the attic, the underlying OSB sheathing is often already compromised and far more vulnerable to rain intrusion than it appears from the ground.

The area's clay-heavy soils retain water rather than draining it, so a roof breach during a wet-season storm doesn't just threaten the attic, water migrates laterally through saturated insulation and can reach wall cavities within hours. A tarp installed promptly and weighted or strapped correctly (not just draped) is the difference between a roofing repair and a mold remediation job.

Broken windows and compromised doors after a fire or forced entry carry a separate risk in this part of Pierce County: the combination of mild temperatures and persistent humidity creates near-ideal conditions for mold colonization. The IICRC S520 standard notes that mold can begin colonizing wet organic material in as little as 24 to 48 hours, a timeline that makes same-day board-up critical, not optional.

## Our Emergency Board-Up and Tarping Process in South Hill

When you call, the first questions we ask are practical: What type of opening, window, door, roof breach, or wall? What's the weather doing right now, and what's the forecast for the next 48 hours? Those answers shape what crew and materials we load before leaving Lakewood.

**Board-up:** We use 3/4-inch plywood cut and fitted to the opening, secured with structural screws rather than nails, and sealed at the edges with foam backer rod where gaps exist. For fire-damaged frames that can't hold fasteners reliably, we build a secondary frame anchored to surrounding structure. Every board-up is photographed before and after for your insurance file.

**Roof tarping:** We use 6-mil polyethylene tarps minimum, extended at least four feet beyond the damaged area on all sides and anchored with 2×4 battens screwed through the tarp into the decking, not sandbags, which shift in wind. On steeper pitches common to South Hill's 1990s-era two-stories, we use fall-arrest equipment and ridge-line anchoring. The tarp is inspected at the ridge and eave to confirm water will shed off the roof edge, not pool and funnel into the breach.

All work is documented with timestamped photos and a written scope that your adjuster can use directly.

## Local Note

South Hill's newer subdivisions, particularly those built on graded lots in the eastern portions of the community, often have roof pitches steeper than the regional average, a design choice that sheds snow load but creates a tarping challenge. A standard flat-lay tarp on a 10:12 or 12:12 pitch will migrate downslope within hours in wind-driven rain. Our crews carry extra batten stock specifically for high-pitch applications, and we pre-drill the battens before going up to reduce time on a steep, potentially wet surface. It's a small adjustment that makes a real difference in how long the tarp holds before your roofer can get there.
