## Why Tumwater Properties See Repeated Storm Damage

The geography that makes Tumwater scenic also makes it vulnerable. The city's position at the base of the Capitol State Forest means that storm cells stall and intensify before moving northeast toward Olympia. Sustained winds strip shingles, push water under flashing, and send mature Douglas fir and big-leaf maple limbs through rooflines with enough force to breach structural framing, not just surface materials.

The housing stock compounds the exposure. Much of Tumwater's residential development happened in waves: modest post-war construction in older sections near the Deschutes, then suburban expansion through the 1970s and 1980s, and more recent infill closer to the I-5 corridor. Homes built before the 1990s often have original cedar shake or composition shingles that have lost their storm rating over time, and wall assemblies that lack modern weather-resistant barriers. When wind-driven rain finds a gap in that envelope, even a small one, it can migrate into wall cavities and attic spaces before a homeowner notices anything from inside.

## Our Storm Damage Restoration Process in Tumwater

The first priority after a storm is stopping the loss from growing. That means emergency tarping of breached roofs, boarding compromised windows, and removing debris that is still in contact with the structure. Frontline carries tarping and shoring materials on every response vehicle, so stabilization happens the same visit as the damage assessment.

Once the structure is secured, the work shifts to moisture mapping. Wind-driven rain is deceptive, it enters through one point and travels laterally through insulation, along rafters, and down interior wall cavities, surfacing as a wet spot feet away from the actual entry point. Thermal imaging and calibrated moisture meters let us trace the full path of intrusion before any drying equipment is placed, which matters for both effective drying and accurate documentation for your insurance claim.

Structural drying in Tumwater's cool, humid climate requires equipment sized for the ambient conditions. Standard drying timelines assume indoor temperatures and vapor pressure gradients that the Pacific Northwest doesn't always cooperate with. We adjust airflow, dehumidifier placement, and monitoring intervals based on daily psychrometric readings rather than a fixed schedule.

If tree impact has damaged framing, we scope the structural repair concurrent with drying so the rebuild phase begins as soon as materials are dry, not weeks later when a separate contractor finally arrives.

## Local Note

One thing that catches homeowners off guard in Tumwater is how quickly the ground saturates during a multi-day storm event. The soils in much of Thurston County have a significant clay component, and once that clay layer is at capacity, surface water has nowhere to go, it backs up against foundation walls, finds any crack in a crawl space vent, and enters below grade. After a major windstorm, the visible roof and siding damage gets attention first, but the crawl space is often where the secondary moisture problem is already building. A thorough storm damage assessment in this area always includes a crawl space inspection, not just the above-grade envelope.
