Kent sits in the Green River Valley, one of the wettest corridors in the Puget Sound region, where marine air funnels inland and settles against the Cascade foothills. That persistent dampness, compounded by the valley's historically high water table and the region's clay-heavy soils that drain poorly after heavy rain, creates conditions where mold doesn't just appear after a visible leak. It colonizes quietly behind drywall, under subfloor sheathing, and inside wall cavities long before a homeowner notices a musty smell or a discolored patch. Frontline Fire & Flood's IICRC-certified mold remediation team responds 24/7 to properties across Kent, working to contain the problem, remove affected materials properly, and restore air quality.

## Why Kent Properties See Mold Issues

The Green River Valley's geography does a lot of the work for mold. Clay-dominant soils throughout the valley floor retain water after the region's frequent fall and winter rain events, keeping ground moisture elevated for weeks at a time. Homes built on slab foundations, common in Kent's residential developments from the 1970s and 1980s, are particularly susceptible to moisture intrusion at the slab edge and through concrete block walls in garages and utility rooms.

Older housing stock adds another layer of risk. Kent has a meaningful share of homes built before modern vapor barriers were standard practice. In those structures, crawl spaces often rely on passive ventilation that becomes inadequate during extended wet seasons. Standing moisture in an unencapsulated crawl space can raise relative humidity throughout the floor system above 60%, the threshold at which mold colonization accelerates significantly. Condensation on cold-water supply lines, slow drips at P-traps, and even improperly vented bathroom exhaust fans are all common mold triggers in this building era.

## Our Mold Remediation Process in Kent

Every remediation starts with a thorough moisture investigation, not just a visual inspection. Mold visible on a surface is rarely the full story, thermal imaging and calibrated moisture meters help locate saturation behind finishes before any demolition begins. Once the scope is defined, the affected area is isolated with negative air pressure containment to prevent spores from migrating to clean areas of the home during work.

Affected porous materials, drywall, insulation, compromised wood framing, are removed and bagged per EPA guidelines. Structural surfaces that can be retained are HEPA-vacuumed, treated with an EPA-registered antimicrobial, and dried to documented moisture levels before any rebuild begins. The team holds IICRC AMRT (Applied Microbial Remediation Technician) certification, meaning the remediation protocol follows a defined standard rather than a judgment call made on the fly. Where lead-based paint is a possibility in older Kent homes, the crew works under EPA Lead-Safe certification requirements.

Air scrubbers with HEPA filtration run throughout the project and during final clearance. Depending on the scope of the loss and any HOA or lender requirements, an independent industrial hygienist can be coordinated for post-remediation air sampling before the containment comes down.
