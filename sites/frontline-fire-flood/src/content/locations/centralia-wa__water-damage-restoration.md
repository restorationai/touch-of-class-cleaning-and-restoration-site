## Why Centralia Properties See Water Damage Issues

The geography here works against homeowners in ways that aren't always obvious until something goes wrong. The Chehalis River and its tributaries have historically flooded portions of Lewis County, and even properties well outside the floodplain can experience saturated soil that pushes groundwater against foundation walls during sustained rain events, which are common from October through March.

Centralia's housing stock adds another layer of complexity. A significant portion of the city's residential neighborhoods were built before 1960, meaning many homes have original plaster-and-lath wall systems, older cast-iron or galvanized steel plumbing, and crawl spaces with minimal vapor barriers. Galvanized pipe corrodes from the inside out over decades; pinhole leaks often go undetected behind walls until a ceiling stain or musty smell prompts investigation. By that point, the cavity behind the plaster may have been wet for weeks.

Crawl spaces in this region are particularly vulnerable. Inadequate cross-ventilation combined with soil moisture from seasonal rain creates conditions where vapor accumulates under the subfloor, softening joists and promoting mold colonization, sometimes without any visible leak above.

## Our Water Damage Restoration Process in Centralia

Every job starts with a thorough moisture assessment using thermal imaging and calibrated moisture meters. Water visible on the surface is rarely the full picture; in older construction, water migrates laterally through subfloor assemblies and wicks up into wall cavities well beyond the obvious wet zone. We map the actual moisture boundary before any equipment is placed.

Once the scope is established, extraction comes first, truck-mounted or portable units pull standing water from flooring, carpets, and crawl spaces. Structural drying follows using industrial-grade desiccant or refrigerant dehumidifiers and high-velocity air movers positioned to create directed airflow through wall cavities and under subfloor systems. Drying is monitored against the IICRC S500 standard, with daily moisture readings logged until affected materials reach acceptable equilibrium.

In homes with plaster walls, common throughout older Centralia neighborhoods, drying timelines run longer than they would in a comparable modern drywall structure. Plaster releases absorbed moisture slowly, and rushing the process by closing out equipment too early leads to secondary damage and mold growth behind the surface. We document every reading so your insurance adjuster has a clear, defensible drying log.
