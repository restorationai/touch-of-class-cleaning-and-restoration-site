## Why Graham Properties See Distinctive Fire Damage Patterns

Much of Graham's housing stock was built between the 1980s and early 2000s, with a significant share of homes relying on propane or oil heat rather than natural gas, Pierce County's rural fringe never received the same utility infrastructure as Puyallup or Lakewood to the north. Propane-related ignition events tend to produce intense, fast-moving fires that char structural framing quickly and leave behind a particularly oily, protein-heavy soot. That residue bonds to surfaces differently than wood-smoke soot: it smears when wiped dry and requires chemical sponging before any wet cleaning.

Graham's lots are also larger than most suburban parcels, which means homes often have detached garages, outbuildings, and covered equipment storage. Fire can spread laterally across those structures before it's contained, expanding the restoration scope well beyond the primary dwelling. Volunteer fire coverage in this part of Pierce County means response times to outlying addresses can run longer than in denser municipalities, by the time suppression is complete, smoke has had more time to migrate.

## Our Fire Damage Restoration Process in Graham

Restoration begins with a full structural and contents assessment, not just the rooms with visible char, but every space smoke could have reached. In Graham homes with vaulted ceilings or open-truss construction, common in the area's ranch and craftsman-style builds, smoke stratifies at the peak and deposits heavily on framing members that are easy to overlook on a quick walkthrough.

From there, the process moves through emergency board-up and tarping to secure the structure, controlled demolition of unsalvageable materials, HEPA-filtered air scrubbing, and chemical soot removal calibrated to the surface type, drywall, wood paneling, concrete block, or OSB sheathing each responds to different cleaning agents. Structural drying runs concurrently if suppression water is present. Odor neutralization uses thermal fogging and hydroxyl generation rather than masking agents, because smoke odor trapped in wall cavities will return through the paint if the source isn't addressed. The IICRC FSRT (Fire & Smoke Restoration Technician) certification our team holds governs the sequencing of these steps, it's not a linear checklist but a decision tree based on what the inspection reveals.

## Local Note

Graham homes built in the 1990s on larger lots frequently used OSB (oriented strand board) sheathing rather than plywood for exterior walls, a cost-effective choice at the time that creates a specific challenge after a fire. OSB absorbs smoke odor more deeply than plywood and swells significantly when wetted by suppression water, which can make it look structurally compromised even when the framing behind it is sound. We assess OSB sheathing carefully before recommending replacement versus restoration, because the distinction affects both the insurance scope and the project timeline. It's a detail that matters in this part of Pierce County and gets missed when crews treat every job as a generic drywall-and-stud rebuild.
