## Why Auburn Properties See Particular Fire Damage Challenges

Auburn's housing stock spans a wide range of eras and construction types. The older neighborhoods closer to downtown Auburn, many built in the postwar decades, often feature original wood-framed walls with minimal cavity insulation. In those homes, smoke travels through wall cavities and attic spaces faster and farther than it does in tightly built modern construction, meaning the odor and soot footprint of a contained kitchen fire can extend two or three rooms beyond the origin point. Combustion byproducts settle into plaster, original hardwood, and older HVAC ducting in ways that require more targeted cleaning than a simple wipe-down.

Auburn also sits within a region where wood-burning stoves and pellet inserts are common supplemental heat sources, particularly in homes along the valley's eastern edges. Chimney-related ignitions, creosote buildup, cracked flue liners, are a recurring pattern in this climate, and they tend to produce dense, oily smoke that penetrates porous surfaces deeply. That type of residue requires chemical sponges, dry-ice blasting on structural members, and hydroxyl or thermal fogging rather than standard wet cleaning alone.

## Our Fire Damage Restoration Process in Auburn

Every job begins with a systematic scope assessment before any cleaning starts. The structure is evaluated for safety, load-bearing integrity, electrical panel status, gas shutoff confirmation, before crews enter affected areas. From there, the process moves through distinct phases:

**Emergency stabilization** covers board-up, roof tarping if the fire breached the envelope, and temporary power or dehumidification if the structure is secure enough to re-enter. Auburn's rainfall patterns mean an unprotected roof opening can introduce secondary water damage within hours during the wet season.

**Smoke and soot removal** is calibrated to the surface type. Textured ceilings common in Auburn homes built in the 1970s and 1980s trap particulate differently than flat drywall, dry chemical sponges work first, followed by low-moisture cleaning agents to avoid raising the texture. Ductwork is inspected and, when contaminated, cleaned or sealed before the HVAC system is operated again.

**Structural drying and odor neutralization** follows. Even when water wasn't used to suppress the fire, firefighting efforts introduce significant moisture. The IICRC S700 standard guides our drying protocol, and hydroxyl generators run continuously to break down odor-causing volatile organic compounds without requiring occupants to vacate for extended periods.

**Documentation and scope reporting** is produced throughout, photo logs, moisture readings, material inventories, formatted for insurance carrier review.
