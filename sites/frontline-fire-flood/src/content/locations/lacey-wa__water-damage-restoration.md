## Why Lacey Properties See Water Damage Issues

The geology under much of Lacey doesn't help. Glacially deposited soils common throughout Thurston County drain slowly, which means groundwater pressure against foundation walls and crawl space vapor barriers stays elevated through the rainy season. Homes built in the 1970s and 1980s, a large share of Lacey's residential stock, often have original galvanized supply lines that have spent decades narrowing from the inside out. When those pipes finally fail, they tend to fail completely rather than weeping slowly, releasing significant water volume before the shutoff is found.

Crawl space flooding is a recurring pattern across Lacey neighborhoods. Inadequate vapor barriers, missing or undersized sump systems, and grading that pitches toward the foundation rather than away from it all contribute. Water that collects in a crawl space doesn't stay there, it migrates into subfloor sheathing, floor joists, and eventually into finished flooring above, often without any visible sign at the surface until the damage is already structural.

## Our Water Damage Restoration Process in Lacey

The first step on any Lacey job is moisture mapping, using thermal imaging and calibrated moisture meters to locate water that has already moved beyond the visible wet zone. In homes with plywood subfloors over unconditioned crawl spaces, water routinely travels six to eight feet from the source before it shows up on a meter reading at the surface. Skipping this step means leaving hidden moisture that feeds mold within days.

Once the full extent is documented, water extraction begins with truck-mounted and portable units sized to the loss. Structural drying follows the IICRC S500 standard, using desiccant or refrigerant dehumidifiers and high-velocity air movers positioned to create a drying chamber around affected assemblies. Thurston County's ambient humidity during the rainy season means equipment runs longer here than in drier climates, drying goals are set to the psychrometric conditions of the space, not to a fixed number of days. Daily moisture readings track progress and determine when materials have reached acceptable drying targets before any reconstruction begins.

Frontline Fire & Flood holds IICRC certifications in Water Restoration Technician (WRT) and Applied Structural Drying (ASD), and the team is EPA Lead-Safe Certified, relevant in Lacey's older housing stock where disturbing water-damaged materials can expose lead paint in walls and window assemblies.

## Lacey Insurance & HOA Coordination

Most standard homeowners policies in Washington cover sudden and accidental water discharge, a burst pipe qualifies; slow seepage from a long-neglected fitting typically does not. ## Local Note

Homes built on the flatter sections of Lacey near the Martin Way corridor frequently have slab-on-grade foundations rather than crawl spaces. When water migrates under a concrete slab, from a slab leak or from exterior water intrusion, it doesn't evaporate the way it would through a wood subfloor assembly. Drying a slab-on-grade loss requires desiccant dehumidification and sometimes specialty drying mats placed directly on the concrete surface to draw moisture up through the slab. It takes longer and requires different equipment than a standard crawl space or above-grade loss, and skipping that step leaves moisture that will eventually telegraph through flooring finishes above.
