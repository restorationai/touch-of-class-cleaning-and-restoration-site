## Why Bonney Lake Homes Carry Elevated Asbestos Risk

Pierce County's suburban expansion pushed into the Bonney Lake plateau during decades when asbestos was everywhere in residential construction. Homes built between roughly 1950 and 1985 are the ones that warrant the closest attention. In this area, that often means:

- **Popcorn ceilings** applied with a spray texture that frequently contained chrysotile asbestos, a common finish through the early 1980s.
- **Vinyl floor tiles and the black mastic adhesive beneath them**, particularly in basements and kitchens. The tile itself may test clean while the adhesive underneath is positive.
- **Duct wrap and pipe insulation** in crawl spaces and utility rooms, where the material can become friable over time, especially in homes that have experienced moisture intrusion from Bonney Lake's wet winters.
- **Roofing materials**, including felt underlayment and some shingle compositions used through the mid-1980s.

The region's climate adds a layer of urgency: sustained fall and winter rainfall accelerates deterioration of older insulation and building materials, increasing the chance that asbestos-containing materials become damaged or friable before a homeowner is even aware of the risk.

## Our Asbestos Abatement Process in Bonney Lake

Abatement is not a single step, it's a sequence, and cutting corners at any point creates liability and health exposure.

**Step 1, Inspection and sampling.** A trained inspector collects bulk samples from suspect materials. Samples go to an accredited laboratory; results typically return within a few business days, though rush analysis is available when timelines are tight.

**Step 2, Scope and permitting.** Washington State requires notification to the Puget Sound Clean Air Agency (PSCAA) for regulated asbestos projects above threshold quantities. We handle the notification paperwork so the project stays compliant before a single tool touches the material.

**Step 3, Containment.** Work areas are sealed with poly sheeting and negative air pressure is established using HEPA-filtered air scrubbers. This prevents fiber migration into unaffected living spaces, critical in occupied homes.

**Step 4, Removal and disposal.** Asbestos-containing materials are wetted to suppress fiber release, carefully removed, double-bagged in labeled poly, and transported to a licensed disposal facility. Washington State has specific manifest requirements for asbestos waste, and every load we transport is documented.

**Step 5, Clearance.** Air sampling after abatement confirms fiber levels have returned to background before containment is removed. We coordinate with an independent third-party industrial hygienist for clearance testing when project scope or client preference calls for it.

One pattern worth knowing: homes in Bonney Lake's older subdivisions that were built on crawl-space foundations, common on the hillside lots with views toward Mount Rainier, often have original pipe insulation in the crawl space that was never disturbed or replaced.
