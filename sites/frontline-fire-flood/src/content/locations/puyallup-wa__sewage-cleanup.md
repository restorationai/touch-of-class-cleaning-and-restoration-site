## Why Puyallup Properties See Sewage Backup Problems

The Puyallup River valley's geology works against older sewer infrastructure in ways that aren't obvious until something fails. Clay soils shift with the seasonal wet-dry cycle, and that movement gradually deflects buried sewer laterals, the pipes connecting a home to the city main. A deflected lateral holds standing water, invites root intrusion from the mature trees common in Puyallup's established neighborhoods, and eventually backs up into the lowest fixture in the house, usually a basement floor drain or first-floor toilet.

Septic systems are another factor. Puyallup's eastern and southern edges include parcels that predate connection to municipal sewer, and a saturated drainfield after a heavy November rain can push effluent back toward the tank and into the structure. Pierce County Health Department regulations govern how those systems are repaired and pumped, which affects the sequencing of any cleanup, the county may require inspection before a failed system is returned to service.

Homes built before the mid-1970s in Puyallup often have cast-iron drain stacks that have corroded to the point where a camera inspection reveals partial blockages that were never symptomatic until a heavy-use event, a holiday gathering, a basement laundry load, tipped them over.

## Our Sewage Cleanup and Sanitization Process in Puyallup

Category 3 water, the classification for raw sewage, requires a different protocol than a clean-water pipe burst. Every porous material that sewage contacted is treated as contaminated: drywall, insulation, carpet, and wood subfloor all have defined removal thresholds under IICRC S500 and S520 standards.

The process on a Puyallup job typically follows this sequence:

**Containment and PPE setup**, The affected area is isolated with poly sheeting and negative air pressure where the layout allows, preventing cross-contamination to living spaces above.

**Solid waste and standing water removal**, Industrial wet-extraction equipment pulls liquid sewage and debris. What can't be vacuumed is bagged and disposed of per Pierce County solid waste requirements.

**Controlled demolition**, Contaminated drywall, insulation, and flooring are removed to clean, unaffected material. In Puyallup's older ranch-style homes with concrete slab floors, we assess whether the slab itself absorbed sewage and treat accordingly.

**Hospital-grade disinfection**, EPA-registered disinfectants are applied to all affected framing, concrete, and mechanical components. We allow appropriate dwell time, not a quick spray-and-wipe.

**Structural drying**, Desiccant or refrigerant dehumidifiers and air movers run until moisture readings in framing and concrete return to regional baseline levels. Puyallup's ambient humidity in fall and winter means drying takes longer than it would in a drier climate; we monitor daily rather than pulling equipment on a fixed schedule.

**Post-remediation verification**, ATP surface testing or third-party clearance sampling can be arranged before reconstruction begins, which some Puyallup-area insurance carriers now request on Category 3 losses.

Puyallup's downtown core and the neighborhoods immediately surrounding it include a number of homes built on pier-and-beam foundations rather than full basements, a construction style common in the valley through the mid-20th century. When sewage backs up under a pier-and-beam floor, it pools in the crawl space, which is often vented but rarely accessed.
