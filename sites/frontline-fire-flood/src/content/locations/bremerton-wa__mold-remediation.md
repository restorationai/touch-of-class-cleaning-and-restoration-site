## Why Bremerton Properties See Mold Issues

The Kitsap Peninsula sits in one of the wetter corners of Washington State. Bremerton averages well over 50 inches of rain annually, and the surrounding water, the Port Washington Narrows to the east, Sinclair Inlet to the south, keeps relative humidity elevated even between storms. That sustained moisture load is hard on building envelopes, especially in older housing stock.

A significant portion of Bremerton's residential neighborhoods were built between the 1940s and 1970s, when vapor barriers were minimal or absent and crawl space ventilation standards were far less rigorous than today. Homes of that era often have kraft-faced insulation, wooden subfloor systems, and plaster or early drywall that absorbs and holds moisture for extended periods. Once mold colonizes the paper facing on insulation or the backside of drywall, surface cleaning accomplishes nothing, the affected material has to come out.

Naval Base Kitsap and the surrounding residential areas also see a high rate of rental and transitional housing, where delayed maintenance reports can let a slow leak run for weeks before anyone acts. By the time a musty smell becomes undeniable, mold has typically been growing for far longer than it looks.

## Our Mold Remediation Process in Bremerton

Every job starts with a thorough inspection, not a visual scan, but a systematic assessment using moisture meters, thermal imaging, and air sampling to locate hidden growth behind walls, under flooring, and in crawl spaces. Mold that reads dry at the surface can be actively spreading through wet framing underneath.

Once the scope is confirmed, the work follows a structured sequence:

- **Containment**, negative-air pressure barriers isolate the affected area so spores aren't redistributed through HVAC or foot traffic during removal.
- **HEPA filtration**, air scrubbers run continuously throughout the remediation, capturing airborne particulates down to 0.3 microns.
- **Controlled demolition**, compromised materials (drywall, insulation, subfloor sections) are removed, bagged, and disposed of per Washington State guidelines.
- **Antimicrobial treatment**, structural surfaces are treated to address residual contamination and inhibit regrowth.
- **Drying and verification**, moisture readings are taken across the remediated area before any reconstruction begins. We don't close a job until the numbers support it.

The team holds IICRC AMRT (Applied Microbial Remediation Technician) certification, and the work is performed under EPA Lead-Safe protocols, relevant in Bremerton's older housing stock where lead paint is a realistic co-hazard.

Crawl spaces under Bremerton homes built before 1970 frequently have dirt floors and minimal cross-ventilation, a combination that keeps ground moisture in contact with wood framing year-round.
