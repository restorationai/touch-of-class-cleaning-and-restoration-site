## Why University Place Properties See Asbestos Issues

The Pierce County waterfront communities that grew up around the Puget Sound shoreline in the mid-twentieth century share a construction profile that shows up repeatedly in abatement work: slab-on-grade foundations with vinyl asbestos tile (VAT) underneath later flooring layers, plaster walls reinforced with asbestos-containing joint compound, and duct insulation wrapping that was never disturbed because the original furnace ran for decades without major service. University Place's proximity to the Sound also means homes here experienced the kind of persistent interior humidity that accelerates deterioration of older insulation and ceiling textures, the same dampness that keeps the moss growing on north-facing roofs can slowly degrade the binders holding asbestos fibers in place, shifting material from "non-friable" to a more fragile state over time.

Popcorn ceilings applied before the mid-1980s are among the most common finds during pre-renovation inspections in this area. A ceiling that looks intact and painted over can still contain chrysotile fibers that become airborne the moment a scraper or sanding block touches it. The same applies to the acoustic ceiling tiles found in older split-level homes and the pipe wrap around cast-iron plumbing in crawl spaces, materials that were never meant to be disturbed and often weren't, until a pipe leak or a kitchen remodel changes the plan.

## Our Asbestos Abatement Process in University Place

Every project starts with a certified inspection and bulk sampling before any removal work begins. Samples go to an accredited laboratory, and results drive the scope, there is no reason to disturb material that tests negative, and there is no safe shortcut when material tests positive.

Once a positive identification is confirmed, the work follows a structured sequence:

- **Containment setup**: Polyethylene sheeting seals the work area from the rest of the structure. Negative air pressure units with HEPA filtration run continuously to prevent fiber migration.
- **Wet methods**: Regulated asbestos-containing material (RACM) is wetted before and during removal to suppress fiber release, a requirement under Washington's asbestos NESHAP regulations and a practical control measure.
- **Waste handling**: All removed material is double-bagged in 6-mil poly, labeled per EPA and state requirements, and transported to a permitted disposal facility. The chain of custody is documented.
- **Air clearance**: Post-abatement air monitoring confirms fiber counts are below clearance thresholds before containment comes down and the space is returned to the homeowner or contractor.

The team holds EPA Lead-Safe certification and OSHA training, relevant here because University Place homes of the same vintage that contain asbestos frequently contain lead-based paint as well, and disturbing both in the same renovation scope requires coordinated handling.
