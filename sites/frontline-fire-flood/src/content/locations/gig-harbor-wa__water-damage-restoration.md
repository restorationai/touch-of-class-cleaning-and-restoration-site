## Why Gig Harbor Properties See Water Damage Differently

The peninsula's geography creates conditions that amplify water intrusion. Much of the residential housing stock was built during the 1970s and 1980s, a period when crawl space vapor barriers were thinner, drainage planes less detailed, and subfloor materials more vulnerable to prolonged moisture. Older homes in the hillside neighborhoods above the harbor often have foundation walls that were never designed to manage the hydrostatic pressure that builds during a heavy November or February rain event.

Gig Harbor also sits in a climate zone where temperatures rarely drop hard enough to freeze pipes solid, but cold snaps do arrive, and when they do, supply lines in uninsulated crawl spaces and garage walls are the first to go. The failure is often slow: a pinhole leak behind a wall, a slow drip under a bathroom vanity, moisture accumulating for weeks before the ceiling shows a stain. By then, the structural drying job is considerably larger than it would have been if the loss had been caught on day one.

## Our Water Damage Restoration Process in Gig Harbor

Every job starts with a moisture assessment, thermal imaging and calibrated moisture meters across walls, floors, and ceilings to map where water has traveled, not just where it's visible. Water extraction follows immediately: truck-mounted and portable extraction units pull standing water from carpet, hardwood, and subfloor assemblies. What remains is addressed with a drying system engineered for the specific materials and ambient conditions on site.

In Gig Harbor's crawl space-heavy housing stock, that often means deploying desiccant or refrigerant dehumidifiers below the floor as well as in the living space above. Structural drying in this region takes longer than in drier climates, ambient outdoor humidity during fall and winter routinely runs high enough that simply opening windows accomplishes nothing. Our IICRC ASD-certified technicians set drying targets based on the S500 standard and monitor daily until materials reach acceptable equilibrium moisture content. We document every reading, which matters when you're working through an insurance claim.

## Local Note

Gig Harbor's older crawl space homes often have fiberglass batt insulation hung between floor joists, and once that insulation absorbs water, it loses its R-value, holds moisture against the wood framing, and becomes a mold substrate within days. Many homeowners don't realize the insulation needs to come out before the structural drying process can actually work. Leaving saturated batts in place and running dehumidifiers above the floor is a common mistake that extends drying times significantly and can lead to mold colonization in the joist bays before the job is declared dry. We assess crawl space insulation on every job and communicate clearly about what needs to be removed before drying equipment is set.
