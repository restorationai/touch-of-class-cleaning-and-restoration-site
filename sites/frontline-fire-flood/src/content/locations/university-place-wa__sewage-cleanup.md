## Why University Place Properties See Sewage Backup Issues

The residential areas of University Place were developed heavily through the 1950s, 1960s, and 1970s, which means a significant portion of the city's sewer laterals, the pipes connecting individual homes to the main municipal line, are now 50 to 70 years old. Clay and cast-iron pipes from that era are prone to root intrusion, joint separation, and slow collapse, any of which can cause raw sewage to reverse course into the lowest drain in the house. Pierce County's wet winters compound the problem: when ground saturation is high and municipal mains are running near capacity, even a minor blockage can push sewage back up through floor drains, toilets, or washing machine standpipes.

Homes in University Place that still rely on older septic systems face a separate but related risk. Saturated soil in late fall and winter reduces the drain field's ability to absorb effluent, and an overloaded or aging tank can back up into the home before any visible warning sign appears outside. Either scenario, municipal sewer backup or septic overflow, is classified as Category 3 (black water) contamination under IICRC standards, requiring full protective protocols from the first moment of contact.

## Our Sewage Cleanup and Sanitization Process in University Place

The process begins with containment. Before any extraction starts, the affected area is isolated to prevent cross-contamination into clean rooms, a step that matters especially in the open floor plans common in 1960s and 1970s University Place ranch homes, where a backup in the utility room can wick into adjacent carpeted living space within hours.

Extraction comes next: truck-mounted and portable units remove standing sewage and saturated material. Porous materials, carpet, pad, drywall below the flood line, insulation, are removed rather than dried in place, because no drying process can render contaminated porous material safe for reuse. Once bulk material is out, all affected hard surfaces are treated with EPA-registered disinfectants formulated for Category 3 losses. Structural drying follows using calibrated air movers and dehumidifiers, with moisture readings documented throughout so the drying log is available for your insurance adjuster.

For homes with crawl spaces, a common configuration in University Place, the crawl is inspected separately. Sewage that migrates under a subfloor can saturate insulation and floor joists without any visible sign from inside the living area, and that hidden contamination is where long-term odor and mold problems originate.
