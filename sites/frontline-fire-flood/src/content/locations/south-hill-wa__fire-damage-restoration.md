## Why South Hill Properties See Fire Damage Spread Differently

The housing stock throughout South Hill skews toward the construction boom years of the late 1980s through the 2000s, a period when open floor plans became standard. Those layouts move air efficiently under normal conditions, which is exactly why smoke and combustion gases travel so far so fast during a fire. A kitchen fire that stays contained to one room can push soot proteins into bedrooms on the opposite end of the house within minutes.

Pierce County's damp climate compounds the problem after the fire is out. The Pacific Northwest's persistent moisture means that smoke residue, which is hygroscopic, meaning it pulls water out of the air, begins bonding more aggressively to painted surfaces, cabinetry, and textiles within hours of the fire being extinguished. Waiting even a day or two before beginning professional cleanup allows those residues to set into surfaces that might otherwise have been salvageable.

Fire season in this region also overlaps with the months when homes are sealed tightest against rain, which concentrates odor-causing particles inside the building envelope rather than allowing them to off-gas naturally.

## Our Fire Damage Restoration Process in South Hill

The work starts with a full structural and contents assessment before any cleaning begins. The IICRC FSRT standard that guides our fire and smoke restoration work calls for identifying the type of smoke residue, wet, dry, protein-based, or synthetic, because each responds to different cleaning chemistry. A grease fire in a kitchen leaves a different residue than a synthetic-material fire in a garage, and treating them the same way damages surfaces rather than restoring them.

Once the scope is documented, the sequence typically runs:

- **Emergency board-up and tarping** to secure the structure against Pierce County's rain while interior work is underway
- **Controlled demolition** of materials that cannot be cleaned, drywall, insulation, and flooring that have absorbed smoke beyond the threshold for restoration
- **HEPA vacuuming and dry-sponge wiping** of structural surfaces to remove loose soot before chemical cleaning begins
- **Thermal fogging and hydroxyl generation** to neutralize odor compounds embedded in framing, subfloor, and wall cavities
- **Duct cleaning and HVAC decontamination**, which is frequently skipped by less thorough crews but is essential when a forced-air system has been running during or after the fire
- **Reconstruction coordination**, returning the home to pre-loss condition

Because we hold EPA Lead-Safe Certification, homes built before 1978, and South Hill does have older pockets of housing, are handled under the appropriate protocols when fire damage requires disturbing painted surfaces.
