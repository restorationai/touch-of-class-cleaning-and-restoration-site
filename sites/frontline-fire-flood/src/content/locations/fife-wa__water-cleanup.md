## Why Fife Properties See Water Damage Differently

Fife's position along the Puyallup River delta means the water table sits close to grade across much of the city. During sustained Pacific Northwest rainfall, the kind that stacks up between November and March, that table rises quickly, and crawl spaces in older slab-on-grade or pier-and-beam homes can take on groundwater even when no pipe has failed. The soil in this part of Pierce County is heavily influenced by river alluvium: fine-grained, slow-draining, and prone to holding moisture long after a storm passes.

The commercial and light-industrial character of much of Fife also matters. Warehouse-adjacent properties and older retail strips along Pacific Highway often have flat or low-slope roofing that pools water and eventually fails at seams and drains. When those roofs leak into drop-ceiling systems or concrete-block walls, the damage is less visible but often more extensive than a flooded residential basement, because no one notices it until the ceiling tiles sag or a musty odor reaches the sales floor.

## Our Water Cleanup Process in Fife

Water cleanup is not a single step, it is a sequence, and the sequence matters for how quickly a structure dries and whether secondary damage like mold colonization follows. Mold can begin establishing within 24 to 48 hours of a moisture event in a warm, enclosed space, which is why the first hours on-site are spent stopping the spread, not just mopping the floor.

When our crew arrives, the first priority is identifying the moisture boundary, how far water has migrated beyond the visible wet area. We use thermal imaging and calibrated moisture meters to map saturation in walls, subfloors, and insulation before any equipment is placed. That map drives the drying plan.

Extraction comes next: truck-mounted and portable units pull standing water and surface moisture from carpet, hardwood, and concrete. Once bulk water is removed, we set a calculated array of commercial desiccant or refrigerant dehumidifiers and air movers, sized to the affected volume and adjusted daily based on psychrometric readings. Every reading is logged, that documentation matters for insurance claims and for confirming the structure has reached dry standard before we demobilize. All structural drying work follows IICRC S500 protocols, performed by our IICRC WRT- and ASD-certified technicians.

## Fife Insurance Coordination

Most sudden and accidental water losses, a failed appliance supply line, a burst pipe, an overflow, are covered under standard homeowner's or commercial property policies. Gradual leaks and groundwater intrusion are treated differently by carriers, and Fife's proximity to the Puyallup floodplain means some properties carry separate flood endorsements through the National Flood Insurance Program. Knowing which policy applies at the start of a job affects how documentation is structured.

We photograph affected materials, generate moisture logs, and prepare scope documentation that carriers and their adjusters can work from directly. We bill most major insurance carriers and can communicate with your adjuster throughout the claim so you are not translating between the technical crew and the insurance side on your own.

## Local Note

Fife has a notable stock of light commercial and mixed-use buildings constructed in the 1970s and 1980s along its Pacific Highway corridor. Many of those structures used early-generation vinyl composition tile over concrete slabs, and some of that tile contains asbestos-containing materials. When water cleanup requires removing flooring to dry a slab, that tile needs to be tested before it is disturbed. Frontline Fire & Flood is EPA Lead-Safe Certified and OSHA trained, and we coordinate asbestos testing with a third-party industrial hygienist when building age and material type raise that question, before any flooring comes up, not after.
