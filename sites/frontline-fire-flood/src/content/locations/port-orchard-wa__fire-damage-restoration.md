## Why Port Orchard Properties Face Compounded Fire Damage

Kitsap County's housing stock skews older, and Port Orchard is no exception. Homes built in the mid-20th century, common throughout the city's hillside neighborhoods and older residential corridors, were typically framed with old-growth Douglas fir, insulated with materials that predate modern fire-resistance standards, and finished with plaster-and-lath walls rather than drywall. When fire moves through these structures, the char pattern is different: old-growth fir burns slower but holds heat longer, and plaster walls trap smoke particulates in the lath cavity behind them. Surface cleaning alone won't address the odor source.

The region's humidity also means that post-fire water damage, from suppression efforts, doesn't dry on its own schedule. Firefighting water sitting in a plaster-walled Port Orchard home during a wet November can begin supporting mold colonization within 24 to 48 hours. Fire restoration here isn't a single-trade job; it requires coordinated smoke remediation, structural drying, and mold prevention running in parallel.

## Our Fire Damage Restoration Process in Port Orchard

Every fire loss is different, but the sequence follows a proven structure calibrated to local conditions:

**Emergency stabilization**, We assess structural integrity, board or tarp breached openings against incoming weather (critical given Port Orchard's rain exposure), and document the full scope of damage with photos and moisture readings before any material is moved.

**Smoke and soot removal**, Dry soot from fast-flaming fires and wet, oily soot from smoldering fires require different chemical approaches. We match the cleaning agent to the residue type, working systematically from ceilings down across every surface, including inside HVAC cavities, which carry smoke odor through an entire house long after visible soot is gone.

**Structural drying**, Suppression water trapped in plaster, subfloor assemblies, and wall cavities is extracted and dried using commercial desiccant and refrigerant dehumidifiers, with moisture readings logged daily. Our team holds IICRC ASD (Applied Structural Drying) certification and follows the IICRC S500 standard.

**Odor neutralization**, Thermal fogging and hydroxyl generation address smoke odor at the molecular level. In Port Orchard's older homes, where porous plaster and uninsulated crawl spaces hold odor longer than modern construction, this step often takes more time than property owners expect, we set that expectation upfront.

**Reconstruction coordination**, Once the structure is clean and dry, we coordinate or perform repairs to bring the property back to pre-loss condition, working within Kitsap County permit requirements.
