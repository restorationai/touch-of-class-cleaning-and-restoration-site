## Why Tacoma Properties See Mold Issues

Tacoma sits at the southern end of Puget Sound, where prevailing westerly winds push moisture-laden air inland almost year-round. Average annual rainfall is moderate by Pacific Northwest standards, but the real driver of mold growth here isn't rainfall volume, it's duration. Extended stretches of drizzle and overcast from October through April keep exterior materials wet and interior relative humidity high, often hovering in the range where mold colonization begins within 24 to 48 hours of a water intrusion.

The city's housing stock compounds the problem. Tacoma has a substantial inventory of homes built before 1960, many featuring balloon-frame construction, original cedar siding, and crawl spaces with minimal vapor barriers. In neighborhoods like Hilltop and the North End, it's common to find homes where the crawl space is the primary moisture entry point, dirt floors, inadequate ventilation, and foundation vents that face the prevailing weather. Mold in these spaces often goes undetected for months because it's out of sight and the symptoms (musty odor, worsening allergy-like reactions indoors) are easy to attribute to other causes.

Multi-family buildings and older commercial properties along the Sixth Avenue corridor present a different pattern: shared plumbing chases and stacked units mean a leak on an upper floor can introduce moisture into wall cavities two or three floors below before anyone notices.

## Our Mold Remediation Process in Tacoma

Every remediation starts with a thorough inspection, not a visual scan, but moisture mapping with thermal imaging and calibrated meters to locate hidden saturation behind plaster, under subfloor sheathing, and inside wall assemblies. In Tacoma's older homes, surface readings can be deceptive: plaster and lathe may read dry at the face while the wood framing behind stays saturated, which is exactly the environment mold thrives in.

Once the scope is established, the process follows a defined sequence:

- **Containment**, negative air pressure barriers isolate the affected area so disturbed spores don't migrate to clean spaces during removal.
- **HEPA filtration**, air scrubbers run continuously throughout the project, capturing airborne particulates down to 0.3 microns.
- **Removal and disposal**, compromised materials (drywall, insulation, wood framing where necessary) are bagged and removed per Washington State Department of Ecology guidelines for mold-contaminated debris.
- **Antimicrobial treatment**, structural surfaces are treated after removal, not instead of it. Treatment alone on visibly colonized material is not remediation.
- **Structural drying**, if residual moisture remains in framing or subfloor, drying equipment stays on-site until clearance readings are met, following the IICRC S520 standard.
- **Clearance verification**, we coordinate post-remediation air sampling with an independent industrial hygienist when the project scope warrants it, so the clearance comes from a neutral third party.

Our team holds the IICRC AMRT (Applied Microbial Remediation Technician) credential, and the firm is EPA Lead-Safe Certified, relevant on any pre-1978 property where disturbing building materials can also mean disturbing lead-based paint.

## Local Note

In Tacoma's older Craftsman and Victorian-era homes, particularly those in the North End and Hilltop built before the 1940s, original fir subfloor and wall framing absorbs and releases moisture much more slowly than modern OSB or engineered lumber. That means drying timelines can run longer than the industry average, and mold that appears to be limited to the surface layer often has deeper colonization into the wood grain. Skipping adequate drying time before closing walls is the most common reason mold returns in these homes. We don't close a job until moisture readings confirm the assembly is genuinely dry, not just dry at the surface.
