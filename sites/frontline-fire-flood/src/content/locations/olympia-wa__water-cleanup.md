Olympia sits at the southern tip of Puget Sound, where marine air keeps relative humidity elevated for much of the year and annual rainfall routinely tops 50 inches. That moisture load means water damage here rarely behaves the way it does in drier climates, materials stay wet longer, drying windows are narrower, and secondary mold growth can begin within 24 to 48 hours of a loss. When a pipe fails, an appliance overflows, or stormwater backs up into a crawl space, the clock starts immediately.

## Why Olympia Properties See Water Damage Issues

The South Sound's geology and climate create conditions that accelerate water intrusion. Olympia's soils, particularly the glacially deposited silts and clays common throughout Thurston County, drain slowly, which means groundwater can press against foundation walls and crawl space membranes during sustained rain events. Homes built before the 1970s, which make up a significant portion of the city's residential stock, frequently have original cast-iron or galvanized supply lines that are well past their service life. When those pipes fail in winter, the combination of cold outdoor temperatures and damp indoor air slows evaporation and makes surface readings from a moisture meter misleading, the wall can feel dry to the touch while the framing behind it holds 25 percent moisture content or more.

Flood water cleanup in Olympia also involves crawl spaces more often than in many other markets. A large share of the city's single-family homes sit on pier-and-beam or continuous perimeter foundations with unencapsulated crawl spaces. Standing water in those spaces wicks into subfloor sheathing and floor joists quickly, and the confined airflow makes mechanical drying significantly slower than above-grade work.

## Our Water Cleanup Process in Olympia

Every water cleanup job starts with a full moisture mapping inspection, thermal imaging and calibrated pin and pinless meters across every affected surface, including walls, ceilings, subfloor, and crawl space framing where applicable. That baseline tells us where water has traveled, not just where it's visible.

Once the scope is documented, standing water removal comes first. We use truck-mounted and portable extraction units sized to the loss, a flooded crawl space under a 1,500-square-foot bungalow requires different equipment staging than a finished basement with several inches of water. After bulk extraction, we set commercial-grade desiccant or refrigerant dehumidifiers and high-velocity air movers calibrated to Olympia's ambient conditions. Because outdoor dew points here stay elevated through much of the rainy season, simply opening windows is not a viable drying strategy, closed-system drying with monitored equipment is standard practice.

Drying progress is documented daily with moisture readings logged against the IICRC S500 standard. We don't call a job dry until the numbers say so.

## Olympia Insurance Coordination

Most homeowner policies cover sudden and accidental water losses, a burst pipe, a failed water heater, an appliance supply line that lets go. What they typically don't cover is gradual seepage or long-term moisture intrusion, which is why the initial documentation we produce matters so much. We photograph affected materials before any removal, capture moisture readings with timestamps, and compile a scope of loss that aligns with the format most carriers expect. That documentation reduces the back-and-forth with adjusters and gives you a clear record if questions arise later. We bill carriers directly on covered losses so you're not managing reimbursement on top of an already stressful situation.

## Local Note

Olympia's older homes, particularly those built in the 1940s and 1950s in the neighborhoods surrounding the downtown core, frequently have original fir subfloor planking rather than plywood sheathing. Solid fir is denser than OSB or plywood and releases absorbed moisture more slowly, which extends the drying timeline compared to what a moisture map might suggest on day one. Crews who aren't familiar with this construction pattern sometimes pull equipment too early based on surface readings that look acceptable while the plank edges and tongue-and-groove joints are still holding significant moisture. We account for this in our drying targets and extend monitoring on any job where original plank subfloor is present.
