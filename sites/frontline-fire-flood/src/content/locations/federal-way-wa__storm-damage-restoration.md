## Why Federal Way Properties Are Particularly Vulnerable to Storm Damage

Federal Way's housing stock spans several decades and construction styles, and that variety matters when a storm rolls through. Neighborhoods developed in the 1970s and 1980s, many of them built on the plateau above Interstate 5, often have cedar shake roofs that have been re-roofed once or twice. Those layered assemblies can look intact from the street while storm water is migrating between layers and soaking into the roof deck below. Homes closer to the waterfront along Puget Sound face a different problem: salt-laden wind accelerates the deterioration of caulking, flashing, and window seals, so what looks like minor storm damage on the surface can mask years of slow moisture intrusion that a single bad storm finally pushes past the tipping point.

The region's clay-heavy soils compound the problem. During extended rain events, and Western Washington delivers them reliably from October through April, that soil reaches saturation quickly and sheds water laterally rather than absorbing it. ## Our Storm Damage Restoration Process in Federal Way

The first step on any storm call is a systematic exterior and interior assessment, roof, attic, exterior walls, and below-grade spaces, before any work begins. Storm damage is rarely limited to the obvious breach. A falling tree limb punches through a roof, but wind-driven rain may have entered through a compromised soffit on the opposite side of the house at the same time.

Once the scope is documented, the process moves in a defined sequence:

- **Emergency stabilization**: tarping, board-up, and temporary waterproofing to stop ongoing water entry.
- **Water extraction and moisture mapping**: standing water is removed and calibrated moisture meters and thermal imaging cameras identify wet materials behind walls and under flooring that look dry at the surface.
- **Structural drying**: industrial desiccant dehumidifiers and high-velocity air movers are positioned according to a drying plan calibrated to the specific materials, plywood sheathing, OSB, dimensional lumber, and concrete all release moisture at different rates.
- **Debris and damaged material removal**: storm-damaged roofing, insulation, drywall, and flooring are removed to the extent needed to allow complete drying and prevent mold colonization.
- **Reconstruction coordination**: once the structure is dry and documented, rebuild work begins, roofing, framing, insulation, drywall, and finish work.

All drying is performed to the IICRC S500 and S520 standards. The IICRC-certified team holds credentials in Water Restoration (WRT) and Applied Structural Drying (ASD), which means drying goals are set by the material and the science, not by a gut estimate.
