Parkland sits in one of the wetter corners of Pierce County, where the marine climate pushes annual rainfall well above the regional average and the ground stays saturated from November through April. That moisture has a way of finding its way into homes, through aging crawl spaces, around foundation walls that have shifted over decades, and through roof penetrations that go unnoticed until a ceiling stain turns into a cascade. When water gets inside a Parkland home, the clock starts immediately: mold can begin colonizing wet framing within 24 to 48 hours, and the region's consistently cool, humid air slows evaporation enough that surface readings can look dry while structural cavities stay dangerously wet.

## Why Parkland Properties See Water Damage Issues

Parkland's housing stock tells the story of Pierce County's postwar growth, a mix of mid-century ramblers, split-levels built in the 1960s and 70s, and more recent infill construction. The older homes in the area frequently have original galvanized or early copper plumbing that has reached or passed its service life, making supply-line failures more common than in newer neighborhoods. Crawl space construction is prevalent here, and those crawl spaces are often vented to outside air, a design that made sense in drier climates but creates a moisture trap in western Washington, where outdoor humidity rarely drops low enough to dry out the space naturally. A slow drip from a water heater or a washing machine supply hose can saturate crawl space insulation and subfloor sheathing for weeks before anyone notices the soft spot in the floor above.

The clay-heavy soils common across this part of Pierce County also contribute. Clay sheds water rather than absorbing it, which means heavy rain events push groundwater laterally toward foundation walls rather than straight down. Homes without functional perimeter drains or with aging drain tile are particularly vulnerable to seepage along the base of foundation walls, the kind of intrusion that soaks into carpet, floats baseboards, and wicks up into wall cavities before it's visible at the surface.

## Our Water Damage Restoration Process in Parkland

The process starts with a full moisture assessment before any equipment gets staged. Thermal imaging and calibrated moisture meters map the actual extent of saturation, not just what's visible, because water in a Parkland home rarely stays where it entered. It follows framing cavities, runs along subfloor seams, and pools in low spots that aren't obvious from a visual inspection. That mapping drives every decision that follows.

Once the scope is clear, standing water is extracted using truck-mounted and portable extraction units. Structural drying then begins with a calculated placement of commercial-grade desiccant and refrigerant dehumidifiers alongside high-velocity air movers, the ratio and positioning calibrated to the specific materials affected. Drying in Parkland's climate requires more dehumidification capacity than in drier regions because the outdoor air brought in during ventilation carries significant moisture load of its own. Daily monitoring tracks psychrometric data, temperature, relative humidity, and material moisture content, until readings confirm the structure has returned to baseline. The process follows IICRC S500 standard throughout.

When affected materials include older plaster, engineered hardwood, or laminate over concrete slab, all common in Parkland's varied housing stock, drying timelines extend. Plaster in particular releases moisture slowly and can read near-dry at the surface while the underlying lath stays saturated. We account for that in the drying plan rather than pulling equipment prematurely.

## Local Note

One pattern worth knowing about in Parkland: homes with concrete slab foundations, particularly those built in the 1980s and early 1990s, often have copper supply lines embedded in or running beneath the slab. A pinhole leak in that configuration can saturate the slab and wick moisture up through flooring for weeks before a water bill spike or a warm spot on the floor gives it away. By the time the flooring is removed, the slab itself may need extended drying time with specialty drying mats before any new flooring can go down. If you notice an unexplained increase in your water bill alongside any soft or discolored flooring, that combination is worth investigating before it becomes a larger project.
