## Why Burien Homes Are Particularly Vulnerable to Fire Damage

A significant portion of Burien's residential housing stock was built between the 1940s and 1970s, decades before modern fire-blocking requirements became standard in Washington State construction codes. In homes of that era, wall cavities run uninterrupted from floor to ceiling, which means smoke and superheated gases can migrate vertically through an entire structure even when the fire itself was contained to one room. Older electrical panels, knob-and-tube wiring remnants, and original plumbing chases create pathways that modern construction simply doesn't have.

Burien's proximity to Puget Sound also matters after a fire. The marine air that rolls in off the water keeps humidity elevated for much of the year. Wet smoke residue, the dense, sticky byproduct of low-oxygen fires common in smoldering upholstery or insulation, absorbs ambient moisture and becomes harder to remove the longer it sits. That combination of aging construction and persistent coastal humidity makes professional fire cleanup here more demanding than in drier inland communities.

## Our Fire Damage Restoration Process in Burien

Every fire loss is different, but the sequence of decisions that protects your property follows a consistent logic. When we arrive, the first priority is a room-by-room scope: what burned, what was smoke-affected, and what structural materials need testing before any cleaning begins. In Burien homes built before 1978, that assessment includes checking for lead-based paint and asbestos-containing materials in textured ceilings, floor tiles, and pipe insulation, our team is EPA Lead-Safe Certified and OSHA trained, so we can handle those discoveries without stopping the job to bring in a separate contractor.

Once the scope is clear, we move through a defined process:

- **Board-up and tarping** to secure the structure against weather and unauthorized entry
- **Debris removal** of charred materials that cannot be restored
- **Dry ice blasting or chemical sponge cleaning** of framing and structural surfaces, calibrated to the soot type
- **Hydroxyl or ozone treatment** for odor neutralization in affected rooms
- **HEPA air scrubbing** to reduce airborne particulates before any reconstruction begins
- **Moisture monitoring** throughout, because fire suppression water left in walls accelerates secondary damage

Documentation runs parallel to every step, photos, moisture readings, and material logs that your insurance adjuster will need.
