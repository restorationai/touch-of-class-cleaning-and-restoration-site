## Why Gig Harbor Properties Present Unique Fire Damage Challenges

The peninsula's housing stock tells a story in wood. Many homes built during Gig Harbor's growth decades feature cedar siding, exposed timber framing, and tongue-and-groove interior paneling, materials that char deeply and hold smoke odor long after the fire is out. Unlike fiber cement or vinyl-clad construction common in newer developments, wood-dominant exteriors require careful assessment of how far heat penetration has traveled into structural members before any restoration scope is finalized.

Salt-laden air accelerates oxidation on metal components, HVAC ductwork, electrical conduit, plumbing fittings, that were already stressed by heat. What looks like surface corrosion on ductwork after a kitchen fire may indicate deeper damage that would go undetected without thermal imaging and a thorough mechanical inspection. The combination of fire damage and the coastal environment means the restoration window is shorter here than in drier regions: moisture in the air can begin interacting with fire-damaged materials within hours, making rapid stabilization critical.

Pierce County's permitting office handles structural repair permits for Gig Harbor properties, and any work that touches load-bearing elements, electrical systems, or the building envelope will require documented permits and inspections before walls are closed. Frontline coordinates that documentation from the start so repairs don't stall mid-project waiting on paperwork.

## Our Fire Damage Restoration Process in Gig Harbor

The first hours after a fire are about stopping secondary damage, not rebuilding. The process begins with a structural safety assessment and board-up or tarp-over to secure the property against weather, important on the peninsula, where rain can arrive with little warning and an open roof or broken window becomes a water intrusion problem on top of a fire problem.

From there, the work moves through a defined sequence:

- **Soot and smoke mapping**, identifying which materials are salvageable and which require removal, using moisture meters and thermal cameras to find heat damage behind finished surfaces
- **Controlled demolition**, removing charred framing, drywall, insulation, and flooring to the point of clean, structurally sound material
- **Odor neutralization**, thermal fogging and hydroxyl treatment to address smoke odor in cavities, ductwork, and porous finishes that surface cleaning alone won't reach
- **Structural drying**, because water from suppression efforts saturates materials quickly, drying runs concurrently with smoke remediation rather than after it
- **Reconstruction scoping**, a documented scope handed off to the rebuild phase, with photos and measurements that support the insurance claim

As an EPA Lead-Safe Certified firm, Frontline follows required protocols when fire damage affects pre-1978 construction, relevant in Gig Harbor's older harborside neighborhoods where lead paint in original trim and window frames is common.

## Local Note

Cedar shake roofs, still present on a number of older Gig Harbor homes, present a specific hazard after a fire that doesn't involve the structure itself. Shakes that have been heat-stressed but not fully consumed can appear intact while the underlying skip sheathing has lost structural integrity. Walking a cedar shake roof for inspection after a fire requires the same caution as a fully damaged roof, and any scope that includes roof work should treat the sheathing as suspect until it's confirmed sound from the attic side. It's a detail that matters on the peninsula in a way it simply doesn't in neighborhoods where composition shingles are the norm.
