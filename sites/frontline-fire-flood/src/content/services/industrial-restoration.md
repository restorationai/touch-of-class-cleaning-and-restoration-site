**Industrial water or fire damage doesn't just damage a building, it stops production.** A burst supply line in a warehouse ceiling, a scorched electrical panel in a plant bay, or a sprinkler head that discharges onto a pallet of finished goods can shut down a line, trigger a business interruption clock, and put racking, machinery, and inventory at risk within hours. We answer 24/7 and bring the crew, extraction equipment, and documentation process built for facility-scale losses, not single-room jobs.

## What industrial restoration actually involves

Industrial restoration covers the mitigation and recovery work specific to warehouses, manufacturing plants, distribution centers, and processing facilities, properties where the scale, the equipment on site, and the cost of downtime change how the job has to run. A 40,000 square foot warehouse with standing water under racking behaves differently than a flooded bedroom: the slab itself can hold moisture for weeks, HVAC systems distribute soot or humidity across the whole envelope, and machinery exposed to water or smoke residue can corrode or short out long after the visible cleanup is done. Work typically involves industrial-grade extraction units, desiccant dehumidification sized for large open volumes, moisture mapping of concrete and sub-slab areas, and coordination with your facilities or maintenance team so production can restart in sections rather than waiting on the whole building.

## Our process

1. **Facility assessment and shutdown coordination.** We walk the affected zones with your facilities manager or safety officer, identify utility shutoffs, hazardous materials, and equipment that needs isolating before any extraction begins.
2. **Large-volume extraction and residue removal.** Truck-mounted and portable extraction equipment handles standing water across concrete, epoxy, or sealed flooring, while fire-related losses get soot and residue removal scoped to the surface type, drywall, steel, or exposed ductwork.
3. **Moisture mapping and machinery protection.** Moisture meters and thermal imaging track water migration into slab, mezzanine framing, and wall cavities, and we flag equipment or electrical panels that need a licensed electrician's clearance before power is restored.
4. **Structural drying at scale.** Desiccant dehumidifiers and air movers are sized to the cubic footage of the space, not a single room, with daily moisture readings logged for the duration of the dry-out.
5. **Documentation and handoff.** Photos, moisture logs, and a written scope go to your insurance carrier and property manager, so the business interruption and property damage portions of the claim have the paper trail adjusters expect.

## What separates a good industrial restoration response from a bad one

The most common mistake on industrial losses is treating them like oversized residential jobs: a crew pulls visible water, runs a few fans, and calls it dry. Slab-on-grade concrete and sub-floor vapor barriers hold moisture far longer than drywall does, and if drying stops before the slab reads dry, mold and corrosion show up under racking or equipment pads weeks later. Another common miss is HVAC: a plant fire or heavy smoke event pulls soot into return air and distributes it through ductwork into areas that never saw visible flame, and skipping duct cleaning leaves an odor problem that resurfaces every time the system cycles. Adjusters on commercial and industrial claims also look closely at documentation of production downtime, so a restoration crew that logs moisture readings daily, photographs affected racking and inventory, and separates mitigation costs from reconstruction costs gives your claim a cleaner path than one that hands over a single invoice at the end.

## What does industrial restoration cost?

Industrial losses vary more than almost any other restoration category because the cost depends on square footage, the number of production lines or equipment runs affected, and whether contamination reached electrical or mechanical systems. These are typical industry ranges, not a quote, every facility loss gets a written scope of work before any mitigation begins, and most commercial property policies cover sudden water and fire damage along with a portion of business interruption, though coverage specifics depend on your policy language.

## Seasonal & regional considerations

Lakewood's proximity to Puget Sound and the American Lake watershed means industrial sites on lower ground see elevated groundwater pressure during the wet season, which pushes moisture into slab joints and loading dock seams even without a visible leak. Older warehouse and light-industrial buildings around the JBLM corridor and South Tacoma often run unheated storage bays, where a hard freeze can crack an exposed supply line overnight and go unnoticed until a shift arrives to standing water. Facilities with rooftop HVAC or exposed ductwork are also worth a seasonal check before the first cold snap, since a frozen coil or cracked line above a production floor causes far more damage than the same failure in a conditioned office space.
