When a sewer line backs up or a septic system overflows, the clock starts immediately, not because of the water, but because of what's in it. Raw sewage carries bacteria, viruses, and parasites classified as Category 3 "black water" under the IICRC S500 standard, meaning any porous material it contacts is considered contaminated and typically cannot be restored. Within hours, pathogens begin migrating beyond the visible wet zone. What looks like a contained mess in a basement bathroom is often already inside the subfloor, wicking up drywall, and soaking into insulation you can't see from the doorway.

## What sewage cleanup and sanitization actually involves

Sewage cleanup is not a mop-and-bleach job. The visible waste is only the starting point. A proper response involves removing all contaminated materials that cannot be decontaminated, flooring, drywall, insulation, cabinetry, then treating the structural shell that remains with EPA-registered hospital-grade disinfectants formulated for Category 3 losses. After disinfection, the space must be dried to IICRC S500 structural drying targets before any reconstruction begins, because sealing moisture behind new drywall creates the exact conditions mold needs to colonize.

Equipment on a sewage job typically includes truck-mounted or portable extraction units capable of handling solids, commercial air movers, dehumidifiers, and negative air machines with HEPA filtration to prevent aerosolized contaminants from spreading through the rest of the structure. Depending on the source, a main line backup, a failed ejector pump, or a septic overflow, the affected area can range from a single bathroom to an entire lower level.

Timeline varies by scope, but most residential sewage losses require two to five days of active drying after the initial extraction and demo, followed by a clearance check before reconstruction begins.

## Our process

1. **Containment and source confirmation.** Before extraction begins, the affected area is isolated with physical barriers and negative air pressure to prevent cross-contamination. The source of the backup is identified, whether it's a blocked main line, a failed ejector pump, or a septic overflow, because the source determines the full extent of affected zones.

2. **Category 3 material removal.** All porous materials that contacted black water are removed: flooring, baseboards, drywall to a minimum of 12 inches above the waterline (often higher depending on wicking), insulation, and any cabinetry with direct contact. Contaminated debris is bagged and disposed of per applicable regulations for Category 3 waste.

3. **Extraction and surface decontamination.** Standing water and residual waste are extracted using equipment rated for solids. Structural surfaces, concrete slab, wall framing, joists, are cleaned of visible contamination, then treated with EPA-registered disinfectants at dwell times specified by the product label. A second application is standard on porous substrates like wood framing.

4. **Structural drying and monitoring.** Commercial drying equipment is deployed and calibrated to the affected material types. Moisture readings are logged daily against drying targets. The space is not cleared for reconstruction until readings confirm the structural assembly has returned to acceptable moisture content, not just at the surface, but at depth.

5. **Documentation and scope delivery.** Every step is photographed, moisture readings are logged with timestamps, and a written scope is provided for insurance and reconstruction purposes. This documentation is what adjusters need to approve a claim without pushback.

## What separates a good sewage response from a bad one

The most common mistake in sewage cleanup is treating it like a Category 1 water loss, extracting the visible water, running fans, and calling it done. Category 3 material that isn't removed becomes a permanent reservoir for pathogens and mold. Bleach applied to a contaminated surface without prior cleaning does not disinfect, it reacts with organic matter before it can kill anything. And drywall that reads dry at the surface can still carry moisture in the paper facing and gypsum core, which is enough to sustain mold growth behind a freshly painted wall.

Insurance adjusters on sewage claims look specifically for documentation of the contamination category, a material removal log, and evidence that disinfection was performed with a product registered for the pathogen class involved. Adjusters also look for moisture logs that show drying was completed to standard, not just started. A claim without this documentation is far more likely to be disputed or underpaid.

## Seasonal and regional considerations

Lakewood and the surrounding South Sound area sit on a mix of clay-heavy soils that drain poorly during the wet season, which runs roughly October through April. Heavy rainfall raises the water table and increases hydrostatic pressure on older sewer laterals, the pipes connecting your home to the municipal main. Combined sewer systems in older parts of Pierce County can also surcharge during peak storm events, pushing water backward through floor drains and basement fixtures. Homes built before the 1980s in Lakewood, Tacoma, and University Place are particularly prone to root intrusion in clay tile laterals, which is one of the most common causes of recurring backups in the region.
