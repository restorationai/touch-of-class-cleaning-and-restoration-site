Call now for emergency service.** We answer 24/7 and send a crew to start extraction before standing water has a chance to migrate into wall cavities, subfloor, or stored belongings. Basements fail differently than other rooms: water collects fast, drains slowly, and hides behind finished walls long after the surface looks dry, which is exactly why a fast, methodical response matters more here than almost anywhere else in the house.

## What basement flooding cleanup actually involves

A flooded basement is rarely just water on a concrete floor. Depending on the source, sump pump failure, foundation seepage, a burst supply line, or sewer backup, the water carries different contamination levels and behaves differently once it's inside the structure. Clean groundwater can still wick up drywall twelve inches or more within a day. Backed-up sewage is classified as Category 3 water and requires removal of porous materials it touched, not just drying. Our crews bring truck-mounted extraction units, submersible pumps for standing water over an inch deep, and thermal imaging cameras to find saturation behind baseboards and under flooring that a moisture meter alone would miss. Depending on square footage and how long the water sat before we arrived, extraction and setup typically take a few hours, with structural drying running several days after that.

## Our process

1. **Water category and source identification.** Before anything else, we determine whether the water is clean, gray, or black (Category 1, 2, or 3) because that classification drives what gets dried versus what gets removed.
2. **Extraction and standing water removal.** Submersible pumps and extraction wands clear bulk water from the floor, including water trapped under carpet pad and in floor drains.
3. **Containment and demolition where needed.** For Category 2/3 water or drywall that's wicked past the flood cut line, we remove affected baseboards, drywall, and insulation to stop contamination and mold from spreading into dry framing.
4. **Moisture mapping and structural drying.** Air movers, low-grain-refrigerant dehumidifiers, and daily moisture readings target the slab, framing, and any wall cavities that tested wet, logged against IICRC S500 drying standards.
5. **Monitoring to dry standard.** We don't pull equipment on a schedule; we pull it when moisture content readings in the framing and subfloor match dry, unaffected materials elsewhere in the house.

## What separates a good basement flooding response from a bad one

The most common mistake in basement cleanup is treating the floor as the whole problem. Water that soaked into a stud wall or under a finished floor's underlayment can read dry at the surface while the material underneath stays saturated for weeks, which is how basements end up with hidden mold growth that surfaces as a musty smell months later. A second mistake is skipping a flood cut on drywall that's been wet for more than 24 to 48 hours, leaving contaminated gypsum core in place because it looks fine from the front. Insurance adjusters reviewing a basement claim typically want to see moisture logs showing readings over multiple days, photos of the affected materials before removal, and documentation of the water category, so thorough records matter as much as the drying equipment itself.

## Seasonal & regional considerations

Lakewood sits on heavy clay soils with a high water table in many neighborhoods, which means basements and crawlspaces here are prone to hydrostatic pressure pushing groundwater through foundation cracks during the Puget Sound region's wet season, roughly October through March. Older homes with older foundation footings are especially vulnerable when fall storms arrive back-to-back and the ground doesn't get a chance to drain between them. Sump pumps and French drains that handle a single storm fine can fail under sustained rainfall, so basement flooding calls in this area tend to cluster during multi-day rain events rather than single downpours.
