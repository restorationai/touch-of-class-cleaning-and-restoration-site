## Restoration emergencies common in Anderson Island

Anderson Island sits entirely within Puget Sound, and that geography drives most of the damage we see. Homes here face near-constant marine humidity, wind-driven rain off the water, and winter storms that knock out power for hours at a time, which matters when a sump pump or sewage ejector pump stops running mid-storm. Many properties were built as seasonal cabins and sit vacant for stretches between visits, so a slow roof leak or a failed supply line can run unnoticed for days before anyone finds standing water or smells the mustiness of early mold growth. Because the island has no municipal sewer system, septic backups and drain field saturation during heavy rain are also a recurring call, alongside the usual burst-pipe and storm-damage work.

## Services we provide in Anderson Island

Water damage restoration here often starts with a cabin that's been closed up for a few weeks. We extract standing water, pull baseboards and flooring where needed, and dry the structure to the IICRC S500 standard, checking crawlspaces carefully since that's where moisture tends to sit longest on island properties.

Fire and smoke damage cleanup on Anderson Island frequently follows a chimney fire, wood stove malfunction, or electrical fault in an older cabin, since wood heat is still common where properties are off the natural gas grid. We clear soot and smoke odor from framing, insulation, and contents and coordinate rebuild where the fire has compromised structure.

Mold remediation gets called in after the water damage nobody caught in time, often in a crawlspace or behind an exterior wall that stayed damp through a wet winter. We isolate the affected area, remove what can't be salvaged, and dry the space before closing it back up.

Storm damage response covers wind-driven roof and siding damage, tree limb impacts, and the flooding that follows when gutters and drainage can't keep up with a heavy system moving through the Sound. We also handle emergency board-up and tarping to stop further water intrusion while repairs are scheduled.

## Coverage and how fast we can get there

Anderson Island has no bridge. The only way on or off by vehicle is the Pierce County ferry running out of Steilacoom, which means our response planning always accounts for ferry schedule and loading, not just drive time. From our Lakewood headquarters, the route runs down I-5 to Steilacoom to catch the ferry crossing to the island. We build sailing times into dispatch so equipment and crew arrive on the first available crossing rather than getting delayed at the dock, and we keep extraction and drying equipment staged and ready to load. We do not promise a specific on-site arrival time for island calls given the ferry dependency, but we do answer the phone 24/7 and start logistics the moment you call.

## Building stock, site conditions, and permits in Anderson Island

Much of Anderson Island's housing stock began as a platted summer-cabin community in the early-to-mid 1900s, and that history shows up in the mix of original wood-frame cabins, additions built over decades, and newer year-round homes on the same lots. Crawlspace foundations are common, which gives water an easy path to collect under flooring where it goes unnoticed. Expect older plumbing runs, including galvanized supply lines and cast iron drains in cabins from that earlier era, alongside wells and septic systems rather than municipal water and sewer. Pierce County is the permitting authority for the island, since it's unincorporated, and shoreline proximity means many properties fall under shoreline setback and critical-area review for structural repair or rebuild work, something we flag early so a restoration scope doesn't stall waiting on a permit.
