## Restoration emergencies common in Fort Lewis

Fort Lewis sits in the South Puget Sound lowlands, where the Pacific Northwest's wet season runs long and heavy. Months of steady rain saturate ground that's already dealing with a seasonally high water table, which means crawlspaces and below-grade areas can stay damp well after a storm has passed. Add in the area's mix of older post-war construction and newer infill, and you get two very different failure patterns: aging plumbing and roofing on one side, and poorly sealed penetrations or settling foundations on the newer builds. Wind-driven rain during winter storms is a frequent cause of roof and window leaks, and a slow leak behind drywall or under flooring here can turn into active mold growth within 24 to 48 hours if it isn't caught.

## Services we provide in Fort Lewis

Water damage restoration is our most frequent call in this area, usually tied to failed supply lines, ice-maker connections, or water finding its way in through roof flashing during a hard rain. We extract standing water, pull moisture readings from subfloor and wall cavities, and set drying equipment based on what those readings actually show, not just what's visible on the surface.

Fire and smoke damage cleanup addresses both the structural loss and the residue that spreads through HVAC ducting and soft materials long after the flames are out. Smoke odor clings to insulation and framing in ways a surface cleaning won't fix, so we scope the full extent before starting repairs.

Mold remediation becomes necessary when the region's damp winters go unnoticed, often behind baseboards or inside crawlspaces where airflow is already limited. We contain the affected area, remove compromised material, and verify conditions before closing the job back up.

Storm damage response covers wind-torn roofing, downed-tree impacts, and the board-up or tarping work that keeps a structure protected while permanent repairs are scheduled. Biohazard and trauma cleanup is handled with the same documentation standard we use for insurance-covered losses, following OSHA bloodborne pathogen guidelines for safe removal and disposal.

## Building stock, site conditions, and permits in Fort Lewis

Much of the housing in and around Fort Lewis dates from the mid-20th century military expansion era through more recent infill construction, which means a wide range of plumbing vintages on any given street, from galvanized and early copper supply lines to modern PEX. Crawlspace foundations are common in the older stock, and galvanized pipe in particular tends to corrode from the inside out, giving no visible warning before it fails. The region's clay-heavy, poorly draining soils hold water against foundations and crawlspace vapor barriers longer than sandier ground would, which is a factor in how long a structure stays wet after a leak or storm event. Structural repair work tied to Fort Lewis properties generally falls under Pierce County or installation-specific permitting depending on whether the property sits on base or off, and Washington's energy and building code editions apply to any rebuild scope.
