## What does fire and smoke restoration actually involve?

Fire and smoke restoration is the full process of returning a fire-damaged structure to a safe, livable condition. It covers emergency stabilization, soot removal, odor elimination, water extraction from firefighting efforts, and structural repair. A complete restoration job addresses all of these under one coordinated scope, not as separate contracts.

The confusion most homeowners run into is thinking fire restoration is just cleaning up char and painting over smoke stains. It is not. Smoke particles are acidic and microscopic. They penetrate drywall, insulation, HVAC ductwork, and soft contents within hours of a fire. Soot left on metal surfaces causes corrosion within 72 hours. Smoke odor that is not treated at the molecular level with thermal fogging or hydroxyl generators will return every time humidity rises, which in the Las Vegas Valley happens seasonally even in a desert climate.

The [IICRC S700 Standard for Professional Smoke and Soot Restoration](https://www.iicrc.org) defines the technical protocols certified firms follow. A technician holding the FSRT (Fire and Smoke Restoration Technician) credential has been trained to these standards. Ask for that credential before signing any restoration contract.

## What happens in the first 24 hours after a fire in Enterprise?

The first 24 hours determine how much secondary damage you accumulate and how strong your insurance claim will be. Act on these steps immediately.

**1. Get a board-up and tarp if needed.** If the fire compromised windows, doors, or the roof, the structure needs to be secured before nightfall. Unsecured openings invite theft, weather, and additional liability. A restoration company can handle emergency board-up the same night.

**2. Document everything before anyone touches it.** Walk every room and photograph or video every surface, including ceilings, inside cabinets, and the HVAC registers. This visual record is your evidence for the insurance adjuster. Do not discard any damaged contents until the adjuster has seen them or your contractor has created a contents inventory.

**3. Call your insurance company to open a claim.** You do not need a restoration estimate before you call. Open the claim immediately so the clock on their response obligation starts running. Nevada law requires insurers to acknowledge a claim within 20 days and make a coverage decision within 30 days of receiving proof of loss.

**4. Call a certified restoration contractor.** A firm with IICRC FSRT certification can provide an emergency response, begin stabilization, and generate a written scope that your adjuster can work from. This scope should be line-item detailed, not a lump-sum estimate.

**5. Do not run your HVAC system.** Smoke particles in the ductwork will be redistributed through every room the moment the system runs. Leave it off until a technician has assessed the ductwork.

## How does the fire damage restoration process work, phase by phase?

A properly managed fire restoration follows a defined sequence. Skipping phases or reordering them creates problems that show up months later as lingering odor, corrosion, or failed paint adhesion.

**Phase 1: Emergency stabilization (Day 1-2).** Secure the structure, extract standing water from suppression efforts, set drying equipment, and begin air quality control with HEPA air scrubbers. The goal is stopping the damage clock.

**Phase 2: Contents inventory and pack-out (Day 2-5).** Salvageable contents are inventoried, packed, and moved to a climate-controlled storage facility for cleaning. Non-salvageable items are documented for the insurance claim. This step protects your belongings from ongoing smoke exposure and gives the crew clear access to the structure.

**Phase 3: Structural cleaning and deodorization (Day 3-14, depending on scope).** Soot is removed from all surfaces using dry chemical sponges, HEPA vacuuming, and wet cleaning methods matched to the surface type. Porous materials that cannot be cleaned, such as heavily contaminated drywall or insulation, are removed. Thermal fogging or hydroxyl generation treats odor at the molecular level throughout the structure. HVAC ductwork is cleaned separately.

**Phase 4: Reconstruction (varies).** Once the structure is clean, dry, and deodorized, rebuild begins. This includes drywall, insulation, flooring, cabinetry, and paint. A general contractor with restoration experience can handle this phase as a continuation of the same project rather than a handoff to a separate trade.

## Will homeowners insurance cover fire and smoke restoration in Enterprise, NV?

Standard homeowners insurance policies cover fire damage, including smoke and soot damage, as a named peril. This means both the structural repair and the contents loss are typically covered, subject to your deductible and policy limits.

What affects your payout:

- **Documentation quality.** A line-item scope from a certified contractor gives the adjuster a defensible number. A vague estimate invites lowballing.
- **Contents inventory.** Every item you can document with photos, receipts, or serial numbers strengthens your claim. Your restoration contractor should produce a formal contents list.
- **Cause of fire.** Fires caused by the homeowner's negligence are still covered under most policies. Intentional fires are not. Fires caused by a tenant in a rental property are a more complex coverage question.
- **Additional living expenses (ALE).** If the home is uninhabitable, most policies cover temporary housing and meal costs while restoration is underway. Open this coverage with your insurer on day one.

The [Insurance Information Institute](https://www.iii.org) has a plain-language guide to homeowners fire coverage that is worth reading before you meet with your adjuster.

One practical note: your insurance company may suggest a preferred contractor. You are not required to use them. In Nevada, you have the right to choose your own licensed contractor. The insurer pays the claim; you control who does the work.
