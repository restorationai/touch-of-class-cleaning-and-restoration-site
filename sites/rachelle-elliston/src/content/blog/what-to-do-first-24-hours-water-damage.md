If water is actively spreading through your home right now, stop the source first. Shut off the main water supply valve (usually near the meter, in a utility closet, or under a sink), then cut power to any rooms where water is touching outlets, baseboards, or appliances. Those two steps alone reduce how much damage the next 24 hours will cause. Everything after that is about slowing the spread and documenting what happened before materials start to deteriorate.

## Why the First 24 Hours Are the Critical Window

Water does not sit still. Within minutes of a pipe burst or appliance failure, water wicks into drywall, travels under flooring, and soaks into insulation inside wall cavities. Within a few hours, wood subfloor begins to swell and buckle. By the 24-to-48-hour mark, mold spores already present in the environment can begin colonizing wet organic material, especially in a warm interior. In Las Vegas and North Las Vegas, where summer indoor temperatures can stay high even with air conditioning running, that window can be shorter than homeowners expect.

The goal of the first 24 hours is not to restore anything. It is to stop the damage from compounding.

## Immediate Steps to Take Right Now

Work through these in order. Do not skip ahead.

1. **Stop the water source.** Turn off the main shutoff valve if you cannot isolate the break. If a roof is leaking during a storm, place buckets and lay towels to channel water away from walls and electrical panels.

2. **Cut power to affected areas.** Go to your breaker panel and switch off circuits for any room where water is present on the floor or near outlets. Do not walk through standing water to reach a switch or appliance while the power is still on.

3. **Call your insurance company.** Most homeowner policies require prompt notice of a loss. Call the claims line, get a claim number, and ask whether they require you to use a specific contractor or whether you may choose your own. Write down the name of every person you speak with.

4. **Document everything before you move it.** Walk through the affected areas and take photos and video of standing water, stained ceilings, wet walls, and damaged belongings. Capture the source of the water if visible. This documentation supports your claim and helps a restoration crew understand the scope before they arrive.

5. **Remove standing water if you can do so safely.** A wet-dry vacuum, mop, or towels can pull surface water off hard floors. Every gallon you remove is one less gallon soaking into the subfloor beneath. Do not use a standard household vacuum.

6. **Move salvageable belongings to a dry area.** Furniture left sitting in water will stain flooring and absorb moisture into its own structure. Lift upholstered pieces onto blocks or move them to a dry room. Remove rugs from wet hard floors, since rugs trap moisture against the surface underneath.

7. **Increase air circulation.** Open interior doors, run ceiling fans, and if outdoor humidity is lower than indoor humidity (common in the Mojave desert climate), open windows. Do not run the HVAC system if water has reached the air handler, ductwork, or return vents.

## What NOT to Do in the First 24 Hours

Some common instincts make the situation worse.

- **Do not use a standard household fan as your only drying method.** Moving air over wet drywall and flooring without also dehumidifying the space drives moisture deeper into building materials rather than removing it from the structure. Professional drying equipment pulls moisture out of the air as it evaporates.

- **Do not tear out wet drywall or flooring yourself before documentation.** Once materials are removed, it is much harder to demonstrate the extent of the loss to an insurance adjuster. Photograph first, then ask a professional whether removal is necessary.

- **Do not assume the surface tells the whole story.** If the leak is behind drywall, you may see only a small stain or soft spot at the surface while the interior cavity holds significant moisture. Flooring can read dry to the touch while the subfloor underneath is saturated. Moisture meters and thermal imaging reveal what eyes cannot.

- **Do not use electric heaters or heat guns to speed drying.** Rapid surface drying without addressing moisture inside the wall assembly can trap water and accelerate mold growth behind a surface that looks dry.

- **Do not discard damaged items before the adjuster has seen them.** Your insurance company may need to inspect belongings before approving replacement value. Ask before throwing anything away.

## When to Call a Water Damage Restoration Professional

Some water losses are manageable with a shop vac and a few fans. Many are not, and the ones that look small are often the ones that cause the most expensive problems later.

Call a professional restoration company if any of the following apply:

- The water source was a sewage line, toilet overflow, or any water that may have contacted waste. This is a contamination issue, not just a moisture issue.
- Water has been present for more than a few hours before you discovered it.
- The affected area is larger than a single small room.
- Water has reached wall cavities, insulation, or the subfloor.
- You can smell a musty or sour odor, which can indicate microbial growth has already begun.
- The structure involved is older construction, which in North Las Vegas often means plumbing and building materials that respond differently to moisture than newer builds.

A trained restoration crew uses moisture meters, thermal cameras, and calibrated drying equipment to dry a structure to the IICRC S500 standard, which is the industry benchmark for structural drying. That process typically takes several days and is documented with daily moisture readings, giving you and your insurance company a clear record of the work performed.

## What Happens After the First 24 Hours

Once the immediate emergency is stabilized, the recovery process shifts into a longer drying and assessment phase. Restoration technicians typically place industrial air movers and dehumidifiers throughout the affected area and return daily to take moisture readings and adjust equipment placement. Drying times vary based on the materials involved, the extent of saturation, and ambient conditions, but most residential water losses require several days of active drying before materials reach acceptable moisture levels.

After drying is confirmed, the assessment turns to what needs to be repaired or replaced. Drywall that was saturated often needs to be cut out and replaced. Flooring that buckled or delaminated typically cannot be saved. Insulation inside wall cavities almost always requires removal once it has absorbed water.

Throughout this process, keep all receipts, all communication with your insurance company, and all moisture logs provided by the restoration crew. This paperwork is your record if any dispute arises during the claims process.
