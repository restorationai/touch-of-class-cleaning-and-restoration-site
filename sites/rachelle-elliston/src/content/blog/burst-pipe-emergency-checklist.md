## What to Do Right Now

If a pipe just burst in your home, the first sixty seconds matter more than anything else. Get to your main water shutoff valve and turn it off completely. Then cut power to any rooms where water is pooling near outlets, fixtures, or the electrical panel. Once the water stops flowing and the electricity is off, you can breathe and work through the rest of this checklist without the situation getting worse underneath your feet.

---

## Step-by-Step: The First 30 Minutes

Work through these in order. Each step assumes the previous one is done.

1. **Shut off the main water supply.** In most North Las Vegas homes, the main shutoff is near the water meter, often at the front of the property along the curb, or inside the garage. Turn it clockwise until it stops. If the valve is corroded and won't budge, call the water utility to shut it at the meter.

2. **Kill the electricity to affected areas.** Water and live circuits are a life-safety issue. Go to your breaker panel and switch off any circuit that serves the room or rooms with standing water. If the panel itself is in a wet area, do not touch it. Call an electrician or your utility.

3. **Open faucets to drain pressure.** After the main is off, open the lowest faucet in the house, usually a hose bib outside or a sink on the ground floor. This relieves residual pressure in the lines and reduces the volume of water still draining from the break.

4. **Document everything before you touch it.** Use your phone to photograph and video the source of the break, the path the water traveled, and every surface that is visibly wet. Ceiling bubbles, soaked drywall, warped baseboards, water stains on concrete slab, capture it all. Your insurance adjuster will want this, and so will any restoration contractor.

5. **Move valuables and furniture off wet flooring.** Water under furniture legs can wick up into upholstery and wood within minutes. Get rugs, furniture, and anything stored on the floor moved to a dry area. Place aluminum foil or plastic sheeting under furniture legs if you cannot move a piece entirely.

6. **Start airflow if it is safe to do so.** Open windows and interior doors. If you have box fans, point them across wet hard floors. Do not use fans near standing water or anywhere electricity is still on. Airflow alone will not dry a structural loss, but it slows secondary damage while you wait for help.

7. **Call your insurance company.** Report the loss as soon as you have photos and the immediate hazards are controlled. Most homeowner policies cover sudden and accidental pipe bursts. Get a claim number before any contractor starts work.

## What NOT to Do After a Pipe Bursts

Some instincts that feel helpful can actually make the damage worse or complicate your insurance claim.

- **Do not use a wet/dry vacuum as your primary drying strategy.** A shop vac removes surface water but does nothing for moisture inside walls, under flooring, or in the subfloor. Materials that feel dry at the surface can stay saturated for days. Mold can begin colonizing damp building materials within 24 to 48 hours in a warm indoor environment, and Las Vegas summers mean your interior temperatures are already elevated.

- **Do not turn your heat up to "dry things out."** Warmth speeds microbial growth. If anything, keep the space cooler.

- **Do not discard damaged materials before your adjuster or a restoration contractor documents them.** Wet drywall, flooring, and insulation are physical evidence of the scope of loss. Throwing them out before documentation can reduce your claim payout.

- **Do not assume the damage stops where you can see it.** Water follows gravity and the path of least resistance. A burst pipe in a second-floor bathroom can travel down wall cavities and appear as a stain on a first-floor ceiling twenty feet away. If the leak ran for any length of time before you noticed it, the hidden moisture often exceeds the visible damage.

- **Do not ignore a slow drip that preceded the burst.** If you noticed reduced water pressure, discoloration at a joint, or a damp smell in a cabinet before the pipe fully failed, mention that timeline to your adjuster and your contractor. It affects how the loss is categorized.

## When the Damage Is Behind the Walls

This is where DIY response hits its limit. If the break was inside a wall cavity, under a slab, or behind a finished ceiling, you will not be able to assess the true extent of moisture without professional equipment.

Restoration contractors use thermal imaging cameras and calibrated moisture meters to map saturation levels through finished surfaces without tearing everything open first. That data drives a targeted drying plan: where to place commercial dehumidifiers, which wall cavities need air movers directed into them, and whether any materials are too saturated to save.

The same applies to appliance-related leaks. A washing machine supply line failure or a refrigerator ice maker line that has been seeping slowly behind cabinetry can saturate the subfloor and wall framing long before the water reaches the finished floor surface. By the time you see the buckle or the stain, the structural material underneath may have been wet for days.

If your loss involves any of these hidden-moisture scenarios, professional water damage restoration is the appropriate next step, not a longer drying period with household fans.

## The Recovery Timeline: What Comes After the Emergency

Once the source is stopped and the immediate hazards are controlled, the recovery process typically moves through three phases.

**Extraction and drying.** Standing water is removed first, then commercial drying equipment is set up and monitored over several days. Drying logs are kept for insurance documentation. In the desert climate of the Las Vegas Valley, low ambient humidity can help, but it does not eliminate the need for equipment inside wall cavities and under flooring where outdoor air does not reach.

**Moisture verification.** Before any repairs begin, moisture readings need to confirm that structural materials have returned to acceptable levels. Rebuilding over wet framing or subfloor is one of the most common causes of mold problems showing up months after a water loss.

**Repairs and reconstruction.** Drywall replacement, flooring installation, painting, and any plumbing repairs happen in this phase. Depending on the scope, this may involve coordinating multiple trades.
