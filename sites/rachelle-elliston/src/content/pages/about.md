Desert Valley Contracting Inc has been responding to property emergencies in the Las Vegas Valley since 2000. That's more than two decades of showing up when a pipe bursts at 2 a.m., when smoke has worked its way into every closet, or when a homeowner lifts a baseboard and finds the wall cavity black with mold. The company is based in North Las Vegas and holds contractor licenses #0072652-B and B6 with the State of Nevada.

## What we do

On any given day, the work looks like this: a crew is on a water loss pulling saturated flooring while a project manager photographs damage for an insurance adjuster. Another technician is running air scrubbers on a mold job in a house built before the neighborhood had paved streets. The phone rings with a fire call, and someone is already mapping the route. Because the work is available around the clock, the team is structured to respond at any hour, not just during business hours. That rhythm, urgent, detail-driven, and accountable to both the property owner and the insurance carrier, defines the day-to-day more than any service list does.

Working insurance claims is a significant part of the job. That means producing documentation carriers can actually use: moisture readings at multiple depths, photo logs tied to specific affected areas, and written scopes that reflect what the drying equipment recorded, not what sounds reasonable. When documentation is thorough, the claim process moves faster and homeowners are not left arguing over what happened.

## Our certifications and licensure

The IICRC is the body that sets the technical standards restoration contractors work from, covering everything from how long a structure must dry before it is considered complete to what constitutes proper containment on a mold job. Holding IICRC certification as a firm, and having technicians credentialed in water restoration (WRT), structural drying (ASD), applied microbial remediation (AMRT), and fire and smoke restoration (FSRT), means the work follows those standards rather than guesswork.

The EPA Lead-Safe certification matters specifically for homes built before 1978. Federal law requires certified firms to follow specific work practices when disturbing lead-based paint during renovation or remediation. In a metro area where older housing stock is scattered throughout established neighborhoods, that credential is not optional, it is a legal requirement for doing the work correctly.

OSHA training rounds out the safety side, covering the protocols that protect both crews and occupants when conditions on a job site are hazardous.

## Where we work

The company serves North Las Vegas, Las Vegas, Henderson, Boulder City, and the broader Clark County area. The Las Vegas Valley presents a specific set of conditions: intense summer heat that accelerates mold colonization after a water loss, flash flooding that can push water into structures faster than drains can handle it, and a housing stock that ranges from mid-century block construction to newer stucco-and-frame builds, each with its own moisture behavior. Knowing how the region's climate and construction interact with water, fire, and mold damage is part of doing the job well here.
