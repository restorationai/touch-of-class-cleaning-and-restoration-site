## Why Spring Valley Properties See Water Damage Differently Than Most

Spring Valley's housing stock spans several decades and construction styles, from mid-century ranch homes with original copper or galvanized plumbing to tract developments built in the 1990s and 2000s with PEX supply lines and slab-on-grade foundations. Each presents its own water damage pattern.

Slab foundations are common throughout the area, and that matters for water cleanup. When a slab-on-grade home takes on water, there is no crawl space to inspect and no basement to pump. Water migrates laterally through the slab, wicking into drywall bottom plates and flooring adhesive before it ever becomes visible at the surface. Moisture meters and thermal imaging cameras reveal what the eye misses, and that kind of equipment-driven assessment is what separates a thorough extraction from a cleanup that leaves hidden saturation behind.

Spring Valley also sits in a basin that channels stormwater quickly during the monsoon months, roughly July through September. Clark County's drainage infrastructure handles typical storm loads, but an intense cell can push water into garages, through sliding-glass-door thresholds, and along the low sides of properties that back up to drainage corridors. That kind of intrusion carries sediment and contaminants that require more than simple water removal.

## Our Emergency Water Cleanup Process in Spring Valley

Every job starts with a thorough inspection before any equipment is placed. Technicians document moisture readings room by room, photograph affected materials, and identify the water source category: clean supply water, gray water from an appliance or drain, or black water from a sewage backup or exterior flood. The category determines the extraction method, the personal protective equipment the crew uses, and what materials can be dried in place versus what needs to be removed.

Extraction comes next. Truck-mounted and portable extraction units pull standing water from carpet, flooring, and subfloor cavities. In slab-on-grade homes, this step is critical because water has nowhere to drain downward; it stays in contact with flooring materials and framing until it is physically removed.

After extraction, the drying phase begins. Commercial-grade air movers and refrigerant or desiccant dehumidifiers are positioned to create a controlled drying environment. In Spring Valley's climate, outdoor humidity is often low, but that doesn't mean drying is automatic. Interior humidity rises sharply once water saturates walls and flooring, and without active dehumidification the evaporated moisture simply redistributes to adjacent materials. Drying is monitored daily against the IICRC S500 standard until readings confirm the structure has returned to acceptable moisture levels.
