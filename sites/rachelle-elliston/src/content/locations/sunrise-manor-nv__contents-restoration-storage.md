## Why Sunrise Manor Properties See Elevated Contents Damage

The Mojave Desert climate creates a deceptive environment for stored belongings. Extreme heat accelerates off-gassing from smoke residue, meaning soot that might stay inert in a cooler climate actively bonds to upholstery fibers and wood finishes faster here. Summer temperatures regularly exceed 110°F in unshaded spaces, and a garage or enclosed room without air conditioning can reach temperatures that warp vinyl records, delaminate laminate furniture, and crack leather within days of a fire or flood event.

Sunrise Manor's housing stock includes a significant number of single-story ranch homes built during the 1960s and 1970s, many with original cabinetry, tile, and soft furnishings that have accumulated decades of value. These older interiors often contain materials that require specialized cleaning chemistry rather than standard commercial detergents. Asbestos-containing floor tiles and lead-based paint in pre-1978 construction are a real consideration when contents must be moved through affected areas. The Desert Valley Contracting team is EPA Lead-Safe Certified and OSHA trained, which matters when the path from a damaged room to a storage vehicle runs through disturbed building materials.

## Our Contents Restoration and Storage Process in Sunrise Manor

The first step on any contents job is a room-by-room inventory. Every item is photographed, catalogued, and assigned a condition code before anything leaves the property. This documentation is not just for the homeowner's peace of mind; it is the foundation of any insurance claim and prevents disputes about pre-loss condition.

Items that can be cleaned on-site are treated first. Upholstered furniture is inspected for moisture content using thermal imaging and pin-type meters, because fabric that feels dry to the touch can still hold enough residual moisture to grow mold during storage. Hard surfaces, electronics, and artwork are evaluated separately. Items that cannot be safely restored in place are packed, sealed, and transported to a climate-controlled facility, which is a non-negotiable step in a region where ambient heat would continue degrading smoke-affected belongings left in an uncontrolled environment.

Ozone treatment, ultrasonic cleaning for smaller hard goods, and dry-ice blasting for certain porous surfaces are among the methods used depending on the loss type. The IICRC FSRT (Fire and Smoke Restoration) and IICRC WRT (Water Damage Restoration) certifications held by the Desert Valley Contracting team mean the selection of method follows a documented standard, not guesswork.

Once the structure is cleared and dried to IICRC S500 standards, contents are returned, unpacked, and placed according to the homeowner's layout. The full chain of custody is documented at every handoff.

## Local Note

In Sunrise Manor, many of the older ranch-style homes were built with evaporative coolers rather than central air conditioning, and some still rely on them seasonally. After a water loss, an evaporative cooler that continues running will pull outside air, dust, and additional humidity into the structure, actively working against the drying process and recontaminating contents that have already been cleaned. Shutting down the cooler and switching to recirculated air is one of the first things the crew addresses on arrival, and it is a step that is easy to overlook if the team is not familiar with how common this setup is in this part of the valley.
