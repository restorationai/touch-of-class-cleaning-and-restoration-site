## Why Boulder City Properties See Sewage Backup Issues

Boulder City's development history is unlike almost any other Nevada city. Built in the early 1930s to house Hoover Dam workers, much of the original residential core was constructed under federal oversight with infrastructure that has now been in the ground for nine decades. Cast-iron drain lines and clay sewer laterals common in that era are prone to joint separation and root intrusion, especially as the desert soil shifts through extreme heat cycles. Summer ground temperatures in the region regularly stress buried pipe materials that were never engineered for modern water-use volumes.

Beyond age, the city's position near Lake Mead and its reliance on a managed water system means that pressure fluctuations during high-demand periods can push debris back through aging laterals. Homes on the western residential streets and properties closer to the historic downtown corridor tend to sit on original sewer connections that have seen minimal replacement. Septic systems on larger lots at the city's edges carry their own risk: when a leach field becomes saturated or a tank lid fails, the overflow can migrate toward a foundation quickly in compacted caliche soil that doesn't absorb liquid the way looser desert sand might.

## Our Sewage Cleanup and Sanitization Process in Boulder City

Raw sewage is classified as Category 3 water, the most contaminated designation under IICRC standards, and the response protocol reflects that. From the moment a crew arrives, the affected area is treated as a biohazard zone. That means personal protective equipment, containment barriers to prevent cross-contamination into unaffected rooms, and immediate extraction of standing waste.

After bulk removal, every porous material that absorbed sewage, including drywall, insulation, and subfloor sections, is assessed for removal. In Boulder City's older bungalow-style homes, original hardwood subfloors and tongue-and-groove decking are common beneath finished flooring, and those materials hold contamination in ways that surface cleaning cannot address. Structural cavities are treated with EPA-registered disinfectants, and drying equipment is deployed to bring affected assemblies to verified moisture targets before any reconstruction begins. The team documents moisture readings throughout so there is a clear record for insurance purposes.
