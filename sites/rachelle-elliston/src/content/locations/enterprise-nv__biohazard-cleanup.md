## Our Biohazard Cleanup Process in Enterprise

Every job begins with a thorough assessment before any materials are touched. The crew identifies the full affected area, including surfaces that are not visibly compromised but may have absorbed contaminants. This matters especially in Enterprise's newer construction, where engineered flooring and composite baseboards are common and can trap material beneath the surface layer.

Once the scope is established, the process follows a structured sequence:

- **Containment** to prevent cross-contamination to unaffected areas of the property
- **Removal of porous materials** that cannot be decontaminated to a safe standard
- **Application of EPA-registered disinfectants** appropriate for the specific biological hazard
- **Air quality management** using HEPA filtration where aerosolization is a concern
- **Disposal** through licensed biohazard waste carriers in compliance with Nevada Department of Health and Human Services requirements
- **Documentation** of all materials removed and methods used, which is essential for insurance claims and, in some cases, property resale disclosure

All sharps, needles, and infectious materials are handled and packaged to regulatory standard before leaving the property.

## Local Note

One pattern that comes up in Enterprise and the surrounding southern valley is the prevalence of tile flooring throughout the living areas of newer homes. Tile itself is non-porous and easier to decontaminate than carpet or hardwood, but the grout lines between tiles are a different story. Grout is highly porous and can hold biological material at depth even after the surface appears clean. Proper remediation in these homes requires specific attention to grout treatment, and in some cases, grout removal and replacement is the only way to reach a verifiable standard.
