## Why Sunrise Manor Properties See Sewage Backup Issues

Sunrise Manor sits northeast of the Las Vegas Strip in a corridor that saw dense residential construction through the 1960s and 1970s. Many homes in this area were built on concrete slab foundations with original cast-iron or early PVC sewer laterals that are now decades past their expected service life. Cast iron corrodes from the inside out, and the caliche soil layer that runs through much of Clark County can shift enough during soil saturation events to crack those lines at joints.

During the monsoon season, July through September, the valley's storm drain and sewer systems can receive more water in a single afternoon than in several months combined. When municipal sewer mains back up under that kind of hydraulic pressure, the path of least resistance is often a floor drain, a toilet, or a washing machine standpipe inside a home. The result is not just standing water but Category 3 contaminated water carrying bacteria, viruses, and parasites that require a different response than a clean-water pipe burst.

Older homes in this part of the valley also tend to have finished garage conversions and room additions that were permitted under older code cycles, meaning drain lines may run through unconventional paths. That affects where sewage travels and where it hides after a backup.

## Our Sewage Cleanup and Sanitization Process in Sunrise Manor

Category 3 sewage cleanup follows a defined sequence, and skipping steps creates health and structural risks that show up weeks later.

**Containment and safety assessment** come first. Before extraction begins, the affected area is isolated to prevent cross-contamination to unaffected rooms. In slab-foundation homes common to Sunrise Manor, sewage can wick laterally under flooring faster than it appears at the surface, so the containment boundary is often wider than the visible stain.

**Extraction and removal of porous materials** follows. Drywall, carpet, pad, and insulation that have contacted Category 3 water are not salvageable and are removed to a line above the saturation point. In homes with tile floors over a mortar bed, the mortar layer itself can hold contaminated moisture even after the tile surface looks dry.

**Antimicrobial treatment and structural drying** run concurrently. EPA-registered disinfectants are applied to all affected hard surfaces and structural cavities. HEPA air scrubbers run continuously to capture aerosolized contaminants. Our IICRC WRT and ASD-certified technicians monitor moisture readings daily using penetrating meters and thermal imaging until the structure reaches target dryness levels.

**Post-remediation verification** closes the job. Surfaces are tested before any rebuild work begins to confirm contamination levels are within acceptable limits.
