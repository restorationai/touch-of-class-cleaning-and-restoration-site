## Why Summerlin Properties See Water Damage Differently

Summerlin's master-planned communities were built in phases stretching from the early 1990s through the mid-2000s, and the plumbing vintage matters. Homes built in that first wave often used polybutylene or early CPVC supply lines that have now aged past their reliable service window. A slow pinhole leak inside a stucco wall can saturate the framing cavity for weeks before the paint blisters, by then, moisture readings at the surface tell you almost nothing about what's happening behind it.

The climate adds a second wrinkle. Summerlin averages fewer than five inches of rain a year, so most residents don't think of their property as flood-prone. But the valley's monsoon season, typically July through September, can push several inches of rain through a wash in a matter of hours. Flat or low-graded lots in the newer western villages can collect runoff faster than storm drains handle it, and that water finds its way through garage slabs and door thresholds. The dry air between storms also creates a false sense of security: surfaces dry fast, but building cavities don't.

## Local Note

One pattern that comes up repeatedly in Summerlin's single-story homes built on post-tension slabs: when a slab leak is repaired and the water source is stopped, the concrete itself holds residual moisture that reads elevated for an extended period.
