## Why Sunrise Manor Homes Present Unique Renovation Challenges

The desert climate is the most obvious factor, but it is not the only one. Sunrise Manor's older neighborhoods include homes built in the 1960s and 1970s when construction standards, insulation requirements, and ventilation codes looked very different than they do today. Flat and low-slope rooflines common in that era were designed for a dry climate, but they concentrate heat load in attics and create thermal bridging issues that become obvious the moment you open a wall for a remodel. Ductwork in these homes is often undersized by current standards, meaning a kitchen or bathroom renovation that changes the room's layout can expose an HVAC system that was already struggling.

Soil conditions in the valley also matter. The caliche layer common throughout Clark County can complicate any work that touches a foundation, a slab, or exterior drainage. If a remodel involves adding a bathroom, extending a laundry room, or rerouting plumbing, that subsurface hardpan affects how and where lines can run. Knowing this before design decisions are finalized saves time and avoids costly change orders mid-project.

Because the company holds an EPA Lead-Safe certification, work on homes built before 1978 follows proper containment and disposal protocols. That is not a minor detail in a neighborhood where a meaningful portion of the housing stock predates modern renovation standards.
