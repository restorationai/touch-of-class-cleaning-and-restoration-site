## Why Summerlin Properties See Water Damage Differently Than the Rest of the Valley

Summerlin's master-planned communities were built in phases stretching from the early 1990s through the 2000s, which means the housing stock spans a wide range of plumbing vintages. Homes built during the first wave of development in areas like The Trails and The Arbors used polybutylene and early CPVC supply lines that have now aged past their expected service life. Those materials become brittle in the temperature swings the valley experiences, where summer attic spaces can exceed 150 degrees and winter nights drop into the 30s. A pinhole leak in a wall cavity can saturate the bottom plate and subfloor for days before a homeowner notices a soft spot underfoot or a musty smell near the baseboard.

The stucco-and-wood-frame construction common throughout Summerlin also behaves in ways that a surface reading can miss. A moisture meter at the drywall face may show acceptable levels while the OSB sheathing behind it holds water. Our team uses thermal imaging alongside calibrated moisture meters to map the actual wet boundary, not just the visible stain.

## Our Water Damage Restoration Process in Summerlin

When a call comes in, the first priority is stopping active water intrusion if it has not already stopped. From there, the process follows a defined sequence:

**Water extraction and removal.** Truck-mounted and portable extraction units pull standing water from hard floors, carpet, and subfloor assemblies. In Summerlin's tile-heavy homes, water migrates under grout lines and into the mortar bed, so extraction is thorough rather than surface-level.

**Drying system placement.** High-capacity air movers and low-grain refrigerant dehumidifiers are positioned based on psychrometric readings, not guesswork. The dry desert air is an asset here: when equipment is sized correctly, Summerlin's low ambient humidity accelerates evaporation from structural materials compared to coastal climates.

**Daily monitoring.** A technician records temperature, relative humidity, and material moisture content at each visit. Drying is not complete until readings meet the IICRC S500 standard for the affected material class, not just until things feel dry.

**Controlled demolition when necessary.** Wet insulation does not dry in place. Saturated drywall below the flood cut is removed to expose the framing and allow airflow. We document every step with photographs for your insurance file.

## Local Note

Summerlin's newer villages built after 2010 frequently use engineered hardwood and luxury vinyl plank over concrete slab, and that combination creates a hidden-water problem worth knowing. Water that gets under a floating floor spreads laterally much farther than the visible damage suggests, sometimes traveling 15 to 20 feet from the source before it finds a wall or a transition strip. Homeowners who pull up only the visibly buckled planks and stop there often find mold colonizing the slab edge weeks later. A full perimeter moisture check of the affected room, including inside closets and under toe-kicks, is standard practice on every Summerlin job we take.
