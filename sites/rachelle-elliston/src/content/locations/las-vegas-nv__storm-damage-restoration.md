Las Vegas sits in the Mojave Desert, so storm damage might sound like someone else's problem, until a monsoon microburst tears through a neighborhood in July, or a rare winter storm drops enough ice to buckle carport roofs across the valley. Desert Valley Contracting Inc. has been responding to those moments since 2000, operating out of North Las Vegas and reaching affected properties around the clock. ## Why Las Vegas Properties See Distinctive Storm Damage

The Mojave's storm calendar is short but violent. Late-summer monsoon season, typically July through September, pushes moisture up from the Gulf of California and generates fast-moving microbursts that can drop an inch of rain in under an hour. On soil that has almost no organic matter to absorb runoff, that water moves horizontally across hardscape and into any gap it can find: garage door seals, stucco cracks, low-lying window wells, and the expansion joints that run through concrete block construction common throughout the valley.

Winter brings a different threat. Temperatures in Las Vegas drop below freezing on more nights than most residents expect, and a slow-moving Pacific storm system can deposit ice or wet snow on roofs that were designed for radiant heat load, not precipitation weight. Flat or low-slope roofs, found on a large share of both residential and commercial buildings here, are particularly vulnerable to pooling and membrane stress when drainage is blocked by debris.

High-wind events compound both patterns. The valley's topography channels wind between mountain ranges, and gusts during storm cells routinely damage fascia, soffits, and the tile roofing systems used on most homes built after the 1990s. Displaced tiles are not cosmetic, they expose the underlayment to whatever comes next.

## Our Storm Damage Restoration Process in Las Vegas

When a storm call comes in, the first priority is stopping active intrusion before secondary damage compounds the loss. That means tarping exposed roof sections, boarding compromised windows or doors, and extracting any standing water before it migrates into wall cavities or under flooring. In Las Vegas construction, stucco exteriors can mask water intrusion for days, moisture reads dry at the painted surface while the substrate and framing behind it stay saturated. Thermal imaging and moisture mapping are part of every assessment, not an upgrade.

Once the structure is stabilized and documented, the drying process follows IICRC S500 and S520 standards, with equipment calibrated to the desert's low ambient humidity. Low relative humidity actually accelerates surface drying, which can create a false reading on a pin-type meter. Desert Valley's IICRC-certified technicians account for this by monitoring both surface and subsurface readings across the drying cycle rather than relying on a single pass.

Structural repairs, debris removal, and coordination with your insurance adjuster follow sequencing that keeps the claim documentation clean and the project moving. The team holds an EPA Lead-Safe certification, which matters for any pre-1978 construction disturbed during storm repairs.

## Las Vegas Insurance and HOA Coordination

Most homeowner policies in Nevada cover sudden and accidental storm damage, but the documentation requirements vary by carrier and the specifics of how the loss is categorized. ## Local Note

One thing that catches property owners off guard in Las Vegas: the valley's caliche layer. Caliche is a hard, calcium-carbonate-rich soil deposit found a few feet below the surface across much of Clark County. During a heavy monsoon rain, water that cannot penetrate the caliche layer has nowhere to go but sideways, and it finds the path of least resistance, often toward foundations, stem walls, and garage slabs. Properties with landscaping that grades toward the structure rather than away from it are especially exposed. If storm-driven water entered your home at or near the foundation, that drainage pattern is worth addressing as part of the restoration scope, not after the fact.
