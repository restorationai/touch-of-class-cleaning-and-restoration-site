## Why Spring Valley Properties See Sewage Backup Problems

The Las Vegas Valley's soil is largely caliche-heavy, a dense calcium carbonate layer that sits close to the surface in many parts of Clark County. Caliche does not absorb groundwater or drain the way loamy soil does, which means that during a heavy monsoon event, water that cannot percolate into the ground pressurizes aging sewer laterals from the outside while storm runoff overwhelms the system from above. The result is a sewer line backup that pushes sewage up through floor drains, toilets, and washing machine standpipes simultaneously.

Spring Valley also has a wide range of housing vintages. Subdivisions built in the 1980s and early 1990s along the western corridors often used clay or Orangeburg pipe laterals that have since cracked, root-intruded, or collapsed. Newer master-planned communities closer to the 215 Beltway tend to have PVC laterals in better condition, but those homes are served by shared mains that can still surcharge during peak demand. The age and material of your lateral matters enormously when diagnosing why a backup happened and whether it is likely to recur.

## Our Sewage Cleanup and Sanitization Process in Spring Valley

Raw sewage is classified as Category 3 water, the most contaminated classification in the IICRC S500 standard. That means the response protocol is more involved than a clean-water pipe burst, and cutting corners on sanitization creates health risks that linger long after surfaces look dry.

The process Desert Valley Contracting follows on every sewage call in Spring Valley:

1. **Containment and PPE setup.** Affected areas are isolated to prevent cross-contamination to unaffected rooms. Technicians work in full personal protective equipment throughout.
2. **Solid waste removal.** Any sewage solids are bagged and removed per Clark County waste handling requirements before extraction begins.
3. **Extraction.** Truck-mounted and portable extractors pull standing sewage from hard floors and pull contaminated water from carpet and pad, which is then removed and disposed of rather than dried in place.
4. **Antimicrobial treatment.** EPA-registered disinfectants are applied to all affected surfaces, including subfloor, wall base, and any cabinetry that absorbed contamination.
5. **Structural drying.** Industrial air movers and desiccant or refrigerant dehumidifiers run until moisture readings in the subfloor and wall cavities return to pre-loss baselines, verified with thermal imaging and pin meters.
6. **Post-remediation verification.** Final readings and documentation are provided for your insurance carrier and, if required, for any HOA architectural review.

## Local Note

Spring Valley's stucco-and-wood-frame construction, standard across most of the community's residential stock, creates a specific challenge with sewage backups that affect bathrooms on concrete slab foundations. The gap between the bottom plate of the wall framing and the slab is often sealed with foam or caulk, which traps contaminated moisture inside the wall cavity even after the visible floor area looks clean. Technicians use wall cavity probes and thermal imaging to confirm whether contamination has wicked up into the framing before any drying equipment is placed, because an undetected wet wall base is where mold colonization typically begins within the first 24 to 48 hours after a sewage event.
