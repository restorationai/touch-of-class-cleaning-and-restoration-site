Spring Valley sits in the southwestern corner of the Las Vegas Valley, where the Mojave Desert's weather patterns can shift from scorching calm to violent microburst in under an hour. When those storms roll through, they carry enough wind, hail, and flash-flood runoff to peel roofing, splinter fencing, drive water under door thresholds, and deposit debris against foundations before most homeowners have time to move their cars. Desert Valley Contracting Inc. has been responding to storm damage across the valley since 2000, and the team is available around the clock when severe weather hits.

## Why Spring Valley Properties See Distinct Storm Damage

The Las Vegas Valley's desert floor is largely caliche-hardened soil that sheds water rather than absorbing it. During a monsoon-season downpour, runoff has nowhere to go quickly, and it finds the path of least resistance: low-lying yards, window wells, garage slabs, and any exterior wall penetration that wasn't perfectly sealed. Spring Valley's residential mix includes a substantial share of single-story stucco homes built in the 1980s and 1990s. That era of construction often used a three-coat stucco system over wood framing, and when wind-driven rain works its way behind a cracked or aging exterior coat, the sheathing and framing behind it can begin absorbing moisture long before a homeowner notices a stain on the interior drywall.

High-wind events in this part of the valley also carry fine desert grit at speed. That abrasive load strips roof coatings, degrades caulk around skylights and HVAC curbs, and scours window seals. What looks like cosmetic surface damage after a storm can mask compromised weatherproofing that will allow the next rain event to penetrate much deeper.

## Our Storm Damage Restoration Process in Spring Valley

The first priority after any storm call is a systematic exterior and interior assessment before any materials are removed or covered. The crew documents damage with photographs and moisture readings, establishing a baseline that supports the insurance claim and guides the scope of work. For roof and structural damage, tarping and board-up happen the same visit to stop additional water intrusion.

Water that entered during the storm gets addressed with commercial extraction equipment and air-mover and dehumidifier arrays calibrated to the low-humidity desert environment. That dry climate is a double-edged factor: it accelerates surface drying, which can give a misleading "dry" reading on the face of stucco or drywall while the framing cavity behind it stays damp. The team uses thermal imaging and penetrating moisture meters to map what the surface reading misses. Structural drying is tracked daily against drying logs until materials reach documented target moisture levels.

Tree and debris removal from the structure is handled as part of the restoration scope, not as a separate call. Once the site is stabilized and dried, repair work follows, including roofing, siding, window and door systems, and interior finishes damaged by water intrusion.

## Local Note

Spring Valley's west side includes a number of communities where block-wall perimeter fencing is nearly universal. After a high-wind event, sections of that block wall frequently fail and fall inward toward the structure. Beyond the obvious safety concern, a collapsed block wall can redirect subsequent rainfall directly against the home's foundation rather than toward the street. It is worth having the wall section assessed and temporarily stabilized before the next storm in the forecast, even if the home itself appears undamaged. This is a pattern the team sees repeatedly in the western valley's walled residential communities, and it is easy to overlook when the focus is on the roof.
