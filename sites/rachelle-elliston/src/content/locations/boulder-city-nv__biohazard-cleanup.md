## Why Boulder City Properties Present Unique Biohazard Considerations

Boulder City's development history is unlike any other city in Nevada. Built in the early 1930s to house Hoover Dam construction workers, the housing stock includes a meaningful share of older single-family homes with original or minimally updated interiors. Plaster walls, hardwood subfloors, and older HVAC configurations are common in the historic core of the city. These materials are porous in ways that modern construction is not. When biological material contacts plaster, unsealed concrete, or aged wood, it penetrates below the visible surface layer quickly. Standard surface cleaning is not enough. Proper biohazard remediation in this type of housing requires moisture mapping, controlled material removal, and treatment of the substrate, not just the surface.

Boulder City's desert climate adds another layer. The region sees intense heat for much of the year, and high temperatures accelerate the breakdown of biological material and increase the risk of airborne contamination inside enclosed spaces. A property that sits unaddressed in summer conditions for even a short period presents a more complex remediation challenge than the same situation in a cooler climate. Prompt response and proper containment are especially important here.
