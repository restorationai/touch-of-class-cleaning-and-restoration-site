## Why Enterprise Properties See Sewage Backup Issues

Enterprise developed heavily through the 2000s and 2010s, meaning a large share of its housing stock sits on concrete slab foundations with plumbing chases that run beneath the slab. When a main sewer line bellies, roots intrude, or a grease blockage builds far enough back, the backup has nowhere to go except up through floor drains, toilets, and tub surrounds. Because the soil beneath those slabs is predominantly caliche-heavy desert hardpan, any sewage that escapes the pipe has almost no percolation path. It stays put, migrating laterally under the slab and creating contamination well beyond the visible wet area.

Enterprise also has a significant share of properties served by private septic systems rather than the municipal Clark County Water Reclamation District connection. Septic tanks in this climate can reach capacity faster during summer months when water use spikes and bacterial activity in the tank slows under extreme heat. An overflow at the tank or at the distribution lines leading from it is a Category 3 biohazard event, the most serious classification under IICRC standards, and it requires full personal protective equipment, contained waste removal, and EPA-compliant disposal.

## Our Sewage Cleanup and Sanitization Process in Enterprise

Every sewage call starts with a scope assessment before any equipment is staged. Our IICRC-certified technicians identify the contamination boundary using moisture meters and visual inspection, because the visible sewage line rarely matches the actual affected area. In slab-on-grade construction common throughout Enterprise, that boundary check includes probing wall cavities at the base plate and testing subfloor materials if the backup reached a carpeted or wood-floored room.

Once the scope is confirmed, the process moves in this order:

1. **Containment and PPE establishment** to prevent cross-contamination to unaffected rooms.
2. **Solid and liquid waste extraction** using truck-mounted extraction equipment and manual removal of saturated porous materials that cannot be sanitized.
3. **Antimicrobial treatment** applied to all hard surfaces, framing, and concrete that contacted Category 3 water. We use EPA-registered disinfectants at label-required dwell times, not a quick spray-and-wipe.
4. **Structural drying** with commercial dehumidifiers and air movers calibrated to the low ambient humidity of the Mojave. Dry desert air accelerates surface drying but can mask residual moisture in wall cavities, so we monitor with thermal imaging and pin meters throughout.
5. **Post-sanitization verification** before any reconstruction work begins.

Materials that cannot be returned to a sanitary condition, including drywall that wicked sewage, carpet, pad, and certain types of insulation, are bagged, documented, and disposed of per Clark County solid waste requirements.
