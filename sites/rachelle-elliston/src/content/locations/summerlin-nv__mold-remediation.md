The desert climate creates a specific trap: homes run air conditioning hard for eight or nine months a year, condensation collects inside walls and around poorly sealed ducts, and by the time a musty smell is noticeable, mold colonies are already established behind drywall or under flooring. ## Why Summerlin Properties See Mold Issues

The paradox of desert mold is real. Summerlin's master-planned communities were built in waves starting in the 1990s, and a significant portion of that housing stock uses stucco exteriors over wood framing. Stucco is durable in dry heat, but it is not waterproof. When irrigation systems oversaturate landscaping near foundation walls, or when a slow roof leak goes unnoticed through a mild winter, moisture wicks into the framing cavity. Inside, the AC system keeps surface temperatures cool enough for condensation to persist. That combination, exterior moisture intrusion plus interior condensation, is exactly the condition mold needs to colonize.

Flat and low-slope rooflines common in Summerlin's newer developments also pool water during the monsoon season, which runs roughly July through September. A single monsoon storm can deposit more rain in two hours than some months see combined. Roof membrane failures from that kind of sudden saturation often go undetected until a ceiling stain appears weeks later, well after mold has begun growing in the attic or along the top plate.

## Our Mold Remediation Process in Summerlin

Remediation starts with containment, not cleaning. Before any mold material is disturbed, the affected area is isolated using negative air pressure and physical barriers so spores cannot migrate to unaffected rooms. This step matters more in Summerlin's tightly built planned communities, where shared HVAC return pathways can carry airborne spores from one area of a home to another quickly.

From there, the process follows the IICRC S520 standard for mold remediation:

- **Moisture mapping**: Thermal imaging and moisture meters identify the full extent of saturation, including areas that look dry at the surface but are still wet behind the finish layer.
- **Controlled demolition**: Affected porous materials, drywall, insulation, carpet padding, are removed and bagged for disposal. Only what is genuinely contaminated comes out.
- **HEPA vacuuming and antimicrobial treatment**: Structural surfaces are cleaned, treated, and allowed to dry to acceptable moisture levels before any reconstruction begins.
- **Clearance verification**: Moisture readings are confirmed before the containment is removed.

As an IICRC Certified Firm with AMRT (Applied Microbial Remediation Technician) certification, the crew working your job is trained specifically in mold, not just general restoration.

## Local Note

Summerlin homes built between roughly 1995 and 2008 frequently used a paper-faced drywall product that is particularly susceptible to mold once the paper facing gets wet. Unlike fiberglass-faced drywall, the paper backing can sustain mold growth even after the surface appears to have dried. Thermal imaging during the inspection phase is especially important in these homes because a moisture meter reading at the surface can look acceptable while the paper facing on the back side of the board is still supporting active growth. This is a detail that matters in Summerlin's housing stock specifically, and it changes how aggressively the moisture mapping phase needs to be conducted.
