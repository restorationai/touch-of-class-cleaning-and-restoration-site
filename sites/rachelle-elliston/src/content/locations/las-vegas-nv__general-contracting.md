Whether you're updating a kitchen in a 1970s-era home near the Arts District or rebuilding a bathroom suite after water damage in a newer development, the conditions here shape every decision from materials selection to permitting timelines. ## Why Las Vegas Homes Present Unique Renovation Challenges

The desert climate does things to a structure that most building guides don't account for. Stucco exteriors, common across Las Vegas, develop hairline cracks as temperatures swing from over 110°F in summer to near-freezing winter nights. Those cracks let moisture in during the brief but intense monsoon season, and once water gets behind stucco into wood framing or drywall, the damage compounds quickly in the heat. Older homes, particularly those built in the 1960s and 1970s, often have galvanized plumbing that has corroded from the inside, meaning a bathroom remodel can surface a plumbing problem that wasn't visible before demo began. Caliche, the calcium carbonate hardpan layer common in Clark County soil, can also affect drainage around foundations, which matters when a renovation involves any exterior grading or utility work.

HOA requirements add another layer. Many Las Vegas communities, from Summerlin to Henderson-adjacent neighborhoods, require design review board approval before exterior changes, window replacements, or even certain roofing materials. Skipping that step can result in stop-work orders that delay a project by weeks. Knowing which communities require pre-approval and how to prepare a submittal package is part of working here, not an afterthought.

## Our Renovation and General Contracting Process, Built for the Valley

Every project starts with a detailed scope walkthrough. For remodels, that means identifying what's behind the walls before committing to a timeline, because surprises in demo, whether it's asbestos-containing texture in a pre-1980 home or outdated wiring in a mid-century kitchen, affect both schedule and budget. Desert Valley Contracting holds an EPA Lead-Safe Certification, which matters in Las Vegas's older housing stock where lead-based paint is still present in homes built before 1978.

After scope is confirmed, we handle permitting through the City of Las Vegas or Clark County, depending on jurisdiction, and coordinate all licensed trades under our general contractor license (#0072652-B & B6). For post-damage rebuilds, we work from the remediation scope forward, aligning reconstruction with what was documented during the mitigation phase so the insurance claim stays clean and consistent. Our IICRC certifications across water, fire, smoke, and mold disciplines mean we understand the damage side of the work, not just the finish side, which is a meaningful difference when a remodel follows a loss.

Materials are selected with the climate in mind. Tile and stone hold up better than laminate in high-heat environments. Grout and caulk choices in bathrooms matter more here because the dry air causes certain products to shrink and crack faster than they would in a more humid climate. These are the kinds of decisions that don't show up on a bid sheet but affect how the finished work holds up over five and ten years.

## Coordinating with Insurance After a Covered Loss

A significant share of renovation work in Las Vegas follows a covered event: a pipe failure, a kitchen fire, or water intrusion during monsoon season. When that's the case, the rebuild scope needs to match the adjuster's estimate line by line, and any supplements for hidden damage discovered during demo need to be documented and submitted properly. Desert Valley Contracting handles that coordination directly, providing photo documentation, material specifications, and scope narratives that support the claim rather than complicate it. Homeowners don't need to act as intermediaries between the contractor and the carrier.

## Local Note

One thing that catches out-of-area contractors working in Las Vegas: Clark County and the City of Las Vegas are separate permitting jurisdictions, and the boundary runs through neighborhoods that look continuous from the street. A project on one side of a block may pull permits from the City; the house across the street falls under the County. Inspection schedules, fee structures, and code adoption timelines can differ between the two. Knowing which jurisdiction you're in before submitting plans saves days of back-and-forth and keeps a project on schedule.
