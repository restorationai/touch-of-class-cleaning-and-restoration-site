## What Renovations, Remodels and General Contracting actually involves

A kitchen that stopped working for your family years ago. A bathroom with tile from a previous decade and a layout that never made sense. A room gutted by water or fire damage that now sits framed in plastic sheeting while you figure out the next step. Renovation and remodeling work covers a wide range of scopes, but the thread running through all of them is the same: bringing a space from where it is to where it needs to be, with the permits, licensed trades, and sequencing to make it last.

Desert Valley Contracting Inc holds a Nevada General Contractor license (#0072652-B & B6), which means the full scope of a remodel can be managed under one contract rather than pieced together across unrelated subcontractors. That matters in North Las Vegas and the surrounding valley, where the combination of hard water, extreme heat cycling, and slab-on-grade construction creates specific wear patterns that a contractor unfamiliar with the region may not anticipate.

Typical renovation scopes include kitchen remodels (cabinet replacement or refacing, countertop installation, appliance rough-ins, tile backsplash), bathroom remodels (shower and tub surrounds, vanity and fixture replacement, waterproofing membrane installation), room additions, flooring replacement, interior and exterior painting, drywall repair and replacement, and post-damage rebuild following water, fire, or mold remediation. The team is also EPA Lead-Safe Certified, which is relevant in older Las Vegas Valley housing stock where lead-based paint may still be present beneath layers of renovation history.

## Our process

1. **Scope of work and site walkthrough.** Before any number is put on paper, the project is walked in person. Existing conditions are documented, including anything behind walls or under flooring that affects the plan, such as older plumbing, non-standard framing, or prior repair work that was never finished correctly. A written scope is produced before work begins.

2. **Permitting and trade coordination.** Projects that require permits are submitted to the City of North Las Vegas or the relevant jurisdiction before demolition starts. Electrical, plumbing, and HVAC subcontractors are scheduled in the sequence that inspections require, not the sequence that is most convenient for the schedule.

3. **Demolition and rough-in.** Existing finishes come out. Framing, plumbing rough-in, and electrical rough-in are completed and inspected before any walls close. In bathrooms, waterproofing membranes are installed and inspected before tile goes down.

4. **Finish installation.** Cabinets, countertops, tile, flooring, fixtures, and trim are installed in the correct sequence to avoid damage to finished surfaces. Paint is the last step, not an afterthought.

5. **Final walkthrough and punch list.** Every project closes with a documented walkthrough. Items that do not meet the scope are corrected before the project is signed off.

## What separates a good renovation from a bad one

The most common failure point in residential remodeling is the work that happens before the finish materials go in. Tile installed over a substrate that was not properly waterproofed will fail within a few years in a wet environment. Cabinets hung on walls where the stud layout was not confirmed will shift. Flooring installed before the subfloor moisture content stabilizes, which is a real consideration in Las Vegas Valley slab construction after any water intrusion, will buckle or cup.

For post-damage rebuilds specifically, the sequencing between remediation and reconstruction is critical. Rebuilding before a structure has reached documented drying goals traps residual moisture inside new assemblies, which creates conditions for secondary mold growth. A contractor who also holds IICRC certifications in water damage restoration and structural drying understands where those handoff points are and does not rush past them.

On the permitting side, unpermitted work in Clark County creates problems at resale and can complicate insurance claims on future losses. Work that is permitted and inspected is documented, and that documentation has real value.

## Seasonal and regional considerations

Las Vegas Valley summers push interior temperatures in unoccupied or under-insulated spaces well above what finish materials are rated for. Adhesives, grout, and caulk applied during peak summer months need adequate cure time before HVAC is shut off or a space is left unventilated. Conversely, the low desert humidity that is typical most of the year means wood materials acclimate quickly, which is an advantage for flooring installation compared to more humid climates.

Older housing in North Las Vegas, particularly homes built before the 1980s, may have asbestos-containing materials in floor tile, popcorn ceilings, and duct insulation. Renovation work that disturbs those materials requires proper testing and handling before demolition proceeds.
