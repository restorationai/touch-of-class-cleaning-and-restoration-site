## What Is Crawlspace Encapsulation?

Crawlspace encapsulation is the process of sealing a crawlspace from outside air and ground moisture using a reinforced polyethylene vapor barrier, sealed vents, and in most cases a dedicated dehumidifier. Instead of a vented crawlspace that breathes humid Johnston County air all summer, the space becomes a conditioned, controlled environment, similar to a basement.

The barrier, usually 12 to 20 mil thick polyethylene, covers the dirt floor, runs up the foundation walls, and seals around piers and support posts with tape or mechanical fasteners. Crawlspace vents get closed off or replaced with sealed foam covers, and any existing gaps around plumbing or utility penetrations get sealed with spray foam or mastic. A standalone dehumidifier, sized to the square footage, keeps relative humidity under 55 percent year-round, which is the threshold the [EPA](https://www.epa.gov/mold) cites as the point where mold growth becomes unlikely.

## How Much Does Crawlspace Encapsulation Cost in Smithfield, NC?

Most Smithfield homeowners pay between $5,500 and $13,000 for a full encapsulation, with the number driven mostly by square footage, moisture severity, and whether existing mold has to be remediated before the barrier goes in. A small 1,000 square foot crawlspace with no standing water history lands on the low end; a larger home with a history of water intrusion, sagging insulation, and visible mold growth costs more because that work has to happen first.

A few things that move the price:

- **Square footage and crawlspace height.** Tighter, lower crawlspaces take longer to work in and cost more per square foot.
- **Existing moisture damage.** Rotted joists, sagging batt insulation, or mold on the subfloor add remediation and insulation replacement costs before encapsulation starts.
- **Drainage needs.** Homes near low-lying streets close to the Neuse River sometimes need a interior perimeter drain and sump pump added before the barrier goes down, since encapsulation alone doesn't stop active water intrusion.
- **Dehumidifier sizing.** A crawlspace-rated dehumidifier with a condensate pump adds to the project but is what actually keeps humidity controlled after the barrier is installed.

## What's Included in a Professional Crawlspace Encapsulation?

A professional encapsulation job moves through five phases: assessment, remediation (if needed), barrier installation, sealing, and dehumidification. Skipping any one of them is usually why a cheaper encapsulation fails within a year or two.

1. **Assessment.** A technician checks moisture readings at the subfloor and foundation walls, looks for standing water history, checks existing insulation condition, and tests for visible mold.
2. **Remediation, if needed.** Wet or moldy insulation gets removed, any active mold gets treated per industry guidance before sealing anything in, and damaged wood is flagged for repair.
3. **Vapor barrier installation.** The polyethylene sheeting goes down over the dirt floor, up the foundation walls, and around every pier, with seams taped and overlapped so there are no gaps.
4. **Sealing vents and penetrations.** Foundation vents get sealed or replaced, and any gaps around plumbing, wiring, or HVAC lines get closed with foam or mastic.
5. **Dehumidification.** A crawlspace-rated unit with an automatic condensate drain or pump gets installed and set to maintain humidity below the threshold where mold and wood rot take hold.

If your crawlspace already has water intrusion history, read [Water in Crawl Space After Heavy Rain: What to Do Before Mold Moves In](/blog/water-in-crawl-space-after-heavy-rain/) for what to do in the meantime, since encapsulation isn't a substitute for fixing an active leak.

Properties on low-lying streets near the Smithfield Neuse Riverwalk see groundwater push up through vented crawlspaces for weeks after a heavy rain event, which keeps humidity high long after the storm passes.

That sustained humidity is what drives the mold calls Go Green Restoration of NC sees most often in older Johnston County housing stock, where crawlspaces were built vented per older code and never updated. An encapsulated crawlspace with a dehumidifier holds a stable humidity level no matter what's happening outside, which protects subfloor wood, HVAC ductwork, and indoor air quality at the same time.

It's a different calculation for a newer build on higher ground with no moisture history. In that case, encapsulation is more of a preventive upgrade than an urgent fix, and the payback shows up more in energy savings and comfort than in stopping an active problem.
