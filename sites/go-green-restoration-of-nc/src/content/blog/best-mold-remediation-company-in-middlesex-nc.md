## What to Look for When Hiring a Mold Remediation Company in NC

A legitimate mold remediation company should be able to walk you through their process before work begins. Here is what to ask and what to look for.

**Containment protocol.** The IICRC S520 standard requires physical containment of the affected area using poly sheeting and negative air pressure to prevent spores from spreading to unaffected rooms during removal. If a company skips this step, spores will redistribute through your home.

**HEPA filtration.** Air scrubbers with HEPA filters should run throughout the job. HEPA captures particles as small as 0.3 microns, which is small enough to catch mold spores. Standard shop vacuums do not qualify.

**Moisture source identification.** Mold cannot grow without a moisture source. A company that removes visible mold without identifying and fixing the underlying moisture problem is setting you up for a return visit. Ask explicitly: what is causing this, and how will you address it?

**Written scope of work.** Get the remediation plan in writing before work starts. This protects you if the job scope changes and gives your insurance adjuster the documentation they need to process a claim. If the mold followed a covered water loss, your homeowner's policy may cover remediation costs. The [EPA's mold guidance](https://www.epa.gov/mold) is a useful reference for understanding what a proper remediation process looks like.

**Post-remediation verification.** A clearance test (air sampling or surface swab, analyzed by a third-party lab) confirms the mold count has returned to normal background levels. Some companies offer this in-house; others recommend an independent industrial hygienist. Either way, you want documented proof the job is done.

## How Much Does Mold Remediation Cost in NC?

Mold remediation in North Carolina typically runs between $1,500 and $6,000 for most residential jobs, depending on the size of the affected area, the materials involved (drywall, framing, insulation), and whether the moisture source requires repair. Crawl space mold remediation often falls in the $2,000 to $5,000 range and may include encapsulation to prevent recurrence.

Insurance coverage depends on the cause. If mold resulted from a sudden, covered water loss (a burst pipe, an appliance failure), your homeowner's policy will often cover remediation. Mold from a slow, undetected leak or long-term humidity is typically excluded. Document everything before work begins: photos, moisture readings, and a written scope from the remediation company.
