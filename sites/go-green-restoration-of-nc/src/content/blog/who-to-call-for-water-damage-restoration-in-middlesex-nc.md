## When to Call a Plumber vs. a Restoration Company

A restoration company and a plumber do different jobs, and you often need both. Knowing which to call first saves time.

Call a **plumber** when the source of water is still active and needs a physical repair: a burst pipe, a failed supply line, a sewer backup that requires line clearing. The plumber stops the water. They don't dry the structure or document damage for insurance.

Call a **restoration company** as soon as the water source is controlled (or simultaneously, so they're en route while the plumber works). Go Green Restoration of NC handles water extraction, structural drying with commercial-grade LGR dehumidifiers and air movers, moisture mapping per the [IICRC S500 standard](https://www.iicrc.org), and the written documentation your insurance adjuster will need.

A common mistake: homeowners wait until the plumber finishes before calling restoration. Every hour of delay lets water migrate further into wall cavities, subfloor, and insulation. In North Carolina's humid September conditions, secondary mold growth can begin within 24 to 48 hours of a moisture event. Speed is the single biggest factor in keeping a claim manageable.

## What About My Insurance Company?

Call your insurance company after mitigation is underway, not before. This is counterintuitive for a lot of homeowners, but it's the right sequence.

Restoration needs to start immediately to limit damage. Most homeowners policies cover sudden, accidental water damage (burst pipe, appliance failure, storm intrusion) but exclude gradual leaks and flooding from outside the home. The documentation Go Green Restoration of NC produces, including moisture maps, daily drying logs, and a written scope of work, is exactly what adjusters use to evaluate and approve a claim.

For a plain-English breakdown of what policies typically cover, see [Does Homeowners Insurance Cover Water Damage?](/blog/does-homeowners-insurance-cover-water-damage/)

If you have a separate flood insurance policy through the [National Flood Insurance Program (NFIP)](https://www.fema.gov/flood-insurance), notify that carrier as well. Flood policies and homeowners policies are separate claims.
