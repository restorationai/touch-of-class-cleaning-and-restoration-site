Go agree Restoration of NC was built around a straightforward idea: when water is soaking through drywall at 11 p.m. or smoke smell has settled into every room of a house, the people who show up matter as much as the equipment they bring. The company is based in Middlesex, NC, and focuses exclusively on property restoration — the kind of work that starts when something has already gone wrong.

## What we do

Most days look like this: a call comes in from a homeowner who just found standing water under their kitchen flooring, or a property manager dealing with a unit where a pipe burst over the weekend. We assess the damage, explain what needs to happen and in what order, then do the work — drying, cleaning, removing compromised materials, and coordinating with insurance adjusters so the claim moves forward instead of stalling. Restoration work sits at an uncomfortable intersection of construction, insurance paperwork, and crisis response. Homeowners shouldn't have to navigate all three on their own, and we try to make sure they don't have to.

Insurance billing is a real part of what we do. We document affected areas with photos and moisture readings, write scopes of loss that adjusters can actually work with, and bill carriers directly when possible. That process doesn't eliminate stress, but it removes the part where a homeowner has to front tens of thousands of dollars and wait for reimbursement.

## Our certifications and licensure

We'll update this section as we confirm the specific credentials held by our team. What we can say is that restoration work — done correctly — follows published technical standards. The IICRC (Institute of Inspection, Cleaning and Restoration Certification) sets the benchmarks most of the industry works from: the S500 for water damage, the S520 for mold remediation, the S700 for fire and smoke. Those standards govern things like acceptable drying targets, containment protocols, and documentation requirements. When a contractor follows them, you have a reference point for what "done" actually means.

For homes built before 1978, lead-safe work practices are a federal requirement under EPA RRP rules — not optional, not a upsell. We'll note our specific certifications and license numbers on this page once confirmed.
