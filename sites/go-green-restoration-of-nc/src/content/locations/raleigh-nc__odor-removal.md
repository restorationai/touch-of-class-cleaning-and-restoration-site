## Why Raleigh Properties Hold Onto Odors

Triangle summers run hot and wet, and that moisture load gives smoke residue, pet odors, and cooking grease an easy path into porous materials. Historic Oakwood's early 1900s homes often have original plaster, heart pine flooring, and horsehair insulation, all of which absorb and slowly release odor compounds over months. Newer construction near Brier Creek and NC State University tends to have tighter building envelopes, which actually traps cooking and smoke odor inside HVAC systems and recirculates it room to room. Both patterns need different fixes, and guessing wrong means the smell comes back in a few weeks once the weather turns humid again.

## Our Odor Removal and Deodorization Process in Raleigh

We start by identifying the source material, whether that's char on framing after a kitchen fire, urine-soaked subfloor padding, or mildew in a crawlspace vapor barrier. Surface cleaning and source removal come first because no deodorizing technology performs well over an active odor source. From there we select treatment based on the structure: thermal fogging for fire and smoke residue that has worked into wall cavities, ozone treatment for vacant properties where off-gassing time isn't a problem, and hydroxyl deodorization when the home is occupied or pets and people need to stay on-site during treatment. HVAC systems get attention too, since a return vent pulling air through a smoke-damaged attic in a North Hills condo will just reintroduce odor after the walls are clean.

## Equipment & Methods We Use for Odor Removal

Ozone generators oxidize odor molecules but require the space to be unoccupied by people, pets, and plants during treatment and ventilated afterward, so they're best suited to vacant rentals or properties between tenants. Hydroxyl generators produce a similar oxidizing effect without the occupancy restrictions, which makes them the better fit for a lived-in home near Five Points where the family isn't relocating during the job. Thermal fogging vaporizes a deodorizing solution into a fine fog that mimics how smoke penetrated the structure in the first place, reaching into the same cracks, seams, and wall cavities. We match the method, or combination of methods, to the material and the occupancy situation rather than running one process on every job.
