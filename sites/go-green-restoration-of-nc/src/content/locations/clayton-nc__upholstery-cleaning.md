## Why Clayton Homes See Upholstery Wear from Humidity and Pollen

Johnston County's clay-heavy soil and the area's rapid subdivision growth mean a lot of Clayton households are dealing with newer homes, but newer doesn't mean immune. Tight, energy-efficient construction traps humidity indoors during the shoulder seasons, and that moisture gets pulled straight into upholstery fibers, especially in sunrooms and finished basements where airflow is limited. Add in the region's heavy spring pine pollen, which settles on fabric near windows and entryways, and you get a combination of trapped moisture and embedded particulate that surface vacuuming never fully resolves. Homes near wooded lots on the edges of town also see more pet traffic tracked in from yards bordering undeveloped land, which shows up fastest on light-colored fabric arms and cushion seams.

## Our Upholstery Cleaning Process for Clayton Fabrics and Leather

We start by identifying the fabric type and testing colorfastness on a hidden seam, because a lot of the furniture we see in Clayton is a mix of synthetic blends built for durability and real leather pieces bought for formal living rooms that rarely dry evenly once they're wet. For fabric upholstery, we use low-moisture steam extraction that lifts embedded soil and pollen without oversaturating the cushion fill, which matters in a climate where anything left too damp for too long invites musty odor or mildew. Leather gets a different approach entirely: a pH-balanced cleaner followed by a conditioning treatment that restores the oils humidity and AC cycling strip out over a North Carolina summer. Drying time runs faster here than in coastal or mountain climates, but we still set fans and monitor moisture levels before furniture goes back against walls, since trapped humidity behind a couch is a common cause of the mildew smell homeowners call us about.

One thing that catches homeowners off guard in Clayton: furniture stored in garages or unfinished bonus rooms over the winter often comes out smelling fine but tests damp to the touch, because unconditioned spaces in this area swing through wide humidity ranges between January cold snaps and the warm, wet stretches that follow.
