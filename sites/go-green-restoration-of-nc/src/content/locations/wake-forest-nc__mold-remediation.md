## Why Wake Forest Properties See Mold Issues

Wake Forest experienced a significant residential building boom through the late 1990s and 2000s, and a large share of that housing used engineered wood products — OSB sheathing, LVL beams, and composite subfloors — that absorb moisture faster than older solid lumber and can begin to delaminate before visible mold is obvious. Crawl space construction is extremely common in this part of Wake County, and unencapsulated or poorly ventilated crawl spaces trap ground moisture year-round. During July and August, outdoor relative humidity in the Wake Forest area regularly climbs above 80%, which means even a small vapor intrusion event can push crawl space humidity well past the 60% threshold where mold growth accelerates.

The clay-rich soil throughout the area also drains slowly after heavy rain. When grading around a foundation settles over time — common in neighborhoods that were developed quickly during the growth years — water pools against foundation walls and wicks inward. Homeowners in subdivisions off Burlington Mills Road and in the Heritage community have called us after noticing musty odors in finished basements and bonus rooms built over garages, both of which are classic moisture traps in this region's housing stock.

## Our Mold Remediation Process in Wake Forest

Every job starts with a thorough moisture mapping inspection — not just a visual scan. We use thermal imaging and pin-type moisture meters to trace the water source that fed the mold, because removing mold without fixing the moisture pathway means the problem returns within a season. Once the source is identified and controlled, we establish containment using negative air pressure and 6-mil poly barriers to prevent spores from migrating to clean areas of the home during the removal process.

Affected porous materials — drywall, insulation, carpet padding — are removed, double-bagged, and disposed of per North Carolina solid waste guidelines. Structural wood surfaces are HEPA-vacuumed, treated with an EPA-registered antimicrobial, and dried to manufacturer-specified moisture content before any reconstruction begins. Air scrubbers with HEPA filtration run continuously throughout the project. For homes in the 27587 ZIP code area with encapsulated crawl spaces, we pay particular attention to the liner seams and pier wrap — those are the spots where moisture most commonly re-enters after a remediation if they aren't properly resealed.
