## Why Rocky Mount Properties See Insulation Problems

Edgecombe and Nash counties sit in a climate band that delivers both extremes: summer dew points that hover in the uncomfortable 70s and winter cold snaps that push through poorly sealed attic hatches and rim joists. That swing matters because insulation doesn't just lose R-value from age — it loses it from repeated moisture cycling. Fiberglass batts in a crawl space absorb humidity, sag away from the subfloor, and become a substrate for mold. Blown-in cellulose in an attic can settle 20–30% over ten years, leaving the outer edges of the floor system under-insulated.

Homes near City Lake Park and the older mill neighborhoods often predate modern vapor barrier standards. Many were built when a single layer of kraft-faced batts was considered adequate, and the crawl spaces were left open to "breathe" — a practice that does the opposite of what was intended in a humid Carolina climate. The result is that insulation removal in Rocky Mount frequently uncovers not just degraded material but also mold colonies, pest debris, and damaged vapor barriers that all need to be addressed before new insulation goes in.
