## Why Selma Properties See Water Damage Issues

Selma sits in the coastal plain transition zone of central North Carolina, where the water table runs high and soil drains slowly after heavy rain. That combination is rough on crawl spaces. A lot of Selma housing stock, especially homes built before municipal sewer expansion reached further into the county, still relies on crawl space foundations rather than slabs, and those crawl spaces are where we find the slow, hidden leaks: a dripping HVAC condensate line, a slow leak at a cast iron drain joint, or groundwater intrusion after a multi-day rain event. Add in Johnston County's humid summers, and a wet crawl space that goes unnoticed for even a week or two starts feeding mold into the floor joists above it.

## Our Water Damage Restoration Process in Selma

The first call is about stopping the source and getting water off the floor, whether that's standing water in a kitchen, a soaked carpet pad in a living room, or a saturated crawl space vapor barrier. From there we document everything with moisture readings and photos for the insurance file, then move into extraction with truck-mount or portable units depending on access. Structural drying comes next: air movers and dehumidifiers set to pull moisture out of subfloor, framing, and drywall at a pace that matches the material, not a generic schedule. Older plaster-and-lath sections found in some of Selma's historic homes near downtown hold moisture longer than modern drywall, so we adjust drying time and monitor with a moisture meter rather than guessing by the calendar.

## Local Note

One thing that trips up a lot of Selma homeowners: a crawl space can read "dry" at the vents while the vapor barrier underneath is holding standing water against the joists. Johnston County's heavy clay soil doesn't let water drain away from the foundation quickly, so after a hard rain, water can migrate under a house even when the yard itself looks fine the next day. We always pull back the vapor barrier and check joist moisture directly rather than relying on a surface check, especially on older Selma properties where the crawl space was never regraded for drainage.
