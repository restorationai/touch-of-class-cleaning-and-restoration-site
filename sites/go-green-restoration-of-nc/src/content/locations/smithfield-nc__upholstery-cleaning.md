## Our Upholstery Cleaning Process

We start with a fabric and fill assessment, because a linen-blend sofa from a Downtown Smithfield bungalow and a microfiber sectional in a newer West Smithfield build don't respond the same way to the same method. From there:

- **Pre-inspection and test spot**: we check colorfastness and fiber type before any solution touches the piece.
- **Dry soil removal**: vacuum extraction to pull out grit and dander before wet cleaning, which prevents smearing.
- **Low-moisture steam or hot water extraction**: chosen based on fill density and how much ambient humidity the room already holds, since oversaturating a cushion in a high-humidity Smithfield home slows drying and invites the exact mildew smell we're trying to remove.
- **Targeted spot treatment**: for pet stains, food spots, or edge soiling along arms and seat fronts.
- **Controlled drying**: air movers positioned to speed evaporation, which matters more here than in drier inland towns because ambient moisture in the room competes with the drying process.

For leather upholstery, the approach shifts entirely. Leather doesn't absorb moisture the way fabric does, so cleaning focuses on conditioning and pH-balanced cleaners that won't strip the finish, followed by a light conditioning pass to prevent cracking, which is a bigger concern in Smithfield's swings between humid summers and drier winter heating seasons.
