## Why Zebulon Properties Face Particular Fire Damage Challenges

Zebulon's building stock tells two different stories. The older homes closer to the downtown core were built when balloon-frame construction was standard — a method where wall cavities run uninterrupted from the basement to the attic. In a fire, that open channel acts like a chimney, pulling smoke and soot vertically through every floor. Even a contained kitchen fire can deposit residue in the attic insulation and along roof sheathing that a surface wipe-down will never reach.

On the rural edges of town, well-and-crawl-space homes add another layer of complexity. Crawl spaces trap smoke particulate and hold it against the subfloor. Because these spaces are often unventilated or poorly ventilated, odor-causing compounds from combustion linger far longer than they would in a slab-on-grade home. Thermal fogging or ozone treatment alone rarely solves the problem — the crawl space has to be addressed as part of the same scope.

Zebulon's climate also matters. Hot, humid summers mean that soot-coated surfaces absorb ambient moisture quickly, accelerating the corrosive reaction between smoke residue and metal fixtures, appliances, and wiring. The window to clean and neutralize smoke damage before secondary corrosion sets in is shorter here than in drier climates.

## Our Fire Damage Restoration Process in Zebulon

Every fire loss starts with a room-by-room assessment — not just of the burn area, but of every space smoke could have traveled. That means checking crawl spaces, attic cavities, and HVAC returns before a single cleaning task begins. A scope built on incomplete information leads to callbacks; a scope built on the full picture leads to a closed-out job.

From there, the process moves through controlled demolition of unsalvageable materials, dry and wet sponge cleaning of smoke-affected surfaces, and odor neutralization using hydroxyl generators or thermal fogging depending on the materials involved. Porous materials — insulation, carpet, upholstered cabinetry — are almost always removed rather than cleaned; attempting to clean them in place typically traps odor compounds rather than eliminating them.

Structural drying runs concurrently when firefighting water is part of the picture, which it usually is. Walls that look dry at the surface can hold elevated moisture readings for days, particularly in the plaster-and-lath sections of older Zebulon homes. Readings are logged and tracked until materials reach acceptable dryness before any reconstruction begins.

Older homes in and around Downtown Zebulon frequently have original heart pine subfloors and interior trim — materials that absorb smoke oils deeply and are difficult to clean without damaging the wood.
