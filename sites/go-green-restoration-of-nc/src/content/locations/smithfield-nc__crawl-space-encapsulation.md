Homes throughout the area — many of them built in the mid-twentieth century on pier-and-beam foundations — routinely develop standing water, wood rot, and musty odors that creep upward into living spaces long before a homeowner notices anything is wrong. Crawl space encapsulation addresses that problem at the source, sealing the ground and foundation walls against the moisture cycle that Johnston County's soil and weather keep feeding.

## Why Smithfield Properties See Crawl Space Moisture Problems

Johnston County's soil composition is a significant part of the story. Much of the land around Smithfield is underlain by clay-heavy soils that drain poorly after rain. When the ground stays saturated — which happens regularly after storms move up from the coast — water vapor migrates upward through bare dirt crawl space floors and condenses on floor joists, insulation batts, and HVAC ductwork running beneath the house. The relative humidity in an unencapsulated crawl space here can stay above 70 percent for weeks at a stretch during spring and fall, which is the threshold at which mold begins colonizing wood surfaces within 24 to 48 hours.

Older housing stock compounds the issue. Homes built before 1975 — a common vintage in established Smithfield neighborhoods — typically have vented crawl spaces designed around building science that has since been revised. Those foundation vents were meant to flush out humidity, but in a humid climate like this one they often do the opposite: they pull in warm, moist outside air that then condenses when it hits the cooler surfaces below the floor deck. Encapsulation closes those vents and replaces the old passive ventilation model with a sealed, conditioned or dehumidified environment.

## Our Crawl Space Encapsulation Process in Smithfield

Every encapsulation project starts with a thorough inspection of the crawl space before any materials go down. That means checking for existing standing water, assessing the condition of floor joists and rim joists for rot or mold, and measuring current humidity levels. If there is active wood decay or microbial growth, that work is addressed first — encapsulating over a damaged structure traps the problem rather than solving it.

Once the space is clean and dry, the process moves in a deliberate sequence:

- **Ground cover installation**: A heavy-duty polyethylene vapor barrier — typically 20-mil reinforced liner — is laid across the entire crawl space floor and lapped up the foundation walls, overlapped and sealed at seams with moisture-resistant tape.
- **Foundation wall sealing**: The liner is mechanically fastened and sealed to the foundation walls, closing off the primary pathway for ground moisture and outside air.
- **Vent sealing**: Existing foundation vents are closed and sealed. In Johnston County homes, this step often reveals vents that have been partially blocked by debris or damaged by previous pest activity.
- **Drainage and dehumidification**: Depending on the site's drainage profile, a crawl space dehumidifier, a sump pump, or both may be installed to manage any residual moisture load. Given how frequently Smithfield sees heavy rainfall events, a sump system is often the right call even when standing water is not currently present.
