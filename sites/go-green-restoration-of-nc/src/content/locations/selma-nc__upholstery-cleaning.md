## Why Selma Homes See Upholstery Issues

Eastern North Carolina's humid subtropical climate keeps indoor relative humidity elevated for months at a time, and Johnston County's clay-heavy soil holds groundwater close to the surface after heavy rain, which pushes extra moisture into crawlspaces and, eventually, into the air your furniture sits in. A lot of Selma's housing stock includes older farmhouses and ranch homes built before modern HVAC humidity control was standard, so fabric sofas and upholstered chairs in those homes tend to absorb and release moisture more slowly than furniture in a newer, tightly sealed build. Add in well water common on properties outside town limits, and it's not unusual for us to see mineral staining or mildew spotting on arm rests and cushion seams that homeowners assumed was just wear.

## Our Upholstery Cleaning Process in Selma

We start with a fabric and fill assessment, because a cotton-blend sofa, a leather recliner, and a microfiber sectional all need different chemistry and different dwell times. For fabric upholstery, we use hot water extraction calibrated to pull moisture back out fast, since the ambient humidity here works against natural air-drying. Leather upholstery gets a pH-balanced cleaner and conditioner instead of steam, since heat and excess moisture can dry out or discolor leather finishes over time. On pieces with visible mildew spotting or a musty smell, we treat the padding layer, not just the surface fabric, because odor that's settled into foam or batting will come right back if you only clean the top. We finish with targeted air movement to cut drying time, which matters more in Selma than in drier inland climates.
