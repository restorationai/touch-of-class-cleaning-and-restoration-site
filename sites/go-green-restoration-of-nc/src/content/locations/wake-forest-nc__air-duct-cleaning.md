## Why Wake Forest Homes See Duct Buildup Faster Than You'd Expect

Wake Forest sits at the edge of the Piedmont, where warm, humid summers and mild but damp winters create a climate that keeps HVAC systems running hard for most of the year. That extended run time means more air — and more particulates — cycling through your duct system than you'd see in a drier climate. Pollen counts around Falls Lake and the surrounding greenways are consistently high from February through May, and that fine pollen infiltrates return-air grilles and coats duct interiors with a sticky film that traps everything that follows it. Add in the red clay soil common throughout northern Wake County, which gets tracked indoors and pulled into return vents, and the inside of a typical duct system here can look significantly worse than its age would suggest.

Homes in the Historic Downtown Wake Forest area present a different set of conditions. Older construction often means original or partially updated ductwork with more joints, more potential for gaps, and sometimes fiberglass duct board interiors that degrade over time and shed particles into the airstream. These aren't problems unique to Wake Forest, but the combination of older downtown housing stock and the town's newer fast-build subdivisions means the duct cleaning needs here span a wider range than in a more uniformly aged community.

## What the Cleaning Process Actually Involves

The work starts before any equipment comes out — a visual inspection of accessible duct sections, the air handler, and the return-air plenum. In newer Wake Forest builds, we frequently find flex duct that was installed with too many bends, which restricts airflow and creates low spots where debris settles. We photograph what we find so you can see the condition before and after.

Cleaning uses truck-mounted or high-powered portable negative-air equipment that puts the duct system under negative pressure while mechanical agitation — rotating brushes or compressed-air whips — breaks loose debris from duct walls. Everything dislodged gets pulled toward the collection point rather than redistributed into the house. Supply and return sides are cleaned separately. If you've requested dryer vent cleaning at the same visit, that's handled independently — dryer vents require their own access points and a different approach than HVAC ductwork.

Air duct sanitization is available as an add-on for systems where mold growth, musty odors, or pest activity has been confirmed. We apply an EPA-registered antimicrobial product to duct interiors after mechanical cleaning — not as a substitute for it.
