## Why Raleigh Properties See Duct Buildup Faster Than You'd Expect

Raleigh sits in the Piedmont, where red clay soil is everywhere — on boots, on paws, on construction sites. That fine clay dust gets tracked inside and pulled into return-air vents, coating duct walls with a layer that standard filters don't fully catch. The metro's rapid growth has also meant a wide mix of housing stock: tight ranch homes from the 1960s in neighborhoods like Five Points, newer construction near Brier Creek with longer duct runs and more flex-duct connections, and everything in between. Older homes often have ductwork that was never sized for modern high-efficiency systems, which means lower air velocity and more opportunity for particulates to settle rather than stay suspended.

Raleigh's spring pollen season is among the most intense in the country — NC State University's campus turns yellow-green every March, and that pollen infiltrates even well-sealed homes. Combine that with the area's high relative humidity from June through September, and duct interiors can hold enough moisture to support microbial growth between filter changes.

## Our Air Duct Cleaning Process in Raleigh

The process starts with a camera inspection of the main supply and return trunks before any equipment is connected. That footage tells us whether we're dealing with simple dust accumulation, debris from a recent renovation, or something that warrants sanitization after cleaning. We use negative-pressure equipment — a high-powered vacuum unit connected to the main trunk — so that dislodged debris moves toward the machine rather than back into the living space.

Agitation is done with rotary brush systems sized to the duct diameter. Flex duct, which is common in newer Brier Creek and North Hills builds, requires a lighter touch than rigid sheet metal to avoid tearing the inner liner. Once the main runs are cleared, we clean each supply and return register individually. If sanitization is requested — particularly relevant after water damage or visible microbial growth — we apply an EPA-registered antimicrobial agent to duct surfaces after cleaning, not before, so it's contacting clean metal rather than a layer of dust.

Dryer vent cleaning is often added to the same visit. Raleigh's mix of townhomes and two-story colonials frequently have long dryer vent runs with one or two elbows, which are prime spots for lint accumulation and a documented fire risk.

In Historic Oakwood and other pre-1960 Raleigh neighborhoods, it's common to find ductwork running through unconditioned crawl spaces rather than through interior walls or attic space.
