## Why Rolesville Properties See Water Damage Issues

Rolesville sits in the northern Piedmont of Wake County, where the native soil is heavy red clay that drains slowly after a hard rain. That matters for water damage in two ways. First, grading around newer subdivisions built on former farmland does not always move water away from foundations the way it should, so crawlspaces and slab edges take on moisture during summer thunderstorms and the tail end of tropical systems that move through central North Carolina. Second, the town's housing stock is a genuine mix: older homes near the historic downtown core with original plumbing runs that are decades past their expected service life, alongside a wave of recent construction where PEX and PVC lines are common but builder-grade fittings can still fail. ## Our Water Damage Restoration Process in Rolesville

We start with extraction, pulling standing water with truck-mount or portable units depending on how much water is involved and how far it has traveled into the structure. From there we use moisture meters and thermal imaging to map what is actually wet, which in a lot of Rolesville homes means checking subfloor under hardwood or tile that reads dry on the surface while the plywood underneath is saturated. Drying follows the IICRC S500 structural drying standard: strategically placed air movers, dehumidification sized to the affected square footage, and daily moisture readings until materials hit dry-standard numbers, not just until the carpet feels dry to the touch. For crawlspace intrusions, which we see often given the clay soil drainage pattern, we also check for standing water pooled against vapor barriers before we call the space dry.
