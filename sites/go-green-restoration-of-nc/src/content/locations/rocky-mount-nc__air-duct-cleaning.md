## Why Rocky Mount Properties See Duct Buildup Faster

The region's climate does a lot of the work here. Eastern NC summers are long and humid, which means air conditioning runs almost continuously from May through September. That constant airflow pulls fine particulates — pollen from the loblolly pines, dust stirred up by the red clay soil common across Nash and Edgecombe counties, and microscopic debris from older insulation — into the duct system and deposits it on interior surfaces. Homes built in the 1960s through 1980s, which make up a significant share of the housing stock in areas like Candlewood, often have flex duct or older sheet metal runs that weren't sealed to modern standards. Gaps at joints let unconditioned attic air — and whatever is floating in it — pull into the supply side of the system.

The Tar River's flooding history adds another layer. Properties that have experienced even minor water intrusion in crawl spaces or basements can develop mold colonies that eventually migrate into ductwork. Spores don't need a visible flood to travel; elevated crawl space humidity alone is enough to seed growth inside uninsulated duct runs.

## Our Duct Cleaning Process in Rocky Mount

The work starts with a camera inspection of the main trunk lines and accessible branch runs. That step matters more in older Rocky Mount homes than in newer construction because it tells us whether we're dealing with simple dust accumulation, biological growth, or a mechanical issue like a disconnected duct section — each of which calls for a different approach.

Once we know what we're working with, we connect a commercial-grade negative-air vacuum to the system and work section by section with rotary brush agitation tools, dislodging debris from duct walls while the vacuum pulls it toward a contained collection point rather than back into the living space. Supply registers, return grilles, and the air handler cabinet are cleaned as part of the same visit. For systems showing biological contamination — visible mold, musty odor, or confirmed moisture history — we apply an EPA-registered antimicrobial treatment to interior duct surfaces after the mechanical cleaning is complete.

Dryer vent cleaning is available as an add-on and is worth considering if your dryer is taking more than one cycle to dry a normal load. Lint accumulation in the vent run is a fire risk, and it's a straightforward task to address while the equipment is already on-site.

## Local Note: Crawl Space Humidity and Duct Contamination

One pattern that shows up repeatedly in Rocky Mount homes — particularly those built before 1990 on pier-and-beam foundations — is that duct contamination traces back to an unsealed or poorly ventilated crawl space rather than to the air handler itself. The clay-heavy soil under much of Nash County holds moisture, and without a proper vapor barrier and cross-ventilation, crawl space relative humidity can stay above 70 percent year-round. Flex duct resting on or near that environment wicks moisture through the outer jacket over time. When we find heavy biological growth concentrated in the lower duct runs but not the upper system, that's almost always the cause. Addressing the crawl space conditions alongside the duct cleaning produces a more durable result than cleaning alone.
