## Why Raleigh Properties See Water Damage Issues

Raleigh sits on Piedmont red clay, a soil type that swells when saturated and shrinks hard when it dries out. That cycle shifts foundations and puts stress on buried supply and sewer lines year after year, which is a common root cause of slab leaks in newer construction around Brier Creek and North Hills. Meanwhile neighborhoods like Historic Oakwood and Five Points carry a housing stock built well before modern plumbing codes, so galvanized and early copper lines are past or near the end of their service life. Add in the Triangle's humid subtropical summers, where afternoon thunderstorms can drop an inch of rain in under an hour, and you get two distinct failure patterns in the same city: slow slab leaks in the newer subdivisions and sudden pipe or roof failures in the older in-town streets.
