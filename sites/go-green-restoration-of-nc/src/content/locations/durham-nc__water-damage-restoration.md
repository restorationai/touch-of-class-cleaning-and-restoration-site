## Why Durham Properties See Water Damage Differently

Durham sits in the Piedmont Trident — a zone where warm Gulf moisture collides with cooler air off the Appalachians, producing intense localized downpours that can drop two inches of rain in under an hour. Storm drains in older sections of the city, particularly around the Northgate Park and Duke Park neighborhoods, were sized for mid-20th-century runoff volumes and can back up quickly during those events, pushing water through basement windows, floor drains, and crawl space vents.

The housing stock adds another layer of complexity. A significant portion of Durham's residential inventory was built before 1970, when pier-and-beam crawl space construction was standard. Those crawl spaces are prone to ground moisture intrusion year-round, and when a plumbing failure or storm event adds standing water to an already-humid void space, the structural wood — floor joists, sill plates, girders — begins absorbing moisture within hours. Detecting the full extent of saturation requires thermal imaging and moisture mapping, not just a visual walk-through.

Durham also has a large stock of converted mill housing near the Golden Belt and Brightleaf districts. These properties often feature original hardwood subfloors over concrete slabs, a combination that traps water between layers and is easy to underestimate without calibrated moisture meters reading both surfaces.

## Our Water Damage Restoration Process in Durham

Every job starts with a thorough assessment before any equipment is staged. Technicians use thermal cameras and pin-type moisture meters to map the full boundary of saturation — walls, ceilings, subfloors, and any adjacent cavities. That map drives the drying plan and becomes part of the documentation package used for insurance claims.

Extraction comes next. Truck-mounted and portable extraction units pull standing water from hard surfaces and carpet. For crawl spaces — common in the older neighborhoods of Watts-Hillandale and Trinity Park — submersible pumps handle bulk water before desiccant or refrigerant dehumidifiers are positioned in the void space.

Structural drying follows the IICRC S500 standard: a calculated number of air movers and dehumidifiers placed to create a controlled drying environment, with daily moisture readings to verify the structure is tracking toward dry standard. Durham's humidity — routinely above 70% in summer months — means dehumidifier capacity matters more here than in drier climates. Undersized equipment stalls the drying curve and extends the timeline, which is why equipment sizing is calculated, not estimated.

Once materials reach dry standard, the team documents final readings and photographs affected areas for the claims file before any reconstruction begins.
