## Our Water Damage Restoration Process in Raleigh

Every job starts with a moisture assessment before any equipment is placed. Thermal imaging and calibrated moisture meters map exactly where water has traveled — behind baseboards, under hardwood, inside insulated wall cavities. In older homes like those in Oakwood or Mordecai, this step matters more than usual because plaster-over-lath construction holds moisture differently than modern drywall and can mask saturation that a visual inspection would miss.

Once the scope is mapped, water extraction begins using truck-mounted and portable extraction units sized to the loss. Standing water comes out first; then the slower work of pulling residual moisture from structural materials begins. Drying equipment — industrial-grade dehumidifiers and axial air movers — is positioned based on the moisture map, not just placed in the center of a room. Readings are logged daily so the drying curve is documented and the equipment is adjusted as materials release moisture.

Before any affected materials are removed or rebuilt, we photograph and document everything for your insurance adjuster. That documentation package — moisture readings, equipment placement logs, photo records — is what keeps a claim from stalling.

## Local Note

One pattern that comes up repeatedly on jobs in Raleigh's older bungalow neighborhoods — particularly in areas like Five Points and Belmont — is that the original hardwood floors were installed directly over board subfloor with no vapor barrier in between. When water gets under those floors, it doesn't just cup the surface boards; it saturates the subfloor boards beneath, and both layers need to reach drying targets before the floor can be assessed for salvage. Homeowners sometimes see the surface boards flatten out after a few days and assume the problem is solved, but the subfloor can still be holding moisture that will cause secondary mold growth or fastener corrosion months later. Checking both layers before closing out a job is standard practice on these houses.
