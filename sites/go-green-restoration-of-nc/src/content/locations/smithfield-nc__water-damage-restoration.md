## Why Smithfield Properties See Water Damage Issues

Johnston County averages roughly 46 inches of rainfall per year, and Smithfield's position along the Neuse River corridor makes it particularly susceptible to both surface flooding and elevated groundwater. During wet seasons, saturated soil pushes hydrostatic pressure against crawl space walls and slab edges — a pattern that shows up repeatedly in older neighborhoods clustered around Market Street and the downtown historic district, where homes were built before modern vapor barriers and drainage codes were standard practice.

The housing stock in and around Smithfield's historic core skews older, with a meaningful number of properties dating to the mid-20th century or earlier. A slow leak behind a bathroom wall in one of these houses can go undetected for weeks, saturating insulation and floor joists before any visible staining appears on the ceiling below.

Smithfield's proximity to the Neuse River also means that major storm events — tropical remnants are not uncommon in eastern NC — can overwhelm municipal storm drains and send water into basements and low-lying crawl spaces across the 27577 ZIP code area within hours.

## Our Water Damage Restoration Process in Smithfield

When we arrive at a Smithfield property, the first priority is stopping any active water source and assessing the full extent of saturation — not just the visible wet area. We use thermal imaging cameras and calibrated moisture meters to map hidden moisture in wall cavities, under flooring, and inside ceiling assemblies. What looks like a two-foot water stain on drywall is often a six-foot moisture column inside the wall.

Once the scope is documented, we move into water extraction using truck-mounted and portable extraction units capable of pulling standing water from hardwood floors, carpet, and concrete. For Smithfield's older homes with pier-and-beam construction, that often means getting equipment into a low crawl space to extract pooled water and remove saturated insulation before structural drying can begin.

Structural drying follows a psychrometric approach — the right number of air movers and dehumidifiers placed according to the square footage and material types involved, not a one-size-fits-all setup. We monitor moisture readings daily and adjust equipment placement until affected materials reach acceptable dryness levels. Documentation of those readings is provided to your insurance carrier as part of the claim file.
