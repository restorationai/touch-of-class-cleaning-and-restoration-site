## Why Middlesex Properties See Duct Buildup Faster Than You'd Expect

Middlesex sits in Johnston and Nash County line territory where red clay soil is everywhere. That clay dust is fine enough to pass through a standard 1-inch filter, and once it's inside the plenum and branch lines, it coats the interior walls of the ductwork in a thin, sticky film. Over a few seasons that film traps pet dander, pollen from the surrounding farmland, and humidity-driven mold spores into a layer that no amount of filter-changing will address. Homes built in the 1970s and 1980s — a common vintage in the 27557 ZIP code — often have flex duct runs that sag or kink over time, creating low spots where debris accumulates and airflow slows. Slower airflow means longer run times, higher energy bills, and more opportunity for biological growth to take hold.

## Our Air Duct Cleaning Process in Middlesex

The process starts with a camera inspection of the main trunk lines and at least a representative sample of branch runs. This tells us what we're actually dealing with — whether it's primarily dust and debris, visible mold colonization, or pest intrusion — before any equipment is committed. From there, a high-powered truck-mounted vacuum is connected to the system at the air handler, creating negative pressure throughout the entire duct network. Technicians work supply and return registers one by one with agitation tools that dislodge buildup from the duct walls and drive it toward the vacuum. Nothing is blown into the living space.

If the inspection reveals biological growth or a musty odor that persists after mechanical cleaning, we follow up with an EPA-registered sanitizing agent applied via a fogging process inside the ducts. We also inspect and clean the air handler coil and blower wheel when accessible — these components are often the original source of the odor problem, especially in systems that have experienced any moisture intrusion.

Dryer vent cleaning is frequently added at the same visit. In Middlesex's older ranch-style homes, dryer vents often run long horizontal distances through interior walls before exiting, which means lint accumulates faster and the fire risk climbs quietly over years.

One pattern that comes up repeatedly in Middlesex homes built before 1990: the original ductwork was often installed with sheet metal mastic joints that dry out and crack as the system ages.
