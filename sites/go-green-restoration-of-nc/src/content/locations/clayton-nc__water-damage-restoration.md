## Why Clayton Properties See Water Damage Issues

Johnston County's red clay soil doesn't drain the way sandy or loamy soil does. During the area's heavy summer thunderstorms, water tends to pool against foundations instead of soaking away, which pushes moisture into crawl spaces faster than most homeowners expect. Clayton's rapid growth over the past two decades also means a lot of homes sit on newer grading that hasn't fully settled, so drainage patterns can shift year to year. Add in the region's humid subtropical climate, where summer humidity alone can keep framing lumber from fully drying between rain events, and you get conditions where a small leak turns into a mold conversation within days rather than weeks. Older homes closer to Clayton's downtown core often have crawl space foundations and original plumbing runs that are more prone to slow leaks nobody notices until the flooring starts to cup.

## Our Water Damage Restoration Process in Clayton

We start with extraction, pulling standing water out with truck-mounted or portable pumps depending on how much water is involved and where it's sitting. From there we inspect framing, subfloor, and crawl space insulation for moisture that hasn't shown up on the surface yet, since water in a crawl space can wick upward into subfloor and baseboards long after the visible puddle is gone. Structural drying follows, dried per the IICRC S500 standard, with air movers and dehumidifiers placed based on moisture readings rather than guesswork. In Clayton's clay-heavy soil areas, we pay close attention to crawl space vapor barriers, because a compromised barrier lets ground moisture keep feeding the problem even after the original leak is fixed. We re-check moisture levels daily until materials read dry, not just damp to the touch.
