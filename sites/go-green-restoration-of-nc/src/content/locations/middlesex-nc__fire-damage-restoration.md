## Our Fire Damage Restoration Process in Middlesex

Every fire loss is different, but the sequence of work follows a disciplined order that protects your property and your insurance claim.

**Board-up and roof tarping** happen first — open structures invite weather, vandalism, and secondary moisture intrusion. Middlesex's humid summers mean a roof open overnight can introduce enough moisture to complicate an otherwise straightforward smoke-cleanup job.

**Controlled demolition of unsalvageable material** comes next. Soot-saturated drywall, char-damaged framing, and melted insulation are removed and documented photographically before disposal. Documentation matters: your adjuster needs a clear record of what was removed and why.

**Structural drying** runs concurrently with smoke remediation. Suppression water soaks into subfloors, wall cavities, and ceiling assemblies. Industrial desiccant dehumidifiers and air movers run until moisture readings return to pre-loss baselines — not just until surfaces feel dry to the touch.

**Smoke and soot cleaning** addresses both visible residue and the invisible protein film that causes persistent odor. Dry-chemical sponges, wet cleaning agents, and thermal fogging are selected based on the surface type and the fire's burn characteristics — a slow, smoldering fire leaves a different residue profile than a fast, hot burn.

**Odor neutralization** closes the loop. Ozone treatment or hydroxyl generation is used in sealed spaces to break down odor-causing compounds at the molecular level rather than masking them.
