## Why Raleigh Properties See Insulation Problems

North Carolina's Piedmont sits in IECC Climate Zone 4A — mixed-humid — which means insulation has to handle both summer latent heat and winter conductive loss. That dual demand accelerates degradation faster than in drier climates. Older neighborhoods like Historic Oakwood, where homes date to the late 1800s and early 1900s, often have original wood-framed attics with settled or missing insulation and no modern air sealing at all. Crawl spaces throughout the 27601 and 27603 ZIP codes frequently show vapor intrusion from Wake County's clay-heavy soil, which holds groundwater close to the surface after heavy rain events — a pattern that saturates kraft-faced batts and promotes mold colonization within 48 to 72 hours of prolonged exposure.

Newer construction in the North Hills corridor and the Brier Creek area isn't immune either. Blown-in fiberglass installed during the building boom of the early 2000s often falls short of current NC Energy Code minimums (R-38 in attics for Climate Zone 4A), and poorly detailed knee walls in two-story homes bleed heat at a rate that shows up clearly on a thermal camera.

## Our Insulation Removal & Installation Process in Raleigh

Every project starts with a visual and thermal assessment — we photograph existing R-values, check for moisture readings above 16% in wood framing, and identify any pest or rodent activity before a single bag of old material leaves the attic. Contaminated insulation gets bagged and removed under negative pressure when needed; standard settled or degraded material is vacuumed out with truck-mounted equipment.

Once the cavity is clear, we address the substrate: sealing attic bypasses at top plates, rim joists, and penetrations before any new product goes in. In crawl spaces, that means inspecting and replacing failed vapor barriers and ensuring cross-ventilation or encapsulation is appropriate for the specific moisture profile of the space. Installation follows — blown-in fiberglass or cellulose for attics, closed-cell spray foam or rigid board at crawl space walls and rim joists where the conditions call for it. We depth-mark blown-in jobs so the installed R-value is verifiable, not estimated.

## Local Note

Homes in Historic Oakwood and other pre-WWII Raleigh neighborhoods were commonly built with balloon framing — a construction style where the wall cavity runs continuously from the foundation sill to the roof rafters without the fire blocking you'd find in modern platform-framed homes. That open channel acts like a chimney flue for air movement, pulling conditioned air out of living spaces year-round. Standard attic air sealing misses this entirely if the installer doesn't know to look for it. When we work in these older neighborhoods, we inspect the top-of-wall plate line specifically for balloon-frame bypasses and seal them before installing new insulation — it's a step that makes a measurable difference in how the finished job actually performs.
