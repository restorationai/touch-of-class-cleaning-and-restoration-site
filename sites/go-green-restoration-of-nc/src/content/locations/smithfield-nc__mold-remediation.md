## Why Smithfield Properties See Mold Problems

Johnston County averages over 46 inches of rainfall per year, and Smithfield's position along the Neuse River floodplain means soil moisture stays high even between rain events. Crawl spaces beneath older homes in the neighborhoods surrounding Market Street and the downtown core frequently show elevated relative humidity readings — sometimes above 70% — which is well into the range where Cladosporium, Aspergillus, and Stachybotrys (commonly called black mold) colonize wood framing and insulation.

The housing stock compounds the issue. A significant portion of Smithfield's residential properties were built before modern vapor barriers became standard practice. Pier-and-beam foundations with inadequate ventilation, original wood subflooring, and older HVAC ductwork running through unconditioned crawl spaces create exactly the dark, damp, still-air conditions mold needs. Post-storm flooding — which Johnston County sees periodically during hurricane season — accelerates the timeline dramatically. After Hurricane Florence in 2018, many Smithfield homeowners discovered mold behind walls that had appeared dry within days of the water receding.

## Our Mold Remediation Process in Smithfield

Every job begins with a visual inspection and moisture mapping using thermal imaging and calibrated moisture meters. We identify not just visible growth but the moisture source driving it — because removing mold without correcting the underlying moisture condition means it returns.

Once the scope is established, we establish containment barriers with negative air pressure to prevent spores from migrating to unaffected areas of the home. HEPA air scrubbers run continuously throughout the remediation. Affected porous materials — drywall, insulation, compromised wood framing — are removed and bagged for proper disposal. Hard surfaces are treated with EPA-registered antimicrobial agents. Structural wood that can be preserved is HEPA-vacuumed, cleaned, and encapsulated where appropriate.

Drying and dehumidification follow removal. In Smithfield's climate, this step takes longer than in drier regions — we don't pull equipment early just to close a job faster. Final clearance involves post-remediation verification, which can include third-party air sampling if the scope warrants it or if your insurance carrier requires documentation.

## Smithfield Insurance Coordination

Mold claims in North Carolina can be complicated. Many standard homeowner policies include mold coverage only when the growth is a direct result of a covered peril — a burst pipe, for example — and exclude mold that developed from long-term humidity or deferred maintenance. We document the loss thoroughly: moisture readings, photographs of affected materials, scope of work, and removal records. That documentation is what adjusters need to process a claim, and having it organized from the start reduces back-and-forth.

If your policy does cover the loss, we work directly with your carrier. If coverage is limited or denied, we walk you through what the remediation actually requires so you can make an informed decision about how to proceed.
