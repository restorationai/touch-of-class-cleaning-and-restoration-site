## Why Middlesex Properties See Mold Issues

The geography around Middlesex creates conditions that restoration crews in drier parts of the state rarely deal with. The area's clay-heavy soil doesn't drain quickly, which means moisture migrates upward through foundation slabs and into crawl spaces long after a rainstorm ends. Homes built in the 1960s through the 1980s — a common era for the residential stock in this corridor — often have vapor barriers that have degraded or were never installed to modern standards. Fiberglass batt insulation tucked against a crawl space rim joist holds moisture like a sponge, and once mold colonizes that insulation, it typically needs full removal rather than surface treatment.

The warm, humid shoulder seasons — April through May and September through November — are when we see the most new mold calls in this area. Air conditioning systems cycle off at night when outdoor humidity spikes, and condensation forms on ductwork and subfloor surfaces. That recurring wet-dry cycle is exactly what accelerates mold growth on wood framing.

## Our Mold Remediation Process in Middlesex

Every job starts with a thorough assessment — not just a visual check, but moisture readings with a calibrated meter at the wall cavity and subfloor level. In Middlesex homes with pier-and-beam or block foundations, we pay particular attention to the crawl space, because that's where the source moisture almost always originates. Treating visible mold on a bathroom ceiling without addressing a wet crawl space underneath is a temporary fix at best.

Once we've mapped the affected areas, we establish containment using 6-mil poly sheeting and negative air pressure with HEPA-filtered air scrubbers. This keeps mold spores from migrating to unaffected rooms during the removal process. Affected porous materials — drywall, insulation, compromised wood sheathing — are removed, bagged, and disposed of properly. Structural wood surfaces are treated with an EPA-registered antimicrobial agent, then dried to the moisture content the material held before the moisture event.

After remediation, we verify clearance with post-remediation moisture readings. If a client wants a third-party air quality test — which some insurance carriers and buyers' agents request — we can coordinate with an independent industrial hygienist before the job is closed out.

## Middlesex Insurance Coordination

Mold claims in North Carolina can be complicated. Many homeowner policies cover mold remediation only when it results directly from a covered peril — a burst pipe, for example — and exclude mold that developed from long-term elevated humidity or a slow leak that went unreported. We document the loss thoroughly: photographs, moisture readings, written scope of work, and material quantities. That documentation is what adjusters need to process a claim efficiently, and it's what protects you if there's a dispute about coverage.

If your policy does cover the loss, we work directly with your adjuster and can bill the carrier for covered portions. If the damage falls outside your policy, we'll give you a clear written estimate so you know exactly what you're looking at before any work begins.

One thing we've learned working in Middlesex specifically: homes in this area that were built with a partial masonry foundation — a common construction pattern on rural lots developed in the 1970s — often have a section of poured concrete block that sits directly against the soil grade.
