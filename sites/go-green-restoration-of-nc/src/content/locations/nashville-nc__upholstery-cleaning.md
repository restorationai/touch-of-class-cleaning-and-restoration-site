## Why Nashville Furniture Wears Differently

Nashville sits in the transition zone between the Piedmont and the Coastal Plain, which means summers run hot and heavy with humidity that doesn't fully let up at night. Fabric upholstery in homes without strong air circulation, especially in older houses near downtown Nashville with window units instead of central air, can trap moisture in the cushions and padding long after the room itself feels dry. That trapped moisture is what causes the faint mildew smell homeowners often can't place. Add in pollen season, which coats porches and gets tracked in on clothing every spring, and pet hair from the area's many rural and semi-rural properties, and you get upholstery that looks fine on the surface but is holding a lot more underneath.

## Our Upholstery Cleaning Process

We start every job with a fabric or leather identification step, because a hot water extraction that's perfectly safe on a synthetic-blend couch can damage aniline leather or a delicate natural-fiber piece. Once we know what we're working with, we pretest an inconspicuous spot for colorfastness, then move into the actual cleaning: low-moisture steam and gentle agitation for fabric, and a pH-balanced cleaner followed by conditioning for leather. In Nashville's humid stretches we pay close attention to drying time and often bring in air movers to push moisture out of dense cushion foam faster than the ambient air would on its own. That extra drying step matters more here than it would in a drier climate, since foam that stays damp too long is exactly what invites the musty smell back.

## Local note

A lot of the furniture we're asked to clean in Nashville has been passed down through a family for a generation or two, which is common in a town this size where people tend to stay put or inherit the family home. Older upholstered pieces, especially anything reupholstered decades ago with natural fiber fill like cotton batting or horsehair, react very differently to moisture than modern foam-and-polyester furniture. We test more conservatively on these pieces and lean toward drier cleaning methods, because a heavier water-based extraction on old batting can take days to dry fully in Nashville's humidity and risks setting in the very odor we're trying to remove.
