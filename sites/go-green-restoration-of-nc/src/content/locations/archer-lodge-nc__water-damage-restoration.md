## Why Archer Lodge Properties See Water Damage Issues

Archer Lodge sits on the kind of dense Piedmont clay that doesn't drain quickly once it's saturated. After a heavy summer thunderstorm or the remnants of a tropical system move through, that clay holds water at grade level longer than sandier soil would, and it pushes moisture toward foundation walls instead of carrying it away. In the newer subdivisions built over former farmland, slab foundations are common and grading can be inconsistent from lot to lot, so water finds the low corner of a garage or utility room during a storm. On older parcels throughout Johnston County, homes sit on crawl spaces, and a failed supply line or a water heater that gives out can soak floor joists and insulation for days before anyone notices the smell or the give in the flooring. Rural properties on well and septic systems add another layer: a failed sump or a backed-up line doesn't just mean wet carpet, it can mean standing water that needs to be treated as contaminated until we test it.

## Our Water Damage Restoration Process in Archer Lodge

We start by identifying the source and shutting it off, then move to extraction with truck-mounted or portable pumps depending on how much water is involved and where the home's access points are. From there we map moisture in the walls, subfloor, and any crawl space using meters rather than guesswork, because a floor that reads dry on the surface can still be holding water in the subfloor underneath. Drying equipment, air movers and dehumidifiers sized to the affected square footage, goes in next, and we track moisture readings daily until materials reach normal levels, following the drying benchmarks set out in the IICRC S500 standard rather than pulling equipment early just because carpet looks dry. For crawl space homes, that often means running equipment down there separately from the living space above, since a damp crawl space can keep re-wetting a dried floor if it's left untreated.
