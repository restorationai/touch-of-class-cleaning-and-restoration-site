## Our Black Mold Removal Process in Corona

Black mold remediation is not a bleach-and-paint job. Stachybotrys produces mycotoxins that bind to surfaces and become airborne during disturbance — which is why containment comes before anything else. When a crew arrives at a Corona property, the first steps are establishing negative-air pressure containment using 6-mil poly barriers and HEPA-filtered air scrubbers, then conducting a moisture mapping survey with a thermal camera and pin-type meters to define the true boundary of the affected zone. Visible mold is rarely the full picture.

Contaminated drywall, insulation, and porous materials are removed in sealed bags and disposed of per California Department of Public Health guidelines. Structural framing and hard surfaces are HEPA-vacuumed, wire-brushed where needed, and treated with an EPA-registered antimicrobial. Drying equipment — commercial dehumidifiers and air movers — runs until moisture readings return to baseline for the specific material type, documented per the IICRC S520 standard for mold remediation. The team holds IICRC WRT certification (#70145645), which means moisture mapping and drying protocol are part of every job, not an afterthought.

Post-remediation, an independent clearance test — air sampling and surface swabs analyzed by a third-party lab — confirms the space is safe before reconstruction begins. That documentation matters for insurance claims and for future resale disclosures under California law.
