## Why Corona Properties See More Sewage Backup Issues

Corona sits at the edge of the Santa Ana River watershed, and a significant portion of its residential neighborhoods — particularly those developed in the 1970s and 1980s along the south and west sides of the city — rely on aging clay or Orangeburg sewer laterals that were never designed for today's household water loads. These older pipes crack, root-intrude, and collapse with little warning. When they fail, the backup doesn't just hit the floor drain; it follows the path of least resistance through shower pans, washing machine standpipes, and low-set toilet flanges.

Corona's soil composition adds another layer of complexity. The expansive clay soils common in the area shift seasonally — contracting in summer heat and swelling after the region's infrequent but intense rain events. That ground movement stresses sewer laterals at every joint, and a pipe that holds through a dry August can split open after the first significant rain in November. Properties near the 91 Freeway corridor and those in the lower-elevation pockets of the city tend to see this pattern repeat.

## Our Sewage Cleanup and Sanitization Process in Corona

Sewage is classified as Category 3 water — the most contaminated type — and the cleanup protocol reflects that. Here's how the work actually unfolds on a Corona property:

**Containment and assessment first.** Before any extraction begins, the affected zone is isolated to prevent cross-contamination into clean areas of the home. We identify the source, confirm the sewer line has been cleared or capped by a plumber, and document the full extent of affected materials with photos and moisture readings.

**Extraction and removal.** Standing sewage is extracted using truck-mounted units. Saturated porous materials — drywall, baseboards, carpet, pad, and in some cases sections of subfloor — are removed and bagged for disposal per California Department of Public Health guidelines. On Corona properties with concrete slab foundations (common in tracts built before 1990), we check carefully for sewage wicking into the slab itself.

**Antimicrobial treatment and drying.** All affected surfaces are treated with EPA-registered disinfectants. Commercial air movers and dehumidifiers are deployed and monitored daily until moisture readings return to pre-loss baselines. In Corona's low-humidity summer months, structural drying often completes faster than in coastal areas — but that same dry heat can mask residual contamination that still needs chemical treatment.

**Clearance documentation.** Before equipment is removed, we provide written moisture logs and photo documentation — the same records your insurance adjuster and, if applicable, your HOA will need.
