## Why Angleton Properties See Water Damage Issues

Angleton sits on the flat coastal plain of Brazoria County, where the native soil is heavy in clay and drains slowly even after a routine thunderstorm. That slow drainage, combined with a high water table common across the area, means yards and crawlspaces can stay saturated for days after rain moves through. Older homes built on pier-and-beam foundations, common throughout the older sections of town, are especially vulnerable to moisture wicking up from damp ground into subfloor framing. Newer slab-foundation construction isn't immune either: expansive clay soil shifts with moisture content, and a slow slab leak can go unnoticed for weeks before it surfaces as warped flooring or a musty smell near baseboards. Gulf humidity year-round also slows natural evaporation, so water that would dry out in a drier climate tends to linger in wall cavities and insulation here.

## Our Water Damage Restoration Process in Angleton

Every call starts with identifying the water category and mapping how far it has traveled, which in clay-soil, pier-and-beam homes often means checking subfloor framing in addition to visible flooring. We extract standing water first, then set up air movers and dehumidifiers sized to the humidity load, not just the square footage, since Gulf Coast moisture in the air works against standard drying equipment. Moisture readings are taken at multiple depths in drywall, baseboards, and subfloor material and tracked daily so drying isn't called finished just because the surface feels dry; clay-soil moisture can keep wicking into a structure from below even after the original leak is stopped. Once materials read dry and stable across repeated checks, we document the process for the homeowner and for insurance.
