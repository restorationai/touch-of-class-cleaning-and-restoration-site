## Why Freeport Properties See Water Damage Issues

Freeport's flat, low-lying terrain and proximity to the Intracoastal Waterway mean the water table sits close to the surface for much of the year. Homes and small commercial buildings here deal with slow-draining soil during heavy rain events, and that same soil holds moisture against foundations longer than it would in a higher, sandier part of the county. Add in the Gulf Coast's year-round humidity, and a water intrusion that would dry out in a few days somewhere drier can linger, feeding mold growth inside wall cavities within 24 to 48 hours. Older homes near the city's industrial corridor, many built with slab foundations and minimal vapor barriers, are particularly prone to trapped moisture after a pipe failure or roof leak.

## Our Water Damage Restoration Process in Freeport

Water extraction starts the same way on every job: we pull standing water with truck-mount or portable extraction equipment, then map moisture behind walls and under flooring with meters rather than guessing from surface appearance. In Freeport specifically, we pay close attention to slab moisture readings, since concrete here can wick groundwater upward during wet months and mimic a fresh leak weeks after the original water event was handled. Structural drying is set up and monitored to the IICRC S500 standard, with drying goals based on each material's actual moisture content rather than a fixed number of days. Because humidity outside rarely drops low enough to help the drying process along, we rely on commercial dehumidification to pull moisture out of the air faster than the Gulf Coast climate would on its own.
