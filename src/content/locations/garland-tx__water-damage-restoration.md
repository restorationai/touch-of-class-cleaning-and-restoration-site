## Why Garland Properties See Water Damage Issues

Garland sits on the heavy clay soil common across North Texas, and that clay expands and contracts hard between summer drought and spring downpours. Slab foundations shift with it, which opens hairline cracks that let groundwater migrate upward during saturated stretches and lets slab plumbing joints work loose over the years. Add in Dallas County's pattern of fast-moving spring and early-summer thunderstorms, sometimes dropping several inches in under an hour, and you get a city where water damage shows up from both plumbing failure and storm intrusion in roughly equal measure. Older Garland neighborhoods built during the postwar growth boom also still carry a fair amount of original cast iron and galvanized supply piping, which corrodes from the inside and fails without much warning.

## Our Water Damage Restoration Process in Garland

We start with extraction, pulling standing water with truck-mounted or portable units depending on how far the loss sits from access points, since a lot of Garland's older homes have narrower side entries than newer construction allows for. From there we moisture-map the affected rooms with meters and sometimes thermal imaging to find water that's traveled under flooring or behind baseboards rather than relying on what's visible at the surface. Drywall and insulation that can't be dried in place come out; everything else gets dried in position using air movers and dehumidifiers set up according to the IICRC S500 structural drying standard, with daily moisture readings logged until materials hit dry targets. On slab-foundation homes we pay particular attention to the slab itself, since concrete holds moisture longer than people expect and calling a job finished too early is how secondary mold problems start weeks later.

## Garland Insurance Coordination

Most Garland water damage claims come down to how clearly the loss is documented at the start. We photograph and log affected materials room by room before any demolition happens, which gives your adjuster a clear record of what the water touched and what had to be removed. Slab leak claims in particular tend to draw more scrutiny from carriers, since insurers want to see a clear distinction between sudden pipe failure and long-term seepage, so we document moisture patterns in a way that supports that distinction rather than leaves it ambiguous.

## Local note

One thing that catches Garland homeowners off guard: because the clay soil here swells after heavy rain, a slab that tests dry on the surface a week after a leak can still be holding moisture underneath, and that moisture works its way back up through grout and flooring once the soil around the foundation starts drying and shifting again. We factor that delayed re-wetting into how long we keep drying equipment on site rather than pulling it the moment surface readings look good.
