## Why Garland Properties See Water Damage Issues

Garland's housing stock leans heavily on post-war and mid-century construction, a lot of it built directly on concrete slabs rather than raised foundations. That's standard across North Texas, but it means plumbing runs through or under the slab itself instead of an accessible crawlspace. When the area's expansive clay soil swells after heavy rain and then contracts during the long dry stretches of a Texas summer, it puts constant stress on those buried lines. Add in the humidity that settles over the Dallas-Fort Worth area for much of the year, and a leak that goes unnoticed for even a few days has ideal conditions for mold colonization to start well before most homeowners ever see standing water.

## Our Water Damage Restoration Process in Garland

We start with extraction, pulling standing water out with truck-mounted or portable pumps depending on how much volume we're dealing with and whether the loss is contained to one room or has traveled under flooring. From there we map moisture with meters, checking not just the surface but what's happening inside slab-adjacent walls and under cabinetry, because a Garland home's concrete slab holds and transfers moisture differently than a raised wood-frame floor would. Structural drying equipment gets placed based on that map, not guesswork, and we adjust the drying plan as humidity readings change day to day, since summer air here can slow evaporation even with fans and dehumidifiers running. We recheck moisture levels before we call a job finished rather than drying on a fixed schedule.

## Garland Insurance Coordination

Slab leaks raise a question almost every Garland homeowner asks us: does my policy cover this? Many Texas homeowners policies cover the water damage caused by a sudden plumbing failure even when they exclude the cost of actually repairing the slab or the pipe itself, and that distinction matters for how a claim gets filed. We document the affected materials, moisture readings, and extent of the loss with photos and detailed notes so you have what you need when you're talking to your adjuster, and we can walk you through which parts of the damage are typically treated as water mitigation versus which fall under a separate foundation or plumbing repair.
