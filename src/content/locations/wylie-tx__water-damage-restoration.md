## Why Wylie Properties See Water Damage Issues

Much of Wylie's housing stock was built from the 1990s through the present as the city expanded along the Collin County line, and nearly all of it sits on concrete slab foundations rather than basements or crawlspaces. Blackland prairie clay underneath those slabs absorbs moisture during wet stretches and pulls away from foundations during dry, hot summers, and that movement stresses copper and PEX lines running through or under the slab. Add in North Texas's pattern of sudden, heavy spring storms followed by long dry spells, and you get a cycle that's hard on plumbing and roofing both. Water intrusion here tends to start quietly, a slow slab leak raising the moisture content in carpet pad or baseboards weeks before a homeowner notices warped flooring or a musty smell.

## Our Water Damage Restoration Process in Wylie

Because so much of the water damage we see in Wylie starts below the slab or behind drywall rather than from an obvious flood, the first step on site is always moisture mapping with a meter, not a visual walkthrough. We extract standing water with truck-mount or portable units depending on access, then pull baseboards and drill small relief points in drywall where needed to let trapped moisture escape from wall cavities. Drying is tracked and documented against the IICRC S500 structural drying standard, with humidity, temperature, and material moisture content logged daily so there's a clear record showing the structure reached a dry standard before equipment comes out. For slab leak cases specifically, we coordinate with a plumber to confirm the leak is located and repaired before we start structural drying, since drying around an active leak just wastes time and money.
