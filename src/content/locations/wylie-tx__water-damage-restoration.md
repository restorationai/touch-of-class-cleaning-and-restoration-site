## Why Wylie Properties See Water Damage Issues

Much of Wylie sits on Blackland Prairie clay, the same expansive soil found across this part of North Texas. It swells when wet and shrinks hard during the dry months, which stresses the slab foundations that most homes here are built on. Over time that movement cracks the concrete around plumbing penetrations and opens gaps where water can migrate under flooring without ever showing on the surface. Add in a housing stock that grew fast through the late 1990s and 2000s, much of it built with PEX or poly plumbing run through the slab, and you get a pattern we see often: a slow slab leak that goes unnoticed for weeks until carpet starts smelling musty or a floor feels soft underfoot. Summer storms rolling in off the Lake Lavon watershed add a second pressure point, with heavy rain runoff backing up against foundations that don't always have working French drains or grading built for the volume.

## Our Water Damage Restoration Process in Wylie

We start with extraction, pulling standing water with truck-mounted or portable units depending on how much access the property allows. From there we run moisture meters across walls, subfloor, and slab to find out how far the water actually traveled, not just where it's visible. Because clay soil keeps shifting under a dried-out slab, we pay close attention to any pre-existing foundation cracks and document them before we set up drying equipment, so there's a clear record of what caused what. Drying follows the IICRC S500 structural drying standard: air movers positioned to push moisture out of wall cavities and flooring, dehumidifiers sized to the affected square footage, and daily moisture checks until the materials read dry, not just dry to the touch. For slab leaks, we coordinate with a plumber on locating and repairing the source before we close out drying, since restarting the process after a second leak wastes everyone's time and money.

## Local note

A lot of Wylie's slab-on-grade homes don't have a sump pump or interior drain system, since the clay soil here doesn't drain the way sandier soil does elsewhere in North Texas. That means water from a slab leak or heavy rain intrusion tends to spread laterally under flooring instead of pooling in one visible spot, which is why we check moisture readings well beyond the obvious wet area before calling a room dry.
