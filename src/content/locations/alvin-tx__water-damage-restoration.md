## Why Alvin Properties See Water Damage Issues

Most homes around Alvin sit on concrete slab foundations, a standard for Gulf Coast construction, which changes how water moves through a structure compared to homes with crawlspaces or basements. When a supply line or water heater fails, water doesn't fall to a basement, it spreads laterally across the slab under cabinets, baseboards, and interior walls before anyone notices. Brazoria County's heavy clay soil, often called gumbo clay locally, swells when saturated and shrinks when it dries, and that constant movement stresses slab joints and plumbing connections over time. Add in Alvin's exposure to tropical storm systems and prolonged heavy rain events, and groundwater intrusion through slab cracks or foundation penetrations becomes a recurring pattern rather than a one-off accident.

## Our Water Damage Restoration Process in Alvin

We start with extraction, pulling standing water with truck-mount or portable units depending on how much access the property allows, then move into assessment. On slab homes, that means checking moisture levels under baseboards, inside wall cavities, and around cabinet toe-kicks, because water can travel several feet from the original source line before it ever shows as a visible stain. Structural drying follows, using air movers and dehumidifiers positioned to pull moisture out of drywall and framing at a rate that matches how the material actually releases water, not a fixed schedule. We monitor with moisture meters throughout the drying period and only pull equipment once materials read dry, not when a calendar date says they should be.
