## Why McKinney Properties See Water Damage Issues

Collin County's expansive clay is the main driver. As it dries out in a Texas summer and then swells again after a heavy spring rain, slab foundations shift just enough to stress copper and PEX lines running underneath them. That shows up as a slow, hard-to-pinpoint slab leak long before anyone notices standing water. McKinney's housing stock adds another layer: the historic core around downtown has homes from the early-to-mid 1900s, some still on pier-and-beam foundations with original cast iron or galvanized plumbing, while the newer subdivisions built during the city's rapid growth over the past two decades are almost all slab-on-grade. Each foundation type fails differently, and each one dries differently once water gets into it.

## Our Water Damage Restoration Process in McKinney

We start by tracing the source, because a slab home and a pier-and-beam home point to very different culprits. On slab properties we use moisture meters and thermal imaging to map how far water has traveled under flooring before we ever cut into drywall. On older pier-and-beam homes, the crawlspace itself often holds moisture long after the surface looks dry, so we check subfloor framing and insulation directly rather than relying on what the living space tells us. From there it's standard extraction with truck-mount or portable pumps, then structural drying equipment set and monitored daily, with drying logged against the IICRC S500 standard so your insurance adjuster has a clear record of what was wet, what we did, and when it cleared.
