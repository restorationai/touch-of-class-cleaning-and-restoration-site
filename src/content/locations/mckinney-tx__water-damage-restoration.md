## Why McKinney Properties See Water Damage Issues

McKinney sits on the Blackland Prairie, and the clay-heavy soil common across Collin County expands and contracts hard with North Texas's swing between summer drought and sudden downpours. That soil movement stresses slab foundations and the plumbing running through them, which is a frequent source of slow leaks that go unnoticed until a baseboard swells or a floor starts to feel soft underfoot. Add in the area's rapid growth since the 1990s, newer subdivisions built fast to keep pace with demand, alongside pockets of much older homes near the historic core, and you get two very different failure patterns: PEX and PVC fittings giving out in newer construction, versus aging galvanized pipe and original plumbing stacks in older properties. Both can flood a home just as fast.

## Our Water Damage Restoration Process in McKinney

We start with extraction, pulling standing water with truck-mounted and portable units before it migrates further into subfloor and framing. From there, moisture mapping tells us what's actually happening behind the walls, because surface-dry drywall or baseboard can be hiding a saturated stud cavity underneath. Structural drying equipment goes in next, calibrated to the specific materials involved: older plaster and solid wood subfloor in McKinney's established neighborhoods release moisture more slowly than the engineered flooring and drywall common in newer builds, which usually means a longer drying window even when the initial extraction looks complete. We monitor daily with moisture meters and only pull equipment once readings confirm the structure has actually reached dry standard, not just dry to the touch.
