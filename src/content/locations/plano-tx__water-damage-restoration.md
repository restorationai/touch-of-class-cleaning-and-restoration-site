## Why Plano Properties See Water Damage Issues

Most of Plano was built out during the suburban growth boom of the 1970s through the 1990s, which means a huge share of the housing stock sits on slab foundations poured over Blackland Prairie clay. That clay expands when it absorbs water and contracts hard when it dries, and the cycle puts constant stress on the copper and PEX supply lines running under and through those slabs. We see a steady pattern of slab leaks, especially in homes now thirty to fifty years old where original plumbing is reaching the end of its service life. Add in North Texas's occasional hard freezes, the kind that catch homeowners off guard because the rest of the winter runs mild, and burst pipes in attics, garages, and exterior walls become a recurring call pattern across the city.

## Our Water Damage Restoration Process in Plano

Water on a slab behaves differently than water on a raised wood floor, and our approach reflects that. We start with extraction to pull standing water off tile, laminate, and concrete before it has a chance to migrate under baseboards and into wall cavities. Moisture meters and thermal imaging let us map how far water has traveled under the slab edge and inside drywall, which matters in Plano because a slab leak can push water laterally for several feet before it ever shows as a visible stain. Drying is calibrated to the materials involved and follows IICRC S500 drying principles, with air movers and dehumidifiers placed to pull moisture out of both the structure and the subfloor assembly rather than just drying the surface. We document moisture readings at each visit so you and your adjuster can see the drying progress in measurable terms, not just a technician's opinion.
