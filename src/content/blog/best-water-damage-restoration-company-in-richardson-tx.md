## What Should You Look for When Choosing a Water Damage Company in Richardson?

The single most useful screening question is whether the company will give you a written scope of work before they start, not a verbal ballpark. A legitimate restoration company documents moisture readings room by room, states what's being dried versus removed, and puts that in writing so you and your insurance adjuster are looking at the same facts.

Ask how drying is monitored. Structural drying should follow the [IICRC S500 standard](https://www.iicrc.org), which means daily moisture-meter readings in affected materials until they return to a dry standard, not just "a few days of fans running." Ask how many visits are included to confirm the structure is actually dry before equipment comes out.

Ask about mold risk specifically if standing water sat for more than 24-48 hours. The [EPA's mold guidance](https://www.epa.gov/mold) notes that mold can begin growing within 24 to 48 hours on wet porous materials, which is why fast extraction matters more than almost anything else on this list. If you're not sure whether you're already past that window, our [first-24-hours checklist](/blog/what-to-do-first-24-hours-water-damage/) covers what to do right now, before anyone arrives.

Finally, ask who handles the insurance paperwork. Most homeowners policies cover sudden water events like burst pipes but exclude damage from long-term, unaddressed leaks, so the documentation a company provides at the start of the job often determines whether a claim gets approved cleanly. Our breakdown of [what homeowners insurance actually covers for water damage](/blog/does-homeowners-insurance-cover-water-damage/) is a useful companion read before you file.

## Who Should You Call First After Water Damage in Richardson?

Call whichever company can get a technician on-site soonest with a moisture meter and extraction equipment, not whichever has the biggest sign. Every hour water sits on hardwood, under cabinet toe-kicks, or inside a wall cavity adds to the materials that may need to be removed instead of dried in place.

When you call, have the source of water identified (or shut off, if it's a supply line you can reach), the approximate square footage affected, and your insurance information ready. A company that asks good questions on that first call, rather than just quoting a flat number sight unseen, is usually the one that documents the job properly later.
