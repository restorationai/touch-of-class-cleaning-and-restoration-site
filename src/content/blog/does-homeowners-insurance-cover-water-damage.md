Most homeowners insurance policies cover water damage when it's sudden and accidental, like a burst pipe or a washing machine hose that fails overnight. They generally do not cover water damage that builds up gradually over time, such as a slow leak you ignored for months, and they almost never cover flooding from rising water outside your home, that requires a separate flood policy. The distinction between "sudden" and "gradual" is the single biggest factor in whether a claim gets paid, so understanding it before you call your agent can save you a denied claim and a lot of frustration.

## What Your Policy Actually Covers

A standard HO-3 homeowners policy, the most common type, typically covers water damage caused by what insurers call a "discharge or overflow" that happens abruptly. That includes a supply line that splits under a sink, a water heater tank that ruptures, a washing machine hose that blows, or a pipe that freezes and cracks inside a wall. The policy usually pays to repair the source of the damage (within limits) and the resulting damage to your home's structure and contents.

What it typically excludes:

- **Gradual leaks.** If a pipe has been seeping slowly for weeks and the adjuster can see mold growth, rotted framing, or mineral staining that indicates a long-term drip, the claim is often denied as a "maintenance issue" rather than a covered loss.
- **Flooding.** Water that enters from outside, whether from a storm, a rising creek, or saturated ground, is excluded from standard homeowners policies and requires flood insurance through the National Flood Insurance Program or a private flood carrier.
- **Sewer backup.** Water or sewage that backs up through a drain or sump pump is usually excluded unless you've added a sewer and drain backup endorsement, which is a separate line item most policies don't include by default.
- **Lack of maintenance.** If an adjuster determines the damage resulted from deferred maintenance, like a known roof leak you never patched, coverage is typically denied.

## The Richardson Situation: Why This Matters Locally

A lot of homes in Richardson were built from the 1960s through the 1980s, which means original copper supply lines and cast iron drain stacks in many houses are well past their expected service life. Slab foundations are standard construction here, so when a water line under the slab starts to fail, the first sign is often a warm spot on the floor or a spike in the water bill rather than visible water. North Texas also gets the occasional hard freeze, and older homes with pipes running through unconditioned attics or exterior walls are the ones most likely to see a pipe split during a cold snap. None of this changes what your policy covers, but it does explain why slab leaks and freeze-related pipe bursts are two of the most common water claims homeowners in this area file.

## Immediate Steps If You Have Water Damage

1. **Stop the water at the source.** Shut off the main water valve if you can't isolate the specific fixture. Every minute water keeps flowing adds to the damage and, in the adjuster's eyes, raises questions about whether you acted reasonably.
2. **Document everything before you touch anything.** Take photos and video of standing water, affected flooring, furniture, and any visible source of the leak. This record matters more for your claim than almost anything else you'll do.
3. **Call your insurance company to open a claim.** Most carriers want to know within a reasonable window, not necessarily the same day, but don't sit on it for a week.
4. **Start mitigating damage yourself if it's safe to do so.** Moving furniture off wet carpet, mopping up standing water, and running a dehumidifier are all reasonable steps. Insurers generally expect you to prevent further damage once you're aware of the problem; this is sometimes called your "duty to mitigate."
5. **Keep receipts for anything you spend.** Shop vac rental, a hotel stay if the home is unlivable, a plumber's invoice to stop the leak, all of it may be reimbursable.

## What NOT to Do

- **Don't wait to see if it dries out on its own.** Drywall, insulation, and subfloor that stay wet for more than 24 to 48 hours start to support mold growth, and a claim that was once a simple water loss can turn into a mold dispute with the carrier.
- **Don't throw away damaged items before documenting them.** Once it's in the trash, it's much harder to prove it existed and what condition it was in.
- **Don't assume verbal approval from an adjuster is the same as a check in hand.** Get the scope of work and the payout amount in writing before you sign a contract for repairs.
- **Don't skip the moisture mapping.** Surface-dry drywall can hide saturated framing behind it. A contractor or restoration company using a moisture meter can find what a visual inspection misses.

## When to Call a Restoration Professional

If the water came from a clean source (a supply line, not a sewer backup) and it's a small, contained spot, like a few square feet of wet flooring from a dishwasher leak, you may be able to dry it out yourself with fans and a dehumidifier within a day or two. Call a professional when any of the following apply: water has spread under flooring or behind walls where you can't see or reach it, the affected area is larger than a small room, water sat for more than a day before you found it, or there's any sign of contamination (the water came from a toilet, a washing machine drain, or outside ground). A restoration company can also document the loss with the kind of photos, moisture readings, and drying logs that insurance adjusters expect to see, which tends to move claims along faster than homeowner-only documentation.

## The Recovery Process

Once a professional is involved, the general sequence looks like this: extract standing water, remove materials that can't be salvaged (wet carpet pad, saturated drywall below the waterline), set up air movers and dehumidifiers to dry the structure, and monitor moisture levels daily until the materials read dry per the IICRC S500 standard, the industry benchmark for water damage restoration. Drying a typical affected room usually takes a few days, not hours, because wood framing and subfloor hold moisture longer than they appear to on the surface. Only after the structure reads dry does reconstruction, replacing drywall, flooring, trim, begin. Throughout this, keep every invoice and photo; most restoration companies will also work directly with your carrier to bill the claim, which can reduce how much you have to front out of pocket.
