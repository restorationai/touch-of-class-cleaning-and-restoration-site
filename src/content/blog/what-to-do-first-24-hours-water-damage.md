If a pipe just burst or a water heater let go, the first 24 hours decide how bad the damage gets. Shut off the water at the source, cut power to any affected outlets if it's safe to reach the breaker, then start removing standing water and moving anything that can soak up moisture. Mold doesn't wait for your insurance claim to process. On drywall, carpet pad, and other porous materials, colonization can start within 24 to 48 hours of sitting wet, so speed matters more than getting everything perfect.

## Understand What You're Dealing With

Not all water damage behaves the same way. A burst supply line under a sink is usually clean water, at least at first. A sewage backup or a washing machine overflow that's been sitting for a day is a different category entirely, and the cleanup approach changes with it. Water that's traveled through walls, under flooring, or around a foundation often carries dirt, bacteria, and sometimes sewage even if it started clean.

Pay attention to where the water came from and how long it had to spread before you noticed. A slow leak behind a dishwasher that's been dripping for a week behaves very differently than a supply line that blew out an hour ago. The longer water sits against drywall, subfloor, or baseboards, the more it wicks upward and outward, often well past the visible wet line.

## Immediate Steps for the First 24 Hours

1. **Stop the source.** Shut off the main water valve or the fixture's local shutoff. If you don't know where your main shutoff is, find it now, before you need it in a hurry.
2. **Cut power if there's any risk of water meeting electrical outlets, panels, or appliances.** If the breaker box itself is wet or hard to reach safely, don't attempt it. Call an electrician.
3. **Document everything before you move anything.** Photos and short videos of the water level, affected walls, furniture, and flooring will matter later for an insurance claim.
4. **Remove standing water.** A wet/dry shop vac handles small amounts. For anything beyond a few inches, you're better off with equipment built for extraction.
5. **Move furniture, rugs, and belongings off wet flooring.** Use foil, wood blocks, or plastic to lift furniture legs off wet carpet so the finish doesn't bleed or the wood doesn't stain the fibers.
6. **Open windows and run fans if the weather allows.** In the humid Gulf Coast climate around Richwood, outdoor air isn't always drier than indoor air, so don't rely on open windows alone, especially during warmer, muggier stretches.
7. **Call your insurance carrier** to start the claim and ask what documentation they require before repairs begin.

## What Not to Do

- Don't run a regular household fan directly on wet drywall or flooring and assume it's drying evenly. Surface air movement dries the top layer fast while moisture stays trapped underneath, which is exactly the setup that invites mold inside wall cavities.
- Don't pull up carpet and discard it before your insurance adjuster has seen it or you've photographed it thoroughly, unless it's a contamination risk.
- Don't use a household vacuum (non-wet/dry) to remove standing water. It will damage the vacuum and may create an electrical hazard.
- Don't assume a dry-looking surface means the material underneath is dry. Drywall, baseboards, and subfloor can look fine on top while staying saturated for days, which is exactly why moisture meters exist.
- Don't close up the space and leave for an extended period, hoping it dries on its own. In humid climates, trapped moisture with no airflow is one of the fastest paths to a mold problem.

## When to Call a Professional

A small, caught-early leak, a few cups of water from a dishwasher hose, for example, is often manageable with towels, a fan, and some patience. Water damage restoration professionals come in once the scope grows past what a shop vac and a box fan can realistically fix:

- Water has been sitting for more than a few hours, or you're not sure how long it's been there
- The water came from a source other than clean supply water (appliance overflow, backup, groundwater intrusion)
- Water has reached carpet padding, subfloor, drywall interiors, or insulation
- You can smell a musty odor, which often means moisture has already reached hidden cavities
- The affected area is large, spans multiple rooms, or involves a finished basement
- You're filing an insurance claim and need documented moisture readings and a drying plan the carrier will accept

Professional water damage restoration uses moisture meters and thermal imaging to find water that's migrated behind walls or under flooring, not just what's visible. Drying is then tracked against the IICRC S500 standard, the industry benchmark for how long structural materials need to reach a safe moisture level before repairs begin. That tracking matters for your insurance claim as much as for the actual drying, since adjusters often ask for daily moisture logs.

## The Recovery Process After the First Day

Once the source is stopped and standing water is gone, the real work is drying the structure, not just the surface. Expect a few phases:

**Extraction and assessment.** Any remaining water gets pulled, and affected materials get checked with moisture meters to map how far the water traveled.

**Structural drying.** Air movers and dehumidifiers run for several days, not hours. Drying times depend on the materials involved, how saturated they got, and ambient humidity, so there's no universal timeline. This is also the phase where decisions get made about what can be dried in place and what needs to come out, like saturated drywall or carpet pad that's held water too long.

**Monitoring.** Materials get re-checked over the following days until moisture readings return to a normal range, not just until they look dry.

**Repairs.** Once everything reads dry, drywall, flooring, trim, or paint get addressed. This is where reconstruction work picks up if materials had to be removed.
