## Restoration emergencies common in Carrollton

Carrollton sits in the North Texas clay belt, where long dry stretches followed by heavy spring and fall rain cause the ground to swell and shrink against foundations. That movement stresses slab plumbing and can open small cracks that let water track under flooring for weeks before anyone notices a problem. Summer brings sustained high heat and humidity, which speeds mold growth once a roof leak or appliance failure goes unaddressed, and the occasional severe thunderstorm or hailstorm that rolls through the Metroplex can mean wind-driven water intrusion and damaged roofing in the same week. Winter freezes are infrequent but sharp when they happen, and homes with exposed or poorly insulated pipework are the ones that end up calling about a burst line.

Much of Carrollton's housing stock dates from the 1970s through the 1990s, with slab-on-grade foundations and a mix of copper and early plastic supply lines, along with cast iron drain stacks in the older sections of the city.
