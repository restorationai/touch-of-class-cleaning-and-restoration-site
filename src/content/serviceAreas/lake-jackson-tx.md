## Restoration emergencies common in Lake Jackson

Lake Jackson sits close to the Gulf, which means heavy, sustained humidity most of the year and a hurricane season that regularly brings tropical storms and the occasional direct hit. Heavy rain events can overwhelm drainage faster than homeowners expect, especially on slab foundations where water finds its way in through expansion joints, exterior doors, and low-lying garage entries. The combination of high humidity and slow-drying building materials also means mold has a narrower window to take hold after any kind of water intrusion, whether that's a roof leak, a failed water heater, or flooding from a nearby bayou or drainage ditch during a named storm. ## Building stock, site conditions, and permits in Lake Jackson

Much of Lake Jackson was built from the 1950s through the 1970s as a planned community, and that era shows up in the housing stock: slab-on-grade foundations are standard, which means water intrusion tends to travel laterally under flooring rather than pooling in a basement or crawlspace. Homes from that period commonly have original cast iron or galvanized drain lines and, in some cases, aluminum wiring, both of which a restoration scope has to account for separately from the moisture damage itself.
