## Restoration emergencies common in Garland

Garland sits in Dallas County on expansive clay soil that swells and contracts with North Texas rainfall swings, which puts steady stress on slab foundations and the plumbing lines running through them. Add in a housing stock that's heavily mid-century, with many homes built from the 1950s through the 1970s, and you get a predictable pattern: aging galvanized or early copper supply lines, cast iron drain stacks, and slab leaks that show up as a warm spot on the floor or a spike in the water bill before anyone sees a drop of water. Summer hailstorms and the occasional spring tornado add roof and window damage to the mix, and the humidity that follows a North Texas storm gives mold a head start on drywall and insulation that stays wet more than a day or two.

## Services we provide in Garland

**Water damage restoration** in Garland often starts below the surface, a slab leak, a failed water heater, or a supply line that gave out inside a wall, long before standing water is visible. **Mold remediation** addresses growth that follows a slow leak, a roof failure, or humidity trapped behind drywall after storm damage. We contain the affected area, remove materials that can't be salvaged, and work to get the space back to a normal condition.

**Storm damage cleanup** responds to wind, hail, and heavy rain events, covering roof and window intrusion, water that entered through compromised flashing, and the drying work that has to happen fast once a structure is exposed.

Most Garland homes are slab-on-grade, built during the city's mid-century growth, with brick veneer or wood-frame exteriors and plumbing that ranges from original galvanized and cast iron to later copper and PEX retrofits. On expansive clay, slab movement is a routine stressor on buried supply and drain lines, and a slow leak under a slab can saturate the subfloor and baseboards for months before it's caught.
