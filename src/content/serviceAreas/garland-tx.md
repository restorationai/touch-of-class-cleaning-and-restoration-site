## Restoration emergencies common in Garland

Garland sits on the clay-heavy soils typical of North Texas, which swell and contract hard between the wet spring months and the baked-dry summers. That movement stresses slab foundations and the plumbing running through and under them, and it's a major reason older homes here develop slab leaks that show up first as a warm spot on the floor or an unexplained jump in the water bill. Summer storms off the Gulf can also stall over Dallas County and drop heavy rain fast, overwhelming roof drainage and yard grading before it ever reaches a storm drain. Add in the ice events that hit North Texas every few winters, and frozen or burst pipes become a seasonal pattern rather than a rare event. Each of these starts as a plumbing or weather problem and becomes a restoration problem the moment water sits against drywall, baseboards, or subfloor long enough for mold to start colonizing.

## Building stock, site conditions, and permits in Garland

Much of Garland's housing stock dates to the postwar boom through the 1970s, brick-veneer ranch homes on slab foundations with a wave of newer construction filling in since. Slab-on-grade means a plumbing leak under the floor doesn't just wet a room, it can travel along the slab and show up well away from its source, which is why tracing the leak matters as much as drying the surface damage. Homes from that era commonly carry galvanized or early copper supply lines, and both are well past the point where corrosion and pinhole leaks become routine. Expansive clay soil across Dallas County puts ongoing pressure on slabs and foundation walls, which is part of why slab leaks recur even after a repair. Structural repair or rebuild work typically requires a permit through the City of Garland's building department, and older homes may also need an asbestos or lead-paint check before demolition begins, depending on age and materials.
