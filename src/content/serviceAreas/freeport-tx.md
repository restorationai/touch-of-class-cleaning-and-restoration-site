## Restoration emergencies common in Freeport

Freeport sits on the Texas Gulf Coast in Brazoria County, low-lying and close to the Brazos River and the Gulf itself, which means standing water and saturated ground are a normal part of life here during hurricane season. Tropical systems bring storm surge risk and heavy rainfall that can overwhelm drainage faster than it can be pumped out, and the area's high water table means crawlspaces and slab edges stay damp long after a storm passes. Add in the year-round humidity typical of the Gulf Coast and you get conditions where mold can take hold within a day or two of any unaddressed leak or flood, whether it is roof damage from wind, a sewage backup after heavy rain, or a slow plumbing leak that went unnoticed under a slab.

## Building stock, site conditions, and permits in Freeport

Much of Freeport's housing stock dates to the mid-20th century, with slab-on-grade construction dominant given the flat coastal terrain, alongside some older pier-and-beam homes near the historic core. Slab foundations here sit on sandy, water-table-saturated soil, which means moisture wicking and foundation movement are ongoing concerns rather than one-time events. Homes built before the 1980s may carry galvanized supply lines or cast iron drains nearing the end of their service life, and older finishes can mean asbestos-containing materials or lead paint that a restoration scope has to account for before demolition work begins. Structural repair or rebuild work in Freeport requires a permit through the city's building department, and given the flood-prone geography, flood-zone rules under FEMA guidance often factor into how repairs are approached, particularly for homes that have flooded before.
