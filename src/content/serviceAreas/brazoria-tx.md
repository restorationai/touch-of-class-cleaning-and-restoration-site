## Restoration emergencies common in Brazoria

Brazoria sits low in the Brazos River floodplain, and that geography shapes the kind of damage calls the city sees. Heavy rain events and storm surge risk during Gulf hurricane season (June through November) push water into homes that may be well above any everyday flood line, and ground saturation can keep crawlspaces and slab perimeters wet for days after the rain stops. High humidity along the coastal plain means any water event that isn't dried quickly has a real head start toward mold colonization, often within 24 to 48 hours on drywall and framing. Older housing stock in and around the city center, mixed with newer construction further out, means we see a wide range of plumbing and roofing vintages, which affects how a leak behaves once it's inside a wall.

## Building stock, site conditions, and permits in Brazoria

Much of Brazoria's housing stock is slab-on-grade, consistent with coastal Texas construction practice, which means water intrusion tends to travel laterally under flooring rather than pooling visibly, and it can wick into drywall and baseboards well past where the water first entered. Homes built before the 1980s may still carry galvanized supply lines or older cast iron drain stacks, both of which are more prone to pinhole leaks and backups as they age, and any pre-1980s construction raises the possibility of asbestos-containing materials or lead paint that a remediation scope has to account for before demolition begins.

The area's clay-heavy soil and high water table common to the Brazos River floodplain put ongoing pressure on foundations and slab edges, which is part of why repeat water intrusion at the same point in a home isn't unusual here. Structural repair or rebuild work tied to a restoration job typically requires a permit through the local building authority, and homeowners should expect that any load-bearing repair or significant drywall and electrical work following a loss will need inspection sign-off before it's closed up.
