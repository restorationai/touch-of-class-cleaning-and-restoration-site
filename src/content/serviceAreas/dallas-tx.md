## Restoration emergencies common in Dallas

North Texas weather does a lot of the damage here. Spring and early summer bring fast-moving thunderstorms and hail that can compromise roofing and push wind-driven rain into attics and wall cavities before anyone notices a leak. Summers run hot and humid, which accelerates mold colonization once materials stay wet, sometimes within 24 to 48 hours of a slow leak going undetected. Winter is shorter but not harmless: hard freezes periodically hit the Dallas area and burst supply lines in homes that weren't built with deep freeze protection in mind, since that's not the norm for this climate. Add in the area's heavy clay soil, which expands and contracts with moisture swings and stresses slab foundations and buried plumbing, and you get a steady mix of storm damage, pipe failures, and slow foundation-related leaks across the city.

Mold remediation requests tend to follow a water event that went unaddressed, whether that's a hidden roof leak or a crawlspace that stayed damp after a plumbing failure. We contain the affected area, remove compromised materials, and address the moisture source so the problem doesn't return.

Biohazard cleanup covers situations property managers and homeowners don't want to handle themselves, trauma scenes, sewage backups, and similar cleanup that requires proper containment and disposal.

## Building stock, site conditions, and permits in Dallas

Dallas has a wide mix of building stock, from older brick homes built in the first half of the 20th century through slab-on-grade ranch construction from the 1950s through 1970s and newer suburban builds. Older homes frequently still carry galvanized supply lines or cast iron drains, both of which corrode from the inside out and fail without warning; homes from the 1980s sometimes have polybutylene piping, a material known for brittle failure at fittings. Slab foundations dominate, which means a plumbing leak under the slab often shows up first as a damp patch of flooring rather than visible water, and foundation repair work typically needs a permit from the city's development services department. The region's expansive clay soil, part of the Blackland Prairie, shifts with moisture and is a frequent contributor to slab cracking and the leaks that follow.
