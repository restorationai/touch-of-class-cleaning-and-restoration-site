## What water damage restoration actually involves

Water damage restoration is not a shop-vac and a box fan. It starts with pulling standing water out fast using truck-mounted or portable extraction units, then moves into a structural drying phase that can run anywhere from two to seven days depending on how much material got wet and how deep the water traveled. Technicians use penetrating and non-penetrating moisture meters to map how far water migrated behind baseboards, under flooring, and inside wall cavities, because what looks dry on the surface is often still saturated in the framing. Commercial-grade air movers and low-grain-refrigerant or desiccant dehumidifiers get set up based on that moisture map, not guesswork, and the equipment stays in place until readings confirm the structure is back to a dry standard, not just until it looks dry.

## Our process

1. **Water extraction.** Remaining standing water is pulled from flooring, carpet, and pad using extraction equipment matched to the volume and category of water involved.
2. **Moisture mapping.** Moisture meters and thermal imaging locate water that has traveled into subfloor, drywall, and framing beyond what's visible.
3. **Structural drying setup.** Air movers and dehumidifiers are positioned based on the moisture map, with containment barriers added for Category 2 (gray water) or Category 3 (black water, sewage-contaminated) losses to keep affected areas isolated.
4. **Daily moisture monitoring.** Readings are logged and equipment is adjusted through the drying period, not left running blind until a final check.
5. **Verification and documentation.** Final moisture readings confirm the structure meets a dry standard before equipment is pulled, and the readings are documented for your insurance file.

## What separates a good water damage response from a bad one

The most common mistake is treating visible wet carpet as the whole job and missing the water that traveled underneath it into subfloor or down into wall cavities. Drywall can read dry on a surface scan while the insulation behind it stays saturated for days, quietly feeding mold colonization. A rushed job pulls equipment early because the room "feels" dry, instead of waiting for moisture meter readings to confirm it. Insurance adjusters look specifically for daily moisture logs, photos of the affected materials before demolition, and a clear category and class designation for the water loss; jobs without that documentation are the ones that run into coverage disputes later. A thorough response also distinguishes between water that can be dried in place and material that has to be removed, like insulation or particleboard subfloor that won't dry fast enough to avoid secondary damage.
