## What renovations, remodels and general contracting actually involves

A kitchen that hasn't been touched since the original build, a bathroom with a subfloor that's starting to flex underfoot, or a section of wall that came out during a water or fire repair and now needs to go back together properly. This is where general contracting work picks up: it's not just cosmetic updates, it's the structural and mechanical work behind the walls, under the floors, and above the ceiling that makes a space safe and functional again.

For Richwood homes, much of this work involves houses built from the 1970s through the 1990s, where original plumbing, electrical, and insulation often don't meet current code. A bathroom remodel might start as a tile and vanity update and turn into a full repipe once the crew opens the wall and finds galvanized pipe or undersized electrical runs. A kitchen remodel frequently means relocating gas or water lines, replacing subfloor damaged by years of slow leaks under the sink, and bringing cabinetry and load points up to current structural standards. Post-damage rebuild work, framing, drywall, flooring, and finish carpentry after a water, fire, or storm loss, follows a different path: it starts from an insurance scope of work rather than a design brief, and the goal is to restore the space to pre-loss condition or better, with documentation at every stage for the adjuster.

## Our process

1. **Scope of work and walkthrough.** We document existing conditions, discuss what's changing (layout, fixtures, finishes) versus what's being restored to match pre-loss condition, and put the scope in writing before any demo starts.
2. **Permitting.** Structural changes, electrical, plumbing, and most post-damage rebuilds in Brazoria County require permits through the appropriate local building department. We pull what's required and schedule inspections around the work, not the other way around.
3. **Demolition and rough-in.** Framing repairs, electrical and plumbing rough-in, and any structural corrections happen here. This is also when hidden damage, old wiring, rot, undersized framing, tends to surface and gets addressed before it's buried behind new finishes.
4. **Inspections and finishing.** Rough-in inspections, then drywall, flooring, cabinetry, tile, paint, and trim. Fixtures and appliances go in last.
5. **Final walkthrough.** We go through the finished space against the original scope line by line before calling the job complete.

## What separates a good renovation response from a bad one

The most common problem on remodel and rebuild jobs isn't the visible finish work, it's what gets skipped before the walls close up. A contractor in a hurry will drywall over a rough-in that hasn't passed inspection, or match new framing to damaged lumber instead of replacing it because it's faster. On post-damage rebuilds specifically, adjusters look for whether the rebuild scope actually matches the documented damage, whether moisture levels were confirmed dry before framing closed in, and whether materials and labor are itemized clearly enough to reconcile against the claim. A rebuild that skips that documentation often stalls the claim or gets flagged for a second inspection.

On renovation work, the recurring issue is scope creep that isn't priced or written down. A bathroom remodel that uncovers a leaking shower pan, or a kitchen remodel that finds knob-and-tube wiring behind a wall, needs a change order before work continues, not an invoice surprise at the end. A contractor who treats the original scope as fixed regardless of what's found behind the wall is cutting corners somewhere to stay on budget.

## What does renovations, remodels and general contracting cost?

Costs vary widely depending on scope, materials, permitting requirements, and whether the project is a straightforward update or a rebuild tied to a specific loss. These are typical industry ranges, not a quote: every project gets its own written scope of work from Touch of Class Cleaning and Restoration before work begins, so you know the cost before anything is demolished. For post-damage rebuild work, homeowners insurance typically covers the cost to restore the space to its pre-loss condition under the dwelling portion of the policy; upgrades beyond that are usually an out-of-pocket or negotiated add-on.

## Seasonal & regional considerations

Richwood's humidity and clay-heavy soil put real demands on renovation work. Framing and subfloor replacements need time to acclimate before finish flooring goes down, especially in summer months when ambient moisture is high. Houses on pier-and-beam or slab foundations common to the area can shift seasonally, which is one reason bathroom and kitchen remodels sometimes reveal cracked tile or uneven subfloor that wasn't visible before demo. For post-damage rebuilds tied to hurricane season flooding or wind damage, scheduling can tighten up in the months following a major storm event as permitting offices and material suppliers across the Gulf Coast see higher demand.
