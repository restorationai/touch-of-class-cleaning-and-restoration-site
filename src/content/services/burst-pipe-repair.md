**Water coming through a ceiling or pooling around a slab crack usually means a supply line has already let go somewhere out of sight.** Call now for emergency service if you've got active water: shut off the main if you can reach it safely, then get a crew on the way before the leak migrates into insulation, subflooring, and the underside of cabinetry.

A burst pipe is different from a slow leak. The water volume is sudden and the damage window is measured in minutes, not days. Copper pipe failures from a hard freeze, a corroded fitting letting go behind a wall, or a slab leak pushing water up through a floor, the cleanup math is the same: find where the water traveled, pull it out fast, and get the structure dry before the next problem (swelling wood, lifting flooring, mold colonization) sets in.

## What burst pipe cleanup and repair actually involves

This is a two-part job: stopping and addressing the water source, and reversing what the water already did to the structure. The water itself usually falls into clean-water category at the moment of the break, but if it sat for more than a day or picked up contaminants from wall cavities or subfloor insulation, it gets treated with more caution. Crews work with wet/dry extraction units, moisture meters, and often thermal imaging to trace water that has already run behind baseboards, under cabinet toe-kicks, or down into a slab penetration. Drywall and insulation that absorbed water get cut back to a clean line rather than dried in place, because saturated gypsum rarely dries fast enough to avoid mold risk. On slab leaks, that may mean coordinating with a plumber to access the pipe through the concrete, which is a separate scope from the drying work itself.

## Our process

1. **Locate and confirm the source.** Before any drying equipment goes down, the water path gets traced back to the break, whether that's a visible pipe failure, a slab leak signature, or water tracking from an upper floor.
2. **Extract standing water.** Pumps and extraction wands remove bulk water from flooring, carpet pad, and any low points before it has more time to wick into materials.
3. **Open up and remove saturated materials.** Wet drywall, baseboard, and insulation that won't dry in place get cut out to the waterline so the drying equipment isn't fighting a losing battle.
4. **Dry and monitor.** Air movers and dehumidifiers run against daily moisture readings on framing and subfloor, following IICRC S500 drying guidelines, until materials read dry, not just dry to the touch.
5. **Document for repair and rebuild.** Photos, moisture logs, and a written scope hand off cleanly to whoever handles the pipe repair and any drywall or flooring replacement.

## What separates a good burst pipe response from a bad one

The most common mistake is drying the visible surface and stopping there. Water that ran behind a baseboard or under a cabinet kick plate can sit hidden for weeks if nobody pulls the trim or checks behind the toe-kick with a moisture meter. Adjusters specifically look for documented moisture readings over several days, not a single photo, because that's what proves the structure was actually dry before it got closed back up. Another frequent miss: treating a slab leak like a surface puddle instead of recognizing that water pushing up through a crack has likely already traveled under adjacent flooring. A pipe break near a ceiling also deserves a hard look upward, insulation above a soaked drywall ceiling holds water long after the ceiling itself looks dry.

## What does burst pipe cleanup and repair cost?

Costs vary with how much water escaped before the source was found and how far it traveled into flooring, drywall, and cabinetry. Every loss is different, so Touch of Class Cleaning and Restoration provides a written scope before any work begins rather than a phone estimate. Homeowners insurance typically covers sudden and accidental water damage from a burst pipe, though coverage for the plumbing repair itself and for gradual or neglect-related leaks can differ by policy.

## Seasonal & regional considerations

North Texas doesn't stay cold long, but the hard freezes that do roll through Richardson catch a lot of homes with exposed or poorly insulated pipe runs in attics, garages, and exterior walls. A pipe that freezes and cracks often doesn't show the leak until the thaw, when pressure returns and water starts moving, so a cold snap followed by a sudden warm-up is a common trigger window. Slab leaks are also common in this area given the number of homes built on slab foundations with copper supply lines now decades old, and shifting clay soil can stress those lines over time.
