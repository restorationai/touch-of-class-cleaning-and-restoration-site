A cracked copper line behind a washing machine, a split PEX fitting in a slab home, a water heater supply that lets go overnight: these failures don't announce themselves slowly. One minute the floor is dry, the next the baseboards are swelling and water is tracking under the cabinets toward the next room. Burst pipe cleanup and repair is about stopping that spread fast, pulling the water out of materials before it migrates further, and coordinating the actual pipe repair so the same failure doesn't happen again in six months.

## What Burst Pipe Cleanup and Repair Actually Involves

This isn't a mop-and-fan job. A real pipe break response starts with locating and confirming the water is fully shut off at the main or the affected branch, because a "fixed" leak that's still weeping behind drywall will keep feeding mold for weeks. From there it's extraction with truck-mount or portable pumps on standing water, moisture mapping with a meter to find where water traveled under flooring and inside wall cavities, and then placement of air movers and dehumidifiers sized to the square footage and the materials involved. Baseboards and lower drywall often need to come off to dry the stud cavity and insulation behind them. Cabinets that sat in standing water get checked for swelling at the toe kick and base, which is usually the first sign the particleboard has absorbed water it won't give back up.

The pipe repair itself, whether that's a plumber re-piping a section of copper or replacing a cracked PEX fitting, is typically handled separately or coordinated alongside the water mitigation, since cleanup and plumbing repair are different trades with different scopes.

## Our Process

1. **Shut-off confirmation and source assessment** – verify the water is fully stopped and identify whether this is a clean supply-line break (Category 1) or a failure that's picked up contamination from flooring or wall materials.
2. **Extraction** – remove standing water from flooring, carpet pad, and any pooled water trapped under vinyl or laminate before it wicks further.
3. **Moisture mapping** – meter readings across walls, subfloor, and baseboards to mark the actual boundary of the wet zone, which is almost always bigger than what's visible.
4. **Controlled drying** – air movers and dehumidifiers set per the IICRC S500 drying standard, with daily moisture checks until materials read dry, not just damp to the touch.
5. **Repair coordination and reassembly** – once the pipe itself is repaired and materials are confirmed dry, baseboards, drywall, and flooring go back in.

## What Separates a Good Burst Pipe Response from a Bad One

The most common mistake is treating the surface as the whole problem. A floor can feel dry underfoot while the subfloor and the bottom plate of a wall are still saturated, and if drying equipment comes out too early, that moisture turns into a mold problem inside the wall within a few days. Another frequent miss is skipping cabinets and baseboards entirely because removing them is extra labor, which leaves a hidden wet cavity behind finished surfaces.

Insurance adjusters reviewing a pipe break claim generally want to see documented moisture readings over time (not just a single reading), photos of the affected cavity before it's closed back up, and a clear record of when drying equipment went in and came out. A scope that just says "dried affected areas" without supporting data tends to get questioned.

## Seasonal & Regional Considerations

Richwood sits in a Gulf Coast climate where hard freezes are rare but not unheard of, and that's exactly the kind of cold snap that catches homes off guard. Pipes here aren't typically insulated or routed the way they would be further north, so a sudden overnight freeze can split an exposed line in a garage, attic, or crawlspace in a part of the county that doesn't normally plan for it. Older PEX and copper fittings in homes built before more recent plumbing codes also show up more often in pipe break calls, simply from age and fitting fatigue.
