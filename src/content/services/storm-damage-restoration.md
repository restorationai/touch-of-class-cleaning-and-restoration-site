## What storm damage restoration actually involves

Storm damage restoration covers the work that happens after wind, hail, or heavy rain breaches a structure: temporary protection to stop ongoing water entry, extraction of any water that's already inside, and drying of the materials that got wet before mold or rot sets in. It's different from a routine water damage call because the source is still active or just stopped, and because there's often exterior damage (roofing, siding, fencing, downed trees) layered on top of interior moisture. A typical response involves tarping or board-up the same day, then extraction and structural drying equipment staged over the following days depending on how much material got saturated. Hurricane and tropical storm events in this part of Texas tend to bring both wind damage and standing water at once, which means the restoration scope often covers both roof-level intrusion and ground-level flooding in the same visit.

## Our process

1. **Emergency tarping and board-up.** Open roof sections, broken windows, or damaged exterior walls get covered first to stop additional water and debris from entering while the rest of the loss is assessed.
2. **Water extraction from intrusion points.** Standing water pooled on flooring, in ceiling cavities, or inside wall assemblies gets pulled out with extraction equipment before it migrates further into subfloor or insulation.
3. **Moisture mapping.** Moisture meters and, where needed, thermal imaging identify how far water traveled behind walls and under flooring, since storm intrusion rarely stays confined to the room where it entered.
4. **Structural drying and monitoring.** Air movers and dehumidifiers are positioned based on the moisture map, and readings are tracked over the following days to confirm materials are drying rather than trapping moisture behind a dry-looking surface.
5. **Debris and damaged material removal.** Saturated insulation, ruined drywall, and storm debris that can't be dried are removed and documented before reconstruction scoping begins.

## What separates a good storm damage response from a bad one

The biggest mistake in storm damage cleanup is treating it like a simple water loss and skipping the moisture mapping step. Wind-driven rain often enters at a roofline or window and travels laterally before it shows up as a stain on a ceiling, so drying only the visible wet spot leaves saturated material hidden behind drywall or under flooring. A second common gap is tarping that isn't secured well enough to survive the next band of wind or rain, which turns a one-time intrusion into a repeated one. Adjusters reviewing storm claims generally want to see moisture readings taken at the time of loss, photos of the intrusion point before it's covered, and a clear timeline between when the storm hit and when mitigation started, since that timeline affects what's considered sudden damage versus pre-existing deterioration.

## What does storm damage restoration cost?

Costs vary widely because storm losses combine different problems, wind damage, water intrusion, debris, tree impact, in different proportions on every job. Every loss gets a written scope before work begins so there are no surprises once drying equipment and materials are on site.
