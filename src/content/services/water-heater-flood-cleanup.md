**Water heater leaking or already burst?** Call now for emergency service before the water finds its way into your subfloor, baseboards, and the room below. A failed water heater rarely stays contained to the closet or garage it sits in. Tanks hold 40 to 80 gallons, and when a seam splits or a fitting lets go, that volume moves fast across flooring and under walls before most homeowners even notice the sound of running water that shouldn't be there.

## What water heater flood cleanup actually involves

A flooded water heater is a different animal from a slow plumbing drip. You're usually dealing with a sudden volume of water, sometimes mixed with rust and mineral sediment from inside an aging tank, spreading across a finished floor and soaking into whatever is nearby: drywall, baseboards, cabinetry, and subfloor. The cleanup starts with stopping the source, then moves into extraction, moisture mapping, and structural drying. Depending on the tank's age and condition, the water itself may carry sediment and corrosion byproducts that push the loss into a higher water category, which changes how materials get treated and what can be dried in place versus what needs to come out.

## Our process

1. **Shut off and secure.** We confirm the water supply and, where applicable, the gas or electric connection to the tank are off before anything else starts. This protects both the property and anyone working in the space.
2. **Standing water extraction.** Pumps and wet vacuums pull free water off flooring and out of low points before it has more time to wick into subfloor and baseboards.
3. **Moisture mapping.** Meters and sometimes thermal imaging trace where water traveled beyond the visible wet area, including under vinyl, behind baseboards, and into adjoining rooms or the space below if the heater was on an upper floor.
4. **Structural drying.** Air movers and dehumidifiers go in to dry framing, subfloor, and wall cavities to a dry standard, not just a dry-looking surface. Readings get logged and rechecked over several days.
5. **Contaminated material handling.** If the water carried sediment or tank corrosion, affected porous materials like baseboard, insulation, or carpet pad may need removal rather than drying, since they can't be reliably sanitized in place.

## What separates a good water heater flood response from a bad one

The most common mistake is treating a water heater flood like a simple mop-up job. Surface water gets wiped up, a fan runs for a day, and everyone assumes it's handled. What gets missed is the water that traveled under the vinyl plank or laminate, into the wall cavity behind the baseboard, or down through a floor joint into the unit below. That hidden moisture is exactly what turns into a mold problem two or three weeks later.

Adjusters reviewing a water heater claim want documented moisture readings, not just a description of the damage. They're also looking at whether the water source was addressed quickly enough to limit secondary damage, and whether contaminated materials were handled appropriately rather than just dried and left in place. Corrosion and sediment from an older tank can mean the water doesn't qualify as clean, which affects what can be saved and what has to be replaced. A careful operator tests and documents before deciding, rather than guessing.

## What does water heater flood cleanup cost?

Costs depend heavily on how far the water traveled before it was caught, whether it reached a floor below, and whether the tank's condition pushed the water into a contaminated category. Every loss is different, and Touch of Class Cleaning and Restoration provides a written scope before any work begins so you know what's included. Most homeowners insurance policies cover sudden water heater failures as a covered peril, though gradual leaks over time are often excluded, so documentation of the sudden event matters.

## Seasonal & regional considerations

North Texas winter freezes put extra strain on water heater fittings and supply lines, especially in garages and exterior utility closets that aren't well insulated, and a cracked fitting after a hard freeze often isn't discovered until the thaw. Richardson's housing stock spans slab-foundation homes from the 1960s and 70s alongside newer two-story construction, and tank placement varies a lot between them: ground-floor utility rooms in older homes versus attic or upstairs closets in newer builds, which changes how far water can travel before anyone notices.
