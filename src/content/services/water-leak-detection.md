A water bill that creeps up for no visible reason, a warm spot on the slab, a musty smell behind a baseboard that won't go away no matter how much you clean: these are the quiet signs of a leak that hasn't announced itself yet. By the time water actually shows on a ceiling or pools on a floor, it's often been moving through framing, slab, or wall cavities for weeks. Water leak detection finds the source before it becomes a structural or mold problem, without tearing out walls to guess.

## What water leak detection actually involves

Leak detection is a diagnostic service, not a repair. The goal is to pinpoint exactly where water is entering or escaping a system, whether that's a supply line under a slab, a drain pipe inside a wall, a roof penetration, or a fitting behind a shower surround, without unnecessary demolition. Technicians use moisture meters to map how far water has traveled through drywall, subfloor, and framing, thermal imaging cameras to spot temperature differences that often correlate with wet building materials, and acoustic listening devices that pick up the sound of pressurized water moving through pipe under concrete or flooring. For suspected slab leaks, a line pressure test can confirm whether the plumbing system is holding pressure at all before any probing starts. The process usually takes one to three hours depending on the size of the home and how many fixtures or zones need to be isolated.

## Our process

1. **Intake and symptom mapping.** We ask what you've noticed: sound of running water with no fixture on, a spike in the water bill, a damp spot that returns after cleaning, or a specific stain pattern. This narrows which systems to test first.
2. **Non-invasive scanning.** Thermal imaging and moisture meters sweep the suspected area to identify where materials read wetter than the surrounding assembly, without opening anything up yet.
3. **Acoustic and pressure testing.** For slab leaks or leaks inside wall cavities, acoustic equipment listens for the leak's signature sound, and pressure testing isolates whether the hot side, cold side, or drain line is the source.
4. **Point confirmation.** Once the likely leak location is narrowed to a small area, a minimal access point (a small drywall cut, a single tile, a small core in a slab) confirms the exact spot before any repair plan is made.
5. **Written findings.** You get a clear explanation of where the leak is, how it likely got there, and what moisture has already reached, so you can make an informed decision about repair and any drying work needed.

## What separates a good leak detection response from a bad one

The most common mistake is guessing based on where water shows up on the surface instead of where it actually originated. Water travels along the path of least resistance, which means a stain on a first-floor ceiling can trace back to a leak several feet away near an upstairs bathroom, or a wet spot on a slab can be fed by a pipe run that curves well outside the visible damp area. Operators who skip thermal imaging or acoustic testing and go straight to cutting drywall often open up the wrong wall, which adds cost and delay without finding the source.

A careful leak detection visit also documents moisture readings at the time of inspection, not just a verbal description. That record matters if the claim later involves an insurance adjuster, because it shows the extent of the moisture intrusion before any drying or repair work began. Adjusters generally want to see how the leak was located, what readings confirmed it, and whether the intrusion was sudden and accidental versus a long-term, gradual condition, since that distinction affects coverage.

## What does water leak detection cost?

Costs depend on how accessible the suspected leak is and how much equipment is needed to confirm it. A single-fixture leak with an obvious access point costs far less to diagnose than a slab leak under a concrete foundation that requires acoustic and pressure testing across multiple zones. These are typical industry ranges, and every property is different: Touch of Class Cleaning and Restoration provides a written scope before any diagnostic or repair work begins so there are no surprises.

| Scenario | Typical range |

Homeowners insurance typically covers the cost of locating a leak when it's part of a covered water damage claim, though policies often draw a line between sudden, accidental leaks and long-term seepage, so coverage depends on how the leak originated and how long it had been active.
