A water bill that jumped without explanation, a warm spot on the slab, a faint hiss behind a wall when the house is quiet: these are the symptoms homeowners in Richardson usually notice weeks before any visible water damage shows up. By the time a stain appears on drywall or baseboards start to cup, the leak has often been running for a while. Water leak detection finds the source before it turns into a bigger repair, using listening equipment and moisture readings instead of guesswork and open-ended demolition.

## What water leak detection actually involves

Leak detection is a diagnostic process, not a repair. A technician combines moisture meters, infrared thermal imaging, and acoustic listening equipment to trace where water is actually moving, whether that's a supply line under a slab, a drain line inside a wall cavity, or a fitting behind a tub surround. Slab leak detection typically adds pressure testing on isolated plumbing zones to narrow down which line is losing water, since Richardson's post-tension and conventional slab foundations don't give visual access the way a crawlspace does. Most inspections run one to three hours depending on the size of the home and how many fixtures or zones need to be isolated. The goal at the end is a specific, marked location, not a general area, so that any digging, cutting, or slab penetration is as limited as possible.

## Our process

1. **Symptom intake.** We start with what the homeowner has already noticed: a spike in the water bill, a damp patch on carpet or tile, a musty smell in one room, or a sound of running water with every fixture off.
2. **Moisture and thermal survey.** A moisture meter and infrared camera scan walls, floors, and ceilings near the suspected area to identify temperature differences and elevated readings that point toward the source.
3. **Acoustic and electronic tracing.** Ground microphones and electronic pipe tracers listen for the distinct sound signature of pressurized water escaping a line, narrowing the search from a general room to a specific section of pipe.
4. **Isolation testing for slab leaks.** When the leak is suspected under the foundation, we isolate plumbing zones and pressure test each one to confirm which line is losing water before anyone touches the slab.
5. **Documentation and findings report.** The homeowner gets a written summary of the detected location, photos or thermal images where applicable, and a clear next step, whether that's a plumber for repair or a remediation scope if water has already reached framing or subfloor.

## What separates a good leak detection response from a bad one

The most common mistake is treating leak detection as an excuse to cut first and listen later. A rushed job opens multiple sections of drywall or slab based on where water is pooling, which isn't always where the leak originates, since water travels along pipe runs, vapor barriers, and framing before it surfaces. A more careful approach cross-references acoustic data with thermal imaging and moisture mapping before any cutting happens, so the access point matches the actual source. Insurance adjusters reviewing a claim also want to see that the leak was pinpointed, not estimated, and that moisture readings were documented before repair began, since that record supports the difference between a sudden plumbing failure and long-term, gradual damage that some policies treat differently. Missing a secondary leak path, like water that has tracked down a wall cavity and saturated insulation several feet from where it entered, is another gap that shows up later as a mold or odor complaint.

## What does water leak detection cost?

Costs vary with how accessible the suspected leak is and whether it's a single fixture, a slab leak, or a whole-home search with no obvious starting point. Every property and plumbing layout is different, which is why Touch of Class Cleaning and Restoration provides a written scope of findings before any repair or access work begins. Homeowners insurance typically covers water damage that results from a sudden leak, but the detection service itself is sometimes a separate line item unless it's bundled into an active water damage claim, so it's worth checking policy language directly.

## Seasonal & regional considerations

Richardson sits on expansive clay soil that shifts with moisture content, and that movement puts ongoing stress on slab plumbing, which is a common contributor to slab leaks in North Texas homes built on post-tension or conventional foundations. Summer heat dries and contracts the clay, winter cold snaps occasionally freeze exposed or poorly insulated lines, and both extremes can stress joints that were fine the rest of the year. Homes built from the 1970s through the 1990s, common throughout Richardson's older neighborhoods, often have original copper or galvanized supply lines that are more prone to pinhole leaks as they age.
