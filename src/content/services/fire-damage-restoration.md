## What fire damage restoration actually involves

Fire and smoke restoration is a different discipline from general cleaning because the damage isn't just visible char. A grease fire in the kitchen leaves a thick, greasy protein soot that's nearly invisible but smells overwhelming. A synthetic fire involving furniture, electronics, or plastics leaves a dry, powdery soot that spreads farther through the HVAC system and settles on surfaces rooms away from the actual fire. Each type reacts differently to cleaning agents, which is why soot characterization happens before any wiping or scrubbing starts.

The work covers structural materials (framing, subfloor, drywall), contents (furniture, textiles, electronics), and air quality. Smoke odor isn't surface-level: it penetrates porous materials like drywall, insulation, and upholstery, which is why odor control requires more than an air freshener or a few open windows. Depending on the severity, structural fire damage repair may also mean removing and replacing charred framing, scorched subfloor, or sections of roof decking before any cosmetic work begins.

## Our process

1. **Soot and damage assessment.** We identify the soot type (protein, synthetic, or a mix), map which rooms were affected by direct flame versus smoke travel, and photograph everything for the insurance file.
2. **Water removal, if present.** Most structure fires involve firefighting water. Standing water and saturated materials get extracted and dried before soot cleanup starts, so we're not grinding wet residue deeper into surfaces.
3. **Soot and residue cleaning.** Affected surfaces are cleaned using methods matched to the soot type, dry sponging for some residues, wet cleaning or specialized solvents for others. HVAC ducts and registers get cleaned or sealed off to stop recontamination.
4. **Odor neutralization.** Thermal fogging or ozone treatment breaks down smoke odor molecules trapped in porous materials rather than masking the smell. This step is matched to the severity and type of smoke, not applied as a blanket treatment.
5. **Contents pack-out and structural repair prep.** Salvageable belongings are inventoried, packed out, and cleaned off-site if needed. Any structural fire damage, charred framing, scorched drywall, compromised flooring, gets scoped for repair or rebuild.

## What separates a good fire damage response from a bad one

The most common mistake is treating soot like regular dirt and scrubbing it with the wrong product. Rubbing dry soot with a wet rag can grind it deeper into drywall and upholstery fibers, turning a cleanable surface into a replacement job. Protein soot in particular needs a different approach than synthetic soot, and using one method on the wrong type often just smears residue instead of lifting it.

Another miss is skipping HVAC inspection. If the system ran during or after the fire, soot and odor get pulled through the ductwork and redistributed into rooms that look untouched. A thorough post-fire restoration checks the system before calling a job complete.

Adjusters typically look for documented pre-cleaning photos, a clear breakdown of what's salvageable versus what needs replacing, and whether water damage from firefighting efforts was addressed separately from smoke damage. A written scope that separates these categories moves claims along faster and avoids disputes over what was actually damaged by fire versus what was already in poor condition.
