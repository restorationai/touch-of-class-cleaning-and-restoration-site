**Flood damage in Richwood?** Call now for emergency service. Floodwater doesn't behave like a burst pipe or a leaking roof. It carries silt, runoff, and whatever the water crossed before it reached your property, and it often rises through foundations and slabs rather than dripping from above. That difference changes everything about how the loss gets handled, from what can be saved to how the claim gets documented.

## What flood damage restoration actually involves

Flood damage restoration addresses water that has entered a structure from an external source: storm surge, overflowing drainage, a swollen bayou or creek, or sustained heavy rainfall that overwhelms the ground around a foundation. Because this water has traveled outside the building envelope, it's treated as contaminated (Category 3) from the start, which means more materials come out and fewer get dried in place. Crews pump standing water, remove saturated carpet pad and drywall that has wicked water above the baseboard line, clear silt and debris from crawlspaces and slabs, and set up commercial dehumidifiers and air movers to bring the structure back to a normal moisture range. Depending on how much water entered and how long it sat, the drying phase alone can run several days, with moisture readings taken daily until wood framing, subfloor, and wall cavities test dry.

## Our process

1. **Safety check and water categorization.** Before anything is touched, we confirm it's safe to enter, cut power to affected circuits if needed, and classify the water as contaminated given its external source.
2. **Extraction and demolition of unsalvageable material.** Standing water comes out first with pumps and truck-mount extractors, followed by removal of flood-contact drywall, insulation, carpet, and pad that can't be sanitized.
3. **Sediment and debris removal.** Floodwater leaves behind silt, organic debris, and residue in low points like crawlspaces and slab perimeters; this gets cleared and the area is treated with an antimicrobial to slow bacterial and mold growth.
4. **Structural drying with monitoring.** Dehumidifiers and air movers run until moisture meters and hygrometer readings confirm the studs, subfloor, and remaining materials are back to a stable, dry range, not just dry to the touch.
5. **Documentation for your claim.** Photos, moisture logs, and a written scope of the work performed are compiled so you have a clear record for your flood insurance carrier or adjuster.

## What separates a good flood damage response from a bad one

The most common mistake is treating floodwater like a clean-water leak: drying carpet and pad in place instead of removing them, or leaving drywall up because it "looks fine" from the face. Flood contamination lives in the material itself, not just on the surface, so anything porous that touched the water generally needs to come out, not just dry out. Another gap is skipping the crawlspace or slab perimeter where silt settles and keeps feeding bacterial growth long after the visible water is gone. Adjusters reviewing a flood claim typically look for documented moisture readings over time (not a single snapshot), photos of what was removed and why, and a clear line between pre-existing conditions and flood-caused damage, so thorough documentation matters as much as the physical work.

## What does flood damage restoration cost?

Costs vary widely based on how much water entered, how long it sat, and how much of the structure and flooring was affected. These are typical industry ranges, not a quote. Every loss is different, and Touch of Class Cleaning and Restoration provides a written scope of work before any restoration begins. Standard homeowners insurance generally does not cover damage from external flooding, rising groundwater, or storm surge; that type of loss is typically covered only under a separate flood insurance policy, so it's worth confirming your coverage before assuming what's included.

## Seasonal & regional considerations

Richwood sits in the Gulf Coastal Plain, where flat terrain and heavy clay soil mean rainwater doesn't drain quickly even outside of named storms. Tropical storm and hurricane season brings the highest risk of rapid, significant flooding, but slow-moving heavy rain events earlier or later in the year can produce the same kind of standing water around foundations and crawlspaces. Because the clay soil stays saturated for days after a heavy rain, homes near drainage ditches or low-lying lots are more prone to repeat flood intrusion through weep holes and slab penetrations than homes on higher ground.
