**Raw sewage backup in Richardson? Call now for emergency service.** Sewage cleanup is not a job to delay or tackle with a shop vac and bleach. Sewer line backups and septic overflows carry bacteria, viruses, and parasites that spread through a home fast, soaking into subfloors, baseboards, and drywall within hours. Whether the source is a clogged main line, a failed septic system, or a toilet that backed up during a storm, the longer contaminated water sits, the more material has to come out.

## What sewage cleanup and sanitization actually involves

Sewage intrusion is classified as Category 3 water, the most contaminated tier under IICRC S500 guidelines, meaning it's treated as grossly unsanitary regardless of the source. That classification changes the whole job. Porous materials that touch raw sewage, carpet, carpet pad, drywall up to the flood line, insulation, often can't be saved and have to be removed rather than dried in place. The work involves extracting standing liquid, removing and bagging contaminated porous materials for disposal, cleaning and disinfecting hard surfaces with hospital-grade antimicrobials, and running air movers and dehumidifiers to bring the structure back to a normal moisture range. A straightforward bathroom backup might wrap up in a day or two; a whole-house sewer line failure that's been sitting for a day or more usually takes longer because more material is compromised.

## Our process

1. **PPE staging and containment.** Technicians suit up in protective gear before entering, and the affected area gets sealed off with poly sheeting to keep contamination from tracking into clean parts of the house.
2. **Sewage extraction and contaminated material removal.** Standing waste is pumped or vacuumed out first, then soaked carpet, pad, and any drywall or insulation that contacted the water is cut out and bagged for disposal.
3. **Cleaning and enzymatic or antimicrobial treatment.** Remaining hard surfaces, subfloor, framing, and fixtures are scrubbed and treated with hospital-grade disinfectant to break down bacterial load and organic residue.
4. **Deodorization and drying.** Air scrubbers and controlled airflow address the odor that sewage leaves behind in porous surfaces, while dehumidifiers bring the structure's moisture content back down.
5. **Clearance check.** Before the area is cleared for rebuild, surfaces are checked visually and with moisture meters to confirm the space is dry and sanitized, not just dry.

## What separates a good sewage cleanup response from a bad one

The most common shortcut is drying sewage-contaminated materials instead of removing them. Carpet pad, drywall, and insulation are porous, and once Category 3 water soaks in, cleaning the surface doesn't remove what's absorbed into the material itself. A crew that pulls out a dehumidifier and calls it done on a sewage loss is skipping the part of the job that actually matters.

Adjusters reviewing a sewage claim typically look for documentation that contaminated materials were removed and disposed of correctly, not just dried, along with before-and-after moisture readings and photos of the affected categories of water. Jobs that get flagged or underpaid are usually missing that paper trail. A thorough response also traces the source, whether the backup originated from a municipal sewer line, a private lateral, or a failed septic system, because that affects both the cleanup scope and how the claim gets handled.
