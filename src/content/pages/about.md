Touch of Class Cleaning and Restoration opened its doors in 2025 with a simple focus: when water, fire, or storm damage hits a home or business, the owner needs someone who shows up, explains what's actually happening to the structure, and gets the restoration process moving without delay. Restoration isn't a side service we added on. It's the whole business, which means our scheduling, our equipment, and our training all point toward one thing: getting a damaged property stable and dry or clean again as fast as the situation allows.

## What we do

Most calls come in on what is, for the homeowner, one of the worst days of the year. A supply line let go overnight. A grease fire scorched a kitchen. A storm pushed water under the slab. Our job starts with listening to what happened and where, then getting a technician out to assess moisture levels, smoke penetration, or contamination before it spreads further into drywall, subfloor, or framing. A lot of the work is unglamorous: pulling baseboards to check for wicking, running air movers and dehumidifiers for days at a time, documenting every affected material with photos for the insurance file. We also talk directly with adjusters when asked to, since most property owners are dealing with a claim for the first time and don't want to manage that process alone on top of everything else.

## Our certifications and licensure

Restoration work follows established standards for a reason. The IICRC, for example, sets the drying and cleaning protocols the industry uses to judge whether a structure has actually dried to a safe standard, not just to the point where it looks dry on the surface. EPA Lead-Safe rules exist because homes built before 1978 can contain lead paint that becomes hazardous once it's disturbed during repair work. We structure our process around those kinds of industry benchmarks, because a job that's rushed or undocumented tends to come back as a mold claim or a liability issue months later. We're a new company, and we'll update this page with specific credentials as they're added; in the meantime, ask us directly about licensing or insurance documentation for your project and we'll provide what's current.

## Where we work

We're based in Richwood, in Brazoria County, and most of our calls come from the surrounding Gulf Coast communities: Lake Jackson, Clute, Freeport, Angleton, and the stretch of Highway 288 running toward Houston's southern suburbs. It's a region where humidity sits high most of the year, hurricane season brings real flood risk from the Brazos River and heavy rainfall, and a lot of the housing stock sits on slab foundations over expansive clay soils that shift with moisture swings. That combination means water intrusion here behaves a little differently than it does further inland, and it's part of why local response matters as much as the equipment we bring.
