# Before/After Pair Standards — the knowledge base

Read this BEFORE generating any before/after slider pair. Born 2026-09-26 from
Santino's review of the Frontline + DryCor first batch. These rules are on top
of the base imagery rules (documentary phone-photo realism, one coherent scene,
no people, no branding/trucks, no readable text, no floating objects).

## The core law: restoration REPLACES, it does not rewind

The single biggest tell that a pair is fake: the after is the before with the
damage erased. Real restoration replaces materials. The after must show
plausible REPLACEMENT, not magical cleaning:

1. **Porous/absorbent items that took damage are REPLACED, never identical.**
   Water-soaked wood furniture (coffee tables, chairs), soiled mattresses and
   bedding, smoke-saturated fabric couches — in the after these are either
   gone, visibly NEW (different but similar-style piece), or dressed fresh
   (new sheets/covers). A soiled bed must never carry the same wrinkles into
   the after. A water-line couch may stay only if cleaning is plausible;
   default is replace.
2. **Burnt built-ins can NEVER return with the same grain/pattern.** Charred
   wood cabinets replaced with the "same" cabinets and identical wood grain is
   physically impossible. Fire-damage afters show a NEW kitchen/built-in —
   different door style or finish. Prefer a modest visible UPGRADE (the
   insurance-rebuild reality: homeowners often come out slightly ahead).
3. **Small set-dressing must not survive the disaster.** The same coffee cup
   sitting in the same spot before and after breaks the story instantly.
   Clear loose objects from the after or replace them differently.
4. **Structural finishes change believably.** Water: new flooring planks/tile
   (slightly different tone is GOOD), fresh baseboards, repainted walls.
   Mold: the before should show invaded drywall — bubbling paint, cut-out
   sections, staining spread — so the after (new drywall patch, uniform fresh
   paint) reads as remediation, not washing.

## What MUST carry over (the slider only works if the room is the same room)

- Camera angle, room geometry, window position, ceiling/wall layout.
- Weather and light direction OUT THE WINDOW — see next rule.

## Environment continuity rules

5. **The weather must match the timeline.** Storm-damage befores may show rain
   or overcast — but NEVER trees actively bent sideways by wind (the photo is
   taken AFTER the event, during mitigation). Afters are calm; if the after
   is sunny, nothing outside may still look mid-storm.
6. **Windows/openings: pick one story.** If the window shattered, the before
   shows it broken/boarded and the after shows a NEW window (frame may look
   new — that's correct). If the window didn't break, it must be identical in
   both. Never silently swap an undamaged window.

## Process

- Generate the BEFORE first. Then create the AFTER by editing the before
  (same room, same angle) while APPLYING rules 1-6: name explicitly in the
  edit prompt which items get replaced/upgraded and what fresh materials look
  like. "Fully restored" alone produces magical-rewind afters — always
  enumerate the replacements.
- QC every pair side by side: (a) same room? (b) anything damaged that came
  back identical? (c) any set-dressing that survived impossibly? (d) outside
  weather coherent? (e) base imagery rules.

## Per-service scene rules (added 2026-09-26, Katofsky review)

7. **Native landscape only.** Every pair image is generated 16:9 landscape
   (1376x768). NEVER a portrait phone photo pillarboxed with blurred or
   scenic side bars — the slider needs edge-to-edge landscape scenes.
8. **Air duct cleaning**: shoot the duct from INSIDE the room — a wall or
   ceiling register opening with the grille swung aside, interior of the
   duct lit hard by camera flash / technician flashlight (the ACS
   Enterprise reference look: sites/aldredo-moreno duct pair). Before =
   matted dust, lint webs, debris lining the metal; after = same register,
   clean bright galvanized interior, same flash lighting. NEVER a duct
   tube you can see straight through with houses/scenery on the far side —
   ducts connect to the HVAC system, not to the neighborhood.
9. **Sewage cleanup**: interior spaces only (basement floor drain, bath,
   laundry). No outdoor backdrop, no houses.
10. **Fire scenes must burn coherently.** Char is heaviest at a plausible
    origin and gradients outward; drywall/plaster near the origin shows
    real burn-through or deep scorching, not just uniform soot. Any
    combustion appliance at or near the origin (fireplace, stove, space
    heater) is destroyed in the before and REPLACED or absent in the
    after — never the identical unit. No chimney-less open-coal
    fireplaces: open solid-fuel grates need a flue; if a heater is shown,
    make it a plausibly enclosed unit, and it still gets replaced.
